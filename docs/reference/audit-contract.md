# Audit event contract

This is the hand-written event contract. The generated field table is [audit.md](audit.md). Additions follow [the audit-event recipe](../cards/README.md#adding-an-audit-event-kind).

## Kinds and fields

`audit::Kind` has the 32 kinds below and their typed fields, defined in `audit/kind.rs` (the generated [audit.md](audit.md) prints the same list): `journal.open`, `journal.recover`, `write.begin`, `write.end`, `refuse`, `queue.propose`, `queue.accept`, `queue.reject`, `ai.begin`, `ai.call`, `ai.pack`, `ai.paste`, `ask`, `mcp.session`, `mcp.call`, `chat.session`, `chat.pack`, `backup.begin`, `backup.end`, `backup.verify`, `backup.prune`, `restore.begin`, `restore.end`, `settings.change`, `purge`, `policy.change`, `label.change`, `audit.export`, `store.missing`, `store.adopted`, `sync.begin` and `sync.end`. There is no `claim`, `restore.drill` or `migrate` kind. `Kind::ALL` lists the 32; `tests/audit-samples.jsonl` holds one synthetic sample line per kind, with a fixed time. Every kind has a field list in this contract, or the words "envelope only; the raising card adds fields". The fields that other cards fill are typed here, with their words in `vocab.rs`. `mcp.session` (once for each process, after initialization supplies the protocol revision): `protocol` (the revision used), `client_sha256` (`Option<Sha256>`: omitted when initialization supplied no client name, otherwise exactly 64 lowercase hex characters; empty or malformed values refuse) and `allow` (a `LabelAllow`: the labels the `mcp` destination allows this session, after the owner's list is applied). `mcp.call`: `tool`, `args_sha256`, `result_ids` (a list of `RecordRef`), `dest`, `labels`, `policy` and `outcome` (`ok`, `invalid` or `failed`). `chat.session`: `phase` (`start`, `stop` or `idle`) and `calls` (a count). `chat.pack`: `phase` (`save` or `delete`), `name_sha256` and `count`. `ai.pack`: `prompt_sha256` (64 hex characters), `context_ids` (a list of `RecordRef`; empty when the pack is not a context pack), `dest`, `labels` and `policy`. `ai.paste`: `pack_seq` (the sequence number of the `ai.pack`; absent for `new note`, which has no pack) and `output_sha256` (64 hex characters). None of them holds argument text, result text, a question, an answer or a pack name. A sample line of each is in `crates/fieldbook-core/tests/audit-samples.jsonl`.
   **The envelope.** `Journal` writes four keys on every event and no kind sets them: `actor` (the `Principal`), `role`, `store` (the `StoreId` the event acts on) and `via`. `journal.open` also carries `writer` (the store's writer digits; 0 with no company store), `event_schema` (1), `tool_version` (`N.N.N`) and `stores` (a list of the `store_uid` of every store the run opened). The parser denies a missing envelope key as it denies an unknown key.

   **The label fields.** `write.begin` and `write.end` carry `labels` (the union of the labels of the write set); an export also carries `dest`, `path_sha256` and `policy`. `refuse` carries `code`, the `sha256` and `name_sha256` of the file where there is one, `labels` where they are known, a `dest` when the refusal was at a destination, and a `reason` from the closed list `label`, `unlabelled`, `marked`, `no-row`, `expired`, `url`, `client`, `closed`, `mismatch`, `store`, `git`, `authz`, `shipped`, `root`, `place`, `adopt`. A `refuse` also carries `verb` (a `Verb`) where a verb raised it, and `system` and `stream` (each a `StreamKey`) where a sync stream is the subject; each of the three is optional, and there is no other key and no free text. `queue.propose`, `queue.accept` and `queue.reject` carry the `labels` of the proposal. `ai.begin` carries `dest`, `labels` and `policy`; `ai.paste` and `ask` carry `labels`; `backup.begin` carries `labels`, `dest` and `policy`; `restore.begin` carries `labels`, and `dest` and `policy` when the restored folder lies outside the data root. `policy` is the SHA-256 of the policy file in force, and for the floor it is the SHA-256 of the text `fieldbook-floor-policy-1`. `Kind::EGRESS` lists `ai.begin`, `ai.pack`, `mcp.call` and `backup.begin`; each always carries `dest`, `labels` and `policy`, and the parser refuses a `dest` on any event other than `refuse` that lacks `labels` or `policy`; a `refuse` may carry a `dest` with `labels` where known, and never carries `policy`. The seven new kinds are defined here and emitted by later cards: `policy.change` (`policy`, `prev`, `revision`, `outcome`, `labels_n`, `rows_n`), `label.change` (`ids`, `added`, `removed`, `origin`), `audit.export` (`from_seq`, `to_seq`, `head`, `policy`, `store_uid`), `store.missing` (`store_uid`), `store.adopted` (`store_uid`, `mode` (`retired` or `writer`), `writer`), `sync.begin` (`system`, `stream`, `adapter`, `root_sha256`, `policy`) and `sync.end` (`system`, `stream`, `scope` (`full` or `incremental`), `seen`, `proposed`, `present`, `skipped`, `refused`); the `system` and `stream` of both are `StreamKey`s. No credential, name or path goes in the journal.
   Affected references identify the records changed, including direct additions and new rows; filtering by ID checks them. They are empty only for store-level configuration, derived artifacts with no record subject, or format-only writes preserving parsed values. Paths and content are never event fields.

   **The other kinds.** Types come from `vocab.rs`. `journal.open` has the four keys above. `journal.recover`: `reason`, `last_seq` and `last_hash`. `write.begin`: `verb` (a `Verb`), `labels`, `files` (a count of the files in the write set), `file_hashes` (for an in-root write, sorted `{ path_sha256, sha256 }` pairs computed from its root-relative paths and after bytes; absent for an export) and `affected` (sorted, distinct `RecordRef`s); `write.end`: `verb` (a `Verb`), `labels`, the same `affected` references and `outcome` (`ok` or `failed`). `queue.propose`, `queue.accept` and `queue.reject`: the [queue payload](#queue-payloads), installed by review-queue; before it lands their samples have labels and the envelope only. `ai.begin`: `tx` (the `seq` this event gets, written again as a field), `purpose`, `dest`, `labels`, `policy`, `adapter`, `endpoint`, `model`, `template`, `prompt_sha256` and `context_ids`. `ai.call`: `tx` (the `tx` of its `ai.begin`), `outcome` (`ok`, `failed`, `invalid`, `truncated` or `cancelled`), `code`, `attempts`, `output_sha256`, `tokens_in` and `tokens_out`. `ask`: `asked` (the row id; optional), `asked_via` (`search`, `context`, `ask` or `chat`), `question_sha256`, `result_count`, `outcome` and `labels`. `backup.begin`: `labels`, `dest`, `policy` and `snapshot`. `backup.end`: `outcome`, `files`, `bytes`, `manifest_sha256`, `journal_seq`, `journal_head`, `fingerprint` and `target_kind` (`folder`, `it-location` or `synced`). `backup.verify`: `verification` (`snapshot` or `raw`), `outcome` (`ok` or `failed`), and either `target_sha256`, `snapshot`, `manifest_sha256` for a numbered snapshot or `path_sha256` plus optional `public_support_sha256` for a raw copy; the variants cannot be mixed, and the optional field is permitted only with raw `outcome = "ok"` ([Public support verification](#public-support-verification)). `backup.prune`: `snapshots` (the numbers removed) and `reason` (`retention`). `restore.begin`: `snapshot`, `mode` (`drill` or `real`) and `labels`, and `dest` and `policy` when the folder lies outside the data root. `restore.end`: `snapshot`, `mode`, `outcome` and `code`. `settings.change`: `names` (sorted distinct SettingsGroup values) and `baseline` (the typed SettingsBaseline below). `purge`: `what` (`asked` or `calls`) and `count`.

## Live anchors

`audit verify --anchor-seq N --anchor-head HEX` validates the entire live journal and compares the hash at N to the full hash retained in an export manifest. A valid tail is allowed. Both flags are required together and cannot combine with --expect-* or --repair. Missing or mismatched anchors give FB0802; a corrupt tail fails normal verification. Exact --expect-seq/--expect-head checks remain for snapshot restore.

## Source-root fingerprint

The optional sync.begin field `root_sha256: Option<Sha256>` fingerprints the canonical policy-bound root after resolving `drop`. Omit it when no source-policy row exists; that route remains unlabelled. Hash the domain bytes `fieldbook-source-root-1`, one platform byte (0 Unix, 1 Windows), then a u64 big-endian byte length and canonical native path bytes: Unix OsStr bytes, or Windows UTF-16 code units in little-endian order. Use the same canonical path used for the component containment check, with no lossy text conversion. No root path enters the event. The I/O layer canonicalizes/encodes; pure core hashes bytes.

## Queue payloads

The event discriminator remains `kind`. After the envelope, queue payload keys have this order: `labels` (LabelSet), `proposal` (ProposalId), `proposal_kind` (the closed proposal-kind word), optional `sha256` (candidate hash, when that kind has one), then `adapters` (an object in extractor, classifier, correlator, admit, decide order). Each adapter key is optional and its value is a versioned AdapterId; an empty object is valid for a manually produced non-file proposal. Do not fill missing roles with invented adapter names. queue.accept appends `edited` (unsigned count of changed fields, including a changed source choice) and `ids` (sorted distinct RecordRefs of the accepted plan, including an empty-file decision's spec). queue.reject appends `reason` (the closed reject word); propose has neither suffix. All kinds require proposal_kind and adapters, including non-file kinds; candidate-free kinds omit sha256. Duplicate/unknown keys refuse, especially a second kind. Kind words and adapter-id shapes use typed vocabularies, never free text. Completed proposals retain the effective selected kind and accepted references; correlation alternatives remain available in their saved proposal.

## Settings baseline

`SettingsGroup` is the closed list `role`, `backup`, `asked`, `model.order`, `model.endpoint.allow`, `model.endpoint.base_url`, `model.handoff.allow`, `mcp.allow`, `chat`, `other`. `SettingsBaseline { schema: u32, present: bool, file_sha256: Option<Sha256>, groups: BTreeMap<SettingsGroup, Option<Sha256>> }` has schema 1 and exactly one entry per group. Missing file means present=false, no file hash and every group None; an empty file is present=true with the SHA-256 of empty bytes. An absent group differs from a present empty table/array. `other` covers every remaining parsed settings key. This hashes values without retaining settings text, keys outside the closed group list, endpoints or credentials in the journal.

Canonical group encoding uses type tags, u64 big-endian lengths/counts, UTF-8 strings, signed integers as minimal decimal, booleans as 0/1, f64 big-endian bits (one canonical NaN), parsed datetime in canonical TOML form, array order and tables sorted by UTF-8 key bytes. Hash the domain `fieldbook-settings-group-1` plus the encoded value. For each endpoint leaf group, build a table keyed by endpoint name containing that leaf only where present; sort names by UTF-8 bytes, preserve each leaf’s array order, and use None when no endpoint has that leaf. Thus a rename changes the digest and an explicit empty allow list differs from absence. The other group is the residual parsed tree after removing the named groups; prune empty ancestor tables. Use None when that tree is empty. Formatting-only changes affect file_sha256 but no group digest and emit names=[]. Removal changes the corresponding optional group digest; ungrouped changes name other.

Under the journal lock, compare to the last successfully recorded settings.change baseline across all segments; initial recording emits one baseline even for an absent file. An unchanged reopen emits no settings.change; ordinary journal events remain permitted. Invalid TOML follows the existing settings-read error and does not establish a new baseline. Strict AI-key validation belongs to ai-config-and-keys. Update the typed sample and generated event table from this same contract.

## Public support verification

Both packaging producers write one data-only catalogue of the public Corpus support files they actually stage, at `edition/evidence/corpus-support.toml` inside the Raw and Site edition trees, and an exact byte copy at `out/dist/corpus-support.toml` with the separate digest `out/dist/corpus-support.sha256`. Paths inside the file are relative to the source Corpus root and carry no `corpus/` prefix. The file is metadata about public bytes, not a copy of those bytes, and it may bind a support file that does not itself ship (`AGENTS.md`, a source template); the existing policy that omits those files from the edition payload is unchanged. Both producers call the one pure catalogue helper in `crates/fieldbook-core/src/manifest.rs`; no dependency, port or generic serialization framework is added.

Strict schema 1:

```toml
support_schema = 1
corpus_schema = 1
commit = "1111111111111111111111111111111111111111"

[[file]]
path = "README.md"
sha256 = "c9bda0b40940d8ac5e2a25f44febdb435b6f38fdcad65eaa4c1ab5e488523c06"
```

This is a synthetic one-entry byte-format vector. Its `README.md` body is exactly UTF-8 `synthetic public support` followed by LF. The complete catalogue above, starting at `support_schema` and ending with exactly one LF after the final quote, is 194 bytes and has SHA-256 `088869d68222f925aa1e96f5d06a7bf5491188f04689209b8f3b28301349514c`. It is not a production catalogue or a release claim.

`support_schema` is exactly 1. `corpus_schema` is the supported positive Corpus schema version. `commit` is exactly 40 lowercase hexadecimal characters and identifies the source commit; it is not proof of origin. Each `[[file]]` has exactly `path` and a full lowercase 64-hex `sha256`. Unknown or duplicate keys, duplicate paths, wrong types, unsupported schemas and malformed hashes refuse. Bound the input to 1 MiB and 4096 file entries.

Producer bytes are UTF-8 without a byte order mark, LF endings, field order as above, a blank line before each `[[file]]`, entries sorted by the path's UTF-8 bytes, and one final LF. Quote path strings with the existing TOML string rules and reject path control characters. Readers hash the exact file bytes supplied before parsing: no TOML reserialization, whitespace normalization or hash of a parsed map. A comment or formatting edit changes the digest. The file contains no digest of itself and no self-entry.

Paths must be canonical relative paths: `/` separators, and no absolute, drive or UNC form, backslash, empty component, `.` or `..`, NUL or control character, link or junction escape, or normalized-path collision. The file names only recognized support roles: the exact root files `AGENTS.md`, `ASSISTANT.md`, `README.md`, `corpus.toml`, `changes.md` and `scan-allow.toml`; the registered page templates `templates/<type>.md` and workbook CSV templates `templates/workbooks/*.csv`; `eval/regression.toml`; and optional `pipeline/rules.toml` when its producer exists. Reuse the actual schema, template and layout registrations. No extension or directory alone declares a file public, and an unknown role or path refuses rather than expanding the supported set. Registering another file through an existing supported template role is data; its current bytes or hash never enter code.

The producer takes one coherent, validated public Corpus generation under the existing edition PublicAct and marking checks. It includes every present recognized support file and refuses an unsupported or ambiguous registration. It includes no pages, register contents, originals, extraction files, company content, settings, State, site, journal or this catalogue; those keep their existing kinds. An optional absent support file has no entry. The runtime checks every support file it actually copies; a catalogue entry for a nonshipped or absent source file does not itself require that file to be installed, and existing layout-required files remain required by their own checks.

### Artifact and independent digest delivery

The digest sidecar `out/dist/corpus-support.sha256` is exactly one existing `manifest::write_sums` line, `<full lowercase SHA-256>  corpus-support.toml` and one LF. The existing two-ZIP `out/dist/SHA256SUMS` format is unchanged. The catalogue payload travels inside the edition ZIP; the small digest accompanies the ZIP hash list through the same already-approved independent hash route. Never derive the expected digest from a file beside the candidate catalogue. No additional channel or PKI is introduced. Verification of the sidecar at home runs from `out/dist`, where its named file exists.

The catalogue and digest are public release evidence in the existing immutable archive flow. The explicit artifact upload, copy and verification lists of both producers and the shared archive helpers name them so they are not omitted. This is evidence, not a third payload ZIP. Verify byte equality between the staged catalogue, the `out/dist` copy, the internal checksum entry and the separately published digest. Preserve deterministic payload comparisons and the existing attestation exceptions; the catalogue is never an attestation exception.

### Optional verification on the raw verifier

```text
fieldbook backup verify --raw PATH --support-catalogue FILE --expect-support-sha256 HEX
```

Both flags are required together and only with `--raw`; either combined with a numbered-snapshot selector is a usage error, exit 2. `HEX` is a full lowercase SHA-256 received through the independent hash route. It is an explicit expected value, never read automatically from adjacent `SHA256SUMS`, `edition.toml`, the catalogue, the raw copy's journal or a default. `FILE` is only an input locator. A missing or invalid digest is not an instruction to fall back to ordinary raw verification.

The optional mode first performs ordinary raw integrity verification, then hashes the exact catalogue bytes against `HEX`, strictly parses the supported schema, checks the Corpus schema, and compares each present recognized support path in the raw copy's public Corpus root with its declared full hash. It uses the raw verifier's existing layout resolution, not arbitrary folder names; zero or ambiguous public Corpus roots refuses this optional mode. A stale or missing binding for a present file refuses. A descriptor may bind absent nonshipped source support, as above. No proof is created for Overlay, Working, arbitrary State files, unknown paths or the raw copy as a whole. Ordinary `raw copy consistent` says nothing about public provenance, admission, claim verification or eligibility for an outbound snapshot.

FB0811 with exit 1 covers a bad catalogue structure, digest or binding; FB0810 with exit 1 covers a present selected support file whose bytes differ. Original, extraction and journal failures and I/O exit 3 keep their existing meanings. No new diagnostic code is allocated. The successful ordinary line remains `raw copy consistent`; only successful optional completion adds `public support catalogue verified: <first 12 hex>`. No success or provenance event is written after an optional-mode failure.

### Typed journal binding and retained catalogue

The typed event variant extends without a new event kind:

```rust
BackupVerified::Raw {
    path_sha256: Sha256,
    public_support_sha256: Option<Sha256>,
}
```

On the wire, the event retains `verification = "raw"`, `outcome` and `path_sha256`; the optional field is serialized only as `public_support_sha256`. It is permitted only with raw `outcome = "ok"`, and its value is the exact expected-and-verified catalogue hash. A failed event cannot confer it. The Snapshot variant remains exactly its existing `target_sha256`, `snapshot` and `manifest_sha256`, and forbids both raw fields. Ordinary raw success omits the new field. Raw verification still does not mark a numbered snapshot, update LastBackup, change backup age or satisfy a recovery or delivery gate.

After all validation and the current policy and admission checks, the exact catalogue bytes are retained atomically at the logical State-relative path `public-support/<full-catalogue-sha256>.toml`, under the actual State root (`.fieldbook/` at home or the Layout's laptop State root). Retention reuses the existing exclusive store lease, `Journal::state_scope` and atomic writer; no raw filesystem write or new writer door is added. Refuse an existing hash-path whose bytes differ. The retained copy is durable before the successful raw event is appended. If the copy or the event fails, exit through the existing failure path and grant no proof. A retry may reuse identical bytes; an orphan copy without a matching success event remains untrusted and is not silently discarded to obtain a clean backup.

`public-support/<hash>.toml` is known text-bearing support metadata, never a text-free control. Its full filename hash, parsed schema and successful event binding must match before it supplies any fact. Its exact bytes are classified through the existing verified edition PublicAct and current markings, and current State admission is enforced before retention. It is machine-retained public edition evidence, not owner-authored settings, and it is no excuse to give all State a public default. The existing owner-supplied, settings, cache and default/admission rules are unchanged. Unknown, malformed or unanchored cache content contributes `unlabelled`.

The binding is consumed from the run's already-admitted live journal context, not from an arbitrary raw candidate's journal. A raw copy may contain a self-consistent journal or forged `backup.verify` text; reading it for integrity does not install its events as provenance, and no first-use path bootstraps public trust from those candidate events. After a real restore, the copied journal and catalogue are usable only through the restore's existing verified snapshot and journal-head context. Catalogue, path and hash identity is portable with that verified recovery copy; validity never depends on the old machine's absolute folder spelling.

Normal operation uses verified schema-valid journal history. The existing append-refused backup recovery path may consume facts only from the schema-valid, hash-verified admitted prefix before the first fault, never an unverified tail or a random retained `journal.broken-*` folder. It appends no new support verification while append is refused. If repair starts a new admitted chain without a usable binding, re-run optional raw verification with the independent digest; do not infer the old fact from cache presence. Recovery tests establish their permitted support facts before planting the intended chain fault. Schema-invalid journal or repair bytes still contribute `unlabelled` and retain their existing floor behavior.
