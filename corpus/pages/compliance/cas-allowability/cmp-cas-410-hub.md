## Purpose

Cost Accounting Standard 410 establishes criteria for allocating business unit general and administrative expenses to final cost objectives based on their beneficial or causal relationship. This concept hub sets out what the standard requires and links the detailed reference and allocation pages across the corpus.

## The rule

The purpose of CAS 410 is to provide criteria for allocating business unit general and administrative expenses to business unit final cost objectives based on their beneficial or causal relationship [@CL-EST-2910]. Business unit general and administrative expenses represent the cost of the management and administration of the business unit as a whole [@CL-EST-2911]. The standard also provides criteria for allocating home office expenses received by a segment to the cost objectives of that segment [@CL-EST-2912], increasing the likelihood of objectivity in allocating expenses to final cost objectives and comparability of cost data among contractors in similar circumstances [@CL-EST-2913]. Under the fundamental requirement, business unit general and administrative expenses are grouped in a separate indirect cost pool and allocated only to final cost objectives [@CL-CMP-0213]. The pool is allocated to final cost objectives of the cost accounting period by a cost input base representing the total activity of the business unit, selected as the base that best represents the total activity of a typical cost accounting period [@CL-CMP-0214; @CL-CMP-8198]. Final cost objectives that receive benefits significantly different from other final cost objectives receive their allocation by special allocation [@CL-CMP-0215]. Costs that do not satisfy the definition of general and administrative expense may remain in the pool unless they can be allocated to business unit cost objectives on a beneficial or causal relationship best measured by a base other than a cost input base [@CL-CMP-8199; @CL-CMP-8200].

Home office expenses received by a segment are allocated to segment cost objectives as required by the standard [@CL-CMP-7887]. A business unit transitioning from a cost of sales or sales base to a cost input base may use the transition method described in the appendix [@CL-CMP-3444], under which the inventory suspense account equals the beginning inventory of contracts subject to the CAS clause for the cost accounting period in which the unit must first allocate costs under the standard [@CL-CMP-3445]. The standard shall not apply to contracts and grants with state, local, and Federally recognized Indian tribal governments [@CL-CMP-8657]. CAS 410 became effective on April 17, 1992, and contractors with prior fully covered contracts continue applicability upon receipt of an applicable contract [@CL-CMP-3422], while contractors with no previous contracts subject to the standard apply it beginning with the next full fiscal year after receipt of an applicable contract [@CL-CMP-3423].

## How it works

To allocate means to assign an item or group of items of cost to one or more cost objectives, including both direct assignment and reassignment of a share from an indirect cost pool [@CL-RTE-9298]. A cost objective is a function, organizational subdivision, contract or other work unit for which cost data are desired [@CL-RTE-9296], and a final cost objective is one that has allocated to it both direct and indirect costs and is a final accumulation point [@CL-RTE-9297]. General and administrative expense means any management, financial and other expense incurred by or allocated to a business unit for the general management and administration of the business unit as a whole [@CL-RTE-0671], excluding management expenses whose beneficial or causal relationship to cost objectives can be more directly measured by a base other than a cost input base representing total activity [@CL-RTE-0672]. Cost input means the cost, except general and administrative expenses, allocable to the production of goods and services during a cost accounting period [@CL-RTE-9249].

The cost input base selected to represent total activity during a cost accounting period may be total cost input, value-added cost input or single element cost input, judged on the circumstances of each business unit [@CL-RTE-0675; @CL-RTE-0013]. A total cost input base is generally acceptable as an appropriate measure [@CL-RTE-0677]. Value-added cost input is total cost input less material and subcontract costs, and must be used where including material and subcontract costs would significantly distort allocation and non-labor costs are significant [@CL-RTE-0676; @CL-RTE-0014]. A single element base is inappropriate where it is an insignificant part of the total cost of some final cost objectives [@CL-RTE-0678]. The pool may be combined with other expenses if the base is appropriate for both and pool components are identified separately [@CL-RTE-0673; @CL-RTE-0674], and insignificant non-G&A expenses may be included in the pool [@CL-RTE-0679]. The base must include all significant elements of cost input representing total activity [@CL-RTE-9250], but excludes raw materials not charged to production during the period [@CL-CMP-2812], while stock inventory costs enter the base in the year produced rather than when issued [@CL-CMP-2813; @CL-CMP-2891].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A business unit incurs 1,200,000 in general and administrative expenses during a cost accounting period across two final cost objectives, Contract A and Contract B. Total cost input is 12,000,000, comprising 8,000,000 on Contract A and 4,000,000 on Contract B. Under a total cost input base, the G&A rate is 10 percent (1,200,000 divided by 12,000,000), allocating 800,000 to Contract A and 400,000 to Contract B. If material and subcontract costs of 5,000,000 on Contract A and 1,000,000 on Contract B would significantly distort the allocation, the unit uses a value-added cost input base. Total value-added base is 6,000,000 (12,000,000 minus 6,000,000 in material and subcontract costs), split evenly at 3,000,000 for Contract A and 3,000,000 for Contract B. The resulting value-added G&A rate is 20 percent (1,200,000 divided by 6,000,000), allocating 600,000 to Contract A and 600,000 to Contract B. This example is illustrative only, and the contract itself governs.

## Never confuse

CAS 410 governs how a business unit groups its general and administrative expenses into a separate indirect cost pool and allocates them only to final cost objectives using a cost input base representing total activity [@CL-CMP-0213; @CL-CMP-0214], whereas CAS 403 governs allocating home office expenses to segments and explicitly stops short of reallocating those expenses to individual contracts or other cost objectives [@CL-EST-2495; @CL-EST-2499]. When a segment receives home office expenses, their subsequent allocation to segment cost objectives is governed by the criteria of CAS 410 [@CL-CMP-7887; @CL-EST-2912].
