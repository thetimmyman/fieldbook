## Purpose

Shows how the cost of special tooling and special test equipment is charged, and when that cost may only be recovered through depreciation or amortization.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-0863] |
| Codified citation | FAR 31.205-40 [@CL-CMP-0863] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-0863] |
| Applies to | Not established. |

The general rule is that special tooling and special test equipment used in performing one or more Government contracts is allowable and is allocated to the specific Government contract or contracts for which it was acquired [@CL-CMP-0863]. Items acquired by the contractor before the effective date of the contract, and replacements of such items, whether or not altered or adapted for use in performing the contract, are an exception to that allocation rule [@CL-CMP-1401].

Those items, and items the contract schedule specifically excludes, are allowable only as depreciation or amortization [@CL-CMP-0864; @CL-CMP-0865].

## How it works

An item is disqualified as special tooling or special test equipment where, at relatively minor expense, it can be made suitable for general purpose use and has a value as such commensurate with its value as special tooling or special test equipment [@CL-CMP-0866]. For items disqualified on that basis, the cost of adapting them for use under the contract and the cost of returning them to their prior configuration are allowable [@CL-CMP-0867]. So the acquisition date and the contract schedule decide whether the cost is allocated to the contract directly or is allowable only as depreciation or amortization [@CL-CMP-0864].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contract needs a 300,000 test rig bought for that contract, so its cost is allowable and allocated to the contract. The company also holds a 90,000 tool bought before the contract took effect, and a 20,000 item the contract schedule excludes; both are allowable only as depreciation or amortization. A general purpose jig, disqualified as special tooling because minor work made it suitable for general use and its general value matches its value as tooling, needed 4,000 of work to suit the contract. That 4,000 adaptation cost is allowable.

## Never confuse

An item disqualified because it can be made suitable for general purpose use is not the same as an item the contract schedule specifically excludes. For the disqualified item the section makes the adaptation and return costs allowable [@CL-CMP-0867], and for the excluded item it allows the cost only as depreciation or amortization [@CL-CMP-0865]. The general test for a charge is in [Allowability factors](../cas-allowability/cmp-allowability-factors.md).

## Company calibration

Whether tooling and test equipment purchases are identified by acquisition date and contract exclusion so each cost takes the right route into the pools. [@Q-044]
