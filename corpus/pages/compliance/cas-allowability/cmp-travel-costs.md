## Purpose

Sets allowability boundaries, maximum per diem limitations, and documentation requirements for contractor business travel.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-0109] |
| Codified citation | FAR 31.205-46 [@CL-CMP-0109] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 31.205-46 [@CL-CMP-8933; @CL-CMP-8934] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-0109] |
| Applies to | Government contracts subject to the cost principles [@CL-CMP-0109] |

Costs incurred by contractor personnel on official company business are allowable, subject to the limitations contained in the travel cost principle [@CL-CMP-0109]; those limitations include a daily maximum per diem for lodging, meals, and incidental expenses [@CL-CMP-8820; @CL-CMP-9750]. When actual costs above the maximum per diem rates are allowed in special or unusual situations, one of the conditions that must all be met is that a receipt is required for each expenditure of $75.00 or more [@CL-CMP-0110]. Airfare costs in excess of the lowest priced airfare available to the contractor during normal business hours are unallowable except in the listed circumstances, and for excess airfare to be allowable the applicable condition must be documented and justified [@CL-CMP-0111; @CL-CMP-0812; @CL-CMP-0813].

## How it works

Except in the unusual situations described next [@CL-CMP-8583], costs incurred for lodging, meals, and incidental expenses are reasonable and allowable only to the extent that they do not exceed on a daily basis the maximum per diem rates in effect at the time of travel, as set forth in the Federal Travel Regulation for travel in the contiguous United States and the other regulations the paragraph lists [@CL-CMP-8820; @CL-CMP-9750]. In special or unusual situations, actual costs above the maximum per diem rates are allowable, up to the higher amounts authorized for Federal civilian employees [@CL-CMP-8583], only if all of these conditions are met: a condition warranting the actual expense method under the cited travel regulations exists [@CL-CMP-8584], a written justification is approved by an officer of the contractor's organization or designee [@CL-CMP-8585], advance contracting officer approval is obtained for repetitive or continuing use in a particular area [@CL-CMP-8813], and the actual costs are documented, with a receipt for each expenditure of $75.00 or more [@CL-CMP-0110]. Airfare above the lowest priced airfare available during normal business hours is unallowable unless one of the listed conditions applies, such as circuitous routing, unreasonable hours or the traveler's physical or medical needs, and that condition is documented and justified [@CL-CMP-0111; @CL-CMP-0812; @CL-CMP-0813].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 31 text in place of the codified text [@CL-CMP-9350], effective immediately under Revision 1 signed on 2026-08-04 [@CL-CMP-9351]. The RFO section 31.205-46 keeps the rule that costs incurred by contractor personnel on official company business are allowable, subject to the limitations in the section [@CL-CMP-8933]. In unusual situations it allows actual costs above the maximum per diem rates, up to the higher amounts authorized for Federal civilian employees [@CL-CMP-8934]. For those higher amounts to be allowable, the following conditions must be met [@CL-CMP-8935], including a written justification approved by an official of the contractor's organization [@CL-CMP-8936], advance approval from the contracting officer if the higher actual expense method is used repetitively or on a continuing basis in a particular area [@CL-CMP-8937], and documentation in accordance with the contractor's established practices, with a receipt for each expenditure of $75.00 or more [@CL-CMP-8938]. It says the maximum per diem rates generally would not be a reasonable daily charge when no lodging costs are incurred and/or on partial travel days [@CL-CMP-8939], and that the contractor must make appropriate downward adjustments from the maximum per diem rates under these circumstances [@CL-CMP-8940]. It keeps the rule that costs must be allowable only if the date and place, the purpose of the trip, and the traveler's name and title or relationship to the contractor are documented [@CL-CMP-8941]. It makes airfare in excess of the lowest priced airfare available to the contractor during normal business hours unallowable except when the lowest price airfare results in one of the listed circumstances [@CL-CMP-8942; @CL-CMP-8943], and says excess airfare meeting those criteria is allowable if documented and justified [@CL-CMP-8944].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An employee travels on official business where the standard per diem ceiling for lodging is 150 per night. The employee incurs actual lodging of 220 per night due to an emergency conference. If a condition warranting the actual expense method under the cited travel regulations exists, 220 does not exceed the higher amount authorized for Federal civilian employees, and the written justification is approved by a company officer and retained, the full 220 is allowable, with a receipt required because the lodging expenditure is 75.00 or more.

## Never confuse

Official travel on company business is allowable subject to the limitations of the travel cost principle [@CL-CMP-0109], including the daily per diem ceilings for lodging, meals and incidental expenses [@CL-CMP-8820; @CL-CMP-9750], and only if the date and place, purpose of the trip, and traveler's name and title or relationship to the contractor are documented [@CL-CMP-8819]. Using the higher actual expense method requires, among other conditions, an approved written justification and a receipt for each expenditure of $75.00 or more rather than automatic reimbursement [@CL-CMP-8585; @CL-CMP-0110]. Including unallowable travel costs in rate submissions exposes the contractor to penalties; see [Unallowable-cost penalties](cmp-unallowable-cost-penalty.md).

The codified text says that for airfare above the lowest priced airfare to be allowable the applicable condition must be documented and justified [@CL-CMP-0813], whereas the RFO paragraph (b)(2) says airfare above the lowest priced airfare that meets the paragraph (b)(1) criteria is allowable if documented and justified [@CL-CMP-8944].

For the higher actual-cost amounts, the codified text says in special or unusual situations [@CL-CMP-8583], whereas the RFO text says in unusual situations [@CL-CMP-8934]. The codified text says all of the following conditions must be met [@CL-CMP-8584], whereas the RFO text says the following conditions must be met [@CL-CMP-8935]. The codified text has the written justification approved by an officer of the contractor's organization or designee [@CL-CMP-8585], whereas the RFO text has it approved by an official of the contractor's organization [@CL-CMP-8936].
