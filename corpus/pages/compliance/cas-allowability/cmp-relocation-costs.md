## Purpose

Sets out allowable and unallowable relocation costs, ceiling limits on real estate reimbursements, and refund requirements when an employee resigns early.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-0806] |
| Codified citation | FAR 31.205-35 [@CL-CMP-0806] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |

Relocation costs are costs incident to the permanent change of assigned work location, for a period of 12 months or more, of an existing employee or upon recruitment of a new employee [@CL-CMP-0806]. The listed types of relocation costs are allowable as noted, subject to the limitations in paragraphs (b) and (f) of the principle [@CL-CMP-1123].

The listed types include travel of the employee and immediate family and transportation of the household and personal effects [@CL-CMP-1124], costs of finding a new home and temporary lodging [@CL-CMP-1125], closing costs on the residence being sold [@CL-CMP-1126], continuing ownership costs of the vacant former residence [@CL-CMP-1127], and other necessary and reasonable expenses normally incident to relocation [@CL-CMP-1128].

The closing costs on the residence being sold, when added to the continuing ownership costs of the vacant former residence, shall not exceed 14 percent of the sales price of the property sold [@CL-CMP-0807].

Costs incident to acquiring a home in the new work location are not allowable for employees who were not homeowners before the relocation, and the total shall not exceed 5 percent of the purchase price of the new home [@CL-CMP-1129]. Mortgage interest differential payments are not allowable for employees who were not homeowners before the relocation, and the total is limited to an amount determined under the following subparagraphs [@CL-CMP-1130]. That amount is the difference between the mortgage interest rates of the old and new residences times the current balance of the old mortgage times 3 years [@CL-CMP-1131]. When such payments are made on a lump-sum basis and the employee leaves or is transferred again in less than 3 years, the amount initially recognized is proportionately adjusted to reflect payments only for the actual time of the relocation [@CL-CMP-1132].

Rental differential payments cover situations where relocated employees retain ownership of a vacated home in the old location and rent at the new location [@CL-CMP-1133]. The rented quarters must be comparable to those vacated, and the payments may not exceed the actual rental costs for the new home, less the fair market rent for the vacated home times 3 years [@CL-CMP-1134].

The costs must also meet the paragraph (b) criteria. The move must be for the benefit of the employer [@CL-CMP-1135]. Reimbursement must be in accordance with an established policy or practice that is consistently followed by the employer and is designed to motivate employees to relocate promptly and economically, and the costs must not be otherwise unallowable under the cost principles subpart [@CL-CMP-1136]. Amounts reimbursed shall not exceed the employee's actual expenses, except as paragraphs (b)(5) and (b)(6) provide [@CL-CMP-1137].

For miscellaneous costs of the type described in paragraph (a)(5), a lump-sum amount not to exceed $5,000 may be allowed in lieu of actual costs [@CL-CMP-0808]. Reimbursement on a lump-sum basis may also be allowed for listed relocation costs when adequately supported by data on the individual elements comprising the build-up of the lump-sum amount [@CL-CMP-1138]. Those costs are the costs of finding a new home and of travel to the new location, but not transportation of household goods [@CL-CMP-1139], and costs of temporary lodging [@CL-CMP-1148]. When reimbursement on a lump-sum basis is used, any adjustments to reflect actual costs are unallowable [@CL-CMP-1140].

Loss on the sale of a home is unallowable [@CL-CMP-1141]. So are real estate brokers' fees and commissions, costs of litigation, real and personal property insurance against damage or loss of property, and mortgage life insurance incident to acquiring a home in the new location [@CL-CMP-1142]. Owner's title policy insurance not previously carried on the old residence, and property taxes and operating or maintenance costs, are also unallowable, although the cost of a mortgage title policy is allowable [@CL-CMP-1143]. Continuing mortgage principal payments on a residence being sold, and costs of furnishing equity or nonequity loans or arranging lower-than-market rate mortgage loans, are unallowable too [@CL-CMP-1144].

If relocation costs for an employee have been allowed as an allocable indirect or direct cost, and the employee resigns within 12 months for reasons within the employee's control, the contractor shall refund or credit the relocation costs to the Government [@CL-CMP-0809].

Relocation costs, both outgoing and return, of employees hired for specific contracts or long-term field projects are allowable if, among other conditions, the term of employment is 12 months or more [@CL-CMP-1145]. The employment agreement must specifically limit the duration of employment to the time spent on the contract or field project [@CL-CMP-1146], and must provide for return relocation to the employee's permanent and principal home immediately prior to the outgoing relocation, or other location of equal or lesser cost [@CL-CMP-1150]. The costs must be determined under the rules of paragraphs (a) through (d) [@CL-CMP-1147]. The costs to return employees released upon completion of field assignments under their employment agreements are not subject to the refund or credit requirement [@CL-CMP-1149].

## How it works

The principle first lists the relocation costs that are allowable as noted, then adds criteria every cost must meet and a short list of unallowable items [@CL-CMP-1123; @CL-CMP-1141]. Home sale and ownership costs share one ceiling, and home purchase, mortgage differential and rental differential payments carry their own limits [@CL-CMP-0807; @CL-CMP-1129; @CL-CMP-1131; @CL-CMP-1134]. A lump-sum option may replace actual costs for miscellaneous expenses and for supported home-finding, travel and lodging amounts [@CL-CMP-0808; @CL-CMP-1138]. If the employee resigns early for reasons within the employee's control, the allowed costs are refunded or credited [@CL-CMP-0809].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An existing employee is permanently transferred to a new facility. The employee sells their former residence for 400,000, incurring 35,000 in closing costs and 25,000 in continuing ownership costs while the home was on the market, totaling 60,000. Under the 14 percent ceiling, the maximum allowable reimbursement for these combined residence costs is 56,000 (which is 14 percent of 400,000), leaving the remaining 4,000 unallowable. The contractor also pays an allowable lump sum of 5,000 for miscellaneous expenses. Nine months later, the employee voluntarily resigns to accept another job; because the resignation was within 12 months for reasons within the employee's control, the contractor must credit the 61,000 of allowed relocation costs back to the Government.

## Never confuse

Relocation costs are costs incident to a permanent change of assigned work location for a period of 12 months or more [@CL-CMP-0806]; see [Travel costs](cmp-travel-costs.md) for travel cost rules. Loss on the sale of a home and continuing mortgage principal payments on a residence being sold are expressly unallowable relocation items [@CL-CMP-1141; @CL-CMP-1144]; see [Compensation for personal services](cmp-compensation-personal-services.md).
