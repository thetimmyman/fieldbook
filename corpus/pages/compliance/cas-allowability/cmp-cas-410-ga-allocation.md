## Purpose

Shows how a business unit groups and allocates its general and administrative expenses.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | CAS [@CL-CMP-0213] |
| Codified citation | 48 CFR 9904.410-40(a) [@CL-CMP-0213] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-0213] |
| Applies to | Business unit general and administrative expenses [@CL-CMP-0213] |

Business unit general and administrative expenses are grouped in a separate indirect cost pool and allocated only to final cost objectives [@CL-CMP-0213]. The pool is allocated to final cost objectives of the period by a cost input base representing the total activity of the business unit, except as provided in subparagraph (b)(2) of the subsection [@CL-CMP-0214]. The allocation of the pool to any particular final cost objectives which receive benefits significantly different from the benefits accruing to other final cost objectives is determined by special allocation [@CL-CMP-0215].

## How it works

The cost input base selected is the one that best represents the total activity of a typical cost accounting period. The base must be a cost input base representing the total activity of the business unit, except for final cost objectives subject to special allocation. The standard provides criteria for allocating home office expenses received by a segment to that segment's cost objectives [@CL-EST-2912]. Home office expenses received by a segment shall be allocated to segment cost objectives as required by 9904.410-50(g) [@CL-CMP-7887]. Costs that do not meet the definition of G&A expense but that the business unit has classified as G&A can remain in the pool unless they can be allocated to business unit cost objectives on a beneficial or causal relationship best measured by a base other than a cost input base.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A business unit has 800,000 of general and administrative expenses and a total cost input base of 8,000,000, giving a ten percent rate applied to each final cost objective. One contract receives significantly different benefit from the G&A expense pool than the others, so its share is set by special allocation instead of the ten percent rate.

## Never confuse

General and administrative expense is not the same as the overhead of a producing department. General and administrative expense is grouped in its own pool and spread over the total activity of the business unit, while a department overhead pool is spread over the activity of that department.
