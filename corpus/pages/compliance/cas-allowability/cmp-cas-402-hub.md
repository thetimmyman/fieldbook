## Purpose

Cost Accounting Standard 402 requires that all costs incurred for the same purpose in like circumstances are treated as direct costs only or indirect costs only with respect to final cost objectives. This concept hub sets out what the standard requires and links the detailed reference and estimation pages across the corpus.

## The rule

The purpose of CAS 402 is to require that each type of cost is allocated only once and on only one basis to any contract or other cost objective [@CL-EST-2906]. Allocation criteria must be identical for all similar cost objectives [@CL-EST-2907] to guard against overcharging and prevent double counting [@CL-EST-2908], which occurs most commonly when cost items are allocated directly without eliminating like items from indirect pools allocated to that objective [@CL-EST-2909]. Under the fundamental requirement, all costs incurred for the same purpose in like circumstances are either direct costs only or indirect costs only with respect to final cost objectives [@CL-CMP-0204]. No final cost objective may receive an indirect allocation of any cost if other costs incurred for the same purpose in like circumstances have been included as a direct cost of that or any other final cost objective [@CL-CMP-0205]. By distinguishing direct from indirect costs and describing criteria and circumstances for items that are sometimes direct and sometimes indirect, the contractor's Disclosure Statement is determinative of whether costs are incurred for the same purpose [@CL-CMP-8034; @CL-CMP-0206].

If a contractor has not submitted a Disclosure Statement, whether specific costs are directly allocable is based on the practices used at the time of proposal [@CL-CMP-8035]. Whenever costs serving the same purpose cannot equitably be indirectly allocated under disclosed practices, the contractor may either reassign all such costs to provide an equitable distribution or assign all such costs directly to their specific final cost objectives [@CL-CMP-8806; @CL-CMP-8807], and must amend its Disclosure Statement accordingly [@CL-CMP-8808]. For practical reasons, a direct cost of minor dollar amount may be treated as indirect if consistently applied to all final cost objectives [@CL-CMP-8809] and the results are substantially the same as direct treatment [@CL-CMP-8810]. CAS 402 provides no exemption [@CL-CMP-8652], took effect on April 17, 1992 [@CL-CMP-8653], and applies to all contractors under full or modified coverage [@CL-CMP-1515].

## How it works

A direct cost is defined as any cost identified specifically with a particular final cost objective [@CL-CMP-2782]. An indirect cost is defined as any cost not directly identified with a single final cost objective, but identified with two or more final cost objectives or with at least one intermediate cost objective [@CL-CMP-2783].

In the travel illustration, travel costs of personnel whose time is accounted for as direct labor on contracts are incurred for the same purpose and may no longer remain in indirect cost pools for allocation to any covered contract [@CL-CMP-2784]. Conversely, in the tooling illustration, allocating general purpose tooling from an indirect pool while directly charging special tooling to a contract does not violate the standard when disclosed criteria establish that the two types of cost were not incurred for the same purpose [@CL-CMP-2786; @CL-CMP-2787].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor maintains an indirect pool of 500,000 that includes 40,000 of travel expenses. Contract A receives a 25 percent allocation of the pool, amounting to 125,000 (25 percent of 500,000), which embeds 10,000 of travel cost (25 percent of 40,000). If the contractor also assigns that same 40,000 of travel directly to Contract A, the contract bears travel twice: 40,000 directly and 10,000 indirectly through the pool, resulting in a 10,000 double count. Removing the 40,000 from the indirect pool leaves 460,000 (500,000 minus 40,000), reducing Contract A's 25 percent indirect share to 115,000 (25 percent of 460,000) and eliminating the duplicated cost. This example is illustrative only, and the contract itself governs.

## Never confuse

CAS 402 governs direct and indirect cost classifications by requiring that costs incurred for the same purpose in like circumstances are direct only or indirect only [@CL-CMP-0204], unlike CAS 401, which requires that practices used in estimating costs match those used in accumulating and reporting costs [@CL-CMP-0201]. Furthermore, CAS 402 is not the allowability standard of FAR 31.201-2(a), under which compliance with applicable cost accounting standards is only one necessary condition among several requirements including reasonableness and allocability [@CL-CMP-0101]; a cost may fully satisfy CAS 402 classification rules and yet remain unallowable.
