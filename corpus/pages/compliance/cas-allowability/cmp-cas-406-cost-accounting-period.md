## Purpose

Shows which period a contractor uses to accumulate costs and to establish the allocation base.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | CAS [@CL-CMP-0210] |
| Codified citation | 48 CFR 9904.406-40(a) [@CL-CMP-0210] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-0210] |
| Applies to | A contractor's cost accounting period [@CL-CMP-0210] |

A contractor uses its fiscal year as its cost accounting period, subject to the listed exceptions [@CL-CMP-0210]. The same period is used to accumulate costs in an indirect cost pool and to establish the allocation base, unless the parties agree to a different period for the base [@CL-CMP-0211]. When the fiscal year changes, the contractor uses a transitional period of a length other than a year [@CL-CMP-0212].

## How it works

Costs of an indirect function that exists for only part of a cost accounting period may be allocated to cost objectives of that same part of the period. An annual period other than the fiscal year may be used as the cost accounting period if its use is an established practice of the contractor. The contracting parties may also agree to use a different period for establishing an allocation base [@CL-CMP-0211].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor accumulates 1,200,000 of overhead and a matching allocation base over its fiscal year. Mid-cycle it changes its fiscal year, so it uses a transitional period to split the overhead between the old period and the new one rather than spreading it across a full year.

## Never confuse

The cost accounting period is not the same as the allocation base. The period is the time span used to accumulate costs; the base is the measure of activity those costs are spread over. The rule requires the same period for both unless the parties agree otherwise.
