## Purpose

Establishes the criteria a contractor cost must meet to be allowable on government contracts and the documentation required to support it.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-0101] |
| Codified citation | FAR 31.201-2 [@CL-CMP-0101] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 31.201-2 [@CL-CMP-0101] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-0101] |
| Applies to | Government contracts subject to the cost principles [@CL-CMP-0101] |

A cost is allowable only when it complies with reasonableness, allocability, applicable Cost Accounting Standards or generally accepted accounting principles, terms of the contract, and limitations in FAR subpart 31.2 [@CL-CMP-0101]. A contractor is responsible for accounting for costs appropriately and for maintaining records, including supporting documentation, adequate to demonstrate that costs claimed have been incurred, are allocable to the contract and comply with the applicable cost principles, and the contracting officer may disallow all or part of a claimed cost that is inadequately supported [@CL-CMP-0102].

## How it works

Allowability is tested against five cumulative requirements, and failing any single test makes the cost unallowable. When accounting practices depart from the cost principles, excess costs resulting from the inconsistency are unallowable. Certain cost principles incorporate the measurement, assignment and allocability rules of selected cost accounting standards and limit allowability to the amounts those standards produce; on contracts that are not CAS-covered, only the standards or portions specifically made applicable by the cost principles apply, and only to determine allowability, without subjecting the business unit to any other CAS rules. The contractor bears the responsibility to maintain supporting documentation adequate to demonstrate that costs claimed have been incurred and are allocable.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor incurs 100,000 for consulting services under a cost-reimbursement contract. The work is reasonable in amount and benefits the contract, meeting reasonableness and allocability. However, the contract contains an express clause limiting external consulting fees to 60,000. Because the cost exceeds contract limitations, the excess 40,000 fails the contract terms factor and is disallowed.

## Never confuse

Allowability is whether the government will pay for a cost under the contract; allocability is how a cost is assigned to cost objectives. A cost may be completely allocable to a project but remain unallowable because of an express cost principle or contract term. Screen unallowable costs early to avoid statutory penalties; see [Unallowable-cost penalties](cmp-unallowable-cost-penalty.md). Cost Accounting Standards coverage is distinct from cost principle incorporation; see [CAS applicability](cmp-cas-applicability.md).
