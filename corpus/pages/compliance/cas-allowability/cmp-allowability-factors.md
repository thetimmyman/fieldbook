## Purpose

Establishes the criteria a contractor cost must meet to be allowable on government contracts and the documentation required to support it.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-0101] |
| Codified citation | FAR 31.201-2 [@CL-CMP-0101] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 31.201-2 [@CL-CMP-9932; @CL-CMP-9933] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-0101] |
| Applies to | Government contracts subject to the cost principles [@CL-CMP-0101] |

A cost is allowable only when it complies with all of the following requirements: reasonableness, allocability, standards promulgated by the CAS Board if applicable (otherwise generally accepted accounting principles and practices appropriate to the circumstances), the terms of the contract, and any limitations set forth in the subpart [@CL-CMP-0101; @CL-CMP-8030]. A contractor is responsible for accounting for costs appropriately and for maintaining records, including supporting documentation, adequate to demonstrate that costs claimed have been incurred, are allocable to the contract, and comply with applicable cost principles in this subpart and agency supplements [@CL-CMP-8031; @CL-CMP-9618]. The contracting officer may disallow all or part of a claimed cost that is inadequately supported [@CL-CMP-0102].

## How it works

A cost is allowable only when it complies with all five requirements, so a cost that fails any one of them is not allowable [@CL-CMP-0101; @CL-CMP-8030]. When accounting practices depart from the cost principles, excess costs resulting from the inconsistency are unallowable [@CL-CMP-9617]. Certain cost principles incorporate the measurement, assignment and allocability rules of selected cost accounting standards and limit allowability to the amounts those standards produce [@CL-CMP-9613]; on contracts that are not CAS-covered, only the standards or portions specifically made applicable by the cost principles apply [@CL-CMP-9614], and only to determine allowability [@CL-CMP-9615], without subjecting the business unit to any other CAS rules [@CL-CMP-9616]. The contractor bears the responsibility to maintain supporting documentation adequate to demonstrate that costs claimed have been incurred, are allocable to the contract, and comply with applicable cost principles in this subpart and agency supplements [@CL-CMP-8031; @CL-CMP-9618].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers must use the RFO FAR part 31 text in place of the codified text [@CL-CMP-9350], effective immediately under Revision 1 signed on 2026-08-04 [@CL-CMP-9351]. RFO FAR 31.201-2(a) keeps the five requirements described above: reasonableness, allocability, Cost Accounting Standards or generally accepted accounting principles, the terms of the contract and the limitations in the subpart [@CL-CMP-9932; @CL-CMP-9933]. It keeps the rule that costs resulting from inconsistent accounting practices in excess of the amount consistent practices would have produced are unallowable [@CL-CMP-9935], and the contracting officer's power to disallow all or part of a claimed cost that is inadequately supported [@CL-CMP-9937]. RFO paragraph (b)(2) says that only those CAS or portions of standards specifically made applicable by the cost principles are mandatory unless the contract is CAS-covered, and refers to 48 CFR chapter 99 for CAS coverage [@CL-CMP-9934], where the codified paragraph (b) refers to 48 CFR 9903 [@CL-CMP-9614]. RFO paragraph (d)(1) makes the contractor responsible for records adequate to show that costs claimed comply with applicable cost principles "in this subpart", and prints no reference to agency supplements [@CL-CMP-9936], where the codified paragraph (d) adds "and agency supplements" [@CL-CMP-9618].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor incurs 100,000 for consulting services under a cost-reimbursement contract. The work is reasonable in amount and benefits the contract, meeting reasonableness and allocability. However, the contract contains an express clause limiting external consulting fees to 60,000. Because the cost exceeds contract limitations, the excess 40,000 fails the contract terms factor and is disallowed.

## Never confuse

A cost is allowable only when it complies with all of the listed requirements, the first two being reasonableness and allocability [@CL-CMP-0101; @CL-CMP-8030]. The penalties for unallowable costs are covered in [Unallowable-cost penalties](cmp-unallowable-cost-penalty.md). The applicability of the CAS rules and regulations is determined by the CAS clause, if any, in the contract and the requirements of the standards themselves [@CL-CMP-8032]; see [CAS applicability](cmp-cas-applicability.md).
