## Purpose

Gives the exact meaning of the main terms used by the pension cost standard, so that an unfunded liability, an assignable cost limit and a prepayment credit are not mixed up.

## The rule

| Field | Value |

| Codified citation | 48 CFR 9904.412-30 [@CL-CMP-3375] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |

The source defines actuarial accrued liability as pension cost attributable, under the actuarial cost method in use, to years prior to the current period considered by a particular actuarial valuation [@CL-CMP-3375]. The excess of the actuarial accrued liability over the actuarial value of the assets of a pension plan is the unfunded actuarial liability [@CL-CMP-3376]. The reverse excess is an actuarial surplus, which is treated as a negative unfunded actuarial liability [@CL-CMP-3377].

Three terms describe how cost can be limited or carried to other periods. The assignable cost limitation is the excess, if any, of the actuarial accrued liability and the normal cost for the current period over the actuarial value of the assets of the pension plan [@CL-CMP-3378]. An assignable cost deficit is the increase in unfunded actuarial liability that results when the pension cost computed for a qualified defined-benefit pension plan exceeds the maximum tax-deductible amount for the cost accounting period determined in accordance with the Internal Revenue Code [@CL-CMP-3379]. An assignable cost credit is the decrease in unfunded actuarial liability that results when the pension cost computed for a cost accounting period is less than zero [@CL-CMP-3380].

A prepayment credit is the amount funded in excess of the pension cost assigned to a cost accounting period that is carried forward for future recognition [@CL-CMP-3381]. A qualified pension plan is a pension plan comprising a definite written program communicated to and for the exclusive benefit of employees which meets the criteria deemed essential by the Internal Revenue Service [@CL-CMP-3382]. The criteria deemed essential are those set forth in the Internal Revenue Code for preferential tax treatment regarding contributions, investments, and distributions, and any other plan is a nonqualified pension plan [@CL-CMP-3425].

## How it works

The terms fit together as a chain. Actuarial accrued liability set against the actuarial value of the assets gives an unfunded actuarial liability or a surplus [@CL-CMP-3375; @CL-CMP-3376; @CL-CMP-3377]. The assignable cost limitation compares liability and normal cost with assets to set a ceiling [@CL-CMP-3378]. A deficit or a credit is the movement in unfunded actuarial liability that follows when computed cost is above the tax-deductible maximum for a qualified plan, or below zero [@CL-CMP-3379; @CL-CMP-3380]. Money funded above assigned cost becomes a prepayment credit carried forward [@CL-CMP-3381]. The deficit definition speaks of a qualified defined-benefit plan, so whether a plan is qualified matters for that term [@CL-CMP-3379; @CL-CMP-3425].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A plan has an actuarial accrued liability of 50 and an actuarial value of assets of 44. The unfunded actuarial liability is 6. If the assets were 53 instead, the plan would show an actuarial surplus of 3, which is an unfunded actuarial liability of negative 3.

In a separate year, the plan funds 10 more than the pension cost assigned to the period. The extra 10 is a prepayment credit carried forward.

## Never confuse

An assignable cost credit is not an actuarial surplus. A credit arises when the pension cost computed for a period is less than zero [@CL-CMP-3380], while a surplus arises when the actuarial value of the assets exceeds the actuarial accrued liability [@CL-CMP-3377]. A prepayment credit is also a different thing: it is the amount funded above assigned cost [@CL-CMP-3381].
