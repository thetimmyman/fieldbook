## Purpose

Gives the exact meaning of the main terms used by the pension cost standard, so that an unfunded liability, an assignable cost limit and a prepayment credit are not mixed up.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified CAS [@CL-CMP-3375] |
| Codified citation | 48 CFR 9904.412-30 [@CL-CMP-3375] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |

Actuarial accrued liability means pension cost attributable, under the actuarial cost method in use, to years prior to the current period considered by a particular actuarial valuation [@CL-CMP-3375]. The excess of the actuarial accrued liability over the actuarial value of the assets of a pension plan is the unfunded actuarial liability [@CL-CMP-3376]. The excess of the actuarial value of the assets of a pension plan over the actuarial accrued liability is an actuarial surplus and is treated as a negative unfunded actuarial liability [@CL-CMP-3377].

The assignable cost limitation is the excess, if any, of the actuarial accrued liability and the normal cost for the current period over the actuarial value of the assets of the pension plan [@CL-CMP-3378]. An assignable cost deficit is the increase in unfunded actuarial liability that results when the pension cost computed for a qualified defined-benefit pension plan exceeds the maximum tax-deductible amount for the cost accounting period determined in accordance with the Internal Revenue Code [@CL-CMP-3379]. An assignable cost credit is the decrease in unfunded actuarial liability that results when the pension cost computed for a cost accounting period is less than zero [@CL-CMP-3380].

A prepayment credit is the amount funded in excess of the pension cost assigned to a cost accounting period that is carried forward for future recognition [@CL-CMP-3381]. A qualified pension plan is a pension plan comprising a definite written program communicated to and for the exclusive benefit of employees which meets the criteria deemed essential by the Internal Revenue Service [@CL-CMP-3382]. The criteria deemed essential by the Internal Revenue Service are those set forth in the Internal Revenue Code for preferential tax treatment regarding contributions, investments, and distributions, and any other plan is a nonqualified pension plan [@CL-CMP-3425].

## How it works

The excess of the actuarial accrued liability over the actuarial value of the assets of a pension plan is the unfunded actuarial liability, and the reverse excess is an actuarial surplus treated as a negative unfunded actuarial liability [@CL-CMP-3376; @CL-CMP-3377]. The assignable cost limitation is the excess, if any, of the actuarial accrued liability and the normal cost for the current period over the actuarial value of the assets of the pension plan [@CL-CMP-3378]. An assignable cost deficit is the increase in unfunded actuarial liability that results when the pension cost computed for a qualified defined-benefit pension plan exceeds the maximum tax-deductible amount for the cost accounting period determined in accordance with the Internal Revenue Code [@CL-CMP-3379], and an assignable cost credit is the decrease in unfunded actuarial liability that results when the pension cost computed for a cost accounting period is less than zero [@CL-CMP-3380]. A prepayment credit is the amount funded in excess of the pension cost assigned to a cost accounting period that is carried forward for future recognition [@CL-CMP-3381]. The assignable cost deficit definition refers to a qualified pension plan [@CL-CMP-3379], and a qualified pension plan meets the criteria deemed essential by the Internal Revenue Service as set forth in the Internal Revenue Code for preferential tax treatment regarding contributions, investments, and distributions [@CL-CMP-3425].

For how pension cost is built and measured, see the [composition card](cmp-cas-412-pension-composition.md). The [techniques card](cmp-cas-412-techniques.md) shows how these terms are used to assign and allocate pension cost, the [illustrations card](cmp-cas-412-illustrations.md) shows worked outcomes, and the [effective date card](cmp-cas-412-effective-date-transition.md) says when the standard applies to a contractor.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A plan has an actuarial accrued liability of 50 and an actuarial value of assets of 44. The unfunded actuarial liability is 6. If the assets were 53 instead, the plan would show an actuarial surplus of 3, which is an unfunded actuarial liability of negative 3.

In a separate year, the plan funds 10 more than the pension cost assigned to the period. The extra 10 is a prepayment credit carried forward.

## Never confuse

An assignable cost credit and a prepayment credit are different credits: an assignable cost credit is the decrease in unfunded actuarial liability that results when the pension cost computed for a cost accounting period is less than zero [@CL-CMP-3380], while a prepayment credit is the amount funded in excess of the pension cost assigned to a cost accounting period that is carried forward for future recognition [@CL-CMP-3381]. An actuarial surplus is the excess of the actuarial value of the assets of a pension plan over the actuarial accrued liability [@CL-CMP-3377].
