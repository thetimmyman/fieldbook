## Purpose

Defines when a cost is reasonable for government contracting and establishes that no presumption of reasonableness attaches to incurred costs.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-0103] |
| Codified citation | FAR 31.201-3 [@CL-CMP-0103] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 31.201-3 [@CL-CMP-8921] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-0103] |
| Applies to | Government contracts subject to the cost principles [@CL-CMP-0103] |

A cost is reasonable if, in its nature and amount, it does not exceed that which would be incurred by a prudent person in the conduct of competitive business [@CL-CMP-0103]. No presumption of reasonableness shall be attached to the incurrence of costs by a contractor [@CL-CMP-0104]. If an initial review of the facts results in a challenge of a specific cost by the contracting officer or the contracting officer's representative, the burden of proof shall be upon the contractor to establish that such cost is reasonable [@CL-CMP-8581].

## How it works

What is reasonable depends upon a variety of considerations and circumstances, including whether the cost is of a type generally recognized as ordinary and necessary for the conduct of the contractor's business or the contract performance [@CL-CMP-8815]. The considerations also include (2) generally accepted sound business practices, arm's length bargaining, and Federal and State laws and regulations and (3) the contractor's responsibilities to the Government, other customers, the owners of the business, employees, and the public at large [@CL-CMP-8816], and (4) any significant deviations from the contractor's established practices [@CL-CMP-8378]. Reasonableness of specific costs must be examined with particular care in connection with firms or their separate divisions that may not be subject to effective competitive restraints [@CL-CMP-8814].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers must use the RFO FAR part 31 text in place of the codified text [@CL-CMP-9350], effective immediately under Revision 1 signed on 2026-08-04 [@CL-CMP-9351]. Under RFO FAR 31.201-3(a), a cost is reasonable if, in its nature and amount, it does not exceed that which would be incurred by a reasonable person in the conduct of competitive business [@CL-CMP-8921]. Under paragraph (a)(1), no presumption of reasonableness must be attached to the incurrence of costs [@CL-CMP-8922], and under paragraph (a)(2), if the contracting officer, or the contracting officer's representative, challenges a specific cost, the burden of proof is on the contractor to prove that the cost is reasonable [@CL-CMP-8923]. Under paragraph (b), determining cost reasonableness depends on a number of factors, including (1) whether it is the type of cost generally recognized as ordinary and necessary for the conduct of the contractor's business or contract performance [@CL-CMP-8924]; (2) generally accepted sound business practices, arm's length bargaining, and Federal and State laws and regulations, and (3) the contractor's responsibilities to the Government, other customers, the owners of the business, employees, and the public at large [@CL-CMP-8925]; and (4) any significant deviations from the contractor's established practices [@CL-CMP-8926]. Under paragraph (c), reasonableness of specific costs must be examined with particular care in connection with firms or their separate divisions that may not be subject to effective competitive restraints [@CL-CMP-8927].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor purchases specialized test gear from an affiliated business unit for 150,000 without competitive quotes. Market analysis shows commercial equivalents sell under competitive conditions for 90,000. The contracting officer challenges the 60,000 premium, so the burden of proof is on the contractor to establish that the cost is reasonable. The contractor cannot demonstrate that the 60,000 markup reflects prudent commercial bargaining.

## Never confuse

For the factors that determine cost allowability, see [Factors determining cost allowability](cmp-allowability-factors.md), and for penalties on unallowable costs, see [Unallowable-cost penalties](cmp-unallowable-cost-penalty.md).

The two texts measure reasonableness against different persons: the codified text measures reasonableness against a prudent person [@CL-CMP-0103], and the RFO text measures it against a reasonable person [@CL-CMP-8921]. The codified text says that if an initial review of the facts results in a challenge of a specific cost by the contracting officer or the contracting officer's representative, the burden of proof shall be upon the contractor to establish that such cost is reasonable [@CL-CMP-8581]. The RFO text says that if the contracting officer, or the contracting officer's representative, challenges a specific cost, the burden of proof is on the contractor to prove that the cost is reasonable [@CL-CMP-8923]. The codified text says no presumption of reasonableness shall be attached to the incurrence of costs by a contractor [@CL-CMP-0104], and the RFO text says no presumption of reasonableness must be attached to the incurrence of costs [@CL-CMP-8922].
