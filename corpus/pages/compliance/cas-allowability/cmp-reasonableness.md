## Purpose

Defines when a cost is reasonable for government contracting and establishes that no presumption of reasonableness attaches to incurred costs.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-0103] |
| Codified citation | FAR 31.201-3 [@CL-CMP-0103] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 31.201-3 [@CL-CMP-0103] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-0103] |
| Applies to | Government contracts subject to the cost principles [@CL-CMP-0103] |

A cost is reasonable if, in its nature and amount, it does not exceed that which would be incurred by a prudent person in the conduct of competitive business [@CL-CMP-0103]. No presumption of reasonableness attaches to the incurrence of costs by a contractor [@CL-CMP-0104]. If an initial review of the facts results in a challenge of a specific cost by the contracting officer or the contracting officer's representative, the burden of proof is on the contractor to establish that the cost is reasonable.

## How it works

What is reasonable depends on a variety of considerations and circumstances, including whether the cost is of a type generally recognized as ordinary and necessary for the conduct of the contractor's business or the contract performance. Considerations include sound business practices, arm's length bargaining, legal compliance, responsibilities to customers and the public, and significant deviations from established practices. When business units or divisions are not subject to effective competitive restraints, the reasonableness of specific costs must be examined with particular care.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor purchases specialized test gear from an affiliated business unit for 150,000 without competitive quotes. Market analysis shows commercial equivalents sell under competitive conditions for 90,000. Because the contractor cannot demonstrate that the 60,000 markup reflects prudent commercial bargaining, the contracting officer disallows the 60,000 premium as unreasonable.

## Never confuse

Reasonableness concerns whether an expense is prudent in nature and amount; allocability concerns whether it benefits a cost objective. A cost may directly benefit a contract but still be unreasonable in amount. Unreasonable costs are unallowable and cannot be reimbursed; see [Unallowable-cost penalties](cmp-unallowable-cost-penalty.md).
