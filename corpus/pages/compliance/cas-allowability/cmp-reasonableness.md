## Purpose

Defines when a cost is reasonable for government contracting and establishes that no presumption of reasonableness attaches to incurred costs.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-0103] |
| Codified citation | FAR 31.201-3 [@CL-CMP-0103] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 31.201-3 [@CL-CMP-8921] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-0103] |
| Applies to | Government contracts subject to the cost principles [@CL-CMP-0103] |

A cost is reasonable if, in its nature and amount, it does not exceed that which would be incurred by a prudent person in the conduct of competitive business [@CL-CMP-0103]. No presumption of reasonableness attaches to the incurrence of costs by a contractor [@CL-CMP-0104]. If an initial review of the facts results in a challenge of a specific cost by the contracting officer or the contracting officer's representative, the burden of proof is on the contractor to establish that the cost is reasonable [@CL-CMP-8581].

## How it works

What is reasonable depends upon a variety of considerations and circumstances, including whether the cost is of a type generally recognized as ordinary and necessary for the conduct of the contractor's business or the contract performance [@CL-CMP-8815]. Further considerations include generally accepted sound business practices, arm's length bargaining, Federal and State laws and regulations, and the contractor's responsibilities to the Government, other customers, the owners of the business, employees, and the public at large [@CL-CMP-8816]. Reasonableness of specific costs must be examined with particular care in connection with firms or their separate divisions that may not be subject to effective competitive restraints [@CL-CMP-8814].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 31 text in place of the codified text [@CL-CMP-9350], effective immediately under Revision 1 signed on 2026-08-04 [@CL-CMP-9351]. The RFO text of 31.201-3 says a cost is reasonable if, in its nature and amount, it does not exceed that which would be incurred by a reasonable person in the conduct of competitive business, where the codified text says a prudent person [@CL-CMP-8921; @CL-CMP-0103]. It says no presumption of reasonableness must be attached to the incurrence of costs [@CL-CMP-8922], and that if the contracting officer, or the contracting officer's representative, challenges a specific cost, the burden of proof is on the contractor to prove that the cost is reasonable [@CL-CMP-8923]. It keeps the factors in paragraph (b) [@CL-CMP-8924; @CL-CMP-8925; @CL-CMP-8926] and places the rule on examining costs with particular care for firms or divisions that may not be subject to effective competitive restraints in paragraph (c) [@CL-CMP-8927].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor purchases specialized test gear from an affiliated business unit for 150,000 without competitive quotes. Market analysis shows commercial equivalents sell under competitive conditions for 90,000. Because the contractor cannot demonstrate that the 60,000 markup reflects prudent commercial bargaining, the contracting officer disallows the 60,000 premium as unreasonable.

## Never confuse

Reasonableness concerns whether an expense is prudent in nature and amount; allocability concerns whether it benefits a cost objective. A cost may directly benefit a contract but still be unreasonable in amount. Unreasonable costs are unallowable and cannot be reimbursed; see [Unallowable-cost penalties](cmp-unallowable-cost-penalty.md).

The codified text measures reasonableness against a prudent person [@CL-CMP-0103], whereas the RFO text measures it against a reasonable person [@CL-CMP-8921]. The codified text starts the burden of proof when an initial review of the facts results in a challenge of a specific cost by the contracting officer or the contracting officer's representative, and puts it on the contractor to establish that the cost is reasonable [@CL-CMP-8581], whereas the RFO text starts it when the contracting officer or the contracting officer's representative challenges a specific cost, and puts it on the contractor to prove that the cost is reasonable [@CL-CMP-8923]. The codified text says no presumption of reasonableness is attached to the incurrence of costs by a contractor [@CL-CMP-0104], whereas the RFO text says it without the words by a contractor [@CL-CMP-8922].
