## Purpose

Shows when a contracting officer may warn a contractor that costs will be disallowed, who answers the contractor's response, which contracts carry the clause, and how the DoD supplement splits voucher duties between the auditor and the administrative contracting officer.

At any time during performance of a contract of a type referred to in the clause prescription, the cognizant contracting officer responsible for administering the contract may issue the contractor a written notice of intent to disallow specified costs incurred or planned for incurrence [@CL-CMP-0651]. Before issuing it, that officer shall make every reasonable effort to reach a satisfactory settlement through discussions with the contractor [@CL-CMP-0652]. As a minimum, the notice shall, among other listed items, refer to the contract's Notice of Intent to Disallow Costs clause and state the contractor's name and list the numbers of the affected contracts [@CL-CMP-2577]. Two listed items are a description of the costs to be disallowed, with estimated dollar value by item, applicable time periods and reasons, and a description of the potential impact on billing rates and forward pricing rate agreements [@CL-CMP-2578]. The contracting officer shall insert the clause in solicitations and contracts when a cost-reimbursement contract, a fixed-price incentive contract, or a contract providing for price redetermination is contemplated [@CL-CMP-2581]. Failure to issue a notice under the clause shall not affect the Government's rights to take exception to incurred costs [@CL-CMP-2674].

If the contractor disagrees with a notice, it may submit a written response, which shall be answered by withdrawal of the notice or by a written decision within 60 days [@CL-CMP-2576]. Under the clause, if the contractor responds within 60 days, the contracting officer shall, within 60 days of receiving the response, either make a written withdrawal of the notice or issue a written decision [@CL-CMP-2671]. When the contractor has responded with a disagreement, the contracting officer who issued the notice shall either withdraw the notice or issue the written decision, except that when elements of indirect cost are involved the contracting officer responsible for determining final indirect cost rates shall issue the decision [@CL-CMP-2576; @CL-CMP-1101]. A notice that involves elements of indirect cost shall not be issued without coordination with the contracting officer or auditor having authority for final indirect cost settlement [@CL-CMP-2579].

In DoD, the contract auditor is the authorized representative of the contracting officer for, among listed duties, receiving vouchers [@CL-CMP-2582] and issuing DCAA Forms 1 to deduct costs where allowability is questionable [@CL-CMP-2583]. The administrative contracting officer approves all completion and final vouchers and may issue or direct the issuance of DCAA Form 1 on any cost when there is reason to believe it should be suspended or disallowed [@CL-CMP-2584].

## How it works

The administering contracting officer may issue the notice, after trying to settle by discussion, and the officer responsible for final indirect cost rates issues the decision on a disagreement when elements of indirect cost are involved [@CL-CMP-0652; @CL-CMP-2576; @CL-CMP-1101]. In DoD the auditor may issue DCAA Forms 1 to deduct costs where allowability is questionable, and the administrative contracting officer may issue or direct the issuance of one on any cost [@CL-CMP-2583; @CL-CMP-2584]. The contractor's path in these sections is a written response, answered by withdrawal or a written decision within 60 days [@CL-CMP-2576]. The notice sections printed here do not state a further appeal route after the written decision. How disallowance of incurred costs and vouchers works under the main FAR section is on [Disallowing costs after incurrence](../audit-retention/cmp-disallowing-costs-after-incurrence.md).

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 42 text in place of the codified text under Class Deviation 2026-O0050, effective March 16, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9759] [@CL-CMP-9761], and use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text under Class Deviation 2026-O0038, effective February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. RFO FAR 42.600 says the subpart prescribes policies and procedures for (a) issuing notices of intent to disallow costs and (b) disallowing costs already incurred during the course of performance [@CL-CMP-8954], the same scope that codified FAR 42.800 states for its subpart [@CL-CMP-10012]. RFO FAR 42.601(a) says contracting officers must make reasonable efforts to proactively address potentially unallowable costs with contractors [@CL-CMP-6714], where codified FAR 42.801(a) says the contracting officer shall make every reasonable effort to reach a satisfactory settlement through discussions with the contractor before issuing the notice [@CL-CMP-0652]. RFO FAR 42.601(a) also says that, if unable to settle the matter through discussion, the cognizant contracting officer responsible for administering the contract may issue the contractor a written notice of intent to disallow specified costs incurred or planned for incurrence during the performance of the following contract types [@CL-CMP-6715]: cost-reimbursement contracts, cost-reimbursement portions of fixed-price contracts, letter contracts that provide for reimbursement of costs, time-and-material and labor-hour contracts, and fixed-price incentive contracts [@CL-CMP-6716]. Codified FAR 42.801(a) instead refers to a contract of a type referred to in FAR 42.802 [@CL-CMP-0651]. RFO FAR 42.601(b) has the same substance, with "In case of disagreement" and "must be answered" where the codified paragraph says "In the event of disagreement" and "shall be answered": the contractor may submit to the contracting officer a written response, which must be answered by withdrawal of the notice or by making a written decision within 60 days [@CL-CMP-6717; @CL-CMP-2576]. RFO FAR 42.601(c) has the same listed items of the notice, with "must" where the codified paragraph says "shall", including the reference to the contract's Notice of Intent to Disallow Costs clause, the contractor's name and the numbers of the affected contracts [@CL-CMP-6718; @CL-CMP-2577], and the description of the costs to be disallowed and of the potential impact on billing rates and forward pricing rate agreements [@CL-CMP-6719; @CL-CMP-2578]. RFO FAR 42.601(e) says a notice that involves elements of indirect cost must not be issued without coordination with the contracting officer or auditor having authority for final indirect cost settlement (see 42.505) [@CL-CMP-6720], where the codified paragraph refers to FAR 42.705 [@CL-CMP-2579]. RFO FAR 42.601(f) has the same substance, with "must" where the codified paragraph says "shall": in the event the contractor submits a response that disagrees with the notice, the contracting officer who issued the notice must either withdraw the notice or issue the written decision, except when elements of indirect cost are involved [@CL-CMP-6721; @CL-CMP-1101]. In that case the contracting officer responsible under 42.505 for determining final indirect cost rates must issue the decision [@CL-CMP-9869], where the codified paragraph refers to FAR 42.705 [@CL-CMP-1101]. RFO FAR 42.602 has the same substance, with "must" where the codified section says "shall": the contracting officer must insert the Notice of Intent to Disallow Costs clause in solicitations and contracts when a cost-reimbursement contract, a fixed-price incentive contract, or a contract providing for price redetermination is contemplated [@CL-CMP-6722; @CL-CMP-2581]. Under the RFO Notice of Intent To Disallow Costs clause, if the contractor responds within 60 days, the contracting officer will, within 60 days of receiving the response, either make a written withdrawal of the notice or issue a written decision [@CL-CMP-6723], where the codified clause says the contracting officer shall do so [@CL-CMP-2671]. The RFO clause says failure to issue a notice does not affect the Government's rights to take exception to incurred costs [@CL-CMP-6724], where the codified clause says it shall not affect those rights [@CL-CMP-2674].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

During performance of a cost-reimbursement contract, the contracting officer sees 80,000 of consulting cost charged to the contract. Discussions fail to settle it, so the officer issues a notice of intent to disallow that states the costs, the periods and the reasons. The contractor sends a written response within 60 days, and the officer must, within 60 days of receiving it, withdraw the notice or issue a written decision. If the consulting cost sits in an indirect cost pool, the officer responsible for final indirect cost rates issues the decision.

## Never confuse

A notice of intent to disallow is a written notice to the contractor, issued during performance, of intent to disallow specified costs incurred or planned for incurrence [@CL-CMP-0651]. A DCAA Form 1 is a notice of contract costs suspended or disapproved that, in DoD, the contract auditor issues to deduct costs where allowability is questionable [@CL-CMP-2583], and that the administrative contracting officer may issue or direct on any cost when there is reason to believe it should be suspended or disallowed [@CL-CMP-2584]. The two are different notices. How the FAR treats the notice of costs suspended or disapproved after a voucher is examined is on [Disallowing costs after incurrence](../audit-retention/cmp-disallowing-costs-after-incurrence.md). The roles are on [Oversight roles](../audit-retention/cmp-oversight-roles.md), and the final indirect rate procedure is on [Final indirect cost rates: contracting officer determination procedure](../../indirect-rates/rate-lifecycle/rte-co-final-indirect-rates.md).

The codified section ties the notice to a contract of a type referred to in FAR 42.802 [@CL-CMP-0651], whose clause prescription calls for the clause when a cost-reimbursement contract, a fixed-price incentive contract or a contract providing for price redetermination is contemplated [@CL-CMP-2581]. RFO FAR 42.601(a) instead lists five contract types, including time-and-material and labor-hour contracts [@CL-CMP-6716], while RFO FAR 42.602 prints the same clause prescription as the codified section [@CL-CMP-6722; @CL-CMP-2581].
