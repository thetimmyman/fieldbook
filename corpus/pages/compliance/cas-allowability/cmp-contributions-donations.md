## Purpose

Explains why contributions and donations are barred as contract costs whatever their form or recipient, and where the only exception sits.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-0351] |
| Codified citation | FAR 31.205-8 [@CL-CMP-0351] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 31.205-8 [@CL-CMP-0351] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-0351] |
| Applies to | Not established. |

Contributions and donations are unallowable, and the bar reaches cash, property and services alike, whatever the recipient [@CL-CMP-0351].

The exception sits in another section rather than in this one: the codified text cites 31.205-1(e)(3) [@CL-CMP-0352].

## How it works

Allowability turns on what the payment is, not on who receives it, so the identity of the recipient does not rescue the cost [@CL-CMP-0351]. Cash, property and services are treated the same way. Because the only route to allowability is an exception defined in a different section, the cost stays unallowable unless that separate test is met [@CL-CMP-0352].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

The company pays 5,000 to a local school and 3,000 to a national charity, and books both as community relations. Both are contributions or donations of cash, so both are unallowable unless the exception the section cross-references applies; the recipient differs but the answer does not.

## Never confuse

Contributions and donations are often relabelled as marketing, community relations or employee relations spend. The section tests neither the label nor the recipient, so a renamed donation stays unallowable. Barred balances are screened out before they reach a rate, as described in [Penalties for unallowable costs in final rate proposals](cmp-unallowable-cost-penalty.md).
