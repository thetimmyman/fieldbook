## Purpose

Shows the two worked outcomes for cost of money on a project under construction and gives the effective dates of the standards on general and administrative expense and on facilities capital cost of money.

In the first construction illustration, appropriate consideration must be given to the rate at which costs have been incurred in developing a method for determining a representative investment amount [@CL-CMP-3442]. The capitalized project was put into service at the recognized cost of acquisition of $1,541,467, consisting of regular costs of $1,500,000 plus $17,558 and $23,909 of cost of money [@CL-CMP-3420]. In the second construction illustration, the costs were incurred at a fairly uniform rate throughout the period, so the contractor used beginning and ending balances of the cost accounting period to find the representative amounts [@CL-CMP-3443]. The capitalized project was put into service at the recognized cost of acquisition of $1,549,192, consisting of regular costs of $1,500,000 plus $26,875 and $22,317 of imputed cost of money [@CL-CMP-3421].

CAS 410 is effective as of April 17, 1992, and contractors with prior CAS-covered contracts with full coverage shall continue its applicability upon receipt of a contract to which it is applicable [@CL-CMP-3422]. For contractors with no previous contracts subject to that standard, it shall be applied beginning with the contractor's next full fiscal year beginning after the receipt of a contract to which it is applicable [@CL-CMP-3423]. CAS 414 is effective as of April 17, 1992 [@CL-CMP-3424].

The general and administrative section also holds an appendix. A business unit may use the method described in the appendix for transition from the use of a cost of sales or sales base to a cost input base [@CL-CMP-3444]. Under that method, the amount of the inventory suspense account shall be equal to the beginning inventory of contracts subject to the CAS clause of the cost accounting period in which the business unit must first allocate costs in accordance with the requirements of the standard [@CL-CMP-3445].

The facilities capital section holds an appendix with form instructions. Each facilities capital cost of money factor is the quotient of the cost of money for the cost accounting period divided by the corresponding overhead or G&A expense allocation base, and each computation is carried to five decimal places [@CL-CMP-3446].

The exemption section of the general and administrative standard says it shall not apply to contracts and grants with state, local, and Federally recognized Indian tribal governments [@CL-CMP-8657]. The exemption section of the standard on capital assets under construction says there is no exemption for it [@CL-CMP-8663]. The illustrations section of the facilities capital standard says the use of Form CASB CMF and other computations anticipated for that standard are illustrated in appendix B to 9904.414 [@CL-CMP-8660].

## How it works

The two construction illustrations reach different acquisition costs, one at $1,541,467 and the other at $1,549,192, each made up of regular costs plus cost of money [@CL-CMP-3420; @CL-CMP-3421]. The first applies the rule that the rate at which costs have been incurred is considered in choosing a representative investment amount [@CL-CMP-3442], and the second uses beginning and ending balances because the costs were incurred at a fairly uniform rate [@CL-CMP-3443]. The general and administrative standard is effective as of April 17, 1992, and for a contractor with no previous contracts subject to it, it shall be applied beginning with the contractor's next full fiscal year beginning after the receipt of a contract to which it is applicable [@CL-CMP-3422; @CL-CMP-3423]. The [construction card](cmp-cas-417-capital-assets-under-construction.md), the [general and administrative card](cmp-cas-410-ga-allocation.md) and the [facilities capital card](cmp-cas-414-facilities-capital-cost-of-money.md) hold the rules. The [general and administrative illustrations card](cmp-cas-410-illustrations.md) holds worked outcomes for the general and administrative standard.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A project's representative balance is 200 for half a year and the cost of money rate is 10 percent. The cost of money added to the construction-in-progress account is 200 times 10 percent times one half, or 10.

A contractor with no previous covered contracts receives a covered contract in March. Its fiscal year is the calendar year, so the general and administrative standard applies from the January that begins its next full fiscal year.

## Never confuse

The general and administrative standard is effective as of April 17, 1992 [@CL-CMP-3422]. For contractors with no previous contracts subject to it, it shall be applied beginning with the contractor's next full fiscal year beginning after the receipt of a contract to which it is applicable [@CL-CMP-3423]. Contractors with prior CAS-covered contracts with full coverage shall continue its applicability upon receipt of a contract to which it is applicable [@CL-CMP-3422].
