## Purpose

Walks through the printed illustrations of the general and administrative expense pool and the cost input base. This card draws out six of the illustrations, and the others are in the printed section. The rule itself is on [Allocation of business unit general and administrative expenses](../cas-allowability/cmp-cas-410-ga-allocation.md).

In the scientific computer illustration, the computer is used predominantly for research and development rather than for the management and administration of the business unit as a whole [@CL-CMP-2890]. The costs may remain in the general and administrative expense pool despite not satisfying the standard's definition of that expense, unless they can be allocated to business unit cost objectives on a beneficial or causal relationship best measured by a base other than a cost input base [@CL-CMP-2810]. In the budgeting illustration, the cost of preparing budgets for another segment should be removed from the segment's general and administrative expense pool and transferred to the other segment [@CL-CMP-2811]. In the home office expenses illustration, home office residual expenses should be in the general and administrative expense pool, and staff management expenses relative to manufacturing and engineering should be in the manufacturing overhead and engineering overhead pools respectively [@CL-CMP-2815].

In the raw materials illustration, raw materials that have not been charged to the production of goods and services during the period are not part of the total cost input base for that period [@CL-CMP-2812]. In the stock inventory illustration, the business unit does not include general and administrative expense as part of the inventory cost of the items, and a production run is started, finished and placed into inventory in a single cost accounting period with the items issued during the next period [@CL-CMP-2891]. The cost of items produced for stock or product inventory should be included in the general and administrative base in the same year they are produced, and is not to be included on the basis of when the items are issued to final cost objectives [@CL-CMP-2813]. In the base operating facility illustration, the business unit does not usually supply the equipment it agrees to acquire for the contract, and the cost of the equipment constitutes a significant part of the contract cost [@CL-CMP-2892]. A special general and administrative allocation to the contract shall be agreed to by the parties if they agree that the contract as a whole receives substantially less benefit from the pool than a total cost input allocation would represent [@CL-CMP-2814].

## How it works

Three illustrations address what belongs in the pool. Scientific computer costs that fail the definition of the expense may stay unless a base other than cost input measures their benefit better [@CL-CMP-2810], budget work for another segment moves out [@CL-CMP-2811], and home office residual expenses sit in the pool while manufacturing and engineering staff expenses go to the matching overhead pools [@CL-CMP-2815]. Three address the cost input base or its allocation. Only materials charged to production count in the period [@CL-CMP-2812], stock items count when produced and not when issued, where the unit does not include general and administrative expense in inventory cost [@CL-CMP-2813; @CL-CMP-2891], and a special allocation follows if the parties agree that the contract receives substantially less benefit [@CL-CMP-2814; @CL-CMP-2892].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A business unit buys 800,000 of raw material in a period and charges 600,000 of it to cost objectives. The other 200,000 stays in inventory. Only the 600,000 counts in the cost input base for that period. Assume the unit does not include general and administrative expense in inventory cost. A stock item finished in the period counts in that period's base even if it is issued next period.

## Never confuse

Cost input is not simply purchases. Raw materials count when charged to production, so bought but uncharged materials are left out [@CL-CMP-2812]. Where the business unit does not include general and administrative expense in inventory cost, stock items count in the year produced and not when issued [@CL-CMP-2813; @CL-CMP-2891]. For techniques on pools and bases, see [General and administrative pool and base techniques](../../indirect-rates/absorption/rte-ga-pool-and-base-techniques.md). The home office expenses illustration above deals with where home office residual expenses go [@CL-CMP-2815]; illustrations of home office pools and allocation bases are on [Illustrations of home office pools and allocation bases](../cas-allowability/cmp-cas-403-illustrations-and-interpretation.md). Illustrations of pool design are on [Illustrations of direct and indirect classification and of research and proposal costs](../cas-allowability/cmp-cas-418-and-420-illustrations.md), of the period used for pools and bases on [Illustrations of the cost accounting period](../cas-allowability/cmp-cas-406-illustrations-and-interpretation.md), and of unallowable costs in an allocation base on [Illustrations of identifying unallowable and directly associated costs](../cas-allowability/cmp-cas-405-illustrations.md).
