## Purpose

Describes the negotiation, execution, and legal limitations of advance agreements between contracting officers and contractors regarding cost treatments.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-0516] |
| Codified citation | FAR 31.109 [@CL-CMP-0516] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |

| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-0516] |
| Applies to | Contracts subject to the FAR cost principles [@CL-CMP-0516] |

An advance agreement is not an absolute requirement, and the absence of an agreement on any cost will not in itself affect the reasonableness, allocability, or allowability of that cost [@CL-CMP-0516].

Advance agreements must be in writing, executed by both contracting parties, incorporated into applicable contracts, and state their applicability and duration [@CL-CMP-0517].

The contracting officer is not authorized to agree to any cost treatment inconsistent with the contract cost principles, such as agreeing that interest is allowable [@CL-CMP-0518].

## How it works

Contracting officers and contractors negotiate advance agreements to establish the treatment of special, unusual, or disputed cost items before costs are incurred. Agreements may be negotiated with a particular contractor for a single contract, a group of contracts, or all the contracts of a contracting office, an agency, or several agencies. The cognizant administrative contracting officer negotiates advance agreements, except that an agreement affecting only one contract, or a class of contracts from a single contracting office, is negotiated by a contracting officer in that office (or the ACO when delegated), and the Government negotiator informs other offices with significant unliquidated balances with the same contractor before negotiating. Advance agreements are sought to avoid possible subsequent disallowance or dispute, but a contracting officer is not authorized to agree to a treatment of costs inconsistent with the cost principles of the part. An advance agreement may not provide, for example, that interest is allowable notwithstanding the cost principle that makes it unallowable [@CL-CMP-0518].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

Prior to relocating key engineering personnel to a remote test site, a contractor negotiates an advance agreement with the administrative contracting officer establishing a special location allowance of 1,000 per month per employee. When audited, the incurred allowances are compared with the monthly rate in the written agreement, which was incorporated into the contract and must state its applicability and duration [@CL-CMP-0517].

## Never confuse

An advance agreement establishes mutual understanding on ambiguous or complex costs, but it is not an absolute prerequisite for cost allowability. Furthermore, an advance agreement cannot validate an unallowable cost that violates the cost principles.
