## Purpose

Cost Accounting Standard 412 provides guidance for determining and measuring the components of pension cost and establishes the basis on which pension costs are assigned to cost accounting periods. This concept hub sets out what the standard requires and links the detailed reference and pension cost pages across the corpus.

## The rule

The purpose of CAS 412 is to provide guidance for determining and measuring the components of pension cost and to establish the basis on which pension costs are assigned to cost accounting periods [@CL-EST-2914; @CL-EST-2915]. These provisions enhance uniformity and consistency in accounting for pension costs to increase the probability that those costs are properly allocated to cost objectives [@CL-EST-2916]. The standard does not cover the cost of Employee Stock Ownership Plans meeting the definition of a pension plan, which are treated as deferred compensation covered under CAS 415 [@CL-EST-2917; @CL-EST-3036]. For a defined-contribution plan, pension cost is the net contribution required for the period, after dividends and other credits where applicable [@CL-CMP-0558]. For defined-benefit pension plans outside the pay-as-you-go cost method, the components of pension cost for a cost accounting period are the normal cost of the period, a part of any unfunded actuarial liability, an interest equivalent on the unamortized portion of any unfunded actuarial liability, and an adjustment for any actuarial gains and losses [@CL-CMP-8038; @CL-CMP-0557]. Pension cost for such defined-benefit plans is determined using an immediate-gain actuarial cost method [@CL-CMP-0559]. Each actuarial assumption used to measure pension cost must be separately identified, represent the contractor's best estimates of anticipated experience under the plan, and be evaluated solely with respect to that assumption [@CL-CMP-0560; @CL-CMP-8039]. The amount of pension cost computed for a cost accounting period is assignable only to that period, subject to specific exceptions for costs assigned to future periods [@CL-CMP-0561; @CL-CMP-8040].

For defined-benefit plans other than pay-as-you-go, pension cost is assignable only if the sum of unamortized assignable unfunded actuarial liability and unassignable unfunded actuarial liability equals the total unfunded actuarial liability [@CL-CMP-7058; @CL-CMP-7059]. Except for nonqualified defined-benefit plans, assigned pension costs are allocable to cost objectives to the extent that they are funded [@CL-CMP-3395]. Funding is considered to take place within the period if accomplished by the corporate tax filing date, including permissible extensions [@CL-CMP-3429]. An excise tax assessed because of excess, inadequate, or delayed funding is not a component of pension cost [@CL-CMP-3430]. There is no exemption for CAS 412 [@CL-CMP-8658]. The standard is effective as of February 27, 2012 and applicable for cost accounting periods after June 30, 2012 [@CL-CMP-3404]. Following receipt of an applicable contract, contractors apply the amended standard beginning with the next cost accounting period after the later of the implementation date or award date, marking the Applicability Date of the CAS Pension Harmonization Rule, while following the prior version before that date [@CL-CMP-3405; @CL-CMP-3406; @CL-CMP-3407; @CL-CMP-3434; @CL-CMP-3435; @CL-CMP-3436].

## How it works

Actuarial accrued liability means pension cost attributable under the actuarial cost method in use to years before the current period considered by a valuation [@CL-CMP-3375]. Unfunded actuarial liability is the excess of actuarial accrued liability over the actuarial value of plan assets, whereas an actuarial surplus is an excess of assets over liability treated as a negative unfunded liability [@CL-CMP-3376; @CL-CMP-3377]. The assignable cost limitation is the excess, if any, of the actuarial accrued liability and the normal cost for the current period over the actuarial value of plan assets [@CL-CMP-3378]. An assignable cost deficit is the increase in unfunded actuarial liability when computed cost for a qualified defined-benefit plan exceeds the maximum tax-deductible amount under the Internal Revenue Code [@CL-CMP-3379], while an assignable cost credit is the decrease in unfunded actuarial liability when computed cost is less than zero [@CL-CMP-3380]. A prepayment credit is the amount funded in excess of assigned pension cost carried forward for future recognition [@CL-CMP-3381]. A qualified pension plan meets Internal Revenue Code criteria for preferential tax treatment regarding contributions, investments, and distributions, while any other plan is a nonqualified plan [@CL-CMP-3382; @CL-CMP-3425].

Portions of unfunded actuarial liability are included in pension cost in equal annual installments of amortized liability plus an interest equivalent, generally amortized over 10 to 30 years, whereas assignable cost deficits and credits are amortized over 10 years [@CL-CMP-3383; @CL-CMP-8041; @CL-CMP-3384; @CL-CMP-3386]. For pay-as-you-go defined-benefit plans, assignable cost equals net periodic benefits paid plus a 15-year level installment amortizing irrevocable settlement payments [@CL-CMP-3387; @CL-CMP-3388]. For qualified defined-benefit plans, measured costs are assigned subject to sequential adjustments: a negative measured cost is set to zero and deferred as an assignable cost credit [@CL-CMP-3389; @CL-CMP-3390], assigned cost cannot exceed the assignable cost limitation [@CL-CMP-3391; @CL-CMP-8042], and cost exceeding the sum of the maximum tax-deductible amount and accumulated prepayment credits is deferred to future periods as an assignable cost deficit [@CL-CMP-7061; @CL-CMP-3427]. Nonqualified defined-benefit plans are assigned like qualified plans if the contractor elects that treatment, funds the plan through a funding agency, and provides nonforfeitable communicated rights, but otherwise must use the pay-as-you-go cost method [@CL-CMP-3392; @CL-CMP-3393; @CL-CMP-3394].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor computes annual pension cost for a qualified defined-benefit plan. Normal cost is 1,200,000 and the annual installment amortizing unfunded actuarial liability is 300,000, yielding a measured pension cost of 1,500,000. Plan assets are 9,000,000 and actuarial accrued liability is 10,000,000, so the assignable cost limitation is 2,200,000 (10,000,000 plus 1,200,000 minus 9,000,000), meaning the limitation does not cap the 1,500,000 measured cost. Under the Internal Revenue Code, the maximum tax-deductible amount for the period is 1,100,000 and the plan has no prepayment credits, so assignable pension cost for the period is capped at 1,100,000, and the remaining 400,000 (1,500,000 minus 1,100,000) is established as an assignable cost deficit amortized over 10 years. During the period, the contractor deposits 900,000 into the funding agency by its corporate tax filing date. Because allocability is limited to the amount funded, only 900,000 of the 1,100,000 assigned cost is allocable to cost objectives for the period, and the remaining unfunded 200,000 must be segregated and cannot be reassigned to future periods. This example is illustrative only, and the contract itself governs.

## Never confuse

CAS 412 establishes criteria for measuring the components of pension cost and assigning those costs to cost accounting periods [@CL-EST-2914; @CL-EST-2915], whereas CAS 413 governs adjusting pension costs through actuarial gains and losses, valuing plan assets, and allocating pension costs to organizational segments, including mandatory adjustments upon segment closing or benefit curtailment [@CL-EST-2918; @CL-EST-2919; @CL-MNA-0392]. Furthermore, CAS 412 explicitly excludes Employee Stock Ownership Plans meeting the definition of a pension plan [@CL-EST-2917], whereas CAS 415 provides criteria for measuring and assigning deferred compensation and specifically covers ESOPs, measuring their cost by the amounts contributed by the contractor [@CL-EST-3036; @CL-EST-2505; @CL-CMP-0573].
