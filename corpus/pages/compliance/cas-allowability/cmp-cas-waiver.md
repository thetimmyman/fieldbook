## Purpose

Shows the routes to a waiver of the applicability of the Cost Accounting Standards to a single contract or subcontract, who may decide, and what the decision or request has to say.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | CAS [@CL-CMP-1758] |
| Codified citation | 48 CFR 9903.201-5 [@CL-CMP-1758] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-1758] |
| Applies to | A contract or subcontract of an executive agency; the commercial-item route is limited to a value below 100 million dollars [@CL-CMP-1758; @CL-CMP-1760] |

The head of an executive agency may waive the applicability of the Cost Accounting Standards for a contract or subcontract with a value of less than $100 million if the official determines, in writing, certain facts about the business unit that will perform the work [@CL-CMP-1758].

The determination must be that the unit is primarily engaged in the sale of commercial items and would not otherwise be subject to the standards under that chapter [@CL-CMP-1759].

A second route lets the agency head waive the standards for a contract or subcontract under exceptional circumstances when necessary to meet the needs of the agency [@CL-CMP-1760].

## How it works

The commercial-item route depends on a written determination about the business unit that will perform the work, so the decision is recorded rather than assumed [@CL-CMP-1758; @CL-CMP-1759].

The exceptional-circumstances waiver likewise has to be set forth in writing, with a statement of the circumstances that justify it [@CL-CMP-1761].

The agency head may not delegate either authority to any official below the senior policymaking level in the agency [@CL-CMP-1762].

Each agency head reports the waivers granted under those two routes to the Cost Accounting Standards Board on an annual basis, not later than 90 days after the close of the Government's fiscal year [@CL-CMP-1763].

Separately, upon request of an agency head or his designee, the Cost Accounting Standards Board may waive all or any part of the standards clause requirements or of the disclosure and consistency requirements [@CL-CMP-1764]. A request must contain, among other things, an unequivocal statement that the proposed contractor or subcontractor refuses to accept a contract containing all or a specified part of a CAS clause, with the specific reason for the refusal [@CL-CMP-1901].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A business unit that is primarily a commercial seller seeks relief on a subcontract worth 40,000,000. The agency head signs a written determination that the unit is primarily engaged in the sale of commercial items and would not otherwise be subject to the standards. The commercial-item route is available because the value is under 100,000,000 and both findings are recorded. If the value had been 150,000,000, that route would be closed, and only the exceptional-circumstances route, with its written statement of the circumstances, or a Board waiver on an agency request could be used. The answer: the same business unit can qualify at 40,000,000 but needs a different authority at 150,000,000.

## Never confuse

The commercial-item waiver is not an exemption that follows from commercial-item status by itself; it is a written agency-head decision that has to rest on both findings for a contract or subcontract below the value limit [@CL-CMP-1758; @CL-CMP-1759].

An exemption built into the coverage rules and a waiver granted for one contract are different mechanisms, and coverage itself is described in [CAS applicability](../cas-allowability/cmp-cas-applicability.md).
