## Purpose

Cost Accounting Standard 415 provides criteria for measuring the cost of deferred compensation and assigning that cost to cost accounting periods. This concept hub sets out what the standard requires and links the detailed reference, compliance and estimation pages across the corpus.

## The rule

The purpose of CAS 415 is to provide criteria for the measurement of the cost of deferred compensation and the assignment of such cost to cost accounting periods [@CL-EST-2505]. Applying the CAS 415 criteria should increase the probability that the cost of deferred compensation is allocated to cost objectives in a uniform and consistent manner [@CL-EST-2639]. The standard is applicable to the cost of all deferred compensation except the cost for compensated personal absence and the cost for pension plans that do not meet the definition of an Employee Stock Ownership Plan, which are covered in other Cost Accounting Standards [@CL-EST-2506]. Deferred compensation cost is assigned to the period in which the contractor incurs the obligation to compensate the employee [@CL-CMP-0570]. If no obligation is incurred before payment, the cost is the amount paid and is assigned to the period in which the payment is made [@CL-CMP-0571]. For deferred compensation other than an ESOP, the cost is the present value of the future benefits to be paid by the contractor [@CL-CMP-0572]. For an ESOP, the cost is the amount contributed to the ESOP by the contractor [@CL-CMP-0573]. The cost of each award of deferred compensation must be considered separately for purposes of measurement and assignment to cost accounting periods [@CL-CMP-8114]. However, if the cost of deferred compensation for employees covered by a plan can be measured and assigned with reasonable accuracy on a group basis, separate computations for each employee are not required [@CL-CMP-0574].

The exemption section of CAS 415 says there is no exemption for that standard [@CL-CMP-8661]. The standard is effective as of June 2, 2008 [@CL-EST-2507], and must be followed by each contractor on or after the start of its next cost accounting period beginning after receipt of an applicable contract or subcontract [@CL-EST-2640]. Contractors with prior CAS-covered contracts with full coverage continue to follow the standard in effect prior to June 2, 2008 until the revised standard becomes applicable following receipt of a contract or subcontract to which it applies [@CL-EST-2508]. For contractors and subcontractors that established advance agreements prior to June 2, 2008 regarding the recognition of the costs of existing ESOPs, the awarding agency and contractor must comply with those advance agreements regardless of whether the ESOP was previously subject to CAS 412 or 415 [@CL-EST-2509; @CL-EST-2641]. These advance agreements may be modified, by mutual agreement, to incorporate the requirements effective on June 2, 2008 [@CL-EST-2510].

## How it works

Deferred compensation means an award made by an employer to compensate an employee in a future cost accounting period or periods for services rendered in one or more cost accounting periods before the date of receipt of compensation by the employee [@CL-CMP-3415]. The definition does not include the amount of year end accruals for salaries, wages, or bonuses that are to be paid within a reasonable period of time after the end of a cost accounting period [@CL-CMP-3416]. The contractor is deemed to have incurred an obligation for the cost of deferred compensation when all of the listed conditions have been met [@CL-CMP-0838]. The first two conditions are a requirement to make future payments that the contractor cannot unilaterally avoid, and an award to be satisfied by a future payment of money, other assets, or shares of stock of the contractor [@CL-CMP-0840]. The next two conditions are that the amount of the future payment can be measured with reasonable accuracy and that the recipient of the award is known [@CL-CMP-0841]. The last two conditions are a reasonable probability that any events the award requires before an employee is entitled to the benefits will occur, and, for stock options, a reasonable probability that the options ultimately will be exercised [@CL-CMP-0842].

For awards that require the employee to perform future service to receive the benefits, the obligation is deemed incurred as the future service is performed, for the part of the award attributable to that future service [@CL-CMP-0839]. If any of the conditions is not met, the cost of deferred compensation is assignable only to the cost accounting period or periods in which the compensation is paid to the employee [@CL-CMP-0843]. In one illustration, the total cost of stock options is measured by multiplying the number of shares by the difference between the option price and the market price [@CL-CMP-3417].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor grants a deferred compensation award requiring two future years of service to an identified employee. The future benefits total 100,000, and their present value is measured at 80,000. Because all recognition conditions are satisfied, the contractor incurs the obligation as future service is rendered, assigning 40,000 to Year 1 and 40,000 to Year 2. In contrast, if a required entitlement condition has no reasonable probability of occurring, no obligation is deemed incurred during the service years, and the cost is assignable only when the 100,000 is actually paid. For a separate award under an ESOP, the contractor contributes 50,000 to the plan trust, and the cost assigned to that period is the 50,000 contributed rather than a discounted present value. This example is illustrative only, and the contract itself governs.

## Never confuse

CAS 415 provides criteria for measuring the cost of deferred compensation and assigning that cost to cost accounting periods [@CL-EST-2505], applying to all deferred compensation except compensated personal absence and pension plans that do not meet the definition of an Employee Stock Ownership Plan [@CL-EST-2506]. For deferred compensation other than an ESOP, the cost is the present value of future benefits to be paid [@CL-CMP-0572], while for an ESOP the cost is the amount contributed by the contractor [@CL-CMP-0573]. In contrast, CAS 412 establishes criteria for determining and measuring the components of pension cost and assigning those costs to cost accounting periods [@CL-EST-2914; @CL-EST-2915]. CAS 412 explicitly does not cover employee stock ownership plans that meet the definition of a pension plan [@CL-EST-2917], treating them instead as deferred compensation covered under CAS 415 [@CL-EST-3036].
