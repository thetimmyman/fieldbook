## Purpose

Shows how insurance premiums, self-insurance charges, and actual losses are treated for allowability on government contracts.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-0501] |
| Codified citation | FAR 31.205-19 [@CL-CMP-0501] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |

Costs of insurance required or approved pursuant to the contract are allowable [@CL-CMP-0501].

If purchased insurance is available, self-insurance charges plus administration expenses exceeding the cost of comparable purchased insurance plus associated administration expenses are unallowable [@CL-CMP-0502].

Actual losses are unallowable unless expressly provided for in the contract, except that losses incurred under the nominal deductible provisions of purchased insurance in keeping with sound business practice, and minor losses in the ordinary course of business that are not covered by insurance (such as spoilage, breakage, and disappearance of small hand tools), are allowable [@CL-CMP-0503; @CL-CMP-0828].

## How it works

Contractors may cover risks through purchased policies or approved self-insurance programs. Captive insurers are treated as self-insurance unless the captive sells substantial insurance to the public under competitive market forces. Self-insurance charges are unallowable to the extent they exceed comparable purchased insurance costs when purchased insurance is available, and self-insurance charges for risks of catastrophic losses are unallowable. The cost of insurance to protect the contractor against the costs of correcting its own defects in materials or workmanship is unallowable, but insurance costs to cover fortuitous or casualty losses resulting from such defects are allowable as a normal business expense [@CL-CMP-0844]. The Government is obligated to indemnify the contractor only to the extent authorized by law and as expressly provided for in the contract, except as the section provides for actual losses.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor buys business liability insurance for a commercial premium of 100,000 and incurs an allowable nominal deductible loss of 5,000 after an accident. It also buys a separate policy for a premium of 20,000 whose only purpose is to pay for correcting its own defective workmanship. The 100,000 premium and the 5,000 deductible are allowable. The 20,000 premium is unallowable because it insures the cost of correcting the contractor's own defects. The correction work itself is a separate cost and is not what this rule disallows. Had the 20,000 policy covered only fortuitous or casualty losses resulting from defective workmanship, its premium would be allowable as a normal business expense.

## Never confuse

Purchased commercial insurance premiums differ from actual uninsurable losses. Nominal deductibles under sound commercial policies are allowable, but unreserved catastrophic losses and premiums that insure the cost of correcting the contractor's own defects cannot be converted into allowable insurance charges.
