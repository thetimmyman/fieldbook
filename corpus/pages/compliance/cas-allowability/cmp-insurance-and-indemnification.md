## Purpose

Shows how insurance premiums, self-insurance charges, and actual losses are treated for allowability on government contracts.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-0501] |
| Codified citation | FAR 31.205-19 [@CL-CMP-0501] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 31.205-19 [@CL-CMP-9426; @CL-CMP-9427; @CL-CMP-9429] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-0501] |
| Applies to | Contracts subject to the FAR cost principles [@CL-CMP-0501] |

Costs of insurance required or approved pursuant to the contract are allowable [@CL-CMP-0501].

If purchased insurance is available, self-insurance charges plus administration expenses exceeding the cost of comparable purchased insurance plus associated administration expenses are unallowable [@CL-CMP-0502].

Actual losses are unallowable unless expressly provided for in the contract, except that losses incurred under the nominal deductible provisions of purchased insurance in keeping with sound business practice, and minor losses in the ordinary course of business that are not covered by insurance (such as spoilage, breakage, and disappearance of small hand tools), are allowable [@CL-CMP-0503; @CL-CMP-0828].

## How it works

Captive insurers are treated as self-insurance unless the captive insurer also sells insurance to the general public in substantial quantities and it can be demonstrated that the charge to the contractor is based on competitive market forces. Self-insurance charges plus insurance administration expenses are unallowable to the extent they exceed the cost of comparable purchased insurance plus associated insurance administration expenses when purchased insurance is available [@CL-CMP-0502], and self-insurance charges for risks of catastrophic losses are unallowable [@CL-CMP-7068]. The cost of insurance to protect the contractor against the costs of correcting its own defects in materials or workmanship is unallowable, but insurance costs to cover fortuitous or casualty losses resulting from such defects are allowable as a normal business expense [@CL-CMP-0844]. The Government is obligated to indemnify the contractor only to the extent authorized by law, as expressly provided for in the contract, except as provided in paragraph (d)(3) of the subsection [@CL-CMP-8122].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 31 text in place of the codified text [@CL-CMP-9350], effective immediately under Revision 1 signed on 2026-08-04 [@CL-CMP-9351]. RFO FAR 31.205-19 provides that costs of insurance required or approved pursuant to the contract are allowable [@CL-CMP-9426], that a self-insurance charge plus insurance administration expenses in excess of the cost of comparable purchased insurance plus associated expenses is unallowable when purchased insurance is available [@CL-CMP-9427], and that self-insurance charges for risks of catastrophic losses are unallowable [@CL-CMP-9428]. It also provides that actual losses are unallowable unless expressly provided for in the contract, except for losses incurred under the nominal deductible provisions of purchased insurance, in keeping with sound business practice [@CL-CMP-9429] and minor losses in the ordinary course of business that are not covered by insurance [@CL-CMP-9430]. RFO paragraph (b)(1) says that, for purposes of applying the provisions of the section, the Government considers insurance provided by captive insurers (insurers owned by or under control of the contractor) as self-insurance [@CL-CMP-9431], and paragraph (b)(2) says that if the captive insurer also sells insurance to the general public in substantial quantities and it can be demonstrated that the charge to the contractor is based on competition, the Government will consider the insurance as purchased insurance [@CL-CMP-9432]. The codified defect-insurance paragraph (e)(3) [@CL-CMP-0844] appears in the RFO text as two paragraphs: paragraph (e)(3) makes the cost of insurance against correcting the contractor's own defects unallowable [@CL-CMP-9433], and paragraph (e)(4) makes the cost of insurance for fortuitous or casualty losses resulting from defects allowable as a normal business expense [@CL-CMP-9434]. The rule that the Government is obligated to indemnify the contractor only to the extent authorized by law, as expressly provided for in the contract, except as provided in paragraph (d)(3), is RFO paragraph (e)(6) where the codified text has paragraph (e)(5) [@CL-CMP-8122; @CL-CMP-9435].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor buys business liability insurance for a commercial premium of 100,000 and incurs an allowable nominal deductible loss of 5,000 after an accident. It also buys a separate policy for a premium of 20,000 whose only purpose is to pay for correcting its own defective workmanship. The 100,000 premium and the 5,000 deductible are allowable. The 20,000 premium is unallowable because it insures the cost of correcting the contractor's own defects. The correction work itself is a separate cost and is not what this rule disallows. Had the 20,000 policy covered only fortuitous or casualty losses resulting from defective workmanship, its premium would be allowable as a normal business expense.

## Never confuse

Actual losses are unallowable unless expressly provided for in the contract, except losses incurred under the nominal deductible provisions of purchased insurance in keeping with sound business practice [@CL-CMP-0503]. Self-insurance charges for risks of catastrophic losses are unallowable [@CL-CMP-7068], and so is the cost of insurance to protect the contractor against the costs of correcting its own defects in materials and workmanship [@CL-CMP-0844]. The codified text states the defect-insurance prohibition and the fortuitous-loss allowance in one paragraph, (e)(3) [@CL-CMP-0844], while the RFO text puts them in separate paragraphs (e)(3) and (e)(4) [@CL-CMP-9433; @CL-CMP-9434].

The clause on indemnification and medical liability insurance for health care services is covered in [Indemnification and medical liability insurance](../regulatory-framework/cmp-medical-liability-indemnity-and-insurance.md).
