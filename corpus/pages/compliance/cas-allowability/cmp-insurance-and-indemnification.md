## Purpose

Shows how insurance premiums, self-insurance charges, and actual losses are treated for allowability on government contracts.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-0501] |
| Codified citation | FAR 31.205-19 [@CL-CMP-0501] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-0501] |
| Applies to | Contracts subject to the FAR cost principles [@CL-CMP-0501] |

Costs of insurance required or approved pursuant to the contract are allowable [@CL-CMP-0501].

If purchased insurance is available, self-insurance charges plus administration expenses exceeding the cost of comparable purchased insurance plus associated administration expenses are unallowable [@CL-CMP-0502].

Actual losses are unallowable unless expressly provided for in the contract, except that losses incurred under the nominal deductible provisions of purchased insurance in keeping with sound business practice are allowable [@CL-CMP-0503].

## How it works

Contractors may cover risks through purchased policies or approved self-insurance programs. Captive insurers are treated as self-insurance unless the captive sells substantial insurance to the public under competitive market forces. Self-insurance charges are unallowable to the extent they exceed comparable purchased insurance costs when purchased insurance is available, and self-insurance charges for risks of catastrophic losses are unallowable. Premiums to protect against correcting the contractor's own defects in materials or workmanship are unallowable. The Government is obligated to indemnify the contractor only to the extent authorized by law and as expressly provided for in the contract, except as the section provides for actual losses.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor purchases business liability insurance with a commercial premium of 100,000 and incurs an allowable nominal deductible loss of 5,000 following an accident. The contractor also incurs an uninsured defect correction expense of 25,000 on its own workmanship. The 100,000 premium and the 5,000 deductible are allowable costs, whereas the 25,000 defect correction expense is unallowable.

## Never confuse

Purchased commercial insurance premiums differ from actual uninsurable losses. Nominal deductibles under sound commercial policies are allowable, but unreserved catastrophic losses or unallowable defect correction costs cannot be converted into allowable insurance charges.
