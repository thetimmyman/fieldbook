## Purpose

Explains what the Disclosure and Consistency of Cost Accounting Practices clause requires of the contractor, and how a change to a cost accounting practice is handled and paid for.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-1600] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.230-3 [@CL-CMP-1600] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-1600] |
| Applies to | Contracts that include the clause [@CL-CMP-1600] |

The contractor must comply with four cost accounting standards: consistency in estimating, accumulating, and reporting costs; consistency in allocating costs incurred for the same purpose; accounting for unallowable costs; and the cost accounting period [@CL-CMP-0951]. They apply as in effect on the date of award of the contract [@CL-CMP-0952].

The contractor must follow its cost accounting practices consistently, but either the Government or the contractor may propose a change, and the contractor agrees to negotiate the terms with the contracting officer [@CL-CMP-0955]. Once the terms are agreed, the change is applied prospectively and the Disclosure Statement is amended if it is affected [@CL-CMP-0956].

## How it works

On a contract covered by the cost accounting standards, a business unit of a company required to submit a Disclosure Statement must disclose its cost accounting practices in writing [@CL-CMP-0953]. A Disclosure Statement that the contractor has told the contracting officer contains privileged and confidential trade secrets and commercial or financial information is protected and not released outside the Government [@CL-CMP-0954]. When the parties agree to a change and the contracting officer has made the required finding that the change is desirable and not detrimental to the interests of the Government, the contractor negotiates an equitable adjustment [@CL-CMP-0957]. Without that finding, no agreement may be made that will increase costs paid by the United States [@CL-CMP-0958]. Where the contractor or a subcontractor fails to comply with applicable standards or to follow a practice and the failure increases costs paid by the United States, the contractor agrees to an adjustment that recovers the increase with interest [@CL-CMP-0959; @CL-CMP-0960]. A failure to agree on compliance or on a demanded cost adjustment is a dispute under chapter 71 of title 41 of the United States Code [@CL-CMP-0961]. The contractor must also let authorized Government representatives examine and copy records on compliance [@CL-CMP-0962].

The contractor must pass the substance of the clause, except the dispute paragraph, to its negotiated subcontracts and require it in other subcontracts of any tier, subject to listed exceptions [@CL-CMP-0963]. The requirement reaches only negotiated subcontracts above the lower cost accounting standards threshold on the date of subcontract award [@CL-CMP-0965], and it does not reach negotiated subcontracts that are otherwise exempt [@CL-CMP-0966]. A subcontract awarded to a business unit under other types of coverage takes the applicable clause instead [@CL-CMP-0964].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A business unit proposes a change to its cost accounting practice after award, and the price effect is 100,000. The contracting officer makes the required finding, so the parties agree an equitable adjustment of 100,000 that applies only to performance after the change.

## Never confuse

Following a practice consistently is not the same as freezing it: either party may propose a change, and the change then moves forward prospectively [@CL-CMP-0955; @CL-CMP-0956]. The disclosure duty is a separate obligation and arises only for a business unit of a company required to submit a Disclosure Statement [@CL-CMP-0953]. The foreign-concern clause names fewer standards; see [Disclosure and consistency for foreign concerns](cmp-consistency-cost-accounting-practices.md). The Cost Accounting Standards clause has its own page, [Cost Accounting Standards clause](cmp-cost-accounting-standards.md).
