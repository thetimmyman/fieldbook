## Purpose

Gives the exact definitions that the allowability and associated-cost rules rely on, and the cost objective definitions that carry carve-outs for named subparts.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-2700] |
| Codified citation | FAR 31.001 [@CL-CMP-2700] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 31.002 [@CL-CMP-9656; @CL-CMP-9657; @CL-CMP-9658; @CL-CMP-9659; @CL-CMP-9660; @CL-CMP-9661] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-2700] |
| Applies to | The terms as used in this part, subject to the carve-outs printed in each definition [@CL-CMP-2700; @CL-CMP-2703; @CL-CMP-2704; @CL-CMP-2705] |

The section states that its definitions apply as the terms are used in this part [@CL-CMP-2700]. A directly associated cost means any cost which is generated solely as a result of the incurrence of another cost, and which would not have been incurred had the other cost not been incurred [@CL-CMP-2701]. An expressly unallowable cost means a particular item or type of cost which, under the express provisions of an applicable law, regulation, or contract, is specifically named and stated to be unallowable [@CL-CMP-2702].

A cost objective means, except for subpart 31.6, a function, organizational subdivision, contract, or other work unit for which cost data are desired and for which provision is made to accumulate and measure the cost of processes, products, jobs, capitalized projects and similar items [@CL-CMP-2703]. A final cost objective means, except for subparts 31.3 and 31.6, a cost objective that has allocated to it both direct and indirect costs and, in the contractor's accumulation system, is one of the final accumulation points [@CL-CMP-2704]. Indirect cost pools means, except for subparts 31.3 and 31.6, groupings of incurred costs identified with two or more cost objectives but not identified specifically with any final cost objective [@CL-CMP-2705].

## How it works

The section is a glossary for the terms the cost principles use. The two allowability terms printed on this card are directly associated cost and expressly unallowable cost [@CL-CMP-2701; @CL-CMP-2702]. The three cost objective terms carry carve-outs for named subparts, so check the subpart before relying on them [@CL-CMP-2703; @CL-CMP-2704; @CL-CMP-2705]. The section does not define allowable cost, allocable cost, reasonable cost, direct cost or indirect cost as terms of their own. For the allowability tests, see [Factors determining cost allowability](../cas-allowability/cmp-allowability-factors.md). For the subpart that each of the three carve-outs names, see [Cost principles for State, local and tribal government contracts](../cas-allowability/cmp-cost-principles-state-local-tribal-governments.md) [@CL-CMP-2703; @CL-CMP-2704; @CL-CMP-2705].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 31 text in place of the codified text [@CL-CMP-9350], effective immediately under Revision 1 signed on 2026-08-04 [@CL-CMP-9351]. The RFO text renumbers the section: its definitions are in RFO FAR 31.002, which says they apply as the terms are used in this part [@CL-CMP-9656], and RFO FAR 31.001 is now titled Applicability [@CL-CMP-9655]. RFO FAR 31.002 keeps the definitions of directly associated cost [@CL-CMP-9657] and expressly unallowable cost [@CL-CMP-9658], and the three cost objective definitions with their carve-outs for named subparts [@CL-CMP-9659; @CL-CMP-9660; @CL-CMP-9661]. It drops the codified entries for accumulating costs and actual cash value [@CL-CMP-9662], compensated personal absence [@CL-CMP-9663], labor and material standards, labor market and moving average cost [@CL-CMP-9664], original complement of low cost equipment [@CL-CMP-9665], termination of employment gain or loss [@CL-CMP-9666] and weighted average cost [@CL-CMP-9667].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor incurs 10,000 for an activity that a cost principle names as unallowable, so the cost is expressly unallowable. A further 1,000 of cost arises only because of that activity and would not exist without it. The answer: the 1,000 is a directly associated cost under the definition, and the accounting rules then decide whether it must be excluded.

## Never confuse

Directly associated cost and indirect cost answer different questions, and a cost can be both. A directly associated cost is defined by being generated solely by another cost [@CL-CMP-2701], while an indirect cost pool is defined as a grouping of incurred costs identified with two or more cost objectives [@CL-CMP-2705], so a directly associated cost may sit in an indirect cost pool. For pools and bases, see [Indirect costs: pools and allocation bases](../../indirect-rates/pools-bases/rte-indirect-cost-pools-bases.md). For how unallowable costs and their directly associated costs are identified and excluded, see [Accounting for unallowable and directly associated costs](../cas-allowability/cmp-accounting-for-unallowable-and-associated-costs.md). For how unallowable costs are accounted for under the cost accounting standard, see [Accounting for unallowable costs](../cas-allowability/cmp-cas-405-unallowable-costs.md), and for penalties on expressly unallowable costs, see [Unallowable-cost penalties](../cas-allowability/cmp-unallowable-cost-penalty.md). The codified definitions are in FAR 31.001 [@CL-CMP-2700], but in the RFO text 31.001 is Applicability [@CL-CMP-9655] and the definitions are in 31.002 [@CL-CMP-9656].
