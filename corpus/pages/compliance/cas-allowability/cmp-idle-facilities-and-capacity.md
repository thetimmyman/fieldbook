## Purpose

Separates the cost of capacity a facility did not use from the cost of a facility that stands completely unused and is excess to current needs.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-0763] |
| Codified citation | FAR 31.205-17 [@CL-CMP-0763] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 31.205-17 [@CL-CMP-9415; @CL-CMP-9416; @CL-CMP-9418] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-0763] |
| Applies to | Unused capacity of partially used facilities [@CL-CMP-0763] and completely unused facilities [@CL-CMP-0766] |

Idle capacity is the unused capacity of partially used facilities [@CL-CMP-0763]. The measurement begins with what a facility could achieve under 100 percent operating time on a one-shift basis [@CL-CMP-0764]. That baseline is reduced by operating interruptions resulting from time lost for repairs, setups, unsatisfactory materials and other normal delays, and is compared with the extent to which the facility was actually used to meet demands during the accounting period [@CL-CMP-1112]. A multiple-shift basis may be used in the calculation instead of a one-shift basis if it can be shown that this amount of usage could normally be expected for the type of facility involved [@CL-CMP-0765]. Idle facilities, by contrast, are completely unused facilities that are excess to the contractor's current needs [@CL-CMP-0766].

Costs of idle facilities are unallowable unless the facilities are necessary to meet fluctuations in workload [@CL-CMP-0767], or were necessary when acquired and are now idle because of changes in requirements, production economies, reorganization, termination or other causes which could not have been reasonably foreseen [@CL-CMP-0768]. A parenthetical after those two exceptions says costs of idle facilities are allowable for a reasonable period, ordinarily not to exceed 1 year, depending upon the initiative taken to use, lease or dispose of the idle facilities [@CL-CMP-0769]. Costs of idle capacity are allowable provided the capacity is necessary or was originally reasonable and is not subject to reduction or elimination by subletting, renting or sale, in accordance with sound business, economics or security practices [@CL-CMP-0770].

## How it works

The contractor measures capacity first, setting actual use for the accounting period against what the facility could achieve under 100 percent operating time on a one-shift basis, or on a multiple-shift basis where that usage can be shown to be normally expected [@CL-CMP-0764; @CL-CMP-0765]. The unused capacity of a partially used facility is idle capacity [@CL-CMP-0763], while a facility that is completely unused and excess to current needs is an idle facility [@CL-CMP-0766]. Costs of the first kind are allowable while the capacity is necessary or was originally reasonable and is not subject to reduction or elimination by subletting, renting or sale, in accordance with sound business, economics or security practices [@CL-CMP-0770]. Costs of the second kind are unallowable unless an exception fits [@CL-CMP-0767; @CL-CMP-0768], and the parenthetical after the exceptions allows them for a reasonable period, ordinarily not to exceed 1 year [@CL-CMP-0769]. Widespread idle capacity across a plant or a group of similar assets may itself be treated as idle facilities [@CL-CMP-0771], and capacity the Government pays for directly to hold defense mobilization production requires a separate agreement [@CL-CMP-0772].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 31 text in place of the codified text [@CL-CMP-9350], effective immediately under Revision 1 signed on 2026-08-04 [@CL-CMP-9351]. RFO FAR 31.205-17 keeps the rules described above [@CL-CMP-9415; @CL-CMP-9418]. Idle capacity is the unused capacity of partially used facilities [@CL-CMP-9421], and its measure starts from what a facility could achieve under 100 percent operating time on a one-shift basis, less operating interruptions resulting from time lost for repairs, setups, unsatisfactory materials, and other normal delays [@CL-CMP-9422], compared with the extent to which the facility was actually used to meet demands during the accounting period [@CL-CMP-9423]. A multiple-shift basis may be used instead of a one-shift basis if it can be shown that this amount of usage could normally be expected for the type of facility involved [@CL-CMP-9424]. Idle facilities are completely unused facilities that are excess to the contractor's current needs [@CL-CMP-9425]. Costs of idle facilities are unallowable unless the facilities are necessary to meet fluctuations in workload [@CL-CMP-9415] or, as the second alternative, were necessary when acquired and are now idle because of changes in requirements, production economies, reorganization, termination, or other causes that could not have been reasonably foreseen [@CL-CMP-9416], and the parenthetical keeps the allowance for a reasonable period, ordinarily not to exceed one year [@CL-CMP-9417]. Costs of idle capacity are allowable when the capacity is necessary or was originally reasonable and is not subject to reduction or elimination by subletting, renting, or sale, in accordance with sound business, economics, or security practices [@CL-CMP-9418], and widespread idle capacity throughout an entire plant or among a group of assets having substantially the same function may be idle facilities [@CL-CMP-9419]. Two wordings change: the parenthetical introduces its cross-reference to 31.205-42 with "except see" where the codified text says "but see" [@CL-CMP-9417], and paragraph (d), the separate-agreement rule for costs the Government pays directly, now ends with a cross-reference to 31.110(b)(9) [@CL-CMP-9420].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A plant could achieve 2,500 hours a year on a one-shift basis after normal delays, but actually ran 1,500 hours. The 1,000 unused hours carry rent and maintenance that are idle capacity costs, allowable while the capacity stays necessary and cannot be sublet. A second building stood empty all year and is excess to needs, so its costs are unallowable unless it is necessary to meet fluctuations in workload or was necessary when acquired and is now idle for causes that could not have been reasonably foreseen.

## Never confuse

Idle capacity is not the same thing as idle facilities. Idle capacity is the unused capacity of a partially used facility [@CL-CMP-0763], and its costs are allowable provided the capacity is necessary or was originally reasonable and is not subject to reduction or elimination by subletting, renting or sale [@CL-CMP-0770], whereas idle facilities are completely unused and excess to needs, with costs that are unallowable unless an exception applies [@CL-CMP-0767]. Capacity that is merely underused across a whole plant may still be treated as idle facilities [@CL-CMP-0771]. Rent and depreciation on idle assets are inputs to these amounts, not separate questions; see [Rental costs](cmp-rental-costs.md).

## Company calibration

Whether the company measures idle capacity on a documented shift basis and separates it from idle facilities inside its pools [@Q-024].
