## Purpose

Identifies entertainment, diversion, and social club expenses as expressly unallowable on government contracts.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-0107] |
| Codified citation | FAR 31.205-14 [@CL-CMP-0107] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 31.205-14 [@CL-CMP-9952] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-0107] |
| Applies to | Government contracts subject to the cost principles [@CL-CMP-0107] |

Costs of amusement, diversions, social activities, and directly associated costs such as tickets to shows or sports events, meals, lodging, rentals, transportation, and gratuities are unallowable [@CL-CMP-0107]. Costs made specifically unallowable under this cost principle are not allowable under any other cost principle [@CL-CMP-0108]. Costs of membership in social, dining or country clubs or other organizations having the same purposes are also unallowable, regardless of whether the cost is reported as taxable income to the employees [@CL-CMP-9623].

## How it works

Costs of amusement, diversions, and social activities are unallowable [@CL-CMP-0107]. Any directly associated costs, such as tickets to shows or sports events, meals, lodging, rentals, transportation, and gratuities, are also unallowable [@CL-CMP-0107]. Costs made specifically unallowable under this cost principle are not allowable under any other cost principle [@CL-CMP-0108]. Costs of membership in social, dining, or country clubs or other organizations having the same purposes are also unallowable [@CL-CMP-9623].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers must use the RFO FAR part 31 text in place of the codified text [@CL-CMP-9350], effective immediately under Revision 1 signed on 2026-08-04 [@CL-CMP-9351]. Under RFO FAR 31.205-14(a), costs of amusement, diversions, social activities, and any directly associated costs, such as tickets to shows or sporting events, meals, lodging, rentals, transportation, and gratuities are unallowable [@CL-CMP-9952]. RFO paragraph (b) says costs of membership in social, dining, country clubs, or other organizations having the same purposes are also unallowable, regardless of whether the cost is reported as taxable income to the employees [@CL-CMP-9953]. RFO paragraph (c) says costs made specifically unallowable under this cost principle are not allowable under any other cost principle [@CL-CMP-9954].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor hosts an annual customer appreciation banquet costing 25,000 for dinner and entertainment, along with 5,000 for charter bus transportation for attendees. Both the 25,000 banquet cost and the 5,000 directly associated transportation cost are expressly unallowable. The entire 30,000 must be identified and removed from indirect cost rate pools.

## Never confuse

Costs of amusement, diversions and social activities, and directly associated costs such as meals, lodging, rentals, transportation and gratuities, are unallowable under this principle and cannot be made allowable under any other cost principle [@CL-CMP-0107; @CL-CMP-0108]. Any directly associated costs of amusement, diversions or social activities are unallowable [@CL-CMP-0107]. For penalties on unallowable costs in indirect cost rate proposals, see [Unallowable-cost penalties](cmp-unallowable-cost-penalty.md).
