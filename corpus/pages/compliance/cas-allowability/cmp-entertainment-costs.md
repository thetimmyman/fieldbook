## Purpose

Identifies entertainment, diversion, and social club expenses as expressly unallowable on government contracts.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-0107] |
| Codified citation | FAR 31.205-14 [@CL-CMP-0107] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 31.205-14 [@CL-CMP-0107] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-0107] |
| Applies to | Government contracts subject to the cost principles [@CL-CMP-0107] |

Costs of amusement, diversions, social activities, and directly associated costs such as tickets to shows, meals, lodging, rentals, transportation, and gratuities are unallowable [@CL-CMP-0107]. Costs made specifically unallowable under the entertainment cost principle cannot be made allowable under any other cost principle [@CL-CMP-0108]. Costs of membership in social, dining or country clubs or other organizations having the same purposes are also unallowable, regardless of whether the cost is reported as taxable income to the employees.

## How it works

The entertainment cost principle establishes a strict prohibition against charging amusement or social expenses to government work. The ban extends to any directly associated costs, such as tickets to shows or sports events, meals, lodging, rentals, transportation and gratuities [@CL-CMP-0107]. Contractors cannot circumvent the disallowance by claiming entertainment under employee morale or other general cost categories. All prohibited social and dining club membership costs must be excluded from indirect pools and incurred cost proposals.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor hosts an annual customer appreciation banquet costing 25,000 for dinner and entertainment, along with 5,000 for charter bus transportation for attendees. Both the 25,000 banquet cost and the 5,000 directly associated transportation cost are expressly unallowable. The entire 30,000 must be identified and removed from indirect cost rate pools.

## Never confuse

Costs of amusement, diversions and social activities, and directly associated costs such as meals, lodging, rentals, transportation and gratuities, are unallowable under this principle and cannot be made allowable under any other cost principle [@CL-CMP-0107; @CL-CMP-0108]. Directly associated expenses tied to an unallowable social outing are also tainted and barred. Leaving expressly unallowable entertainment in indirect cost rate proposals risks penalties; see [Unallowable-cost penalties](cmp-unallowable-cost-penalty.md).
