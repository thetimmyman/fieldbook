## Purpose

Cost Accounting Standard 413 provides guidance for adjusting pension cost by measuring actuarial gains and losses and assigning them to cost accounting periods, and establishes the bases on which pension cost is allocated to segments of an organization. This concept hub sets out what the standard requires and links the detailed reference, pension adjustment, and segment allocation pages across the corpus.

## The rule

The purpose of CAS 413 is to provide guidance for adjusting pension cost by measuring actuarial gains and losses and assigning them to cost accounting periods [@CL-EST-2918], and to provide the bases on which pension cost shall be allocated to segments of an organization [@CL-EST-2919], enhancing uniformity and consistency in accounting for pension costs [@CL-EST-2920]. Actuarial gains and losses are calculated annually and assigned to the period for which the valuation is made and to later periods [@CL-CMP-0562]. The actuarial value of plan assets must be determined under an asset valuation method that takes into account unrealized appreciation and depreciation of market value, includes the accumulated value of permitted unfunded accruals, and is used in measuring the components of pension costs [@CL-CMP-0563; @CL-CMP-0832]. Pension cost is allocated to each segment that has participants in a pension plan [@CL-CMP-0564]. A separate calculation of pension costs for a segment is required when specified conditions are present [@CL-CMP-0565], but when those conditions are not present, allocations may be made by calculating a composite pension cost for two or more segments and allocating this cost by means of an allocation base [@CL-CMP-0833]. When pension costs are separately computed for a segment or segments, the assignable cost limitation is based on the actuarial value of assets, actuarial accrued liability and normal cost for the segment or segments [@CL-CMP-8112; @CL-CMP-8113], and the amount assignable to a segment or segments shall not exceed the sum of the maximum tax-deductible amount computed for the plan as a whole and the accumulated value of prepayment credits not already allocated to segments apportioned among them [@CL-CMP-0566; @CL-CMP-7060].

If a segment is closed, if there is a pension plan termination, or if there is a curtailment of benefits, the contractor must determine the difference between the actuarial accrued liability for the segment and the market value of the assets allocated to the segment, irrespective of whether or not the pension plan is terminated [@CL-MNA-0392; @CL-MNA-1579]. That difference represents an adjustment of previously-determined pension costs [@CL-MNA-0393]. The Government's share of the adjustment amount for a segment is the product of the adjustment amount and a fraction, reduced for any excise tax imposed on assets withdrawn from the funding agency of a qualified pension plan [@CL-MNA-0399]. The numerator of that fraction is the sum of pension plan costs allocated to all contracts and subcontracts, including Foreign Military Sales, subject to the Standard during a period of years representative of Government participation [@CL-MNA-0463], and the denominator is the total pension costs assigned to cost accounting periods during those same years [@CL-MNA-0464]. There is no exemption for CAS 413 [@CL-CMP-8659]. The standard became effective on February 27, 2012, and is applicable for cost accounting periods after June 30, 2012 [@CL-CMP-3413]. Under the transition method, all adjustments shall be prospective only, although costs and prices of prior and existing contracts not subject to price adjustment may be considered in determining the appropriate transition method or adjustment amount [@CL-CMP-3414].

## How it works

A segment closing means that a segment has been sold or ownership has been otherwise transferred, has discontinued operations, or has discontinued doing or actively seeking Government business under contracts subject to the Standard [@CL-MNA-0391]. The actuarial accrued liability for the adjustment is determined using the accrued benefit cost method with assumptions consistent with current and prior long term assumptions [@CL-MNA-0394], or upon plan termination as the amount paid to irrevocably settle all benefit obligations or paid to the Pension Benefit Guarantee Corporation [@CL-MNA-0459]. The market value of segment assets is reduced by the accumulated value of prepayment credits and increased by the current value of any separately identified unfunded actuarial liability [@CL-MNA-0395]. The difference is calculated as of the date of the event that caused the segment closing, plan termination, or curtailment of benefits [@CL-MNA-0396; @CL-MNA-0092], or an agreed date if the event date is not readily determinable or inequitable [@CL-MNA-0460]. Plan improvements adopted within 60 months of the event date that increase the liability are recognized on a prorata phase-in basis, unless mandated by law or collective bargaining agreement [@CL-MNA-0397; @CL-MNA-0461]. When a segment is closed through an ownership transfer to a successor in interest and all plan assets and liabilities are transferred, no adjustment amount is required [@CL-MNA-1580; @CL-MNA-0398]; if only some are transferred, the adjustment is based on assets and liabilities remaining with the contractor [@CL-MNA-0462]; and in either case the effect of transferred assets and liabilities carries forward at the successor contractor [@CL-MNA-0117].

In the standard's illustrations, the actuarial value of the assets of the pension plan must fall within a corridor from 80 to 120 percent of market value [@CL-CMP-3412]. When the asset value reached falls outside that corridor, it must be adjusted to equal the nearest boundary of the corridor [@CL-CMP-3441]. Actuarial gains and losses measured for periods beginning on or after the Applicability Date for the CAS Pension Harmonization Rule are amortized over a ten-year period [@CL-CMP-3411]. In contrast, if the actuarial gain or loss is measured for a period beginning before that Applicability Date, the gain or loss is amortized over a fifteen-year period [@CL-CMP-3440].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor calculates a composite pension cost of 10,000,000 across two segments because conditions requiring separate calculation are absent. Using pensionable payroll as the allocation base, segment A with 60 percent of payroll receives 6,000,000 and segment B with 40 percent receives 4,000,000. For plan asset valuation, the market value of assets is 50,000,000, establishing an 80 to 120 percent corridor of 40,000,000 to 60,000,000; because an initial valuation of 38,000,000 falls below the corridor, it is adjusted to the nearest boundary of 40,000,000. In addition, an actuarial gain of 1,000,000 measured after the harmonization applicability date is amortized over ten years, producing an annual gain adjustment of 100,000 before interest equivalents. In a separate segment closing where assets allocated to the closed segment are 25,000,000 and actuarial accrued liability is 20,000,000, the 5,000,000 difference is adjusted, and with a representative Government participation fraction of 40 percent, the Government share is 2,000,000. This example is illustrative only, and the contract itself governs.

## Never confuse

CAS 413 provides guidance for adjusting pension cost by measuring actuarial gains and losses and assigning them to cost accounting periods, and establishes the bases on which pension cost is allocated to segments of an organization [@CL-EST-2918; @CL-EST-2919; @CL-CMP-0564], whereas CAS 412 provides guidance for determining and measuring the components of pension cost and establishes the basis on which pension costs are assigned to cost accounting periods [@CL-EST-2914; @CL-EST-2915]. CAS 412 identifies the core components of pension cost, such as normal cost and amortization of unfunded actuarial liability, and governs period assignability for the plan as a whole [@CL-CMP-0557; @CL-CMP-0561], while CAS 413 governs the subsequent allocation of pension cost to individual segments, the asset valuation corridor, and mandatory cost adjustments upon a segment closing, plan termination or benefit curtailment [@CL-CMP-0564; @CL-CMP-3412; @CL-MNA-0392].
