## Purpose

Introduces the Cost Accounting Standards: what they govern, when they apply, and what coverage brings with it.

## The rule

The Cost Accounting Standards govern how a contractor measures, assigns and allocates costs to contracts: consistency between estimating and accounting, consistent allocation, the cost accounting period, unallowable costs, and the allocation of indirect costs [@S-0014-v1]. Negotiated contracts and subcontracts at or below the exemption value are exempt from all of them [@CL-CMP-0001]. A business unit with a large enough award, or enough covered awards in the prior period, has full coverage [@CL-CMP-0002], and a unit selected for a large covered award must first submit a Disclosure Statement [@CL-CMP-0003].

## How it works

Coverage is tested at award and then by business unit. A covered contract carries the standards clause, the contractor follows its disclosed practices, and a change to a practice needs a cost impact process with the contracting officer. Each standard has a fundamental requirement and techniques for applying it; the clause cards for single standards give the detail. See [CAS applicability](cmp-cas-applicability.md) for the thresholds.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A business unit wins its first negotiated award of 60 million. The award exceeds the exemption, so it is covered, but the unit has no prior covered awards large enough for full coverage, so modified coverage is the likely result, pending the clause the contract carries.

## Never confuse

The standards decide how costs are measured and allocated; the FAR cost principles decide whether a cost is allowable at all. A cost can follow the standards and still be unallowable.

## Company calibration

Which business units have full coverage, modified coverage or neither, and whether a Disclosure Statement is on file. [@Q-033]
