## Purpose

The cost principles establish the allowability rules for selected items of cost on government contracts. This page serves as a starting point explaining what a cost principle decides, the factors every cost must pass to be allowable, and where to find each individual principle.

## The rule

FAR part 31 contains cost principles and procedures for pricing contracts, subcontracts, and modifications whenever cost analysis is performed, and for determining, negotiating, or allowing costs when required by a contract clause [@CL-CMP-8711]. Costs are allowable to the extent they are reasonable, allocable, and determined to be allowable under FAR 31.201, 31.202, 31.203, and 31.205, and these criteria apply to all selected items of cost even if particular guidance is provided for certain items for emphasis or clarity [@CL-CMP-2733].

A cost is allowable only when it complies with all listed requirements: reasonableness, allocability, standards promulgated by the Cost Accounting Standards Board if applicable (or otherwise generally accepted accounting principles and practices appropriate to the circumstances), the terms of the contract, and any limitations set forth in subpart 31.2 [@CL-CMP-0101; @CL-CMP-8030]. A cost is reasonable if, in its nature and amount, it does not exceed that which would be incurred by a prudent person in the conduct of competitive business [@CL-CMP-0103], and no presumption of reasonableness attaches to the incurrence of costs by a contractor [@CL-CMP-0104]. A cost is allocable if it is assignable or chargeable to one or more cost objectives on the basis of relative benefits received or other equitable relationship [@CL-CMP-0105]. The contracting officer may disallow all or part of a claimed cost that is inadequately supported [@CL-CMP-0102].

Section 31.205 does not cover every element of cost, and the omission of an item implies neither that it is allowable nor that it is unallowable, with allowability determined from the principles and standards of the subpart and the treatment of similar or related selected items [@CL-CMP-2738]. When more than one subsection in FAR 31.205 is relevant to a contractor cost, the cost shall be apportioned among the applicable subsections and judged under each applicable subsection's guidance [@CL-CMP-2739], but if it cannot be apportioned, allowability shall be based on the subsection that most specifically deals with, or best captures the essential nature of, the cost at issue [@CL-CMP-2773]. Although the total cost of a contract includes all costs properly allocable to it, allowable costs to the government are limited to those allocable costs that are allowable under Part 31 and applicable agency supplements [@CL-CMP-7510].

## How it works

A cost principle decides whether a selected item of cost is allowable, partly allowable, or unallowable, such as the complete unallowability of amusement and social activities [@CL-CMP-0107], alcoholic beverages [@CL-CMP-0371], and bad debts including collection and legal costs [@CL-CMP-0304]. Costs that are expressly unallowable or mutually agreed to be unallowable, including mutually agreed unallowable directly associated costs, shall be identified and excluded from any billing, claim, or proposal applicable to a government contract [@CL-CMP-2724]. A directly associated cost is any cost generated solely as a result of incurring another cost that would not have been incurred had the other cost not been incurred, and when an unallowable cost is incurred, its directly associated costs are also unallowable [@CL-CMP-2725].

Certain cost principles in subpart 31.2 incorporate the measurement, assignment, and allocability rules of selected Cost Accounting Standards and limit the allowability of costs to the amounts determined using the criteria in those selected standards [@CL-CMP-9613]. Under codified FAR 31.201-2(b), only those Cost Accounting Standards or portions of standards specifically made applicable by the cost principles in this subpart are mandatory unless the contract is CAS-covered under 48 CFR 9903 [@CL-CMP-9614].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor business unit prepares an interim billing reflecting 500,000 dollars of recorded costs under a cost-reimbursement contract. Within that amount, the unit identifies 12,000 dollars of entertainment costs, 3,000 dollars of alcoholic beverages, and 4,000 dollars of directly associated costs generated solely by the entertainment event. Subtracting the 19,000 dollars of unallowable costs (12,000 dollars plus 3,000 dollars plus 4,000 dollars) from the 500,000 dollars leaves 481,000 dollars of costs to test for reasonableness and allocability. These figures are illustrative only, and the contract itself governs.

## Never confuse

An allowable cost is not the same as a merely reasonable or allocable cost, because a cost is allowable only when it complies with all listed requirements under FAR 31.201-2(a), reasonableness and allocability being only the first two [@CL-CMP-0101; @CL-CMP-8030]. In addition, a FAR 31.205 cost principle is not a general Cost Accounting Standard: including selected standards in the cost principles does not subject the business unit to any other CAS rules and regulations [@CL-CMP-9616], and the applicability of CAS rules and regulations is determined by the CAS clause, if any, in the contract and the requirements of the standards themselves [@CL-CMP-8032].
