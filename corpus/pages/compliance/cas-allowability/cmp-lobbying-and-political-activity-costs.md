## Purpose

Governs the unallowability of political campaign, election, and legislative lobbying expenses and requires their separate identification in indirect cost proposals.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-0311] |
| Codified citation | FAR 31.205-22 [@CL-CMP-0311] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 31.205-22 [@CL-CMP-0311] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-0311] |
| Applies to | Government contracts subject to the cost principles [@CL-CMP-0311] |

Costs associated with attempts to influence the outcomes of any Federal, State, or local election, referendum, initiative, or similar procedure, through in kind or cash contributions, endorsements, publicity, or similar activities, are unallowable [@CL-CMP-0311]. Costs of political parties and of attempts to influence legislation by the means listed in paragraph (a) are also unallowable, subject to the exceptions in paragraph (b). Providing a technical and factual presentation on a topic directly related to contract performance in response to a documented request is excepted, provided the information is readily obtainable and can be readily put in deliverable form, and transportation, lodging and meals remain unallowable unless incurred to offer testimony at a regularly scheduled Congressional hearing under a written request from the Chairman or Ranking Minority Member [@CL-CMP-0312; @CL-CMP-0819; @CL-CMP-0820; @CL-CMP-0821; @CL-CMP-0822]. When a contractor seeks reimbursement for indirect costs, total lobbying costs must be separately identified in the indirect cost rate proposal and adequate records maintained [@CL-CMP-0313; @CL-CMP-0314].

## How it works

Costs of attempts to influence the outcomes of Federal, State, or local elections, referenda, initiatives or similar procedures are unallowable [@CL-CMP-0311]; costs of attempts to influence legislation by the means listed in paragraph (a) are also unallowable, subject to the paragraph (b) exceptions. The unallowability extends to grassroots lobbying campaigns and legislative liaison activities conducted in preparation for unallowable lobbying. A specific exception permits providing technical and factual contract performance information in response to a documented legislative request, provided the information is readily obtainable and can be readily put in deliverable form, and subject to the restriction on transportation, lodging and meals [@CL-CMP-0312; @CL-CMP-0819; @CL-CMP-0820; @CL-CMP-0821]. When submitting indirect cost rate proposals, contractors must separately identify total lobbying costs and treat them as unallowable activity costs [@CL-CMP-0313]. Adequate records must be maintained to demonstrate that the certification of costs as being allowable or unallowable complies with the requirements of the subsection [@CL-CMP-0314].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor incurs 80,000 for government affairs personnel advocating for pending legislative appropriations, and 15,000 for travel and preparation to deliver technical contract progress testimony requested in writing by a congressional committee chairman. The 80,000 in legislative lobbying expense is unallowable and must be separately identified in the indirect rate submission. The 15,000 is allowable under the documented legislative request exception only if the information is readily obtainable and the travel is to offer testimony at a regularly scheduled Congressional hearing under the committee chairman's written request; otherwise the travel part is unallowable.

## Never confuse

Attempts to influence legislation by the means listed in paragraph (a) are unallowable lobbying, whereas a technical and factual presentation on a topic directly related to contract performance, given in response to a documented request and meeting the stated provisos, is excepted [@CL-CMP-0312; @CL-CMP-0819; @CL-CMP-0820; @CL-CMP-0821; @CL-CMP-0822]. Legislative liaison activities are unallowable when carried on in support of or in knowing preparation for an effort to engage in unallowable activities. See [Unallowable-cost penalties](cmp-unallowable-cost-penalty.md) for the consequences of including unallowable costs in indirect cost proposals.
