## Purpose

Governs the unallowability of political campaign, election, and legislative lobbying expenses and requires their separate identification in indirect cost proposals.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-0311] |
| Codified citation | FAR 31.205-22 [@CL-CMP-0311] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 31.205-22 [@CL-CMP-8556; @CL-CMP-8560] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-0311] |
| Applies to | Government contracts subject to the cost principles [@CL-CMP-0311] |

Costs associated with attempts to influence the outcomes of any Federal, State, or local election, referendum, initiative, or similar procedure, through in kind or cash contributions, endorsements, publicity, or similar activities, are unallowable [@CL-CMP-0311]. Costs of political parties and of attempts to influence legislation by the means listed in codified paragraph (a) are also unallowable, subject to the exceptions in codified paragraph (b) [@CL-CMP-8811; @CL-CMP-8812; @CL-CMP-8586; @CL-CMP-8587; @CL-CMP-8588]. Providing a technical and factual presentation on a topic directly related to contract performance in response to a documented request made by the recipient member, legislative body or subdivision, or a cognizant staff member is excepted, provided the information is readily obtainable and can be readily put in deliverable form, and transportation, lodging and meals remain unallowable unless incurred to offer testimony at a regularly scheduled Congressional hearing under a written request from the Chairman or Ranking Minority Member [@CL-CMP-0312; @CL-CMP-0819; @CL-CMP-0820; @CL-CMP-0821; @CL-CMP-0822]. When a contractor seeks reimbursement for indirect costs, total lobbying costs must be separately identified in the indirect cost rate proposal and adequate records maintained [@CL-CMP-0313; @CL-CMP-0314].

## How it works

Costs of attempts to influence the outcomes of Federal, State, or local elections, referenda, initiatives or similar procedures are unallowable [@CL-CMP-0311]; costs of attempts to influence legislation by the means listed in codified paragraph (a) are also unallowable, subject to the codified paragraph (b) exceptions [@CL-CMP-8811; @CL-CMP-8586; @CL-CMP-8587; @CL-CMP-8588]. The unallowability extends to attempts to influence legislation by preparing, distributing or using publicity or propaganda [@CL-CMP-8811; @CL-CMP-8587]. A specific exception permits providing technical and factual contract performance information in response to a documented request made by the recipient member, legislative body or subdivision, or a cognizant staff member, provided the information is readily obtainable and can be readily put in deliverable form, and subject to the restriction on transportation, lodging and meals [@CL-CMP-0312; @CL-CMP-0819; @CL-CMP-0820; @CL-CMP-0821]. When submitting indirect cost rate proposals, contractors must separately identify total lobbying costs and treat them as unallowable activity costs [@CL-CMP-0313]. Adequate records must be maintained to demonstrate that the certification of costs as being allowable or unallowable complies with the requirements of the subsection [@CL-CMP-0314].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 31 text in place of the codified text [@CL-CMP-9350], effective immediately under Revision 1 signed on 2026-08-04 [@CL-CMP-9351]. The RFO section 31.205-22 keeps the rule that costs associated with the activities listed in paragraph (a) are unallowable, beginning with attempts to influence the outcomes of any Federal, State, or local election, referendum, initiative, or similar procedure [@CL-CMP-8556]. It places attempts to influence legislation in paragraph (a)(3), item (i) for communication with legislators and officials [@CL-CMP-8557; @CL-CMP-8558] and item (ii) for publicity, propaganda and mass campaigns [@CL-CMP-8559]. Its paragraph (b) says costs associated with the listed activities are excepted from paragraph (a) [@CL-CMP-8560], including a technical and factual presentation of information on a topic directly related to the performance of a contract through a hearing testimony, statements, or letters to the Congress or a state legislature, or subdivision, member, or cognizant staff member thereof [@CL-CMP-8561], in response to a documented request [@CL-CMP-8562] made by the recipient member, legislative body, or subdivision, or a cognizant staff member thereof, provided the information is readily obtainable and can be readily put in deliverable form [@CL-CMP-8563]. It keeps the limit that costs under this section for transportation, lodging or meals are unallowable unless incurred for the purpose of offering testimony at a regularly scheduled Congressional hearing [@CL-CMP-8564], pursuant to a written request made by the Chairman or Ranking Minority Member of the Committee or Subcommittee conducting the hearing [@CL-CMP-8565]. Paragraph (b)(2) also excepts any lobbying made unallowable by paragraph (a)(3) to influence state or local legislation in order to directly reduce contract cost, or to avoid material impairment of the contractor's authority to perform the contract [@CL-CMP-8566]. The RFO text keeps the rules that, when a contractor seeks reimbursement for indirect costs, total lobbying costs must be separately identified in the indirect cost rate proposal [@CL-CMP-8567], and that contractors must maintain adequate records to demonstrate that the certification of costs as being allowable or unallowable (see 42.703-2) pursuant to this section complies with the requirements of this section [@CL-CMP-8568].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor incurs 80,000 for government affairs personnel advocating for pending legislative appropriations, and 15,000 for travel and preparation to deliver technical contract progress testimony requested in writing by a congressional committee chairman. The 80,000 in legislative lobbying expense is unallowable and must be separately identified in the indirect rate submission. The 15,000 is allowable under the documented legislative request exception only if the information is readily obtainable and the travel is to offer testimony at a regularly scheduled Congressional hearing under the committee chairman's written request; otherwise the travel part is unallowable.

## Never confuse

Attempts to influence legislation by the means listed in codified paragraph (a) are unallowable lobbying [@CL-CMP-8811; @CL-CMP-8586; @CL-CMP-8587], whereas a technical and factual presentation on a topic directly related to contract performance, given in response to a documented request and meeting the stated provisos, is excepted [@CL-CMP-0312; @CL-CMP-0819; @CL-CMP-0820; @CL-CMP-0821; @CL-CMP-0822]. See [Unallowable-cost penalties](cmp-unallowable-cost-penalty.md) for the consequences of including unallowable costs in indirect cost proposals.

In the RFO text, the paragraph (b)(2) exception refers to paragraph (a)(3), which holds both the item for communication with legislators and officials and the item for publicity and mass campaigns [@CL-CMP-8566; @CL-CMP-8557; @CL-CMP-8559], so a reference to paragraph (a)(3) in the RFO text reaches both. In the codified text, paragraph (a)(3) covers communication with members and employees of the Congress or state legislature [@CL-CMP-8586] and publicity or propaganda sits in paragraph (a)(4) [@CL-CMP-8587], so the codified exception for lobbying made unallowable by paragraph (a)(3) [@CL-CMP-8588] refers to the paragraph on communication and not to paragraph (a)(4).
