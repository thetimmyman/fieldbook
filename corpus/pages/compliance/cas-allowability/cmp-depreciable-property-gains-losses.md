## Purpose

Specifies how gains and losses from the sale, retirement, or impairment of depreciable and capital assets affect contract cost groupings.

## The rule

|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-0510] |
| Codified citation | FAR 31.205-16 [@CL-CMP-0510] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |

Gains and losses from the sale, retirement, or other disposition of depreciable property must be included in the year they occur as credits or charges to the cost groupings where depreciation was included, subject to the exceptions stated in that section [@CL-CMP-0510; @CL-CMP-0829].

Gains and losses arising from the sale or exchange of capital assets other than depreciable property are excluded in computing contract costs [@CL-CMP-0511].

No loss is allowed for an impairment write-down from carrying value to fair value for long-lived tangible or identifiable intangible assets held for use [@CL-CMP-0512].

## How it works

Dispositions of depreciable equipment or facilities adjust previously recognized depreciation costs. The net gain or loss reflects the difference between the net proceeds realized and the undepreciated asset balance. The gain recognized for contract costing purposes is limited to the difference between the acquisition cost of the asset (or the value at which the leased asset is capitalized for capital leases) and its undepreciated balance. No loss is allowed for a write-down from carrying value to fair value caused by impairments, such as environmental damage or idle facilities arising from a declining business base. When an impaired asset is subsequently disposed of, the allowable gain or loss is calculated as if no write-down had occurred.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor sells manufacturing machinery for 40,000 cash when its undepreciated book balance is 30,000, yielding a gain of 10,000. The original acquisition cost of the equipment was 60,000. The full 10,000 gain is credited to the manufacturing overhead pool where depreciation was previously charged, reducing indirect expenses for the year.

## Never confuse

Gains on depreciable property adjust prior depreciation and flow to overhead pools, whereas gains and losses on non-depreciable capital assets like land or securities are entirely excluded from contract costing. In addition, an impairment write-down is not allowed as a loss, and on later disposition the gain or loss is the amount that would have been allowed had the asset not been written down.
