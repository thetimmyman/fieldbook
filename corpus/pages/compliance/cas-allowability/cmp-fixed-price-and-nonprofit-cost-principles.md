## Purpose

Explains when the cost principles reach fixed-price work and which subpart supplies the principles for a contract with a nonprofit organization.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-7500] |
| Codified citation | FAR 31.102 [@CL-CMP-7500] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-7500] |
| Applies to | Fixed-price contracts, subcontracts and modifications where cost analysis is performed or a clause requires costs to be determined or negotiated [@CL-CMP-7500] |

The applicable subparts of FAR part 31 shall be used in the pricing of a fixed-price contract, subcontract or modification to either, whenever cost analysis is performed or a fixed-price contract clause requires the determination or negotiation of costs [@CL-CMP-7500]. The section does not name a deciding official: the duty attaches to the pricing of the action when either trigger is met.

Using the principles that way must not be construed as a requirement to negotiate agreements on individual elements of cost in arriving at agreement on the total price, and the final price the parties accept reflects agreement only on the total price [@CL-CMP-7501; @CL-CMP-7502]. Notwithstanding the mandatory use of the principles, the objective continues to be a price that is fair and reasonable, cost and other factors considered [@CL-CMP-7503].

## How it works

A pricing action on a fixed-price contract, subcontract or modification reaches the cost principles through either trigger: cost analysis is performed, or a fixed-price contract clause requires the determination or negotiation of costs [@CL-CMP-7500]. Either trigger is enough; the section does not require both.

Contracts with nonprofit organizations are priced under the subpart that fits the organization. Subpart 31.7 gives the principles for nonprofit organizations other than educational institutions (covered by subpart 31.3) and State and local governments (covered by subpart 31.6) [@CL-CMP-7504]. Nonprofit organizations exempted under the OMB Uniform Guidance take their cost principles from subpart 31.2 instead [@CL-CMP-7505].

Neither section names a deciding official, an approval route, a threshold or a deadline.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A fixed-price subcontract is priced with cost analysis, so the applicable subparts of FAR part 31 shall be used in pricing it [@CL-CMP-7500]. The parties agree a total price of 800,000 and sign nothing that addresses individual cost elements. That is permitted, because applying the principles is not a requirement to negotiate agreements on individual elements of cost [@CL-CMP-7501]. The signed price reflects agreement only on the total price, not on any single element used in the build-up [@CL-CMP-7502]. The parties still aim for a price that is fair and reasonable, cost and other factors considered [@CL-CMP-7503].

## Never confuse

Applying the cost principles to a fixed-price contract is not the same as negotiating each cost element [@CL-CMP-7501; @CL-CMP-7502]. The mandatory use of the principles does not displace the aim of a fair and reasonable price [@CL-CMP-7503]. Which subpart supplies the principles for a nonprofit contract depends on the type of organization, and an educational institution or a State or local government is not under the general nonprofit subpart [@CL-CMP-7504]. For the other subparts, see [cost principles for commercial organizations](../cas-allowability/cmp-cost-principles-commercial-organizations.md) and [cost principles for State, local and tribal government contracts](../cas-allowability/cmp-cost-principles-state-local-tribal-governments.md).
