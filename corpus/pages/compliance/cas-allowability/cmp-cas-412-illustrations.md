## Purpose

Restates the most consequential worked illustrations in the pension cost standard, with their facts and outcomes, so a reader sees how the limits bite.

In one illustration, a multiemployer defined-benefit plan under which the contractor pays six cents into the fund for each hour worked by the covered employees under a collective bargaining agreement shall be considered to be a defined-contribution pension plan [@CL-CMP-3396]. In another, the assignable cost allocable to cost objectives of the period is $29,000, the sum of $24,000 of benefits actually paid in that period and a $5,000 second annual installment amortizing the prior year's lump sum settlements [@CL-CMP-3397].

In the illustration of Contractor K, where the assignable cost limitation is $1.3 million, the contractor's assignable pension cost for 2017 is limited to $1.3 million [@CL-CMP-3398]. The standard applies that limit when the pension cost equals or exceeds the assignable cost limitation: the amount of pension cost, adjusted pursuant to paragraph (c)(2)(i) of that subsection, shall not exceed the assignable cost limitation [@CL-CMP-3391]. In a second illustration of Contractor K, the pension cost computed for 2017 is $1.5 million, the assignable cost limitation is $1.7 million and the accumulated value of prepayment credits is $0 [@CL-CMP-3431]. Contractor K cannot fund more than $1 million without incurring an excise tax, which the standard does not permit to be a component of pension cost, and that contractor's assignable pension cost for the period is limited to $1 million [@CL-CMP-3399; @CL-CMP-3430]. In a variant with $700,000 of accumulated prepayment credits, the amount of pension cost assigned to the current period shall not exceed $1,700,000, the sum of the $1 million maximum tax-deductible amount and the $700,000 accumulated value of prepayment credits [@CL-CMP-3432].

In the illustration of Contractor M, the contractor computes $1 million of pension cost for a cost accounting period but, pursuant to a waiver granted under the provisions of ERISA, is required to fund only $800,000 [@CL-CMP-3448]. The remaining $200,000 shall be accounted for as an assignable cost deficit and assigned to the next five cost accounting periods in accordance with the terms of the waiver [@CL-CMP-3400]. In the illustration of Contractor M without a waiver, where only $800,000 was funded against $1 million of assigned pension cost for the period, only $800,000 may be allocated to the contractor's intermediate and final cost objectives [@CL-CMP-3401], and the remaining $200,000 of assigned cost, which has not been funded, shall be separately identified and maintained so that it will not be reassigned to any future accounting periods [@CL-CMP-3447].

In the illustration of Contractor P, the provisions limit the assigned cost allocable within the cost accounting period to the percentage of cost funded, $59,800 divided by $65,000 or 92 percent, which results in allocable cost of $92,000, being 92 percent of $100,000 [@CL-CMP-3402]. The facts there are those of the first Contractor P illustration except that the contribution to the Trust is $59,800 [@CL-CMP-3433]. In another illustration, at least 32 percent of these benefit payments shall be made from sources other than the funding agency, and the contractor therefore draws $238,000 from the funding agency assets and pays the remaining $112,000 using general corporate funds [@CL-CMP-3403].

In the pension harmonization illustration at 9904.412-60.1(b)(3)(i), because the contractor separately computes pension costs by segment, or aggregation of segments, the applicability of 9904.412-50(b)(7)(i) is determined separately for Segment 1 and Segments 2 through 7 [@CL-CMP-3439].

## How it works

In the first Contractor K illustration, the assignable cost limitation is $1.3 million and the assignable pension cost for 2017 is limited to $1.3 million [@CL-CMP-3398]. In the second, the assignable pension cost is limited to $1 million because the contractor cannot fund more than $1 million without incurring an excise tax [@CL-CMP-3399; @CL-CMP-3430]. With $700,000 of accumulated prepayment credits, the amount of pension cost assigned to the current period shall not exceed the sum of the $1 million maximum tax-deductible amount and the $700,000 accumulated value of prepayment credits [@CL-CMP-3432; @CL-CMP-3427]. In the Contractor M illustration without a waiver, only $800,000 may be allocated where only $800,000 was funded against $1 million of assigned pension cost [@CL-CMP-3401]; with a waiver, the remaining $200,000 shall be accounted for as an assignable cost deficit and assigned to the next five cost accounting periods in accordance with the terms of the waiver [@CL-CMP-3400].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A qualified plan has an assignable cost limitation of 40, a computed pension cost of 50 and no prepayment credits, so the assigned cost is capped at 40. The maximum tax-deductible amount is 35, so the assigned cost is further capped at 35, and the 15 excess of the computed pension cost over that cap is assigned to future periods as an assignable cost deficit.

Separately, a contractor with a qualified plan funds 90 of 120 assigned cost with no waiver. Only 90 may be allocated to cost objectives, and the remaining 30 is separately identified and not reassigned to any future period.

## Never confuse

In the second Contractor K illustration the assignable cost limitation is $1.7 million, and the assignable pension cost for the period is limited to $1 million [@CL-CMP-3431; @CL-CMP-3399]. With $700,000 of accumulated prepayment credits, the amount of pension cost assigned to the current period shall not exceed $1,700,000 [@CL-CMP-3432]. A waiver changes the outcome: with a waiver, the remaining $200,000 shall be accounted for as an assignable cost deficit and assigned to the next five cost accounting periods in accordance with the terms of the waiver [@CL-CMP-3400]; without one, only $800,000 may be allocated to the contractor's intermediate and final cost objectives [@CL-CMP-3401].
