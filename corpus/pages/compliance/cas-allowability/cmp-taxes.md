## Purpose

Sets out which taxes are allowable as contract costs, which are listed as unallowable, and how property taxes and refunds are handled.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-0780] |
| Codified citation | FAR 31.205-41 [@CL-CMP-0780] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 31.205-41 [@CL-CMP-9636; @CL-CMP-9638; @CL-CMP-9639] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-0780] |
| Applies to | Federal, State and local taxes required to be and paid or accrued in accordance with generally accepted accounting principles [@CL-CMP-0780] |

Federal, State and local taxes are allowable where they are required to be and are paid or accrued in accordance with generally accepted accounting principles, subject to the paragraph that lists unallowable taxes [@CL-CMP-0780]. Fines and penalties are not considered taxes [@CL-CMP-0781].

Federal income and excess profits taxes are unallowable [@CL-CMP-0782], as are taxes in connection with financing, refinancing, refunding operations or reorganizations [@CL-CMP-0783]. Taxes from which exemptions are available to the contractor directly, or based on an exemption afforded the Government, are also among the listed types of costs that are not allowable [@CL-CMP-0784; @CL-CMP-1114]. The exclusion does not apply when the contracting officer determines that the administrative burden incident to obtaining the exemption outweighs the corresponding benefits accruing to the Government [@CL-CMP-0788].

The Environmental Tax, also called the Superfund Tax, is among the listed types of costs that are allowable [@CL-CMP-0785; @CL-CMP-1115]. Taxes on property used solely in connection with either Government or non-Government work should be considered directly applicable to that category of work unless the amounts involved are insignificant or comparable results would otherwise be obtained [@CL-CMP-1116]. Taxes on property used in both Government and non-Government work are apportioned by the use of that property on the respective final cost objectives [@CL-CMP-0787], and taxes, interest or penalties allowed as contract costs that are later refunded must be credited or paid to the Government as it directs [@CL-CMP-0786].

## How it works

The principle starts from allowability for required taxes that are properly accrued, then carves out listed categories of unallowable taxes, some with exceptions [@CL-CMP-0780; @CL-CMP-1114]. Some carve-outs are absolute, such as Federal income and excess profits taxes [@CL-CMP-0782]; others turn on facts, as with an available exemption, where the contracting officer weighs administrative burden against benefit [@CL-CMP-0784; @CL-CMP-0788]. Taxes on property used solely in connection with Government or non-Government work are generally treated as applicable to that category of work unless the amounts are insignificant or comparable results would otherwise be obtained [@CL-CMP-1116], and taxes on property used in both are apportioned by use on the respective final cost objectives [@CL-CMP-0787]. A later refund reverses the charge, and the Government receives it as it directs [@CL-CMP-0786].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 31 text in place of the codified text [@CL-CMP-9350], effective immediately under Revision 1 signed on 2026-08-04 [@CL-CMP-9351]. RFO FAR 31.205-41 moves the allowable list from paragraph (a) to paragraph (b), where Federal, State, and local taxes remain allowable if required to be and paid or accrued in accordance with generally accepted accounting principles [@CL-CMP-9636] and the Superfund Tax is item (b)(4) [@CL-CMP-9638]. The list of costs that are not allowable moves to paragraph (c), starting with Federal income and excess profits taxes and taxes in connection with financing, refinancing, refunding operations, or reorganizations [@CL-CMP-9639]. Its item (c)(3) lists taxes from which exemptions are available among the costs that are not allowable [@CL-CMP-9639; @CL-CMP-9640], with the exception for administrative burden [@CL-CMP-9641]. The rule on property used solely in connection with non-Government or Government work moves to paragraph (d) [@CL-CMP-9643], where apportionment for property used in both is item (d)(3) [@CL-CMP-9644], and the refund rule moves to paragraph (e), which says refunded amounts must be credited or paid to the Government as directed by the contracting officer [@CL-CMP-9645]. The RFO text moves the definition of exemption into its own paragraph (a) [@CL-CMP-9646].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor pays 80,000 of State property tax on a building used equally on Government and non-Government work, plus 30,000 of Federal income tax and 5,000 of Superfund Tax. The property tax is apportioned by use, so 40,000 follows each category of work; the income tax is unallowable in full; the Superfund Tax is allowable.

## Never confuse

A fine or penalty is not a tax, so the tax allowability rule does not make it chargeable [@CL-CMP-0781]. An available exemption is not a saving the contractor keeps: it reduces what may be charged unless the contracting officer finds the administrative burden outweighs the benefit [@CL-CMP-0784; @CL-CMP-0788]. A refunded tax that was allowed as a contract cost is not retained, because it is credited or paid to the Government [@CL-CMP-0786]. Unallowable taxes sit with the other unallowable costs; see [Unallowable costs under CAS](cmp-cas-405-unallowable-costs.md) [@CL-CMP-0782]. The codified text makes the allowable taxes subject to the paragraph that lists unallowable taxes [@CL-CMP-0780], but RFO item (b)(1) prints no words making it subject to another paragraph [@CL-CMP-9636]. The codified text says fines and penalties are not considered taxes [@CL-CMP-0781], but RFO paragraph (b)(1) prints no such sentence [@CL-CMP-9637]. The codified text ends its partial-exemption sentence with the words are unallowable [@CL-CMP-0784], but the RFO sentence as printed ends without them [@CL-CMP-9642].
