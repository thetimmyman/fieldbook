## Purpose

Sets out the allowable and unallowable tax costs, and the provisions on property taxes and refunds.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-0780] |
| Codified citation | FAR 31.205-41 [@CL-CMP-0780] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 31.205-41 [@CL-CMP-9636; @CL-CMP-9637; @CL-CMP-9638; @CL-CMP-9639; @CL-CMP-9642] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-0780] |
| Applies to | Federal, State and local taxes required to be and paid or accrued in accordance with generally accepted accounting principles [@CL-CMP-0780] |

Under FAR 31.205-41(a), the following types of costs are allowable [@CL-CMP-1115], including (1) Federal, State, and local taxes (see part 29), except as otherwise provided in paragraph (b) below, that are required to be and are paid or accrued in accordance with generally accepted accounting principles [@CL-CMP-0780]. Fines and penalties are not considered taxes [@CL-CMP-0781]. The Environmental Tax found at section 59A of the Internal Revenue Code, also called the Superfund Tax, is among the allowable types of costs [@CL-CMP-0785].

Under paragraph (b), the following types of costs are not allowable [@CL-CMP-1114]: (1) Federal income and excess profits taxes [@CL-CMP-0782], (2) taxes in connection with financing, refinancing, refunding operations, or reorganizations (see 31.205-20 and 31.205-27) [@CL-CMP-0783], and (3) taxes from which exemptions are available to the contractor directly, or available to the contractor based on an exemption afforded the Government [@CL-CMP-0784], except when the contracting officer determines that the administrative burden incident to obtaining the exemption outweighs the corresponding benefits accruing to the Government [@CL-CMP-0788].

Under paragraph (c), taxes on property used solely in connection with either non-Government or Government work should be considered directly applicable to the respective category of work unless the amounts involved are insignificant or comparable results would otherwise be obtained [@CL-CMP-1116]. The cost of taxes incurred on property used in both Government and non-Government work shall be apportioned to all such work based upon the use of such property on the respective final cost objectives [@CL-CMP-0787]. Under paragraph (d), any taxes, interest, or penalties that were allowed as contract costs and are refunded to the contractor shall be credited or paid to the Government in the manner it directs [@CL-CMP-0786].

## How it works

Paragraph (a) lists types of costs that are allowable [@CL-CMP-1115], and paragraph (b) lists types of costs that are not allowable [@CL-CMP-1114]. Under paragraph (b)(3), the exception for taxes from which exemptions are available applies when the contracting officer determines that the administrative burden incident to obtaining the exemption outweighs the corresponding benefits accruing to the Government [@CL-CMP-0788]. Taxes, interest, or penalties that were allowed as contract costs and are refunded to the contractor shall be credited or paid to the Government in the manner it directs [@CL-CMP-0786].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers must use the RFO FAR part 31 text in place of the codified text [@CL-CMP-9350], effective immediately under Revision 1 signed on 2026-08-04 [@CL-CMP-9351]. Under RFO FAR 31.205-41(a), exemption, as used in this section, means freedom from taxation, in whole or in part, and includes a tax abatement or reduction resulting from mode of assessment, method of calculation, or otherwise [@CL-CMP-9646]. Under paragraph (b), the following types of costs are allowable, including (1) Federal, State, and local taxes (see part 29) that are required to be and are paid or accrued in accordance with generally accepted accounting principles [@CL-CMP-9636], and (4) the Environmental Tax, also known as the Superfund Tax, (section 59A of the Internal Revenue Code) [@CL-CMP-9638]. Under paragraph (c), the following types of costs are not allowable: (1) Federal income and excess profits taxes and (2) taxes in connection with financing, refinancing, refunding operations, or reorganizations (see 31.205-20 and 31.205-27) [@CL-CMP-9639]; and (3) taxes from which exemptions are available to the contractor directly, or available to the contractor based on an exemption afforded the Government [@CL-CMP-9640], except when the contracting officer determines that the administrative burden incident to obtaining the exemption outweighs the corresponding benefits accruing to the Government [@CL-CMP-9641]. Under paragraph (d), taxes on property used solely in connection with non-Government or Government work should be considered directly applicable to the respective category of work unless the amounts involved are insignificant or comparable results would otherwise be obtained [@CL-CMP-9643], and under paragraph (d)(3), the cost of taxes incurred on property used in both Government and non-Government work must be apportioned to all such work based upon the use of such property on the respective final cost objectives [@CL-CMP-9644]. Under paragraph (e), any taxes, interest, or penalties that were allowed as contract costs and are refunded to the contractor must be credited or paid to the Government as directed by the contracting officer [@CL-CMP-9645].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor pays 80,000 of State property tax on a building used equally on Government and non-Government work, plus 30,000 of Federal income tax and 5,000 of Superfund Tax. The property tax is apportioned by use, so 40,000 follows each category of work; the income tax is unallowable in full; the Superfund Tax is allowable.

## Never confuse

Under the codified text, fines and penalties are not considered taxes [@CL-CMP-0781], and the allowable Federal, State, and local taxes are listed with the words except as otherwise provided in paragraph (b) below [@CL-CMP-0780]. Under the RFO text, the allowable Federal, State, and local taxes are listed at paragraph (b)(1) as taxes that are required to be and are paid or accrued in accordance with generally accepted accounting principles [@CL-CMP-9636]. The codified refund rule says refunded amounts shall be credited or paid to the Government in the manner it directs [@CL-CMP-0786], and the RFO refund rule says they must be credited or paid to the Government as directed by the contracting officer [@CL-CMP-9645]. Federal income and excess profits taxes are among the listed unallowable costs [@CL-CMP-0782]; for unallowable costs generally, see [Unallowable costs under CAS](cmp-cas-405-unallowable-costs.md).

## Company calibration

Whether the company identifies exempt, income and non-Government property taxes and keeps them out of its billing pools [@Q-044].
