## Purpose

Cost Accounting Standard 417 establishes criteria for the measurement of the cost of money attributable to capital assets under construction, fabrication or development as an element of the cost of those assets. This concept hub sets out what the standard requires and links the detailed reference, compliance and estimating pages across the corpus.

## The rule

The purpose of the Standard is to establish criteria for the measurement of the cost of money attributable to capital assets under construction, fabrication or development as an element of the cost of those assets [@CL-EST-2897], and consistent application of those criteria will improve cost measurement by providing for recognition of the cost of contractor investment in assets under construction and will provide greater uniformity in accounting for asset acquisition costs [@CL-EST-2973]. Under the fundamental requirement, the cost of money applicable to the investment in tangible and intangible capital assets being constructed, fabricated or developed for a contractor's own use shall be included in the capitalized acquisition cost of such assets [@CL-CMP-1792]. An intangible capital asset has no physical substance, has more than minimal value, and includes right-of-use assets acquired under leases [@CL-EST-2898], and a tangible capital asset has physical substance, more than minimal value, and is expected to be held for continued use or possession beyond the current accounting period for the services it yields [@CL-EST-2899].

The exemption section of CAS 417 says there is no exemption for that Standard [@CL-CMP-8663]. The Standard is effective as of April 17, 1992 [@CL-EST-2900]. Contractors with prior CAS-covered contracts with full coverage continue the Standard's applicability upon receipt of a contract to which it is applicable [@CL-EST-2900], and for contractors with no previous contracts subject to it, it is applied beginning with the contractor's next full fiscal year beginning after that receipt [@CL-EST-2901].

## How it works

The cost of money rate used shall be based on interest rates determined by the Secretary of the Treasury pursuant to Public Law 92-41 [@CL-CMP-1793]. A representative investment amount is determined each cost accounting period for each capital asset being constructed, fabricated or developed, giving appropriate consideration to the rate at which costs of construction are incurred [@CL-CMP-1794]. Other methods for calculating the cost of money to be capitalized, such as the method used for financial accounting and reporting, may be used provided the resulting amount does not differ materially from the amount the prescribed rate and investment amount paragraphs give [@CL-CMP-1795].

Capitalization stops when the asset leaves capital in progress: if substantially all the activities necessary to get the asset ready for its intended use are discontinued, cost of money shall not be capitalized for the period of discontinuance [@CL-CMP-1796]. Cessation is not required where the discontinuance arises out of causes beyond the control and without the fault or negligence of the contractor [@CL-CMP-1797].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor builds a production line for its own use over two full-year cost accounting periods and spends 200,000 in the first and 600,000 in the second, evenly through each period. The representative investment amount is 100,000 for the first period. At a Treasury-based annual rate of 4 percent, the first period's cost of money is 4,000. The second period opens with 204,000, including that capitalized cost, and closes with 804,000 before the second period's cost of money. Its representative investment is 504,000, giving cost of money of 20,160. The line's capitalized acquisition cost is therefore 824,160. If substantially all the activities necessary to get the line ready for its intended use then stop for a period for a reason within the contractor's control, no cost of money is capitalized for that period. This example is illustrative only, and the contract itself governs.

## Never confuse

CAS 417 capitalizes the cost of money attributable to an asset the contractor is building for its own use into that asset's acquisition cost [@CL-EST-2897; @CL-CMP-1792], whereas CAS 414 measures facilities capital and allocates the facilities capital cost of money computed on it to contracts during each cost accounting period [@CL-CMP-8690; @CL-CMP-0567]. The start rules also differ: a contractor with no previous contracts subject to CAS 417 applies it from its next full fiscal year beginning after receipt of an applicable contract [@CL-EST-2901], whereas such a contractor applies CAS 418 from its second full fiscal year beginning after that receipt [@CL-EST-2905].
