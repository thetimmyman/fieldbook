## Purpose

Identifies bad debts, uncollectible accounts receivable losses, and directly associated collection and legal expenses as unallowable on government contracts.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-0304] |
| Codified citation | FAR 31.205-3 [@CL-CMP-0304] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 31.205-3 [@CL-CMP-0304] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-0304] |
| Applies to | Government contracts subject to the cost principles [@CL-CMP-0304] |

Bad debts, including actual or estimated losses arising from uncollectible accounts receivable due from customers and other claims, are unallowable [@CL-CMP-0304]. Any directly associated costs, such as collection costs and legal costs, are also unallowable [@CL-CMP-0305].

## How it works

The cost principle makes bad debts and their directly associated costs unallowable [@CL-CMP-0304]. The prohibition applies equally to actual write-offs of worthless receivables and additions to allowance reserves for estimated credit losses [@CL-CMP-0304]. Directly associated expenditures incurred in pursuing recovery, including fees paid to collection agencies and retained legal counsel, are tainted and disallowed along with the underlying bad debt [@CL-CMP-0305]. Bad debts and their directly associated costs are unallowable [@CL-CMP-0304].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A commercial customer defaults on payment, resulting in an uncollectible accounts receivable balance of 50,000 that the contractor writes off as bad debt expense. The contractor also incurs 8,000 in attorney fees attempting to collect the unpaid balance through litigation. The entire 50,000 receivable loss is unallowable, and the 8,000 in directly associated legal expenses is also unallowable. Neither amount may be allocated to government overhead pools.

## Never confuse

Bad debts include actual or estimated losses arising from uncollectible accounts receivable due from customers and other claims [@CL-CMP-0304]. Even when legal costs are otherwise ordinary business expenses, fees incurred specifically for debt collection are tainted and unallowable [@CL-CMP-0305]. See [Unallowable-cost penalties](cmp-unallowable-cost-penalty.md) for the consequences of including unallowable costs in indirect cost proposals.
