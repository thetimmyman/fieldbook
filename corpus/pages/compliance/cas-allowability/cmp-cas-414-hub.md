## Purpose

Cost Accounting Standard 414 establishes criteria for the measurement and allocation of the cost of capital committed to facilities as an element of contract cost. This concept hub sets out what the standard requires and links the detailed reference and measurement pages across the corpus.

## The rule

The purpose of CAS 414 is to establish criteria for the measurement and allocation of the cost of capital committed to facilities as an element of contract cost [@CL-CMP-8690]. Consistent application of these criteria improves cost measurement by providing for allocation of the cost of contractor investment in facilities capital to negotiated contracts [@CL-CMP-8691]. Under the fundamental requirement, facilities capital is measured and allocated under the standard, and the allocated amount is the base to which a cost of money rate is applied [@CL-CMP-0567]. The cost of money rate is based on rates determined by the Secretary of the Treasury under the public law named in the standard [@CL-CMP-0568]. The cost of capital committed to facilities is computed separately for each contract using facilities capital cost of money factors computed for each cost accounting period [@CL-CMP-0569]. The investment base used in computing the cost of money for facilities capital must be computed from accounting data used for contract cost purposes, using the form and instructions stipulated in the standard [@CL-IND-0035].

The standard became effective on April 17, 1992 [@CL-CMP-3424]. For a contractor that was not subject to full coverage as of the date the part was published as a final rule, the standard applies only to fully-covered contracts with subsequent dates of award and pricing certification [@CL-EST-2921]. The standard does not apply where compensation for the use of tangible capital assets is based on use rates or allowances provided for by other appropriate Federal procurement regulations [@CL-EST-2922]. Examples of such regulations include those governing educational institutions, State, local and federally recognized Indian tribal governments, and construction equipment rates under 48 CFR 31.105(d) [@CL-EST-2923].

## How it works

Cost of capital committed to facilities is an imputed cost determined by applying a cost of money rate to facilities capital [@CL-IND-0031]. Facilities capital is the net book value of tangible capital assets and of those intangible capital assets that are subject to amortization [@CL-IND-0032]. A tangible capital asset has physical substance, more than minimal value, and is expected to be held by an enterprise for continued use or possession beyond the current accounting period for the services it yields [@CL-IND-0034]. An intangible capital asset has no physical substance, more than minimal value, and is expected to be held for continued use or possession beyond the current accounting period for the benefits it yields, including right-of-use assets acquired under leases [@CL-IND-0033]. A business unit is any segment of an organization, or an entire business organization which is not divided into segments [@CL-IND-0030].

The cost of money rate for any cost accounting period is the arithmetic mean of the interest rates specified by the Secretary of the Treasury under the public law named in the standard, or the most recent available rate when determined prospectively [@CL-IND-0036; @CL-IND-0037]. A facilities capital cost of money factor is determined for each indirect cost pool to which a significant amount of facilities capital has been allocated and which is used to allocate indirect costs to final cost objectives [@CL-IND-0038]. In accordance with Form CASB CMF, each factor is the quotient of the cost of money for the cost accounting period divided by the corresponding overhead or G&A expense allocation base, carried to five decimal places, and one form serves for all indirect cost pools of a business unit [@CL-CMP-3446; @CL-IND-0039; @CL-CMP-8660]. For each CAS-covered contract, the cost of capital committed to facilities is the sum of products obtained by multiplying the contract's allocation base units, such as direct labor hours or dollars of total cost input, by the factor for the corresponding indirect cost pool, or an agreed statistical measure in process cost systems [@CL-IND-0040; @CL-IND-0041; @CL-IND-0042].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A business unit records facilities capital of 10,000,000, consisting of 9,000,000 net book value of tangible capital assets and 1,000,000 of amortizable intangible assets. With an applicable Treasury cost of money rate of 4.0 percent, total cost of money for the period is 400,000. Facilities capital is allocated between two indirect cost pools: an engineering overhead pool receives 6,000,000 of capital (yielding 240,000 of cost of money) with an allocation base of 2,000,000 direct labor dollars, producing a factor of 0.12000; and a G&A pool receives 4,000,000 of capital (yielding 160,000 of cost of money) with a total cost input base of 16,000,000 dollars, producing a factor of 0.01000. A CAS-covered contract incurs 100,000 of engineering direct labor dollars and 500,000 of total cost input during the period. The contract is allocated 12,000 from the engineering pool (100,000 multiplied by 0.12000) and 5,000 from the G&A pool (500,000 multiplied by 0.01000), resulting in an imputed facilities capital cost of money of 17,000 for that contract. This example is illustrative only, and the contract itself governs.

## Never confuse

CAS 414 establishes criteria for measuring and allocating the cost of capital committed to facilities as an element of contract cost allocated to contracts during each cost accounting period [@CL-CMP-8690; @CL-CMP-0569], whereas CAS 417 establishes criteria for measuring the cost of money attributable to capital assets under construction, fabrication or development for a contractor's own use [@CL-EST-2897]. Under CAS 417, that cost of money is included in the capitalized acquisition cost of the assets rather than charged directly to contracts for the period [@CL-CMP-1792]. Furthermore, while CAS 414 measures and allocates facilities capital [@CL-CMP-0567], FAR 31.205-10 separately governs allowability and requires the estimated facilities capital cost of money to be specifically identified and proposed in cost proposals [@CL-CMP-0761], while actual interest in lieu of imputed cost of money is unallowable [@CL-CMP-0762].
