## Purpose

Shows how contingency costs are treated for historical costing and in estimates of future costs.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-0751] |
| Codified citation | FAR 31.205-7 [@CL-CMP-0751] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 31.205-7 [@CL-CMP-9378] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-0751] |
| Applies to | Contingencies, as the term is used in the cost principles subpart [@CL-CMP-0751] |

A contingency is a possible future event or condition, from known or unknown causes, whose outcome is indeterminable at the present time [@CL-CMP-0751]. For historical costing, contingency costs are generally unallowable, because that costing deals with costs already incurred and recorded on the contractor's books [@CL-CMP-0752].

However, in some cases, as for example, terminations, a contingency factor may be recognized when it is applicable to a past period, to give recognition to minor unsettled factors in the interest of expediting settlement [@CL-CMP-0753]. In connection with estimates of future costs, contingencies fall into two categories, the first being contingencies that may arise from presently known and existing conditions, the effects of which are foreseeable within reasonable limits of accuracy [@CL-CMP-1110]; they are to be included in the estimates of future costs so as to provide the best estimate of performance cost [@CL-CMP-0754]. The second category is contingencies that may arise from presently known or unknown conditions, the effect of which cannot be measured so precisely as to provide equitable results [@CL-CMP-1111]; they are to be excluded from cost estimates under the several items of cost but should be disclosed separately, including the basis upon which the contingency is computed [@CL-CMP-0755].

## How it works

Costs for contingencies are generally unallowable for historical costing purposes because such costing deals with costs incurred and recorded on the contractor's books [@CL-CMP-0752]. However, in some cases, a contingency factor may be recognized when it is applicable to a past period to give recognition to minor unsettled factors in the interest of expediting settlement [@CL-CMP-0753]. In connection with estimates of future costs, contingencies fall into two categories [@CL-CMP-1110; @CL-CMP-1111]. Contingencies of the first category are to be included in the estimate [@CL-CMP-0754], and those of the second category are to be excluded from cost estimates under the several items of cost but should be disclosed separately, with the basis on which each is computed, to facilitate the negotiation of appropriate contractual coverage [@CL-CMP-0755].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers must use the RFO FAR part 31 text in place of the codified text [@CL-CMP-9350], effective immediately under Revision 1 signed on 2026-08-04 [@CL-CMP-9351]. RFO FAR 31.205-7 keeps the definition of a contingency [@CL-CMP-9378] and the rule that contingency costs are generally unallowable for historical costing purposes [@CL-CMP-9379]. It keeps the two categories of contingencies in estimates of future costs [@CL-CMP-9381; @CL-CMP-9383], with the first category included in the estimates [@CL-CMP-9382] and the second excluded from the estimates under the several items of cost but disclosed separately with the basis of computation [@CL-CMP-9384]. RFO FAR 31.205-7(b)(2) says a contingency factor may be recognized when it is applicable to a past period to give recognition to minor unsettled factors in the interest of expediting settlement (e.g., terminations) [@CL-CMP-9380], where the codified text begins "However, in some cases" [@CL-CMP-0753].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A proposal carries a 250,000 estimate for a production line. Anticipated rework of 15,000 is foreseeable, so it is built into the line. A pending dispute is estimated at 100,000, but its effect cannot be measured precisely, so that amount is left out of the line and disclosed separately with its basis.

## Never confuse

A contingency is a possible future event or condition arising from presently known or unknown causes, the outcome of which is indeterminable at the present time [@CL-CMP-0751]. The two contingency categories get different treatment: in estimates of future costs, contingencies of the first category are to be included and those of the second category are to be excluded but disclosed separately [@CL-CMP-0754; @CL-CMP-0755]. Results of pending litigation are given as an example of the second category [@CL-CMP-1111]; see [Costs of legal proceedings](cmp-legal-proceedings-costs.md).

## Company calibration

Whether the company keeps foreseeable contingencies inside its estimates and discloses the unmeasurable ones, with their basis, before negotiation [@Q-043].
