## Purpose

Shows how unallowable costs and their directly associated costs are identified and kept out of billings, claims and proposals, how materiality and statistical sampling are handled, and where the cost accounting standard and advance agreements fit.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-2724] |
| Codified citation | FAR 31.201-6 [@CL-CMP-2724] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 31.201-6 [@CL-CMP-9352; @CL-CMP-9353] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-2724] |
| Applies to | Expressly unallowable, mutually agreed unallowable and officer-designated costs in billings, claims and proposals applicable to a Government contract [@CL-CMP-2724; @CL-CMP-2726; @CL-CMP-7778] |

Costs that are expressly unallowable or mutually agreed to be unallowable, including mutually agreed to be unallowable directly associated costs, shall be identified and excluded from any billing, claim, or proposal applicable to a Government contract [@CL-CMP-2724]. Costs that specifically become designated as unallowable, or as unallowable directly associated costs of unallowable costs, as a result of a written decision furnished by a contracting officer shall be identified if included in or used in computing any billing, claim, or proposal applicable to a Government contract [@CL-CMP-2726; @CL-CMP-7778]. The identification requirement for costs designated by a contracting officer's written decision applies also to any costs incurred for the same purpose under like circumstances as the costs specifically identified as unallowable under the first or the second paragraph of the section [@CL-CMP-2756].

When an unallowable cost is incurred, its directly associated costs are also unallowable [@CL-CMP-2725], and the section also sets materiality rules for directly associated costs, covered in the next paragraph and under How it works. For a selected item of cost under FAR 31.205 that provides that directly associated costs be unallowable, they are unallowable only if determined to be material in amount under the section's criteria [@CL-CMP-2731], and the same sentence carries an exception where allowance would be considered contrary to public policy [@CL-CMP-2732].

## How it works

The practices for accounting for and presentation of unallowable costs must be those described in 48 CFR 9904.405 [@CL-CMP-2727]; see [Accounting for unallowable costs under the standard](../cas-allowability/cmp-cas-405-unallowable-costs.md). If a directly associated cost is in a cost pool that is allocated over a base that includes the unallowable cost it is associated with, the directly associated cost remains in the pool [@CL-CMP-2729]. In all other cases, the directly associated costs, if material in amount, must be purged from the cost pool as unallowable costs [@CL-CMP-2730].

### Materiality of directly associated costs

In determining the materiality of a directly associated cost, consideration should be given to the significance of the actual dollar amount, the cumulative effect of all directly associated costs in a cost pool, and the ultimate effect on the cost of Government contracts [@CL-CMP-2757]. Salary expenses of employees who participate in activities that generate unallowable costs shall be treated as directly associated costs to the extent of the time spent on the proscribed activity [@CL-CMP-2758], provided the costs are material in accordance with the materiality factors, except when the salary expenses are themselves unallowable [@CL-CMP-2759]. The time spent in proscribed activities should be compared to total time spent on company activities to determine if the salary-expense costs are material [@CL-CMP-2760]. In that comparison, time spent by employees outside the normal working hours should not be considered except when it is evident that an employee engages so frequently in company activities during periods outside normal working hours as to indicate that such activities are a part of the employee's regular duties [@CL-CMP-2761].

### Statistical sampling

Statistical sampling is an acceptable practice for contractors in accounting for and presenting unallowable costs, provided the three listed criteria are met [@CL-CMP-2752]. The first is that the sampling results in an unbiased sample that is a reasonable representation of the sampling universe [@CL-CMP-2753]. The other two are that any large dollar value or high risk transaction is separately reviewed for unallowable costs and excluded from the sampling process, and that the sampling permits audit verification [@CL-CMP-2754]. For any indirect cost in the selected sample that is subject to the penalty provisions at FAR 42.709, the amount projected to the sampling universe from that sampled cost is also subject to the same penalty provisions [@CL-CMP-2755]. Use of statistical sampling methods for identifying and segregating unallowable costs should be the subject of an advance agreement with the cognizant administrative contracting officer or Federal official [@CL-CMP-2728]; see [Advance agreements on particular costs](../cas-allowability/cmp-advance-agreements.md). In the absence of an advance agreement, if an initial review of the facts results in a challenge of the statistical sampling methods by the contracting officer or the contracting officer's representative, the burden of proof is on the contractor [@CL-CMP-2750]. That burden is to establish that the method meets the sampling criteria [@CL-CMP-2751].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 31 text in place of the codified text [@CL-CMP-9350], effective immediately under Revision 1 signed on 2026-08-04 [@CL-CMP-9351]. RFO FAR 31.201-6 keeps the rule that expressly unallowable costs and costs mutually agreed to be unallowable must be identified and excluded from any billing, claim, or proposal applicable to a Government contract [@CL-CMP-9352], and the rule that costs designated unallowable by a contracting officer's written decision must be identified if included in or used in computing any billing, claim, or proposal [@CL-CMP-9353]. It keeps the statistical sampling criteria [@CL-CMP-9354; @CL-CMP-9355], the contractor's burden of proof after a challenge to the sampling methods [@CL-CMP-9358; @CL-CMP-9359] and the cost pool rules for directly associated costs [@CL-CMP-9360; @CL-CMP-9361]. It requires the cognizant administrative contracting officer or Federal official to request input from the cognizant auditor before entering into an advance agreement on sampling [@CL-CMP-9357]. The RFO sentence on advance agreements for statistical sampling cites 31.110 and does not name the parties to the agreement [@CL-CMP-9356]; the codified sentence cites 31.109 and names the contractor and the cognizant administrative contracting officer or Federal official as the parties [@CL-CMP-2728].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A cost pool of 100,000 is allocated over a base that includes 5,000 of an expressly unallowable cost. A directly associated cost of 400 sits in the pool. Because the base includes the unallowable cost the 400 is associated with, the 400 stays in the pool and the unallowable cost attracts its share. The answer: no separate purge of the 400 is needed.

In a second pool, a directly associated cost of 300 sits in a pool whose allocation base does not include the unallowable cost it is associated with. The answer: if the 300 is material in amount, judged by its dollar amount, its cumulative effect with the pool's other directly associated costs and its effect on the cost of Government contracts, it must be purged from the pool. If it is not material, the rule printed in the section does not require the purge.

A contractor uses a statistical sample to find unallowable costs, with no advance agreement. A contracting officer challenges the method after an initial review. The answer: the contractor must establish that the method meets the sampling criteria.

## Never confuse

Expressly unallowable and mutually agreed costs must be identified and excluded [@CL-CMP-2724], while costs designated by a contracting officer's written decision must be identified if included in or used in computing a billing, claim or proposal applicable to a Government contract [@CL-CMP-2726; @CL-CMP-7778]. For the penalties that apply to unallowable costs, see [Unallowable-cost penalties](../cas-allowability/cmp-unallowable-cost-penalty.md). For the terms directly associated cost and expressly unallowable cost, see [Definitions used in the FAR cost principles](../cas-allowability/cmp-far-cost-principles-definitions.md). For construction equipment, actual cost data shall be used when it can be determined for both ownership and operating costs for each piece of equipment, or groups of similar serial or series equipment, from the contractor's accounting records [@CL-CMP-2718]. When such costs cannot be so determined, the contracting agency may specify a particular schedule of predetermined rates or any part of one to determine ownership and operating costs of construction equipment [@CL-CMP-2719], but costs otherwise unallowable under FAR part 31 do not become allowable through the use of any schedule [@CL-CMP-2720]; see [Cost principles for construction and architect-engineer contracts](../cas-allowability/cmp-cost-principles-construction-architect-engineer.md).
