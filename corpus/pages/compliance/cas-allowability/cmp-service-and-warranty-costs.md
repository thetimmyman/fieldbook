## Purpose

Explains the allowability of contractor costs incurred to fulfill contractual service and warranty obligations without duplicating cost allowances.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-0810] |
| Codified citation | FAR 31.205-39 [@CL-CMP-0810] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |

Service and warranty costs include those arising from fulfillment of any contractual obligation of a contractor to provide services such as installation, training, correcting defects in products, replacing defective parts, and making refunds for inadequate performance [@CL-CMP-0810]. When not inconsistent with the terms of the contract, service and warranty costs are allowable, but care should be exercised to avoid duplicating the allowance as an element of both estimated product cost and risk [@CL-CMP-0811].

## How it works

The cost principle addresses costs incurred to satisfy contractual commitments to support delivered items, rectify defects, provide user training, or issue refunds [@CL-CMP-0810]. When not inconsistent with the terms of the contract, these costs are allowable [@CL-CMP-0811]. Care should be exercised to avoid duplicating the allowance as an element of both estimated product cost and risk [@CL-CMP-0811].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor delivers specialized equipment under a contract that obligates the contractor to correct manufacturing defects and provide on-site technical support during initial deployment. The contractor incurs 45,000 in labor and parts to correct defective wiring discovered after installation. Because the work directly fulfills a contractual warranty obligation and was not separately recovered through an explicit warranty pricing contingency, the 45,000 is allowable under the contract.

## Never confuse

Service and warranty costs arise from fulfilling a contractual obligation to provide services such as installation, training, correcting defects, replacing defective parts, and making refunds in the case of inadequate performance [@CL-CMP-0810]. They are allowable when not inconsistent with the terms of the contract, and care should be exercised to avoid duplicating the allowance [@CL-CMP-0811]; see [Allowability factors](cmp-allowability-factors.md) and [Reasonableness](cmp-reasonableness.md).
