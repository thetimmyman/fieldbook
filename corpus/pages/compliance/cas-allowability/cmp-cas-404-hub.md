## Purpose

Cost Accounting Standard 404 establishes criteria for the capitalization of tangible assets. This concept hub sets out the requirements and current status of the standard and links the detailed compliance, clause, and valuation pages across the corpus.

## The rule

The Cost Accounting Standards Board's final rule on CAS 404, 408, 409 and 411 wholly rescinds CAS 408 and 411 and rescinds most provisions of CAS 404 and 409 [@CL-CMP-1500]. The final rule on CAS 404, 408, 409 and 411 has an effective date of August 7, 2026 [@CL-CMP-1501]. The rule removes and reserves subpart 9904.404, which held the sections of CAS 404 [@CL-CMP-1505]. The printed codified text of 48 CFR part 9904 shows section 9904.404 as reserved [@CL-CMP-1522].

The Board does not expect the CAS 404 rescission to justify treating a contractor's change in capitalization of the acquisition costs of tangible assets as a required change under 9903.201-6(a) [@CL-CMP-1509]. The Board states that no exemption from the adjustment requirements is warranted for the CAS 404 rescission [@CL-CMP-1512].

## How it works

Not established. The rule removes and reserves subpart 9904.404, which held the sections of CAS 404 [@CL-CMP-1505], and the printed codified text shows section 9904.404 as reserved [@CL-CMP-1522]. Under the final rule, most provisions of CAS 404 are rescinded [@CL-CMP-1500] with an effective date of August 7, 2026 [@CL-CMP-1501].

The Board does not expect the rescission to justify treating a contractor's change in the capitalization of tangible asset acquisition costs as a required change under 9903.201-6(a) [@CL-CMP-1509], and it found no exemption from the adjustment requirements warranted [@CL-CMP-1512].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor's disclosed practice capitalizes any tangible asset costing 5,000 or more. After the rescission of CAS 404, it considers raising the threshold to 25,000 to match its commercial accounting policy, which would expense assets costing between 5,000 and 25,000 and move 40,000 of cost into the current period's indirect pool. The contractor does not assume that the rescission itself makes the change a required change under 9903.201-6(a), because the Board does not expect it to, and it does not assume the rescission exempts the change from the adjustment requirements, because the Board found no exemption warranted. The contractor therefore treats the change like any other change in capitalization practice and measures its cost impact before it adopts the new threshold. This example is illustrative only, and the contract itself governs.

## Never confuse

Although the Board's final rule rescinded most provisions of both CAS 404 and CAS 409 and removed and reserved subparts 9904.404 and 9904.409 [@CL-CMP-1500; @CL-CMP-1505; @CL-CMP-1506], for CAS 409 the Board retained the specific requirements of CAS 409-50(e)(5), 409-50(j)(1) and 409-50(j)(4) [@CL-CMP-1503]. Furthermore, contractor changes related to depreciation costs under the CAS 409 rescission are evaluated under 9903.201-6(b)(2) [@CL-CMP-1510], whereas changes in capitalization of acquisition costs under the CAS 404 rescission are evaluated under 9903.201-6(a) [@CL-CMP-1509]. While subpart 9904.404 is shown as reserved in codified text [@CL-CMP-1522], related capital asset standards such as CAS 414 for facilities capital cost of money remain active and require facilities capital to be measured and allocated to contracts [@CL-CMP-0567; @CL-CMP-0758; @CL-CMP-0759].
