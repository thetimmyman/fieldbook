## Purpose

Explains the foreign-concern version of the disclosure and consistency clause.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-1601] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.230-4 [@CL-CMP-1601] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.230-4 [@CL-CMP-0276] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-1601] |
| Applies to | Contracts that include the foreign-concerns clause [@CL-CMP-1601] |

The contractor must comply with the standards on consistency in estimating, accumulating, and reporting costs and on consistency in allocating costs incurred for the same purpose [@CL-CMP-0967]. Both apply as in effect on the date of award of the contract [@CL-CMP-0968]. A business unit of a company required to submit a Disclosure Statement must disclose its cost accounting practices in writing, as required by 48 CFR 9903.202-1 through 9903.202-5, on contracts covered by the cost accounting standards only [@CL-CMP-0969].

## How it works

The contractor shall follow consistently its cost accounting practices, and a change to such practices may be proposed, however, by either the U.S. Government or the contractor, and the contractor agrees to negotiate with the contracting officer the terms and conditions under which a change may be made [@CL-CMP-0970]. After those terms and conditions are agreed, the change must be applied prospectively and the Disclosure Statement, if affected, must be amended [@CL-CMP-0976]. When the parties agree to a change and the contracting officer has made the finding required in 48 CFR 9903.201-6(c) that the change is desirable and not detrimental to the interests of the United States Government, the contractor shall negotiate an equitable adjustment [@CL-CMP-0971]. The contractor agrees to an adjustment of the contract price or cost allowance if the contractor or a subcontractor fails to comply with the applicable CAS or to follow any cost accounting practice and the failure results in increased costs paid by the U.S. Government [@CL-CMP-1605]. That adjustment provides for recovery of the increased costs with interest, running from the time the payment by the U.S. Government was made to the time the adjustment is effected [@CL-CMP-0972; @CL-CMP-8046]. A failure to agree whether the contractor has complied with an applicable CAS rule or regulation, and as to any cost adjustment the U.S. Government demands, constitutes a dispute [@CL-CMP-0973]. The contractor must permit authorized representatives of the U.S. Government to examine and copy documents, papers and records relating to compliance with the clause [@CL-CMP-0975]. Under paragraph (d)(1), if a subcontract is awarded to a business unit that is subject to other types of CAS coverage under 48 CFR 9903.201-2, the substance of the applicable clause prescribed in FAR 30.201-4 must be inserted [@CL-CMP-0974].

Under the RFO text, contracting officers shall use the RFO FAR part 52 text in lieu of the codified text, effective February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. The RFO clause keeps the two standards, 48 CFR 9904.401 and 48 CFR 9904.402, in effect on the date of award of the contract [@CL-CMP-0277; @CL-CMP-0967; @CL-CMP-0968]. It keeps the consistent following of cost accounting practices, with either the U.S. Government or the contractor able to propose a change whose terms are negotiated with the contracting officer [@CL-CMP-0278; @CL-CMP-0970]. It keeps the prospective application of an agreed change, with the Disclosure Statement amended if it is affected [@CL-CMP-0279; @CL-CMP-0976], and the negotiation of an equitable adjustment when the contracting officer has made the finding required in 48 CFR 9903.201-6(c) that the change is desirable and not detrimental to the interests of the U.S. Government [@CL-CMP-0280; @CL-CMP-0971]. In the absence of the required finding, the RFO clause bars any agreement under the clause that will increase costs paid by the U.S. Government [@CL-CMP-0323]. It keeps the adjustment of the contract price or cost allowance when a failure to comply or to follow a practice increases costs paid by the U.S. Government [@CL-CMP-0281; @CL-CMP-1605], with interest at the annual rate established under section 6621(a)(2) of the Internal Revenue Code of 1986, computed for the period from the time the payment by the U.S. Government was made to the time the adjustment is effected [@CL-CMP-0282; @CL-CMP-0324; @CL-CMP-0972; @CL-CMP-8046]. For a subcontract awarded to a business unit under other types of coverage, the RFO clause refers to the applicable clause prescribed in FAR 30.205 [@CL-CMP-0283], where the codified clause refers to FAR 30.201-4 [@CL-CMP-0974].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A foreign-concern contract carries 250,000 of cost in a pool, of which 150,000 was incurred before the allocation practice changed and 100,000 after. The contracting officer makes the required finding and the change is agreed. Only the 100,000 incurred after the change follows the new practice, so the earlier 150,000 stays where it was booked.

## Never confuse

The two clauses require different standards: the foreign-concern clause requires compliance with 48 CFR 9904.401, Consistency in Estimating, Accumulating, and Reporting Costs, and 48 CFR 9904.402, Consistency in Allocating Costs Incurred for the Same Purpose [@CL-CMP-0967], while the clause on [the neighbouring page](cmp-disclosure-consistency-modified-cas.md) requires compliance with 48 CFR 9904.401, 9904.402, 9904.405, Accounting for Unallowable Costs, and 9904.406 [@CL-CMP-0951].

For a subcontract awarded to a business unit under other types of CAS coverage, the codified clause points to FAR 30.201-4 [@CL-CMP-0974] and the RFO clause points to FAR 30.205 [@CL-CMP-0283].
