## Purpose

Sets out the components of pension cost and the method a contractor must use to measure it, so a segment does not choose its own way of computing the annual charge.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified CAS [@CL-CMP-0557] |
| Codified citation | 48 CFR 9904.412-40 [@CL-CMP-0557] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-0557] |
| Applies to | Defined-benefit plans other than pay-as-you-go plans [@CL-CMP-0557], and defined-contribution plans [@CL-CMP-0558] |

For a defined-benefit plan outside the pay-as-you-go method, pension cost for a period has four parts: the normal cost, part of any unfunded actuarial liability, an interest equivalent on the unamortized liability, and an adjustment for actuarial gains and losses [@CL-CMP-0557]. A defined-contribution plan's cost for a period is the net contribution due for that period, after dividends and other credits [@CL-CMP-0558]. Measurement of a defined-benefit plan outside the pay-as-you-go method uses an immediate-gain actuarial cost method, and the validity of each assumption behind it is evaluated on its own [@CL-CMP-0559; @CL-CMP-0560]. Cost computed for a period is assignable to that period only, apart from amounts the standard permits to be assigned to future periods [@CL-CMP-0561]. For a defined-benefit plan other than one accounted for under the pay-as-you-go cost method, the pension cost is assignable only if the sum of (1) the unamortized portions of assignable unfunded actuarial liability developed and amortized pursuant to 9904.412-50(a)(1) [@CL-CMP-7058], and (2) the unassignable portions of unfunded actuarial liability separately identified and maintained pursuant to 9904.412-50(a)(2) equals the total unfunded actuarial liability [@CL-CMP-7059].

## How it works

The standard fixes both the pieces of pension cost and the method used to measure them, so the annual amount is not a free management choice [@CL-CMP-0557; @CL-CMP-0559]. Each actuarial assumption is identified separately and evaluated separately, against past experience and reasonable expectations [@CL-CMP-0560]. Because the computed amount is assignable only to its own period, apart from costs the standard permits to be assigned to future periods, it is not moved to another period at will [@CL-CMP-0561]. The adjustment for actuarial gains and losses connects this standard to the companion standard on adjustment and allocation of pension cost [@CL-CMP-0562].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A plan has a normal cost of 2 million, an amortized slice of an unfunded liability of 1 million, interest on the unamortized liability of 0.4 million, and an actuarial gain adjustment that removes 0.1 million. Pension cost for the period is 3.3 million before any assignable cost limitation.

## Never confuse

A defined-contribution plan's cost is a contribution figure, while the cost of a defined-benefit plan outside the pay-as-you-go method is built from components and measured with an immediate-gain actuarial cost method [@CL-CMP-0558; @CL-CMP-0557; @CL-CMP-0559]. Measuring the amount is not the same as spreading it; the annual gain and loss calculation and the split across segments sit in the companion pension standard [@CL-CMP-0562; @CL-CMP-0564].
