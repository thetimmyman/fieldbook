## Purpose

Sets out the components of pension cost and the method a contractor must use to measure it, so a segment does not choose its own way of computing the annual charge.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified CAS [@CL-CMP-0557] |
| Codified citation | 48 CFR 9904.412-40 [@CL-CMP-0557] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-0557] |
| Applies to | Defined-benefit plans other than pay-as-you-go plans [@CL-CMP-0557], and defined-contribution plans [@CL-CMP-0558] |

For defined-benefit pension plans, except for plans accounted for under the pay-as-you-go cost method, the components of pension cost for a cost accounting period are the normal cost of the period, a part of any unfunded actuarial liability, an interest equivalent on the unamortized portion of any unfunded actuarial liability, and an adjustment for any actuarial gains and losses [@CL-CMP-8038; @CL-CMP-0557]. For defined-contribution pension plans, the pension cost for a cost accounting period is the net contribution required to be made for that period, after taking into account dividends and other credits, where applicable [@CL-CMP-0558]. For defined-benefit pension plans other than those accounted for under the pay-as-you-go cost method, the amount of pension cost of a cost accounting period is determined by use of an immediate-gain actuarial cost method [@CL-CMP-0559]. The validity of each actuarial assumption used is evaluated solely with respect to that assumption [@CL-CMP-8039]. Except costs assigned to future periods by 9904.412-50(c)(2) and (5), the amount of pension cost computed for a cost accounting period is assignable only to that period [@CL-CMP-8040; @CL-CMP-0561]. For a defined-benefit plan other than one accounted for under the pay-as-you-go cost method, the pension cost is assignable only if the sum of (1) the unamortized portions of assignable unfunded actuarial liability developed and amortized pursuant to 9904.412-50(a)(1) [@CL-CMP-7058], and (2) the unassignable portions of unfunded actuarial liability separately identified and maintained pursuant to 9904.412-50(a)(2) equals the total unfunded actuarial liability [@CL-CMP-7059].

## How it works

The standard lists the components of pension cost and requires an immediate-gain actuarial cost method for defined-benefit plans other than those accounted for under the pay-as-you-go cost method [@CL-CMP-0557; @CL-CMP-0559]. Each actuarial assumption used to measure pension cost is separately identified and represents the contractor's best estimates of anticipated experience under the plan, taking into account past experience and reasonable expectations [@CL-CMP-0560]. The computed amount is assignable only to its own period, except costs assigned to future periods by 9904.412-50(c)(2) and (5) [@CL-CMP-8040; @CL-CMP-0561]. Actuarial gains and losses are calculated annually and assigned to the cost accounting period for which the actuarial valuation is made and subsequent periods under the companion standard on adjustment and allocation of pension cost [@CL-CMP-0562].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A plan has a normal cost of 2 million, an amortized slice of an unfunded liability of 1 million, interest on the unamortized liability of 0.4 million, and an actuarial gain adjustment that removes 0.1 million. Pension cost for the period is 3.3 million before any assignable cost limitation.

## Never confuse

A defined-contribution plan's cost is a contribution figure, while the cost of a defined-benefit plan outside the pay-as-you-go method is built from components and measured with an immediate-gain actuarial cost method [@CL-CMP-0558; @CL-CMP-0557; @CL-CMP-0559]. The companion pension standard requires actuarial gains and losses to be calculated annually [@CL-CMP-0562] and pension costs to be allocated to each segment having participants in a pension plan [@CL-CMP-0564].
