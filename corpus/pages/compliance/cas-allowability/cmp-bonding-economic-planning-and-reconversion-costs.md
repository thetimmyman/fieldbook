## Purpose

Covers bonding costs, economic planning costs and plant reconversion costs together, showing what is allowable, what needs prior agreement and what must not be duplicated.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-7513] |
| Codified citation | FAR 31.205-4 [@CL-CMP-7513] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 31.205-4 [@CL-CMP-9362]; RFO FAR 31.205-12 [@CL-CMP-9366]; RFO FAR 31.205-31 [@CL-CMP-9369] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-7513] |
| Applies to | Bonding costs, economic planning costs and plant reconversion costs [@CL-CMP-7513; @CL-CMP-7517; @CL-CMP-7520] |

Bonding costs arise when the Government requires assurance against financial loss to itself or others by reason of the act or default of the contractor [@CL-CMP-7513]. They arise also where the contractor requires similar assurance, and the category includes bid, performance, payment, advance payment, infringement and fidelity bonds [@CL-CMP-7514]. The costs of bonding required pursuant to the terms of the contract are allowable [@CL-CMP-7515]. The costs of bonding required by the contractor in the general conduct of its business are allowable to the extent that the bonding is in accordance with sound business practice and the rates and premiums are reasonable under the circumstances [@CL-CMP-7516].

Economic planning costs are the costs of general long-range management planning that is concerned with the future overall development of the contractor's business [@CL-CMP-7517], and they may take into account the eventual possibility of economic dislocations or fundamental alterations in the markets in which the contractor currently does business [@CL-CMP-7630]. Economic planning costs are allowable [@CL-CMP-7518]. They do not include organization or reorganization costs, which are covered by FAR 31.205-27 [@CL-CMP-7519], and market planning costs other than economic planning costs are dealt with at FAR 31.205-38 [@CL-CMP-7631].

Plant reconversion costs are those incurred in restoring or rehabilitating the contractor's facilities to approximately the same condition that existed immediately before the start of the Government contract, fair wear and tear excepted [@CL-CMP-7520]. Reconversion costs are unallowable except for the cost of removing Government property and the restoration or rehabilitation costs caused by that removal [@CL-CMP-7521]. In special circumstances where equity so dictates, additional costs may be allowed to the extent agreed upon before the costs are incurred [@CL-CMP-7522]. Care should be exercised to avoid duplication through allowance as contingencies, additional profit or fee, or in other contracts [@CL-CMP-7523].

## How it works

Bonding turns on why the bond is carried. A bond required by the terms of the contract is stated to be allowable, while a bond the contractor requires in the general conduct of its business is allowable only to the extent that it follows sound business practice and the rates and premiums are reasonable under the circumstances [@CL-CMP-7515; @CL-CMP-7516]. Both paragraphs describe the cost as allowable; neither names an official who approves a bond or a premium.

Economic planning is allowable as a category, and the section sets no condition on that allowance. The line to watch is the boundary: organization and reorganization costs are outside it, and market planning costs other than economic planning costs are handled elsewhere [@CL-CMP-7518; @CL-CMP-7519; @CL-CMP-7631].

Reconversion is unallowable by default. The exception covers removal of Government property together with the restoration or rehabilitation that the removal caused [@CL-CMP-7521]. Beyond that, additional costs may be allowed only where special circumstances make equity dictate it, and only to the extent agreed upon before the costs are incurred [@CL-CMP-7522]. The section does not say who agrees; the allowance is a "may", not a right. Fair wear and tear is not part of the reconversion cost [@CL-CMP-7520]. The caution on duplication applies to how these costs are allowed: through contingencies, additional profit or fee, or in other contracts [@CL-CMP-7523].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 31 text in place of the codified text [@CL-CMP-9350], effective immediately under Revision 1 signed on 2026-08-04 [@CL-CMP-9351]. RFO FAR 31.205-4 keeps the bonding rules [@CL-CMP-9362] but prints the list of bonds as paragraph (b) [@CL-CMP-9363], so bonding required by the terms of the contract is allowable under paragraph (c) [@CL-CMP-9364] and bonding required by the contractor in the general conduct of its business is allowable under paragraph (d) to the extent that it is in accordance with sound business practice and the rates and premiums are reasonable under the circumstances [@CL-CMP-9365]. RFO FAR 31.205-12 keeps the definition of economic planning costs [@CL-CMP-9366; @CL-CMP-9368] and the exclusion of organization and reorganization costs, with its pointer to 31.205-38 for market planning costs, and it prints the allowability sentence last, as paragraph (c) [@CL-CMP-9367]. RFO FAR 31.205-31 keeps the rule that reconversion costs are unallowable except for the cost of removing Government property and the restoration or rehabilitation costs caused by that removal [@CL-CMP-9370], and the caution against duplication [@CL-CMP-9372]. It excepts reasonable wear and tear where the codified text excepts fair wear and tear [@CL-CMP-9369; @CL-CMP-7520], and it allows additional costs, to the extent agreed upon before costs are incurred, in circumstances where equity so dictates, without the word special [@CL-CMP-9371; @CL-CMP-7522].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor ends a Government contract and incurs three costs at its facility. It spends 8,000 removing Government-owned equipment, 3,000 restoring the floor that the removal damaged, and 30,000 restoring the rest of the bay to approximately its condition before the contract began, excluding fair wear and tear. The two restoration costs are reconversion costs [@CL-CMP-7520]. The 8,000 for removing Government property and the 3,000 for the restoration caused by that removal fall within the exception, so 11,000 is allowable [@CL-CMP-7521]. The 30,000 is unallowable [@CL-CMP-7521], unless special circumstances where equity so dictates apply and the additional cost was agreed upon before it was incurred, in which case it may be allowed to the extent agreed [@CL-CMP-7522]. If no such agreement existed before the work, nothing under that route is allowed. Care should be exercised so that the 11,000 is not also allowed as a contingency, through additional profit or fee, or in other contracts [@CL-CMP-7523].

## Never confuse

Economic planning is not organization or reorganization work, which is outside the category [@CL-CMP-7519]; see [organization costs](../cas-allowability/cmp-organization-costs.md) and, for market planning, [selling costs](../cas-allowability/cmp-selling-costs.md) [@CL-CMP-7631]. Plant reconversion is restoring or rehabilitating facilities to approximately their condition before the Government contract, fair wear and tear excepted, and it is unallowable apart from the removal exception and any equity allowance agreed in advance [@CL-CMP-7520; @CL-CMP-7521; @CL-CMP-7522]. Contract-required bonding and business bonding also differ, because only the second carries the condition that the bonding follow sound business practice and carry reasonable rates and premiums [@CL-CMP-7515; @CL-CMP-7516]. The equity allowance for reconversion costs is worded differently in the two texts: the codified text says "in special circumstances where equity so dictates" [@CL-CMP-7522], while the RFO text says "in circumstances, where equity so dictates" [@CL-CMP-9371].

Whether any reconversion work is covered by a written agreement made before the costs were incurred. [@Q-043]
