## Purpose

Walks through the five printed illustrations on the cost accounting period, and says what the interpretation decides about restructuring costs. The rule itself is on [Cost accounting period](../cas-allowability/cmp-cas-406-cost-accounting-period.md).

In the first illustration, the contractor estimates the allocable expenses based solely on the estimated amount of the general management expense pool and the amount of the total cost input base estimated to be incurred during the eight months in which performance is scheduled to be commenced and completed [@CL-CMP-2887]. Such a proposal would violate the requirement that the calculation of both the indirect cost pools and the allocation bases be based on the contractor's cost accounting period [@CL-CMP-2802]. In the computer service center illustration, the operating expense of the new service center is expected to be material in amount, will be accumulated in a separate indirect cost pool and will be allocated to the benefiting cost objectives on the basis of measured usage [@CL-CMP-2888]. The total operating expenses for the eight-month part of the cost accounting period may be allocated to the benefiting cost objectives of that same eight-month period [@CL-CMP-2803]. In the fiscal-year-change illustration, the five-month transitional period must be used as the transitional cost accounting period and may not be combined as the standard provides for combining periods, because the transitional period would be longer than fifteen months [@CL-CMP-2804]. In the model-year illustration, where the parties agree to a model year, to overhead rates on that basis and to a technique for prorating home office expenses between model years, the practice is permitted by the standard [@CL-CMP-2805]. In the vacation-year illustration, vacation expenses are estimated uniformly during each vacation year, adjustments are made each October to adjust the accrued liability to actual, and the estimating rates are modified to the extent deemed appropriate [@CL-CMP-2889]. The use of a separate annual period for determining the amounts of vacation expense is permitted [@CL-CMP-2806].

For restructuring costs, the interpretation says costs as it defines them may be accumulated as deferred cost and subsequently amortized over a period during which the benefits of restructuring are expected to accrue [@CL-CMP-2807]. A contractor proposal to expense restructuring costs for a specific event in a current period is also acceptable when the Contracting Officer agrees that the treatment will result in a more equitable assignment of costs in the circumstances [@CL-CMP-2808]. The amortization period for deferred restructuring costs shall not exceed five years [@CL-CMP-2809].

## How it works

The illustrations test the period used for pools and bases. A proposal that estimates the expenses from the pool and base for only the eight months of scheduled performance fails [@CL-CMP-2887; @CL-CMP-2802]. In the computer service center illustration, an expense that is expected to be material, is pooled separately and is allocated by measured usage may be allocated over the eight-month part of the period [@CL-CMP-2888; @CL-CMP-2803]. In the fiscal-year-change illustration, the transitional period is kept separate because combining it would make it longer than fifteen months [@CL-CMP-2804]. Agreed or separate annual periods are permitted in the model-year and vacation-year illustrations as they describe the facts [@CL-CMP-2805; @CL-CMP-2806; @CL-CMP-2889]. For restructuring, the interpretation allows deferral with amortization or a current-period expense agreed by the Contracting Officer [@CL-CMP-2807; @CL-CMP-2808].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor installs a service center that starts operating partway through its cost accounting period. The center's operating expense is expected to be material, its pool is separate and its cost is allocated by measured usage. The contractor may allocate the center's cost to cost objectives of the part of the period in which it operated.

## Never confuse

A deferred restructuring cost is not an open-ended deferral, because the amortization period shall not exceed five years [@CL-CMP-2809]. A proposal to expense restructuring costs in the current period is also acceptable when the Contracting Officer agrees to it on grounds of more equitable assignment [@CL-CMP-2808]. The base rule on the cost accounting period is on [Cost accounting period](../cas-allowability/cmp-cas-406-cost-accounting-period.md). The model-year illustration also covers a technique for prorating home office expenses between model years [@CL-CMP-2805]; illustrations of home office pools and allocation bases are on [Illustrations of home office pools and allocation bases](../cas-allowability/cmp-cas-403-illustrations-and-interpretation.md). The first illustration allocates on a total cost input base; illustrations of what counts in that base are on [Illustrations of the general and administrative pool and cost input base](../cas-allowability/cmp-cas-410-illustrations.md).

## Company calibration

Whether the company has restructuring events, whether its practice defers or expenses them, and whether its Disclosure Statement covers that practice. [@Q-033]
