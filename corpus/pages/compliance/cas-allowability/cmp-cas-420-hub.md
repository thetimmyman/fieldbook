## Purpose

Cost Accounting Standard 420 provides criteria for the accumulation of independent research and development costs and bid and proposal costs, and for the allocation of those costs to cost objectives on the basis of the beneficial or causal relationship between the costs and the cost objectives. This concept hub sets out what the standard requires and links the detailed reference, compliance and estimating pages across the corpus.

## The rule

CAS 420 states that its purpose is to provide criteria for the accumulation of independent research and development costs and bid and proposal costs [@CL-CMP-8694], and for the allocation of such costs to cost objectives based on the beneficial or causal relationship between such costs and cost objectives [@CL-CMP-8695], and that consistent application of those criteria will improve cost allocation [@CL-CMP-8696]. The basic unit for the identification and accumulation of those costs shall be the individual project [@CL-CMP-0575]. Project costs consist of all allocable costs except business unit general and administrative expenses [@CL-CMP-0576], and the cost pools consist of all project costs and other allocable costs, except business unit general and administrative expenses [@CL-CMP-8115].

The cost pools of a home office are allocated to segments on the basis of the beneficial or causal relationship between the costs and the segments reporting to that home office [@CL-CMP-0577], and the pools of a business unit are allocated to the final cost objectives of that business unit on the same basis [@CL-CMP-0578]. Bid and proposal cost incurred in a cost accounting period shall not be assigned to any other cost accounting period [@CL-CMP-0579], and independent research and development cost is not assigned to another period either, except as provisions of existing laws, regulations and other controlling factors may permit [@CL-CMP-0580].

The Standard shall not apply to contracts and grants with State, local, and federally recognized Indian tribal governments [@CL-CMP-8665]. It is effective as of April 17, 1992 [@CL-EST-2940]. Contractors with prior CAS-covered contracts with full coverage continue its applicability upon receipt of a contract to which it is applicable [@CL-EST-2941], and for contractors with no previous contracts subject to it, it is applied beginning with the contractor's second full fiscal year beginning after that receipt [@CL-EST-2942].

## How it works

Bid and proposal cost means the cost incurred in preparing, submitting or supporting any bid or proposal whose effort is neither sponsored by a grant nor required in the performance of a contract [@CL-EST-2926]. Independent research and development means the cost of effort which is neither sponsored by a grant nor required in the performance of a contract and which falls within basic and applied research, development, or systems and other concept formulation studies [@CL-EST-2933]. The individual project is the unit that is opened and charged [@CL-CMP-0575], and the pool it feeds is later allocated to the segments or the final cost objectives that benefit from it [@CL-CMP-0577; @CL-CMP-0578].

Allocation sits inside the standard, while allowability sits in the cost principles: costs for independent research and development and bid and proposal efforts are allowable as indirect expenses on contracts to the extent that those costs are allocable and reasonable, except as other paragraphs of FAR 31.205-18 or agency regulations provide [@CL-CMP-0112]. For a major contractor's independent research and development costs to be allowable on covered contracts, the contractor reports the projects generating those costs to the Defense Technical Information Center [@CL-CMP-1403] and updates its inputs at least annually, no later than three months after the end of its fiscal year, and when the project is completed [@CL-CMP-0883].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A business unit opens three projects: two independent research projects costing 900,000 and 300,000 and one bid and proposal project costing 800,000. None of the three projects is sponsored by a grant or required in the performance of a contract, and none of that cost is business unit general and administrative expense, so the pool is 2,000,000. The unit allocates that pool to its final cost objectives on the basis of the benefits received, and with 50,000,000 of total cost input the rate is 4 percent, so a contract with 3,000,000 of cost input receives 120,000. In the same year the home office allocates its own pool of 500,000 to three segments that benefit in the proportions 50, 30 and 20, giving 250,000, 150,000 and 100,000. The 120,000 stands in the year it was incurred and is not carried to another period. This example is illustrative only, and the contract itself governs.

## Never confuse

CAS 420 accumulates these costs by individual project and keeps business unit general and administrative expenses out of the project costs and the pools [@CL-CMP-0575; @CL-CMP-0576; @CL-CMP-8115], and it sends a home office's pools to the segments that report to it [@CL-CMP-0577]. CAS 410 does the opposite with general and administrative expense: it groups that expense in a separate indirect cost pool and allocates it only to final cost objectives [@CL-CMP-0213]. The project costs and the pools of this standard carry none of that expense [@CL-CMP-0576; @CL-CMP-8115].

## Company calibration

Whether the company's business units are covered by this standard, and which disclosed practices apply [@Q-033], and how research and proposal projects are opened, charged and reported [@Q-042].
