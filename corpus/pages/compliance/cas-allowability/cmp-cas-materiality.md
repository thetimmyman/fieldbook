## Purpose

Shows the criteria for judging whether amounts of cost are material or immaterial, and that no one criterion is necessarily determinative.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | CAS [@CL-CMP-1777] |
| Codified citation | 48 CFR 9903.305 [@CL-CMP-1777] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-1777] |
| Applies to | Amounts of cost assessed for materiality [@CL-CMP-1777] |

In determining whether amounts of cost are material or immaterial, the listed criteria shall be considered where appropriate; no one criterion is necessarily determinative [@CL-CMP-1777].

The larger the absolute dollar amount, the more likely it is to be material [@CL-CMP-1778]. The larger the proportion of the amount under consideration to contract cost, the more likely it is to be material [@CL-CMP-1779].

## How it works

Direct cost items, especially if the amounts are themselves part of a base for allocation of indirect costs, will normally have more impact than the same amount of indirect costs [@CL-CMP-1780].

It is appropriate to consider whether the impacts of individually immaterial items tend to offset one another or tend to be in the same direction and hence to accumulate into a material amount [@CL-CMP-1781].

Changes in accounting treatment have more impact if they influence the distribution of costs between Government and non-Government cost objectives than if all cost objectives have Government financial support [@CL-CMP-1903]. The cost of administrative processing of the price adjustment modification must also be considered: where that cost exceeds the amount to be recovered, the amount is less likely to be material [@CL-CMP-1782].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A practice change affects five line items of 2,400 each, and all five run in the same direction, so together they are 12,000. A price adjustment would cost 15,000 to process. The answer: each item looks small, but the same-direction items accumulate to 12,000, and the processing cost of 15,000 exceeds the 12,000 to be recovered, which makes the amount less likely to be material. No one criterion decides, so the judgement weighs all of the criteria that apply.

## Never confuse

In determining whether amounts of cost are material or immaterial, the listed criteria shall be considered where appropriate; no one criterion is necessarily determinative [@CL-CMP-1777].

In judging the cumulative impact of individually immaterial items, it is appropriate to consider whether such impacts tend to offset one another or tend to be in the same direction and hence to accumulate into a material amount [@CL-CMP-1781]. The meaning of a change to a cost accounting practice is described in [Cost accounting practice definitions](../cas-allowability/cmp-cost-accounting-practice-definitions.md), and the audit products that test cost impact in [DCAA audits](../cas-allowability/cmp-dcaa-cas-audits.md).
