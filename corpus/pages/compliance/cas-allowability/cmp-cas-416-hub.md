## Purpose

Cost Accounting Standard 416 provides criteria for the measurement of insurance costs, the assignment of those costs to cost accounting periods, and their allocation to cost objectives. This concept hub sets out what the standard requires and links the detailed reference, compliance and estimating pages across the corpus.

## The rule

CAS 416 states that its purpose is to provide criteria for the measurement of insurance costs, the assignment of those costs to cost accounting periods, and their allocation to cost objectives [@CL-CMP-8692], and that application of those criteria should increase the probability that insurance costs are allocated to cost objectives in a uniform and consistent manner [@CL-CMP-8693]. The amount of insurance cost to be assigned to a cost accounting period is the projected average loss for that period plus insurance administration expenses in that period [@CL-CMP-1783]. The allocation of insurance costs to cost objectives shall be based on the beneficial or causal relationship between the insurance costs and the benefiting or causing cost objectives [@CL-CMP-1784].

The exemption section of CAS 416 says there is no exemption for that Standard [@CL-CMP-8662]. The Standard is effective as of April 17, 1992 [@CL-EST-2646]. Contractors with prior CAS-covered contracts with full coverage continue the Standard's applicability upon receipt of a contract to which it is applicable [@CL-EST-2647], and for contractors with no previous contracts subject to it, it is applied beginning with the contractor's next full fiscal year beginning after that receipt [@CL-EST-2648].

## How it works

For exposure to risk of loss covered by the purchase of insurance or by payments to a trusteed fund, the premium or payment, adjusted in accordance with the listed criteria, represents the projected average loss [@CL-CMP-1904]. The premium cost applicable to a given policy term is assigned pro rata among the cost accounting periods covered by the policy term, except as the listed subdivisions provide [@CL-CMP-1785], and where insurance is purchased specifically for, and directly allocated to, a single final cost objective, the premium need not be prorated among cost accounting periods [@CL-CMP-1786].

For exposure not covered by the purchase of insurance or by payments to a trusteed fund, the contractor follows a program of self-insurance accounting [@CL-CMP-1905]. Except as the two listed subdivisions provide, actual losses do not become a part of insurance costs [@CL-CMP-1787], and the contractor makes a self-insurance charge for each period for each type of self-insured risk which represents the projected average loss for that period [@CL-CMP-1788]. Self-insurance means the assumption or retention of the risk or loss by the contractor, whether voluntarily or involuntarily, and it includes the deductible portion of purchased insurance [@CL-EST-2895]. Projected average loss means the estimated long-term average loss per period for periods of comparable exposure to risk of loss [@CL-EST-2894], and insurance administration expenses are the contractor's costs of administering an insurance program, such as operating a risk-management department, processing claims and paying actuarial fees [@CL-EST-2893].

Insurance costs are allocated on the basis of the factors used to determine the premium, assessment, refund, dividend or self-insurance charge [@CL-CMP-1789], and costs incurred by a segment, or allocated to a segment from a home office, may be combined with the costs of other indirect cost pools where the resulting allocation to each final cost objective is substantially the same as separate allocation would give [@CL-CMP-1906]. Insurance administration expenses which are material in relation to total insurance costs are allocated on the same basis as the related premium costs or self-insurance charge [@CL-CMP-1790], and the contractor maintains records that substantiate the amounts of premiums, refunds, dividends, losses and self-insurance charges, and the measurement and allocation of insurance costs [@CL-CMP-1791].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor buys a policy whose term is two years for 120,000, and the term covers two cost accounting periods equally, so 60,000 of the premium is assigned to each period. Administration expenses of 9,000 belong to the first period and are added to its 60,000, so insurance cost for the first period is 69,000. A second policy bought specifically for, and directly allocated to, one final cost objective at 20,000 is not prorated, so the whole 20,000 is charged to that objective. For a risk the contractor retains inside a deductible, the projected average loss is 55,000, so the self-insurance charge for the period is 55,000, and an actual loss of 180,000 suffered in that period does not become a part of insurance costs. This example is illustrative only, and the contract itself governs.

## Never confuse

CAS 416 measures insurance cost for a period as the projected average loss plus insurance administration expenses [@CL-CMP-1783], and it keeps actual losses out of insurance costs except as the two listed subdivisions provide [@CL-CMP-1787]. Allowability is a separate question that FAR 31.205-19 decides: actual losses are unallowable unless the contract expressly provides for them, except losses under the nominal deductible provisions of purchased insurance [@CL-CMP-0503] and ordinary uninsured minor losses such as spoilage, breakage and disappearance of small hand tools [@CL-CMP-0828], and where purchased insurance is available, a self-insurance charge plus administration expenses above the cost of comparable purchased insurance plus its associated administration expenses is unallowable [@CL-CMP-0502].
