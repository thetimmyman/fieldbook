## Purpose

Walks through the printed illustrations of consistent and inconsistent practices under the standard on consistency in estimating, accumulating and reporting costs, and says what its interpretation decides about direct material estimated by percentage factors. The rule itself is on [Consistency in estimating, accumulating and reporting costs](../cas-allowability/cmp-cas-401-consistency.md).

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | CAS [@CL-CMP-2775] |
| Codified citation | 48 CFR 9904.401-60 and 9904.401-61 [@CL-CMP-2775; @CL-CMP-2779] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-2775] |
| Applies to | Not established. |

The illustrations come in two groups. The first group is described as applications of cost accounting practices that are deemed to be consistent [@CL-CMP-2775]. One listed consistent example pairs an estimated average direct labor rate for manufacturing direct labor by labor category or function with actual cost recorded for each individual and collected by labor category or function [@CL-CMP-2775; @CL-CMP-2776]. The second group is described as applications of cost accounting practices that are deemed not to be consistent [@CL-CMP-2777]. One listed example in that group pairs an estimate of engineering labor by cost function with the accumulation of total engineering labor in one undifferentiated account [@CL-CMP-2777; @CL-CMP-2778]. Another listed example in that group has the contractor estimate a total dollar amount for engineering labor that includes disparate and significant elements or functions of engineering labor and not provide supporting data reconciling that amount to the estimates for the same engineering labor cost functions for which the contractor will separately account in contract performance [@CL-CMP-2777; @CL-CMP-2875; @CL-CMP-8140].

The interpretation says the most serious questions concern percentage factors that the contractor supports neither with accounting, statistical or other relevant data from past experience nor with a program to accumulate actual costs for comparison with the percentage estimates [@CL-CMP-2779]. In that circumstance, accumulating direct costs in an undifferentiated account is a cost accounting practice that is not consistent with estimating a significant part of costs by percentage factors [@CL-CMP-2779; @CL-CMP-2780]. The interpretation leaves the amount of accounting and statistical detail required for the percentage-factor portion of direct material costs to Government procurement authorities, decided on the individual facts and circumstances [@CL-CMP-2781]. It says the standard does not prescribe the amount of detail required in accumulating and reporting costs, and that the amount may vary considerably depending on the percentage factors used, the data presented in justification or lack thereof, and the significance of each situation [@CL-CMP-2876].

## How it works

Each illustration sets an estimating practice beside the accounting practice it is compared with. In the listed consistent example the records collect actual cost by the same labor category or function that the estimate uses [@CL-CMP-2775; @CL-CMP-2776]. In the listed example that is not consistent, the estimate is made by cost function while the records hold one undifferentiated account [@CL-CMP-2777; @CL-CMP-2778]. For direct material estimated by percentage factors, the interpretation says the amount of detail is for Government procurement authorities to decide on the individual facts and circumstances [@CL-CMP-2781], and it does not prescribe the amount of detail for the standard as a whole [@CL-CMP-2876].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor estimates drafting and design as two separate cost functions, with 100 hours of drafting and 200 hours of design. It records all 300 hours of engineering labor in one undifferentiated account. This matches the printed pattern of an estimate by cost function against one undifferentiated account, which the illustrations list as not consistent.

## Never confuse

A listed illustration is not a rule on how finely costs must be recorded, because the interpretation says the standard does not prescribe the amount of detail required in accumulating and reporting costs [@CL-CMP-2876], and for direct material it leaves the amount to Government procurement authorities on the individual facts [@CL-CMP-2781]. A different question, whether costs incurred for the same purpose are charged the same way, is on [Consistency in allocating costs incurred for the same purpose](../cas-allowability/cmp-cas-402-same-purpose.md), and its illustrations are on [Definitions, illustrations and interpretation on consistency in allocating costs incurred for the same purpose](../cas-allowability/cmp-cas-402-definitions-illustrations-and-interpretation.md).

## Company calibration

How the company's estimating practices for direct labor, material and machining line up with its recorded practices, and whether a Disclosure Statement is on file for each business unit. [@Q-033]
