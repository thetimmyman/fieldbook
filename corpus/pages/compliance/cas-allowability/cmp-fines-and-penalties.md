## Purpose

Establishes the unallowability of fines, penalties for regulatory violations, and costs incurred in connection with mischarging, including costs to measure or remedy it.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-0306] |
| Codified citation | FAR 31.205-15 [@CL-CMP-0306] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 31.205-15 [@CL-CMP-8549; @CL-CMP-8550; @CL-CMP-8551] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-0306] |
| Applies to | Government contracts subject to the cost principles [@CL-CMP-0306] |

Costs of fines and penalties resulting from violations of, or failure of the contractor to comply with, Federal, State, local, or foreign laws and regulations are unallowable, except when incurred as a result of compliance with specific terms and conditions of the contract or written instructions from the contracting officer [@CL-CMP-0306; @CL-CMP-0814]. Costs incurred in connection with, or related to, the mischarging of costs on Government contracts are unallowable when the costs are caused by, or result from, alteration or destruction of records, or other false or improper charging or recording of costs [@CL-CMP-0307]. Such costs include those incurred to measure or otherwise determine the magnitude of the improper charging, and costs incurred to remedy or correct the mischarging, such as costs to rescreen and reconstruct records [@CL-CMP-0308].

## How it works

Costs of fines and penalties resulting from violations of, or failure of the contractor to comply with, Federal, State, local, or foreign laws and regulations are unallowable, except when incurred as a result of compliance with specific terms and conditions of the contract or written instructions from the contracting officer [@CL-CMP-0306; @CL-CMP-0814]. Costs incurred in connection with, or related to, the mischarging of costs on Government contracts are unallowable when the costs are caused by, or result from, alteration or destruction of records, or other false or improper charging or recording of costs [@CL-CMP-0307]. Such costs include those incurred to measure or otherwise determine the magnitude of the improper charging, and costs incurred to remedy or correct the mischarging, such as costs to rescreen and reconstruct records [@CL-CMP-0308].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers must use the RFO FAR part 31 text in place of the codified text [@CL-CMP-9350], effective immediately under Revision 1 signed on 2026-08-04 [@CL-CMP-9351]. Under RFO FAR 31.205-15(a), costs of fines and penalties resulting from violations of, or failure of the contractor to comply with, Federal, State, local, or foreign laws and regulations are unallowable, except when incurred as a result of compliance with specific terms and conditions of the contract or written instructions from the contracting officer [@CL-CMP-8549]. Under RFO paragraph (b), costs incurred in connection with, or related to, the mischarging of costs on Government contracts are unallowable when the costs are caused by, or result from, alteration or destruction of records, or other false or improper charging or recording of costs [@CL-CMP-8550]. Such costs include those incurred to measure or otherwise determine the magnitude of the improper charging, and costs incurred to remedy or correct the mischarging, such as costs to rescreen and reconstruct records [@CL-CMP-8551].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An environmental agency assesses a 75,000 civil penalty against a contractor for unauthorized discharge from a manufacturing plant, and an internal review discloses that employees altered timecards to mischarge commercial work to a defense contract. The contractor incurs 30,000 in forensic accounting fees to determine the improper charging and reconstruct time records. The 75,000 regulatory fine is unallowable, and the 30,000 forensic audit and record reconstruction expense is unallowable as a mischarging remedy cost.

## Never confuse

The fines-and-penalties rule covers costs of fines and penalties resulting from violations of, or failure of the contractor to comply with, Federal, State, local, or foreign laws and regulations [@CL-CMP-0306]. The mischarging rule covers costs incurred in connection with, or related to, the mischarging of costs on Government contracts that are caused by, or result from, alteration or destruction of records, or other false or improper charging or recording of costs [@CL-CMP-0307]; such costs include costs to measure the magnitude of the improper charging and to remedy or correct the mischarging [@CL-CMP-0308]. See [Unallowable-cost penalties](cmp-unallowable-cost-penalty.md) for the consequences of including unallowable costs in indirect cost proposals.
