## Purpose

Shows how a contractor must identify and exclude unallowable costs from billings, claims and proposals on a Government contract.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | CAS [@CL-CMP-0207] |
| Codified citation | 48 CFR 9904.405-40(a) [@CL-CMP-0207] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-0207] |
| Applies to | Costs expressly unallowable or mutually agreed unallowable [@CL-CMP-0207] |

Costs that are expressly unallowable or mutually agreed unallowable must be identified and excluded from any billing, claim, or proposal [@CL-CMP-0207]. Unallowable costs that would normally sit in a regular indirect-cost allocation base stay in that base [@CL-CMP-0208]. The visibility required can be met by segregating unallowable costs in separate accounts in the regular books of account [@CL-CMP-0209].

## How it works

The record detail must be enough to keep unallowable costs visible, including directly associated costs, and to show how they are treated for allocation. Unallowable costs follow the same allocability principles as allowable costs. A contractor may use separate accounts, separate workpapers, or a less formal technique that still permits audit verification.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor holds 5,000 of entertainment costs that are expressly unallowable in an overhead account. It keeps them in a separate account so they are visible and excludes them from any billing and proposal; because they would normally be part of the regular indirect-cost allocation base, they remain in that base.

## Never confuse

Accounting for unallowable costs is not the same as the penalty for leaving an expressly unallowable cost in a final rate proposal. The cost accounting standard requires identification and exclusion; the penalty rule adds a monetary penalty for costs left in a proposal. See [Unallowable cost penalty](cmp-unallowable-cost-penalty.md).
