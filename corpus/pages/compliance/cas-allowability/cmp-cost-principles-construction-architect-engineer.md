## Purpose

Shows which contracts the construction category covers, what applies to them, and how equipment, job-site and temporary facility costs are treated.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-2713] |
| Codified citation | FAR 31.105 [@CL-CMP-2711] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 31.105 [@CL-CMP-9569; @CL-CMP-9570; @CL-CMP-9572; @CL-CMP-9573; @CL-CMP-9575] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-2711] |
| Applies to | Cost-negotiated contracts for construction and architect-engineer work, with the organization exclusions stated in the section [@CL-CMP-2711; @CL-CMP-2712] |

As to subject matter, the category covers contracts for construction management or construction, alteration or repair of buildings, bridges, roads, or other kinds of real property, it also includes architect-engineer contracts related to construction projects, and it does not include contracts for vessels, aircraft, or other kinds of personal property [@CL-CMP-2711]. Those contracts are covered when negotiated on the basis of cost with organizations other than educational institutions, State and local governments, and nonprofit organizations except those exempted under OMB Uniform Guidance at 2 CFR part 200, appendix VIII [@CL-CMP-2712].

Except as otherwise provided in the allowability paragraph, the cost principles and procedures in subpart 31.2 shall be used in pricing contracts and contract modifications in this category if cost analysis is performed as required by FAR 15.404-1(c) [@CL-CMP-2713]. The contracting officer shall also incorporate those principles, as modified by the allowability paragraph, by reference as the basis for the purposes the section lists [@CL-CMP-2714]. One listed purpose is determining reimbursable costs under cost-reimbursement contracts, including cost-reimbursement subcontracts thereunder [@CL-CMP-2772]. Four further listed purposes are negotiating indirect cost rates, proposing, negotiating, or determining costs under terminated contracts, price revision of fixed-price incentive contracts, and pricing changes and other contract modifications [@CL-CMP-2771]. Except as otherwise provided in that paragraph, the allowability of costs for construction and architect-engineer contracts shall be determined in accordance with subpart 31.2 [@CL-CMP-2715]. Advance agreements for such items as home office overhead, partners' compensation, employment of consultants, and equipment usage costs are described as particularly important in construction and architect-engineer contracts [@CL-CMP-2716]; see [Advance agreements on particular costs](../cas-allowability/cmp-advance-agreements.md).

## How it works

### Equipment

Equipment gets its own treatment, and the section defines construction equipment as equipment, including marine equipment, in sound workable condition, owned or controlled by the contractor or a subcontractor at any tier or obtained from a commercial rental source, and furnished for use under Government contracts [@CL-CMP-2717]. Allowable ownership and operating costs are determined from actual cost data when such data can be determined for both ownership and operating costs for each piece of equipment, or groups of similar serial or series equipment, from the contractor's accounting records [@CL-CMP-2718]. When such costs cannot be so determined, the contracting agency may specify a schedule of predetermined rates, or any part of one, but costs otherwise unallowable do not become allowable through any schedule [@CL-CMP-2719; @CL-CMP-2720].

### Schedules of predetermined rates

The section gives as examples that schedules need to be adjusted for Government contract costing purposes if they are based on replacement cost, include unallowable interest costs, or use improper cost of money rates or computations [@CL-CMP-2766]. It states that the allowance for ownership costs should include the cost of depreciation and may include facilities capital cost of money, and that the allowance for operating costs may include costs for such items as fuel, filters, oil, and grease; servicing, repairs, and maintenance; and tire wear and repair [@CL-CMP-2767]. Costs of labor, mobilization, demobilization, overhead, and profit are generally not reflected in predetermined schedules, and separate consideration may be necessary [@CL-CMP-2768]. When schedule use rates determine direct costs, all costs of equipment included in the schedule allowances shall be identified and eliminated from the contractor's other direct and indirect costs charged to the contract [@CL-CMP-2721; @CL-CMP-7777]. In periods of suspension of work pursuant to a contract clause, the allowance for equipment ownership shall not exceed an amount for standby cost as determined by the schedule or contract provision [@CL-CMP-2765].

### Rented equipment

Reasonable costs of renting construction equipment are allowable with a cross-reference to a later paragraph, and maintenance and minor or running repairs not in the rental rate are allowable [@CL-CMP-2722]. Major repair and overhaul of rental equipment is unallowable, and charges from organizations under common control follow a separate rule [@CL-CMP-2723].

### Job-site and temporary facility costs

The section lists superintendence, timekeeping and clerical work, engineering, utility costs, supplies, material handling, and restoration and cleanup as examples of costs incurred at the job site incident to performing the work [@CL-CMP-2762]. Job-site costs of those kinds are allowable as direct or indirect costs, provided the accounting practice used is in accordance with the contractor's established and consistently followed cost accounting practices for all work [@CL-CMP-2763]. Rental and other costs of acquiring the temporary use of land, structures, and facilities, and costs of constructing or fabricating structures and facilities of a temporary nature, are allowable, in each case less any applicable credits [@CL-CMP-2764].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 31 text in place of the codified text [@CL-CMP-9350], effective immediately under Revision 1 signed on 2026-08-04 [@CL-CMP-9351]. RFO FAR 31.105 keeps the category: it covers construction management or construction, alteration or repair of real property and related architect-engineer contracts, and not vessels, aircraft or other personal property [@CL-CMP-9569], when negotiated on the basis of cost with organizations other than educational institutions, State and local governments, and nonprofit organizations except those exempted under OMB Uniform Guidance at 2 CFR part 200, appendix VIII [@CL-CMP-9570]. It keeps most of the equipment and job-site rules: actual cost data must be used when it can be determined [@CL-CMP-9577], the ownership and operating allowance guidance [@CL-CMP-9581; @CL-CMP-9582], the elimination of equipment costs included in schedule allowances [@CL-CMP-9583], the standby cap during suspension of work [@CL-CMP-9584], major repair and overhaul of rented equipment being unallowable [@CL-CMP-9586], job-site costs [@CL-CMP-9587; @CL-CMP-9588], and temporary land and facility costs [@CL-CMP-9589]. Several things change. The paragraphs are renumbered: the definition of construction equipment is paragraph (b) [@CL-CMP-9571], the pricing rule is paragraph (c) [@CL-CMP-9572], the incorporation rule is paragraph (d) [@CL-CMP-9573; @CL-CMP-9574], and the allowability rules are in paragraph (e) [@CL-CMP-9575]. The pricing exception and the incorporation rule's modification reference both still point to paragraph (d), not to paragraph (e), where the codified text pointed to its allowability paragraph [@CL-CMP-9572; @CL-CMP-9573; @CL-CMP-2713; @CL-CMP-2714]. In the reimbursable-cost purpose, the RFO text reads cost-reimbursement contracts and subcontracts, without the codified words including cost-reimbursement subcontracts thereunder [@CL-CMP-9573; @CL-CMP-2772]. Advance agreements appear as an exception that refers to 31.110, where the codified text refers to 31.109, and the RFO text does not repeat the codified statement that such agreements are particularly important [@CL-CMP-9576; @CL-CMP-2716]. When actual cost data cannot be determined, the contracting officer, not the contracting agency, may specify a schedule of predetermined rates or any part of one [@CL-CMP-9578; @CL-CMP-2719]. The rule that costs otherwise unallowable do not become allowable is tied to an advance agreement rather than to any schedule [@CL-CMP-9579; @CL-CMP-2720], and the schedule-adjustment examples are kept [@CL-CMP-9580; @CL-CMP-2766]. The rented-equipment paragraph opens directly with its list, with no counterpart to the codified sentence that reasonable costs of renting construction equipment are allowable [@CL-CMP-9585; @CL-CMP-2722], and its common-control reference adds 31.205-36(b)(4) [@CL-CMP-9586; @CL-CMP-2723].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor owns an excavator used on a cost-reimbursement construction contract. Its accounting records support actual ownership and operating cost of 30,000 for the machine on the job. Actual cost data can be determined for both, so they are used and no schedule is needed. The answer: use the actual cost data, not a schedule rate.

The same contractor incurs 8,000 of job-site superintendence and 2,000 of rental for temporary land, less a 500 credit. The answer: the rental cost less the credit, 1,500, is allowable. The superintendence is allowable as a direct or indirect cost if it is accounted for under the contractor's established and consistently followed practice for all work.

## Never confuse

A schedule of predetermined rates is a way to measure ownership and operating cost, not a way to allow cost, because costs otherwise unallowable do not become allowable through the use of any schedule [@CL-CMP-2719; @CL-CMP-2720]. Job-site costs are allowable as direct or indirect costs provided the accounting practice follows the contractor's established and consistently followed practices for all work, which is a different test from the equipment rules [@CL-CMP-2763]. The commercial-organization category excludes construction and architect-engineer contracts [@CL-CMP-2707]; see [Cost principles for contracts with commercial organizations](../cas-allowability/cmp-cost-principles-commercial-organizations.md). For how costs that are unallowable are identified and excluded, see [Accounting for unallowable and directly associated costs](../cas-allowability/cmp-accounting-for-unallowable-and-associated-costs.md). For rental costs under the general cost principles, see [Allowable rental costs](../cas-allowability/cmp-rental-costs.md), and for the allowability tests, see [Factors determining cost allowability](../cas-allowability/cmp-allowability-factors.md).

Under the codified text, costs otherwise unallowable do not become allowable through any schedule [@CL-CMP-2720], but under the RFO text they must not become allowable through the use of an advance agreement [@CL-CMP-9579]. The codified text also states that reasonable costs of renting construction equipment are allowable [@CL-CMP-2722], and the RFO rented-equipment paragraph opens with its list and does not state this [@CL-CMP-9585].

## Company calibration

Whether the company holds construction or architect-engineer contracts, and whether advance agreements cover equipment usage and home office overhead. [@Q-043]
