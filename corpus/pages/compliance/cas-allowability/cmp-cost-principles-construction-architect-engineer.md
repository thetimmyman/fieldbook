## Purpose

Shows which contracts the construction category covers, what applies to them, and how equipment, job-site and temporary facility costs are treated.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-2713] |
| Codified citation | FAR 31.105 [@CL-CMP-2711] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 31.105 [@CL-CMP-9569; @CL-CMP-9570; @CL-CMP-9572; @CL-CMP-9573; @CL-CMP-9575] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-2711] |
| Applies to | Cost-negotiated contracts for construction and architect-engineer work, with the organization exclusions stated in the section [@CL-CMP-2711; @CL-CMP-2712] |

The category includes all contracts and contract modifications [@CL-CMP-8323] negotiated on the basis of cost with organizations other than educational institutions, State and local governments, and nonprofit organizations except those exempted under OMB Uniform Guidance at 2 CFR part 200, appendix VIII [@CL-CMP-2712] for construction management or construction, alteration or repair of buildings, bridges, roads, or other kinds of real property; it also includes architect-engineer contracts related to construction projects, and it does not include contracts for vessels, aircraft, or other kinds of personal property [@CL-CMP-2711].

Except as otherwise provided in the allowability paragraph, the cost principles and procedures in subpart 31.2 shall be used in pricing contracts and contract modifications in this category if cost analysis is performed as required by FAR 15.404-1(c) [@CL-CMP-2713]. In addition, the contracting officer shall incorporate those principles, as modified by paragraph (d), by reference in contracts in this category as the basis for the purposes the section lists [@CL-CMP-2714]. One listed purpose is determining reimbursable costs under cost-reimbursement contracts, including cost-reimbursement subcontracts thereunder [@CL-CMP-2772]. Four further listed purposes are negotiating indirect cost rates, proposing, negotiating, or determining costs under terminated contracts, price revision of fixed-price incentive contracts, and pricing changes and other contract modifications [@CL-CMP-2771]. Except as otherwise provided in paragraph (d), the allowability of costs for construction and architect-engineer contracts shall be determined in accordance with subpart 31.2 [@CL-CMP-2715]. Advance agreements for such items as home office overhead, partners' compensation, employment of consultants, and equipment usage costs are described as particularly important in construction and architect-engineer contracts [@CL-CMP-2716]; see [Advance agreements on particular costs](../cas-allowability/cmp-advance-agreements.md).

## How it works

### Equipment

As used in the section, construction equipment means equipment, including marine equipment, in sound workable condition, owned or controlled by the contractor or a subcontractor at any tier or obtained from a commercial rental source, and furnished for use under Government contracts [@CL-CMP-2717]. Allowable ownership and operating costs shall be determined as follows: actual cost data shall be used when such data can be determined for both ownership and operating costs for each piece of equipment, or groups of similar serial or series equipment, from the contractor's accounting records [@CL-CMP-2718]. When such costs cannot be so determined, the contracting agency may specify the use of a particular schedule of predetermined rates or any part thereof to determine ownership and operating costs of construction equipment [@CL-CMP-2719]. However, costs otherwise unallowable under this part shall not become allowable through the use of any schedule [@CL-CMP-2720].

### Schedules of predetermined rates

The section gives as examples that schedules need to be adjusted for Government contract costing purposes if they are based on replacement cost, include unallowable interest costs, or use improper cost of money rates or computations [@CL-CMP-2766]. It states that the allowance for ownership costs should include the cost of depreciation and may include facilities capital cost of money, and that the allowance for operating costs may include costs for such items as fuel, filters, oil, and grease; servicing, repairs, and maintenance; and tire wear and repair [@CL-CMP-2767]. Costs of labor, mobilization, demobilization, overhead, and profit are generally not reflected in predetermined schedules, and separate consideration may be necessary [@CL-CMP-2768]. When a schedule of predetermined use rates for construction equipment is used to determine direct costs, all costs of equipment that are included in the cost allowances provided by the schedule shall be identified and eliminated from the contractor's other direct and indirect costs charged to the contract [@CL-CMP-2721; @CL-CMP-7777]. In periods of suspension of work pursuant to a contract clause, the allowance for equipment ownership shall not exceed an amount for standby cost as determined by the schedule or contract provision [@CL-CMP-2765].

### Rented equipment

Reasonable costs of renting construction equipment are allowable (but see paragraph (C) below) [@CL-CMP-2722]. Costs, such as maintenance and minor or running repairs incident to operating such rented equipment, that are not included in the rental rate are allowable [@CL-CMP-2722]. Costs incident to major repair and overhaul of rental equipment are unallowable, and the allowability of charges for construction equipment rented from any division, subsidiary, or organization under common control will be determined in accordance with FAR 31.205-36(b)(3) [@CL-CMP-2723].

### Job-site and temporary facility costs

The section lists superintendence, timekeeping and clerical work, engineering, utility costs, supplies, material handling, and restoration and cleanup as examples of costs incurred at the job site incident to performing the work [@CL-CMP-2762]. Job-site costs of those kinds are allowable as direct or indirect costs, provided the accounting practice used is in accordance with the contractor's established and consistently followed cost accounting practices for all work [@CL-CMP-2763]. Rental and other costs of acquiring the temporary use of land, structures, and facilities, and costs of constructing or fabricating structures and facilities of a temporary nature, are allowable, in each case less any applicable credits [@CL-CMP-2764].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers must use the RFO FAR part 31 text in place of the codified text [@CL-CMP-9350], effective immediately under Revision 1 signed on 2026-08-04 [@CL-CMP-9351]. Under RFO FAR 31.105(a), the category includes all contracts and contract modifications negotiated on the basis of cost with organizations other than educational institutions, State and local governments, and nonprofit organizations except those exempted under OMB Uniform Guidance at 2 CFR part 200, appendix VIII [@CL-CMP-9570] for construction management or construction, alteration or repair of buildings, bridges, roads, or other kinds of real property; it also includes architect-engineer contracts related to construction projects, and it does not include contracts for vessels, aircraft, or other kinds of personal property [@CL-CMP-9569]. Under RFO FAR 31.105(e), actual cost data must be used when such data can be determined for both ownership and operating costs for each piece of equipment, or groups of similar serial or series equipment, from the contractor's accounting records [@CL-CMP-9577]. The allowance for ownership costs should include the cost of depreciation and may include facilities capital cost of money, and the allowance for operating costs may include costs for such items as fuel, filters, oil, and grease; servicing, repairs, and maintenance; and tire wear and repair [@CL-CMP-9581]. Costs of labor, mobilization, demobilization, overhead, and profit are generally not reflected in schedules, and separate consideration may be necessary [@CL-CMP-9582]. When a schedule of predetermined use rates for construction equipment is used to determine direct costs, all costs of equipment that are included in the cost allowances provided by the schedule must be identified and eliminated from the contractor's other direct and indirect costs charged to the contract [@CL-CMP-9583]. In periods of suspension of work pursuant to a contract clause, the allowance for equipment ownership must not exceed an amount for standby cost as determined by the schedule or contract provision [@CL-CMP-9584]. Costs incident to major repair and overhaul of rental equipment are unallowable [@CL-CMP-9586]. Costs incurred at the job site, in performance of the work, such as the cost of superintendence, timekeeping and clerical work, engineering, utility costs, supplies, material handling, restoration and cleanup, etc., are allowable as direct or indirect costs, provided the accounting practice used is in accordance with the contractor's established and consistently followed cost accounting practices for all work [@CL-CMP-9587; @CL-CMP-9588]. Rental and any other costs, less any applicable credits, incurred in acquiring the temporary use of land, structures, and facilities are allowable, and costs, less any applicable credits, incurred in constructing or fabricating structures and facilities of a temporary nature are allowable [@CL-CMP-9589]. In RFO FAR 31.105 the definition of construction equipment is paragraph (b) [@CL-CMP-9571], the pricing rule is paragraph (c) [@CL-CMP-9572], the incorporation rule is paragraph (d) [@CL-CMP-9573; @CL-CMP-9574], and the allowability rules are in paragraph (e) [@CL-CMP-9575]. The RFO pricing rule applies except as specified in paragraph (d) [@CL-CMP-9572], and the RFO incorporation rule refers to subpart 31.2 as modified by paragraph (d) [@CL-CMP-9573], where the codified text refers to paragraph (d) as its allowability paragraph [@CL-CMP-2713; @CL-CMP-2714]. RFO FAR 31.105(d)(1) reads cost-reimbursement contracts and subcontracts, where codified FAR 31.105(c)(1) reads cost-reimbursement contracts, including cost-reimbursement subcontracts thereunder [@CL-CMP-9573; @CL-CMP-2772]. RFO FAR 31.105(e)(1)(i) provides an exception when an advance agreement is used to address costs, such as home office overhead, partner's compensation, employment of consultants, and equipment usage costs due to varying factors of construction projects (see 31.110) [@CL-CMP-9575; @CL-CMP-9576], where codified FAR 31.105(d)(1) says advance agreements as set forth in 31.109 for such items are particularly important in construction and architect-engineer contracts [@CL-CMP-2716]. Costs otherwise unallowable under this part must not become allowable through the use of an advance agreement (see 31.110(c)) [@CL-CMP-9579], where the codified text says they shall not become allowable through the use of any schedule [@CL-CMP-2720]. Schedules need to be adjusted for Government contract costing purposes if they are based on replacement cost, include unallowable interest costs, or use improper cost of money rates or computations [@CL-CMP-9580]. RFO FAR 31.105(e)(1)(iii) opens by saying the following costs of renting construction equipment must be treated as follows [@CL-CMP-9585], where codified FAR 31.105(d)(2)(ii) opens by saying reasonable costs of renting construction equipment are allowable (but see paragraph (C) below) [@CL-CMP-2722]. ## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor owns an excavator used on a cost-reimbursement construction contract. Its accounting records support actual ownership and operating cost of 30,000 for the machine on the job. Actual cost data can be determined for both, so they are used and no schedule is needed. The answer: use the actual cost data, not a schedule rate.

The same contractor incurs 8,000 of job-site superintendence and 2,000 of rental for temporary land, less a 500 credit. The answer: the rental cost less the credit, 1,500, is allowable. The superintendence is allowable as a direct or indirect cost if it is accounted for under the contractor's established and consistently followed practice for all work.

## Never confuse

Costs otherwise unallowable under this part shall not become allowable through the use of any schedule [@CL-CMP-2720]; the contracting agency may specify a schedule of predetermined rates to determine ownership and operating costs of construction equipment when such costs cannot be determined from actual cost data [@CL-CMP-2719]. Job-site costs are allowable as direct or indirect costs, provided the accounting practice used is in accordance with the contractor's established and consistently followed cost accounting practices for all work [@CL-CMP-2763]. The commercial-organization category covers all contracts and contract modifications for supplies, services, or experimental, developmental, or research work negotiated with organizations other than educational institutions, construction and architect-engineer contracts, State and local governments and nonprofit organizations on the basis of cost [@CL-CMP-2706; @CL-CMP-2707]; see [Cost principles for contracts with commercial organizations](../cas-allowability/cmp-cost-principles-commercial-organizations.md). For how costs that are unallowable are identified and excluded, see [Accounting for unallowable and directly associated costs](../cas-allowability/cmp-accounting-for-unallowable-and-associated-costs.md). For rental costs under the general cost principles, see [Allowable rental costs](../cas-allowability/cmp-rental-costs.md), and for the allowability tests, see [Factors determining cost allowability](../cas-allowability/cmp-allowability-factors.md).

## Company calibration

Whether the company holds construction or architect-engineer contracts, and whether advance agreements cover equipment usage and home office overhead. [@Q-043]
