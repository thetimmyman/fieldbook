## Purpose

Shows which corporate structure and capital raising expenditures are unallowable as organization costs, and what the compensation carve-out takes out of that bar.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-0357] |
| Codified citation | FAR 31.205-27(a)(1) [@CL-CMP-0357] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 31.205-27(a)(1)(i) to (iii) [@CL-CMP-8569; @CL-CMP-8570; @CL-CMP-8571] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-0357] |
| Applies to | Not established. |

Except for the compensation carve-out, expenditures in connection with each of the following are unallowable: planning or executing the organization or reorganization of the corporate structure, mergers and acquisitions, resisting or planning to resist a reorganization or a change in controlling interest, and raising capital measured as net worth plus long-term liabilities [@CL-CMP-0357; @CL-CMP-0358; @CL-CMP-0359].

The bar reaches the surrounding professional fees, including incorporation fees and the costs of attorneys, accountants, brokers, promoters, organizers, management consultants and investment counselors, whether or not employees of the contractor [@CL-CMP-0360]. The wording "include but are not limited to" means the list is not exhaustive. It also reaches a change in financial structure that alters the rights and interests of security holders, but not the administrative costs of short-term borrowings for working capital, and whether or not additional capital is raised [@CL-CMP-0361]. Spend whose primary purpose is compensation is not organization cost at all: the section sends it to the compensation principle, with stock acquired for executive bonuses, employee savings plans and employee stock ownership plans as its printed examples [@CL-CMP-0362; @CL-CMP-0363].

## How it works

Except for the compensation carve-out, creating or restructuring the entity, resisting a reorganization or change in control, and raising capital are all unallowable [@CL-CMP-0357; @CL-CMP-0358; @CL-CMP-0359]. The listed advisers' fees and costs are described as among the expenditures in connection with those events [@CL-CMP-0360]. The carve-out then redirects compensation spend to the compensation principle, which decides it on its own terms [@CL-CMP-0362].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 31 text in place of the codified text [@CL-CMP-9350], effective immediately under Revision 1 signed on 2026-08-04 [@CL-CMP-9351]. The RFO section 31.205-27 keeps the unallowable expenditures, except as provided in paragraph (b), for organization or reorganization of the corporate structure [@CL-CMP-8569], for resisting or planning to resist a reorganization or a change in controlling interest [@CL-CMP-8570], and for raising capital [@CL-CMP-8571], numbered as items (i) to (iii) of paragraph (a)(1). In paragraph (a)(3) it keeps the rule that unallowable reorganization costs include the cost of any change in the contractor's financial structure, excluding administrative costs of short-term borrowings for working capital, resulting in alterations in the rights and interests of security holders, whether or not additional capital is raised [@CL-CMP-8573]. It keeps the carve-out that the cost of activities primarily intended to provide compensation will not be considered organizational costs subject to this section but will be governed by 31.205-6 [@CL-CMP-8574], with acquiring stock for executive bonuses, employee savings plans and employee stock ownership plans as examples [@CL-CMP-8575]. Its paragraph (a)(2) says such expenditures include incorporation fees and costs of attorneys, accountants, brokers, promoters and organizers, management consultants and investment counselors, whether or not employees of the contractor [@CL-CMP-8572].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

The company pays 400,000 to advisers on an acquisition, 60,000 to resist a change of control, and 20,000 in fees on a short-term working capital line. The advisory and defence costs are unallowable organization costs, and the 20,000 of short-term borrowing administration is excluded from the bar.

## Never confuse

The compensation carve-out is easily mistaken for a general capital-transaction exception. Buying stock for an executive bonus or an employee stock ownership plan looks like a capital transaction, but the section treats it as compensation instead. Unallowable balances of this kind are cleared out before they reach a rate, as set out in [Penalties for unallowable costs in final rate proposals](cmp-unallowable-cost-penalty.md).

The codified sentence on professional fees says such expenditures include but are not limited to the listed fees and costs [@CL-CMP-0360], while the RFO paragraph (a)(2) says that such expenditures include them, without the words "but are not limited to" [@CL-CMP-8572].
