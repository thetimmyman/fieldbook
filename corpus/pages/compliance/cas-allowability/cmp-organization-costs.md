## Purpose

Shows which corporate structure and capital raising expenditures are unallowable as organization costs, and what the compensation carve-out takes out of that bar.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-0357] |
| Codified citation | FAR 31.205-27(a)(1) [@CL-CMP-0357] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 31.205-27(a)(1)(i) [@CL-CMP-0357] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-0357] |
| Applies to | Not established. |

Organization costs are unallowable: planning or executing the organization or reorganization of the corporate structure, mergers and acquisitions, resisting or planning to resist a reorganization or a change in controlling interest, and raising capital measured as net worth plus long-term liabilities [@CL-CMP-0357; @CL-CMP-0358; @CL-CMP-0359].

The bar reaches the surrounding professional fees, including incorporation fees and the costs of attorneys, accountants, brokers, promoters, organizers, management consultants and investment counselors, whether or not they are employees [@CL-CMP-0360]. It also reaches a change in financial structure that alters the rights and interests of security holders, but not the administrative costs of short-term borrowings for working capital [@CL-CMP-0361]. Spend whose primary purpose is compensation is not organization cost at all: the section sends it to the compensation principle, with stock acquired for executive bonuses, employee savings plans and employee stock ownership plans as its printed examples [@CL-CMP-0362; @CL-CMP-0363].

## How it works

Except for the compensation carve-out, creating or restructuring the entity, resisting a reorganization or change in control, and raising capital are all unallowable [@CL-CMP-0357; @CL-CMP-0358; @CL-CMP-0359]. Advisers' bills travel with the transaction they serve, so the fees are unallowable where the underlying event is [@CL-CMP-0360]. The carve-out then redirects compensation spend to the compensation principle, which decides it on its own terms [@CL-CMP-0362].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

The company pays 400,000 to advisers on an acquisition, 60,000 to resist a change of control, and 20,000 in fees on a short-term working capital line. The advisory and defence costs are unallowable organization costs, and the 20,000 of short-term borrowing administration is excluded from the bar.

## Never confuse

The compensation carve-out is easily mistaken for a general capital-transaction exception. Buying stock for an executive bonus or an employee stock ownership plan looks like a capital transaction, but the section treats it as compensation instead. Unallowable balances of this kind are cleared out before they reach a rate, as set out in [Penalties for unallowable costs in final rate proposals](cmp-unallowable-cost-penalty.md).
