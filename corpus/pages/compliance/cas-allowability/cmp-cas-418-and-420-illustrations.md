## Purpose

Walks through the printed illustrations of direct and indirect classification and of the allocation of independent research and development and bid and proposal costs. This card draws out six illustrations on classification and five on the research and proposal costs. The rules are on [Direct and indirect cost classification](../cas-allowability/cmp-cas-418-direct-indirect.md) and [IR&D and B&P cost allocation](../cas-allowability/cmp-cas-420-ird-bp-allocation.md).

In the engineers illustration, the engineers' time is spent in working directly on the production of the goods or services called for by contracts and other final cost objectives [@CL-CMP-2893]. Allocating the labor costs of the engineers as direct labor costs of final cost objectives on the basis of detailed time records is in accordance with the requirements [@CL-CMP-2816]. In the machining and assembling illustration, the machining activity does not have the same or similar beneficial or causal relationship to cost objectives as the assembling activity, and allocating its cost separately would give significantly different results [@CL-CMP-2894]. In that illustration, the business unit's single manufacturing overhead pool is not homogeneous and separate pools must be established [@CL-CMP-2817]. In the occupancy illustration, the contractor establishes that it is impractical to ascertain a measurement of the consumption of resources in relation to the use of facilities by individual cost objectives [@CL-CMP-2895]. A weighted square foot measure that reflects the different levels of resource consumption of different types of facilities is in conformance with the provisions [@CL-CMP-2818]. In the machining overhead illustration, the machining activity occasionally does significant amounts of work for other activities of the business unit, and the labor used for that work is of the same nature as that used for contract work [@CL-CMP-2896], but the machining labor for other activities is not included in the base used to allocate the overhead costs of the machining activity [@CL-CMP-7064]. The practice described is not in conformance, and the contractor must include the cost of labor doing work for the other activities in the allocation base for the machining activity indirect cost pool [@CL-CMP-2819]. In the flight services illustration, the business unit may revise its rate to the expected amount, and if the accumulated variances are significant it must also adjust the costs previously allocated to reflect the revised rates [@CL-CMP-2820]. In the standard cost illustration, the contractor could, by modifying its practice to comply with the definition of production unit, accumulate the standard costs and variances separately for each of the several categories of direct labor, or alternatively for each of several subdepartments with homogeneous output for each of the subdepartments [@CL-CMP-2821; @CL-CMP-8139].

In the typists illustration, a direct charge of typing cost to a proposal project does not meet the requirements because the department's established accounting practice does not charge typing directly to final cost objectives [@CL-CMP-2822]. In the bid and proposal illustration, because the cost of preparing each individual bid and proposal is not material, accumulating those costs in a single project meets the requirements [@CL-CMP-2823]. In the illustration of research projects that benefit three segments, transferring the project costs to the home office and allocating them to the three segments on the basis of the benefits received meets the requirements [@CL-CMP-2824]. In the five-segment illustration, the project will be of general benefit to all five segments [@CL-CMP-7062], the costs are then allocated to the home office by each segment [@CL-CMP-7063], and the project costs are combined with other independent research and development costs that benefit the company as a whole and are allocated to all five segments by means of the same base by which the company allocates its residual home office expense costs of all segments [@CL-CMP-2899]. In the illustration of home office research and proposal costs that generally benefit all segments except one, the company and the contracting officer agree that the nature of that segment's business activity is such that the home office effort is neither caused by nor provides any benefit to that segment [@CL-CMP-2897]. The company removes the data of the excluded segment from the base used for the allocation of its residual expenses [@CL-CMP-2898].

The exemption section of the direct and indirect classification standard says it shall not apply to contracts and grants with state, local, and Federally recognized Indian tribal governments [@CL-CMP-8664]. The exemptions section of the research and proposal cost standard says it shall not apply to contracts and grants with State, local, and federally recognized Indian tribal governments [@CL-CMP-8665].

## How it works

The classification illustrations turn on records and pool design. In the engineers illustration, detailed time records support allocating the labor costs of engineers who work directly on production as direct labor costs of final cost objectives [@CL-CMP-2893; @CL-CMP-2816]. In the machining and assembling illustration, the machining activity does not have the same or similar beneficial or causal relationship to cost objectives as the assembling activity, and separate pools must be established [@CL-CMP-2894; @CL-CMP-2817]. In the occupancy illustration, where it is impractical to ascertain a measurement of the consumption of resources by individual cost objectives, a weighted square foot measure that reflects the different levels of resource consumption is in conformance with the provisions [@CL-CMP-2895; @CL-CMP-2818]. In the machining overhead illustration, the cost of labor doing work for other activities, which is of the same nature as contract work, must be included in the allocation base [@CL-CMP-2896; @CL-CMP-2819]. In the flight services illustration, the business unit may revise its rate to the expected amount and, if the accumulated variances are significant, must also adjust the costs previously allocated [@CL-CMP-2820]. The research and proposal illustrations turn on established practice, materiality and benefit. In the typists illustration, the direct charge does not meet the requirements because the department's established accounting practice does not charge typing directly to final cost objectives [@CL-CMP-2822]. In the bid and proposal illustration, because the cost of preparing each individual bid and proposal is not material, accumulating those costs in a single project meets the requirements [@CL-CMP-2823]. In the research illustrations, project costs are allocated to the three segments on the basis of the benefits received [@CL-CMP-2824], to all five segments by the base used for residual home office expense costs [@CL-CMP-2899], or with the data of an excluded segment removed from that base when the company and the contracting officer agree that the home office effort neither is caused by nor benefits that segment [@CL-CMP-2897; @CL-CMP-2898].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A unit has a fabrication activity and a testing activity. Testing does not have the same or similar beneficial or causal relationship to cost objectives as fabrication, and allocating its cost separately would give significantly different results. The unit combines both in one overhead pool of 90,000. Following the printed machining and assembling pattern, the pool is not homogeneous, so it sets up two pools. Separately, it collects 40 small proposals, each of which is not material in amount, under one proposal project.

## Never confuse

In the typists illustration the direct charge fails because the department's established accounting practice does not charge typing directly [@CL-CMP-2822]. In the three-segment illustration, the project costs are allocated to the three segments on the basis of the benefits received [@CL-CMP-2824], and in the illustration of home office costs that generally benefit all segments except one, a segment that neither causes nor benefits from the home office effort is removed from the allocation base when the company and the contracting officer agree on that [@CL-CMP-2897; @CL-CMP-2898]. Illustrations of home office pools and allocation bases are on [Illustrations of home office pools and allocation bases](../cas-allowability/cmp-cas-403-illustrations-and-interpretation.md). Same-purpose consistency in charging direct and indirect costs is on [Definitions, illustrations and interpretation on consistency in allocating costs incurred for the same purpose](../cas-allowability/cmp-cas-402-definitions-illustrations-and-interpretation.md), and the general and administrative pool and base on [Illustrations of the general and administrative pool and cost input base](../cas-allowability/cmp-cas-410-illustrations.md). For the rules themselves, see [Direct and indirect cost classification](../cas-allowability/cmp-cas-418-direct-indirect.md) and [IR&D and B&P cost allocation](../cas-allowability/cmp-cas-420-ird-bp-allocation.md).
