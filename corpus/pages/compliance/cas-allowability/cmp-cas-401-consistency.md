## Purpose

A contractor's cost estimating practices and its cost accounting practices for accumulating and reporting costs must remain consistent.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | CAS [@CL-CMP-0201] |
| Codified citation | 48 CFR 9904.401-40(a) [@CL-CMP-0201] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-0201] |
| Applies to | A contractor's estimating practices and cost accounting practices [@CL-CMP-0201] |

A contractor must price a proposal with estimating practices consistent with the practices used to accumulate and report costs [@CL-CMP-0201]. The estimating practices and the accumulating and reporting practices must also be consistent as to the direct-or-indirect classification of cost elements or functions, the indirect cost pools to which each is charged or proposed to be charged, and the methods of allocating indirect costs to the contract [@CL-CMP-0202]. Grouping homogeneous costs together in an estimate is not by itself an inconsistency when those costs are accumulated and reported in greater detail on an actual cost basis during contract performance [@CL-CMP-0203].

## How it works

Consistency is judged at the date of award, or at the date of final agreement on price when certified cost or pricing data are submitted. Grouping homogeneous costs is allowed to cover cases where it is not practicable to estimate contract costs by individual cost element or function, provided the costs are accumulated and reported in greater detail on an actual cost basis during contract performance. Any significant cost must be presented so it can be compared with the actual cost accumulated and reported for it. During performance a contractor may change an established practice, but only under the cost accounting standards change rules.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor estimates a proposal treating an engineer's time as a direct cost. After award it accumulates the same engineer's time in an indirect overhead pool. The estimate and the records disagree on the direct-or-indirect classification, so the practices are inconsistent.

## Never confuse

Consistency between estimating and accumulating and reporting is not the same as the same-purpose rule, which requires costs incurred for the same purpose in like circumstances to be either direct or indirect only. The first governs agreement between the estimate and the records; the second governs how one cost is classified.

## Company calibration

Whether each business unit is subject to full CAS coverage, modified coverage or neither, and whether a Disclosure Statement is on file. [@Q-033]
