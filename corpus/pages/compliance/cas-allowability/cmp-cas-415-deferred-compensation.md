## Purpose

Sets the period in which deferred compensation cost is charged and the measure used for it.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified CAS [@CL-CMP-0570] |
| Codified citation | 48 CFR 9904.415-40 [@CL-CMP-0570] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-0570] |
| Applies to | Awards of deferred compensation, including ESOP contributions [@CL-CMP-0572] |

Deferred compensation cost is assigned to the period in which the contractor incurs the obligation to compensate the employee [@CL-CMP-0570]. The obligation is deemed incurred when all of the listed conditions have been met [@CL-CMP-0838; @CL-CMP-0840; @CL-CMP-0841; @CL-CMP-0842]. However, for an award that requires the employee to perform future service in order to receive the benefits, the obligation is deemed to have been incurred as the future service is performed, for the part of the award attributable to that service [@CL-CMP-0839]. If any of the conditions is not met, the cost is assignable only to the cost accounting period or periods in which the compensation is paid to the employee [@CL-CMP-0843]. If no obligation is incurred before payment, the cost is the amount paid and is assigned to the period in which the payment is made [@CL-CMP-0571]. For deferred compensation other than an ESOP the cost is the present value of the future benefits to be paid by the contractor; for an ESOP it is the amount contributed to the ESOP by the contractor [@CL-CMP-0572; @CL-CMP-0573]. The cost of each award is considered separately for measurement and assignment to cost accounting periods [@CL-CMP-8114]. However, if the cost for the employees covered by a plan can be measured and assigned with reasonable accuracy on a group basis, separate computations for each employee are not required [@CL-CMP-0574].

## How it works

The cost is assigned to the cost accounting period in which the contractor incurs an obligation to compensate the employee [@CL-CMP-0570]. The contractor is deemed to have incurred that obligation when all of these conditions have been met: a requirement to make future payments that the contractor cannot unilaterally avoid, an award to be satisfied by a future payment of money, other assets or shares of contractor stock, an amount of future payment that can be measured with reasonable accuracy, a known recipient, if the terms of the award require events before an employee is entitled to the benefits, a reasonable probability that those events will occur, and, for stock options, a reasonable probability that the options ultimately will be exercised [@CL-CMP-0838; @CL-CMP-0840; @CL-CMP-0841; @CL-CMP-0842]. For awards that require future service, the obligation is deemed incurred as the future service is performed for the part of the award attributable to it [@CL-CMP-0839]. If any condition is not met, the cost is assignable only to the cost accounting period or periods in which the compensation is paid [@CL-CMP-0843]. Measurement differs by kind of award: present value of the future benefits for deferred compensation other than ESOPs, and the amount contributed by the contractor for an ESOP [@CL-CMP-0572; @CL-CMP-0573]. Each award is considered separately, and separate computations for each employee are not required where group measurement and assignment can be made with reasonable accuracy [@CL-CMP-8114; @CL-CMP-0574].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A company grants an award with no service requirement, and every recognition condition is met on the grant date. Future benefits are 500,000, payable in later years, and the present value at the obligation date is 400,000. The cost assigned to the period of obligation is 400,000, not the 500,000 eventually paid.

If the same award instead required three more years of service, the 400,000 would be recognized as that service is performed. If a condition, such as a reasonable probability that a required event occurs, were not met, the cost would be assignable only to the period or periods in which the compensation is paid.

## Never confuse

For deferred compensation other than ESOPs, the cost is the present value of the future benefits to be paid by the contractor [@CL-CMP-0572]. For an ESOP, the cost is the amount contributed to the ESOP by the contractor [@CL-CMP-0573].
