## Purpose

Sets the period in which deferred compensation cost is charged and the measure used for it.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified CAS [@CL-CMP-0570] |
| Codified citation | 48 CFR 9904.415-40 [@CL-CMP-0570] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-0570] |
| Applies to | Awards of deferred compensation, including ESOP contributions [@CL-CMP-0572] |

Deferred compensation cost is assigned to the period in which the contractor incurs the obligation to compensate the employee [@CL-CMP-0570]. The obligation is deemed incurred when all of the listed conditions have been met [@CL-CMP-0838; @CL-CMP-0840; @CL-CMP-0841; @CL-CMP-0842]. For an award that requires future service, the obligation is deemed incurred as that service is performed, for the part of the award attributable to it [@CL-CMP-0839]. If any condition is not met, the cost is assignable only to the period or periods in which the compensation is paid [@CL-CMP-0843; @CL-CMP-0571]. For awards other than an ESOP the cost is the present value of the future benefits to be paid; for an ESOP it is the amount contributed [@CL-CMP-0572; @CL-CMP-0573]. Each award is measured and assigned separately, though group measurement is allowed where it is reasonably accurate for the employees covered [@CL-CMP-0574].

## How it works

The trigger is the incurred obligation, not the date of the promise and not the cash payment [@CL-CMP-0570]. An obligation is incurred only when every listed condition is met: a future payment the contractor cannot unilaterally avoid, an award settled in money, other assets or contractor stock, an amount that can be measured with reasonable accuracy, a known recipient, a reasonable probability that any required events occur, and, for stock options, a reasonable probability of exercise [@CL-CMP-0838; @CL-CMP-0840; @CL-CMP-0841; @CL-CMP-0842]. Where the employee must perform future service to earn the award, the cost is recognized as the service is performed, not all at once when the award is made [@CL-CMP-0839]. Where a condition is not met, assignment is deferred and the cost sits in the period of payment, measured by the amount paid [@CL-CMP-0843; @CL-CMP-0571]. Measurement differs by vehicle: present value for ordinary awards, contribution amount for an ESOP [@CL-CMP-0572; @CL-CMP-0573]. The separate-award rule gives way to group treatment where group measurement stays reasonably accurate [@CL-CMP-0574].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A company grants an award with no service requirement, and every recognition condition is met on the grant date. Future benefits are 500,000, payable in later years, and the present value at the obligation date is 400,000. The cost assigned to the period of obligation is 400,000, not the 500,000 eventually paid.

If the same award instead required three more years of service, the 400,000 would be recognized as that service is performed. If a condition, such as a reasonable probability that a required event occurs, were not met, the cost would wait until payment and be assigned to the payment period at the amount paid.

## Never confuse

Deferred compensation for an ordinary award is measured as a present value, which is not the cash the employee will eventually receive [@CL-CMP-0572]. An ESOP contribution is measured at the amount contributed, so no present value step is applied to it [@CL-CMP-0573].
