## Purpose

Defines allowable advertising categories and establishes the unallowability of promotional and image-enhancing public relations expenses.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-0301] |
| Codified citation | FAR 31.205-1 [@CL-CMP-0301] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 31.205-1 [@CL-CMP-0301] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-0301] |
| Applies to | Government contracts subject to the cost principles [@CL-CMP-0301] |

The only allowable advertising costs are those specifically required by contract or arising from Government contract requirements exclusively for acquiring scarce items or disposing of scrap or surplus materials, certain export-promotion activity costs, and costs allowable under the cross-referenced cost principle [@CL-CMP-0301; @CL-CMP-0302]. Costs of activities to promote sales of products normally sold to the Government, including trade shows that contain a significant effort to promote exports from the United States, are allowable, but they do not include memorabilia, alcoholic beverages, entertainment, or facilities used primarily for entertainment rather than product promotion; public relations and advertising costs other than those specified in paragraphs (d) and (e), whose primary purpose is to promote sales by stimulating interest in a product or product line or to enhance the company image to sell its products or services, are unallowable [@CL-CMP-0302; @CL-CMP-0303].

## How it works

The allowable advertising categories are limited to those in paragraph (d): contract-required advertising exclusively for acquiring scarce items or disposing of scrap or surplus materials, certain export-promotion activities, and costs allowable under the cross-referenced principle [@CL-CMP-0301]. Public relations and advertising costs other than those specified in paragraphs (d) and (e), whose primary purpose is to stimulate interest in a product or to enhance the company image to sell the company's products or services, are unallowable [@CL-CMP-0303]. Trade shows for products normally sold to the government are allowable if they contain a significant effort to promote exports from the United States, but the allowance does not include memorabilia, alcoholic beverages, entertainment, or physical facilities used primarily for entertainment rather than product promotion [@CL-CMP-0302]. Allowable public relations costs include costs specifically required by contract, responding to inquiries and communicating with the public, press, stockholders, creditors and customers (limited to necessary communication on matters of public concern), community service participation, plant tours and open houses, and certain ceremonies to the extent provided for by contract. The unallowable public relations and advertising costs are those listed in paragraph (f).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor incurs 40,000 for advertising that its contract specifically requires and that is exclusively for acquiring a scarce item for contract performance, and 60,000 for brand awareness advertising in general aviation magazines. The 40,000 is allowable because it is specifically required by the contract and exclusively for acquiring a scarce item. The 60,000 for general brand enhancement is unallowable and must be removed from indirect expense pools.

## Never confuse

Advertising specifically required by contract is allowable only if exclusively for acquiring scarce items or disposing of scrap or surplus materials, whereas other advertising whose primary purpose is to stimulate interest in a product or promote company image is unallowable [@CL-CMP-0301; @CL-CMP-0303]. Activities to promote sales of products normally sold to the government, including trade shows containing a significant effort to promote exports, are allowable, whereas memorabilia, alcoholic beverages, entertainment, and physical facilities used primarily for entertainment are excluded [@CL-CMP-0302]. See [Unallowable-cost penalties](cmp-unallowable-cost-penalty.md) for the consequences of including unallowable costs in indirect cost proposals.
