## Purpose

Defines allowable advertising categories and establishes the unallowability of promotional and image-enhancing public relations expenses.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-0301] |
| Codified citation | FAR 31.205-1 [@CL-CMP-0301] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 31.205-1 [@CL-CMP-8576; @CL-CMP-8578; @CL-CMP-8919] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-0301] |
| Applies to | Government contracts subject to the cost principles [@CL-CMP-0301] |

The only allowable advertising costs are those specifically required by contract or arising from Government contract requirements exclusively for acquiring scarce items or disposing of scrap or surplus materials, certain export-promotion activity costs, and costs allowable under the cross-referenced cost principle [@CL-CMP-0301; @CL-CMP-0302; @CL-CMP-0815; @CL-CMP-0816; @CL-CMP-0817]. Costs of activities to promote sales of products normally sold to the Government, including trade shows that contain a significant effort to promote exports from the United States, are allowable, but they do not include memorabilia, alcoholic beverages, entertainment, or facilities used primarily for entertainment rather than product promotion; public relations and advertising costs other than those specified in codified paragraphs (d) and (e), whose primary purpose is to promote sales by stimulating interest in a product or product line (except for costs made allowable under 31.205-38(b)(5)), or by disseminating messages calling favorable attention to the contractor for purposes of enhancing the company image to sell the company's products or services, are unallowable [@CL-CMP-0302; @CL-CMP-0303; @CL-CMP-7067; @CL-CMP-0816; @CL-CMP-0817].

## How it works

The allowable advertising categories are limited to those in codified paragraph (d): contract-required advertising exclusively for acquiring scarce items or disposing of scrap or surplus materials, certain export-promotion activities, and costs allowable under the cross-referenced principle [@CL-CMP-0301; @CL-CMP-0815; @CL-CMP-0817]. Public relations and advertising costs other than those specified in codified paragraphs (d) and (e), whose primary purpose is to stimulate interest in a product or product line (except for costs made allowable under 31.205-38(b)(5)), or by disseminating messages calling favorable attention to the contractor for purposes of enhancing the company image to sell the company's products or services, are unallowable [@CL-CMP-0303; @CL-CMP-7067]. Trade shows for products normally sold to the government are allowable if they contain a significant effort to promote exports from the United States, but the allowance does not include memorabilia, alcoholic beverages, entertainment, or physical facilities used primarily for entertainment rather than product promotion [@CL-CMP-0302; @CL-CMP-0816; @CL-CMP-0817]. Allowable public relations costs include costs specifically required by contract, and costs of responding to inquiries on company policies and activities and of communicating with the public, press, stockholders, creditors, and customers, as the first items of the codified paragraph (e) list [@CL-CMP-8817]. The unallowable public relations and advertising costs are those listed in codified paragraph (f) [@CL-CMP-8818].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 31 text in place of the codified text [@CL-CMP-9350], effective immediately under Revision 1 signed on 2026-08-04 [@CL-CMP-9351]. The RFO section 31.205-1 keeps the rule that the only allowable advertising costs are those in its paragraph (c): costs specifically required by contract, or that arise from requirements of Government contracts, and that are exclusively for acquiring scarce items or disposing of scrap or surplus materials [@CL-CMP-8576; @CL-CMP-8577]; costs of activities to promote sales of products normally sold to the Government, including trade shows with a significant effort to promote exports [@CL-CMP-8578]; and costs allowable in accordance with 31.205-34 [@CL-CMP-8916]. Paragraph (c)(2) says the export-promotion costs are allowable notwithstanding paragraphs (e)(1), (e)(3), (e)(4)(ii), and (e)(5) [@CL-CMP-8578], and keeps the exclusion of memorabilia, alcoholic beverages, entertainment and physical facilities used primarily for entertainment [@CL-CMP-8916]. The allowable public relations costs begin in paragraph (d) [@CL-CMP-8917], and the unallowable costs are listed in paragraph (e) [@CL-CMP-8918]. Paragraph (e)(1) makes unallowable public relations and advertising costs whose primary purpose is to promote the sale of products or services by stimulating interest in a product or product line, except for costs made allowable under 31.205-38(b)(5) [@CL-CMP-8919], and costs of disseminating messages calling favorable attention to the contractor to enhance the company image [@CL-CMP-8920].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor incurs 40,000 for advertising that its contract specifically requires and that is exclusively for acquiring a scarce item for contract performance, and 60,000 for brand awareness advertising in general aviation magazines. The 40,000 is allowable because it is specifically required by the contract and exclusively for acquiring a scarce item. The 60,000 for general brand enhancement is unallowable and must be removed from indirect expense pools.

## Never confuse

Advertising specifically required by contract is within the allowable category when it is exclusively for acquiring scarce items or disposing of scrap or surplus materials, whereas other advertising whose primary purpose is to stimulate interest in a product (except for costs made allowable under 31.205-38(b)(5)), or by disseminating messages calling favorable attention to the contractor for purposes of enhancing the company image to sell the company's products or services, is unallowable [@CL-CMP-0301; @CL-CMP-0303; @CL-CMP-7067; @CL-CMP-0815]. Activities to promote sales of products normally sold to the government, including trade shows containing a significant effort to promote exports, are allowable, whereas memorabilia, alcoholic beverages, entertainment, and physical facilities used primarily for entertainment are excluded [@CL-CMP-0302; @CL-CMP-0816; @CL-CMP-0817]. See [Unallowable-cost penalties](cmp-unallowable-cost-penalty.md) for the consequences of including unallowable costs in indirect cost proposals.

Paragraph letters differ between the texts: the codified text lists the only allowable advertising costs in paragraph (d) [@CL-CMP-0301] and the unallowable costs in paragraph (f) [@CL-CMP-0303], while the RFO text uses paragraphs (c) and (e) [@CL-CMP-8576; @CL-CMP-8919]. The codified paragraph (f)(1) begins with all public relations and advertising costs, other than those specified in paragraphs (d) and (e) [@CL-CMP-0303], and the RFO paragraph (e)(1) prints no such words [@CL-CMP-8919]. The codified paragraph (f)(4) letters the ceremonies as (i) corporate celebrations and (ii) new product announcements [@CL-CMP-8580], and the codified export-promotion allowance cites paragraph (f)(4)(ii) [@CL-CMP-0816], whereas the RFO paragraph (e)(4) prints the ceremonies as one item with no lettered parts [@CL-CMP-8579] and the RFO paragraph (c)(2) still cites paragraph (e)(4)(ii) [@CL-CMP-8578].
