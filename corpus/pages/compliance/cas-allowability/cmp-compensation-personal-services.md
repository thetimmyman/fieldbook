## Purpose

Compensation for personal services is allowable subject to the cost principle's general criteria and additional requirements, including current-year work, reasonableness, and conformity to an established compensation plan or practice.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-0451] |
| Codified citation | FAR 31.205-6 [@CL-CMP-0451] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 31.205-6 [@CL-CMP-9939] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-0451] |
| Applies to | Compensation for personal services [@CL-CMP-0451] |

Compensation for personal services must be for work performed in the current year and must not be a retroactive adjustment of prior years' salaries or wages, subject to the cross-referenced exceptions in paragraphs (g), (h), (j), (k), (m), and (o) of the codified cost principle [@CL-CMP-0451]. The total compensation for individual employees or job classes must be reasonable for the work performed [@CL-CMP-0452]. No presumption of allowability exists where the contractor introduces major revisions of existing compensation plans or new plans and has not given the cognizant ACO, either before implementation or within a reasonable period after it, an opportunity to review the allowability of the changes [@CL-CMP-0453; @CL-CMP-0823]. For owners of closely held companies, compensation in excess of the costs that are deductible as compensation under the Internal Revenue Code and regulations under it is unallowable [@CL-CMP-0454].

## How it works

The cost principle requires that compensation be for current-year work and not a retroactive adjustment, subject to the cross-referenced exceptions in paragraphs (g), (h), (j), (k), (m), and (o) of the codified cost principle [@CL-CMP-0451]. Reasonableness is tested on total compensation for each employee or job class [@CL-CMP-0452]; conformity with the compensation practices of other firms of the same size, in the same industry, in the same geographic area and engaged in similar non-Government work under comparable circumstances is one factor that may be relevant, and the codified text tells the reader to consider factors determined to be relevant by the contracting officer [@CL-CMP-9607; @CL-CMP-9608]. If a contractor introduces major revisions of existing compensation plans, or new plans, without giving the cognizant ACO an opportunity to review allowability either before implementation or within a reasonable period after it, no presumption of allowability exists [@CL-CMP-0453; @CL-CMP-0823]. Special individuals, including owners of closely held companies, must have compensation that is reasonable and not a distribution of profits [@CL-CMP-9610].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers must use the RFO FAR part 31 text in place of the codified text [@CL-CMP-9350], effective immediately under Revision 1 signed on 2026-08-04 [@CL-CMP-9351]. RFO FAR 31.205-6(a)(1) says compensation for personal services must be for work performed by the employee in the current year and must not represent a retroactive adjustment of prior years' salaries or wages, and lists the exceptions as paragraphs (f), (g), (i), (j), (l) and (n) of that section [@CL-CMP-9939]. RFO paragraph (a)(2) says the total compensation for individual employees or job classes must be reasonable for the work performed, except as otherwise specified in that section [@CL-CMP-9940], where the codified paragraph says that specific restrictions on individual compensation elements apply when prescribed [@CL-CMP-0452]. RFO paragraph (b)(2)(ii) says to consider factors that may be relevant, including conformity with compensation practices of other firms of the same size, in the same industry, in the same geographic area and engaged in similar non-Government work under comparable circumstances [@CL-CMP-9609], and unlike the codified paragraph it does not say that the factors are those determined to be relevant by the contracting officer [@CL-CMP-9607; @CL-CMP-9609]. RFO paragraph (a)(4) keeps the rule that no presumption of allowability exists where major revisions of existing compensation plans, or new plans, have not been put to the cognizant administrative contracting officer for review, either before implementation or within a reasonable period after it [@CL-CMP-9941; @CL-CMP-9942]. RFO paragraph (a)(6)(iii) keeps the rule that for owners of closely held companies, compensation in excess of the costs deductible as compensation under the Internal Revenue Code is unallowable [@CL-CMP-9943].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An employee receives a base salary plus a year-end bonus. The company keeps both within the total compensation its plan pays for comparable jobs in its industry and region. The compensation is allowable subject to being for current-year work, reasonable for the work performed, and based on and in conformity with the established plan or practice. The bonus is allowable provided it is paid or accrued under a good-faith agreement made before the services were rendered or under an established plan or policy followed so consistently as to imply an agreement to make the payment, and its basis is supported.

## Never confuse

Reasonableness of total compensation is not the same as the specific restrictions on individual compensation elements [@CL-CMP-0452; @CL-CMP-9940]. The cost principle requires total compensation to be reasonable and also imposes separate rules, such as the bar on compensation based on changes in the price of corporate securities [@CL-CMP-9611; @CL-CMP-9612]. An unallowable element stays unallowable even if the total looks reasonable [@CL-CMP-0452; @CL-CMP-9940]. Paragraph letters differ between the two texts: the codified current-year rule lists its exceptions in paragraphs (g), (h), (j), (k), (m) and (o) [@CL-CMP-0451], while the RFO text lists paragraphs (f), (g), (i), (j), (l) and (n) [@CL-CMP-9939], so a codified paragraph cite should be checked against the RFO text before it is reused.

## Company calibration

Whether each compensation plan is established and consistent, and whether major plan revisions were shared with the cognizant ACO for allowability review. [@Q-043]
