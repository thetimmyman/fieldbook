## Purpose

Compensation for personal services is allowable only when it meets the cost principle's general criteria, including current-year work, reasonableness, and an established compensation plan.

Compensation for personal services must be for work performed in the current year and must not be a retroactive adjustment of prior years' salaries or wages, subject to the cross-referenced exceptions in paragraphs (g), (h), (j), (k), (m), and (o) of the cost principle [@CL-CMP-0451]. The total compensation for individual employees or job classes must be reasonable for the work performed [@CL-CMP-0452]. No presumption of allowability exists for major revisions of compensation plans when the contractor has not given the cognizant ACO an opportunity to review the changes [@CL-CMP-0453; @CL-CMP-0823]. For owners of closely held companies, compensation in excess of the amount deductible under the Internal Revenue Code is unallowable [@CL-CMP-0454].

## How it works

The cost principle requires that compensation be for current-year work and not a retroactive adjustment, subject to the cross-referenced exceptions in paragraphs (g), (h), (j), (k), (m), and (o) of the cost principle. Reasonableness is tested on total compensation for each employee or job class; conformity with the compensation practices of comparable firms is one factor that may be relevant, among others the contracting officer determines. If a contractor introduces major revisions of existing compensation plans, or new plans, without giving the cognizant ACO an opportunity to review allowability either before implementation or within a reasonable period after it, no presumption of allowability exists [@CL-CMP-0453; @CL-CMP-0823]. Special individuals, including owners of closely held companies, must have compensation that is reasonable and not a distribution of profits.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An employee receives a base salary plus a year-end bonus. The company keeps both within the total compensation its plan pays for comparable jobs in its industry and region. The compensation is allowable only if it is for current-year work, reasonable for the work performed, and in line with the established plan, and the bonus is paid or accrued under a good-faith agreement made before the services were rendered or under an established plan followed consistently, with its basis supported.

## Never confuse

Reasonableness of total compensation is not the same as the specific restrictions on individual compensation elements. The cost principle requires total compensation to be reasonable and also imposes separate rules, such as the bar on compensation based on changes in the price of corporate securities. An unallowable element stays unallowable even if the total looks reasonable.

## Company calibration

Whether each compensation plan is established and consistent, and whether major plan revisions were shared with the cognizant ACO for allowability review. [@Q-043]
