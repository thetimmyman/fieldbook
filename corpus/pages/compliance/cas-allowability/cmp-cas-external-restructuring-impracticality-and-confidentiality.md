## Purpose

Collects three Cost Accounting Standards program provisions: the relief from the price and cost adjustment requirements for compliant practice changes tied to external restructuring, the agency head route when a Disclosure Statement cannot practically be secured, and the protection of a Disclosure Statement that the offeror or contractor says holds privileged and confidential content [@CL-CMP-8685; @CL-CMP-8686; @CL-CMP-8689].

Under 48 CFR 9903.201-8, the contract price and cost adjustment requirements of part 9903 are not applicable to compliant cost accounting practice changes directly associated with external restructuring activities that are subject to and meet the requirements of section 2325 of title 10 of the United States Code [@CL-CMP-8685]. The section names no official who decides whether a change is compliant or whether an activity meets the statute, and it prints no threshold or deadline [@CL-CMP-8685].

Under 48 CFR 9903.202-2, the agency head may determine that it is impractical to secure the Disclosure Statement, although submission is required, and authorize contract award without obtaining the Statement [@CL-CMP-8686]. The agency head shall, within 30 days of having made that determination and authorized award, submit a report to the Cost Accounting Standards Board setting forth all material facts [@CL-CMP-8687]. The authority may not be delegated [@CL-CMP-8688]. The section sets no dollar threshold and states no other exception [@CL-CMP-8686; @CL-CMP-8687; @CL-CMP-8688].

Under 48 CFR 9903.202-4, if the offeror or contractor notifies the contracting officer that the Disclosure Statement contains trade secrets and commercial or financial information, which is privileged and confidential, the Disclosure Statement shall be protected and shall not be released outside the Government [@CL-CMP-8689]. The notice comes from the offeror or contractor and goes to the contracting officer. The section does not say who within the Government protects the Statement or who decides whether the content is in fact privileged and confidential, and it sets no deadline or threshold for the notice [@CL-CMP-8689].

## How it works

The relief in section 9903.201-8 is narrow [@CL-CMP-8685]. It removes the contract price and cost adjustment requirements of part 9903 for compliant practice changes that are directly associated with external restructuring activities and that both are subject to and meet the cited statute, and the section says nothing about practice changes that are not [@CL-CMP-8685].

The impracticality route in section 9903.202-2 belongs to the agency head [@CL-CMP-8686; @CL-CMP-8688]. The agency head decides whether securing the Statement is impractical although submission is required, and may then authorize award without it [@CL-CMP-8686]. That decision carries a reporting duty: a report setting forth all material facts goes to the Cost Accounting Standards Board within 30 days of having made the determination and authorized award [@CL-CMP-8687]. The agency head cannot delegate the authority [@CL-CMP-8688].

The confidentiality rule in section 9903.202-4 starts with the offeror or contractor [@CL-CMP-8689]. Once that party notifies the contracting officer that the Statement contains trade secrets and commercial or financial information, which is privileged and confidential, the Statement shall be protected and shall not be released outside the Government [@CL-CMP-8689].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

On 2026-03-02 an agency head determines that it is impractical to secure the Disclosure Statement for an award of 3,000,000, although submission is required, and authorizes award without obtaining the Statement [@CL-CMP-8686]. The report of all material facts to the Cost Accounting Standards Board is due within 30 days of having done so, that is, by 2026-04-01 on a count of 30 days from 2026-03-02 [@CL-CMP-8687]. A deputy cannot make the determination in the agency head's place, because the authority may not be delegated [@CL-CMP-8688]. The offeror separately notifies the contracting officer that the Statement contains trade secrets and commercial or financial information, which is privileged and confidential, so the Statement shall be protected and shall not be released outside the Government [@CL-CMP-8689].

## Never confuse

Award without a Statement is not the same as an exemption from the Statement requirement. The agency head may authorize award without obtaining the Statement after determining that securing it is impractical, and the section words that determination as one made although submission is required [@CL-CMP-8686]. A separate section of FAR says that, where the agency head has authorized award without obtaining submission, the contractor shall submit the required Disclosure Statement and the cognizant Federal agency official shall determine adequacy as soon as possible after the award [@CL-CMP-7037].

A notice of confidentiality is not a filing relief. Section 9903.202-4 speaks only of protection and non-release outside the Government, and it does not say that the notice changes any filing or reporting duty [@CL-CMP-8689]. The relief for external restructuring is also limited: it removes only the contract price and cost adjustment requirements of part 9903 and says nothing here about other requirements [@CL-CMP-8685].
