## Purpose

Shows the prohibition on using appropriated funds to pay a person to influence Federal transactions, the declaration and disclosure that go with it, and the exceptions with their conditions.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-2450] |
| Codified citation | FAR 3.800 [@CL-CMP-9705]; FAR 3.801, FAR 3.802, FAR 3.803 and FAR 3.804 [@CL-CMP-2324; @CL-CMP-2450; @CL-CMP-2326; @CL-CMP-2455]; FAR 3.805, FAR 3.806, FAR 3.807 and FAR 3.808 [@CL-CMP-9706; @CL-CMP-9707; @CL-CMP-9708; @CL-CMP-9709; @CL-CMP-9710; @CL-CMP-9711] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-2450] |
| Applies to | A recipient of a Federal contract, grant, loan, or cooperative agreement, for payments to influence covered Federal actions [@CL-CMP-2450; @CL-CMP-2451] |

FAR 3.802(a) says that section 1352 of title 31 of the United States Code prohibits a recipient of a Federal contract, grant, loan, or cooperative agreement from using appropriated funds to pay any person for influencing or attempting to influence an officer or employee of any agency, a Member of Congress, an officer or employee of Congress, or an employee of a Member of Congress in connection with any covered Federal actions [@CL-CMP-2450; @CL-CMP-2451]. A covered Federal action means awarding any Federal contract, making any Federal grant, making any Federal loan, entering into any cooperative agreement, or extending, continuing, renewing, amending, or modifying any Federal contract, grant, loan, or cooperative agreement [@CL-CMP-2324]. For this subpart, the term appropriated funds does not include profit or fee from a covered Federal action [@CL-CMP-2452]. To the extent a person can demonstrate that the person has sufficient monies, other than Federal appropriated funds, the Government shall assume that these other monies were spent for any influencing activities that would be unallowable if paid for with Federal appropriated funds [@CL-CMP-2453].

FAR 3.802(b) says that section 1352 of title 31 of the United States Code also requires offerors to furnish a declaration consisting of both a certification and a disclosure, with periodic updates of the disclosure after contract award [@CL-CMP-2454]. The contracting officer shall obtain certifications and disclosures as required by the provision at 52.203-11, Certification and Disclosure Regarding Payments to Influence Certain Federal Transactions, prior to the award of any contract exceeding $200,000 [@CL-CMP-2455]. The prohibition of paragraph 3.802(a) does not apply under the following conditions in FAR 3.803(a) [@CL-CMP-2326]. Only those communications and services expressly authorized by paragraph (a) of FAR 3.803 are permitted [@CL-CMP-2327].

## How it works

One listed exception is payment of reasonable compensation made to an officer or employee of a person requesting or receiving a covered Federal action, if the payment is for agency and legislative liaison activities not directly related to a covered Federal action [@CL-CMP-2328]. A second is payment of reasonable compensation made to such an officer or employee, if the payment is for professional or technical services rendered directly in the preparation, submission, or negotiation of any bid, proposal, or application for that Federal action, or for meeting requirements imposed by or pursuant to law as a condition for receiving that Federal action [@CL-CMP-2329; @CL-CMP-7688]. A third is any reasonable payment to a person other than an officer or employee of a person requesting or receiving a covered Federal action, if the payment is for professional or technical services rendered directly in the preparation, submission, or negotiation of any bid, proposal, or application for that Federal action, or for meeting requirements imposed by or pursuant to law as a condition for receiving that Federal action [@CL-CMP-2457; @CL-CMP-2458; @CL-CMP-7689].

For those two professional and technical services exceptions, the services are limited to advice and analysis directly applying any professional or technical discipline [@CL-CMP-2459]. Communications with the intent to influence made by a professional or a technical person are not allowable under FAR 3.803 unless they provide advice and analysis directly applying their professional or technical expertise [@CL-CMP-2460], and unless the advice or analysis is rendered directly and solely in the preparation, submission or negotiation of a covered Federal action [@CL-CMP-2461].

On disclosure, the disclosure requirements of paragraph 3.802(b) do not apply with respect to payments of reasonable compensation made to regularly employed officers or employees of a person [@CL-CMP-2330]. With respect to an officer or employee of a person requesting or receiving a Federal contract, regularly employed refers to an officer or employee who is employed by that person for at least 130 working days within 1 year immediately preceding the date of the submission that initiates agency consideration of such person for receipt of such contract [@CL-CMP-2325; @CL-CMP-8376]. An officer or employee employed for less than 130 working days within that period is considered regularly employed as soon as employed by the person for 130 working days [@CL-CMP-2456].

The subpart prescribes policies and procedures implementing section 1352 of title 31 of the United States Code, titled "Limitation on use of appropriated funds to influence certain Federal contracting and financial transactions." [@CL-CMP-9705] The Secretary of Defense may exempt, on a case-by-case basis, a covered Federal action from the prohibitions of the subpart whenever the Secretary determines, in writing, that such an exemption is in the national interest [@CL-CMP-9706], and the Secretary of Defense shall transmit a copy of the exemption to Congress immediately after making the determination [@CL-CMP-9707]. The contracting officer shall report suspected violations of the requirements of section 1352 of title 31 of the United States Code in accordance with agency procedures [@CL-CMP-9708]. Agencies shall impose and collect civil penalties pursuant to the provisions of the Program Fraud and Civil Remedies Act (section 3803 of title 31 of the United States Code, except subsection (c), and sections 3804 through 3808 and 3812), insofar as those provisions are not inconsistent with the requirements of the subpart [@CL-CMP-9709]. FAR 3.808(a) says to insert the provision at 52.203-11, Certification and Disclosure Regarding Payments to Influence Certain Federal Transactions, in solicitations expected to exceed $200,000 [@CL-CMP-9710], and FAR 3.808(b) says to insert the clause at 52.203-12, Limitation on Payments to Influence Certain Federal Transactions, in solicitations and contracts expected to exceed $200,000 [@CL-CMP-9711].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor pays its own proposal engineer a salary for time spent drafting the technical volume of a bid. That payment is within the professional and technical services exception if the work is rendered directly in the preparation, submission, or negotiation of the bid and consists of advice and analysis directly applying the engineer's discipline. If the engineer instead lobbies an agency official to favor the bid, that communication is not allowable under the professional and technical services exception unless it is advice or analysis rendered directly and solely in the preparation, submission or negotiation of the covered action. Where no exception applies, the prohibition bars paying for the influencing with appropriated funds, and profit or fee from the covered action is not appropriated funds for this purpose.

## Never confuse

The subpart prescribes policies and procedures implementing section 1352 of title 31 of the United States Code [@CL-CMP-9705]. For the allowability of lobbying and political activity costs and their separation from indirect cost pools, see [Lobbying and political activity costs](cmp-lobbying-and-political-activity-costs.md). For fees for consultants, see [Professional and consultant service costs](cmp-professional-consultant-service-costs.md) and [Contingent fees](../regulatory-framework/cmp-contingent-fees.md).
