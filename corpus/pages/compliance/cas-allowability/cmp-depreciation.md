## Purpose

Depreciation on plant, equipment, and other capital facilities is an allowable contract cost subject to limits, including a residual-value threshold, consistency with financial accounting, and bars on no-cost and fully depreciated property.

| Codified citation | FAR 31.205-11 [@CL-CMP-0464] |

Depreciation on a contractor's plant, equipment, and other capital facilities is an allowable contract cost, subject to the limitations in this cost principle [@CL-CMP-0464]. For tangible personal property, only estimated residual values that exceed 10 percent of the capitalized cost need be used in establishing depreciable costs [@CL-CMP-0465]. Depreciation, rental, or use charges are unallowable on property acquired from the Government at no cost [@CL-CMP-0466]. No depreciation or rental is allowed on property fully depreciated by the contractor [@CL-CMP-0467].

## How it works

Depreciation is allowable but must stay consistent with the amount used for financial accounting, and with the segment's non-Government depreciation policies, when the CAS standard does not apply, except as indicated in paragraphs (g) and (h) of the cost principle. Depreciation is unallowable on property acquired from the Government at no cost and on fully depreciated property, though a reasonable charge for using fully depreciated property may be agreed upon.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor buys a machine for 100,000 and estimates a residual value of 5,000. The residual value is below the residual-value threshold, so it does not need to be used in establishing depreciable cost, although the depreciation claimed remains subject to the cost principle's other limits, such as not exceeding the amount used for financial accounting purposes.

## Never confuse

Depreciation is not rental: a capital lease is treated as a purchased asset and depreciated, while an operating lease is rented use whose rates are tested for reasonableness. Depreciation for covered contracts must also stay consistent with the CAS depreciation standard when it applies; see [CAS applicability](cmp-cas-applicability.md).
