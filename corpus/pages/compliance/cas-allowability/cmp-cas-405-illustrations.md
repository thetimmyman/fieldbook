## Purpose

Walks through the five printed illustrations of identifying unallowable and directly associated costs. The rule itself is on [Accounting for unallowable costs](../cas-allowability/cmp-cas-405-unallowable-costs.md).

In the first illustration, the contracting officer issues a written decision which supports the auditor's position that the questioned direct labor and direct material costs are unallowable [@CL-CMP-2882]. Following receipt of the contracting officer's decision, the contractor must clearly identify the disallowed direct labor and direct material costs in its accounting records and in reports covering any subsequent submission that includes such costs [@CL-CMP-2796]. If the contractor's base for allocating any relevant indirect cost pool consists of direct labor, direct material, total prime cost, total cost input and similar bases, the contractor must include the disallowed direct labor and material costs in the allocation base for that pool [@CL-CMP-2883]. In the second illustration, where manufacturing overhead is regularly part of the contractor's base for allocating general and administrative or other indirect expenses, the contractor must allocate those expenses to contracts and other final cost objectives [@CL-CMP-2797], by means of a base which includes the identified unallowable manufacturing overhead costs [@CL-CMP-2798]. In the planning-activity illustration, the contracting officer issues a written decision supporting the auditor's position that the total costs questioned are unallowable under the Federal Acquisition Regulation [@CL-CMP-2884]. After receipt of the contracting officer's decision the contractor must identify the disallowed costs and specific other costs incurred for the same purpose in like circumstances in any subsequent estimating, cost accumulation or reporting for Government contracts, in which such costs are included [@CL-CMP-2799].

In the expansion-and-diversification illustration, during negotiation of interim bidding and billing general and administrative rates, the contractor agreed to classify as unallowable various in-house costs incurred for the expansion program and various directly associated costs of the identifiable unallowable costs [@CL-CMP-2886]. Applying the negotiated interim general and administrative rates to proposals, and on an interim basis to billings, for covered contracts constitutes compliance with the standard [@CL-CMP-2800]. In the entertainment-trip illustration, where the contractor does not regularly include general and administrative expenses in any indirect-expense allocation base, the official's travel and subsistence expenses would be directly associated costs for identification with the unallowable entertainment expense [@CL-CMP-2801]. However, unless this type of activity constituted a significant part of the official's regular duties and responsibilities on which the salary was based, no part of the official's salary would be required to be identified as a directly associated cost of the unallowable entertainment expense [@CL-CMP-2885].

## How it works

The illustrations show who acts after a cost is questioned and what must stay visible. Where the contracting officer's decision supports the auditor, the contractor identifies the disallowed costs in its records and later submissions [@CL-CMP-2882; @CL-CMP-2796], and in the planning-activity illustration it also identifies specific other costs incurred for the same purpose in like circumstances [@CL-CMP-2884; @CL-CMP-2799]. In the second illustration, identified unallowable manufacturing overhead costs stay in the allocation base where manufacturing overhead is regularly part of that base [@CL-CMP-2797; @CL-CMP-2798]. In the entertainment-trip illustration, travel and subsistence are directly associated costs for identification with the unallowable entertainment expense [@CL-CMP-2801]. The official's salary is not required to be identified as a directly associated cost unless the activity was a significant part of the official's regular duties [@CL-CMP-2885].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An auditor questions a billed material cost of 30,000, and the contracting officer decides in writing that it is unallowable, in support of the auditor. The contractor then marks the 30,000 as disallowed in its records and in later submissions that include it. If its indirect pool is allocated on a material base, the 30,000 stays in that base.

## Never confuse

In the first two illustrations, identifying an unallowable cost does not take it out of the allocation base. In the first illustration, the disallowed costs go in the allocation base of an indirect cost pool whose base consists of direct labor, direct material, total prime cost, total cost input or similar bases [@CL-CMP-2883]. In the second illustration, where manufacturing overhead is regularly part of the base, the contractor allocates the expenses over a base that includes the identified unallowable manufacturing overhead costs [@CL-CMP-2797; @CL-CMP-2798]. The planning-activity illustration refers to costs incurred for the same purpose in like circumstances [@CL-CMP-2799]; the same-purpose illustrations are on [Definitions, illustrations and interpretation on consistency in allocating costs incurred for the same purpose](../cas-allowability/cmp-cas-402-definitions-illustrations-and-interpretation.md). Illustrations of what counts in a general and administrative allocation base are on [Illustrations of the general and administrative pool and cost input base](../cas-allowability/cmp-cas-410-illustrations.md). For how unallowable and directly associated costs are excluded from billings and proposals, see [Accounting for unallowable and directly associated costs](../cas-allowability/cmp-accounting-for-unallowable-and-associated-costs.md).
