## Purpose

Identifies the allowability restrictions and accounting rules governing legal, administrative, and defense costs in government contracting.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-0504] |
| Codified citation | FAR 31.205-47 [@CL-CMP-0504] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-0504] |

Costs not covered elsewhere in the legal proceedings cost principle are unallowable if incurred in defending against Federal Government claims or appeals or prosecuting claims or appeals against the Federal Government [@CL-CMP-0504].

For proceedings described in paragraph (b) whose costs are not made unallowable by it, the percentage of costs allowed shall not exceed 80 percent [@CL-CMP-0505].

Costs that may be unallowable under the legal proceedings cost principle, including directly associated costs, must be segregated and accounted for separately [@CL-CMP-0506].

## How it works

Legal and proceeding costs incurred in government actions resulting in conviction, civil liability for fraud, or monetary penalties are unallowable. Costs not covered elsewhere in the subsection for defense of antitrust suits are unallowable, and patent infringement litigation costs are unallowable unless otherwise provided for in the contract or subcontract. Where defense costs are allowable following settlement or consent disposition, recovery is capped at 80 percent [@CL-CMP-0505]. Contractors must maintain distinct accounts to isolate potentially unallowable legal expenditures during pending litigation. During the pendency of a proceeding covered by paragraph (b) or the Major Fraud Act and unlawful-assistance paragraphs, the contracting officer generally withholds payment of costs that may be unallowable, but may allow conditional payment if in the Government's best interests, on adequate security and agreement to repay unallowable costs plus interest.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor incurs 100,000 in legal expenses defending a third-party civil proceeding that does not involve fraud. The matter is resolved by consent, and the contracting officer determines that the costs are allowable under the regulatory criteria. Applying the 80 percent ceiling on the percentage of costs allowed, no more than 80,000 of the 100,000 can be allowed, so at least 20,000 is unallowable [@CL-CMP-0505].

## Never confuse

Costs of defending a government proceeding are unallowable only if the result is one of the outcomes listed in paragraph (b), whereas costs of defending Federal Government claims or appeals are unallowable under paragraph (f) when not covered elsewhere in the subsection. In addition, costs that may be unallowable under the legal proceedings cost principle, including directly associated costs, must be segregated and accounted for separately [@CL-CMP-0506].
