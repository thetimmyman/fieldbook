## Purpose

Identifies the allowability restrictions and accounting rules governing legal, administrative, and defense costs in government contracting.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-0504] |
| Codified citation | FAR 31.205-47 [@CL-CMP-0504] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 31.205-47 [@CL-CMP-9451; @CL-CMP-9448; @CL-CMP-9454] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-0504] |
| Applies to | Contracts subject to the FAR cost principles [@CL-CMP-0504] |

Costs not covered elsewhere in the legal proceedings cost principle are unallowable if incurred in connection with the items listed in paragraph (f), which include defense against Federal Government claims or appeals or the prosecution of claims or appeals against the Federal Government [@CL-CMP-0504].

Costs of proceedings described in paragraph (b) that are not made unallowable by it may be allowable to the extent that they are reasonable in relation to the activities required to deal with the proceeding and the underlying cause of action, are not otherwise recovered from the Federal Government or a third party, either directly as a result of the proceeding or otherwise, and are allowed at a percentage that does not exceed the percentage determined to be appropriate considering the complexity of procurement litigation, generally accepted principles governing the award of legal fees in civil actions involving the United States as a party, and such other factors as may be appropriate [@CL-CMP-0913; @CL-CMP-0914; @CL-CMP-7076; @CL-CMP-7077]. For those proceedings the percentage of costs allowed shall not exceed 80 percent, and agreements reached under paragraph (c) are subject to the same limit [@CL-CMP-0505].

Costs that may be unallowable under the legal proceedings cost principle, including directly associated costs, must be segregated and accounted for separately [@CL-CMP-0506].

## How it works

Costs incurred in connection with proceedings brought by a Federal, State, local or foreign government for a violation of, or failure to comply with, law or regulation by the contractor or subcontractor, and with certain whistleblower and False Claims Act proceedings, are unallowable if the result is a criminal conviction; in a civil or administrative proceeding, a finding of liability where the proceeding involves an allegation of fraud or similar misconduct, or the imposition of a monetary penalty or a corrective-action order where it does not; or other specified outcomes such as debarment, suspension or contract termination for default. Costs not covered elsewhere in the subsection for defense of antitrust suits are unallowable, and patent infringement litigation costs are unallowable unless otherwise provided for in the contract or subcontract. Disposition by consent or compromise can itself make costs unallowable where the proceeding could have led to the listed outcomes, subject to paragraphs (c) and (d) [@CL-CMP-0910]. For a proceeding commenced by the United States and resolved by consent or compromise, costs unallowable solely because of paragraph (b) may be allowed to the extent the agreement specifically provides [@CL-CMP-0911; @CL-CMP-0912]. For costs of proceedings described in paragraph (b) that are not made unallowable by that paragraph, the percentage of costs allowed shall not exceed 80 percent, and agreements reached under paragraph (c) are subject to this limitation [@CL-CMP-0913; @CL-CMP-0505]. Costs that may be unallowable, including directly associated costs, shall be segregated and accounted for separately by the contractor or subcontractor [@CL-CMP-0506]. During the pendency of any proceeding covered by paragraph (b) and subparagraphs (f)(4) and (f)(7), the contracting officer shall generally withhold payment of such costs [@CL-CMP-9470], but may provide for conditional payment if in the best interests of the Government, upon provision of adequate security or other adequate assurance and agreement to repay all unallowable costs, plus interest, if the costs are subsequently determined to be unallowable.

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 31 text in place of the codified text [@CL-CMP-9350], effective immediately under Revision 1 signed on 2026-08-04 [@CL-CMP-9351]. The RFO text provides the following. Costs incurred in connection with any proceeding brought by a Federal, State, local, or foreign government for a violation of, or failure to comply with, law or regulation by the contractor (including its agents or employees) [@CL-CMP-9732], or brought by a contractor or subcontractor employee submitting a whistleblower complaint of reprisal or by a third party in the name of the United States under the False Claims Act [@CL-CMP-9733], are unallowable if the result is one of the results listed in paragraph (b) [@CL-CMP-9733]. Those results include a criminal conviction or, in a civil or administrative proceeding, a finding of contractor liability where the proceeding involves an allegation of fraud or similar misconduct [@CL-CMP-9441]; the imposition of a monetary penalty, or an order issued by the agency head to the contractor or subcontractor to take corrective action under 41 USC 4712 or 10 USC 4701, where the proceeding does not involve an allegation of fraud or similar misconduct [@CL-CMP-9734]; a final decision by an appropriate official of an executive agency to debar or suspend the contractor, rescind or void a contract, or terminate a contract for default by reason of a violation or failure to comply with a law or regulation [@CL-CMP-9735]; and disposition by consent or compromise if the proceeding could have led to any of the outcomes listed in paragraphs (b)(1) through (3), but see paragraphs (c) and (d) [@CL-CMP-9442]. costs are not otherwise unallowable, costs incurred in connection with any proceeding commenced by the United States that is resolved by consent or compromise pursuant to an agreement entered into between the contractor and the United States [@CL-CMP-9443], and which are unallowable solely because of paragraph (b), may be allowed if specifically provided in such agreement [@CL-CMP-9444]. Costs of paragraph (b) proceedings that are not made unallowable by that paragraph may be allowable to the extent that stated conditions are met [@CL-CMP-9445], including that they are reasonable in relation to the activities required to deal with the proceeding and the underlying cause of action [@CL-CMP-9446] and are not otherwise recovered from the Government or a third party, either directly as a result of the proceeding [@CL-CMP-9447]. The percentage allowed must not exceed the percentage determined to be appropriate considering the complexity of procurement litigation and generally accepted principles governing the award of legal fees in civil actions involving the United States as a party [@CL-CMP-9448]. That percentage must not exceed 80 percent of the legal costs incurred [@CL-CMP-9449], a limit that applies to agreements reached under paragraph (c) [@CL-CMP-9450], but if an agreement described in paragraph (c)(1) explicitly states the amount of otherwise allowable incurred legal fees and limits the allowable recovery to 80 percent or less of the stated legal fees, no additional limitation need be applied [@CL-CMP-9736]. For any proceeding described in paragraph (c)(2), the amount of reimbursement allowed for legal costs must be determined by the contracting officer but must not exceed 80 percent of otherwise allowable legal costs incurred, and no additional limitations need be applied [@CL-CMP-9737]. Costs not covered elsewhere in the section are unallowable if incurred in connection with the items listed in paragraph (f) [@CL-CMP-9738], including defense against Government claims or appeals or the prosecution of claims or appeals against the Government [@CL-CMP-9451]. Defense of antitrust suits is an item on that list [@CL-CMP-9452], and patent infringement litigation is an item on it unless otherwise provided for in the contract [@CL-CMP-9453]. Costs that may be unallowable, including directly associated costs, must be segregated and accounted for separately by the contractor [@CL-CMP-9454]. When a proceeding covered by paragraph (b) and paragraphs (f)(4) and (f)(7) is pending, the contracting officer must withhold payment of such costs [@CL-CMP-9455], or, when determined to be in the best interests of the Government, may provide for conditional payment upon adequate security or other adequate assurance [@CL-CMP-9456], with the contractor's agreement to repay all unallowable costs, plus interest, if the costs are subsequently determined to be unallowable [@CL-CMP-9457].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor incurs 100,000 in legal expenses in a proceeding commenced by the United States alleging that the contractor violated a regulation. The matter is resolved by a consent agreement with the United States that specifically provides that the legal costs may be allowed. The costs are reasonable and are not otherwise recovered from the Government or a third party. Because the proceeding is one described in paragraph (b) and the agreement is reached under paragraph (c), the 80 percent ceiling applies, so no more than 80,000 of the 100,000 can be allowed and at least 20,000 is unallowable [@CL-CMP-0505].

By contrast, the ceiling does not apply to an ordinary private civil suit between two companies, because that is not a proceeding described in paragraph (b). Whether its costs are allowable is decided under the other parts of the cost principle.

## Never confuse

Costs not covered elsewhere in the subsection are unallowable under paragraph (f) if incurred in connection with the listed items, which include defense against Federal Government claims or appeals [@CL-CMP-0504]. Costs that may be unallowable under the legal proceedings cost principle, including directly associated costs, must be segregated and accounted for separately [@CL-CMP-0506]. The codified paragraph (g) says the contracting officer shall generally withhold payment [@CL-CMP-9470], while RFO paragraph (g)(2)(i) says the contracting officer must withhold payment [@CL-CMP-9455]. The codified paragraph (c)(1) allows costs to the extent the agreement specifically provides [@CL-CMP-0912], while RFO paragraph (c)(1) allows them if specifically provided in the agreement [@CL-CMP-9444]. The codified paragraph (e)(3) caps the percentage at 80 percent [@CL-CMP-0505], while RFO paragraph (e)(3)(i) caps it at 80 percent of the legal costs incurred [@CL-CMP-9449].
