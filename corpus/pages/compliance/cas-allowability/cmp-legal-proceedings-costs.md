## Purpose

Identifies the allowability restrictions and accounting rules governing legal, administrative, and defense costs in government contracting.

## The rule

|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-0504] |
| Codified citation | FAR 31.205-47 [@CL-CMP-0504] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |

Costs not covered elsewhere in the legal proceedings cost principle are unallowable if incurred in defending against Federal Government claims or appeals or prosecuting claims or appeals against the Federal Government [@CL-CMP-0504].

Costs of proceedings described in paragraph (b) that are not made unallowable by it may be allowable only to the extent they are reasonable and not otherwise recovered [@CL-CMP-0913; @CL-CMP-0914]. For those proceedings the percentage of costs allowed shall not exceed 80 percent, and agreements reached under paragraph (c) are subject to the same limit [@CL-CMP-0505].

Costs that may be unallowable under the legal proceedings cost principle, including directly associated costs, must be segregated and accounted for separately [@CL-CMP-0506].

## How it works

Costs incurred in connection with proceedings brought by a government are unallowable if the result is a criminal conviction, a finding of liability in a proceeding involving an allegation of fraud, the imposition of a monetary penalty, or other specified outcomes such as debarment or contract termination for default. Costs not covered elsewhere in the subsection for defense of antitrust suits are unallowable, and patent infringement litigation costs are unallowable unless otherwise provided for in the contract or subcontract. Disposition by consent or compromise can itself make costs unallowable where the proceeding could have led to the listed outcomes, subject to paragraphs (c) and (d) [@CL-CMP-0910]. For a proceeding commenced by the United States and resolved by consent or compromise, costs unallowable solely because of paragraph (b) may be allowed to the extent the agreement specifically provides [@CL-CMP-0911; @CL-CMP-0912]. The 80 percent cap applies to costs of proceedings described in paragraph (b) that are allowable, including costs under a paragraph (c) agreement, and not to legal costs generally [@CL-CMP-0913; @CL-CMP-0505]. Contractors must maintain distinct accounts to isolate potentially unallowable legal expenditures during pending litigation. During the pendency of a proceeding covered by paragraph (b) or the Major Fraud Act and unlawful-assistance paragraphs, the contracting officer generally withholds payment of costs that may be unallowable, but may allow conditional payment if in the Government's best interests, on adequate security and agreement to repay unallowable costs plus interest.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor incurs 100,000 in legal expenses in a proceeding commenced by the United States alleging that the contractor violated a regulation. The matter is resolved by a consent agreement with the United States that specifically provides that the legal costs may be allowed. The costs are reasonable and are not otherwise recovered from the Government or a third party. Because the proceeding is one described in paragraph (b) and the agreement is reached under paragraph (c), the 80 percent ceiling applies, so no more than 80,000 of the 100,000 can be allowed and at least 20,000 is unallowable [@CL-CMP-0505].

By contrast, the ceiling does not apply to an ordinary private civil suit between two companies, because that is not a proceeding described in paragraph (b). Whether its costs are allowable is decided under the other parts of the cost principle.

## Never confuse

Costs of defending a government proceeding are unallowable only if the result is one of the outcomes listed in paragraph (b), whereas costs of defending Federal Government claims or appeals are unallowable under paragraph (f) when not covered elsewhere in the subsection. In addition, costs that may be unallowable under the legal proceedings cost principle, including directly associated costs, must be segregated and accounted for separately [@CL-CMP-0506].
