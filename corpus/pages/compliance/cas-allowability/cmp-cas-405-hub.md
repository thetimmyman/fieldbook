## Purpose

Cost Accounting Standard 405 establishes guidelines for identifying costs specifically described as unallowable and governs the cost accounting treatment of such costs on Government contracts. This concept hub sets out what the standard requires and links the detailed reference, estimating and compliance pages across the corpus.

## The rule

The purpose of CAS 405 is to facilitate the negotiation, audit, administration and settlement of contracts by establishing guidelines covering the identification and accounting treatment of unallowable costs [@CL-EST-2636]. The guidelines cover identifying costs specifically described as unallowable at the time they first become defined or authoritatively designated as unallowable [@CL-EST-2502], and provide the cost accounting treatment to promote consistent application of sound cost accounting principles covering all incurred costs [@CL-EST-2637]. The standard is predicated on the proposition that costs incurred in carrying on the activities of an enterprise, regardless of their allowability under Government contracts, are allocable to cost objectives based on beneficial or causal relationships [@CL-EST-2503]. CAS 405 does not govern the allowability of costs [@CL-EST-2504], which remains a function of the appropriate procurement or reviewing authority [@CL-EST-2638].

Costs that are expressly unallowable or mutually agreed unallowable must be identified and excluded from any billing, claim, or proposal on a Government contract [@CL-CMP-0207]. All unallowable costs covered by paragraphs (a) through (d) of 9904.405-40 are subject to the same cost accounting principles governing cost allocability as allowable costs [@CL-CMP-8123]. Unallowable costs that would normally be part of a regular indirect-cost allocation base remain in that base [@CL-CMP-0208]. Following CAS-to-GAAP conformance, the Board relocated retained content from CAS 404 and 409 on asset accounting and depreciation to paragraph (g) of 9904.405-40 [@CL-CMP-1502; @CL-CMP-1504; @CL-CMP-1525]. There is no exemption from CAS 405 [@CL-CMP-8654]. The standard became effective on April 17, 1992 [@CL-CMP-8655].

## How it works

An unallowable cost cannot be included in prices, cost reimbursements or settlements under a Government contract to which it is allocable [@CL-EST-2874]. An expressly unallowable cost is specifically named and stated to be unallowable under the express provisions of an applicable law, regulation or contract [@CL-EST-2872]. A directly associated cost is generated solely as a result of the incurrence of another cost, and would not have been incurred had the other cost not been incurred [@CL-EST-2871]. Terms defined elsewhere in part 99 apply without modification to CAS 405 [@CL-EST-2960; @CL-EST-2961].

To provide visibility for incurred unallowable costs, contractors may segregate them in separate accounts in the regular books of account, maintain separate records or workpapers, or use techniques permitting audit verification [@CL-CMP-0209; @CL-CMP-8196; @CL-CMP-8197]. When costs are disallowed by a contracting officer's written decision, the contractor must clearly identify those costs in subsequent submissions, while retaining unallowable base costs in regular indirect allocation bases [@CL-CMP-2882; @CL-CMP-2796; @CL-CMP-2883]. For directly associated costs, travel and subsistence on an unallowable entertainment trip are directly associated, while salary is excluded unless the activity represents a significant part of regular duties [@CL-CMP-2801; @CL-CMP-2885].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor incurs 1,000,000 of direct costs on a covered contract, including 50,000 of direct costs that a contracting officer determined in writing to be unallowable. It also incurs an unallowable entertainment expense of 10,000 and 2,000 of travel expenses generated solely to attend that event. The contractor segregates the 10,000 entertainment expense and the 2,000 directly associated travel cost in separate accounts, excluding both from all billings and proposals. For the 50,000 disallowed direct costs, the contractor identifies them in its records and excludes them from reimbursement claims. Because direct costs form the base for allocating its 200,000 indirect expense pool, the entire 1,000,000 of direct costs, including the 50,000 disallowed amount, remains in the allocation base. Leaving the unallowable costs in the base avoids shifting indirect costs onto other contracts, keeping cost allocation consistent with beneficial or causal relationships. This example is illustrative only, and the contract itself governs.

## Never confuse

CAS 405 establishes rules for identifying unallowable costs and allocating them across cost objectives based on causal relationships, but explicitly does not govern whether a cost is allowable [@CL-EST-2503; @CL-EST-2504]. In contrast, the FAR part 31 cost principles determine allowability, establishing which costs cannot be reimbursed under Government contracts [@CL-CMP-0101; @CL-CMP-2733]. While FAR 31.201-6 makes directly associated costs unallowable only when material under procurement criteria [@CL-CMP-2731], CAS 405 defines directly associated costs by causal generation without regard to procurement allowability determinations [@CL-EST-2871; @CL-EST-2638].
