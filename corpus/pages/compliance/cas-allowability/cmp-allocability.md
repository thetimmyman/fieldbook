## Purpose

Sets forth the fundamental requirements and relationship criteria for allocating costs to government contracts.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-0105] |
| Codified citation | FAR 31.201-4 [@CL-CMP-0105] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 31.201-4 [@CL-CMP-0105] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-0105] |
| Applies to | Government contracts subject to the cost principles [@CL-CMP-0105] |

A cost is allocable if it is assignable or chargeable to one or more cost objectives on the basis of relative benefits received or other equitable relationship [@CL-CMP-0105]. Subject to that basic test, a cost is allocable to a Government contract if it is incurred specifically for the contract, benefits both the contract and other work and can be distributed to them in reasonable proportion to the benefits received, or is necessary to the overall operation of the business although a direct relationship to any particular cost objective cannot be shown [@CL-CMP-0106].

## How it works

Allocability measures the link between a cost and one or more cost objectives on the basis of relative benefits received or other equitable relationship [@CL-CMP-0105]. Direct costs are incurred specifically for a single contract cost objective. A cost that benefits both the contract and other work is allocable when it can be distributed to them in reasonable proportion to the benefits received [@CL-CMP-0106]. A cost is allocable to a Government contract when it is necessary to the overall operation of the business, although a direct relationship to any particular cost objective cannot be shown [@CL-CMP-0106].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A company incurs 50,000 for executive leadership and corporate compliance functions. While no single project caused the expense, the functions are necessary to the overall business operation. The company allocates the 50,000 across all final cost objectives using a total cost input base, distributing costs in proportion to relative business activity.

## Never confuse

Allocability assigns costs on the basis of relative benefits received or other equitable relationship; allowability requires a cost to meet all of the allowability requirements, including reasonableness, allocability, CAS or GAAP, the contract terms and the limitations in the subpart [@CL-CMP-0101]. A cost can be properly allocable to a contract but barred by an express unallowability principle. Whether CAS rules apply is determined by the CAS clause, if any, in the contract and the requirements of the standards themselves; see [CAS applicability](cmp-cas-applicability.md).
