## Purpose

Sets forth the fundamental requirements and relationship criteria for allocating costs to government contracts.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-0105] |
| Codified citation | FAR 31.201-4 [@CL-CMP-0105] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 31.201-4 [@CL-CMP-9930; @CL-CMP-9931] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-0105] |
| Applies to | Government contracts subject to the cost principles [@CL-CMP-0105] |

A cost is allocable if it is assignable or chargeable to one or more cost objectives on the basis of relative benefits received or other equitable relationship [@CL-CMP-0105]. Subject to that basic test, a cost is allocable to a Government contract if it meets one of three alternatives: it is incurred specifically for the contract [@CL-CMP-9619]; it benefits both the contract and other work and can be distributed to them in reasonable proportion to the benefits received [@CL-CMP-8029]; or it is necessary to the overall operation of the business although a direct relationship to any particular cost objective cannot be shown [@CL-CMP-0106].

## How it works

Allocability measures the link between a cost and one or more cost objectives on the basis of relative benefits received or other equitable relationship [@CL-CMP-0105]. Subject to that basic test, a cost incurred specifically for the contract is allocable to a Government contract [@CL-CMP-9619]. A cost that benefits both the contract and other work, and can be distributed to them in reasonable proportion to the benefits received, is also allocable [@CL-CMP-8029]. So is a cost that is necessary to the overall operation of the business, although a direct relationship to any particular cost objective cannot be shown [@CL-CMP-0106].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers must use the RFO FAR part 31 text in place of the codified text [@CL-CMP-9350], effective immediately under Revision 1 signed on 2026-08-04 [@CL-CMP-9351]. The RFO section lists the relative-benefits test as paragraph (a) of the conditions that follow "A cost is allocable to a Government contract if it—", with a cost incurred specifically for the contract as paragraph (b) [@CL-CMP-9930]. Its paragraphs (c) and (d) cover a cost that benefits both the contract and other work and can be distributed to them in reasonable proportion to the benefits received, and a cost that is necessary to the overall operation of the business, although a direct relationship to any particular cost objective cannot be shown [@CL-CMP-9931]. The codified text instead opens with the relative-benefits test as its own sentence and introduces its specific-contract, shared-benefit and overall-operation conditions with the words "Subject to the foregoing" [@CL-CMP-0105; @CL-CMP-9619], whereas the RFO text prints no such words and introduces all four conditions with the one lead-in "A cost is allocable to a Government contract if it—" [@CL-CMP-9930].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A company incurs 50,000 for executive leadership and corporate compliance functions. While no single project caused the expense, the functions are necessary to the overall business operation. The company allocates the 50,000 across all final cost objectives using a total cost input base, distributing costs in proportion to relative business activity.

## Never confuse

Allocability assigns costs on the basis of relative benefits received or other equitable relationship; allowability requires a cost to comply with all of the following requirements: reasonableness, allocability, standards promulgated by the CAS Board if applicable (otherwise generally accepted accounting principles and practices appropriate to the circumstances), the terms of the contract, and any limitations set forth in the subpart [@CL-CMP-0101; @CL-CMP-8030]. The applicability of the CAS rules and regulations is determined by the CAS clause, if any, in the contract and the requirements of the standards themselves [@CL-CMP-8032]; see [CAS applicability](cmp-cas-applicability.md). The codified text states the relative-benefits test as its own opening sentence [@CL-CMP-0105], whereas the RFO text lists it as paragraph (a) and moves the other conditions to (b), (c) and (d), so the paragraph letters differ between the two texts [@CL-CMP-9930; @CL-CMP-9931].
