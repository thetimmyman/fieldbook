## Purpose

Cost Accounting Standard 407 establishes criteria for the use of standard costs for direct material and direct labor. This concept hub sets out the requirements and current status of the standard and links the detailed compliance, conformance, and allocation pages across the corpus.

## The rule

The Cost Accounting Standards Board's final rule on CAS 407 rescinds CAS 407 and transfers one definition to a different location within chapter 99 of title 48 [@CL-CMP-1516]. The final rule on CAS 407 has an effective date of October 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-1517].

The CAS 407 rule removes and reserves subpart 9904.407 [@CL-CMP-1521]. The printed codified text of 48 CFR part 9904 shows sections 9904.407 through 9904.409 as reserved [@CL-CMP-1523].

## How it works

Not established. The CAS 407 rule removes and reserves subpart 9904.407 [@CL-CMP-1521], and the printed codified text of 48 CFR part 9904 shows sections 9904.407 through 9904.409 as reserved [@CL-CMP-1523].

Under the Board's final rule, CAS 407 is rescinded and one definition is transferred to a different location within chapter 99 of title 48 [@CL-CMP-1516], with an effective date of October 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-1517].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor operates a manufacturing production unit that uses standard costs to account for direct labor and direct material. Following the rescission of CAS 407, the contractor evaluates whether it may discontinue accumulating labor variances and instead charge standard costs directly to covered contracts. Because the rescission of CAS 407 removed the separate standard while retaining production unit standard costing rules under CAS 418, standard costing remains subject to variance accounting. During a cost accounting period, standard direct labor costs in the production unit total 400,000 based on a pre-established rate of 40 per hour across 10,000 hours, while actual direct labor costs incurred total 420,000, creating an unfavorable labor variance of 20,000. Under the retained standard costing rules, the contractor cannot absorb the variance as a commercial adjustment; it must allocate the 20,000 variance to applicable cost objectives in proportion to standard direct labor costs. If Contract A incurred 100,000 of standard labor (25 percent of the 400,000 total), it receives 5,000 of the variance (25 percent of 20,000), bringing its total direct labor cost to 105,000. This example is illustrative only, and the contract itself governs.

## Never confuse

While the Board's final rule on CAS 407 rescinded the standard, removed and reserved subpart 9904.407, and transferred one definition within chapter 99 [@CL-CMP-1516; @CL-CMP-1521; @CL-CMP-1523], the retained requirements addressing standard costs and related variances at the production unit level were moved to CAS 418 [@CL-CMP-1518]. Under CAS 418, standard costs may be used for estimating, accumulating and reporting costs of direct material and direct labor only when standard costs and related variances are accounted for at the production unit level and under the standard costing paragraph [@CL-RTE-0650; @CL-CMP-1526], with a production unit defined as a grouping of activities using homogeneous inputs or yielding homogeneous outputs appropriate for allocating variances [@CL-CMP-1527]. CAS 418 also broadly governs direct and indirect cost classification under written policies and requires indirect costs to be accumulated in homogeneous pools allocated on beneficial or causal relationships [@CL-CMP-0216; @CL-CMP-0217; @CL-CMP-0218].
