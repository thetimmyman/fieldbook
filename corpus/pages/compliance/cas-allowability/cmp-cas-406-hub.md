## Purpose

Cost Accounting Standard 406 provides criteria for the selection of the time periods to be used as cost accounting periods for contract cost estimating, accumulating, and reporting. This concept hub sets out what the standard requires and links the detailed reference and period pages across the corpus.

## The rule

The purpose of CAS 406 is to provide criteria for the selection of the time periods to be used as cost accounting periods for contract cost estimating, accumulating, and reporting [@CL-EST-2500]. Applying the standard reduces the effects of variations in the flow of costs within each cost accounting period [@CL-EST-2501], enhances objectivity and consistency, and promotes uniformity and comparability in contract cost measurements [@CL-EST-2635]. Under the fundamental requirement, a contractor must use its fiscal year as its cost accounting period, subject to specified exceptions [@CL-CMP-0210]. The same cost accounting period is used to accumulate costs in an indirect cost pool and to establish its allocation base, except that the parties may agree to a different period for the base [@CL-CMP-0211]. A contractor must use a transitional cost accounting period of a length other than a year when its fiscal year changes [@CL-CMP-0212]. Costs of an indirect function which exists for only a part of a cost accounting period may be allocated to cost objectives of that same part of the period [@CL-CMP-0835]. An annual period other than the fiscal year may be used as the cost accounting period if its use is an established practice of the contractor [@CL-CMP-0836]. Where the contractor's cost accounting period differs from the reporting period used for Federal income tax reporting purposes, the tax period may be used for such reporting [@CL-CMP-0837].

When a transitional cost accounting period is required, the contractor may select a period of less than a year extending to the next regular period, or a period in excess of a year but not longer than 15 months combined with the previous or next regular period [@CL-EST-2889; @CL-EST-2971; @CL-EST-2890; @CL-EST-2972]. A change in the contractor's cost accounting period is a change in accounting practices for which an adjustment in contract price may be required [@CL-EST-2891]. Restructuring costs may be accumulated as deferred cost and subsequently amortized over a period not exceeding five years during which benefits are expected to accrue, or expensed in a current period if the Contracting Officer agrees it results in a more equitable assignment [@CL-CMP-2807; @CL-CMP-2808; @CL-CMP-2809]. There is no exemption from CAS 406 [@CL-CMP-8656]. The standard became effective on April 17, 1992 [@CL-EST-2643]. Contractors with prior CAS-covered contracts with full coverage continue applicability upon receipt of an applicable contract [@CL-EST-2644], while contractors with no previous contracts subject to CAS 406 apply the standard beginning with their next full fiscal year beginning after receipt [@CL-EST-2645].

## How it works

A fiscal year is the accounting period for which annual financial statements are regularly prepared, generally a period of 12 months, 52 weeks or 53 weeks [@CL-EST-2877]. An indirect cost pool is a grouping of incurred costs identified with two or more cost objectives but not identified specifically with any final cost objective [@CL-EST-2878]. A cost objective is a function, organizational subdivision, contract or other work unit for which cost data are desired and for which provision is made to accumulate and measure cost [@CL-EST-2876]. To allocate means to assign an item or group of items of cost to one or more cost objectives, encompassing both direct assignment and reassignment from an indirect cost pool [@CL-EST-2875].

A part-period allocation of an indirect function cost is permitted if the cost is material in amount, is accumulated in a separate indirect cost pool, and is allocated on the basis of an appropriate direct measure of activity or output of the function during that part of the period [@CL-EST-2879; @CL-EST-2880]. For example, operating expenses for a new computer service center operating for eight months of a cost accounting period may be accumulated in a separate pool and allocated to benefiting cost objectives of that same eight-month period based on measured usage [@CL-CMP-2803; @CL-CMP-2888]. Conversely, estimating allocable expenses based solely on indirect pool and base amounts expected during an eight-month contract performance window violates the requirement that indirect cost pools and allocation bases be based on the cost accounting period [@CL-CMP-2802; @CL-CMP-2887]. Where recurring expenses such as taxes, insurance or employee leave follow a different fixed annual period, the contractor may continue using that period in accordance with established practices for accruals and deferrals [@CL-EST-2882; @CL-EST-2968; @CL-CMP-2806].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor operates on a 12-month fiscal year. For an upcoming contract scheduled to run for eight months with direct cost input of 1,000,000, the contractor projects total company indirect expenses of 800,000 and total direct cost input of 4,000,000 during those eight months, producing an interim rate of 20 percent. For the full 12-month fiscal year, total indirect pool expenses are projected at 1,200,000 over a total direct cost input base of 10,000,000, establishing an annual indirect allocation rate of 12 percent. Under CAS 406, estimating indirect expenses for the proposal using the 20 percent rate calculated solely across the eight-month period violates the standard. The proposal must instead apply the 12 percent rate developed for the full cost accounting period, allocating 120,000 (12 percent of 1,000,000) rather than 200,000 (20 percent of 1,000,000). If the contractor also opens a new computing facility that operates for only the final six months of the fiscal year, incurring 300,000 in operating costs, it may accumulate those expenses in a separate pool and allocate them across the 1,500 machine hours logged during those six months at 200 per machine hour as an authorized part-period indirect function. This example is illustrative only, and the contract itself governs.

## Never confuse

CAS 406 governs the selection of the time period used as the cost accounting period to accumulate indirect cost pools and establish allocation bases [@CL-EST-2500; @CL-CMP-0210; @CL-CMP-0211], whereas CAS 401 requires consistency between the cost accounting practices used in estimating costs for a proposal and those used in accumulating and reporting costs [@CL-EST-2492; @CL-CMP-0201]. Estimating and accumulating indirect rates over matching partial periods may appear internally consistent under CAS 401, but doing so violates CAS 406 unless an authorized exception applies, because indirect pools and allocation bases must be calculated using the full cost accounting period [@CL-CMP-2802; @CL-CMP-2887].
