## Purpose

Governs the composition, credit adjustments, and pricing of material items and interorganizational transfers charged to government contracts.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-0513] |
| Codified citation | FAR 31.205-26 [@CL-CMP-0513] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 31.205-26 [@CL-CMP-9458; @CL-CMP-9459; @CL-CMP-9463] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-0513] |
| Applies to | Contracts subject to the FAR cost principles [@CL-CMP-0513] |

Material costs include items such as raw materials, parts, subassemblies, and manufacturing supplies, and may include collateral items like inbound transportation and in-transit insurance [@CL-CMP-0513].

Contractors must adjust material costs for income and credits, including available trade discounts, refunds, rebates, allowances, cash discounts, and credits for scrap and vendor returns [@CL-CMP-0514].

Transfers of materials, supplies, and services between affiliated divisions or subsidiaries under common control must be priced on the basis of cost incurred unless strict commercial pricing conditions are satisfied [@CL-CMP-0515; @CL-CMP-0830; @CL-CMP-0831].

## How it works

Materials purchased exclusively for a single contract are directly charged at actual purchase cost. Materials issued from stores may be priced using any generally recognized method if it is consistently applied and the results are equitable. Contractors must credit available discounts, rebates, and scrap and salvage credits either directly to the cost of the material or as a credit to indirect costs, except that lost cash discounts need not be credited when failure to take them was reasonable. Transfers from affiliated divisions are allowed on the basis of cost incurred, but may be at price when it is the established practice to price interorganizational transfers at other than cost for commercial work, the item qualifies for an exception under 15.403-1(b), and the contracting officer has not determined the price to be unreasonable [@CL-CMP-0515; @CL-CMP-0830; @CL-CMP-0831].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 31 text in place of the codified text [@CL-CMP-9350], effective immediately under Revision 1 signed on 2026-08-04 [@CL-CMP-9351]. RFO FAR 31.205-26 keeps the substance of the rules described above, with the restructuring of paragraph (e) described below [@CL-CMP-9458; @CL-CMP-9459; @CL-CMP-9463]. Material costs include the costs of items such as raw materials, parts, subassemblies, components, and manufacturing supplies, and may include collateral items such as inbound transportation and in-transit insurance [@CL-CMP-9458]. The contractor must adjust the costs of material for income and other credits [@CL-CMP-9459] and credit them either directly to the cost of the material or as a credit to indirect costs, except that lost cash discounts need not be credited when failure to take them was reasonable [@CL-CMP-9460]. Materials purchased specifically for and identifiable solely with a contract are charged at actual purchase cost [@CL-CMP-9461], and material issued from stores may use any generally recognized pricing method that is consistently applied and gives equitable results [@CL-CMP-9462]. For transfers between affiliates under common control, the RFO text sets out the cost basis and the at-price alternative as two numbered alternatives joined by "or" [@CL-CMP-9463]. Allowance at price requires both conditions, which are renumbered (e)(2)(i), that it is the established practice of the transferring organization to price interorganizational transfers at other than cost for commercial work [@CL-CMP-9464], and (e)(2)(ii), that the item qualifies for an exception under 15.403-1(b) and the contracting officer has not determined the price to be unreasonable [@CL-CMP-9465].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor purchases components for 200,000 for a defense contract and receives a prompt payment cash discount of 4,000 and a supplier volume rebate of 6,000. The contractor also recovers 2,000 from selling scrap metal generated during fabrication. The contractor credits these sums against material costs, reducing the net allowable direct material expense to 188,000.

## Never confuse

Direct material purchase costs differ from interorganizational transfers from sister corporate divisions. Interorganizational transfers generally cannot include intercompany profit markups unless the specific commercial pricing criteria are met. The codified text numbers the two conditions for transfers at price (e)(1) and (e)(2) [@CL-CMP-0830; @CL-CMP-0831], while the RFO text numbers them (e)(2)(i) and (e)(2)(ii) [@CL-CMP-9464; @CL-CMP-9465].
