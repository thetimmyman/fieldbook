## Purpose

Cost Accounting Standard 418 provides for consistent determination of direct and indirect costs and criteria for the accumulation of indirect costs in indirect cost pools, with guidance on selecting the measures that allocate a pool to cost objectives. This concept hub sets out what the standard requires and links the detailed reference, compliance and estimating pages across the corpus.

## The rule

The purpose of CAS 418 is to provide for consistent determination of direct and indirect costs and criteria for the accumulation of indirect costs, including service center and overhead costs, in indirect cost pools [@CL-EST-2902], and to provide guidance relating to the selection of allocation measures based on the beneficial or causal relationship between an indirect cost pool and cost objectives [@CL-EST-2903]. Consistent application of those criteria and that guidance will improve classification of costs as direct and indirect and the allocation of indirect costs [@CL-EST-2974].

A business unit shall have a written statement of accounting policies and practices for classifying costs as direct or indirect which shall be consistently applied [@CL-CMP-0216]. Indirect costs shall be accumulated in indirect cost pools which are homogeneous [@CL-CMP-0217], and pooled costs shall be allocated to cost objectives in reasonable proportion to the beneficial or causal relationship of the pooled costs to cost objectives [@CL-CMP-0218]. The Standard shall not apply to contracts and grants with state, local, and Federally recognized Indian tribal governments [@CL-CMP-8664]. It is effective as of April 17, 1992 [@CL-EST-2904]. Contractors with prior CAS-covered contracts with full coverage continue its applicability upon receipt of a contract to which it is applicable [@CL-EST-2904], and for contractors with no previous contracts subject to it, it is applied beginning with the contractor's second full fiscal year beginning after that receipt [@CL-EST-2905].

## How it works

Where an indirect cost pool includes a material amount of the costs of management or supervision of activities involving direct labor or direct material costs, and those costs cannot be allocated on measures of a specific beneficial or causal relationship, the allocation base selected shall be a base representative of the activity being managed or supervised [@CL-RTE-9131; @CL-RTE-0665]. Where the pool does not include material amounts of those costs, the pooled costs shall be allocated using an appropriate measure of resource consumption [@CL-RTE-9132; @CL-RTE-0668]. If consumption measures are unavailable or impractical to ascertain, the next best representation of the beneficial or causal relationship is a measure of the output of the activities of the indirect cost pool [@CL-RTE-0669], and if neither resources consumed nor output can be measured practically, a surrogate that varies in proportion to the services received measures the resources consumed [@CL-CMP-8203].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A business unit keeps two pools. The first is 600,000 of machining cost that is mostly supervision of direct labor, so it is allocated on direct labor hours, which is the activity being managed: over 24,000 hours the rate is 25 an hour, and a contract that works 640 hours in the pool receives 16,000. The second is 250,000 of a quality-test activity with no supervision cost, and it is homogeneous, so it is allocated on resource consumption: over 5,000 tests the rate is 50 a test, and a contract that orders 120 tests receives 6,000. The contract's share of the two pools is 22,000. If the number of tests could not be counted, the unit would allocate that pool on a measure of the activity's output instead. This example is illustrative only, and the contract itself governs.

## Never confuse

CAS 418 accumulates indirect costs in homogeneous indirect cost pools and allocates them in reasonable proportion to the beneficial or causal relationship of the pooled costs to cost objectives [@CL-CMP-0217; @CL-CMP-0218]. CAS 410 is narrower: business unit general and administrative expenses are grouped in a separate indirect cost pool and allocated only to final cost objectives [@CL-CMP-0213], by a cost input base that best represents the total activity of a typical cost accounting period [@CL-CMP-0214; @CL-CMP-8198]. In the machining and assembling illustration, a single manufacturing overhead pool is not homogeneous, and separate pools must be established [@CL-CMP-2817].

## Company calibration

Whether the company's business units are covered by this standard, and which disclosed practices apply [@Q-033], and how the pools, bases and the direct-versus-indirect split are defined and reconciled [@Q-024].
