## Purpose

Shows how a cost incurred for one purpose must be treated the same way as every other cost incurred for the same purpose in like circumstances.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | CAS [@CL-CMP-0204] |
| Codified citation | 48 CFR 9904.402-40 [@CL-CMP-0204] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-0204] |
| Applies to | Costs incurred for the same purpose in like circumstances [@CL-CMP-0204] |

All costs incurred for the same purpose in like circumstances are either direct costs only or indirect costs only [@CL-CMP-0204]. A final cost objective cannot take a cost as an indirect cost if other costs incurred for the same purpose in like circumstances were included as a direct cost [@CL-CMP-0205]. For costs that are sometimes direct and sometimes indirect, the criteria and circumstances in the Disclosure Statement decide whether the costs are for the same purpose; if no Disclosure Statement was submitted, the contractor's practices at the time of the proposal are used [@CL-CMP-0206].

## How it works

The rule is stated in terms of costs incurred and applies equally to estimates of costs in proposals. A contractor sets out in its Disclosure Statement the practices that separate direct from indirect costs, and the criteria for costs that are sometimes one and sometimes the other. Where costs serving the same purpose cannot equitably be allocated indirectly under the contractor's disclosed accounting practices, the contractor may use a method that reassigns all such costs to give an equitable distribution to all final cost objectives, or directly assign all such costs to the final cost objectives they are specifically identified with, and must then amend the Disclosure Statement.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor charges travel for one contract engineer as a direct cost but puts the same kind of travel for a second contract engineer in an overhead pool. Both trips serve the same purpose in like circumstances, so one treatment is wrong: the travel must be direct on both contracts or indirect on both.

## Never confuse

The same-purpose rule has one narrow practicality allowance based on dollar size: a direct cost of minor dollar amount may be treated as an indirect cost for reasons of practicality. A direct cost of minor dollar amount may be treated as an indirect cost for practical reasons only when the treatment is applied consistently to all final cost objectives and gives substantially the same result.

## Company calibration

Whether a Disclosure Statement is on file that sets out the criteria separating direct from indirect costs. [@Q-033]
