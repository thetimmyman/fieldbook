## Purpose

Shows how a cost incurred for one purpose must be treated the same way as every other cost incurred for the same purpose in like circumstances.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | CAS [@CL-CMP-0204] |
| Codified citation | 48 CFR 9904.402-40 [@CL-CMP-0204] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-0204] |
| Applies to | Costs incurred for the same purpose in like circumstances [@CL-CMP-0204] |

All costs incurred for the same purpose in like circumstances are either direct costs only or indirect costs only [@CL-CMP-0204]. No final cost objective may have allocated to it as an indirect cost any cost, if other costs incurred for the same purpose, in like circumstances, have been included as a direct cost of that or any other final cost objective [@CL-CMP-0205]. The Disclosure Statement submitted by the contractor, by distinguishing between direct and indirect costs and by describing the criteria and circumstances for allocating items which are sometimes direct and sometimes indirect, will be determinative as to whether or not costs are incurred for the same purpose [@CL-CMP-8034; @CL-CMP-0206]. If a contractor has not submitted a Disclosure Statement, the determination of whether specific costs are directly allocable to contracts is based upon the contractor's cost accounting practices used at the time of contract proposal [@CL-CMP-8035].

## How it works

The rule is stated in terms of costs incurred and applies equally to estimates of costs in proposals. A contractor sets out in its Disclosure Statement the practices that separate direct from indirect costs, and the criteria for costs that are sometimes one and sometimes the other. Whenever costs which serve the same purpose cannot equitably be indirectly allocated to one or more final cost objectives in accordance with the contractor's disclosed accounting practices, the contractor may either use a method for reassigning all such costs which would provide an equitable distribution to all final cost objectives, or directly assign all such costs to final cost objectives with which they are specifically identified [@CL-CMP-8806; @CL-CMP-8807]. If the contractor decides to make a change for either purpose, the Disclosure Statement shall be amended to reflect the revised accounting practices involved [@CL-CMP-8808].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor charges travel for one contract engineer as a direct cost but puts the same kind of travel for a second contract engineer in an overhead pool. Both trips serve the same purpose in like circumstances, so one treatment is wrong: the travel must be direct on both contracts or indirect on both.

## Never confuse

Any direct cost of minor dollar amount may be treated as an indirect cost for reasons of practicality where the accounting treatment for such cost is consistently applied to all final cost objectives, provided that such treatment produces results which are substantially the same as the results which would have been obtained if such cost had been treated as a direct cost [@CL-CMP-8809; @CL-CMP-8810].

## Company calibration

Whether a Disclosure Statement is on file that sets out the criteria separating direct from indirect costs. [@Q-033]
