## Purpose

Sets out which employee morale, health, welfare, food service and dormitory costs are allowable, and which categories stay barred inside an otherwise allowable programme.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-0353] |
| Codified citation | FAR 31.205-13(a) [@CL-CMP-0353] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 31.205-13(a) [@CL-CMP-9945] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-0353] |
| Applies to | Not established. |

Costs of activities designed to improve working conditions, employer-employee relations, morale or employee performance are allowable in the aggregate, net of the income those activities generate, and remain subject to the limits in the section [@CL-CMP-0353].

Two named categories are unallowable inside that allowance, among the section's other limits: gifts, apart from performance awards and achievement awards made under an established plan, and recreation, apart from company sponsored sports teams and employee organizations that foster loyalty, teamwork or fitness [@CL-CMP-0354; @CL-CMP-0355]. Food and dormitory losses are allowable only where the contractor's objective is to operate those services on a break-even basis, apart from the unusual-circumstances losses described below [@CL-CMP-0356; @CL-CMP-9620; @CL-CMP-9621; @CL-CMP-9622].

## How it works

The section allows the activity first and then removes named categories, so each cost is tested twice: once as part of a legitimate welfare activity and once against the gift and recreation bars [@CL-CMP-0354]. Income the activities generate is netted against their cost, which is why the test is an aggregate one [@CL-CMP-0353]. Food and dormitory services carry an extra objective test, so a service run to break even can show an allowable loss, while a loss from a service given away or priced at rates not conducive to break-even is not allowable unless the contractor demonstrates unusual circumstances [@CL-CMP-0356; @CL-CMP-9620; @CL-CMP-9621; @CL-CMP-9622].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers must use the RFO FAR part 31 text in place of the codified text [@CL-CMP-9350], effective immediately under Revision 1 signed on 2026-08-04 [@CL-CMP-9351]. RFO FAR 31.205-13(a) keeps the allowance for aggregate costs of activities designed to improve working conditions, employer-employee relations, morale and employee performance, net of income generated, subject to the limitations in the section [@CL-CMP-9945]. RFO paragraph (c) keeps the bar on recreation apart from company sponsored sports teams and employee organizations [@CL-CMP-9947]. RFO paragraph (d)(1)(i) says that food and dormitory losses are allowable only if the contractor's objective is to operate those services on a break-even basis [@CL-CMP-9948], and paragraph (d)(1)(iii) separately says a loss may be allowed if the contractor can demonstrate that unusual circumstances exist [@CL-CMP-9950; @CL-CMP-9951]. RFO paragraph (b) says costs of gifts are unallowable and cites 31.205-6(e) for awards for performance [@CL-CMP-9946]. RFO paragraph (d)(1)(ii) says that losses sustained because food services or lodging are furnished without charge or at prices or rates "that would not be conducive" to the break-even objective are not allowable, except as described in paragraph (d)(1)(iii), and prints no word "obviously" before "would" [@CL-CMP-9949], where the codified paragraph reads "which obviously would not be conducive" [@CL-CMP-9620]. The codified paragraph (d)(1)(iii) says a loss may be allowed "to the extent that" the contractor can demonstrate unusual circumstances [@CL-CMP-9621; @CL-CMP-9622], and the RFO paragraph says "may be allowed if" the contractor can demonstrate that unusual circumstances exist [@CL-CMP-9950; @CL-CMP-9951].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

The company spends 40,000 on a fitness centre, 15,000 on a staff social evening with gifts, and earns 5,000 from vending machines in the same programme. The 40,000 is an allowable welfare activity and the 5,000 reduces its net cost, while the 15,000 of gifts and social spending is unallowable.

## Never confuse

A welfare activity is not the same as recreation or a gift. A wellness centre, a house publication or an employee counselling service is tested as spend that improves working conditions, while recreation and gifts stay unallowable even when booked to the same programme, except for the stated exceptions for company sponsored sports teams, employee organizations and plan-based awards. The cross-reference for performance awards differs: the codified gifts paragraph cites 31.205-6(f) [@CL-CMP-0354], and the RFO paragraph cites 31.205-6(e) [@CL-CMP-9946]. Screening that split before costs reach a pool is the discipline described in [Penalties for unallowable costs in final rate proposals](cmp-unallowable-cost-penalty.md).
