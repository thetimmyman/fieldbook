## Purpose

Cost Accounting Standard 401 governs consistency between the practices used in estimating costs for a proposal and the practices used in accumulating and reporting costs during contract performance. This concept hub sets out what the standard requires and links the detailed reference and estimation pages across the corpus.

## The rule

The purpose of CAS 401 is to ensure that a contractor's cost accounting practices used in estimating costs for a proposal are consistent with the practices used in accumulating and reporting costs [@CL-EST-2492]. Consistency in applying cost accounting practices is necessary to enhance the likelihood that comparable transactions are treated alike [@CL-EST-2493]. On individual contracts, consistent application facilitates the preparation of reliable cost estimates used in pricing and allows comparison with performance costs [@CL-EST-2494]. Under the fundamental requirement, a contractor's practices used in estimating costs to price a proposal must be consistent with the practices used in accumulating and reporting costs [@CL-CMP-0201]. Consistency is required in three respects: classifying cost elements or functions as direct or indirect, assigning cost elements or functions to indirect cost pools, and choosing methods to allocate indirect costs to the contract [@CL-CMP-0202]. The standard permits the grouping of homogeneous costs when estimating contract costs by individual element or function is not practicable [@CL-CMP-8801]. Proposal grouping of homogeneous costs is not per se an inconsistent application when those costs are accumulated and reported in greater detail on an actual cost basis during contract performance [@CL-CMP-0834]. Estimated costs must be presented in sufficient detail that any significant cost can be compared with the actual cost accumulated and reported for it [@CL-CMP-8802].

Adherence to the consistency requirement is determined as of the date of contract award, unless the contractor has submitted cost or pricing data under statute [@CL-CMP-8803]. When such data were submitted, adherence is determined as of the date of price agreement shown on the signed cost or pricing data document [@CL-CMP-8804]. Changes in established cost accounting practices during contract performance may be made in accordance with part 99 [@CL-CMP-8805]. There is no exemption from CAS 401 [@CL-CMP-8650]. The standard became effective on April 17, 1992 [@CL-CMP-8651]. All contractors under full or modified coverage remain subject to CAS 401 and must consistently follow their disclosed or actual cost accounting practices [@CL-CMP-1515].

## How it works

Estimating costs means forecasting a future result in terms of cost based on information available at the time [@CL-EST-2858]. Accumulating costs means collecting cost data in an organized manner, such as through a system of accounts [@CL-EST-2856]. Actual cost means an amount determined on the basis of cost incurred as distinguished from forecasted cost, including standard cost properly adjusted for applicable variance [@CL-EST-2857]. The standard operates by comparing proposal estimates with the costs accumulated and reported during contract performance [@CL-EST-2494].

The standard illustrates consistency by pairing an estimated average direct labor rate for manufacturing direct labor by category or function with actual costs recorded per individual and collected by labor category or function [@CL-CMP-2776]. In contrast, estimating engineering labor by cost function while accumulating total engineering labor in a single undifferentiated account is an inconsistent practice [@CL-CMP-2778].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A business unit estimates 600,000 of engineering labor for a proposal as a single lump sum covering design, test and drafting, and subsequently records actual engineering labor in one undifferentiated account, meaning no individual function can be compared with its estimate. This approach is inconsistent. If the unit instead estimates design at 300,000, test at 200,000 and drafting at 100,000, summing to 600,000 (300,000 plus 200,000 plus 100,000), and records actual labor in those same three separate functional accounts, each function can be directly compared with its estimate. This example is illustrative only, and the contract itself governs.

## Never confuse

CAS 401 requires consistency between practices used in estimating costs and those used in accumulating and reporting costs [@CL-CMP-0201], whereas CAS 402 requires that all costs incurred for the same purpose in like circumstances are direct costs only or indirect costs only with respect to final cost objectives [@CL-CMP-0204]. CAS 402 ensures each type of cost is allocated only once and on only one basis to any cost objective [@CL-EST-2906], meaning an accounting practice can satisfy CAS 401 consistency yet still violate the separate single-purpose classification rule of CAS 402.

Whether the company's business units are covered by this standard, and which disclosed practices apply. [@Q-033]
