## Purpose

Professional and consultant service costs are allowable only when reasonable in relation to the services rendered and not contingent on recovery from the Government, and are unallowable when the services improperly influence the award process or violate law.

Costs of professional and consultant services are allowable, subject to paragraphs (b) through (f) of the cost principle (but see 31.205-30 and 31.205-47), when reasonable in relation to the services rendered and when not contingent upon recovery of the costs from the Government [@CL-CMP-0458]. They are unallowable when the services are intended to improperly influence the contents of solicitations, the evaluation of proposals or quotations, or the selection of sources for contract award [@CL-CMP-0459]. Retainer fees are allowable only when supported by evidence that the covered services are necessary and customary [@CL-CMP-0460; @CL-CMP-0825; @CL-CMP-0826], together with evidence that the level of past services justifies the amount, that the fee is reasonable compared with maintaining an in-house capability, and that the actual services performed are documented.

## How it works

The cost principle defines professional and consultant services as services by people who are not officers or employees of the contractor. Allowability depends on reasonableness and on the cost not being contingent on recovery from the Government. The contracting officer weighs factors such as the necessity of contracting for the service, past cost patterns, and whether the work could be done more economically by an employee. Retainer fees and fees for services rendered must be supported by documentation of the nature and scope of the service.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor hires an outside engineer for a technical study. The fee is comparable to market rates, the study is needed because the staff lacks the skill, and payment is not tied to recovering the cost from the Government. The cost is allowable, and the company keeps the engagement agreement and the consultant's report as supporting documentation.

## Never confuse

A professional or consultant is not an officer or employee of the contractor. Costs for these outside services are allowable only when reasonable and not contingent on recovery, but are unallowable when the services improperly influence an award or violate a statute. The company also must keep such unallowable costs out of its pools; see [Penalties for unallowable costs](cmp-unallowable-cost-penalty.md).
