## Purpose

Professional and consultant service costs are allowable only when reasonable in relation to the services rendered and not contingent on recovery from the Government, and are unallowable when the services improperly influence the award process or violate law.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-0458] |
| Codified citation | FAR 31.205-33 [@CL-CMP-0458] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 31.205-33 [@CL-CMP-9487] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-0458] |
| Applies to | Costs of professional and consultant services when reasonable and not contingent on recovery from the Government [@CL-CMP-0458] |

Costs of professional and consultant services are allowable, subject to paragraphs (b) through (f) of the cost principle (but see 31.205-30 and 31.205-47), when reasonable in relation to the services rendered and when not contingent upon recovery of the costs from the Government [@CL-CMP-0458]. They are unallowable when the services are intended to improperly influence the contents of solicitations, the evaluation of proposals or quotations, or the selection of sources for contract award [@CL-CMP-0459]. Retainer fees are allowable only when supported by evidence that the covered services are necessary and customary [@CL-CMP-0460], that the level of past services justifies the amount [@CL-CMP-0825], that the fee is reasonable compared with maintaining an in-house capability, and that the actual services performed are documented [@CL-CMP-0826].

## How it works

The RFO cost principle defines professional and consultant services as services provided by persons who are members of a particular profession or possess a special skill and who are not officers or employees of the contractor [@CL-CMP-9486]. Allowability depends on reasonableness and on the cost not being contingent on recovery from the Government. The contracting officer weighs factors such as the necessity of contracting for the service, past cost patterns, and whether the work could be done more economically by an employee. Retainer fees and fees for services rendered must be supported by documentation of the nature and scope of the service.

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 31 text in place of the codified text [@CL-CMP-9350], effective immediately under Revision 1 signed on 2026-08-04 [@CL-CMP-9351]. RFO FAR 31.205-33 keeps the definition of professional and consultant services as services provided by persons who are members of a particular profession or possess a special skill and who are not officers or employees of the contractor [@CL-CMP-9486]. The allowability rule is paragraph (c), subject to "this section" instead of paragraphs (b) through (f), and it keeps the cross-reference to FAR 31.205-30 and 31.205-47 [@CL-CMP-9487]. Paragraph (d) keeps the rule that costs of services performed under the circumstances it lists are unallowable [@CL-CMP-9488], including services intended to improperly influence an award [@CL-CMP-9489] and services in violation of statutes or regulations prohibiting improper business practices or conflicts of interest [@CL-CMP-9490]. Paragraph (e) says the contracting officer must consider the listed factors, and its lead-in omits the codified words "among others" [@CL-CMP-9491], among them the necessity of contracting for the service and the past pattern of acquiring such services [@CL-CMP-9492], and whether the service can be performed more economically by employment [@CL-CMP-9493]. Paragraph (f) says retainer fees "are allowable when supported by evidence" that the services are necessary and customary and that the level of past services justifies the amount [@CL-CMP-9494], and also that the fee is reasonable in comparison with maintaining an in-house capability and that the actual services performed are documented [@CL-CMP-9495]; codified paragraph (e) states the evidence as a condition of allowability [@CL-CMP-0460]. The rule that fees for services rendered are allowable only when supported by evidence of the nature and scope of the service sits inside that paragraph at (f)(4)(i) [@CL-CMP-9496], and sub-paragraph (f)(4)(ii) introduces the evidence necessary to determine that work performed is proper, does not violate law or regulation, and includes the items listed under it [@CL-CMP-9524].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor hires an outside engineer for a technical study. The fee is comparable to market rates, the study is needed because the staff lacks the skill, and payment is not tied to recovering the cost from the Government. The cost is allowable, and the company keeps the engagement agreement and the consultant's report as supporting documentation.

## Never confuse

A professional or consultant is a member of a particular profession or has a special skill, and is not an officer or employee of the contractor. Costs for these outside services are allowable only when reasonable and not contingent on recovery, but are unallowable when the services improperly influence an award or violate a statute. The company also must keep such unallowable costs out of its pools; see [Penalties for unallowable costs](cmp-unallowable-cost-penalty.md). The allowability test is codified paragraph (b) [@CL-CMP-0458] but RFO paragraph (c) [@CL-CMP-9487], and the retainer fee rules are codified paragraph (e) [@CL-CMP-0460] but RFO paragraph (f) [@CL-CMP-9494], so a paragraph letter cited from one text does not match the other.
