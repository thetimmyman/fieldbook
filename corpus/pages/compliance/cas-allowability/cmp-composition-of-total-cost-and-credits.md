## Purpose

Sets out what makes up a contract's total cost and how income, rebates and other credits that relate to allowable costs reach the Government.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-7508] |
| Codified citation | FAR 31.201-1 [@CL-CMP-7508] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 31.201-1 [@CL-CMP-9373]; RFO FAR 31.201-5 [@CL-CMP-9376] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-7508] |
| Applies to | The total cost of a contract and credits relating to allowable costs [@CL-CMP-7508; @CL-CMP-7511] |

The total cost of a contract, including standard costs properly adjusted for applicable variances, is the sum of the direct and indirect costs allocable to the contract, incurred or to be incurred, plus any allocable cost of money pursuant to FAR 31.205-10, less any allocable credits [@CL-CMP-7508]. In ascertaining what constitutes a cost, any generally accepted method of determining or estimating costs that is equitable and consistently applied may be used [@CL-CMP-7509].

While the total cost of a contract includes all costs properly allocable to the contract, the allowable costs to the Government are limited to those allocable costs which are allowable pursuant to FAR part 31 and applicable agency supplements [@CL-CMP-7510]. The applicable portion of any income, rebate, allowance or other credit relating to any allowable cost and received by or accruing to the contractor shall be credited to the Government either as a cost reduction or by cash refund [@CL-CMP-7511].

## How it works

The total cost of a contract is the sum of the direct and indirect costs allocable to the contract, incurred or to be incurred, plus any allocable cost of money pursuant to 31.205-10, less any allocable credits [@CL-CMP-7508]. The cost of money is determined under FAR 31.205-10 (see [cost of money as an imputed cost](../cas-allowability/cmp-cost-of-money.md)), and standard costs enter total cost when properly adjusted for applicable variances [@CL-CMP-7508]. Any generally accepted method of determining or estimating costs that is equitable and consistently applied may be used [@CL-CMP-7509].

While the total cost of a contract includes all costs properly allocable to the contract, the allowable costs to the Government are limited to those allocable costs which are allowable pursuant to Part 31 and applicable agency supplements [@CL-CMP-7510].

The applicable portion of any income, rebate, allowance, or other credit relating to any allowable cost and received by or accruing to the contractor shall be credited to the Government either as a cost reduction or by cash refund [@CL-CMP-7511]. See FAR 31.205-6(j)(3) for rules governing refund or credit to the Government associated with pension adjustments and asset reversions [@CL-CMP-7512]. See [allowable compensation for personal services](../cas-allowability/cmp-compensation-personal-services.md).

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers must use the RFO FAR part 31 text in place of the codified text [@CL-CMP-9350], effective immediately under Revision 1 signed on 2026-08-04 [@CL-CMP-9351]. RFO FAR 31.201-1 keeps the formula for the total cost of a contract [@CL-CMP-9373] and the rule that any generally accepted method of determining or estimating costs that is equitable and consistently applied may be used [@CL-CMP-9374]. It limits the allowable costs to the Government to those allocable costs that are allowable pursuant to this part [@CL-CMP-9375], where the codified text adds applicable agency supplements [@CL-CMP-7510]. RFO FAR 31.201-5 requires a credit relating to an allowable cost under a cost contract, received by or accruing to the contractor, to be credited to the Government [@CL-CMP-9376], as a cost reduction to the contract or a cash refund [@CL-CMP-9377]. It refers to 31.105-6(i)(3) for pension adjustments and asset reversions [@CL-CMP-9377], where the codified text refers to 31.205-6(j)(3) [@CL-CMP-7512].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contract carries 400,000 of direct cost, 100,000 of allocable indirect cost and 50,000 of allocable cost of money, so the total cost before credits is 550,000 [@CL-CMP-7508]. Of the indirect cost, 5,000 is not allowable under part 31, so the allowable costs to the Government before credits are 545,000, because allowable costs are limited to allocable costs that are allowable [@CL-CMP-7510]. A 20,000 rebate relates to an allowable material cost and accrues to the contractor, and the whole 20,000 is assumed to be the applicable portion, so it shall be credited to the Government as a cost reduction or by cash refund [@CL-CMP-7511]. Under the RFO text the credit rule is stated for a credit relating to an allowable cost under a cost contract, so the example assumes a cost contract [@CL-CMP-9376]. Total cost after the credit is 530,000 [@CL-CMP-7508], and the allowable cost to the Government after the credit is 525,000 [@CL-CMP-7510; @CL-CMP-7511].

## Never confuse

Allocable and allowable are different limits: the total cost of a contract includes all costs properly allocable to the contract, and the allowable costs to the Government are limited to those allocable costs which are allowable [@CL-CMP-7510]. The applicable portion of any income, rebate, allowance, or other credit relating to any allowable cost and received by or accruing to the contractor shall be credited to the Government either as a cost reduction or by cash refund [@CL-CMP-7511]. The codified text limits allowable costs to those allowable under FAR part 31 and applicable agency supplements [@CL-CMP-7510], while the RFO text limits them to those allowable pursuant to this part [@CL-CMP-9375]. The codified credit rule covers the applicable portion of any credit relating to any allowable cost [@CL-CMP-7511], while the RFO credit rule covers a credit relating to an allowable cost under a cost contract [@CL-CMP-9376]. The allocability and allowability tests behind the two measures are set out in [allocability](../cas-allowability/cmp-allocability.md) and [allowability factors](../cas-allowability/cmp-allowability-factors.md).
