## Purpose

Cost Accounting Standard 403 establishes criteria for allocating home office expenses to the segments of an organization based on the beneficial or causal relationship between those expenses and the receiving segments. This concept hub sets out what the standard requires and links the detailed reference and allocation pages across the corpus.

## The rule

The purpose of CAS 403 is to establish criteria for allocating home office expenses to organizational segments based on the beneficial or causal relationship between the expenses and the receiving segments [@CL-EST-2495; @CL-CMP-0551]. The standard provides for identifying expenses for direct allocation to segments to the maximum extent practical [@CL-EST-2496; @CL-CMP-0552], accumulating significant nondirectly allocated expenses into logical and relatively homogeneous pools allocated on bases reflecting their relationship to the segments [@CL-EST-2497], and allocating any remaining residual home office expenses to all segments [@CL-EST-2498]. The standard does not govern reallocating a segment's share of home office expenses to individual contracts or other cost objectives [@CL-EST-2499]. Appropriate implementation limits residual expenses to the costs of managing the organization as a whole [@CL-EST-2634].

A segment may not be charged a cost indirectly through a homogeneous expense pool or the residual pool if other costs incurred for the same purpose were allocated directly to a segment [@CL-CMP-0553]. Centralized service function expenses are allocated on the basis of the service furnished to or received by each segment [@CL-CMP-0554]. Residual expenses are allocated to all segments using a base representative of total segment activity, unless required to use the three factor formula [@CL-CMP-0555; @CL-RTE-0686]. Under the residual expense test, thresholds of 3.35 percent of the first $100 million, 0.95 percent of the next $200 million, 0.30 percent of the next $2.7 billion and 0.20 percent of all amounts over $3 billion are applied to aggregate segment revenue from the previous year [@CL-CMP-0556]. The standard became effective on April 17, 1992 [@CL-EST-2511], applying at the start of the next full fiscal year after receipt of an applicable contract for contractors without prior CAS 403 coverage [@CL-EST-2512], while contractors with prior fully covered contracts continue applicability immediately upon receipt [@CL-EST-2642].

## How it works

To allocate means to assign an item or group of items of cost to one or more cost objectives, encompassing both direct cost assignments and the reassignment of shares from an indirect cost pool [@CL-EST-2863]. A home office is an office responsible for directing or managing two or more segments of an organization [@CL-EST-2864]. A segment is a division, product department, plant or other subdivision reporting directly to a home office, typically identified with responsibility for profit or producing a product or service [@CL-EST-2867].

Ordinarily, each home office service or management function must be separately identified for allocation under an appropriate technique [@CL-RTE-0680]. Centralized service functions follow a hierarchy of preferred techniques reflecting causal or beneficial links, where the preferred representation measures the activity of the performing organization [@CL-RTE-0682]. Labor-oriented, machine-oriented or space-oriented supporting functions are allocated by a rate, such as a rate per labor hour, machine hour or square foot, unless such measures are unavailable or impractical to ascertain [@CL-RTE-0683]. When activity rates cannot be ascertained, allocation is based on a measurement of the supporting function's output [@CL-RTE-0684].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A home office incurs 10,000,000 of expenses in a year across two segments, X and Y. Expenses of 2,000,000 benefit only X and 1,000,000 benefit only Y, so each amount is allocated directly to that segment. A centralized data processing service costing 3,000,000 is allocated on machine hours across 1,000 total hours (600 hours for X and 400 hours for Y), yielding 1,800,000 for X (60 percent of 3,000,000) and 1,200,000 for Y (40 percent of 3,000,000). The remaining 4,000,000 of residual expenses (10,000,000 minus 2,000,000 minus 1,000,000 minus 3,000,000) is allocated on a total-activity ratio of 60 percent to 40 percent (assuming the residual test does not mandate the three factor formula), giving X 2,400,000 (60 percent of 4,000,000) and Y 1,600,000 (40 percent of 4,000,000). In total, segment X receives 6,200,000 (2,000,000 plus 1,800,000 plus 2,400,000) and segment Y receives 3,800,000 (1,000,000 plus 1,200,000 plus 1,600,000), which together equal the full 10,000,000 of home office expenses. This example is illustrative only, and the contract itself governs.

## Never confuse

CAS 403 governs the allocation of home office expenses to segments based on beneficial or causal relationships and explicitly stops short of reallocating those expenses to contracts [@CL-EST-2495; @CL-EST-2499], whereas CAS 410 governs how a business unit groups its general and administrative expenses into a separate indirect pool and allocates them only to final cost objectives using a cost input base representing total activity [@CL-CMP-0213; @CL-CMP-0214]. Any home office expenses received by an individual segment are subsequent segment-level allocations governed by CAS 410 rules [@CL-CMP-7887].
