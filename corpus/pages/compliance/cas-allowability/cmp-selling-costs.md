## Purpose

Shows which selling efforts the cost principle addresses, which of them are allowable, and when a payment to a seller or agent qualifies.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-0773] |
| Codified citation | FAR 31.205-38 [@CL-CMP-0773] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-0773] |
| Applies to | Selling efforts to market the contractor's products or services [@CL-CMP-0773] |

Selling is a generic term covering all efforts to market the contractor's products or services, some of which other subsections of the cost principles address [@CL-CMP-0773]. The costs of any selling efforts other than those the selling cost principle addresses are unallowable [@CL-CMP-0774].

Direct selling means acts or actions to induce particular customers to purchase particular products or services of the contractor, and it is characterized by person-to-person contact [@CL-CMP-0775]; its cost is allowable [@CL-CMP-0776]. Market planning costs are allowable except for long-range market planning, which is subject to a separate allowability provision [@CL-CMP-0777], and bid and proposal costs are defined elsewhere and carry that subsection's allowability provisions [@CL-CMP-0779].

Notwithstanding any other provision of the subsection, sellers' or agents' compensation, fees, commissions, percentages, and retainer or brokerage fees are allowable only when paid to bona fide employees or to established commercial or selling agencies maintained by the contractor for the purpose of securing business, whether or not contingent upon the award of contracts [@CL-CMP-1113; @CL-CMP-0778].

## How it works

The principle routes each selling activity to the provision that governs it and treats anything unrouted as unallowable [@CL-CMP-0774]. Direct selling, which is aimed at particular customers, is expressly allowed [@CL-CMP-0775; @CL-CMP-0776], as are market planning costs other than long-range ones [@CL-CMP-0777]. Payments to intermediaries pass only through a gate: the recipient must be a bona fide employee or an established commercial or selling agency maintained by the contractor for the purpose of securing business, and contingency on award does not open that gate [@CL-CMP-1113; @CL-CMP-0778]. Long-range market planning and bid and proposal work are left to their own allowability provisions [@CL-CMP-0777; @CL-CMP-0779].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A company spends 40,000 on a general awareness campaign, 60,000 on direct selling visits to named customers, and 25,000 on a commission to an outside broker who is not an established selling agency. The direct selling cost is allowable; the general awareness spend is not direct selling, and as advertising or corporate image enhancement it is allowable only as the provisions governing those activities allow; the broker commission is unallowable because the broker is neither a bona fide employee nor an established commercial or selling agency maintained by the contractor.

## Never confuse

Selling is not one allowable category: only the efforts the principle addresses are covered, and the rest are unallowable [@CL-CMP-0774]. Direct selling is not broad market awareness work, because it is aimed at inducing particular customers to buy particular products or services [@CL-CMP-0775]. A commission is not allowable merely because a sale followed; the payee must qualify [@CL-CMP-0778]. Bid and proposal cost has its own card at [Bid and proposal costs](cmp-ird-and-bp-costs.md).

## Company calibration

Whether the company tags each selling cost to a category the principle addresses and pays commissions only to qualifying employees or established agencies [@Q-044].
