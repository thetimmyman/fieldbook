## Purpose

Gives the definition of deferred compensation and a few illustrations from the deferred compensation and insurance cost standards, without restating the rule cards.

Deferred compensation means an award made by an employer to compensate an employee in a future cost accounting period or periods for services rendered in one or more cost accounting periods prior to the date of the receipt of compensation by the employee [@CL-CMP-3415]. That definition is subject to an exclusion: it shall not include the amount of year end accruals for salaries, wages, or bonuses that are to be paid within a reasonable period of time after the end of a cost accounting period [@CL-CMP-3416]. The definitions section also defines other terms, which this card does not claim.

In one deferred compensation illustration, the total cost of the stock options is 1,000 shares multiplied by the difference of the option price and the market price ($26-22), or $4,000 [@CL-CMP-3417].

In one insurance illustration, only one-sixth of the premium should have been included in cost in a cost accounting period within which only one-sixth of the policy term fell [@CL-CMP-3418]. In another, had the contractor's plan been to make a self-insurance charge for such losses, any difference between the self-insurance charge and actual losses in that cost accounting period would not have been allocable as an insurance cost [@CL-CMP-3419].

## How it works

The definition marks what counts as deferred compensation and what does not [@CL-CMP-3415; @CL-CMP-3416]. The stock option illustration shows a cost measured by the gap between market price and option price across the shares awarded [@CL-CMP-3417]. The insurance illustrations show two limits: cost belongs to the periods the policy term covers [@CL-CMP-3418], and, had the plan been to make a self-insurance charge, any difference between the charge and actual losses would not have been allocable as an insurance cost [@CL-CMP-3419]. The [deferred compensation card](cmp-cas-415-deferred-compensation.md) and the [insurance card](cmp-cas-416-insurance.md) hold the rules.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A policy term runs for 4 years and the cost accounting period covers one year of it. Only a quarter of the premium belongs in that period.

A contractor awards options on 500 shares. The market price is 30 and the option price is 24, so the total cost of the options is 500 times 6, or 3,000.

## Never confuse

Deferred compensation is not every payment made after the period ends. Year end accruals for salaries, wages or bonuses paid within a reasonable period after the end of the period are outside the definition [@CL-CMP-3416]. A self-insurance charge is also not the same as actual loss: in the illustration, had the plan been to make a self-insurance charge, any difference between the charge and the actual losses in the period would not have been allocable as an insurance cost [@CL-CMP-3419].
