## Purpose

Sets out how the auditor reports business system deficiencies found in other audits, and the contracting officer's authority over withholds that can follow.

## The rule

| Applies to | Business system deficiencies found during non-system audits, and contractors with covered contracts [@CL-CMP-1953; @CL-CMP-1680] |

Material weaknesses or system deficiencies in any of the six DFARS 252.242-7998 contractor business systems during non-system audits, such as forward pricing proposals, incurred cost audits or real-time testing, call for a separate Business System Deficiency Report Assignment that the auditor must promptly establish [@CL-CMP-1953]. That reporting includes both material weaknesses and internal control deficiencies that, while less than material, still warrant the attention of the contractor and the contracting officer [@CL-CMP-1677].

The reported issues found when examining contractors with covered contracts may be subject to payment withholding if the administrative contracting officer makes a final determination to disapprove a contractor's business system in accordance with the clause at DFARS 252.242-7998 [@CL-CMP-1680].

## How it works

The separate deficiency report remains an integral part of the originating GAGAS examination rather than a standalone audit [@CL-CMP-1969]. Because it is part of the primary engagement, auditors do not need to duplicate procedural documentation, such as risk assessments, within the deficiency assignment working papers [@CL-CMP-1678]. To maintain a clear audit trail, both the primary examination report and the deficiency report must cross-reference each other and explicitly state that the deficiency report is a component of the original engagement [@CL-CMP-1679]. The objective is only to establish whether the noncompliance identified in the originating audit is a material noncompliance, and therefore represents a material weakness in compliance with the DFARS criteria, or is a system deficiency in internal control [@CL-CMP-1970].

In certain circumstances, the auditor may determine that material weaknesses and system deficiencies identified during a business system audit require immediate consideration of the contracting official to ensure timely resolution of the identified business system noncompliances [@CL-CMP-1972]. In those rare instances, the auditor should open a business system deficiency report assignment (11090) to report the issue while continuing toward completion of the originating business system audit [@CL-CMP-1971].

The contracting officer has the sole discretion and authority to identify the covered contracts from which to withhold payments [@CL-CMP-1681]. The contracting officer also has the sole authority to promptly notify the contractor, in writing, of the contracting officer's determination to implement payment withholding in accordance with the clause at DFARS 252.242-7998, Contractor Business Systems [@CL-CMP-1682]. The withhold rates and limits are on [Business system withholds](cmp-business-system-withholds.md), and the meaning of a covered contract is on [Contractor business systems](cmp-contractor-business-systems.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

During an incurred cost audit, DCAA finds that a contractor does not reconcile its physical inventory to the material records. This is a possible material weakness in a business system, so DCAA opens a separate deficiency report assignment. The assignment cites the incurred cost audit and carries only the working papers that support the finding. Each report refers to the other. Whether any withhold follows is up to the contracting officer, after a final determination to disapprove the system.
