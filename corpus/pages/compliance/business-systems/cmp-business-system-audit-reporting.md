## Purpose

Shows how a business system audit report classifies findings as material weakness, system deficiency or less than material noncompliance, and where each is placed in the report.

| Applies to | Audit reports on contractor business system compliance [@CL-CMP-1973] |

Reporting on contractor business system compliance with the applicable DFARS criteria, laws and regulations, and contract terms must be made in all audit reports [@CL-CMP-1973]. A material weakness is a deficiency or combination of deficiencies in the internal control over information in contractor business systems, such that there is a reasonable possibility that a material misstatement of such information will not be prevented, or detected and corrected, on a timely basis [@CL-CMP-1672]. A reasonable possibility exists when the likelihood of an event occurring is either probable or more than remote but less than likely [@CL-CMP-1673]. A system deficiency is a deficiency or combination of deficiencies in the internal control over information in contractor business systems that does not rise to the level of a material weakness [@CL-CMP-1674]. A less than material noncompliance that warrants attention of those charged with governance (the contractor and contracting officer) is a noncompliance that does not have a material impact on the acceptability of the business system but is important enough to be communicated [@CL-CMP-1675]. See [Contractor business systems](cmp-contractor-business-systems.md).

## How it works

A material weakness is placed in an Exhibit with a generally adverse opinion type, a system deficiency is placed in an Exhibit with a generally qualified opinion type, and a less than material noncompliance that warrants the attention of those charged with governance is placed in an Appendix with a generally unqualified opinion type [@CL-CMP-1676]. The Statements of Condition and Recommendations Exhibits in the audit report should document any material weaknesses or system deficiencies with DFARS criteria disclosed during the examination [@CL-CMP-1950]. A less than material noncompliance that warrants attention of those charged with governance (the contractor and contracting officer) should be included in an Appendix [@CL-CMP-1951]. For all reported deficiencies including those that are less than material noncompliances, yet warrant the attention of those charged with governance (the contractor and contracting officer), the auditor prepares a statement of condition and recommendation (SOCAR) and includes the SOCAR in the audit report as an exhibit or appendix [@CL-CMP-1952; @CL-CMP-0162].

The auditor is required to identify the audit period in the audit report [@CL-CMP-1954]. When reporting material weaknesses and/or system deficiencies, the auditor identifies an approximate dollar impact or effect of the deficiencies, where applicable, and the calculation used to quantify the impact does not need to be exact but should provide a reasonable estimate [@CL-CMP-1955]. When auditors identify material weaknesses or system deficiencies in any of the six DFARS 252.242-7998 contractor business systems during non-system audits, such as forward pricing proposals, incurred cost audits or real-time testing, they must promptly establish a separate Business System Deficiency Report Assignment [@CL-CMP-1953]. That reporting includes both material weaknesses and internal control deficiencies that, while less than material, still warrant the attention of the contractor and the contracting officer [@CL-CMP-1677].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An audit of a contractor's estimating system finds two issues. The first is a missing control over reconciling actual costs to vendor quotes, with a probable risk of material misstatement in proposals. The auditor reports it as a material weakness in an exhibit, with an adverse opinion in view. The second is a formatting slip in a recurring proposal checklist that does not affect any price. The auditor reports it in an appendix as a less than material noncompliance.

## Never confuse

A system deficiency is a deficiency or combination of deficiencies in the internal control over information in contractor business systems that does not rise to the level of a material weakness [@CL-CMP-1674]. The reported issues found when examining contractors with covered contracts may be subject to payment withholding if the administrative contracting officer makes a final determination to disapprove a contractor's business system in accordance with the clause at DFARS 252.242-7998 [@CL-CMP-1680]. See [Business system withholds](cmp-business-system-withholds.md) and [Accounting system policy](cmp-dod-accounting-system-policy.md).

## Company calibration

Sort every open audit finding into material weakness, system deficiency or less than material noncompliance, so that corrective actions follow the same classes. [@Q-014]
