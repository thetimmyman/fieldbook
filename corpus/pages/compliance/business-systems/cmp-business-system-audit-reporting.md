## Purpose

Shows how a business system audit report classifies findings as material weakness, system deficiency or less than material noncompliance, and where each is placed in the report.

## The rule

| Applies to | Audit reports on contractor business system compliance [@CL-CMP-1973] |

Reporting on contractor business system compliance with the applicable DFARS criteria, laws and regulations, and contract terms must be made in all audit reports [@CL-CMP-1973]. A system deficiency is a deficiency or combination of deficiencies in internal control over information in contractor business systems that does not rise to the level of a material weakness [@CL-CMP-1674]. A less than material noncompliance that warrants the attention of those charged with governance does not have a material impact on the acceptability of the business system but is important enough to be communicated [@CL-CMP-1675].

The manual repeats the clause definition of a material weakness and of a reasonable possibility [@CL-CMP-1672; @CL-CMP-1673]. The definitions are on [Contractor business systems](cmp-contractor-business-systems.md).

## How it works

The manual's table pairs a material weakness with a generally adverse opinion, a system deficiency with a generally qualified opinion, and a less than material noncompliance with a generally unqualified opinion [@CL-CMP-1676]. Material weaknesses and system deficiencies are documented in the Statements of Condition and Recommendations Exhibits in the audit report [@CL-CMP-1950]. A less than material noncompliance that warrants the attention of those charged with governance should be included in an Appendix [@CL-CMP-1951]. For all reported deficiencies, including those less than material noncompliances, the auditor prepares a statement of condition and recommendation [@CL-CMP-1952].

The auditor is required to identify the audit period in the report [@CL-CMP-1954]. When reporting material weaknesses and system deficiencies, the auditor identifies an approximate dollar impact where applicable, and the calculation does not need to be exact [@CL-CMP-1955]. The deficiency reporting the manual requires includes both material weaknesses and internal control deficiencies that, while less than material, still warrant the attention of the contractor and the contracting officer [@CL-CMP-1677].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An audit of a contractor's estimating system finds two issues. The first is a missing control over reconciling actual costs to vendor quotes, with a probable risk of material misstatement in proposals. The auditor reports it as a material weakness in an exhibit, with an adverse opinion in view. The second is a formatting slip in a recurring proposal checklist that does not affect any price. The auditor reports it in an appendix as a less than material noncompliance, with a roughly 4,000 impact noted where applicable.

## Never confuse

A system deficiency is a lesser finding than a material weakness; it does not rise to that level [@CL-CMP-1674]. An audit finding is also not the contracting officer's final determination. Payments may be withheld if the administrative contracting officer makes a final determination to disapprove the system [@CL-CMP-1680]. See [Business system withholds](cmp-business-system-withholds.md) and [Accounting system policy](cmp-dod-accounting-system-policy.md).

## Company calibration

Sort every open audit finding into material weakness, system deficiency or less than material noncompliance, so that corrective actions follow the same classes. [@Q-014]
