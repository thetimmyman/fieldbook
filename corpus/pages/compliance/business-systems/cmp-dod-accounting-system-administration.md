## Purpose

Shows what the Accounting System Administration clause requires of the contractor, how an acceptable accounting system is defined, and the steps that follow a finding of material weaknesses. The criteria list and the review process are on their own pages.

| Codified citation | DFARS 252.242-7006 [@CL-CMP-1602] |

The Contractor shall establish and maintain an acceptable accounting system [@CL-CMP-1604]. An acceptable accounting system is a system that complies with the system criteria in paragraph (c) of the clause to provide reasonable assurance that applicable laws and regulations are complied with, the accounting system and cost data are reliable, risk of misallocations and mischarges is minimized, and contract allocations and charges are consistent with billing procedures [@CL-CMP-0977; @CL-CMP-0978].

Failure to maintain an acceptable accounting system, as defined in the clause, shall result in the withholding of payments if the contract includes the clause at DFARS 252.242-7005, Contractor Business Systems, and also may result in disapproval of the system [@CL-CMP-0982].

## How it works

The clause defines an accounting system as the Contractor's system or systems for accounting methods, procedures, and controls established to gather, record, classify, analyze, summarize, interpret, and present accurate and timely financial data for reporting in compliance with applicable laws, regulations, and management decisions [@CL-CMP-0979], and it may include subsystems for specific areas such as indirect and other direct costs, compensation, billing, labor, and general information technology [@CL-CMP-1603]. The criteria in paragraph (c) are listed on [Accounting system criteria](cmp-accounting-system-criteria.md), and the clause also defines material weakness on the same terms as [Contractor business systems](cmp-contractor-business-systems.md). The Contracting Officer will provide an initial determination to the Contractor, in writing, of any material weaknesses, describing the underlying deficiency in sufficient detail to allow the Contractor to understand the weakness or deficiency [@CL-CMP-0989], and the Contractor shall respond within 30 days to a written initial determination from the Contracting Officer that identifies material weaknesses in the Contractor's accounting system [@CL-CMP-0990]. The Contracting Officer will evaluate the Contractor's response and notify the Contractor, in writing, of the Contracting Officer's final determination concerning remaining material weaknesses, the adequacy of any proposed or completed corrective action, and system disapproval, if the Contracting Officer determines that one or more material weaknesses remain [@CL-CMP-0161; @CL-CMP-0991]. If the Contractor receives the Contracting Officer's final determination of material weaknesses, the Contractor shall, within 45 days of receipt of the final determination, either correct the material weaknesses or submit an acceptable corrective action plan showing milestones and actions to eliminate the material weaknesses [@CL-CMP-0160; @CL-CMP-0992]. If the Contracting Officer makes a final determination to disapprove the Contractor's accounting system, and the contract includes the clause at DFARS 252.242-7005, Contractor Business Systems, the Contracting Officer will withhold payments in accordance with that clause [@CL-CMP-0993]. The review process is on [DoD accounting system policy](cmp-dod-accounting-system-policy.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A final determination lists two material weaknesses in a labor distribution system. Instead of correcting them at once, the contractor submits, within 45 days of receiving the determination, an acceptable corrective action plan with two milestones and the actions that will eliminate the weaknesses.

## Never confuse

An acceptable system and a material weakness sit at opposite ends: an acceptable accounting system is a system that complies with the system criteria in paragraph (c) of the clause to provide reasonable assurance that applicable laws and regulations are complied with, the accounting system and cost data are reliable, risk of misallocations and mischarges is minimized, and contract allocations and charges are consistent with billing procedures [@CL-CMP-0977; @CL-CMP-0978], while a material weakness is a deficiency or combination of deficiencies in the internal control over information in contractor business systems, such that there is a reasonable possibility that a material misstatement of such information will not be prevented, or detected and corrected, on a timely basis [@CL-CMP-0980]. A reasonable possibility exists when the likelihood of an event occurring is probable, or more than remote but less than likely (section 806 of Public Law 116-283) [@CL-CMP-0981]. The Contracting Officer's final determination concerns remaining material weaknesses, the adequacy of any proposed or completed corrective action, and system disapproval, if the Contracting Officer determines that one or more material weaknesses remain [@CL-CMP-0161; @CL-CMP-0991].
