## Purpose

Shows what the Accounting System Administration clause requires of the contractor, how an acceptable accounting system is defined, and the steps that follow a finding of material weaknesses. The criteria list and the review process are on their own pages.

The contractor must establish and maintain an acceptable accounting system [@CL-CMP-1604]. An acceptable accounting system complies with the system criteria in paragraph (c) of the clause to provide reasonable assurance that applicable laws and regulations are complied with and that the other conditions in the definition are met [@CL-CMP-0977]. Those other conditions are that the accounting system and cost data are reliable, that the risk of misallocations and mischarges is minimized, and that contract allocations and charges are consistent with billing procedures [@CL-CMP-0978].

Failure to maintain an acceptable accounting system results in the withholding of payments if the contract includes the contractor business systems clause, and may also result in disapproval of the system [@CL-CMP-0982].

## How it works

The clause defines an accounting system as the contractor's system or systems for accounting methods, procedures and controls established to gather, record, classify, analyze, summarize, interpret and present accurate and timely financial data for reporting in compliance with applicable laws, regulations, and management decisions [@CL-CMP-0979], and it may include subsystems for specific areas such as indirect and other direct costs, compensation, billing, labor, and general information technology [@CL-CMP-1603]. The criteria in paragraph (c) are listed on [Accounting system criteria](cmp-accounting-system-criteria.md), and the clause uses the material weakness definition set out on [Contractor business systems](cmp-contractor-business-systems.md) [@CL-CMP-0980; @CL-CMP-0981]. The contracting officer gives the contractor a written initial determination of any material weaknesses [@CL-CMP-0989], and the contractor responds within 30 days [@CL-CMP-0990]. The final determination concerns remaining weaknesses, the adequacy of corrective action, and system disapproval if one or more weaknesses remain [@CL-CMP-0991]. After receiving a final determination of material weaknesses, the contractor has 45 days to correct them or submit an acceptable corrective action plan [@CL-CMP-0992]. Where the contract includes the contractor business systems clause, a final disapproval brings withholding of payments under that clause [@CL-CMP-0993]; the review process is on [DoD accounting system policy](cmp-dod-accounting-system-policy.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A final determination lists two material weaknesses in a labor distribution system. Instead of correcting them at once, the contractor submits, within 45 days of receiving the determination, an acceptable corrective action plan with two milestones and the actions that will eliminate the weaknesses.

## Never confuse

A system criterion says what the accounting system must provide [@CL-CMP-0977]. A material weakness is a different test: a deficiency or combination of deficiencies in internal control over information in contractor business systems, such that there is a reasonable possibility that a material misstatement will not be prevented, or detected and corrected, on a timely basis [@CL-CMP-0980]. A reasonable possibility exists when the likelihood of the event is probable, or more than remote but less than likely [@CL-CMP-0981]. Disapproval is a separate outcome that the final determination addresses if the contracting officer determines that one or more material weaknesses remain [@CL-CMP-0991].
