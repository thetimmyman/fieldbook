## Purpose

Shows the order in which a contractor must look for electronic parts, the traceability and inspection duties, and the flowdown.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-CMP-3857] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 252.246-7008 [@CL-CMP-3857] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-3857] |
| Applies to | Electronic parts the Contractor obtains under contracts with the clause [@CL-CMP-3857] |

One listed step of supplier selection is to first obtain electronic parts that are in production by the original manufacturer or an authorized aftermarket manufacturer or currently available in stock from, among listed sources, the original manufacturers of the parts or their authorized suppliers [@CL-CMP-3857]. Paragraph (b)(2) covers electronic parts that are not in production by the original manufacturer or an authorized aftermarket manufacturer and are not currently available in stock from a source listed in paragraph (b)(1), which the Contractor obtains from suppliers it identifies as contractor-approved suppliers [@CL-CMP-3858], provided that, in identifying and approving those suppliers, the Contractor uses established counterfeit prevention industry standards and processes, including inspection, testing and authentication [@CL-CMP-7079]. The paragraph states further conditions for this route; two of them are that the Contractor assumes responsibility for the authenticity of the parts those suppliers provide and that the Contractor's selection of them is subject to review, audit, and approval by the Government [@CL-CMP-3859].

## How it works

The actions in paragraph (b)(3)(ii) are to be taken, among other listed circumstances, if the Contractor obtains an electronic part from a source other than those identified in paragraph (b)(1) or (b)(2), due to nonavailability from such sources [@CL-CMP-3907]. If the Contractor obtains an electronic part or cannot confirm an electronic part pursuant to paragraph (b)(3)(i), the listed actions include promptly notifying the Contracting Officer in writing [@CL-CMP-3860]. If the Contractor is not the original manufacturer of, or authorized supplier for, an electronic part, it shall have risk-based processes, taking into consideration the consequences of failure of an electronic part, that enable tracking of electronic parts [@CL-CMP-3861]. If it cannot establish this traceability from the original manufacturer for a specific electronic part, it shall be responsible for inspection, testing, and authentication, in accordance with existing applicable industry standards [@CL-CMP-3862]. The Contractor shall include the substance of the clause, including paragraph (e), in subcontracts, including subcontracts for commercial products, that are for electronic parts or assemblies containing electronic parts, unless the subcontractor is the original manufacturer [@CL-CMP-3863].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor needs 500 capacitors. The original manufacturer still makes them, so the contractor buys from the original manufacturer or an authorized supplier first. If neither the original manufacturer nor an authorized aftermarket manufacturer produces the parts and the first-choice sources have none in stock, it may use a contractor-approved supplier and takes responsibility for the authenticity of the parts. The contractor is neither the original manufacturer nor an authorized supplier for the parts, and it cannot establish traceability from the original manufacturer for 50 of them, so it is responsible for inspecting, testing and authenticating those 50.

## Never confuse

This clause governs where electronic parts come from and what the contractor must do when traceability is missing; the contractor's counterfeit detection and avoidance system, which refers to this clause, is on [Contractor counterfeit electronic part detection and avoidance system clause](cmp-dfars-counterfeit-part-detection-system.md) [@CL-CMP-3903]. Whether the cost of a counterfeit part is allowed is a separate question on [Costs of counterfeit and suspect counterfeit electronic parts](../cas-allowability/cmp-counterfeit-electronic-part-costs.md), which this card does not restate. The first-choice sources are not the same as contractor-approved suppliers, which paragraph (b)(2) covers for parts that are not in production by the original manufacturer or an authorized aftermarket manufacturer and are not currently available in stock from a first-choice source [@CL-CMP-3857; @CL-CMP-3858].
