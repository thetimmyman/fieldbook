## Purpose

Shows the order in which a contractor must look for electronic parts, the traceability and inspection duties, and the flowdown.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-CMP-3857] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 252.246-7008 [@CL-CMP-3857] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-3857] |
| Applies to | Electronic parts the Contractor obtains under contracts with the clause [@CL-CMP-3857] |

Under paragraph (b)(1), the Contractor shall first obtain electronic parts that are in production by the original manufacturer or an authorized aftermarket manufacturer or currently available in stock from the original manufacturers of the parts, their authorized suppliers, or suppliers that obtain such parts exclusively from the original manufacturers of the parts or their authorized suppliers [@CL-CMP-3857; @CL-CMP-0181]. Paragraph (b)(2) covers electronic parts that are not in production by the original manufacturer or an authorized aftermarket manufacturer, and that are not currently available in stock from a source listed in paragraph (b)(1) of the clause, from suppliers identified by the Contractor as contractor-approved suppliers [@CL-CMP-3858], provided that, for identifying and approving such contractor-approved suppliers, the Contractor uses established counterfeit prevention industry standards and processes (including inspection, testing, and authentication) [@CL-CMP-7079], the Contractor assumes responsibility for the authenticity of parts provided by such contractor-approved suppliers, and the Contractor's selection of such contractor-approved suppliers is subject to review, audit, and approval by the Government [@CL-CMP-3859].

## How it works

Under paragraph (b)(3)(i), the Contractor takes the actions in paragraph (b)(3)(ii) of the clause if, among other listed circumstances, the Contractor obtains an electronic part from a source other than any of the sources identified in paragraph (b)(1) or (b)(2) of the clause, due to nonavailability from such sources [@CL-CMP-3907]. Under paragraph (b)(3)(ii), if the Contractor obtains an electronic part or cannot confirm an electronic part pursuant to paragraph (b)(3)(i) of the clause, the listed actions include promptly notifying the Contracting Officer in writing [@CL-CMP-3860]. If the Contractor is not the original manufacturer of, or authorized supplier for, an electronic part, the Contractor shall have risk-based processes (taking into consideration the consequences of failure of an electronic part) that enable tracking of electronic parts from the original manufacturer to product acceptance by the Government, whether the electronic part is supplied as a discrete electronic part or is contained in an assembly [@CL-CMP-3861; @CL-CMP-0182]. If the Contractor cannot establish this traceability from the original manufacturer for a specific electronic part, the Contractor shall be responsible for inspection, testing, and authentication, in accordance with existing applicable industry standards [@CL-CMP-3862]. The Contractor shall include the substance of the clause, including paragraph (e), in subcontracts, including subcontracts for commercial products, that are for electronic parts or assemblies containing electronic parts, unless the subcontractor is the original manufacturer [@CL-CMP-3863].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor needs 500 capacitors. The original manufacturer still makes them, so the contractor buys from the original manufacturer or an authorized supplier first. If neither the original manufacturer nor an authorized aftermarket manufacturer produces the parts and the first-choice sources have none in stock, it may use a contractor-approved supplier and takes responsibility for the authenticity of the parts. The contractor is neither the original manufacturer nor an authorized supplier for the parts, and it cannot establish traceability from the original manufacturer for 50 of them, so it is responsible for inspecting, testing and authenticating those 50.

## Never confuse

A counterfeit electronic part detection and avoidance system includes risk-based processes that enable tracking of electronic parts from the original manufacturer to product acceptance by the Government, whether the electronic parts are supplied as discrete electronic parts or are contained in assemblies, in accordance with paragraph (c) of the clause at DFARS 252.246-7008, Sources of Electronic Parts [@CL-CMP-0174; @CL-CMP-3903]. See [Contractor counterfeit electronic part detection and avoidance system clause](cmp-dfars-counterfeit-part-detection-system.md) and [Costs of counterfeit and suspect counterfeit electronic parts](../cas-allowability/cmp-counterfeit-electronic-part-costs.md). Paragraph (b)(1) covers electronic parts that are in production by the original manufacturer or an authorized aftermarket manufacturer or currently available in stock from the original manufacturers of the parts, their authorized suppliers, or suppliers that obtain such parts exclusively from the original manufacturers of the parts or their authorized suppliers [@CL-CMP-3857; @CL-CMP-0181], while paragraph (b)(2) covers electronic parts that are not in production by the original manufacturer or an authorized aftermarket manufacturer, and that are not currently available in stock from a source listed in paragraph (b)(1) of the clause, from suppliers identified by the Contractor as contractor-approved suppliers [@CL-CMP-3858].
