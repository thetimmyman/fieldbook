## Purpose

Explains how the Department of Defense decides whether a contractor's accounting system is acceptable, and what follows when the review finds material weaknesses.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-CMP-0701] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 242.7502 [@CL-CMP-0701] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-0701] |
| Applies to | Cost-reimbursement, incentive type, time-and-materials and labor-hour contracts, and contracts with progress payments based on costs or stage of completion [@CL-CMP-0701] |

A contractor must maintain an accounting system when it receives a cost-reimbursement, incentive type, time-and-materials or labor-hour contract, or a contract that provides for progress payments based on costs or on a percentage or stage of completion [@CL-CMP-0701].

Acceptability is judged against the system criteria by the contracting officer in consultation with the auditor or functional specialist [@CL-CMP-0702].

## How it works

The auditor documents findings and recommendations for the contracting officer, describing any material weakness in enough detail to be understood [@CL-CMP-1170].

If there are no material weaknesses, the contracting officer promptly notifies the contractor in writing that the system is acceptable and approved [@CL-CMP-1172]. If the contracting officer finds one or more material weaknesses due to the contractor's failure to meet the accounting system criteria, the contracting officer promptly makes an initial written determination [@CL-CMP-1171] and requests a written response within 30 days [@CL-CMP-0703].

A final determination follows, and the contracting officer notifies the contractor in writing either that the system is acceptable and approved or that material weaknesses remain [@CL-CMP-1173]. If material weaknesses remain, the contracting officer requests that the contractor, within 45 days of receipt of the final determination, either correct the weaknesses or submit an acceptable corrective action plan showing milestones and actions to eliminate them [@CL-CMP-0704]. The contracting officer also makes a determination to disapprove the system in accordance with the Accounting System Administration clause [@CL-CMP-1174] and withholds payments under the Contractor Business Systems clause if that clause is included in the contract [@CL-CMP-0705].

A previously disapproved system is approved promptly once the contracting officer determines that no material weaknesses remain [@CL-CMP-0706].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An audit reports 3 material weaknesses and the contracting officer issues an initial determination. The contractor answers within the 30 day window. Two weaknesses are closed, so the final determination still lists 1. The contractor files an acceptable corrective action plan within the 45 day window, and once the last weakness is corrected and the contracting officer determines that no material weaknesses remain, the contracting officer approves the system.

## Never confuse

This policy is not a cost allowability finding, and it is not a Cost Accounting Standards coverage decision.

It tests the accounting system against the system criteria and can end in approval or disapproval of the system itself, while withholding of payments is a separate step that depends on the business systems clause being in the contract [@CL-CMP-0705].

## Company calibration

Whether each contract carries the Accounting System Administration clause, and whether the system is approved, disapproved, not reviewed or not required. [@Q-034]
