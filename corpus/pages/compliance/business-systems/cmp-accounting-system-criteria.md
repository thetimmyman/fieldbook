## Purpose

Shows the criteria an acceptable accounting system must meet, and how the Government handles material weaknesses and disapproval.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-CMP-0403] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 252.242-7006(c) [@CL-CMP-0403] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-0403] |
| Applies to | Contracts that include the accounting system administration clause [@CL-CMP-0403] |

The contractor must establish and maintain an acceptable accounting system, and failure to do so results in payment withholding when the business systems clause is in the contract and may also result in disapproval [@CL-CMP-0403]. An acceptable system gives reasonable assurance that laws are followed, cost data are reliable, misallocation and mischarge risk is minimized, and contract charges match billing procedures [@CL-CMP-0401; @CL-CMP-0402].

## How it works

The criteria require a sound internal control environment, an accounting framework and an organizational structure [@CL-CMP-0404]. Direct costs are segregated from indirect costs [@CL-CMP-0983], direct costs are identified and accumulated by contract [@CL-CMP-0983], and indirect costs are accumulated and allocated by a logical and consistent method [@CL-CMP-0405]. Subsidiary cost ledgers and cost objectives reconcile to the general ledger [@CL-CMP-0406; @CL-CMP-0984], and billings reconcile to the cost accounts for current and cumulative amounts and comply with contract terms [@CL-CMP-0410; @CL-CMP-0987]. A timekeeping system identifies labor by cost objective, costs charged to a contract are determined at least monthly through routine posting of the books of account [@CL-CMP-0408; @CL-CMP-0985], and amounts that are not allowable under FAR part 31 and other contract provisions are excluded from costs charged to Government contracts [@CL-CMP-0407; @CL-CMP-0409; @CL-CMP-0986]. Practices follow Cost Accounting Standards Board standards when applicable and otherwise generally accepted accounting principles [@CL-CMP-0411; @CL-CMP-0988], and after an initial determination of material weaknesses the contractor responds within 30 days, and within 45 days of receiving a final determination it corrects them or submits an acceptable corrective action plan [@CL-CMP-0412; @CL-CMP-0413].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor's billings for the quarter total 500,000 while the cost accounts show 460,000, and the unreconciled 40,000 traces to a labor distribution entry posted against the wrong cost objective. The contractor corrects the entry, reconciles the billings to the cost accounts, and documents the adjusting entry.

## Never confuse

Accounting system criteria are not rules on whether a particular cost is allowable; the system must exclude from contract charges amounts that are not allowable under the cost principles [@CL-CMP-0409]. A disapproval is a separate step from a finding of material weaknesses: withholding follows a final disapproval only when the business systems clause is present [@CL-CMP-0414].

The current government status of each contractor business system: approved, disapproved, not reviewed or not required. [@Q-034]
