## Purpose

Shows who decides on payment withholding after a business system is disapproved, how the withheld amount is capped, and how it is reduced or released.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-CMP-2217] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 242.7000 [@CL-CMP-2217] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-2217] |
| Applies to | Covered contracts that are subject to the Cost Accounting Standards [@CL-CMP-2216] |

The business systems that the business systems clause covers are listed on [Contractor business systems](cmp-contractor-business-systems.md). A covered contract is a contract subject to the Cost Accounting Standards under the statute, as implemented in 48 CFR 9903.201-1 [@CL-CMP-2216].

The contracting officer's withholding duties apply when the contracting officer makes a final determination to disapprove a contractor's business system under the business systems clause [@CL-CMP-2217]. The officer has sole discretion to identify the covered contracts from which to withhold payments [@CL-CMP-2220]. In identifying them, the officer shall ensure that the total payment withholding under the clause does not exceed 10 percent of progress payments, performance-based payments and interim payments under cost-reimbursement, labor-hour and time-and-materials contracts billed under each identified covered contract [@CL-CMP-2218]. For each business system, the withholding does not exceed 5 percent of the same kinds of payments billed under each identified covered contract [@CL-CMP-2219].

After a withhold, the contracting officer, in consultation with the auditor or functional specialist, monitors the contractor's progress and notifies the contractor of any decision to decrease or increase the withholding [@CL-CMP-2249]. When the contractor notifies the officer that it has corrected the material weaknesses, the officer shall request the auditor or functional specialist to review the correction and verify it [@CL-CMP-2214]. The officer's determination that the contractor has corrected all material weaknesses is made after receipt of that verification [@CL-CMP-2250]. Where the contractor has corrected all material weaknesses as directed by the final determination, the officer shall discontinue the withholding, release any payments previously withheld and approve the system, unless other material weaknesses remain [@CL-CMP-2221].

Within 90 days of receiving the contractor's notice that it has corrected the weaknesses, the officer shall take one of two listed actions [@CL-CMP-2222]. The first is to make a determination, which this card does not set out. The other is to reduce the withholding directly related to the weaknesses covered under the corrective action plan by at least 50 percent of the amount being withheld from progress payments and performance-based payments [@CL-CMP-2223]. That option also has the officer direct the contractor in writing to cut the percentage withheld on interim cost vouchers by at least 50 percent, until the officer makes a determination under the first listed option [@CL-CMP-2251]. If the officer determines at any time that the contractor has failed to correct the weaknesses identified in its notification, the officer will continue, reinstate or increase withholding from progress payments and performance-based payments; that paragraph also covers the percentage withheld on interim cost vouchers, which this card does not set out [@CL-CMP-2224].

The printed section on business system deficiencies does not set out the steps of the initial and final determinations that lead to disapproval, and it prints no response deadline for the contractor. The response and plan clocks are on [Contractor business systems](cmp-contractor-business-systems.md), and the clause withhold rates and the deviation text are on [Business-system payment withholds](cmp-business-system-withholds.md). The review procedure for the material management and accounting system is on [Material management and accounting system review procedures](cmp-mmas-review-procedures.md).

## How it works

A final determination to disapprove a system triggers the withholding duties [@CL-CMP-2217]. The contracting officer then picks the covered contracts and keeps the withholding within the two caps [@CL-CMP-2220; @CL-CMP-2218; @CL-CMP-2219]. The officer makes the decisions the section lists, in consultation with the auditor or functional specialist, and asks that specialist to verify a reported correction [@CL-CMP-2249; @CL-CMP-2214]. The contractor's notice of correction starts the officer's decision period [@CL-CMP-2222].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor has two covered contracts with progress payments. The contracting officer disapproves the estimating system and picks one contract, on which 1,000,000 has been billed. The cap for that one system is 50,000. Later the contractor reports the weaknesses corrected. Within 90 days the officer either makes a determination or cuts the related withholding by at least half until a determination is made. If the auditor verifies the fix and no other weakness remains, the officer releases the withheld 50,000 and approves the system.

## Never confuse

A final determination to disapprove a system is not a decision to reduce or end a withhold. The first triggers the withholding duties [@CL-CMP-2217], and the later decisions to decrease or increase the withholding follow the officer's monitoring [@CL-CMP-2249]. The per-system cap and the total cap are different limits [@CL-CMP-2218; @CL-CMP-2219]. For how the audit side reports findings, see [DCAA business system audits](cmp-dcaa-business-system-audits.md).
