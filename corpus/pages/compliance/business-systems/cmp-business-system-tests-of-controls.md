## Purpose

Describes how the auditor tests key business system controls: the types of test, and how the timing and extent of testing are chosen.

| Applies to | Testing of key internal controls related to DFARS compliance in contractor business system audits [@CL-CMP-1661] |

The auditors do not express an opinion on internal controls, but they do test the key internal controls that are related to DFARS compliance [@CL-CMP-1661]. To obtain evidence of the effectiveness of internal control activities, the auditor should perform physical observations, inquiries of appropriate personnel, or inspection of relevant documents [@CL-CMP-1662].

## How it works

No one specific test is always necessary, applicable, or equally effective in every circumstance, and a combination of these types of tests is often required to provide the necessary level of assurance that controls are working effectively [@CL-CMP-1663]. If the control provides documentary evidence, the auditor may decide to inspect the documentation, and for other controls such documentation may not be available or relevant [@CL-CMP-1964]. Segregation of duties controls generally do not provide documentary evidence, and in such circumstances the auditor may obtain evidential matter about the effectiveness of operation through observation or inquiry [@CL-CMP-1965]. If the auditor determines that relevant internal control activities do not exist, no control testing will be performed [@CL-CMP-1957].

Timing of an audit procedure refers to when it is performed or the period or date to which the audit evidence applies [@CL-CMP-1664]. When the evidence relates only to a specific point in time, such as evidence obtained from physical observation, the auditor should obtain additional evidence that the control was effective during the entire audit period [@CL-CMP-1665]. The auditor should determine the extent of testing to be performed, and this determination is a matter of auditor judgment taking into consideration the information gathered in developing an understanding of the internal control structure, the nature of the control to be tested, audit leads and/or prior audit findings, identified risk, the nature and availability of evidential matter, and the contractor's monitoring and testing efforts [@CL-CMP-1966; @CL-CMP-0171]. The extent of testing is also significantly impacted by the office's total audit experience with the contractor [@CL-CMP-1666].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

During an estimating system audit, DCAA tests whether a supervisor approves bill of materials pricing before a proposal goes out. The auditor inspects the approval log for a sample of 25 proposals. A log shows only the moment of each sign-off, so the auditor also interviews five estimators and watches the routing in operation, then uses the procedures manual to conclude that the control worked for the whole audit period.

## Never confuse

The auditors do not express an opinion on internal controls, but they do test the key internal controls that are related to DFARS compliance [@CL-CMP-1661]. How control risk is assessed is on [Assessing control risk](cmp-business-system-control-risk.md).

## Company calibration

Keep documentary records, procedure manuals and named personnel available so that observation, inquiry and inspection can cover the whole audit period. [@Q-014]
