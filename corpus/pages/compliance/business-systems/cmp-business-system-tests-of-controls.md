## Purpose

Describes how the auditor tests key business system controls: the types of test, and how the timing and extent of testing are chosen.

| Applies to | Testing of key internal controls related to DFARS compliance in contractor business system audits [@CL-CMP-1661] |

The auditors do not express an opinion on internal controls, but they do test the key internal controls that are related to DFARS compliance [@CL-CMP-1661]. To obtain evidence of the effectiveness of internal control activities, the auditor should perform physical observations, inquiries of appropriate personnel, or inspection of relevant documents [@CL-CMP-1662].

## How it works

No one specific test is always necessary, applicable, or equally effective in every circumstance, and a combination of these types of tests is often required to provide the necessary level of assurance [@CL-CMP-1663]. If a control provides documentary evidence, the auditor may decide to inspect the documentation, but for other controls such documentation may not be available or relevant [@CL-CMP-1964]. Segregation of duties controls generally do not provide documentary evidence, so the auditor may obtain evidential matter about their operation through observation or inquiry [@CL-CMP-1965]. If the auditor determines that relevant internal control activities do not exist, no control testing will be performed [@CL-CMP-1957].

Timing of an audit procedure refers to when it is performed or the period or date to which the audit evidence applies [@CL-CMP-1664]. When the evidence relates only to a specific point in time, such as evidence from physical observation, the auditor should obtain additional evidence that the control was effective during the entire audit period [@CL-CMP-1665]. The auditor determines the extent of testing as a matter of auditor judgment, taking into consideration the information gathered in developing an understanding of the internal control structure and the nature of the control to be tested, among other factors [@CL-CMP-1966]. The extent of testing is also significantly impacted by the office's total audit experience with the contractor [@CL-CMP-1666].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

During an estimating system audit, DCAA tests whether a supervisor approves bill of materials pricing before a proposal goes out. The auditor inspects the approval log for a sample of 25 proposals. A log shows only the moment of each sign-off, so the auditor also interviews five estimators and watches the routing in operation, then uses the procedures manual to conclude that the control worked for the whole audit period.

## Never confuse

Testing key controls is not an opinion on internal controls; the auditors do not express one [@CL-CMP-1661]. What the testing supports is the auditor's conclusion on compliance and the control risk assessment, described on [Assessing control risk](cmp-business-system-control-risk.md).

## Company calibration

Keep documentary records, procedure manuals and named personnel available so that observation, inquiry and inspection can cover the whole audit period. [@Q-014]
