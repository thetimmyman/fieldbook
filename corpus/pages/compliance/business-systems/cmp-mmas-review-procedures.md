## Purpose

Shows when the Government reviews a contractor's material management and accounting system (MMAS), who decides, and how findings move to a final determination. The clause criteria are on the linked clause page.

The policies, procedures, and standards in the MMAS subpart apply only when the contractor has contracts exceeding the simplified acquisition threshold that are not for the acquisition of commercial products or commercial services and are either cost-reimbursement contracts or fixed-price contracts with progress payments made on the basis of costs incurred by the contractor as work progresses under the contract [@CL-CMP-2275; @CL-CMP-2276]. They do not apply to small businesses, educational institutions, or nonprofit organizations [@CL-CMP-2277]. Within that scope, an MMAS review is conducted when a contractor has $40 million of qualifying sales to the Government during the contractor's preceding fiscal year and the administrative contracting officer (ACO), with advice from the auditor, determines an MMAS review is needed based on a risk assessment of the contractor's past experience and current vulnerability [@CL-CMP-2225; @CL-CMP-0187]. Qualifying sales are sales for which certified cost or pricing data were required under section 3702 of title 10 of the United States Code, as implemented in FAR 15.403, or that are contracts priced on other than a firm-fixed-price or fixed-price with economic price adjustment basis [@CL-CMP-2226]. Sales include prime contracts, subcontracts, and modifications to such contracts and subcontracts [@CL-CMP-2253].

If the contracting officer finds that there are one or more material weaknesses due to the contractor's failure to meet one or more of the MMAS system criteria in the clause at DFARS 252.242-7004, the contracting officer shall promptly make an initial written determination on any material weaknesses and notify the contractor, in writing, providing a description of each material weakness in sufficient detail to allow the contractor to understand the weakness or deficiency [@CL-CMP-2227; @CL-CMP-0188], and request the contractor to respond, in writing, to the initial determination within 30 days [@CL-CMP-2228]. The ACO shall make a final determination and notify the contractor that the contractor's MMAS is acceptable and approved, and no deficiencies remain, or that material weaknesses remain [@CL-CMP-2229]. If material weaknesses remain, the notice shall identify any remaining material weaknesses and indicate the adequacy of any proposed or completed corrective action, and the contracting officer shall take the steps that follow [@CL-CMP-2252]. One listed step is to request that the contractor, within 45 days of receipt of the final determination, either correct the weaknesses or submit an acceptable corrective action plan showing milestones and actions to eliminate the weaknesses [@CL-CMP-2230]. Two further listed steps are to disapprove the system in accordance with the clause at DFARS 252.242-7004 and to withhold payments in accordance with the clause at DFARS 252.242-7005, Contractor Business Systems, if the clause is included in the contract [@CL-CMP-2231]. The contracting officer shall promptly approve a previously disapproved MMAS and notify the contractor when the contracting officer determines that there are no remaining material weaknesses [@CL-CMP-2232].

## How it works

The ACO, with advice from the auditor, determines whether an MMAS review is needed based on a risk assessment of the contractor's past experience and current vulnerability [@CL-CMP-2225; @CL-CMP-0187]. The contracting officer makes the initial written determination on any material weaknesses and notifies the contractor in writing, and the ACO makes the final determination and notifies the contractor [@CL-CMP-2227; @CL-CMP-0188; @CL-CMP-2229]. A final determination that material weaknesses remain leads to the listed steps, which include disapproving the system in accordance with DFARS 252.242-7004 and withholding payments in accordance with DFARS 252.242-7005 if that clause is included in the contract [@CL-CMP-2252; @CL-CMP-2231]. See the linked pages, and see [DoD business system deficiencies](cmp-business-system-deficiency-determinations.md) for the withholding duties and caps.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor had 55,000,000 of qualifying sales last year, so it is above the review threshold. The ACO, advised by the auditor, finds a review is needed. The contracting officer issues an initial determination with one material weakness, and the contractor answers in writing within 30 days. The ACO's final determination says the weakness remains, so the contractor has 45 days to fix it or file a corrective action plan, and the system is disapproved. Another contractor with 35,000,000 of qualifying sales is below the threshold for this rule.

## Never confuse

The $40 million review trigger is based on qualifying sales to the Government during the contractor's preceding fiscal year, defined as sales for which certified cost or pricing data were required under section 3702 of title 10 of the United States Code, as implemented in FAR 15.403, or that are contracts priced on other than a firm-fixed-price or fixed-price with economic price adjustment basis [@CL-CMP-2225; @CL-CMP-2226]. The initial and final determinations differ: under paragraph (c)(2)(ii)(A), the contracting officer promptly makes an initial written determination on any material weaknesses and notifies the contractor in writing, while under paragraph (c)(3)(i), the ACO makes a final determination and notifies the contractor that the contractor's MMAS is acceptable and approved and no deficiencies remain, or that material weaknesses remain [@CL-CMP-2227; @CL-CMP-0188; @CL-CMP-2229]. For what the system must do, see [Material Management and Accounting System clause](../../close-revrec/inventory-wip/cls-material-management-accounting-system.md). For the withhold caps, see [Business-system payment withholds](cmp-business-system-withholds.md).
