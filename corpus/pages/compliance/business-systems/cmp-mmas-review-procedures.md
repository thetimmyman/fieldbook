## Purpose

Shows when the Government reviews a contractor's material management and accounting system (MMAS), who decides, and how findings move to a final determination. The clause criteria are on the linked clause page.

The MMAS subpart applies only when the contractor has contracts exceeding the simplified acquisition threshold that are not for commercial products or commercial services and are either cost-reimbursement contracts or fixed-price contracts with progress payments made on the basis of costs incurred [@CL-CMP-2275; @CL-CMP-2276]. It does not apply to small businesses, educational institutions or nonprofit organizations [@CL-CMP-2277]. Within that scope, a review is conducted when a contractor has $40 million of qualifying sales to the Government in its preceding fiscal year and the administrative contracting officer (ACO), with advice from the auditor, determines a review is needed based on a risk assessment [@CL-CMP-2225]. Qualifying sales are sales for which certified cost or pricing data were required under section 3702 of title 10 of the United States Code, as implemented in FAR 15.403, or that are contracts priced on other than a firm-fixed-price or fixed-price with economic price adjustment basis [@CL-CMP-2226]. Under the same paragraph, sales include prime contracts, subcontracts, and modifications to such contracts and subcontracts [@CL-CMP-2253].

If the contracting officer finds one or more material weaknesses due to the contractor's failure to meet one or more of the system criteria in the clause, the officer shall promptly make an initial written determination [@CL-CMP-2227]. In that case, one listed step is to request the contractor to respond in writing within 30 days [@CL-CMP-2228]. The ACO shall make a final determination and notify the contractor either that the system is acceptable and approved with no deficiencies remaining or that material weaknesses remain [@CL-CMP-2229]. If material weaknesses remain, the notice shall identify them and indicate the adequacy of any proposed or completed corrective action, and the contracting officer shall take the steps that follow [@CL-CMP-2252]. One listed step is to request that the contractor, within 45 days of receipt of the final determination, either correct the weaknesses or submit an acceptable corrective action plan showing milestones and actions [@CL-CMP-2230]. Two further listed steps are to disapprove the system and to withhold payments under the business systems clause if that clause is included in the contract [@CL-CMP-2231]. The contracting officer shall promptly approve a previously disapproved system and notify the contractor once no material weaknesses remain [@CL-CMP-2232].

## How it works

The ACO decides whether a review is needed, using the auditor's advice and a risk assessment [@CL-CMP-2225]. The contracting officer makes the initial written determination, and the ACO makes and gives notice of the final determination [@CL-CMP-2227; @CL-CMP-2229]. A final determination that weaknesses remain leads to the listed steps, which include disapproval and, where the clause is in the contract, withholding [@CL-CMP-2252; @CL-CMP-2231]. The clause criteria and the withhold rates are not restated here. See the linked pages, and see [DoD business system deficiencies](cmp-business-system-deficiency-determinations.md) for the withholding duties and caps.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor had 55,000,000 of qualifying sales last year, so it is above the review threshold. The ACO, advised by the auditor, finds a review is needed. The contracting officer issues an initial determination with one material weakness, and the contractor answers in writing within 30 days. The ACO's final determination says the weakness remains, so the contractor has 45 days to fix it or file a corrective action plan, and the system is disapproved. Another contractor with 35,000,000 of qualifying sales is below the threshold for this rule.

## Never confuse

The review threshold counts qualifying sales, not all sales to the Government [@CL-CMP-2226]. The initial determination is the contracting officer's first written determination on the weaknesses. It is not the final determination, which is the ACO's notice that the system is acceptable or that material weaknesses remain [@CL-CMP-2227; @CL-CMP-2229]. For what the system must do, see [Material Management and Accounting System clause](../../close-revrec/inventory-wip/cls-material-management-accounting-system.md). For the withhold caps, see [Business-system payment withholds](cmp-business-system-withholds.md).

## Company calibration

Whether the company's sales put it above the review threshold, and the Government status of its material management and accounting system. [@Q-034]
