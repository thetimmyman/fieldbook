## Purpose

Describes the follow-up audit that checks whether a contractor corrected previously reported business system deficiencies, including when it starts and what it covers.

| Applies to | Follow-up audits of contractor business systems that were cited for deficiencies [@CL-CMP-1684] |

Material weaknesses and system deficiencies are generally considered outstanding until the contractor has corrected the deficiency and DCAA has performed a follow-up business system audit and found the revised business system practices to be compliant with the DFARS business system requirements [@CL-CMP-1684; @CL-CMP-0186]. The contracting officer makes the final determination that the contractor has corrected the deficiencies [@CL-CMP-1958].

When the contractor notifies the contracting officer that the contractor has corrected the deficiencies, the contracting officer shall request the auditor to review the corrections to determine if the deficiencies have been resolved [@CL-CMP-1683].

## How it works

Field audit offices should initiate a follow-up business system audit when the field audit office is notified by the contractor (either directly or through the contracting officer) that it has implemented the appropriate corrective actions and sufficient transactions or costs are readily available to adequately examine the revised business system practices [@CL-CMP-1685; @CL-CMP-8125]. When the contractor has not changed its business system practices, a follow-up audit should not be performed [@CL-CMP-1687]. The scope of the follow-up business system audit will be limited to determining if the contractor corrected the previously reported material weaknesses and/or system deficiencies and the system complies with DFARS [@CL-CMP-1686]. Less than material noncompliances warranting the attention of those charged with governance (the contractor and contracting officer) that were reported in a previous business system audit do not need to be included in the scope of audit [@CL-CMP-1959].

The field audit office should notify the administrative contracting officer on a real-time basis of any revised business system practices that are determined to be noncompliant with the DFARS business system requirements [@CL-CMP-1688]. If a follow-up business system audit discloses a material weakness or system deficiency that was not previously reported, it should be reported in the follow-up business system audit report [@CL-CMP-1961]. If the corrective actions did not successfully correct the reported deficiencies, this should be discussed with the contracting officer, and the audit report will state that those deficiencies continue to exist and are noncompliant with the DFARS business system requirements [@CL-CMP-1960].

The Defense Contract Management Agency is cognizant over three of the six business systems as defined by DFARS 252.242-7998: Earned Value Management System, Contractor Purchasing System Administration, and Contractor Property Management System Administration [@CL-CMP-1689]. The six systems are listed on [Contractor business systems](cmp-contractor-business-systems.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

After an audit cites a material weakness in labor charging, the contractor automates timesheet approvals and runs them for three months. It tells the contracting officer that the fix is complete. DCAA confirms that enough labor transactions exist, limits its testing to the revised approval controls, and reports that they comply. The contracting officer then makes the final determination on whether the weakness is corrected.

## Never confuse

The scope of the follow-up business system audit will be limited to determining if the contractor corrected the previously reported material weaknesses and/or system deficiencies and the system complies with DFARS [@CL-CMP-1686]. For the baseline that sets the scope of subsequent audits after a full business system audit, see [Scope and baseline](cmp-business-system-audit-scope-baseline.md).

## Company calibration

Check that enough transactions have accumulated under the corrected practices before notifying the contracting officer that the fix is complete. [@Q-014]
