## Purpose

Describes the follow-up audit that checks whether a contractor corrected previously reported business system deficiencies, including when it starts and what it covers.

## The rule

| Text status | Not established. |
| Applies to | Follow-up audits of contractor business systems that were cited for deficiencies [@CL-CMP-1684] |

Material weaknesses and system deficiencies are generally considered outstanding until the contractor has corrected the deficiency and DCAA has performed a follow-up business system audit and found the revised business system practices to be compliant [@CL-CMP-1684]. The contracting officer makes the final determination that the contractor has corrected the deficiencies [@CL-CMP-1958].

When the contractor notifies the contracting officer that it has corrected the deficiencies, the contracting officer must request the auditor to review the corrections to determine whether the deficiencies have been resolved [@CL-CMP-1683].

## How it works

The audit office should initiate a follow-up audit when the contractor notifies it, directly or through the contracting officer, that it has implemented the appropriate corrective actions and sufficient transactions or costs are readily available [@CL-CMP-1685]. When the contractor has not changed its business system practices, a follow-up audit should not be performed [@CL-CMP-1687]. The scope is limited to determining whether the contractor corrected the previously reported material weaknesses and system deficiencies and the system complies with DFARS [@CL-CMP-1686]. Less than material noncompliances reported in a previous audit do not need to be included in that scope [@CL-CMP-1959].

The audit office should notify the administrative contracting officer on a real-time basis of any revised practices determined to be noncompliant [@CL-CMP-1688]. A material weakness or system deficiency that was not previously reported is reported in the follow-up report [@CL-CMP-1961]. If the corrective actions did not correct the deficiencies, the report will state that they continue to exist and are noncompliant [@CL-CMP-1960].

The Defense Contract Management Agency is cognizant over three of the six defined business systems: the Earned Value Management System, Contractor Purchasing System Administration, and Contractor Property Management System Administration [@CL-CMP-1689]. The six systems are listed on [Contractor business systems](cmp-contractor-business-systems.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

After an audit cites a material weakness in labor charging, the contractor automates timesheet approvals and runs them for three months. It tells the contracting officer that the fix is complete. DCAA confirms that enough labor transactions exist, limits its testing to the revised approval controls, and reports that they comply. The contracting officer then makes the final determination on whether the weakness is corrected.

## Never confuse

A follow-up audit is not a new full audit. It examines the previously reported deficiencies and whether the system complies with DFARS [@CL-CMP-1686]. For the baseline that sets the scope of a full audit, see [Scope and baseline](cmp-business-system-audit-scope-baseline.md).

## Company calibration

Check that enough transactions have accumulated under the corrected practices before notifying the contracting officer that the fix is complete. [@Q-014]
