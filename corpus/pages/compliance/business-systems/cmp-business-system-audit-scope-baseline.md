## Purpose

Explains how a full business system audit becomes the baseline for the scope of later audits of the same system, and what the auditor re-examines when the system changes.

| Applies to | Audits of a contractor business system after a full audit of that system has been performed [@CL-CMP-1657] |

Once a full business system audit has been performed, it should serve as a baseline for establishing the scope of subsequent audits of that system [@CL-CMP-1657]. Subsequent audits should cover major system changes, including procedures to validate that the changes have controls in place to comply with the applicable DFARS business system criteria [@CL-CMP-1658].

## How it works

The components of internal control and the relevant control objectives identified within the business systems apply to every contractor [@CL-CMP-1967]. Smaller contractors may have less formal internal controls that accomplish these control objectives [@CL-CMP-1656]. The auditor identifies the control objectives associated with the contractor's compliance with the DFARS business systems requirements, applicable laws and regulations, and contract terms [@CL-CMP-1660].

If a full audit was performed before, audit teams should leverage the information gathered from the contractor walk-through or demonstrations to the maximum extent possible [@CL-CMP-1968]. For key internal controls that have changed, the auditor should request a contractor walk-through and demonstration to gain an understanding, and should use information from the prior audit where possible to prevent duplication [@CL-CMP-1659].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor completed a full accounting system audit two years ago. This year it replaces its timekeeping application and leaves the other accounting subsystems unchanged. DCAA does not repeat the full review. It confirms which key controls changed, asks for a walk-through of the new timekeeping approval steps, validates that the new controls support the accounting system criteria, and reuses what it learned from the earlier walk-throughs for the unchanged controls.

## Never confuse

The baseline sets the scope of later audits. It does not replace coverage of major system changes, which later audits should include [@CL-CMP-1658]. For the audit that checks whether reported deficiencies were fixed, see [Follow-up business system audits](cmp-follow-up-business-system-audits.md).

## Company calibration

Identify major changes to business system policies or automated controls since the last full audit, so that they can be shown to the auditor. [@Q-014]
