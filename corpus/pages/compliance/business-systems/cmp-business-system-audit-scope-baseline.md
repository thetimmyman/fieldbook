## Purpose

Explains how a full business system audit becomes the baseline for the scope of later audits of the same system, and what the auditor re-examines when the system changes.

| Applies to | Audits of a contractor business system after a full audit of that system has been performed [@CL-CMP-1657] |

Once a full business system audit has been performed, it should serve as a baseline for establishing the scope of subsequent audits of that system [@CL-CMP-1657]. Subsequent audits should cover major system changes, including audit procedures to validate that the business system changes have controls in place to comply with the applicable DFARS business system (accounting, MMAS, and estimating systems) criteria, FAR requirements, applicable laws and regulations, and contract terms [@CL-CMP-1658; @CL-CMP-8124].

## How it works

The components of internal control and the relevant control objectives identified within the business systems apply to every contractor and should be considered in the context of the contractor's size, organization and ownership characteristics, nature of business, diversity and complexity of operations, methods of transmitting, processing, maintaining, and accessing information, and applicable legal and regulatory requirements [@CL-CMP-1967; @CL-CMP-8126; @CL-CMP-8127]. Smaller contractors may have less formal internal controls that accomplish these control objectives [@CL-CMP-1656]. The auditor should identify those control objectives associated with the contractor's compliance with the DFARS business systems requirements, applicable laws and regulations, and contract terms [@CL-CMP-1660].

If a full business system audit was previously performed, audit teams should leverage the information previously gathered from the contractor walk-through or demonstrations to the maximum extent possible and confirm whether there have been significant changes to any key internal controls supporting DFARS compliance [@CL-CMP-1968; @CL-CMP-0163]. For key internal controls that have changed, the auditor should request a contractor walk-through and demonstration to gain an understanding, and should leverage information from the prior audit where possible to prevent duplication and increase efficiency [@CL-CMP-1659].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor completed a full accounting system audit two years ago. This year it replaces its timekeeping application and leaves the other accounting subsystems unchanged. DCAA does not repeat the full review. It confirms which key controls changed, asks for a walk-through of the new timekeeping approval steps, validates that the new controls support the accounting system criteria, and reuses what it learned from the earlier walk-throughs for the unchanged controls.

## Never confuse

A subsequent audit differs from the full baseline audit: once a full business system audit has been performed, it should serve as a baseline for establishing the scope of subsequent audits of that system [@CL-CMP-1657], and subsequent audits should also cover major system changes [@CL-CMP-1658]. For the audit that checks whether reported deficiencies were fixed, see [Follow-up business system audits](cmp-follow-up-business-system-audits.md).

## Company calibration

Identify major changes to business system policies or automated controls since the last full audit, so that they can be shown to the auditor. [@Q-014]
