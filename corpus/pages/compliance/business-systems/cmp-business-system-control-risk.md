## Purpose

Explains how the auditor assesses control risk for business system control objectives and when the auditor can rely on tested controls to reduce testing in other audits.

Control risk is the likelihood that a material misstatement or noncompliance in an account or area subject to audit will not be prevented or detected and corrected on a timely basis by the contractor's internal controls [@CL-CMP-1667]. Only when the internal controls have been tested and determined to be effective can the auditor rely on them and reduce the amount of substantive testing in the applicable audit area [@CL-CMP-1669].

## How it works

In assessing control risk, the auditor considers the effectiveness of established control activities to accomplish stated control objectives, and the more effective the control activities are, the lower the control risk [@CL-CMP-1668]. An assessment of low risk for specific control objectives means the auditor can rely on the contractor's internal controls and can reduce testing in other related audits to analytical procedures and/or a combination of tests of details [@CL-CMP-1671]. If internal controls are found not to be operating effectively, the auditor cannot rely on them when performing other than business system audits, and will need to design substantive audit procedures to account for the lack of effective internal controls [@CL-CMP-1670].

Auditors can reduce substantive testing in a specific audit by relying on control tests from a related business system audit, but only if the controls from the prior audit were proven effective and are still current and relevant [@CL-CMP-1956]. How the controls are tested is on [Tests of controls](cmp-business-system-tests-of-controls.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

DCAA tests the receiving and matching controls in a contractor's material management system and finds them effective. The auditor assesses control risk as low for material costs. On a later incurred cost audit with 15,000,000 of direct material charges, the auditor relies on those tests, cites the original assignment and its working papers, and reduces the invoice sample from 200 to 80 items.

## Never confuse

Low control risk reduces testing in other related audits to analytical procedures and/or a combination of tests of details, rather than removing it [@CL-CMP-1671]. Control risk is also not a finding of noncompliance; the findings and their classes are on [Audit reporting and deficiency classes](cmp-business-system-audit-reporting.md).

## Company calibration

Gather and keep evidence that shows each control working, so that the auditor can test it and conclude that control risk is low. [@Q-034]
