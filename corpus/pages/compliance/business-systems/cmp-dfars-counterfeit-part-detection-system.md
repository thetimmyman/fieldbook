## Purpose

Shows who must keep a counterfeit electronic part detection and avoidance system, what the system must cover, and what an unacceptable system can lead to.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-CMP-3851] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 252.246-7007 [@CL-CMP-3851] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-3851] |
| Applies to | Contractors subject to the Cost Accounting Standards [@CL-CMP-3850] |

Paragraphs (a) through (e) of the clause do not apply unless the Contractor is subject to the Cost Accounting Standards under chapter 15 of title 41 of the United States Code, as implemented in regulations found at 48 CFR 9903.201-1 [@CL-CMP-3850]. Under paragraph (b), the Contractor shall establish and maintain an acceptable counterfeit electronic part detection and avoidance system [@CL-CMP-3850; @CL-CMP-3851].

Under the same condition, failure to maintain an acceptable counterfeit electronic part detection and avoidance system, as defined in the clause, may result in disapproval of the purchasing system by the Contracting Officer and/or withholding of payments [@CL-CMP-3850; @CL-CMP-3852], and may affect the allowability of costs of counterfeit electronic parts or suspect counterfeit electronic parts and the cost of rework or corrective action that may be required to remedy the use or inclusion of such parts (see DFARS 231.205-71) [@CL-CMP-3901].

## How it works

Under the same condition, a counterfeit electronic part detection and avoidance system shall include risk-based policies and procedures that address, at a minimum, the listed areas, one of which is the training of personnel [@CL-CMP-3850; @CL-CMP-3853]. Another listed area is the inspection and testing of electronic parts, including criteria for acceptance and rejection, with tests and inspections performed in accordance with accepted Government- and industry-recognized techniques [@CL-CMP-3854]. Another listed area is risk-based processes that enable tracking of electronic parts from the original manufacturer to product acceptance by the Government, whether the electronic parts are supplied as discrete electronic parts or are contained in assemblies, in accordance with paragraph (c) of the clause at DFARS 252.246-7008, Sources of Electronic Parts [@CL-CMP-0174; @CL-CMP-3903]. The duties in that clause are on [Sources of electronic parts clause](cmp-dfars-sources-of-electronic-parts.md). A further listed area is flowdown of counterfeit detection and avoidance requirements, including applicable system criteria provided in the clause, to subcontractors at all levels in the supply chain that are responsible for buying or selling electronic parts or assemblies containing electronic parts, or for performing authentication testing [@CL-CMP-3855; @CL-CMP-7078].

The Contractor shall include the substance of the clause, excluding the introductory text and including only paragraphs (a) through (e), in subcontracts, including subcontracts for commercial products, for electronic parts or assemblies containing electronic parts [@CL-CMP-3856].

Government review and evaluation of the Contractor's policies and procedures will be accomplished as part of the evaluation of the Contractor's purchasing system in accordance with DFARS 252.244-7001, Contractor Purchasing System Administration—Basic, or Contractor Purchasing System Administration—Alternate I [@CL-CMP-0175; @CL-CMP-3902]. The definition of contractor business systems lists the purchasing system, if the contract includes the clause at DFARS 252.244-7001 [@CL-CMP-1202]. See [Contractor business systems](cmp-contractor-business-systems.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor subject to the Cost Accounting Standards buys parts through 30 subcontractors. Its written procedures reach only 10 of them. The flowdown area is not met for the other 20, so the Contracting Officer may find the system unacceptable and may disapprove the purchasing system or withhold payments.

## Never confuse

Failure to maintain an acceptable counterfeit electronic part detection and avoidance system, as defined in the clause, may result in disapproval of the purchasing system by the Contracting Officer and/or withholding of payments [@CL-CMP-3852], and may affect the allowability of costs of counterfeit electronic parts or suspect counterfeit electronic parts and the cost of rework or corrective action that may be required to remedy the use or inclusion of such parts (see DFARS 231.205-71) [@CL-CMP-3901]. What follows a disapproval is on [DoD business system deficiencies: payment withholding and release](cmp-business-system-deficiency-determinations.md), and the cost rules are on [Costs of counterfeit and suspect counterfeit electronic parts](../cas-allowability/cmp-counterfeit-electronic-part-costs.md). The purchasing system itself is covered on [Contractor Purchasing System Administration clause](../../supply-chain/procure-to-pay/sup-contractor-purchasing-system.md), and payment withholds on [Business system withholds](cmp-business-system-withholds.md).
