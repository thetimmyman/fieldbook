## Purpose

Shows how long a contractor must keep contract records available for negotiation, administration and audit, which contracts the retention rules cover, the specific periods for financial, pay and acquisition records, and which cases make the period longer.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-2417] |
| Codified citation | FAR 4.702, FAR 4.703 and FAR 4.705 [@CL-CMP-2500; @CL-CMP-2418; @CL-CMP-2423] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-2417] |
| Applies to | Records generated under contracts that contain the Audit and Records, Sealed Bidding clause or the Audit and Records, Negotiation clause, except Department of Energy contracts for which the Comptroller General allows alternative periods [@CL-CMP-2500; @CL-CMP-2501] |

The retention subpart applies to records generated under contracts that contain the Audit and Records, Sealed Bidding clause or the Audit and Records, Negotiation clause [@CL-CMP-2500]. It is not mandatory on Department of Energy contracts for which the Comptroller General allows alternative records retention periods [@CL-CMP-2501].

Except as stated in the paragraph on longer periods, contractors make available records, including books, documents, accounting procedures and practices, and other data, regardless of type and regardless of whether such items are in written form, in the form of computer data or in any other form, and other supporting evidence [@CL-CMP-2417; @CL-CMP-8206]. To satisfy contract negotiation, administration and audit requirements of the contracting agencies and the Comptroller General, the records are made available for 3 years after final payment or, for certain records, the period set in the specific retention sections, whichever expires first [@CL-CMP-2417; @CL-CMP-2418].

The specific sections apply to the records they identify, for the periods designated, provided retention is required under the applicability section [@CL-CMP-2423; @CL-CMP-2500]. Records are identified in terms of their purpose or use and not by specific name or form number [@CL-CMP-2502].

### Specific periods that matter to finance

Each period below is one of the specific periods designated in the specific retention sections, which apply provided retention is required under the applicability section [@CL-CMP-2423; @CL-CMP-2500].

- Accounts receivable invoices and the documents that detail what was billed: retain 4 years [@CL-CMP-2423; @CL-CMP-2424].
- Material, work order or service order files: retain 4 years [@CL-CMP-2423; @CL-CMP-2503].
- Cash advance recapitulations for employees' travel and related expenses: retain 4 years [@CL-CMP-2423; @CL-CMP-2504].
- Paid, canceled and voided checks, other than those issued for the payment of salary and wages: retain 4 years [@CL-CMP-2423; @CL-CMP-2505].
- Labor cost distribution cards or equivalent documents: retain 2 years [@CL-CMP-2423; @CL-CMP-2506].
- Petty cash records with their supporting documents: retain 2 years [@CL-CMP-2423; @CL-CMP-2507].
- Payroll sheets and registers, change slips and tax withholding statements: retain 4 years [@CL-CMP-2423; @CL-CMP-2508].
- Clock cards or other time and attendance cards: retain 2 years [@CL-CMP-2423; @CL-CMP-2509].
- Paid checks, receipts for wages paid in cash, or other evidence of payments for services rendered by employees: retain 2 years [@CL-CMP-2423; @CL-CMP-2510].
- Store requisitions for materials, supplies, equipment and services: retain 2 years [@CL-CMP-2423; @CL-CMP-2511].
- Work orders for maintenance and other services: retain 4 years [@CL-CMP-2423; @CL-CMP-2512].
- Equipment records, meaning usage and status reports and repair orders: retain 4 years [@CL-CMP-2423; @CL-CMP-2513].
- Expendable property records: retain 4 years [@CL-CMP-2423; @CL-CMP-2514].
- Receiving and inspection report records: retain 4 years [@CL-CMP-2423; @CL-CMP-2515].
- Property records: retain 4 years [@CL-CMP-2423; @CL-CMP-2516].

The specific sections list further records, including accounts payable support and purchase order files, that this card does not set out.

## How it works

Longer periods apply in three listed cases. A contract clause may specify a longer period than the basic rule [@CL-CMP-2419]. A contractor that keeps the records longer for its own purposes is held to the period of its own retention or 3 years after final payment, whichever period expires first [@CL-CMP-2420]. A contractor that misses the original due date for final indirect cost rate proposals has the retention periods in the section on specific retention periods extended one day for each day the proposal is late [@CL-CMP-2421; @CL-CMP-2422]. Images may stand in for original records in an audit if the listed requirements are met [@CL-CMP-2425], including keeping the originals for a minimum of one year after imaging [@CL-CMP-2425; @CL-CMP-2426].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A cost contract receives final payment at the end of year 1, and no clause, specific section or own-retention choice changes the period. The records are made available through the end of year 4, which is 3 years after final payment. Separately, a contract clause on another contract requires the records to be kept 7 years after final payment, so those records are made available through the end of year 8 under that clause. Answer: the basic period runs from final payment, and a longer period specified in a contract clause applies instead of the shorter basic period.

## Never confuse

The duty to make records available is not the Government's audit right over them; the audit-access clause is on [Audit and Records, Negotiation clause](cmp-audit-and-records-negotiation.md). It is also not the agencies' own contract file retention, which is on [Storage, handling and disposal of contract files](cmp-contract-file-storage-and-disposal.md).
