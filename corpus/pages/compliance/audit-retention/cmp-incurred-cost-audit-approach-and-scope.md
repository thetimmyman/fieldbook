## Purpose

Explains the contractor-wide audit approach, reliance on periodic business system reviews, and multi-segment audit coordination.

Incurred cost audits are usually performed on a contractor-wide basis to combine an understanding of management and financial systems with transaction testing across business activities [@CL-CMP-1706]. Periodic audits of relevant business systems establish the understanding of controls needed to determine the nature and extent of transaction testing on individual incurred cost audits [@CL-CMP-1707].

## How it works

The contractor-wide approach recognizes the efficiency of first gaining an understanding of the adequacy of management and financial systems and controls, combined with transaction testing across all business activities [@CL-CMP-1706]. Periodic audits of relevant business systems develop the understanding of systems and controls needed to set the nature and extent of transaction testing on individual incurred cost audits [@CL-CMP-1707]. For smaller contractors, separate audits of individual business systems may not be necessary, and an understanding of the relevant internal controls may be gained at the time of the final indirect cost audit or during other contract audits [@CL-CMP-1708]. Where an incurred cost proposal covers more than one contractor location at a multi-segment contractor, audit responsibilities at each location must be clearly defined and coordinated to ensure appropriate audit coverage [@CL-CMP-1709]. If the contractor has received a significant amount in Federal awards from a non-DoW agency and that agency is unwilling or unable to reimburse DCAA for its portion of the incurred cost audit services, this may constitute a limitation on the scope of audit [@CL-CMP-1710]. The single-agency rule for final indirect cost rates is on [Purpose and policy for final indirect cost rates and billing rates](../../indirect-rates/rate-lifecycle/rte-final-and-billing-rate-purpose.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A multi-segment contractor submits an incurred cost proposal spanning three operating segments. The lead audit office performs an assessment of the central corporate accounting and purchasing systems to set the extent of transaction testing. The lead auditor defines audit instructions for secondary field audit offices located at each operating segment, coordinating sample selections to avoid redundant testing and ensuring consistent indirect cost pool verification.

## Never confuse

A periodic audit of a business system builds the understanding of controls that sizes the testing; it is not the transaction testing done on the incurred cost audit itself [@CL-CMP-1707]. For what happens to a cost the audit finds unallowable, see [Disallowing costs after incurrence](cmp-disallowing-costs-after-incurrence.md).
