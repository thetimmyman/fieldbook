## Purpose

Explains the contractor-wide audit approach, reliance on periodic business system reviews, and multi-segment audit coordination.

Incurred cost audits are usually performed on a contractor-wide basis, which recognizes the efficiency of first gaining an understanding of the adequacy of management and financial systems and controls combined with transaction testing across all business activities as opposed to testing costs applicable to all contracts [@CL-CMP-1706; @CL-CMP-0250]. ## How it works

The contractor-wide approach recognizes the efficiency of first gaining an understanding of the adequacy of management and financial systems and controls combined with transaction testing across all business activities as opposed to testing costs applicable to all contracts [@CL-CMP-1706; @CL-CMP-0250]. Periodic audits of relevant business systems serve to develop an appropriate understanding of systems and controls necessary to determine the nature and extent of transaction testing needed on individual incurred cost audits [@CL-CMP-1707]. For smaller contractors, separate audits of individual contractor business systems may not be necessary, an understanding of the contractor's relevant internal controls may be gained at the time of the final indirect cost audit or during other contract audits [@CL-CMP-1708], and the auditor's understanding of controls gained from these audits should be documented in the permanent file [@CL-CMP-0328]. Auditing incurred costs at multi-segment contractors where an incurred cost proposal covers more than one contractor location requires that audit responsibilities at each location be clearly defined and coordinated to ensure appropriate audit coverage and reduce the potential for duplication of audit effort [@CL-CMP-1709; @CL-CMP-0327]. If the contractor has received a significant amount in Federal awards from a non-DoW agency, and the non-DoW agency is unwilling or unable to reimburse DCAA for its portion of DCAA's incurred cost audit services, this may constitute a limitation on the scope of audit [@CL-CMP-1710]. The single-agency rule for final indirect cost rates is on [Purpose and policy for final indirect cost rates and billing rates](../../indirect-rates/rate-lifecycle/rte-final-and-billing-rate-purpose.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A multi-segment contractor submits an incurred cost proposal spanning three operating segments. The lead audit office performs an assessment of the central corporate accounting and purchasing systems to set the extent of transaction testing. The lead auditor defines audit instructions for secondary field audit offices located at each operating segment, coordinating sample selections to avoid redundant testing and ensuring consistent indirect cost pool verification.

## Never confuse

Periodic audits of relevant business systems serve to develop an appropriate understanding of systems and controls necessary to determine the nature and extent of transaction testing needed on individual incurred cost audits [@CL-CMP-1707], while for smaller contractors, separate audits of individual contractor business systems may not be necessary and an understanding of the contractor's relevant internal controls may be gained at the time of the final indirect cost audit or during other contract audits [@CL-CMP-1708]. For what happens to a cost the audit finds unallowable, see [Disallowing costs after incurrence](cmp-disallowing-costs-after-incurrence.md).
