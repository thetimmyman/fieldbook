## Purpose

Defines qualitative and quantitative materiality criteria in incurred cost audits and how they shape the procedures the auditor performs.

| Applies to | Incurred cost audits, where materiality is judged on qualitative and quantitative factors [@CL-CMP-1711] |

Materiality refers to misstatements or omissions, whether individually or combined, that could reasonably influence the decisions of intended users; it involves both qualitative and quantitative factors, and their significance in each engagement is determined by the auditor's professional judgment [@CL-CMP-1711]. Which proposals are selected for audit at all, including the dollar-volume thresholds for risk-based sampling, is covered on [Risk-based sampling and the ways final indirect rates are settled](../../indirect-rates/rate-lifecycle/rte-risk-based-sampling-settlement.md).

## How it works

Auditors must design and perform procedures on all material balances to support their audit opinion, regardless of the assessed risk [@CL-CMP-1712]. Risk assessment influences the nature, timing and extent of those procedures [@CL-CMP-1712]. Qualitative risks may justify auditing amounts below the quantitative materiality threshold [@CL-CMP-1713]. Qualitative or other risk factors used to identify significant items must be supported by objective evidence, such as a history of questioned costs or documented concerns from the contracting officer [@CL-CMP-1714].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An auditor sets a quantitative materiality threshold of 100,000 for an incurred cost audit. A balance of 400,000 is above the threshold, so the auditor performs procedures on it whatever the assessed risk, and the assessed risk only changes how much testing is done. A second balance of 60,000 is below the threshold, but the account has a history of questioned costs. The auditor documents that history as objective evidence and tests the balance anyway.

## Never confuse

Procedures are performed on all material balances regardless of assessed risk [@CL-CMP-1712], and qualitative risks may justify auditing amounts below the quantitative materiality threshold [@CL-CMP-1713]. It does not decide whether a proposal is audited at all, which is a separate sampling decision. See [Final indirect cost rate proposal](../../indirect-rates/rate-lifecycle/rte-final-indirect-rate-proposal.md).
