## Purpose

Shows how the government flags and stops questionable costs during performance, and how the contractor answers.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-0654] |
| Codified citation | FAR 42.803 [@CL-CMP-0654] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 42.603 [@CL-CMP-0037; @CL-CMP-0038] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-0654] |
| Applies to | Cost-reimbursement contracts, the cost-reimbursement portion of fixed-price contracts, letter contracts that reimburse costs, and time-and-material and labor-hour contracts [@CL-CMP-0654] |

At any time during the performance of a contract of a type referred to in FAR 42.802, the cognizant contracting officer responsible for administering the contract may issue the contractor a written notice of intent to disallow specified costs incurred or planned for incurrence [@CL-CMP-0651]. However, before issuing the notice, the contracting officer will make every reasonable effort to reach a satisfactory settlement through discussions with the contractor [@CL-CMP-0652]. If the contractor disagrees with the notice, the contractor may submit a written response to the contracting officer [@CL-CMP-2576], and any such response shall be answered by withdrawal of the notice or by making a written decision within 60 days [@CL-CMP-0653]. In the event the contractor submits a response that disagrees with the notice, the option to either withdraw the notice or issue the written decision shall be exercised by the contracting officer who issued the notice, except that when elements of indirect cost are involved, the official responsible for exercising that option shall be the contracting officer responsible under FAR 42.705 for determining final indirect cost rates [@CL-CMP-0244; @CL-CMP-1101].

Cost-reimbursement contracts, the cost-reimbursement portion of fixed-price contracts, letter contracts that provide for reimbursement of costs, and time-and-material and labor-hour contracts provide for disallowing costs during the course of performance after the costs have been incurred [@CL-CMP-0654]. If the examination of a voucher raises a question regarding the allowability of a cost under the contract terms, the auditor, after informal discussion as appropriate, may, where authorized by agency regulations, issue a notice of contract costs suspended and/or disapproved [@CL-CMP-0655] simultaneously to the contractor and the disbursing officer, with a copy to the cognizant contracting officer, for deduction from current payments with respect to costs claimed but not considered reimbursable [@CL-CMP-1100]. If the contractor disagrees with the deduction from current payments, the contractor may [@CL-CMP-0245] submit a written request to the cognizant contracting officer to consider whether the unreimbursed costs should be paid and to discuss the findings with the contractor, file a claim under the Disputes clause [@CL-CMP-0656], which the cognizant contracting officer will process in accordance with agency procedures, or do both [@CL-CMP-1102].

## How it works

At any time during the performance of a contract of a type referred to in FAR 42.802, the cognizant contracting officer responsible for administering the contract may issue the contractor a written notice of intent to disallow specified costs incurred or planned for incurrence, after making every reasonable effort to reach a satisfactory settlement through discussions with the contractor [@CL-CMP-0651; @CL-CMP-0652]. In the event the contractor submits a response that disagrees with the notice, any such response shall be answered by withdrawal of the notice or by making a written decision within 60 days [@CL-CMP-0653], and that option shall be exercised by the contracting officer who issued the notice, except that when elements of indirect cost are involved, the official responsible for exercising that option shall be the contracting officer responsible under FAR 42.705 for determining final indirect cost rates [@CL-CMP-0244; @CL-CMP-1101]. If the examination of a voucher raises a question regarding the allowability of a cost under the contract terms, the auditor, after informal discussion as appropriate, may, where authorized by agency regulations, issue a notice of contract costs suspended and/or disapproved simultaneously to the contractor and the disbursing officer, with a copy to the cognizant contracting officer, for deduction from current payments with respect to costs claimed but not considered reimbursable [@CL-CMP-0655; @CL-CMP-1100], and if the contractor disagrees with the deduction from current payments, the contractor may submit a written request to the cognizant contracting officer to consider whether the unreimbursed costs should be paid and to discuss the findings with the contractor, file a claim under the Disputes clause processed by the cognizant contracting officer in accordance with agency procedures, or do both [@CL-CMP-0245; @CL-CMP-0656; @CL-CMP-1102].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 42 text in place of the codified text under Class Deviation 2026-O0050, effective March 16, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9759] [@CL-CMP-9761]. RFO FAR 42.603 states that the contracting officer must apply the procedures in that section when disallowing costs which the contractor has already incurred [@CL-CMP-0037]. Under RFO paragraph (b)(3), if the examination of a voucher raises questions regarding the allowability of a cost under the contract terms, the auditor may issue a notice of contract costs suspended and/or disapproved to the contractor and the disbursing officer, with a copy provided to the cognizant contracting officer [@CL-CMP-0038]; the notice permits a deduction from current payments, after informal discussion and when authorized by agency regulations, for costs claimed but not considered reimbursable [@CL-CMP-0039]. Under RFO paragraph (b)(4), if the contractor disagrees with the deduction from current payments, the contractor may [@CL-CMP-0083] submit a written request to the cognizant contracting officer to consider whether the unreimbursed costs should be paid and to discuss the findings with the contractor, file a claim under the Disputes clause [@CL-CMP-0040], which the cognizant contracting officer will process in accordance with agency procedures, or do both [@CL-CMP-0041]. For the notice of intent to disallow, RFO FAR 42.601(a) states that contracting officers must make reasonable efforts to proactively address potentially unallowable costs with contractors [@CL-CMP-0044], and if unable to settle the matter through discussion, the cognizant contracting officer responsible for administering the contract may issue a written notice of intent to disallow specified costs incurred or planned for incurrence during the performance of any cost-reimbursement contract, the cost-reimbursement portion of any fixed-price contract, a fixed-price incentive contract, letter contracts that provide for reimbursement of costs, or time-and-material and labor-hour contracts [@CL-CMP-0042; @CL-CMP-0043]. RFO paragraph (b) states that in the event of disagreement, the contractor may submit a written response to the contracting officer, and any such response must be answered by withdrawal of the notice or by making a written decision within 60 days [@CL-CMP-0045]. RFO paragraph (f) states that if the contractor submits a response that disagrees with the notice, the contracting officer who issued the notice must either withdraw the notice or issue the written decision, except when elements of indirect cost are involved, in which case the contracting officer responsible under 42.505 for determining final indirect cost rates must issue the decision [@CL-CMP-0082; @CL-CMP-0046].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

During performance the contracting officer spots a cost it considers unallowable and, after discussion fails, issues a notice of intent to disallow. The contractor answers in writing, and the officer issues a written decision within 60 days. Separately, where agency regulations authorize it, the auditor issues a notice suspending 15,000 of a voucher it deems not reimbursable, for deduction from current payments.

## Never confuse

Disallowing a cost during performance differs from the unallowable-cost penalties: cost-reimbursement contracts, the cost-reimbursement portion of fixed-price contracts, letter contracts that provide for reimbursement of costs, and time-and-material and labor-hour contracts provide for disallowing costs during the course of performance after the costs have been incurred [@CL-CMP-0654]. For penalties on unallowable costs in final indirect cost rate proposals, see [Penalties for unallowable costs](../cas-allowability/cmp-unallowable-cost-penalty.md).

Under the codified text, cost-reimbursement contracts, the cost-reimbursement portion of fixed-price contracts, letter contracts that provide for reimbursement of costs, and time-and-material and labor-hour contracts provide for disallowing costs during the course of performance after the costs have been incurred [@CL-CMP-0654], while under the RFO text, RFO FAR 42.603 states that the contracting officer must apply the procedures in that section when disallowing costs which the contractor has already incurred [@CL-CMP-0037] and RFO FAR 42.601(a) lists cost-reimbursement contracts, the cost-reimbursement portion of any fixed-price contract, fixed-price incentive contracts, letter contracts that provide for reimbursement of costs, and time-and-material and labor-hour contracts [@CL-CMP-0042; @CL-CMP-0043].
