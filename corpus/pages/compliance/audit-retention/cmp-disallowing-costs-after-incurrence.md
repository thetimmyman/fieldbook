## Purpose

Shows how the government flags and stops questionable costs during performance, and how the contractor answers.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-0654] |
| Codified citation | FAR 42.803 [@CL-CMP-0654] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 42.603 [@CL-CMP-0037; @CL-CMP-0038] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-0654] |
| Applies to | Cost-reimbursement contracts, the cost-reimbursement portion of fixed-price contracts, letter contracts that reimburse costs, and time-and-material and labor-hour contracts [@CL-CMP-0654] |

During performance of a contract of a type referred to in 42.802, the cognizant contracting officer may flag specified costs incurred or planned by a written notice of intent to disallow, but only after making every reasonable effort to settle through discussions with the contractor [@CL-CMP-0651; @CL-CMP-0652]. In the event of disagreement, the contractor may submit a written response to the contracting officer, and any such response shall be answered by withdrawal of the notice or by a written decision within 60 days [@CL-CMP-2576; @CL-CMP-0653]. If the contractor's response disagrees with the notice, the officer who issued the notice withdraws it or issues the decision, except that when elements of indirect cost are involved, the contracting officer responsible for determining final indirect cost rates issues the decision [@CL-CMP-1101].

After costs are incurred, disallowance runs on cost-reimbursement contracts, the cost-reimbursement portion of fixed-price contracts, letter contracts that reimburse costs, and time-and-material and labor-hour contracts [@CL-CMP-0654]. When a voucher raises an allowability question, the auditor, after informal discussion as appropriate and where authorized by agency regulations, may issue a notice of contract costs suspended or disapproved [@CL-CMP-0655]. The notice goes to the contractor and the disbursing officer, with a copy to the cognizant contracting officer, for deduction from current payments with respect to costs claimed but not considered reimbursable [@CL-CMP-1100]. If the contractor disagrees with the deduction from current payments, it may submit a written request to the cognizant contracting officer to consider whether the unreimbursed costs should be paid and to discuss the findings with the contractor, or file a claim under the Disputes clause [@CL-CMP-0656], which the cognizant contracting officer will process in accordance with agency procedures, or do both [@CL-CMP-1102].

## How it works

The contracting officer watches costs during performance and may issue a written notice of intent to disallow before trouble compounds, after trying to settle by discussion. If the contractor pushes back, the issuing officer either withdraws the notice or writes a decision, except that when elements of indirect cost are involved the officer responsible for final indirect cost rates writes it. For vouchers, the auditor can, where authorized by agency regulations, issue a notice suspending or disapproving questionable costs for deduction from current payments, and the contractor may contest that with a written request to the officer, a Disputes-clause claim, or both.

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 42 text in place of the codified text under Class Deviation 2026-O0050, effective March 16, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9759] [@CL-CMP-9761]. RFO FAR 42.603 introduces its procedures by saying the contracting officer must apply them when disallowing costs which the contractor has already incurred, where the codified section introduces its procedures with the list of contract types that provide for disallowing costs after incurrence [@CL-CMP-0037; @CL-CMP-0654]. Under RFO paragraph (b)(3), if a voucher raises allowability questions, the auditor may issue a notice of contract costs suspended and or disapproved to the contractor and the disbursing officer, and a copy must be provided to the cognizant contracting officer, where the codified sentence has the notice go simultaneously to the contractor and the disbursing officer [@CL-CMP-0038; @CL-CMP-0655; @CL-CMP-1100]; the notice permits deduction from current payments, after informal discussion and when authorized by agency regulations, for costs claimed but not considered reimbursable, where the codified sentence says after informal discussion as appropriate [@CL-CMP-0039; @CL-CMP-0655]. If the contractor disagrees with the deduction from current payments, RFO paragraph (b)(4) keeps the contractor's options of a written request to the cognizant contracting officer, a claim under the Disputes clause processed by the cognizant contracting officer in accordance with agency procedures, or both [@CL-CMP-0083; @CL-CMP-0040; @CL-CMP-0041; @CL-CMP-0656; @CL-CMP-1102]. For the notice of intent to disallow, RFO FAR 42.601(a) says contracting officers must make reasonable efforts to proactively address potentially unallowable costs with contractors, where the codified paragraph requires every reasonable effort to reach a satisfactory settlement through discussions before the notice is issued [@CL-CMP-0044; @CL-CMP-0652], and that if unable to settle the matter through discussion the cognizant contracting officer responsible for administering the contract may issue a written notice of intent to disallow specified costs incurred or planned for incurrence during the performance of the contract types the paragraph lists [@CL-CMP-0042; @CL-CMP-0651], which include fixed-price incentive contracts [@CL-CMP-0043]. RFO paragraph (b) keeps the rule that, in case of disagreement, a written response from the contractor is answered by withdrawal of the notice or a written decision within 60 days [@CL-CMP-0045; @CL-CMP-0653], and paragraph (f) keeps the rule that, if the contractor submits a response that disagrees with the notice, the issuing officer withdraws the notice or issues the decision, except that when elements of indirect cost are involved the contracting officer responsible under 42.505 for determining final indirect cost rates issues it [@CL-CMP-0082; @CL-CMP-0046; @CL-CMP-1101].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

During performance the contracting officer spots a cost it considers unallowable and, after discussion fails, issues a notice of intent to disallow. The contractor answers in writing, and the officer issues a written decision within 60 days. Separately, where agency regulations authorize it, the auditor issues a notice suspending 15,000 of a voucher it deems not reimbursable, for deduction from current payments.

## Never confuse

Disallowance after incurrence is not the penalty for unallowable costs in a final rate proposal. This card covers stopping or recouping a questionable cost during performance; the penalty regime applies to unallowable costs left in the final indirect cost rate proposal. See [Penalties for unallowable costs](../cas-allowability/cmp-unallowable-cost-penalty.md).

The codified section opens with the list of contract types that provide for disallowing costs after incurrence [@CL-CMP-0654], while the RFO opening sentence refers only to disallowing costs which the contractor has already incurred [@CL-CMP-0037] and the RFO notice-of-intent paragraph lists its contract types itself, including fixed-price incentive contracts [@CL-CMP-0043].
