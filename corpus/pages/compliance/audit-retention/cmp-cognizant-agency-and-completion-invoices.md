## Purpose

Shows how the cognizant Federal agency is set, how final indirect cost rates are established, when the contractor must submit a completion invoice, and how rates are settled with an educational institution.

For contractors other than educational institutions and nonprofit organizations, the cognizant Federal agency normally will be the agency with the largest dollar amount of negotiated contracts, including options [@CL-CMP-7267]. For educational institutions (defined as institutions of higher education in the OMB Uniform Guidance at 2 CFR part 200, subpart A, and section 1001 of title 20 of the United States Code) and nonprofit organizations (as defined in the OMB Uniform Guidance at 2 CFR part 200), the cognizant Federal agency for indirect costs is established according to the OMB Uniform Guidance at 2 CFR part 200, appendices III and IV, respectively [@CL-CMP-0193; @CL-CMP-7455].

Once a Federal agency assumes cognizance for a contractor, it should remain cognizant for at least 5 years to ensure continuity and ease of administration [@CL-CMP-7268]. If, at the end of the 5-year period, another agency has the largest dollar amount of negotiated contracts, including options, the two agencies shall coordinate and determine which will assume cognizance [@CL-CMP-7456]. However, if circumstances warrant it and the affected agencies agree, cognizance may transfer prior to the expiration of the 5-year period [@CL-CMP-7457].

## How it works

Final indirect cost rates shall be established on the basis of the contracting officer determination procedure (see FAR 42.705-1) or the auditor determination procedure (see FAR 42.705-2) [@CL-CMP-7458]. Within 120 days (or longer period, if approved in writing by the contracting officer) after settlement of the final annual indirect cost rates for all years of a physically complete contract, the contractor must submit a completion invoice or voucher reflecting the settled amounts and rates [@CL-CMP-7269]. To determine whether a period longer than 120 days is appropriate, the contracting officer should consider whether there are extenuating circumstances, such as the following: pending closeout of subcontracts awaiting Government audit, pending contractor, subcontractor, or Government claims [@CL-CMP-7459], delays in the disposition of Government property, delays in contract reconciliation, and any other pertinent factors [@CL-CMP-7460].

If the contractor fails to submit a completion invoice or voucher within the time specified in paragraph (b), the contracting officer may determine the amounts due to the contractor under the contract and record this determination in a unilateral modification to the contract [@CL-CMP-7270]. This contracting officer determination must be issued as a final decision in accordance with FAR 33.211 [@CL-CMP-7461].

Under cost-reimbursement contracts with educational institutions, postdetermined final indirect cost rates shall be used in the settlement of indirect costs for all cost-reimbursement contracts with educational institutions, unless predetermined final indirect cost rates are authorized and used (see paragraph (b) of FAR 42.705-3) [@CL-CMP-7271]. The OMB Uniform Guidance at 2 CFR part 200, appendix III, assigns each educational institution (defined as an institution of higher education in the OMB Uniform Guidance at 2 CFR part 200, subpart A, and section 1001 of title 20 of the United States Code) to a single Government agency for the negotiation of indirect cost rates [@CL-CMP-7601] and provides that those rates shall be accepted by all Federal agencies [@CL-CMP-7462]. Each cognizant agency for indirect costs is required to establish billing rates and final indirect cost rates at the educational institution [@CL-CMP-7463] consistent with the requirements of FAR subpart 42.7, FAR subpart 31.3, and the OMB Uniform Guidance at 2 CFR part 200, subpart E and appendix III, and the agency shall follow the procedures outlined in FAR 42.705-1(b) [@CL-CMP-7602]. If the cognizant agency cannot reach agreement with an institution, the appeals system of the cognizant agency shall be followed for resolution of the dispute [@CL-CMP-7464].

Under cost-reimbursement research and development contracts with universities, colleges, or other educational institutions (section 4708 of title 41 of the United States Code), payment for reimbursable indirect costs may be made on the basis of predetermined final indirect cost rates [@CL-CMP-7465]. The cognizant agency is not required to establish predetermined rates, but if they are established, their use must be extended to all the institution's Government contracts [@CL-CMP-7466]. In deciding whether the use of predetermined rates would be appropriate for the educational institution concerned, the agency should consider both the stability of the institution's indirect costs and bases over a period of years [@CL-CMP-7603] and any anticipated changes in the amount of the direct and indirect costs [@CL-CMP-7604]. Unless their use is approved at a level in the agency higher than the contracting officer, predetermined rates shall not be used when any one of three alternatives applies: there has been no recent audit of the indirect costs [@CL-CMP-7272]; there have been frequent or wide fluctuations in the indirect cost rates and the bases over a period of years; or the estimated reimbursable costs for any individual contract are expected to exceed $1 million annually [@CL-CMP-7467].

If predetermined rates are to be used and no rates have been previously established for the institution's current fiscal year, the agency shall obtain from the institution a proposal for predetermined rates [@CL-CMP-7605]. If the proposal is found to be generally acceptable, the agency shall negotiate the predetermined rates with the institution [@CL-CMP-7606]. The rates should be based on an audit of the institution's costs for the year immediately preceding the year in which the rates are being negotiated [@CL-CMP-7429]. If this is not possible, an earlier audit may be used, but appropriate steps should be taken to identify and evaluate significant variations in costs incurred or in bases used that may have a bearing on the reasonableness of the proposed rates [@CL-CMP-7607]. In the case of smaller contracts (i.e., contracts that do not exceed the simplified acquisition threshold), an audit made at an earlier date is acceptable provided there have been no significant changes in the contractor's organization [@CL-CMP-7608] and it is reasonably apparent that another audit would have little effect on the rates finally agreed upon and the potential for overpayment of indirect cost is relatively insignificant [@CL-CMP-7609]. If predetermined rates are used, the contracting officer shall include the negotiated rates and bases in the contract Schedule, and see FAR 16.307(g), which prescribes the clause at FAR 52.216-15, Predetermined Indirect Cost Rates [@CL-CMP-7468]. Predetermined indirect cost rates shall be applicable for a period of not more than four years [@CL-CMP-7273]. The agency shall obtain the contractor's proposal for new predetermined rates sufficiently in advance so that the new rates, based on current data, may be promptly negotiated near the beginning of the new fiscal year or other period agreed to by the parties (see paragraphs (b) and (d) of the clause at FAR 52.216-15, Predetermined Indirect Cost Rates) [@CL-CMP-7610]. Contracting officers shall use billing rates established by the agency to reimburse the contractor for work performed during a period not covered by predetermined rates [@CL-CMP-7428]. The OMB Uniform Guidance at 2 CFR part 200, subpart E and appendix III, provides additional guidance on how long predetermined rates may be used [@CL-CMP-7611].

For nonprofit organizations other than educational and state and local governments, FAR 42.705-5 says to see the OMB Uniform Guidance at 2 CFR part 200, subpart E and appendix IV, but to see appendix VIII for nonprofit organizations exempt from subpart E [@CL-CMP-9676].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 42 text in place of the codified text under Class Deviation 2026-O0050, effective March 16, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9759] [@CL-CMP-9761]. RFO FAR 42.002(a) states that for each contractor business unit, except for educational institutions and nonprofits, the cognizant Federal agency is the agency with the largest dollar amount in negotiated contracts, including options [@CL-CMP-0007]. The cognizant Federal agency for educational institutions (defined as institutions of higher education at 2 CFR part 200, subpart A, and section 1001 of title 20 of the United States Code) and nonprofit organizations (as defined at 2 CFR part 200) for indirect costs is determined according to the OMB Uniform Guidance at 2 CFR part 200, appendices III and IV, respectively [@CL-CMP-0194; @CL-CMP-0008]. RFO FAR 42.002(b) states that once a Federal agency assumes cognizance for a contractor, it should remain the cognizant Federal agency for at least 5 years [@CL-CMP-0009]. RFO FAR 42.505(b) states that within 120 days (or longer period, if approved in writing by the contracting officer) after settlement of the final annual indirect cost rates for all years of a physically complete contract, the contractor must submit a completion invoice or voucher reflecting the settled amounts and rates [@CL-CMP-0010]. RFO paragraph (c)(1) states that if the contractor fails to submit a completion invoice or voucher within the time specified in paragraph (b), the contracting officer may determine the amounts due to the contractor under the contract and record this determination in a unilateral modification to the contract [@CL-CMP-0011], and paragraph (c)(2) states that this contracting officer determination must be issued as a final decision in accordance with part 33 [@CL-CMP-0012]. For educational institutions, RFO FAR 42.505-3(a)(1) states that postdetermined final indirect cost rates must be used in the settlement of indirect costs for all cost-reimbursement contracts with educational institutions, unless predetermined final indirect cost rates are authorized and used (see paragraph (b) of that section) [@CL-CMP-0013]. RFO paragraph (a)(3) states that the cognizant agency is required to establish billing rates and final indirect cost rates at the educational institution using the procedures outlined in 42.505-1(b) [@CL-CMP-0014], and paragraph (a)(4) states that if the cognizant agency cannot reach agreement with an institution, the cognizant agency will resolve the dispute in accordance with agency procedures and the OMB Uniform Guidance [@CL-CMP-0015]. RFO paragraph (b)(1) states that for cost-reimbursement research and development contracts with universities, colleges, or other educational institutions (section 4708 of title 41 of the United States Code), agencies may use predetermined final indirect cost rates and, if established, their use must be extended to all the institution's Government contracts [@CL-CMP-0016]. RFO paragraph (b)(3) states that unless their use is approved at a level in the cognizant agency higher than the contracting officer, predetermined rates must not be used when any one of three alternatives applies [@CL-CMP-0017]: there has been no recent audit of the indirect costs; there have been frequent or wide fluctuations in the indirect cost rates and the bases over a period of years; or the estimated reimbursable costs for any individual contract are expected to exceed $1 million annually [@CL-CMP-0018]. Under RFO paragraph (b)(4), if no predetermined rates exist for the institution's current fiscal year, the institution must provide the agency a proposal for predetermined rates, and if the proposal is found to be generally acceptable, the agency must negotiate the predetermined rates with the institution [@CL-CMP-0019]; the rates should be based on an audit of the institution's costs for the year immediately preceding the year that the rates are being negotiated, and if this is not possible, or if not available, earlier audits, if determined appropriate by the cognizant agency, may be used, but appropriate steps should be taken to identify and evaluate significant variations in costs incurred or in bases used that may have a bearing on the reasonableness of the proposed rates [@CL-CMP-0195; @CL-CMP-0020]. RFO paragraph (b)(5) states that if predetermined rates are used, the contracting officer must include the negotiated rates and bases in the contract Schedule (see part 16 for the prescription of the clause at 52.216-15, Predetermined Indirect Cost Rates) [@CL-CMP-0021]. RFO paragraph (b)(6) states that predetermined indirect cost rates must be applicable for a period of not more than four years [@CL-CMP-0022] and that the agency must obtain new rate proposals sufficiently in advance so that the new rates, based on current data, may be promptly negotiated near the beginning of the next fiscal year or other period agreed to by the parties (see paragraphs (b) and (d) of the clause at 52.216-15) [@CL-CMP-0023]. RFO paragraph (b)(7) states that billing rates are to be used for periods not covered by predetermined rates [@CL-CMP-0024], and paragraph (b)(8) states that the OMB Uniform Guidance at 2 CFR part 200, subpart E and appendix III, provides additional guidance on how long predetermined rates may be used [@CL-CMP-0025]. RFO FAR 42.505-5 states that the OMB Uniform Guidance at 2 CFR part 200, subpart E and appendix V, establishes the procedures for determining the cognizant agency for State and local government indirect costs associated with federally-funded programs and activities [@CL-CMP-8949], and that the rates approved by the cognizant agency must be used by all Federal agencies [@CL-CMP-8950].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A cost-reimbursement contract is physically complete, and the final annual indirect cost rates for all its years are settled on day 0. The contractor must submit its completion invoice by day 120 unless the contracting officer has approved a longer period in writing [@CL-CMP-7269]. The contractor submits on day 100, so the deadline is met. On a second contract, the contractor submits nothing by day 120 and holds no written approval of a longer period, so the contracting officer may determine the amounts due and record that in a unilateral modification, issued as a final decision in accordance with FAR 33.211 [@CL-CMP-7270; @CL-CMP-7461].

A university holds a cost-reimbursement research and development contract whose estimated reimbursable costs are expected to be 1.4 million dollars a year. The agency considers predetermined rates, but because the costs for this individual contract are expected to exceed $1 million annually, the rates may not be used unless a level in the agency higher than the contracting officer approves their use [@CL-CMP-7467; @CL-CMP-7272]. Without that approval, postdetermined final indirect cost rates settle the indirect costs [@CL-CMP-7271]. If the higher level approves and predetermined rates are used, the Schedule carries the rates and bases and the rates apply for no more than 4 years, subject to the additional guidance in the OMB Uniform Guidance on how long they may be used [@CL-CMP-7468; @CL-CMP-7273; @CL-CMP-7611].

## Never confuse

For contractors other than educational institutions and nonprofit organizations, the cognizant Federal agency normally will be the agency with the largest dollar amount of negotiated contracts, including options [@CL-CMP-7267], while for educational institutions (defined as institutions of higher education in the OMB Uniform Guidance at 2 CFR part 200, subpart A, and section 1001 of title 20 of the United States Code) and nonprofit organizations (as defined in the OMB Uniform Guidance at 2 CFR part 200), the cognizant Federal agency for indirect costs is established according to the OMB Uniform Guidance at 2 CFR part 200, appendices III and IV, respectively [@CL-CMP-0193; @CL-CMP-7455].

Postdetermined final indirect cost rates shall be used in the settlement of indirect costs for all cost-reimbursement contracts with educational institutions, unless predetermined final indirect cost rates are authorized and used [@CL-CMP-7271], and predetermined rates shall not be used when any one of the three alternatives applies unless their use is approved at a level in the agency higher than the contracting officer [@CL-CMP-7272; @CL-CMP-7467]. See [Contracting officer determination of final indirect rates](../../indirect-rates/rate-lifecycle/rte-co-final-indirect-rates.md).

Under the codified text, the cognizant Federal agency normally will be the agency with the largest dollar amount of negotiated contracts, including options, for contractors other than educational institutions and nonprofit organizations, and once a Federal agency assumes cognizance it should remain cognizant for at least 5 years [@CL-CMP-7267; @CL-CMP-7268]; if at the end of that period another agency has the largest dollar amount of negotiated contracts, including options, the two agencies shall coordinate and determine which will assume cognizance, and if circumstances warrant it and the affected agencies agree, cognizance may transfer prior to expiration of the 5-year period [@CL-CMP-7456; @CL-CMP-7457], while under the RFO text, for each contractor business unit except for educational institutions and nonprofits, the cognizant Federal agency is the agency with the largest dollar amount in negotiated contracts, including options, and once a Federal agency assumes cognizance for a contractor, it should remain the cognizant Federal agency for at least 5 years [@CL-CMP-0007; @CL-CMP-0009].
