## Purpose

Shows how the cognizant Federal agency is set, how final indirect cost rates are established, when the contractor must submit a completion invoice, and how rates are settled with an educational institution.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-7267] |
| Codified citation | FAR 42.003 [@CL-CMP-7267]; FAR 42.705 [@CL-CMP-7269]; FAR 42.705-3 [@CL-CMP-7271] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-7267] |
| Applies to | The cognizant Federal agency for a contractor [@CL-CMP-7267; @CL-CMP-7455]; physically complete contracts awaiting a completion invoice [@CL-CMP-7269]; cost-reimbursement contracts with educational institutions [@CL-CMP-7271] |

For contractors other than educational institutions and nonprofit organizations, the cognizant Federal agency normally will be the agency with the largest dollar amount of negotiated contracts, including options [@CL-CMP-7267]. For educational institutions and nonprofit organizations, the cognizant Federal agency for indirect costs is established according to the OMB Uniform Guidance at 2 CFR part 200, appendices III and IV, respectively [@CL-CMP-7455].

Once a Federal agency assumes cognizance for a contractor, it should remain cognizant for at least 5 years to ensure continuity and ease of administration [@CL-CMP-7268]. If, at the end of the 5-year period, another agency has the largest dollar amount of negotiated contracts, including options, the two agencies must coordinate and determine which will assume cognizance [@CL-CMP-7456]. If circumstances warrant it and the affected agencies agree, cognizance may transfer before the 5-year period expires [@CL-CMP-7457].

## How it works

Final indirect cost rates must be established on the basis of the contracting officer determination procedure or the auditor determination procedure [@CL-CMP-7458]. Within 120 days after settlement of the final annual indirect cost rates for all years of a physically complete contract, or a longer period approved in writing by the contracting officer, the contractor must submit a completion invoice or voucher reflecting the settled amounts and rates [@CL-CMP-7269]. To decide whether a period longer than 120 days is appropriate, the contracting officer should consider whether there are extenuating circumstances, such as pending closeout of subcontracts awaiting Government audit and pending contractor, subcontractor or Government claims [@CL-CMP-7459]. They also include delays in the disposition of Government property, delays in contract reconciliation and any other pertinent factors [@CL-CMP-7460].

If the contractor fails to submit the completion invoice or voucher within that time, the contracting officer may determine the amounts due to the contractor under the contract and record the determination in a unilateral modification [@CL-CMP-7270]. The determination must be issued as a final decision in accordance with FAR 33.211 [@CL-CMP-7461].

For educational institutions, postdetermined final indirect cost rates must be used in the settlement of indirect costs for all cost-reimbursement contracts, unless predetermined final indirect cost rates are authorized and used [@CL-CMP-7271]. Under the OMB Uniform Guidance at 2 CFR part 200, appendix III, each educational institution is assigned to a single Government agency for the negotiation of indirect cost rates [@CL-CMP-7601], and those rates must be accepted by all Federal agencies [@CL-CMP-7462]. The cognizant agency for indirect costs must establish the billing rates and final indirect cost rates at the institution [@CL-CMP-7463], consistent with FAR subpart 42.7, FAR subpart 31.3 and the OMB Uniform Guidance at 2 CFR part 200, subpart E and appendix III, and it must follow the procedures outlined in FAR 42.705-1(b) [@CL-CMP-7602]. If it cannot reach agreement with the institution, the appeals system of the cognizant agency must be followed [@CL-CMP-7464].

Under cost-reimbursement research and development contracts with universities, colleges or other educational institutions, payment for reimbursable indirect costs may be made on the basis of predetermined final indirect cost rates [@CL-CMP-7465]. The cognizant agency is not required to establish them, but if it does, their use must be extended to all the institution's Government contracts [@CL-CMP-7466]. In deciding whether predetermined rates would be appropriate for the institution, the agency should consider both the stability of the institution's indirect costs and bases over a period of years [@CL-CMP-7603] and any anticipated changes in the amount of the direct and indirect costs [@CL-CMP-7604]. Unless their use is approved at a level in the agency higher than the contracting officer, predetermined rates must not be used when there has been no recent audit of the indirect costs [@CL-CMP-7272]. The same limit applies when there have been frequent or wide fluctuations in the indirect cost rates and the bases over a period of years, or when the estimated reimbursable costs for any individual contract are expected to exceed $1 million annually [@CL-CMP-7467].

If predetermined rates are used, the contracting officer must include the negotiated rates and bases in the contract Schedule, and FAR 16.307(g) prescribes the clause at FAR 52.216-15, Predetermined Indirect Cost Rates [@CL-CMP-7468]. Predetermined indirect cost rates apply for a period of not more than 4 years [@CL-CMP-7273], and the OMB Uniform Guidance at 2 CFR part 200, subpart E and appendix III, provides additional guidance on how long they may be used [@CL-CMP-7611]. The agency must obtain the contractor's proposal for new predetermined rates sufficiently in advance so that the new rates, based on current data, may be promptly negotiated near the beginning of the new fiscal year or other period agreed to by the parties [@CL-CMP-7610]. If predetermined rates are to be used and no rates have been previously established for the institution's current fiscal year, the agency must obtain a proposal for predetermined rates from the institution [@CL-CMP-7605]. If the proposal is found to be generally acceptable, the agency must negotiate the predetermined rates with the institution [@CL-CMP-7606]. Predetermined rates should be based on an audit of the institution's costs for the year immediately preceding the year in which they are negotiated [@CL-CMP-7429]. If that is not possible, an earlier audit may be used, but appropriate steps should be taken to identify and evaluate significant variations in costs incurred or in bases used that may have a bearing on the reasonableness of the proposed rates [@CL-CMP-7607]. For contracts that do not exceed the simplified acquisition threshold, an audit made at an earlier date is acceptable if there have been no significant changes in the contractor's organization [@CL-CMP-7608] and it is reasonably apparent that another audit would have little effect on the rates finally agreed upon and the potential for overpayment of indirect cost is relatively insignificant [@CL-CMP-7609]. Contracting officers must use billing rates established by the agency to reimburse the contractor for work performed during a period not covered by predetermined rates [@CL-CMP-7428].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A cost-reimbursement contract is physically complete, and the final annual indirect cost rates for all its years are settled on day 0. The contractor must submit its completion invoice by day 120 unless the contracting officer has approved a longer period in writing [@CL-CMP-7269]. The contractor submits on day 100, so the deadline is met. On a second contract, the contractor submits nothing by day 120 and holds no written approval of a longer period, so the contracting officer may determine the amounts due and record that in a unilateral modification, issued as a final decision [@CL-CMP-7270; @CL-CMP-7461].

A university holds a cost-reimbursement research and development contract whose estimated reimbursable costs are expected to be 1.4 million dollars a year. The agency considers predetermined rates, but because the costs for this individual contract are expected to exceed $1 million annually, the rates may not be used unless a level in the agency higher than the contracting officer approves their use [@CL-CMP-7467; @CL-CMP-7272]. Without that approval, postdetermined final indirect cost rates settle the indirect costs [@CL-CMP-7271]. If the higher level approves and predetermined rates are used, the Schedule carries the rates and bases and the rates apply for no more than 4 years, subject to the additional guidance in the OMB Uniform Guidance on how long they may be used [@CL-CMP-7468; @CL-CMP-7273; @CL-CMP-7611].

## Never confuse

The cognizant Federal agency for a contractor with negotiated contracts is the one with the largest dollar amount, including options, while the agency for an educational institution or nonprofit organization is set by the OMB Uniform Guidance [@CL-CMP-7267; @CL-CMP-7455].

Predetermined rates and postdetermined rates are different settlement bases for an educational institution. Postdetermined rates are the default, and predetermined rates need the conditions above [@CL-CMP-7271; @CL-CMP-7272; @CL-CMP-7467]. The linked cards cover the determination procedures; see [Contracting officer determination of final indirect rates](../../indirect-rates/rate-lifecycle/rte-co-final-indirect-rates.md).
