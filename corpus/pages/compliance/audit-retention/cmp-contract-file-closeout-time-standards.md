## Purpose

Shows how soon each kind of contract file should be closed, what starts each clock, and when a file must stay open.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-2433] |
| Codified citation | FAR 4.804-1 [@CL-CMP-2433; @CL-CMP-2521] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-2433] |
| Applies to | Contract files that the time standards cover, other than files that shall not be closed [@CL-CMP-2521; @CL-CMP-2438] |

The time standards depend on the contract type, and they are subject to the exception for files that shall not be closed [@CL-CMP-2521]. Files for contracts using simplified acquisition procedures should be considered closed when the contracting officer receives evidence of receipt of property and final payment, unless agency regulations say otherwise [@CL-CMP-2521; @CL-CMP-2432]. Files for firm-fixed-price contracts, other than those using simplified acquisition procedures, should be closed within 6 months after the date on which the contracting officer receives evidence of physical completion [@CL-CMP-2521; @CL-CMP-2433]. Files for contracts requiring settlement of indirect cost rates should be closed within 36 months of the month in which that evidence is received [@CL-CMP-2521; @CL-CMP-2434]. Files for all other contracts should be closed within 20 months of the month in which that evidence is received [@CL-CMP-2521; @CL-CMP-2435].

A contract file shall not be closed if the contract is in litigation or under appeal, or if, for a termination, all termination actions have not been completed [@CL-CMP-2438].

## How it works

Three of the four standards start with evidence of physical completion: the firm-fixed-price standard counts from the date, and the other two count from the month [@CL-CMP-2433; @CL-CMP-2434; @CL-CMP-2435]. The simplified acquisition standard starts with evidence of receipt of property and final payment [@CL-CMP-2432]. For the three standards that start from physical completion, the contracting officer shall use the closeout procedures, although those actions may be modified to reflect the extent of administration performed [@CL-CMP-2436]. The procedures are on [Procedures for closing out contract files](cmp-contract-closeout-procedures.md). Quick closeout procedures should be used, when appropriate, to reduce administrative costs and to enable deobligation of excess funds [@CL-CMP-2437]. The quick-closeout procedure and its DoD threshold are on [Quick-closeout procedure](../../indirect-rates/rate-lifecycle/rte-quick-closeout.md) and [DoD quick-closeout threshold for unsettled costs](../../indirect-rates/rate-lifecycle/rte-dod-quick-closeout-threshold.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

Evidence of physical completion arrives in March of year 1 for three contracts. A firm-fixed-price contract of 1,000,000 should be closed within 6 months of that date, so by September of year 1. A contract needing indirect rate settlement should be closed within 36 months of March, so by March of year 4. Any other contract should be closed within 20 months of March, so by November of year 2. If the first contract is under appeal, its file is not closed. Answer: the contract type sets the target, and litigation or an unfinished termination holds the file open.

## Never confuse

These standards are targets that use the word should, while the bar on closing a litigated file or an unfinished termination uses the word shall [@CL-CMP-2433; @CL-CMP-2438]. A closeout target is also not a rate settlement: contracts that require rate settlement have the longest of the month-based standards [@CL-CMP-2434; @CL-CMP-2435; @CL-CMP-2433], and quick closeout is a separate set of procedures for use when appropriate [@CL-CMP-2437], covered on the linked pages.
