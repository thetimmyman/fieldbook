## Purpose

Shows what the contracting officer does at each phase of a classified acquisition, what the solicitation must include, and who approves the security classification specification.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-2410] |
| Codified citation | FAR 4.402 and FAR 4.403 [@CL-CMP-2410; @CL-CMP-2412] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-2410] |
| Applies to | Solicitations and contracts that may require access to classified information [@CL-CMP-2412; @CL-CMP-2414] |

The National Industrial Security Program Operating Manual, at 32 CFR part 117, is one of the listed publications that implement the program [@CL-CMP-2410]. Nondefense agencies that have industrial security services agreements with DoD, and DoD components, shall use the DD Form 254, Contract Security Classification Specification [@CL-CMP-2411]. That form rule is limited to the agencies named, so it is not a blanket rule for every agency [@CL-CMP-2411]. This card does not restate the manual; its ownership and reporting rules are on [NISPOM foreign ownership, control or influence and its mitigation](../../mna/diligence/mna-nispom-foci-and-mitigation.md) and [NISPOM changed conditions and reportable ownership events](../../mna/diligence/mna-nispom-changed-condition-reporting.md).

In the solicitation phase, contracting officers ensure that the classified acquisition is conducted as required by the NISP or agency procedures, and include the items listed [@CL-CMP-2413]. Two listed inclusions are an appropriate Security Requirements clause in the solicitation and, as appropriate, a requirement for security safeguards in addition to those in the clause, in solicitations and contracts where the contract may require access to classified information [@CL-CMP-2414].

## How it works

The duties run in three phases. In the presolicitation phase, the contracting officer reviews every proposed solicitation to decide whether offerors, or a contractor during performance, may need access to classified information [@CL-CMP-2412]. In the award phase, contracting officers inform contractors and subcontractors of the security classifications and requirements assigned to the documents, materials, tasks, subcontracts and components of the classified contract [@CL-CMP-2415]. Where nondefense agencies with industrial security services agreements with DoD, and DoD components, use the DD Form 254, the contracting officer or an authorized agency representative is the approving official for the one associated with the prime contract [@CL-CMP-2524; @CL-CMP-2416]. Finance and accounting roles are not named in the printed text.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A DoD component plans a 3,000,000 services contract, and its contracting officer finds that the contractor may need access to classified information during performance. The solicitation then carries a Security Requirements clause, and the contract carries any extra safeguards the work needs. At award, the contracting officer or an authorized representative approves the classification specification for the prime contract. Answer: the contracting officer decides at each phase, and the manual's own rules stay on the NISPOM pages.

## Never confuse

The contracting officer duties on this card are not the NISPOM rules on a contractor's own eligibility: foreign ownership, control or influence and changed-condition reporting are on the NISPOM pages linked above. They are also not the cyber rules for covered defense information; see [Safeguarding covered defense information](../trade-cyber/cmp-safeguarding-covered-defense-information.md).
