## Purpose

Shows what the contracting officer does at each phase of a classified acquisition, what the solicitation must include, and who approves the security classification specification.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-2410] |
| Codified citation | FAR 4.402 and FAR 4.403 [@CL-CMP-2410; @CL-CMP-2412] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-2410] |
| Applies to | Solicitations and contracts that may require access to classified information [@CL-CMP-2412; @CL-CMP-2414] |

The National Industrial Security Program Operating Manual, at 32 CFR part 117, is one of the listed publications that implement the program [@CL-CMP-2410]. Nondefense agencies that have industrial security services agreements with DoD, and DoD components, shall use the DD Form 254, Contract Security Classification Specification, to provide security classification guidance to United States contractors, and subcontractors as applicable, requiring access to information classified as Confidential, Secret, or Top Secret [@CL-CMP-2411; @CL-CMP-8205]. For the manual's ownership and reporting rules, see [NISPOM foreign ownership, control or influence and its mitigation](../../mna/diligence/mna-nispom-foci-and-mitigation.md) and [NISPOM changed conditions and reportable ownership events](../../mna/diligence/mna-nispom-changed-condition-reporting.md).

In the solicitation phase, contracting officers shall ensure that the classified acquisition is conducted as required by the NISP or agency procedures, as appropriate, and shall include the items listed [@CL-CMP-2413]. Two listed inclusions are an appropriate Security Requirements clause in the solicitation (see FAR 4.404) and, as appropriate, in solicitations and contracts when the contract may require access to classified information, a requirement for security safeguards in addition to those provided in the clause (52.204-2, Security Requirements) [@CL-CMP-2414].

## How it works

In the presolicitation phase, contracting officers shall review all proposed solicitations to determine whether access to classified information may be required by offerors, or by a contractor during contract performance [@CL-CMP-2412]. In the award phase, contracting officers shall inform contractors and subcontractors of the security classifications and requirements assigned to the various documents, materials, tasks, subcontracts, and components of the classified contract as follows [@CL-CMP-2415]: nondefense agencies that have industrial security services agreements with DoD, and DoD components, shall use the Contract Security Classification Specification, DD Form 254, to provide classification guidance to contractors and subcontractors as identified in the requirement documentation [@CL-CMP-2524], where the contracting officer, or authorized agency representative, is the approving official for the DD Form 254 associated with the prime contract [@CL-CMP-2416] and shall ensure the DD Form 254 is properly prepared, distributed by, and coordinated with requirements and security personnel in accordance with agency procedures (see FAR 4.402(d)(1)) [@CL-CMP-0191]; and contracting officers in agencies not covered by the NISP shall follow agency procedures [@CL-CMP-0192].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A DoD component plans a 3,000,000 services contract, and its contracting officer finds that the contractor may need access to classified information during performance. The solicitation then carries a Security Requirements clause, and the contract carries any extra safeguards the work needs. At award, the contracting officer or an authorized representative approves the classification specification for the prime contract. Answer: the contracting officer decides at each phase, and the manual's own rules stay on the NISPOM pages.

## Never confuse

In the presolicitation, solicitation, and award phases, contracting officers review proposed solicitations, include an appropriate Security Requirements clause and, as appropriate, in solicitations and contracts when the contract may require access to classified information, a requirement for security safeguards in addition to those provided in the clause, and inform contractors and subcontractors of assigned security classifications and requirements [@CL-CMP-2412; @CL-CMP-2413; @CL-CMP-2414; @CL-CMP-2415]. For foreign ownership, control or influence, changed-condition reporting, and safeguarding covered defense information, see [NISPOM foreign ownership, control or influence and its mitigation](../../mna/diligence/mna-nispom-foci-and-mitigation.md), [NISPOM changed conditions and reportable ownership events](../../mna/diligence/mna-nispom-changed-condition-reporting.md), and [Safeguarding covered defense information](../trade-cyber/cmp-safeguarding-covered-defense-information.md).
