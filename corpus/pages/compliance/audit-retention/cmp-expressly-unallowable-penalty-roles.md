## Purpose

Sets out who does what when penalties on expressly unallowable costs are considered: the contracting officer decides, and the contract auditor recommends costs and supplies the support.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-1726] |
| Codified citation | FAR 42.709-3 [@CL-CMP-1726] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 42.508-3 [@CL-CMP-0063; @CL-CMP-0064; @CL-CMP-0065] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-1726] |
| Applies to | Final indirect cost proposals for contracts subject to the penalty section [@CL-CMP-1727] |

The FAR makes the cognizant contracting officer responsible for determining whether the penalties in FAR 42.709-2(a) should be assessed and whether they should be waived pursuant to FAR 42.709-6 [@CL-CMP-1726]. The audit manual says the administrative contracting officer determines whether a penalty should be assessed and issues a demand letter for the amount [@CL-CMP-1723].

In the review or determination of final indirect cost proposals for contracts subject to the section, the contract auditor is responsible for recommending to the contracting officer which costs may be unallowable and subject to the penalties in FAR 42.709-2(a) [@CL-CMP-1727]. The audit manual adds that the auditor is responsible for communicating all unallowable costs potentially subject to penalties to the administrative contracting officer regardless of materiality, and has no authority to recommend or impose the penalty, recover it against subsequent public vouchers, or waive it [@CL-CMP-1723; @CL-CMP-1724].

## How it works

A cost is expressly unallowable if the applicable cost principle states in direct terms that the cost is unallowable, leaving little room for interpretation [@CL-CMP-1725]. The auditor's responsibilities in the FAR also include providing rationale and supporting documentation for any recommendation [@CL-CMP-1856]. As written, the FAR has the auditor recommend which costs may be unallowable and subject to the penalties [@CL-CMP-1727], while the manual separately denies the auditor authority to recommend or impose the penalty itself [@CL-CMP-1724]. The decision to assess or waive stays with the contracting officer [@CL-CMP-1726; @CL-CMP-1724]. Each of them is responsible for referring the matter to the appropriate criminal investigative organization if there is evidence that the contractor knowingly submitted unallowable costs, the contracting officer under the first list and the auditor under the second [@CL-CMP-1855; @CL-CMP-1856].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 42 text in place of the codified text under Class Deviation 2026-O0050, effective March 16, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9759] [@CL-CMP-9761]. RFO FAR 42.508-3(a) keeps the cognizant contracting officer's responsibility for determining whether the penalties in 42.508-2(a) should be assessed and whether they should be waived pursuant to 42.508-6 [@CL-CMP-0063; @CL-CMP-1726]. RFO paragraph (b) makes the contract auditor responsible for reviews and/or determinations of final indirect cost proposals for contracts subject to the section, identifying costs that may be subject to penalty, and providing rationale and supporting documentation for any recommendations, where the codified paragraph has the auditor recommend to the contracting officer which costs may be unallowable and subject to the penalties [@CL-CMP-0064; @CL-CMP-1727; @CL-CMP-1856]. RFO paragraph (c) puts the duty to refer matters to the appropriate criminal investigative organization, if there is evidence that the contractor knowingly submitted unallowable costs, on contracting officers and contract auditors together, where the codified section states it in separate paragraphs for each [@CL-CMP-0065; @CL-CMP-1855; @CL-CMP-1856].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor includes 40,000 of entertainment expenses in an indirect overhead rate proposal. The contract auditor identifies the costs as expressly unallowable under the cost principles and flags them in the audit report, communicating the full amount to the administrative contracting officer with the supporting rationale. The auditor does not decide the penalty. The administrative contracting officer decides whether to assess it and issues the demand letter, and also decides any waiver.

## Never confuse

Recommending which costs may be unallowable and subject to the penalties is a different act from deciding to assess or waive the penalty. The FAR gives the first to the contract auditor [@CL-CMP-1727] and the second to the cognizant contracting officer [@CL-CMP-1726], and the manual denies the auditor authority to impose or waive the penalty [@CL-CMP-1724]. For the scope of the penalty see [Penalties for unallowable costs in final rate proposals](../cas-allowability/cmp-unallowable-cost-penalty.md), and for assessment and waiver see [Penalties on expressly unallowable costs: assessment, interest and waiver](cmp-expressly-unallowable-penalty-assessment-and-waiver.md). See also [Contract administration functions](cmp-contract-administration-functions.md).

The codified paragraph has the auditor recommend to the contracting officer which costs may be unallowable and subject to the penalties [@CL-CMP-1727], while the RFO paragraph has the auditor identify costs that may be subject to penalty [@CL-CMP-0064]; in both texts the cognizant contracting officer determines whether to assess or waive the penalties [@CL-CMP-1726; @CL-CMP-0063].
