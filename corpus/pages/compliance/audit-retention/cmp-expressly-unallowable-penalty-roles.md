## Purpose

Sets out who does what when penalties on expressly unallowable costs are considered: the contracting officer decides, and the contract auditor recommends costs and supplies the support.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-1726] |
| Codified citation | FAR 42.709-3 [@CL-CMP-1726] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 42.508-3 [@CL-CMP-0063; @CL-CMP-0064; @CL-CMP-0065] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-1726] |
| Applies to | Final indirect cost proposals for contracts subject to the penalty section [@CL-CMP-1727] |

The cognizant contracting officer is responsible for determining whether the penalties in FAR 42.709-2(a) should be assessed and determining whether such penalties should be waived pursuant to FAR 42.709-6 [@CL-CMP-1726]. The Administrative Contracting Officer (ACO) determines whether a penalty should be assessed and issues a demand letter for the amount [@CL-CMP-1723].

The contract auditor, in the review and/or the determination of final indirect cost proposals for contracts subject to the section, is responsible for recommending to the contracting officer which costs may be unallowable and subject to the penalties in FAR 42.709-2(a) [@CL-CMP-1727]. The auditor is responsible for communicating all unallowable costs potentially subject to penalties to the ACO, regardless of materiality, and has no authority to recommend or impose the penalty, recover it against subsequent public vouchers, or waive the penalty; this authority rests with the ACO [@CL-CMP-1723; @CL-CMP-1724].

## How it works

By definition, a cost is expressly unallowable if the applicable cost principle states in direct terms that the cost is unallowable, leaving little room for interpretation [@CL-CMP-1725]. Under FAR 42.709-3(b), the contract auditor is responsible for recommending to the contracting officer which costs may be unallowable and subject to the penalties in FAR 42.709-2(a) [@CL-CMP-1727], providing rationale and supporting documentation for any recommendation [@CL-CMP-1856], and referring the matter to the appropriate criminal investigative organization for review and for appropriate coordination of remedies, if there is evidence that the contractor knowingly submitted unallowable costs [@CL-CMP-1856]. Under FAR 42.709-3(a), the cognizant contracting officer is responsible for determining whether the penalties in FAR 42.709-2(a) should be assessed, determining whether such penalties should be waived pursuant to FAR 42.709-6 [@CL-CMP-1726], and referring the matter to the appropriate criminal investigative organization for review and for appropriate coordination of remedies, if there is evidence that the contractor knowingly submitted unallowable costs [@CL-CMP-1855]. The auditor has no authority to recommend or impose the penalty, recover it against subsequent public vouchers, or waive the penalty; this authority rests with the ACO [@CL-CMP-1724].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 42 text in place of the codified text under Class Deviation 2026-O0050, effective March 16, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9759] [@CL-CMP-9761]. RFO FAR 42.508-3(a) states that the cognizant contracting officer is responsible for determining whether the penalties in 42.508-2(a) should be assessed and determining if such penalties should be waived pursuant to 42.508-6 [@CL-CMP-0063]. RFO paragraph (b) states that the contract auditor is responsible for reviews and/or making determinations of final indirect cost proposals for contracts subject to the section, identifying costs that may be subject to penalty, and providing rationale and supporting documentation for any recommendations [@CL-CMP-0064]. RFO paragraph (c) states that both contracting officers and contract auditors have a duty to refer matters to the appropriate criminal investigative organization for review and coordination of remedies if there is evidence that the contractor knowingly submitted unallowable costs [@CL-CMP-0065].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor includes 40,000 of entertainment expenses in an indirect overhead rate proposal. The contract auditor identifies the costs as expressly unallowable under the cost principles and flags them in the audit report, communicating the full amount to the administrative contracting officer with the supporting rationale. The auditor does not decide the penalty. The administrative contracting officer decides whether to assess it and issues the demand letter, and also decides any waiver.

## Never confuse

In the review and/or the determination of final indirect cost proposals for contracts subject to the section, the contract auditor is responsible for recommending to the contracting officer which costs may be unallowable and subject to the penalties in FAR 42.709-2(a) [@CL-CMP-1727], whereas the cognizant contracting officer is responsible for determining whether the penalties in FAR 42.709-2(a) should be assessed and whether such penalties should be waived pursuant to FAR 42.709-6 [@CL-CMP-1726], and the auditor has no authority to recommend or impose the penalty, recover it against subsequent public vouchers, or waive the penalty, as that authority rests with the ACO [@CL-CMP-1724]. For the scope of the penalty see [Penalties for unallowable costs in final rate proposals](../cas-allowability/cmp-unallowable-cost-penalty.md), and for assessment and waiver see [Penalties on expressly unallowable costs: assessment, interest and waiver](cmp-expressly-unallowable-penalty-assessment-and-waiver.md). See also [Contract administration functions](cmp-contract-administration-functions.md).

Under codified FAR 42.709-3(b), the contract auditor is responsible for recommending to the contracting officer which costs may be unallowable and subject to the penalties in FAR 42.709-2(a) [@CL-CMP-1727], while under RFO FAR 42.508-3(b), the contract auditor is responsible for reviews and/or making determinations of final indirect cost proposals for contracts subject to the section, identifying costs that may be subject to penalty, and providing rationale and supporting documentation for any recommendations [@CL-CMP-0064]; under both texts, the cognizant contracting officer is responsible for determining whether the penalties should be assessed and determining whether they should be waived [@CL-CMP-1726; @CL-CMP-0063].
