## Purpose

Sets out when the contracting officer assesses the penalty on unallowable costs, how the penalty amount and interest are worked out, and when the penalty must be waived.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-1728] |
| Codified citation | FAR 42.709-4 [@CL-CMP-1728] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-1728] |
| Applies to | Proposals with a submitted cost that is expressly unallowable under a cost principle, or that was determined unallowable for the contractor before the proposal was submitted [@CL-CMP-1728; @CL-CMP-1729] |

Unless a waiver is granted pursuant to FAR 42.709-6, the cognizant contracting officer shall take each action listed in FAR 42.709-4 [@CL-CMP-1857]. The actions are assessing the penalty in FAR 42.709-2(a)(1) when the submitted cost is expressly unallowable under a cost principle in the FAR or an executive agency supplement that defines the allowability of specific selected costs [@CL-CMP-1728], assessing the penalty in FAR 42.709-2(a)(2) when the submitted cost was determined to be unallowable for that contractor before the proposal was submitted [@CL-CMP-1729], and issuing a final decision that includes a demand for payment and states that the determination is a final decision under the Disputes clause [@CL-CMP-1730]. Which contracts are covered is on [Penalties for unallowable costs in final rate proposals](../cas-allowability/cmp-unallowable-cost-penalty.md).

The first penalty equals the disallowed costs allocated to covered contracts, plus interest on the paid portion, if any, of the disallowance [@CL-CMP-1859]. The second penalty is two times the amount of the disallowed costs allocated to covered contracts [@CL-CMP-1860]. The contracting officer shall waive the penalties when the contractor withdraws the proposal before the Government formally initiates an audit of the proposal and submits a revised proposal [@CL-CMP-1733], when the unallowable costs subject to the penalty allocated to the covered contracts are 10,000 dollars or less [@CL-CMP-1734], or when the contractor demonstrates to the contracting officer's satisfaction both that it has established policies, training and an internal control and review system and that the costs were inadvertently incorporated into the proposal [@CL-CMP-1864; @CL-CMP-1863; @CL-CMP-1735].

## How it works

The contracting officer assesses the penalty that fits the cost: the first for a cost that is expressly unallowable, the second for a cost already determined unallowable for that contractor [@CL-CMP-1728; @CL-CMP-1729]. Interest on the paid portion of the disallowed cost is computed under FAR 42.709-5 [@CL-CMP-1861]. The overpayment is considered to have occurred, and interest to have begun accumulating, from the midpoint of the contractor's fiscal year, with an alternate equitable method if the cost was not paid evenly over the year [@CL-CMP-1731]. Interest runs to the date of the demand letter for payment of the penalty [@CL-CMP-1732]. Demanding payment of the penalty is separate from demanding repayment of any paid portion of the disallowed cost [@CL-CMP-1858]. For the withdrawal ground, an audit is deemed formally initiated when the Government gives the contractor written notice, or holds an entrance conference, indicating that audit work on a specific final indirect cost proposal has begun [@CL-CMP-1862].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor includes 8,000 of expressly unallowable costs in an indirect proposal, all allocated to covered contracts. Because the amount subject to the penalty is 10,000 or less, the contracting officer waives the penalty. In a second case, 50,000 of expressly unallowable costs is allocated to covered contracts and part of it was paid during the year. The first penalty is 50,000 plus interest on the paid part, with interest running from the midpoint of the fiscal year to the date of the demand letter. In a third case, a cost the government had already determined unallowable for the contractor is allocated at 20,000, so the second penalty is 40,000.

## Never confuse

The demand for the penalty is not the demand for repayment of the disallowed cost: demanding payment of the penalty is separate from demanding repayment of any paid portion of the disallowed cost [@CL-CMP-1858]. The first penalty and the second differ by trigger and amount: the first covers an expressly unallowable cost and the second a cost already determined unallowable for the contractor, at two times the allocated amount [@CL-CMP-1728; @CL-CMP-1729; @CL-CMP-1860]. For who recommends and who decides, see [Penalties on expressly unallowable costs: auditor and contracting officer roles](cmp-expressly-unallowable-penalty-roles.md).
