## Purpose

Sets out when the contracting officer assesses the penalty on unallowable costs, how the penalty amount and interest are worked out, and when the penalty must be waived.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-1728] |
| Codified citation | FAR 42.709-4 [@CL-CMP-1728] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 42.508-4 [@CL-CMP-0047; @CL-CMP-0048; @CL-CMP-0049; @CL-CMP-0050] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-1728] |
| Applies to | Proposals with a submitted cost that is expressly unallowable under a cost principle, or that was determined unallowable for the contractor before the proposal was submitted [@CL-CMP-1728; @CL-CMP-1729] |

Unless a waiver is granted pursuant to FAR 42.709-6, the cognizant contracting officer shall take the actions listed in FAR 42.709-4 [@CL-CMP-1857]. The first two are alternatives: assessing the penalty in FAR 42.709-2(a)(1) when the submitted cost is expressly unallowable under a cost principle in the FAR or an executive agency supplement that defines the allowability of specific selected costs [@CL-CMP-1728], or assessing the penalty in FAR 42.709-2(a)(2) when the submitted cost was determined to be unallowable for that contractor before the proposal was submitted [@CL-CMP-1729]. The third action is issuing a final decision that includes a demand for payment of any penalty assessed under paragraph (a) or (b) and states that the determination is a final decision under the Disputes clause [@CL-CMP-1730]. Which contracts are covered is on [Penalties for unallowable costs in final rate proposals](../cas-allowability/cmp-unallowable-cost-penalty.md).

The first penalty equals the disallowed costs allocated to contracts that are subject to the section for which an indirect cost proposal has been submitted, plus interest on the paid portion, if any, of the disallowance [@CL-CMP-1859]. The second penalty is two times the amount in paragraph (a)(1)(i), that is, two times those disallowed costs [@CL-CMP-1860; @CL-CMP-1859]. The contracting officer shall waive the penalties when the contractor withdraws the proposal before the Government formally initiates an audit of the proposal and submits a revised proposal [@CL-CMP-1733], when the unallowable costs subject to the penalty allocated to the covered contracts are 10,000 dollars or less [@CL-CMP-1734], or when the contractor demonstrates to the contracting officer's satisfaction both that it has established policies, training and an internal control and review system and that the costs were inadvertently incorporated into the proposal [@CL-CMP-1864; @CL-CMP-1863; @CL-CMP-1735].

## How it works

The contracting officer assesses the penalty that fits the cost: the first for a cost that is expressly unallowable, the second for a cost already determined unallowable for that contractor [@CL-CMP-1728; @CL-CMP-1729]. Interest on the paid portion of the disallowed cost is computed under FAR 42.709-5 [@CL-CMP-1861]. The overpayment is considered to have occurred, and interest to have begun accumulating, from the midpoint of the contractor's fiscal year, with an alternate equitable method if the cost was not paid evenly over the year [@CL-CMP-1731]. Interest runs to the date of the demand letter for payment of the penalty [@CL-CMP-1732]. Demanding payment of the penalty is separate from demanding repayment of any paid portion of the disallowed cost [@CL-CMP-1858]. For the withdrawal ground, an audit is deemed formally initiated when the Government gives the contractor written notice, or holds an entrance conference, indicating that audit work on a specific final indirect cost proposal has begun [@CL-CMP-1862].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 42 text in place of the codified text under Class Deviation 2026-O0050, effective March 16, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9759] [@CL-CMP-9761]. RFO FAR 42.508-4 keeps the rule that, unless a waiver is granted pursuant to 42.508-6, the cognizant contracting officer must take the actions listed [@CL-CMP-0047; @CL-CMP-1857]: assess the penalty in 42.508-2(a)(1) when the submitted cost is expressly unallowable under a cost principle in the FAR or an executive agency supplement that defines the allowability of specific selected costs, or assess the penalty in 42.508-2(a)(2) when the submitted cost was determined to be unallowable for that contractor prior to submission of the proposal [@CL-CMP-0048; @CL-CMP-1728; @CL-CMP-0049; @CL-CMP-1729], and issue a final decision (see part 33, where the codified paragraph says 33.211) that includes a demand for payment and states that the determination is a final decision under the Disputes clause [@CL-CMP-0050; @CL-CMP-1730]. The demand for payment of the penalty stays separate from a demand for repayment of any paid portion of the disallowed cost [@CL-CMP-0051; @CL-CMP-1858]. The penalty amounts are the same under RFO FAR 42.508-2(a): where the indirect cost is expressly unallowable under a cost principle in the FAR or an executive agency supplement that defines the allowability of specific selected costs [@CL-CMP-0081], the penalty equals the disallowed costs allocated to contracts that are subject to the section for which an indirect cost proposal has been submitted, plus interest on the paid portion, if any [@CL-CMP-0052; @CL-CMP-1859], and where the indirect cost was determined to be unallowable for that contractor before proposal submission, the penalty is two times the amount in paragraph (a)(1)(i), without the interest [@CL-CMP-0053; @CL-CMP-1860]. RFO FAR 42.508-5 keeps the interest rules that the overpayment is considered to have occurred from the midpoint of the contractor's fiscal year, with an alternate equitable method if the cost was not paid evenly over the year [@CL-CMP-0054; @CL-CMP-1731], and that interest runs to the date of the demand letter for payment of the penalty [@CL-CMP-0055; @CL-CMP-1732]. RFO FAR 42.508-6 states that the cognizant contracting officer must waive the penalties at 42.508-2(a) when the circumstances described in the paragraphs that follow exist [@CL-CMP-0056], and words the grounds differently. For withdrawal, the RFO paragraph describes formal audit initiation in a parenthetical as written notice to the contractor or an entrance conference with the contractor indicating that audit work on a specific final indirect cost proposal has begun [@CL-CMP-0057; @CL-CMP-1862], and it cites two statutory provisions for the revised proposal [@CL-CMP-0058; @CL-CMP-1733]. RFO paragraph (b) states the $10,000 ground as the unallowable costs subject to the penalty being $10,000 or less, one ground in a list joined by "or" [@CL-CMP-0059; @CL-CMP-1734], where the codified paragraph measures it by the expressly or previously determined unallowable costs that would be allocated to the covered contracts [@CL-CMP-1734]. For the demonstration ground, the RFO paragraph keeps the showing to the cognizant contracting officer's satisfaction [@CL-CMP-0060; @CL-CMP-1864] that the contractor has established policies and personnel training and an internal control and review system that provide assurance that unallowable costs subject to penalties are precluded from being included in its final indirect cost rate proposals, without the examples of controls that the codified paragraph gives [@CL-CMP-0061; @CL-CMP-1863]. It also requires that the costs were inadvertently included into the proposal despite due care, where the codified paragraph defines inadvertent as resulting from an unintentional error notwithstanding the exercise of due care [@CL-CMP-0062; @CL-CMP-1735].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor includes 8,000 of expressly unallowable costs in an indirect proposal, all allocated to covered contracts. Because the amount subject to the penalty is 10,000 or less, the contracting officer waives the penalty. In a second case, 50,000 of expressly unallowable costs is allocated to covered contracts and part of it was paid during the year. The first penalty is 50,000 plus interest on the paid part, with interest running from the midpoint of the fiscal year to the date of the demand letter. In a third case, a cost the government had already determined unallowable for the contractor is allocated at 20,000, so the second penalty is 40,000.

## Never confuse

The demand for the penalty is not the demand for repayment of the disallowed cost: demanding payment of the penalty is separate from demanding repayment of any paid portion of the disallowed cost [@CL-CMP-1858]. The first penalty and the second differ by trigger and amount: the first covers an expressly unallowable cost and the second a cost already determined unallowable for the contractor, at two times the allocated amount [@CL-CMP-1728; @CL-CMP-1729; @CL-CMP-1860]. For who recommends and who decides, see [Penalties on expressly unallowable costs: auditor and contracting officer roles](cmp-expressly-unallowable-penalty-roles.md).

The codified waiver for inadvertent inclusion defines it as an unintentional error notwithstanding the exercise of due care [@CL-CMP-1735], while the RFO waiver says the costs were inadvertently included into the proposal despite due care [@CL-CMP-0062].
