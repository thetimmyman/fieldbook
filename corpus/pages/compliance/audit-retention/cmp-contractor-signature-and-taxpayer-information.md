## Purpose

Shows who signs a contract for each kind of contractor, and how the contractor's taxpayer identification information reaches the payment office.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-2400] |
| Codified citation | FAR 4.102 and FAR 4.203 [@CL-CMP-2400; @CL-CMP-2409] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-2400] |
| Applies to | Contracts with individuals, partnerships, corporations, joint venturers and agents, and the contractor's TIN and type of organization sent to the payment office [@CL-CMP-2400; @CL-CMP-2401; @CL-CMP-2402; @CL-CMP-2403; @CL-CMP-2404; @CL-CMP-2408] |

A contract with an individual shall be signed by that individual [@CL-CMP-2400]. A partnership signs in the partnership name, and before signing for the Government the contracting officer obtains a list of all partners and ensures that the signers have authority to bind the partnership [@CL-CMP-2401]. A corporation signs in the corporate name, followed by the word "by" and the signature and title of the person authorized to sign, and the contracting officer ensures that person has authority to bind the corporation [@CL-CMP-2402]. A contract with joint venturers is signed by each participant in the manner prescribed for its type [@CL-CMP-2403], and when a corporation is participating the contracting officer verifies that it is authorized to participate in the joint venture [@CL-CMP-2523]. When an agent signs, other than in those four cases, the agent's authority to bind the principal must be established by evidence satisfactory to the contracting officer [@CL-CMP-2404].

The taxpayer information in the source is the contractor's TIN and type of organization [@CL-CMP-2408]. Where the contractor furnished a TIN on the solicitation provision at 52.204-3, or on paragraph (l) of the provision at 52.212-3, the contracting officer, unless agency procedures say otherwise, attaches a copy of the completed provision as the last page of the contract copy sent to the payment office [@CL-CMP-2405; @CL-CMP-2406].

## How it works

Three paragraphs of the source give the general routes by which the payment office gets the information, and the source has further paragraphs for Federal Supply Schedule orders and for basic ordering agreements and indefinite-delivery contracts that this card does not cover. Where the provision was completed, the completed provision travels as the last page of the contract copy [@CL-CMP-2405; @CL-CMP-2406]. Where the TIN or type of organization comes from another source, the contracting officer annotates the last page of the contract or order, unless the information reaches the payment office under agency procedures [@CL-CMP-2407; @CL-CMP-2408]. Where the contractor gives the information after award, the contracting officer forwards it within 7 days of receipt [@CL-CMP-2409].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A two-person partnership signs a 600,000 supply contract. Before signing for the Government, the contracting officer obtains the list of both partners and checks that the signers can bind the partnership. The partnership sends its TIN 3 days after award, and the contracting officer forwards it to the payment office within 7 days of receipt, so by day 10 at the latest. Answer: the contract is signed in the partnership name, the partner list is on file, and the TIN reaches the payment office by day 10.

## Never confuse

Signing in the correct form is not the same as having authority to bind: the text has the contracting officer ensure authority for partnership and corporate signers, and requires satisfactory evidence for an agent [@CL-CMP-2401; @CL-CMP-2402; @CL-CMP-2404]. Passing the TIN to the payment office is also not payment itself; see [Making payments](../../contract-finance/billing-payment/ctf-making-payments.md) for how payments are made.
