## Purpose

Lists the contract administration functions that a contracting officer normally delegates to a contract administration office, which of them the contracting officer may retain, and which the office performs only when authorized.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-1006] |
| Codified citation | FAR 42.302 [@CL-CMP-1006] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 42.302 [@CL-CMP-0026; @CL-CMP-0027] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-1006] |
| Applies to | Not established. |

The contracting officer normally delegates the contract administration functions listed in paragraph (a) to a contract administration office [@CL-CMP-1006]. The contracting officer may retain any of these functions, except those in paragraphs (a)(5), (a)(9), (a)(11), and (a)(12), unless the cognizant Federal agency (see FAR 42.003) has designated the contracting officer to perform these functions [@CL-CMP-1007]. Those four are negotiating forward pricing rate agreements (see FAR 15.407-3) [@CL-CMP-1008], establishing final indirect cost rates and billing rates for those contractors meeting the criteria for contracting officer determination in FAR subpart 42.7 [@CL-CMP-1009], the Cost Accounting Standards functions in FAR 42.302(a)(11) [@CL-CMP-1436; @CL-CMP-1437], and determining the adequacy of the contractor's accounting system because the contractor's accounting system should be adequate during the entire period of contract performance [@CL-CMP-1010].

Other functions in paragraph (a) include reviewing and approving or disapproving the contractor's requests for payments under the progress payments or performance-based payments clauses [@CL-CMP-1438]; determining the allowability of costs suspended or disapproved as required (see FAR subpart 42.8), directing the suspension or disapproval of costs when there is reason to believe they should be suspended or disapproved, and approving final vouchers [@CL-CMP-1439]; making payments on assigned contracts as prescribed in agency acquisition regulations [@CL-CMP-1440]; and reviewing, approving or disapproving, and maintaining surveillance of the contractor's purchasing system (see FAR part 44) [@CL-CMP-1441]. The contract administration office shall perform the functions listed in FAR 42.302(b) only when and to the extent specifically authorized by the contracting office [@CL-CMP-1442]. Any additional contract administration functions not listed in FAR 42.302(a) and (b), or not otherwise delegated, remain the responsibility of the contracting office [@CL-CMP-1011].

## How it works

The contracting officer normally delegates the functions in paragraph (a) to a contract administration office [@CL-CMP-1006]. In connection with Cost Accounting Standards (see FAR 30.601 and 48 CFR chapter 99), the functions in paragraph (a)(11) are determining the adequacy of the contractor's disclosure statements, determining whether disclosure statements are in compliance with Cost Accounting Standards and FAR part 31 [@CL-CMP-1436], determining the contractor's compliance with Cost Accounting Standards and disclosure statements, if applicable, and negotiating price adjustments and executing supplemental agreements under the Cost Accounting Standards clauses at 48 CFR 52.230-2, 52.230-3, 52.230-4, 52.230-5, and 52.230-6 [@CL-CMP-1437]. Under paragraph (b), the contract administration office shall perform the listed functions only when and to the extent specifically authorized by the contracting office [@CL-CMP-1442]: negotiating or negotiating and executing supplemental agreements incorporating contractor proposals resulting from change orders issued under the Changes clause, and before completing negotiations, coordinating any delivery schedule change with the contracting office [@CL-CMP-1443; @CL-CMP-0196]; negotiating or negotiating and executing supplemental agreements changing contract delivery schedules [@CL-CMP-1444]; and negotiating changes to interim billing prices [@CL-CMP-1445].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 42 text in place of the codified text under Class Deviation 2026-O0050, effective March 16, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9759] [@CL-CMP-9761]. RFO FAR 42.302(a) states that the contracting officer normally delegates the listed contract administration functions to a contract administration office [@CL-CMP-0026], and that the contracting officer may retain any of these functions, except those in paragraphs (a)(5), (a)(9), (a)(11), and (a)(12), which must be performed by the cognizant Federal agency (see 42.002) [@CL-CMP-0027]. Those four are negotiating forward pricing rate agreements (see part 15 and subpart 42.13) [@CL-CMP-0028]; establishing final indirect cost rates and billing rates for those contractors meeting the criteria for contracting officer determination in subpart 42.5 [@CL-CMP-0029]; in connection with Cost Accounting Standards (see part 30 and 48 CFR chapter 99), determining the adequacy of the contractor's disclosure statements, determining whether disclosure statements are in compliance with Cost Accounting Standards and part 31 [@CL-CMP-0030], determining the contractor's compliance with Cost Accounting Standards and disclosure statements, if applicable, and negotiating price adjustments and executing supplemental agreements under the Cost Accounting Standards clauses at 52.230-2, 52.230-3, 52.230-4, 52.230-5, and 52.230-6 [@CL-CMP-0031]; and determining the adequacy of the contractor's accounting system because the contractor's accounting system should be adequate during the entire period of contract performance [@CL-CMP-0032]. RFO paragraph (a)(13) lists reviewing and approving or disapproving the contractor's requests for financing payments (see part 32) [@CL-CMP-0033]. RFO paragraph (b) states that the contract administration office may perform the listed functions only when and to the extent specifically authorized by the contracting office [@CL-CMP-0034], including negotiating and executing supplemental agreements incorporating contractor proposals resulting from change orders issued under the Changes clause (before completing negotiations, coordinating any delivery schedule change with the contracting office) [@CL-CMP-0080; @CL-CMP-0197] and negotiating and executing supplemental agreements changing contract delivery schedules [@CL-CMP-0035]. RFO paragraph (c) states that any additional contract administration functions not listed in 42.302(a) and (b), or not otherwise delegated, remain the responsibility of the contracting office unless in accordance with 42.202 Assignment of contract administration(c) [@CL-CMP-0036].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contracting office awards a 10,000,000 fixed-price development contract with progress payments and delegates contract administration to a contract administration office. The delegated office negotiates a forward pricing rate agreement, determines whether the contractor's accounting system is adequate, and reviews and approves the contractor's progress payment requests. The contracting office has not authorized the delegated office to change delivery schedules, so a proposed schedule change goes back to the contracting office.

## Never confuse

The functions in paragraph (a) are normally delegated to a contract administration office [@CL-CMP-1006], while those in paragraph (b) shall be performed by the contract administration office only when and to the extent specifically authorized by the contracting office [@CL-CMP-1442]. Any additional contract administration functions not listed in FAR 42.302(a) and (b), or not otherwise delegated, remain the responsibility of the contracting office [@CL-CMP-1011]. For the roles around contract administration, see [Oversight roles](cmp-oversight-roles.md) and [Audit and records negotiation](cmp-audit-and-records-negotiation.md).

Under the codified text, the contracting officer may retain any of the functions in paragraph (a), except those in paragraphs (a)(5), (a)(9), (a)(11), and (a)(12), unless the cognizant Federal agency (see FAR 42.003) has designated the contracting officer to perform these functions [@CL-CMP-1007], while under the RFO text, those four must be performed by the cognizant Federal agency (see 42.002) [@CL-CMP-0027].

## Company calibration

Identifying, for each contract, which contract administration functions were delegated, which were authorized only when needed, and which stayed with the contracting office. [@Q-004]
