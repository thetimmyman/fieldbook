## Purpose

Lists the contract administration functions that a contracting officer normally delegates to a contract administration office, which of them the contracting officer may retain, and which the office performs only when authorized.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-1006] |
| Codified citation | FAR 42.302 [@CL-CMP-1006] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 42.302 [@CL-CMP-0026; @CL-CMP-0027] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-1006] |
| Applies to | Not established. |

The contracting officer normally delegates the contract administration functions listed in paragraph (a) to a contract administration office [@CL-CMP-1006]. The contracting officer may retain any of these functions, except those in paragraphs (a)(5), (a)(9), (a)(11), and (a)(12), unless the cognizant Federal agency has designated the contracting officer to perform them [@CL-CMP-1007]. Those four are negotiating forward pricing rate agreements [@CL-CMP-1008], establishing final indirect cost rates and billing rates for contractors meeting the criteria for contracting officer determination [@CL-CMP-1009], the Cost Accounting Standards functions [@CL-CMP-1436; @CL-CMP-1437], and determining the adequacy of the contractor's accounting system, which should be adequate during the entire period of contract performance [@CL-CMP-1010].

Other listed functions touch payments and business systems: reviewing and approving or disapproving requests for payments under the progress payments or performance-based payments clauses [@CL-CMP-1438], determining the allowability of suspended or disapproved costs and approving final vouchers [@CL-CMP-1439], making payments on assigned contracts when agency regulations prescribe it [@CL-CMP-1440], and reviewing, approving or disapproving, and maintaining surveillance of the contractor's purchasing system [@CL-CMP-1441]. The office performs the functions listed in paragraph (b) only when and to the extent specifically authorized by the contracting office [@CL-CMP-1442]. Any additional functions not listed in paragraphs (a) and (b), or not otherwise delegated, remain the responsibility of the contracting office [@CL-CMP-1011].

## How it works

The contracting officer delegates the functions to the contract administration office, and normally delegates those in paragraph (a) [@CL-CMP-1006]. The Cost Accounting Standards functions are determining the adequacy of the contractor's disclosure statements, determining whether disclosure statements comply with Cost Accounting Standards and part 31 [@CL-CMP-1436], determining the contractor's compliance, if applicable, and negotiating price adjustments and executing supplemental agreements under the Cost Accounting Standards clauses [@CL-CMP-1437]. Functions in paragraph (b), such as supplemental agreements for change orders and for delivery schedule changes, wait for specific authorization from the contracting office [@CL-CMP-1442; @CL-CMP-1443; @CL-CMP-1444]. Negotiating changes to interim billing prices is also in paragraph (b) [@CL-CMP-1445].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 42 text in place of the codified text under Class Deviation 2026-O0050, effective March 16, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9759] [@CL-CMP-9761]. RFO FAR 42.302(a) keeps the rule that the contracting officer normally delegates the listed contract administration functions to a contract administration office [@CL-CMP-0026; @CL-CMP-1006]. It changes who may retain four of them: the contracting officer may retain any listed function except those in paragraphs (a)(5), (a)(9), (a)(11) and (a)(12), which "must be performed by the cognizant Federal agency", where the codified paragraph says the contracting officer may retain any listed function, except those four, unless the cognizant Federal agency has designated the contracting officer to perform them [@CL-CMP-0027; @CL-CMP-1007]. The four are still negotiating forward pricing rate agreements [@CL-CMP-0028; @CL-CMP-1008], establishing final indirect cost rates and billing rates for contractors meeting the criteria for contracting officer determination [@CL-CMP-0029; @CL-CMP-1009], the Cost Accounting Standards functions [@CL-CMP-0030; @CL-CMP-0031; @CL-CMP-1436; @CL-CMP-1437] and determining the adequacy of the contractor's accounting system, which should be adequate during the entire period of contract performance [@CL-CMP-0032; @CL-CMP-1010]. RFO paragraph (a)(13) refers to contractor requests for financing payments (see part 32), where the codified paragraph refers to requests for payments under the progress payments or performance-based payments clauses [@CL-CMP-0033; @CL-CMP-1438]. RFO paragraph (b) keeps the limit that the contract administration office performs the listed functions only when and to the extent specifically authorized by the contracting office, but says the office "may" perform them where the codified paragraph says it "shall" [@CL-CMP-0034; @CL-CMP-1442]. Its items on supplemental agreements incorporating contractor proposals resulting from change orders [@CL-CMP-0080; @CL-CMP-1443] and on supplemental agreements changing contract delivery schedules [@CL-CMP-0035; @CL-CMP-1444] read negotiate and execute where the codified items read negotiate or negotiate and execute. RFO paragraph (c) says additional functions not listed in paragraphs (a) and (b), or not otherwise delegated, remain the responsibility of the contracting office "unless in accordance with 42.202 Assignment of contract administration(c)", where the codified paragraph has no such exception [@CL-CMP-0036; @CL-CMP-1011].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contracting office awards a 10,000,000 fixed-price development contract with progress payments and delegates contract administration to a contract administration office. The delegated office negotiates a forward pricing rate agreement, determines whether the contractor's accounting system is adequate, and reviews and approves the contractor's progress payment requests. The contracting office has not authorized the delegated office to change delivery schedules, so a proposed schedule change goes back to the contracting office.

## Never confuse

The functions in paragraph (a) are normally delegated, while those in paragraph (b) are performed only when the contracting office specifically authorizes them [@CL-CMP-1006; @CL-CMP-1442]. A function that appears in neither paragraph and has not been delegated stays with the contracting office [@CL-CMP-1011]. For the roles around contract administration, see [Oversight roles](cmp-oversight-roles.md) and [Audit and records negotiation](cmp-audit-and-records-negotiation.md).

The codified text says the contracting officer may retain any listed function, except those in paragraphs (a)(5), (a)(9), (a)(11) and (a)(12), unless the cognizant Federal agency has designated the contracting officer to perform them [@CL-CMP-1007], while the RFO text says those four "must be performed by the cognizant Federal agency" [@CL-CMP-0027].

## Company calibration

Identifying, for each contract, which contract administration functions were delegated, which were authorized only when needed, and which stayed with the contracting office. [@Q-004]
