## Purpose

Defines the core audit objectives and opinion standards applied by contract auditors when examining incurred cost representations.

The auditor's primary objective is to examine contractor cost representations, in whatever form they may be presented (such as progress payments, incurred cost proposals, and termination claims), and to express an opinion as to whether such incurred costs meet the following criteria [@CL-CMP-1700]: allowable, reasonable, and allocable to the contract under FAR 31; consistent with Generally Accepted Accounting Principles (GAAP) and Cost Accounting Standards (CAS) applicable in the circumstances; and not prohibited by the contract, by statute or regulation, or by previous agreement with, or decision of, the contracting officer [@CL-CMP-1701; @CL-CMP-1702].

## How it works

The auditor's primary objective is to examine contractor cost representations, in whatever form they may be presented, and to express an opinion as to whether such incurred costs are allowable, reasonable, and allocable to the contract under FAR 31, consistent with GAAP and CAS applicable in the circumstances, and not prohibited by the contract, by statute or regulation, or by previous agreement with, or decision of, the contracting officer [@CL-CMP-1700; @CL-CMP-1701; @CL-CMP-1702]. The discovery of fraud or other unlawful activity is not the primary audit objective; however, audit work should be designed to provide reasonable assurance of detecting abuse or illegal acts that could significantly affect the audit objective [@CL-CMP-1703]. If illegal activity is suspected, the circumstances should be referred to investigative authorities [@CL-CMP-1704]. FAR 42.503-1 states that a single agency shall be responsible for establishing final indirect cost rates for each business unit [@CL-CMP-1705]. Which agency establishes the final indirect cost rates for a business unit is covered on [Purpose and policy for final indirect cost rates and billing rates](../../indirect-rates/rate-lifecycle/rte-final-and-billing-rate-purpose.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor submits an incurred cost proposal claiming 10,000,000 in total costs, including 2,000,000 in indirect costs. The auditor reviews the submission and tests whether the claimed amounts satisfy cost principles, applicable Cost Accounting Standards, and contract terms. The auditor issues an audit report expressing an opinion on the allowability and allocability of the claimed indirect rates and questions 50,000 of costs that violate express regulatory prohibitions.

## Never confuse

The discovery of fraud or other unlawful activity is not the primary audit objective; however, audit work should be designed to provide reasonable assurance of detecting abuse or illegal acts that could significantly affect the audit objective, and if illegal activity is suspected, the circumstances should be referred to investigative authorities [@CL-CMP-1703; @CL-CMP-1704]. What follows when the audit finds expressly unallowable costs is on [Penalties on expressly unallowable costs: auditor and contracting officer roles](cmp-expressly-unallowable-penalty-roles.md).
