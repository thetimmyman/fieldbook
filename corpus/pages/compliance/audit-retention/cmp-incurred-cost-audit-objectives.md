## Purpose

Defines the core audit objectives and opinion standards applied by contract auditors when examining incurred cost representations.

The auditor's primary objective is to examine contractor cost representations, in whatever form they are presented, such as progress payments, incurred cost proposals and termination claims, and to express an opinion on whether the incurred costs meet set criteria [@CL-CMP-1700]. The criteria are that the costs are allowable, reasonable and allocable to the contract under FAR 31, consistent with GAAP and the Cost Accounting Standards that apply in the circumstances, and not prohibited by the contract, by statute or regulation, or by previous agreement with or decision of the contracting officer [@CL-CMP-1701; @CL-CMP-1702].

## How it works

The examination tests the cost representation against the three criteria above [@CL-CMP-1700; @CL-CMP-1701; @CL-CMP-1702]. Discovering fraud or other unlawful activity is not the primary audit objective, but audit work should be designed to provide reasonable assurance of detecting abuse or illegal acts that could significantly affect the audit objective [@CL-CMP-1703]. If illegal activity is suspected, the circumstances should be referred to investigative authorities [@CL-CMP-1704]. A single agency is responsible for establishing final indirect cost rates for each business unit under FAR 42.503-1 [@CL-CMP-1705]. Which agency establishes the final indirect cost rates for a business unit is covered on [Purpose and policy for final indirect cost rates and billing rates](../../indirect-rates/rate-lifecycle/rte-final-and-billing-rate-purpose.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor submits an incurred cost proposal claiming 10,000,000 in total costs, including 2,000,000 in indirect costs. The auditor reviews the submission and tests whether the claimed amounts satisfy cost principles, applicable Cost Accounting Standards, and contract terms. The auditor issues an audit report expressing an opinion on the allowability and allocability of the claimed indirect rates and questions 50,000 of costs that violate express regulatory prohibitions.

## Never confuse

The audit opinion on incurred costs is not a fraud investigation: discovering fraud is not the primary audit objective, although suspected illegal activity is referred on [@CL-CMP-1703; @CL-CMP-1704]. What follows when the audit finds expressly unallowable costs is on [Penalties on expressly unallowable costs: auditor and contracting officer roles](cmp-expressly-unallowable-penalty-roles.md).
