## Purpose

Explains who on the government side decides, administers and audits, so that each request reaches the right role.

## The rule

The contracting officer decides; the auditor advises. The contracting officer normally delegates contract administration functions to a contract administration office and may keep most of them, but may not keep some, such as establishing final indirect cost rates and billing rates, unless the cognizant federal agency has designated the contracting officer to perform them [@S-0005-v1, § 42.302]. The auditor gives information and advice on the acceptability of incurred and estimated costs and reviews the financial and accounting aspects of cost control systems [@S-0005-v1, § 42.101]. Normally, for contractors other than educational institutions and nonprofit organizations, the Defense Contract Audit Agency is the responsible audit agency [@S-0005-v1, § 42.101]. For those contractors, the cognizant federal agency is normally the one with the largest dollar amount of negotiated contracts, including options [@S-0005-v1, § 42.003].

## How it works

A proposal goes to the contracting officer, who may ask the auditor to review it and the administration office to comment. Rates, business systems and incurred costs are administered by the administrative contracting officer, usually in the Defense Contract Management Agency for DoD work, with audit support from the Defense Contract Audit Agency. A finding by the auditor is advice until the contracting officer acts on it, except that where agency regulations authorize it the auditor can suspend payment of questionable costs or issue a notice of costs suspended or disapproved on a voucher [@S-0005-v1, § 42.803].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An audit report questions 300,000 of travel cost. The contractor answers the auditor's draft, but the decision to disallow or accept rests with the administrative contracting officer, who issues the determination.

## Never confuse

The administrative contracting officer administers the contract after award; the procuring contracting officer awards it. The auditor generally recommends rather than decides; the contracting officer decides. See [Audit and control discovery questions](cmp-brief-audit-discovery.md).
