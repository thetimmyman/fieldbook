## Purpose

Shows when the Government performs an in-depth or a special contractor insurance/pension review (CIPR), the sales threshold, the triggers, and who decides.

## The rule

| Codified citation | DFARS 242.7302 [@CL-CMP-2233] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |

| Applies to | In-depth CIPR: contractors with $50 million of qualifying sales to the Government during the contractor's preceding fiscal year when the ACO determines a CIPR is needed [@CL-CMP-0222; @CL-CMP-2233; @CL-CMP-2274] |

An in-depth CIPR as described at DFARS 242.7301(a)(1) shall be conducted only when a contractor has $50 million of qualifying sales to the Government during the contractor's preceding fiscal year and the administrative contracting officer (ACO), with advice from DCMA insurance/pension specialists and DCAA auditors, determines a CIPR is needed based on a risk assessment of the contractor's past experience and current vulnerability [@CL-CMP-0222; @CL-CMP-2233; @CL-CMP-2274]. Qualifying sales are sales for which certified cost or pricing data were required (section 3702 of title 10 of the United States Code, as implemented in FAR 15.403), or that are contracts priced on other than a firm-fixed-price or fixed-price with economic price adjustment basis [@CL-CMP-2234]. Sales include prime contracts, subcontracts, and modifications to such contracts and subcontracts [@CL-CMP-2254].

Under DFARS 242.7301(a), an in-depth CIPR is a joint review by the Defense Contract Management Agency and the Defense Contract Audit Agency that provides an in-depth evaluation of a contractor's insurance programs, pension plans, other deferred compensation plans, and related policies, procedures, practices, and costs [@CL-CMP-2256]; or a review may concentrate on specific areas of the contractor's insurance programs, pension plans, or other deferred compensation plans [@CL-CMP-2257].

A special CIPR that concentrates on specific areas of a contractor's insurance programs, pension plans, or other deferred compensation plans shall be performed for a contractor (including, but not limited to, a contractor meeting the requirements in paragraph (a)) when any one of four circumstances exists, but only if the circumstance may result in a material impact on Government contract costs [@CL-CMP-0223; @CL-CMP-2235]: information or data reveals a deficiency in the contractor's insurance/pension program; the contractor proposes or implements changes in its insurance, pension, or other deferred compensation plans [@CL-CMP-2236]; the contractor is involved in a merger, acquisition, or divestiture; or the Government needs to follow up on contractor implementation of prior CIPR recommendations [@CL-CMP-2237].

The DCAA auditor shall use relevant findings and recommendations of previously performed CIPRs in determining the scope of any audits of insurance and pension costs [@CL-CMP-2238]. When a Government organization believes that a review of the contractor's insurance/pension program should be performed, that organization should provide a recommendation for a review to the ACO [@CL-CMP-2239]. If the ACO concurs, the review should be performed as part of an ACO-initiated special CIPR or as part of a CIPR already scheduled for the near future [@CL-CMP-2255].

## How it works

An in-depth CIPR as described at DFARS 242.7301(a)(1) shall be conducted only when both conditions are met: the contractor has $50 million of qualifying sales to the Government during the contractor's preceding fiscal year, and the ACO, with advice from DCMA insurance/pension specialists and DCAA auditors, determines a CIPR is needed based on a risk assessment of the contractor's past experience and current vulnerability [@CL-CMP-0222; @CL-CMP-2233; @CL-CMP-2274]. A special CIPR that concentrates on specific areas of a contractor's insurance programs, pension plans, or other deferred compensation plans shall be performed for a contractor (including, but not limited to, a contractor meeting the requirements in paragraph (a)) when any of the listed circumstances exists, but only if the circumstance may result in a material impact on Government contract costs [@CL-CMP-0223; @CL-CMP-2235]. Under DFARS 242.7301(a), an in-depth CIPR is a joint review by DCMA and DCAA that provides an in-depth evaluation of a contractor's insurance programs, pension plans, other deferred compensation plans, and related policies, procedures, practices, and costs [@CL-CMP-2256], or a review may concentrate on specific areas of the contractor's insurance programs, pension plans, or other deferred compensation plans [@CL-CMP-2257].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor had 60,000,000 of qualifying sales last year, and the ACO, after advice from the specialists and auditors, finds a review is needed, so an in-depth review may be conducted. A second contractor had 20,000,000 of qualifying sales but is selling a business unit. The sale is a merger, acquisition or divestiture circumstance, so a special review is performed if it may materially affect Government contract costs.

## Never confuse

The CIPR differs from the audits it shapes: the DCAA auditor shall use relevant findings and recommendations of previously performed CIPRs in determining the scope of any audits of insurance and pension costs [@CL-CMP-2238]. The cost rules for insurance and pension are on [Insurance standard](../cas-allowability/cmp-cas-416-insurance.md), [Insurance and indemnification](../cas-allowability/cmp-insurance-and-indemnification.md), and [Pension cost composition](../cas-allowability/cmp-cas-412-pension-composition.md).
