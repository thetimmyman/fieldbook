## Purpose

Shows when the Government performs an in-depth or a special contractor insurance/pension review (CIPR), the sales threshold, the triggers, and who decides.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-CMP-2233] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 242.7302 [@CL-CMP-2233] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-2233] |
| Applies to | In-depth review: contractors with $50 million of qualifying sales in the preceding fiscal year [@CL-CMP-2233] |

An in-depth review is conducted only when a contractor has $50 million of qualifying sales to the Government in its preceding fiscal year and the administrative contracting officer (ACO), with advice from DCMA insurance/pension specialists and DCAA auditors, determines a review is needed based on a risk assessment of the contractor's past experience and current vulnerability [@CL-CMP-2233; @CL-CMP-2274]. Qualifying sales are sales for which certified cost or pricing data were required under section 3702 of title 10 of the United States Code, as implemented in FAR 15.403, or that are contracts priced on other than a firm-fixed-price or fixed-price with economic price adjustment basis [@CL-CMP-2234]. Under the same paragraph, sales include prime contracts, subcontracts, and modifications to such contracts and subcontracts [@CL-CMP-2254].

The general section describes a review in two ways. One is a joint review by the Defense Contract Management Agency and the Defense Contract Audit Agency that provides an in-depth evaluation of a contractor's insurance programs, pension plans, other deferred compensation plans and related policies, procedures, practices and costs [@CL-CMP-2256]. In the alternative, a review concentrates on specific areas of the contractor's insurance programs, pension plans or other deferred compensation plans [@CL-CMP-2257].

A special review shall be performed for a contractor, including but not limited to one meeting the in-depth review requirements, when any listed circumstance exists, but only if the circumstance may result in a material impact on Government contract costs [@CL-CMP-2235]. Two listed circumstances are that information or data reveals a deficiency in the insurance/pension program and that the contractor proposes or implements changes in its insurance, pension or deferred compensation plans [@CL-CMP-2236]. Two further ones are that the contractor is involved in a merger, acquisition or divestiture and that the Government needs to follow up on implementation of prior review recommendations [@CL-CMP-2237].

The auditor shall use relevant findings and recommendations of earlier reviews in setting the scope of any audit of insurance and pension costs [@CL-CMP-2238]. A Government organization that believes a review should be performed should recommend it to the ACO [@CL-CMP-2239]. If the ACO concurs, the review should be performed either as part of an ACO-initiated special review or as part of a review already scheduled for the near future [@CL-CMP-2255].

## How it works

For an in-depth review, the ACO determines, with advice from the specialists and auditors and based on a risk assessment of the contractor's past experience and current vulnerability, whether a review is needed; that determination is one of two conditions, and the other is $50 million of qualifying sales in the preceding fiscal year [@CL-CMP-2233; @CL-CMP-2274]. A special review is not tied to the sales threshold, but it applies only when the circumstance may materially affect Government contract costs [@CL-CMP-2235]. An in-depth review evaluates the contractor's insurance programs, pension plans, other deferred compensation plans and related policies, procedures, practices and costs [@CL-CMP-2256]. The special review concentrates on specific areas of those programs and plans [@CL-CMP-2257].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor had 60,000,000 of qualifying sales last year, and the ACO, after advice from the specialists and auditors, finds a review is needed, so an in-depth review may be conducted. A second contractor had 20,000,000 of qualifying sales but is selling a business unit. The sale is a merger, acquisition or divestiture circumstance, so a special review is performed if it may materially affect Government contract costs.

## Never confuse

A review is not the audit of insurance and pension costs. The auditor uses earlier review findings to set the audit scope [@CL-CMP-2238]. The cost rules for insurance and pension are on other pages: see [Insurance standard](../cas-allowability/cmp-cas-416-insurance.md), [Insurance and indemnification](../cas-allowability/cmp-insurance-and-indemnification.md) and [Pension cost composition](../cas-allowability/cmp-cas-412-pension-composition.md).
