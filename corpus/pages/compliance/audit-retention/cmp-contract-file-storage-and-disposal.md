## Purpose

Shows how agencies handle, store and dispose of contract files, and which retention periods matter to a finance reader and what starts each one.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-2445] |
| Codified citation | FAR 4.805 [@CL-CMP-2445; @CL-CMP-2446] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-2445] |
| Applies to | Agency contract files and the listed record types in the retention table [@CL-CMP-2445; @CL-CMP-2446] |

Agencies must prescribe procedures for the handling, storing and disposing of contract files, in accordance with the NARA General Records Schedule 1.1, Financial Management and Reporting Records [@CL-CMP-2445]. The retention table sets a period of 6 years after final payment for contracts and related records or documents, including successful and unsuccessful proposals, except contractor payrolls submitted under construction contracts [@CL-CMP-2446].

Files for canceled solicitations are kept 6 years after cancellation [@CL-CMP-2447]. One listed row covers investigations, cases pending or in litigation, including protests, and similar matters, including enforcement actions [@CL-CMP-2448]. That row sets the period as until final clearance or settlement, or, if related to a document identified in the earlier rows, the period specified for that document, whichever is later [@CL-CMP-2448; @CL-CMP-2449].

An agency that requires a shorter retention period than those in the retention table shall request approval from NARA through its records officer [@CL-CMP-2519]. Unless law or other regulations require signed originals to be kept, agencies may destroy signed originals after the responsible agency official verifies that record copies on alternate media and copies reproduced from the record copy are accurate, complete, and clear representations of the originals [@CL-CMP-2518].

## How it works

What starts each period matters. For contracts and related records it is final payment [@CL-CMP-2446], for canceled solicitation files it is cancellation [@CL-CMP-2447], and for investigations and litigated matters it is final clearance or settlement, unless a related document's period runs later [@CL-CMP-2449]. The table has its own row for contractor payrolls submitted under construction contracts, which is the exception to the first row [@CL-CMP-2446]. This card does not set out the other rows.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

Final payment on a 4,000,000 contract is made at the end of year 2, so the contract file is kept until the end of year 8. A protest related to that contract is still pending at the end of year 8. Answer: the file is kept until final clearance or settlement, because that is later than the end of year 8.

## Never confuse

The retention table sets periods for contract files and is not the contractor's record-availability rule, which is 3 years after final payment or, for certain records, the period in the specific retention sections, whichever expires first, and is subject to longer periods [@CL-CMP-2418; @CL-CMP-2419]. That rule is on [Contractor records retention policy](cmp-contractor-records-retention-policy.md), and the Government's file contents are on [Government contract files: file types and contents](cmp-government-contract-files.md). A row for pending cases is also not a fixed number of years, because it runs until clearance or settlement or the related document's period, whichever is later [@CL-CMP-2449].
