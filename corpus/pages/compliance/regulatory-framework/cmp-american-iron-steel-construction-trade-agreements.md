## Purpose

Explains when a contract that carries this clause may use only domestic and designated-country construction material, and what a contractor submits to ask for an exception that allows foreign material.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-5404] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.225-23, basic clause [@CL-CMP-5404] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-5404] |
| Applies to | Construction material used in performing a contract that includes the clause [@CL-CMP-5404] |

The Contractor must use only domestic construction material, Recovery Act designated country manufactured construction material, or designated country unmanufactured construction material in performing the contract, except as provided in paragraphs (b)(3) and (b)(4) [@CL-CMP-5404]. The Recovery Act restriction does not apply to Recovery Act designated country manufactured construction material, and the Buy American statute restriction does not apply to designated country unmanufactured construction material [@CL-CMP-5407; @CL-CMP-5408].

The requirement in paragraph (b)(2) does not apply to the construction materials or components the Government lists in paragraph (b)(3), where the Contracting Officer lists the applicable excepted materials or indicates none, so each contract must be read for its own entries [@CL-CMP-6097]. The Contracting Officer may add other construction material to that list if the Government determines that the cost of domestic construction material would be unreasonable [@CL-CMP-5507].

The cost of domestic manufactured construction material is unreasonable when the cumulative cost of that material, compared with comparable foreign manufactured construction material other than Recovery Act designated country construction material, will increase the overall cost of the contract by more than 25 percent [@CL-CMP-5507; @CL-CMP-5405]. The cost of domestic unmanufactured construction material is unreasonable when it exceeds the cost of comparable foreign unmanufactured construction material, other than designated country construction material, by more than 20 percent [@CL-CMP-5406].

Another determination that lets the Contracting Officer add material to the list is that the construction material is not mined, produced, or manufactured in the United States in sufficient and reasonably available commercial quantities of a satisfactory quality [@CL-CMP-6098]. A further determination is that applying the section 1605 restriction to a particular manufactured construction material would be inconsistent with the public interest, or applying the Buy American statute to a particular unmanufactured construction material would be impracticable or inconsistent with the public interest [@CL-CMP-6116].

Implementation. The clause implements section 1605 of the Recovery Act by requiring, unless an exception applies, that all manufactured construction material in the project is manufactured in the United States, with a further requirement for material that consists wholly or predominantly of iron or steel [@CL-CMP-6114]. For such material, the iron or steel must have been produced in the United States, which means that all manufacturing processes of the iron or steel must take place in the United States, except metallurgical processes involving refinement of steel additives [@CL-CMP-6115]. The clause implements the Buy American statute by providing a preference for unmanufactured construction material mined or produced in the United States over unmanufactured construction material mined or produced in a nondesignated country [@CL-CMP-6096].

Definitions. A component is an article, material, or supply incorporated directly into a construction material, and a construction material is an article, material, or supply brought to the construction site by the Contractor or subcontractor for incorporation into the building or work [@CL-CMP-6100]. Domestic construction material includes an unmanufactured construction material mined or produced in the United States, to which the Buy American statute applies [@CL-CMP-6121]. It also includes a manufactured construction material that is manufactured in the United States and, if it consists wholly or predominantly of iron or steel, has iron or steel produced in the United States, to which section 1605 of the Recovery Act applies [@CL-CMP-6122].

## How it works

A contractor that wants to use foreign construction material under paragraph (b)(4) submits a request that includes adequate information for Government evaluation, a description of the foreign and domestic construction materials, and the unit of measure [@CL-CMP-5409]. The request must also include the quantity, cost, time of delivery or availability, location of the construction project, name and address of the proposed supplier, and a detailed justification of the reason for use of foreign construction materials [@CL-CMP-5508].

A request based on unreasonable cost must also include a reasonable survey of the market and a completed cost comparison table in the format in paragraph (d), and the cost of construction material must include all delivery costs to the construction site and any applicable duty [@CL-CMP-5410].

A request for a determination submitted after contract award must explain why the Contractor could not reasonably foresee the need for the determination and could not have requested it before contract award [@CL-CMP-5509]. If the Contractor does not submit a satisfactory explanation, the Contracting Officer need not make a determination [@CL-CMP-5510].

If the Government determines after contract award that an exception applies and the Contracting Officer and the Contractor negotiate adequate consideration, the Contracting Officer will modify the contract [@CL-CMP-6117]. When the basis for the exception is the unreasonable cost of a domestic construction material, adequate consideration is not less than the differential established in paragraph (b)(4)(i) [@CL-CMP-5411].

Unless the Government determines that an exception to section 1605 of the Recovery Act or the Buy American statute applies, use of foreign construction material other than manufactured construction material from a Recovery Act designated country or unmanufactured construction material from a designated country is noncompliant with the applicable statute [@CL-CMP-6118; @CL-CMP-6119].

Data. To permit evaluation of requests based on unreasonable cost, the Contractor must include the information and any applicable supporting data based on the survey of suppliers for foreign (nondesignated country) and domestic construction materials [@CL-CMP-6120]. The data must list the name, address, telephone number and contact for suppliers surveyed, with a copy of each response attached, or a summary if the response was oral [@CL-CMP-6099].

Alternate I. Alternate I adds a definition of Bahraini, Mexican, or Omani construction material, which means a construction material that is wholly the growth, product, or manufacture of Bahrain, Mexico, or Oman, or that meets the second alternative in the definition [@CL-CMP-6101]. A construction material that consists in whole or in part of materials from another country also qualifies if it has been substantially transformed in Bahrain, Mexico, or Oman into a new and different construction material distinct from the materials from which it was transformed [@CL-CMP-6102]. Under Alternate I, the Contractor must use only domestic construction material, Recovery Act designated country manufactured construction material, or designated country unmanufactured construction material, other than Bahraini, Mexican, or Omani construction material, in performing the contract [@CL-CMP-6123].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor wants to buy gravel, an unmanufactured construction material, from a nondesignated country. The foreign gravel costs 85,000 at the quarry, 10,000 to deliver to the site and 5,000 in duty, so its cost for the comparison is 100,000. The domestic gravel delivered to the site costs 120,000. The domestic cost exceeds the foreign cost by 20,000, which is exactly 20 percent and not more than 20 percent, so the unreasonable-cost test is not met. If the domestic delivered cost were 121,000, the excess would be 21 percent and the test would be met. Comparing only the 85,000 quarry price would show a 41 percent gap and would be wrong, because the cost must include delivery and duty. A request on this ground would still need the market survey and the cost comparison data, and until the Government determines that an exception applies, using the foreign gravel is noncompliant with the applicable statute. If the contractor first raised the request after award, it would also have to explain why it could not have foreseen the need earlier, and without a satisfactory explanation the Contracting Officer need not make a determination.

## Never confuse

The clause is not a flat ban on foreign construction material. Designated country unmanufactured material and Recovery Act designated country manufactured material sit outside the restrictions, and the Contracting Officer may add other material to the list after a determination [@CL-CMP-5407; @CL-CMP-5408; @CL-CMP-5507]. Exactly 20 percent is not enough for unmanufactured material, because the test is more than 20 percent [@CL-CMP-5406].
