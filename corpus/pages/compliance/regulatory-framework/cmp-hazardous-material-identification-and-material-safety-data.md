## Purpose

Sets out what an offeror must list about hazardous material it will deliver, when material safety data sheets are due, what follows when they are missing or become inaccurate, the Government's rights in the data, and the shipping copies that Alternate I adds.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-5168] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.223-3 [@CL-CMP-5168] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.223-3 [@CL-CMP-1264; @CL-CMP-1265] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-5168] |
| Applies to | Offerors and contractors under contracts that carry the clause, for hazardous material to be delivered [@CL-CMP-5169] |

Hazardous material includes any material defined as hazardous under the latest version of the Federal Standard the clause names, including revisions adopted during the term of the contract [@CL-CMP-5168].

The offeror must list any hazardous material to be delivered under the contract, properly identified and including any applicable identification number such as a National Stock Number or Special Item Number [@CL-CMP-5169]. The list has a material column, in which the offeror inserts None if there is no hazardous material to list, and an identification number column [@CL-CMP-5268]. The identification information must also be included on the material safety data sheet submitted under the contract [@CL-CMP-5269].

The list must be updated during performance of the contract whenever the contractor determines that any other material to be delivered under the contract is hazardous [@CL-CMP-5170].

## How it works

The apparently successful offeror agrees to submit, for each item as required prior to award, a data sheet meeting the requirements of the cited regulation and the latest version of the Federal Standard, for all hazardous material on the list [@CL-CMP-5171]. The data are submitted in accordance with the Federal Standard whether or not the apparently successful offeror is the actual manufacturer of the items [@CL-CMP-5172].

Failure to submit the data sheet prior to award may result in the apparently successful offeror being considered nonresponsible and ineligible for award [@CL-CMP-5173].

After award, a change in the composition of the items or a revision to the Federal Standard that makes the submitted data incomplete or inaccurate requires the contractor to notify the contracting officer promptly and resubmit the data [@CL-CMP-5174].

Neither the clause nor any Government act or failure to act relieves the contractor of responsibility or liability for the safety of Government, contractor or subcontractor personnel or property [@CL-CMP-5175]. The clause also does not relieve the contractor from complying with applicable Federal, State and local laws, codes, ordinances and regulations, including the obtaining of licenses and permits, in connection with hazardous material [@CL-CMP-5270].

The Government has the right to use, duplicate and disclose any data to which the clause applies [@CL-CMP-5271]. The purposes of that right include apprising personnel of the hazards to which they may be exposed in using, handling, packaging, transporting or disposing of hazardous materials, and obtaining medical treatment for those affected by the material [@CL-CMP-5272]. A further purpose is to have others use, duplicate and disclose the data for the Government for these purposes [@CL-CMP-5273]. The Government may use the data in this way in precedence over any other clause of the contract providing for rights in data [@CL-CMP-5274]. It is not precluded from using similar or identical data acquired from other sources [@CL-CMP-5275].

Alternate I adds a paragraph to the basic clause [@CL-CMP-5276]. Under it, except as the depot rule below provides, the contractor prepares and submits a sufficient number of data sheets [@CL-CMP-5277]. They must meet the same regulation and Federal Standard and cover all hazardous materials on the list [@CL-CMP-5278]. For items shipped to consignees, a copy goes with the packing list or other suitable shipping document that accompanies each shipment [@CL-CMP-5279]. The contractor may instead transmit data sheets to consignees in advance of receipt of shipments, if the contracting officer authorizes that in writing [@CL-CMP-5280]. For items shipped to consignees identified by mailing address as agency depots, distribution centers or customer supply centers, the contractor provides one copy in or on each shipping container [@CL-CMP-5281]. A copy affixed to the outside of a container must be in a weather resistant envelope [@CL-CMP-5282].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text because Class Deviation 2026-O0038 directs it, effective February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. The RFO clause keeps the definition of hazardous material by the latest version of Federal Standard No. 313, including revisions adopted during the term of the contract [@CL-CMP-1264; @CL-CMP-5168], and the offeror's list of hazardous material to be delivered, properly identified with any applicable identification number [@CL-CMP-1265; @CL-CMP-5169]. It keeps the update of the list during performance whenever the contractor determines that any other material to be delivered is hazardous [@CL-CMP-1267; @CL-CMP-5170]. Under the RFO text the apparently successful offeror agrees to submit, for each item as required prior to award, a Safety Data Sheet [@CL-CMP-1268], where the codified text names a material safety data sheet [@CL-CMP-5171]. It keeps the submission in accordance with the Federal Standard whether or not the offeror is the manufacturer [@CL-CMP-1269; @CL-CMP-5172]. Under the RFO text, failure to submit the Safety Data Sheet prior to award may leave the offeror nonresponsible and ineligible for award [@CL-CMP-1270], where the codified text names the Material Safety Data Sheet [@CL-CMP-5173]. It keeps the duty after award to notify the Contracting Officer promptly and resubmit the data when a change in composition or a revision to the Federal Standard makes the data incomplete or inaccurate [@CL-CMP-1271; @CL-CMP-5174]. It keeps the rules that neither the clause nor any Government act or failure to act relieves the contractor of responsibility or liability for safety [@CL-CMP-1272; @CL-CMP-5175], and that the clause does not relieve the contractor from complying with applicable laws, codes, ordinances and regulations [@CL-CMP-1273; @CL-CMP-5270]. It keeps the Government's right to use, duplicate and disclose any data to which the clause applies [@CL-CMP-1274; @CL-CMP-5271], and the rule that the Government is not precluded from using similar or identical data acquired from other sources [@CL-CMP-1275; @CL-CMP-5275]. The RFO text says the identification information shall also be included on the Safety Data Sheet, where the codified text says Material Safety Data Sheet [@CL-CMP-1266; @CL-CMP-5269]. In RFO Alternate I, paragraph (i) provides that, except as provided in paragraph (i)(2), the contractor shall prepare and submit a sufficient number of Safety Data Sheets (SDS's) for all hazardous materials identified in paragraph (b) [@CL-CMP-1276], where the codified Alternate I adds a paragraph (i) [@CL-CMP-5276] that requires material safety data sheets [@CL-CMP-5277; @CL-CMP-5278]. For items shipped to consignees, the RFO text has the contractor include a copy of the SDS with the packing list or other suitable shipping document [@CL-CMP-1277], where the codified text names the data sheet [@CL-CMP-5279]; the contractor is also permitted to transmit Safety Data Sheets to consignees in advance of receipt of shipments, if authorized in writing by the Contracting Officer [@CL-CMP-1302; @CL-CMP-5280]. For consignees identified by mailing address as agency depots, distribution centers or customer supply centers, the RFO text has the contractor provide one copy of the SDS's in or on each shipping container [@CL-CMP-1278; @CL-CMP-5281], and an SDS affixed to the outside of a container must be placed in a weather resistant envelope [@CL-CMP-1279; @CL-CMP-5282].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An offeror will deliver two solvents that are hazardous under the latest version of the Federal Standard, and a set of brushes that is not. Its list names the two solvents and gives a National Stock Number for each, and the brushes are left off. Before award the apparently successful offeror submits a data sheet for each solvent, even though a supplier makes them. During performance it determines that a sealant it also delivers is hazardous, so it adds the sealant to the list. After award the supplier changes the composition of one solvent, which makes the submitted data inaccurate, so the contractor promptly notifies the contracting officer and resubmits that data sheet. Under Alternate I, a shipment to a consignee identified by mailing address as an agency depot carries one copy of the data sheets in or on each shipping container. A different offeror with no hazardous material to deliver writes None on the list.

## Never confuse

The offeror must list any hazardous material to be delivered under the contract [@CL-CMP-5169], and the identification information shall also be included on the material safety data sheet submitted under the contract [@CL-CMP-5269].

Neither the clause's requirements nor any Government act or failure to act relieves the contractor of any responsibility or liability for the safety of personnel or property [@CL-CMP-5175].

The Government is not precluded from using similar or identical data acquired from other sources [@CL-CMP-5275].

The shipping copy duties are in the paragraph (i) that Alternate I adds to the basic clause [@CL-CMP-5276; @CL-CMP-5279].

The codified clause says Material Safety Data Sheet [@CL-CMP-5269], while the RFO clause says Safety Data Sheet in the matching paragraph [@CL-CMP-1266].

The clauses on environmental information and management systems and on notice of radioactive materials are covered in [Environmental information, waste reduction and management systems](cmp-environmental-information-and-management-systems.md) and [Notice of radioactive materials](cmp-notice-of-radioactive-materials.md), and the DFARS clause on hazard warning labels is covered in [Hazard warning labels (DFARS)](cmp-dfars-hazard-warning-labels.md).

## Company calibration

Whether the company can show, item by item, which delivered materials are hazardous, where each data sheet is held, and whether the contract carries Alternate I. [@Q-004]
