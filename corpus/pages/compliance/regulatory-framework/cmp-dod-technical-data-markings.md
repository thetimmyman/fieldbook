## Purpose

Shows how the technical data clause for items that are not commercial controls markings: marking as the way to assert a restriction, the authorized legends, what happens to unmarked and nonconforming data, and how unjustified markings are removed.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-CMP-3809] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 252.227-7013 [@CL-CMP-3809] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-3809] |
| Applies to | Technical data to be delivered under a contract with the clause, when the Contractor or a subcontractor or supplier asserts a restriction on it [@CL-CMP-3809] |

The Contractor, and its subcontractors or suppliers, may only assert restrictions on the Government's rights to use, modify, reproduce, release, perform, display, or disclose technical data to be delivered under the contract by marking the deliverable data subject to restriction [@CL-CMP-3809].

### Authorized legends

Except as provided in a later paragraph of the clause, the only legends authorized under the contract are those on a list that begins with the government purpose rights legend and the limited rights legend [@CL-CMP-3810]. The list also includes the special license rights legend and a notice of copyright as prescribed under sections 401 and 402 of title 17 of the United States Code [@CL-CMP-3811].

Data delivered with government purpose rights are marked with a legend headed Government Purpose Rights that has fields for the contract number, contractor name, contractor address and expiration date [@CL-CMP-3832]. Data delivered with limited rights are marked with a legend headed Limited Rights that has fields for the contract number, contractor name and contractor address [@CL-CMP-3833].

### How to mark

The Contractor, or its subcontractors or suppliers, shall conspicuously and legibly mark the appropriate legend on all technical data that qualify for such markings [@CL-CMP-3812]. The authorized legends shall be placed on the transmittal document or storage container and, for printed material, each page of the printed material containing technical data for which restrictions are asserted [@CL-CMP-3813].

### Omitted markings

Technical data delivered or otherwise provided under the contract without restrictive markings will be presumed to have been delivered with unlimited rights [@CL-CMP-3814]. The Contractor may request permission to have conforming and justified restrictive markings placed on unmarked technical data at its expense [@CL-CMP-3816]. That request must be received by the Contracting Officer within 6 months following the furnishing or delivery of the data, or any extension of that time approved by the Contracting Officer [@CL-CMP-3816; @CL-CMP-3817]. To the extent practicable, if the Contractor has requested permission to correct an inadvertent omission of markings, the Contracting Officer will not release or disclose the technical data pending evaluation of the request [@CL-CMP-3815].

### Nonconforming markings

A nonconforming marking is a marking placed on technical data delivered or otherwise furnished to the Government under the contract that is not in the format authorized by the contract [@CL-CMP-3820]. Correction of nonconforming markings is not subject to the clause on validation of asserted restrictions on technical data [@CL-CMP-3821]. If the Contracting Officer notifies the Contractor of a nonconforming marking and the Contractor fails to remove or correct it within 60 days, the Government may ignore or, at the Contractor's expense, remove or correct any nonconforming marking [@CL-CMP-3822].

### Unjustified markings

The Government may ignore or, at the Contractor's expense, correct or strike a marking if, in accordance with the procedures in the clause on validation of asserted restrictions on technical data, a restrictive marking is determined to be unjustified [@CL-CMP-3819]. That procedure is covered on the card for [validation of asserted restrictions on technical data](cmp-dod-technical-data-restriction-validation.md).

### Contractor procedures

Contractors and their subcontractors or suppliers that will deliver technical data with other than unlimited rights shall have, maintain, and follow written procedures sufficient to assure that restrictive markings are used only when authorized by the terms of the clause [@CL-CMP-3818].

## How it works

The Contractor asserts a restriction by marking the deliverable data with one of the authorized legends [@CL-CMP-3809; @CL-CMP-3810]. Data with no marking are presumed to carry unlimited rights, and the Contractor may ask permission to add markings within a set time [@CL-CMP-3814; @CL-CMP-3816; @CL-CMP-3817]. A marking in the wrong format is a nonconforming marking, and after notice and a period to fix it the Government may ignore or correct it without using the validation procedure [@CL-CMP-3820; @CL-CMP-3821; @CL-CMP-3822]. A marking that is determined to be unjustified goes through the validation procedure instead [@CL-CMP-3819]. The markings tie to the rights categories: limited rights apply to data pertaining to items developed exclusively at private expense and marked with the limited rights legend, subject to listed exceptions [@CL-CMP-3772].

### Data the Contractor has marked

Under a listed acknowledgement, the Contractor, or the party asserting restrictions as identified in the limited rights legend, may require each covered Government support contractor to enter into a nondisclosure agreement [@CL-CMP-3774]. The Government may not, without the written permission of the party asserting limited rights, release or disclose the technical data outside the Government, use it for manufacture, or authorize another party to use it, subject to an exception stated in the definition [@CL-CMP-3765]. The Contractor has the exclusive right, including the right to license others, to use for any commercial purpose technical data in which the Government has obtained government purpose rights under this contract, during the time period specified in the government purpose rights legend [@CL-CMP-3773].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor delivers 10 drawings. It marks 9 with the limited rights legend, in the authorized format, and leaves one unmarked by mistake. The unmarked drawing is presumed to be delivered with unlimited rights. The contractor asks the Contracting Officer in month 2 for permission to add a conforming and justified marking, inside the 6-month window, and the Contracting Officer does not release the drawing while the request is evaluated, where practicable. On a later delivery the contractor writes a restriction note in its own words instead of the authorized legend. That is a nonconforming marking. The Contracting Officer notifies the contractor, and if the contractor has not fixed it within 60 days, the Government may ignore the note or correct it at the contractor's expense, without using the validation procedure.

## Never confuse

A nonconforming marking and an unjustified marking are different. A nonconforming marking is wrong in format, and its correction is outside the validation clause [@CL-CMP-3820; @CL-CMP-3821]. An unjustified marking is wrong in substance and is dealt with through the validation procedure [@CL-CMP-3819]. A marking and the funding of development are also different things: the clause describes limited rights in terms of both private-expense development and the legend [@CL-CMP-3772].

For the general FAR clause, see [Rights in data, general: copyright, withholding and markings](cmp-rights-in-data-general-copyright-withholding-and-markings.md). For the list of assertions made with an offer, see [DoD identification and assertion of restrictions](cmp-dod-assertion-of-data-restrictions.md). For commercial products, see [DoD technical data for commercial products and services](cmp-dod-commercial-technical-data-license.md), which has its own release from liability for data that are not marked [@CL-CMP-3830].
