## Purpose

Shows two employment duties that ride with a contract: reporting a labor dispute that threatens timely performance, and keeping persons serving prison sentences out of contract performance, with the exceptions the convict labor clause lists.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-5214] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.222-1 and 52.222-3 [@CL-CMP-5214; @CL-CMP-5215] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.222-1 [@CL-CMP-1464] and RFO FAR 52.222-3 [@CL-CMP-1465; @CL-CMP-1466] |
| Edition date | FAR 52.222-1: FEB 1997 [@CL-CMP-10236]; FAR 52.222-3: JUN 2003 [@CL-CMP-10237] |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-5214] |
| Applies to | Contracts that carry the labor dispute notice clause or the convict labor clause [@CL-CMP-5214; @CL-CMP-5215] |

If the contractor has knowledge that an actual or potential labor dispute is delaying, or threatens to delay, the timely performance of the contract, it must immediately give notice, including all relevant information, to the Contracting Officer [@CL-CMP-5214]. The convict labor clause bars a contractor from employing in performance of the contract any person undergoing a sentence of imprisonment imposed by a court of a State, the District of Columbia, Puerto Rico, the Northern Mariana Islands, American Samoa, Guam or the United States Virgin Islands [@CL-CMP-5215]. The exceptions are in paragraph (b) of the clause and are described below [@CL-CMP-5216].

## How it works

The notice goes to the Contracting Officer. The Contractor shall give notice if it has knowledge that any actual or potential labor dispute is delaying or threatens to delay the timely performance of the contract [@CL-CMP-5214].

The convict labor bar does not stop the contractor from employing persons on parole or probation to work at paid employment during the term of their sentence, or persons who have been pardoned or who have served their terms [@CL-CMP-5216]. It also does not stop the contractor from employing a person confined for violation of the laws of a listed jurisdiction who is authorized to work at paid employment in the community under that jurisdiction's laws, if the listed conditions are met [@CL-CMP-5216; @CL-CMP-5334]. All of the following conditions must be met [@CL-CMP-5219; @CL-CMP-5334]. The worker is paid or is in an approved work training program on a voluntary basis, and representatives of local union central bodies or similar labor union organizations have been consulted [@CL-CMP-5217]. The employment does not displace employed workers, is not applied in skills, crafts or trades with a surplus of available gainful labor in the locality, and does not impair existing contracts for services [@CL-CMP-5218]. Rates of pay and other conditions of employment are no less than those paid or provided for work of a similar nature in the locality where the work is performed [@CL-CMP-5219]. The Attorney General of the United States has certified that the work-release laws or regulations of the jurisdiction involved conform to Executive Order 11755, as amended by Executive Orders 12608 and 12943 [@CL-CMP-5220].

Under the RFO text, the DoD class deviation directs DoD contracting officers to use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text, effective February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. RFO FAR 52.222-1 keeps the duty to give immediate notice to the Contracting Officer, with all relevant information, when the contractor knows of an actual or potential labor dispute that is delaying or threatens to delay timely performance [@CL-CMP-1464] [@CL-CMP-5214]. RFO FAR 52.222-3 keeps the bar on employing a person undergoing a sentence of imprisonment imposed by a court of a listed jurisdiction, subject to paragraph (b) [@CL-CMP-1465] [@CL-CMP-1466] [@CL-CMP-5215]. It also keeps the exceptions for persons on parole or probation and persons pardoned or who have served their terms [@CL-CMP-1467] [@CL-CMP-5216], and the work-release exception with its conditions: paid or approved voluntary training and union consultation [@CL-CMP-1468] [@CL-CMP-1469] [@CL-CMP-5334] [@CL-CMP-5217], no displacement, surplus trades or impaired contracts [@CL-CMP-1470] [@CL-CMP-5218], local rates of pay and conditions [@CL-CMP-1471] [@CL-CMP-5219], and certification by the Attorney General of the United States [@CL-CMP-1472] [@CL-CMP-5220]. The RFO text prints "must" where the codified text prints "shall" in these two clauses [@CL-CMP-1464] [@CL-CMP-1465] [@CL-CMP-5214] [@CL-CMP-5215].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A subcontractor's machinists vote to strike on a Monday. On Tuesday the prime learns that the strike will stop a scheduled test, and that afternoon it tells the Contracting Officer, with the union notice, the affected work and the revised test date. The prime then looks at two candidates for a task. The first is on parole and works at paid employment during the sentence, so the bar does not reach that person. The second is confined, is authorized to work in the community under the laws of the jurisdiction and would be paid local rates, but the Attorney General has not certified that jurisdiction's work-release laws, so the conditions are not all met and the prime may not employ that person.

## Never confuse

The Contractor shall immediately give notice, including all relevant information, to the Contracting Officer if it has knowledge that any actual or potential labor dispute is delaying or threatens to delay the timely performance of the contract [@CL-CMP-5214]. Except as paragraph (b) provides, the Contractor shall not employ in the performance of the contract any person undergoing a sentence of imprisonment imposed by any court of a listed jurisdiction [@CL-CMP-5215]. The Contractor is not prohibited from employing persons on parole or probation to work at paid employment during the term of their sentence, or persons who have been pardoned or who have served their terms [@CL-CMP-5216]. The bar also does not apply to a person confined for violation of the laws of a listed jurisdiction who is authorized to work at paid employment in the community under that jurisdiction's laws, if the listed conditions are met [@CL-CMP-5334].
