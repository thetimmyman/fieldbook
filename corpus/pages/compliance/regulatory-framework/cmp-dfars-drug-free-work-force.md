## Purpose

Shows what the Contractor must do to keep a drug-free work force, which employees are in a sensitive position, what the program must include, and where the testing rules give way.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-CMP-4449] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 252.226-7003 [@CL-CMP-4449] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-4449] |
| Applies to | Contracts with the Drug-Free Work Force clause, used as prescribed in section 226.570-2 [@CL-CMP-4449] |

The Contractor agrees to institute and maintain a program for achieving the objective of a drug-free work force [@CL-CMP-4451]. While the clause defines criteria for the program, contractors are encouraged to implement alternative approaches comparable to the criteria in paragraph (c) that are designed to achieve the objectives of the clause [@CL-CMP-4473]. An employee in a sensitive position is an employee who has been granted access to classified information, or an employee in another position that the Contractor determines involves national security, health or safety, or functions other than the foregoing requiring a high degree of trust and confidence [@CL-CMP-4450].

Illegal drugs means controlled substances included in Schedules I and II, as defined by section 802(6) of title 21 of the United States Code, the possession of which is unlawful under chapter 13 of that title [@CL-CMP-4474]. The term does not mean the use of a controlled substance pursuant to a valid prescription or other uses authorized by law [@CL-CMP-4475].

Contractor programs shall include employee assistance programs emphasizing high level direction, education, counseling, rehabilitation, and coordination with available community resources, or appropriate alternatives [@CL-CMP-4452]. The listed elements also include supervisory training to assist in identifying and addressing illegal drug use by Contractor employees, and provision for self-referrals as well as supervisory referrals to treatment with maximum respect for individual confidentiality consistent with safety and security issues [@CL-CMP-4453]. They also include provision for identifying illegal drug users, including testing on a controlled and carefully monitored basis [@CL-CMP-4454].

The Contractor shall establish a program that provides for testing for the use of illegal drugs by employees in sensitive positions [@CL-CMP-4455]. The drug testing provisions do not apply to the extent they are inconsistent with state or local law [@CL-CMP-4458], or with an existing collective bargaining agreement, in which case the Contractor agrees that the issues in conflict will be a subject of negotiation at the next collective bargaining session [@CL-CMP-4481].

## How it works

Testing has a required part and optional parts. The Contractor decides the extent of and criteria for the required testing of employees in sensitive positions, based on considerations that include the nature of the work, the employee's duties and the efficient use of its resources [@CL-CMP-4455; @CL-CMP-4478]. The Contractor may in addition test on reasonable suspicion, after an accident or unsafe practice, as part of or after counseling or rehabilitation, or under a voluntary program [@CL-CMP-4456; @CL-CMP-4476], and may test applicants for employment [@CL-CMP-4477]. Testing may be limited to the substances for which the Mandatory Guidelines for Federal Workplace Drug Testing Programs prescribe testing [@CL-CMP-4479]. The Contractor shall adopt appropriate personnel procedures for employees found to be using drugs illegally, and shall not allow such an employee to remain on duty or perform in a sensitive position until it determines under its own procedures that the employee may perform in that position [@CL-CMP-4480; @CL-CMP-4457]. The printed clause does not say which contracts receive it; it points to its prescription section [@CL-CMP-4449].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A Contractor has 200 employees. Of these, 30 hold access to classified information, and the Contractor determines that no other positions are sensitive. The Contractor sets up a testing program for those 30 employees in sensitive positions, and adds a program for employees involved in an accident. One tested employee is found to use illegal drugs. Answer: that employee may not remain on duty or perform in a sensitive position until the Contractor determines, under its procedures, that the employee may do so. The accident program is optional, and it gives way to the extent that a collective bargaining agreement conflicts with it.

## Never confuse

The required testing covers employees in sensitive positions. Testing on reasonable suspicion, after an accident, or of applicants is something the Contractor may add, not something the clause requires [@CL-CMP-4456; @CL-CMP-4477]. The listed program elements may be replaced by appropriate alternatives, which is different from dropping the program [@CL-CMP-4452]. A controlled substance taken under a valid prescription is outside the definition of illegal drugs [@CL-CMP-4475]. The clause is about the Contractor's own program for its employees; the demonstration project provision is about whether an offeror is eligible for award, see [Representation for the demonstration project for contractors employing persons with disabilities](cmp-dfars-disabilities-demonstration-project-representation.md).
