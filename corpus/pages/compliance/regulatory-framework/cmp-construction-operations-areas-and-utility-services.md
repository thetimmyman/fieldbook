## Purpose

States where operations and storage may go, what the contractor builds and removes, what Government possession before completion does to responsibility and price, and how utility services are furnished and paid for.

### Operations and storage areas

On Government premises the contractor must confine all operations, including storage of materials, to areas authorized or approved by the contracting officer [@CL-CMP-4882]. It must hold and save the Government, its officers and agents free and harmless from liability of any nature occasioned by the contractor's performance [@CL-CMP-5050].

Temporary buildings and utilities may be erected by the contractor only with the approval of the contracting officer and must be built with labor and materials furnished by the contractor without expense to the Government [@CL-CMP-5051]. They remain the contractor's property and must be removed by it at its expense upon completion of the work, but with the written consent of the contracting officer they may be abandoned and need not be removed [@CL-CMP-5052].

Under regulations prescribed by the contracting officer, the contractor must use only established roadways, or temporary roadways that it constructs when and as authorized by the contracting officer [@CL-CMP-5053]. When materials are transported in prosecuting the work, vehicles must not be loaded beyond the loading capacity recommended by the manufacturer of the vehicle or prescribed by any Federal, State or local law or regulation [@CL-CMP-5054]. When it is necessary to cross curbs or sidewalks the contractor must protect them from damage, and it must repair or pay for the repair of any damaged curbs, sidewalks or roads [@CL-CMP-5055].

### Use and possession prior to completion

The Government has the right to take possession of or use any completed or partially completed part of the work, and before taking possession or using any work the contracting officer must furnish the contractor a list of items of work remaining to be performed or corrected [@CL-CMP-4883]. The list covers those portions of the work that the Government intends to take possession of or use [@CL-CMP-5056]. Failure of the contracting officer to list any item of work does not relieve the contractor of responsibility for complying with the terms of the contract [@CL-CMP-5057]. The Government's possession or use is not deemed an acceptance of any work under the contract [@CL-CMP-5058].

While the Government has such possession or use, the contractor is relieved of responsibility for loss of or damage to the work resulting from the Government's possession or use, notwithstanding the terms of the permits and responsibilities clause [@CL-CMP-4884]. If prior possession or use by the Government delays the progress of the work or causes additional expense to the contractor, an equitable adjustment is made in the contract price or the time of completion, and the contract is modified in writing accordingly [@CL-CMP-4885].

### Availability and use of utility services

The clause is inserted when a fixed-price construction contract or a fixed-price dismantling, demolition, or removal of improvements contract is contemplated and the contract is to be performed on Government sites [@CL-CMP-5059], and the contracting officer decides that the existing utility system is adequate for the needs of both the Government and the contractor and that furnishing it is in the Government's interest [@CL-CMP-5060]. When it is used, the contracting officer must list the available utilities in the contract [@CL-CMP-5061].

The Government must make all reasonably required amounts of utilities available to the contractor from existing outlets and supplies, as specified in the contract [@CL-CMP-4886]. Unless the contract provides otherwise, the amount of each utility service consumed is charged to or paid for by the contractor at prevailing rates charged to the Government or, where the utility is produced by the Government, at reasonable rates determined by the contracting officer [@CL-CMP-4887]. The contractor must carefully conserve any utilities furnished without charge [@CL-CMP-5062].

The contractor, at its expense and in a workmanlike manner satisfactory to the contracting officer, must install and maintain all necessary temporary connections and distribution lines, and all meters required to measure the amount of each utility used for the purpose of determining charges [@CL-CMP-5063]. Before final acceptance of the work by the Government, it must remove all the temporary connections, distribution lines, meters and associated paraphernalia [@CL-CMP-5064].

## How it works

Area control is a permission rule, so operations and storage stay inside areas the contracting officer authorizes or approves, and the contractor builds its temporary facilities at its own expense and removes them at its own expense unless the officer consents in writing to abandonment [@CL-CMP-4882; @CL-CMP-5051; @CL-CMP-5052]. Government possession is a right that carries a duty to list remaining items, and the list does not excuse the contractor from the terms of the contract [@CL-CMP-4883; @CL-CMP-5057]. While the Government has possession or use, the contractor is relieved of responsibility for the loss of or damage to the work resulting from it, and if it delays the progress of the work or causes additional expense, an equitable adjustment is made [@CL-CMP-4884; @CL-CMP-4885]. Utilities come from existing outlets and supplies as the contract specifies, and the contractor pays for what it consumes unless the contract provides otherwise [@CL-CMP-4886; @CL-CMP-4887].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text from February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. RFO FAR 52.236-10(a) says the contractor must confine all activities and operations on site to areas authorized or approved by the contracting officer, where the codified paragraph says all operations, including storage of materials, on Government premises [@CL-CMP-0680; @CL-CMP-4882]. The hold-harmless sentence says the same in substance [@CL-CMP-0681; @CL-CMP-5050]. For temporary buildings and utilities, the RFO text says they may be erected only with the contracting officer's approval and built with labor and materials furnished by the contractor without additional expense to the Government, where the codified text says without expense to the Government [@CL-CMP-0682; @CL-CMP-5051]; they remain the contractor's property, are removed at its expense, and may be abandoned with the contracting officer's written consent, the same in substance [@CL-CMP-0683; @CL-CMP-5052]. For roadways, the RFO text says as prescribed by the contracting officer, where the codified text says under regulations prescribed by the contracting officer [@CL-CMP-0684; @CL-CMP-5053]. The vehicle loading rule is followed by the end of the RFO clause, so the codified sentences on protecting and repairing curbs, sidewalks and roads have no counterpart text in it [@CL-CMP-0685; @CL-CMP-5054; @CL-CMP-5055]. RFO FAR 52.236-11 says the same in substance on Government possession: the right to take possession or use [@CL-CMP-0686; @CL-CMP-4883], the list of items remaining [@CL-CMP-0687; @CL-CMP-5056], no relief for an unlisted item and no acceptance by possession or use [@CL-CMP-0688; @CL-CMP-5057; @CL-CMP-5058], relief from responsibility for loss or damage resulting from that possession or use [@CL-CMP-0689; @CL-CMP-4884], and an equitable adjustment for delay or added expense [@CL-CMP-0690; @CL-CMP-4885]. For utility services, the prescription line of RFO FAR 52.236-14 says the clause is inserted as prescribed in 36.101-7(f) and prints no further condition, where the codified line prints the conditions for using the clause [@CL-CMP-0691; @CL-CMP-5059; @CL-CMP-5060] and says the contracting officer lists the available utilities in the contract [@CL-CMP-5061]. The RFO clause says the Government will make all reasonably required utilities available from existing outlets and supplies, as specified in the contract [@CL-CMP-0692; @CL-CMP-4886], and says that, unless otherwise provided in the contract, the contractor pays for all utility costs, where the codified clause sets the charge at prevailing rates or reasonable rates determined by the contracting officer and requires the contractor to conserve utilities furnished without charge [@CL-CMP-0693; @CL-CMP-4887; @CL-CMP-5062]. The RFO clause has the contractor install and maintain temporary connections, distribution lines and meters at its expense and in a workmanlike manner, where the codified clause adds a manner satisfactory to the contracting officer [@CL-CMP-0694; @CL-CMP-5063], and has it remove them before final acceptance by the Government [@CL-CMP-0695; @CL-CMP-5064].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

The contractor stores steel on a lot that the contracting officer has not authorized or approved, which does not meet the area duty [@CL-CMP-4882]. It builds a site office with the officer's approval, at its own expense, and removes it at its own expense at completion unless the officer consents in writing to abandoning it [@CL-CMP-5051; @CL-CMP-5052].

The Government takes over the east wing while work continues elsewhere, and the contracting officer lists the items remaining on that wing [@CL-CMP-4883; @CL-CMP-5056]. One punch-list item is missing from the list, and the contractor must still complete it [@CL-CMP-5057]. The Government's use of the wing is not an acceptance of the work [@CL-CMP-5058]. A later leak damages the wing and the damage results from the Government's use, so the contractor is relieved of responsibility for it [@CL-CMP-4884]. Resequencing around the occupied wing delays the work by 15 days and adds 30,000, so an equitable adjustment of 15 days and 30,000 is made and the contract is modified in writing [@CL-CMP-4885]. The contract lists metered power [@CL-CMP-5061] and does not provide otherwise. The contractor uses 4,000 units at a prevailing rate of 0.10 per unit charged to the Government and pays 400. That rate-based charge follows the codified rule [@CL-CMP-4887], and under the RFO text the contractor pays for all utility costs unless the contract provides otherwise [@CL-CMP-0693].

## Never confuse

The Government's possession or use shall not be deemed an acceptance of any work under the contract [@CL-CMP-5058]. Suspension of work is covered in [Suspension and Government delay of work](cmp-suspension-and-government-delay-of-work.md). While the Government has possession or use, the contractor is relieved of responsibility for the loss of or damage to the work resulting from that possession or use [@CL-CMP-4884], and an equitable adjustment is made if it delays the progress of the work or causes additional expense [@CL-CMP-4885]. Unless otherwise provided in the contract, the amount of each utility service consumed is charged to or paid for by the Contractor [@CL-CMP-4887]. The codified clause sets the utility charge at prevailing rates charged to the Government or reasonable rates determined by the contracting officer and requires the contractor to conserve utilities furnished without charge [@CL-CMP-4887; @CL-CMP-5062], while RFO FAR 52.236-14(b) says that, unless otherwise provided in the contract, the contractor pays for all utility costs [@CL-CMP-0693]. The codified paragraph (c) requires the contractor to protect curbs and sidewalks from damage and to repair or pay for the repair of any damaged curbs, sidewalks or roads [@CL-CMP-5055], but RFO FAR 52.236-10(c) ends after the vehicle loading rule [@CL-CMP-0685]. The codified paragraph (a) covers operations, including storage of materials, on Government premises [@CL-CMP-4882], while the RFO paragraph (a) covers activities and operations on site [@CL-CMP-0680].
