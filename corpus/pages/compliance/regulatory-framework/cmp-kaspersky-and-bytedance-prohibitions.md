## Purpose

Shows what the Kaspersky Lab clause and the ByteDance covered application clause prohibit, what the Kaspersky clause requires a contractor to report and by when, and how each clause flows down.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-3563] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.204-23; FAR 52.204-27 [@CL-CMP-3563; @CL-CMP-3567] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.240-91 [@CL-CMP-1372; @CL-CMP-1373; @CL-CMP-1374; @CL-CMP-1376; @CL-CMP-1382]; RFO FAR 52.204-23 and RFO FAR 52.204-27 are reserved [@CL-CMP-1370; @CL-CMP-1371] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-3563] |
| Applies to | Contractors under contracts that include either clause [@CL-CMP-3563; @CL-CMP-3567] |

The contractor is prohibited from providing any Kaspersky Lab covered article that the Government will use on or after October 1, 2018 [@CL-CMP-3563]. It is also prohibited from using any Kaspersky Lab covered article on or after October 1, 2018, in the development of data or deliverables first produced in the performance of the contract [@CL-CMP-3564].

Under the ByteDance clause, the contractor is prohibited from having or using a covered application on any information technology owned or managed by the Government, or on any information technology used or provided by the Contractor under the contract, including equipment provided by the Contractor's employees, subject to a written-notification exception [@CL-CMP-3567]. That prohibition does not apply if the Contracting Officer provides written notification to the Contractor that an exception has been granted in accordance with OMB Memorandum M-23-13 [@CL-CMP-3568].

## How it works

If the contractor identifies a Kaspersky Lab covered article provided to the Government during contract performance, or is notified of such by a subcontractor at any tier or any other source, it shall report in writing, to the Contracting Officer or, in the case of the Department of Defense, to the website at https://dibnet.dod.mil [@CL-CMP-3593; @CL-CMP-8539]. One listed Kaspersky reporting deadline requires the contractor to report, within 3 business days from the date of identification or notification, the contract number, the order number or numbers if applicable, the supplier name and the brand, among the listed items [@CL-CMP-3565]. Within 10 business days of submitting the report, the contractor shall report any further available information about mitigation actions undertaken or recommended [@CL-CMP-3594]. The contractor shall insert the substance of the Kaspersky clause, including its subcontracts paragraph, in all subcontracts, including subcontracts for the acquisition of commercial products or commercial services [@CL-CMP-3566]. The ByteDance clause has the same flowdown wording for all subcontracts, including commercial products or commercial services [@CL-CMP-3569]. The reporting deadlines of related prohibition clauses are on [Covered telecommunications: the offeror representations and the contractor reports](cmp-covered-telecommunications-representation-and-reporting.md) and [FASCSA order clauses: representation, search and reporting](cmp-fascsa-order-representation-search-and-reporting.md).

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text from February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. The RFO text reserves FAR 52.204-23 [@CL-CMP-1370] and FAR 52.204-27 [@CL-CMP-1371], and its clause at FAR 52.240-91 lists a Kaspersky Lab-covered article [@CL-CMP-1374] and a covered application on any information technology owned or managed by the Government, or used or provided by the Contractor under the contract, including equipment provided by the Contractor's employees [@CL-CMP-1373]. Under that clause, unless a waiver or exception applies, the Contractor is prohibited from providing any products or services to the Government or using in the performance of the contract any of the listed items [@CL-CMP-1372]. The Kaspersky item prints neither the October 1, 2018 date, nor the words "that the Government will use", nor the limit to data or deliverables first produced in the performance of the contract that the codified text prints [@CL-CMP-1374; @CL-CMP-3563; @CL-CMP-3564]. The lead-in refers to "a waiver or exception" and does not mention written notification by the Contracting Officer or OMB Memorandum M-23-13, which the codified ByteDance clause names [@CL-CMP-1372; @CL-CMP-1373; @CL-CMP-3568]. The codified ByteDance clause prohibits having or using a covered application [@CL-CMP-3567], while the RFO lead-in prohibits providing products or services to the Government or using the listed items in the performance of the contract and does not print "having" [@CL-CMP-1372; @CL-CMP-1373].

The RFO report is the Contractor's duty and is due in writing within 72 hours, if the Contractor identifies, or is notified by any source including a subcontractor at any tier, that any product or service provided or used during contract performance does not comply with any prohibition in the clause [@CL-CMP-1375; @CL-CMP-1376]. The codified Kaspersky report is instead tied to a covered article provided to the Government, and the Contractor's 3 business days run from the date of the identification or notification [@CL-CMP-3593; @CL-CMP-3565]. The RFO report includes the contract number and order number, if applicable [@CL-CMP-1377], a description of the products or services that includes the brand [@CL-CMP-1378], and any readily available information about mitigation actions implemented or recommended [@CL-CMP-1379]. If the report lacks information that the contractor later discovers, the contractor's subsequent report is due within 72 hours of discovering the new information [@CL-CMP-1380; @CL-CMP-1381], where the codified text requires the Contractor to report further mitigation information within 10 business days of submitting the report [@CL-CMP-3594]. The RFO subcontract paragraph requires the Contractor to insert the substance of the clause, including that paragraph but excluding subparagraphs (d)(1) and (i)(1), in all subcontracts and other contractual instruments, including subcontracts for acquiring commercial products or commercial services [@CL-CMP-1382], where each codified flowdown requires insertion of the substance of the clause in all subcontracts, including subcontracts for the acquisition of commercial products or commercial services [@CL-CMP-3566; @CL-CMP-3569].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor finds that a software tool in a deliverable comes from a Kaspersky Lab covered entity. It reports the contract number, order number, supplier name and brand within 3 business days of finding it. Under the RFO text a report of that finding is due within 72 hours [@CL-CMP-1375; @CL-CMP-1376]. Separately, an employee's own phone, which the contractor uses under the contract, has a covered application on it. The contractor treats the phone as within the ByteDance prohibition unless the Contracting Officer has given written notice of an exception.

## Never confuse

The Kaspersky prohibition covers providing a covered article the Government will use and using one in developing contract data or deliverables [@CL-CMP-3563; @CL-CMP-3564]. The ByteDance prohibition covers having or using a covered application on information technology, including employee equipment used for the contract [@CL-CMP-3567]. The ByteDance prohibition has a written-exception route [@CL-CMP-3568]. The broader telecommunications prohibitions are on [Covered telecommunications equipment or services: definitions and prohibition](../trade-cyber/cmp-covered-telecommunications-definitions-and-prohibition.md).

The codified Kaspersky clause sets a report within 3 business days and a further report within 10 business days [@CL-CMP-3565; @CL-CMP-3594], while the RFO text sets 72 hours for the report and for a subsequent report; the 10 business days run on the Contractor from submitting the report, and the subsequent 72 hours run on the contractor from discovering the new information [@CL-CMP-3594; @CL-CMP-1381].
