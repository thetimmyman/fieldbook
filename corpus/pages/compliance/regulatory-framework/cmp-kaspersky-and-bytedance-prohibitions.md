## Purpose

Shows what the Kaspersky Lab clause and the ByteDance covered application clause prohibit, what the Kaspersky clause requires a contractor to report and by when, and how each clause flows down.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-3563] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.204-23; FAR 52.204-27 [@CL-CMP-3563; @CL-CMP-3567] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-3563] |
| Applies to | Contractors under contracts that include either clause [@CL-CMP-3563; @CL-CMP-3567] |

The contractor is prohibited from providing any Kaspersky Lab covered article that the Government will use on or after October 1, 2018 [@CL-CMP-3563]. It is also prohibited from using any Kaspersky Lab covered article on or after October 1, 2018, in the development of data or deliverables first produced in the performance of the contract [@CL-CMP-3564].

Under the ByteDance clause, the contractor is prohibited from having or using a covered application on any information technology owned or managed by the Government, or on any information technology used or provided by the Contractor under the contract, including equipment provided by the Contractor's employees, subject to a written-notification exception [@CL-CMP-3567]. That prohibition does not apply if the Contracting Officer provides written notification to the Contractor that an exception has been granted in accordance with OMB Memorandum M-23-13 [@CL-CMP-3568].

## How it works

If the contractor identifies a Kaspersky Lab covered article provided to the Government during contract performance, or is notified of such by a subcontractor at any tier or any other source, it shall report in writing [@CL-CMP-3593]. One listed Kaspersky reporting deadline requires the contractor to report, within 3 business days from the date of identification or notification, the contract number, the order number or numbers if applicable, the supplier name and the brand, among the listed items [@CL-CMP-3565]. Within 10 business days of submitting the report, the contractor shall report any further available information about mitigation actions undertaken or recommended [@CL-CMP-3594]. The Kaspersky clause also names the recipient of the report and the further items to describe with the second report, and this card does not set those out. The contractor shall insert the substance of the Kaspersky clause, including its subcontracts paragraph, in all subcontracts, including subcontracts for the acquisition of commercial products or commercial services [@CL-CMP-3566]. The ByteDance clause has the same flowdown wording for all subcontracts, including commercial products or commercial services [@CL-CMP-3569]. The reporting deadlines of related prohibition clauses are on [Covered telecommunications: the offeror representations and the contractor reports](cmp-covered-telecommunications-representation-and-reporting.md) and [FASCSA order clauses: representation, search and reporting](cmp-fascsa-order-representation-search-and-reporting.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor finds that a software tool in a deliverable comes from a Kaspersky Lab covered entity. It reports the contract number, order number, supplier name and brand within 3 business days of finding it. Separately, an employee's own phone, which the contractor uses under the contract, has a covered application on it. The contractor treats the phone as within the ByteDance prohibition unless the Contracting Officer has given written notice of an exception.

## Never confuse

The Kaspersky prohibition covers providing a covered article the Government will use and using one in developing contract data or deliverables [@CL-CMP-3563; @CL-CMP-3564]. The ByteDance prohibition covers having or using a covered application on information technology, including employee equipment used for the contract [@CL-CMP-3567]. The ByteDance prohibition has a written-exception route [@CL-CMP-3568]. The broader telecommunications prohibitions are on [Covered telecommunications equipment or services: definitions and prohibition](../trade-cyber/cmp-covered-telecommunications-definitions-and-prohibition.md).
