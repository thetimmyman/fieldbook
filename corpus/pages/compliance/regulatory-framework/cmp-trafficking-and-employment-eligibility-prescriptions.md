## Purpose

Shows where the trafficking clause and its compliance plan certification provision go, and which solicitations and contracts must carry the employment eligibility clause. The contracting officer applies these rules when preparing a solicitation. The clause and provision text is on the linked pages.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-6811] |
| Codified citation | FAR 22.1705 and FAR 22.1803 [@CL-CMP-6811; @CL-CMP-6815] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-6811] |
| Applies to | Solicitations and contracts, with a $700,000 test for the certification provision and a $150,000 test for the employment eligibility clause [@CL-CMP-6813; @CL-CMP-6815] |

Under FAR 22.1705, the clause at FAR 52.222-50 is inserted in all solicitations and contracts [@CL-CMP-6811]. Alternate I of the clause is used when the contract will be performed outside the United States, as defined at FAR 22.1702, and the contracting officer has been notified of specific United States directives or notices regarding combating trafficking in persons [@CL-CMP-6812]. The directives or notices that call for Alternate I are those that apply to contractor employees at the contract place of performance, such as general orders or military listings of off-limits local establishments [@CL-CMP-6992].

The provision at FAR 52.222-56 is inserted in solicitations if it is possible that at least $700,000 of the value of the contract may be performed outside the United States, which is one of two conditions that must both be met [@CL-CMP-6813]. The second condition is that the acquisition is not entirely for commercially available off-the-shelf items [@CL-CMP-6814].

Under FAR 22.1803, the clause at FAR 52.222-54 is inserted in all solicitations and contracts that exceed $150,000, except those that are only for work that will be performed outside the United States, those that are for a period of performance of less than 120 days, or those described in paragraph (c) of the section [@CL-CMP-6815]. Paragraph (c) names acquisitions that are only for commercially available off-the-shelf items or only for items that would be commercially available off-the-shelf items but for minor modifications (as defined at paragraph (3)(ii) of definition of “commercial product” at 2.101) [@CL-CMP-6816; @CL-CMP-7050]. Its next alternative is acquisitions only for items that would be commercially available off-the-shelf items if they were not bulk cargo [@CL-CMP-6993]. Its last alternative is acquisitions only for commercial services that are part of the purchase of a commercially available off-the-shelf item or an item that would be one but for minor modifications, are performed by the provider of that item, and are normally provided for that item [@CL-CMP-6994].

## How it works

The prescriptions say what to insert in each solicitation and contract. The trafficking clause has no threshold or exception in the prescription, and it goes into all solicitations and contracts [@CL-CMP-6811]. Alternate I is a second step that needs both a place of performance outside the United States and a notice to the contracting officer of specific directives or notices that apply to contractor employees there [@CL-CMP-6812; @CL-CMP-6992].

The certification provision is narrower and needs both conditions, a possibility that at least $700,000 of the value may be performed outside the United States and an acquisition that is not entirely for commercially available off-the-shelf items [@CL-CMP-6813; @CL-CMP-6814]. If either condition fails, the prescription does not call for the provision [@CL-CMP-6813; @CL-CMP-6814].

The employment eligibility clause needs a value above $150,000 and then drops out for any one of the exceptions: work only outside the United States, a period of performance under 120 days, or an acquisition only for the listed commercially available off-the-shelf cases [@CL-CMP-6815; @CL-CMP-6816; @CL-CMP-6993; @CL-CMP-6994]. Each off-the-shelf exception is stated for an acquisition that is only for the items or services it describes [@CL-CMP-6816; @CL-CMP-6993; @CL-CMP-6994]. The exception does not fit an acquisition that is also for something outside what it describes [@CL-CMP-6815; @CL-CMP-6816; @CL-CMP-6994].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contracting officer prepares a solicitation for a 900,000 services contract. Up to 800,000 of the value may be performed in a foreign country, and the work is not entirely for commercially available off-the-shelf items. The trafficking clause goes in [@CL-CMP-6811]. The certification provision goes in because it is possible that at least $700,000 may be performed outside the United States and the acquisition is not entirely for commercially available off-the-shelf items [@CL-CMP-6813; @CL-CMP-6814]. The work will in fact be performed in a foreign country, and the contracting officer has also been notified of specific United States directives that apply to contractor employees at the place of performance, so the clause is used with Alternate I [@CL-CMP-6812; @CL-CMP-6992].

The same contract has a 200-day period of performance and includes some work in the United States. Its value exceeds $150,000, it is not only for work outside the United States, its period of performance is not under 120 days, and it is not within the off-the-shelf exceptions, so the employment eligibility clause goes in [@CL-CMP-6815; @CL-CMP-6816].

A second contract is worth 400,000, all of it for work performed in the United States, with a period of performance of 90 days. The trafficking clause still goes in [@CL-CMP-6811]. The employment eligibility clause does not, because the period of performance is less than 120 days [@CL-CMP-6815].

## Never confuse

The trafficking clause goes in all solicitations and contracts, while the certification provision depends on the two conditions [@CL-CMP-6811; @CL-CMP-6813; @CL-CMP-6814]. Alternate I is a version of the clause, not a separate clause, and it depends on performance outside the United States plus a notice to the contracting officer [@CL-CMP-6812].

The $700,000 test is about the value that may be performed outside the United States, not the whole contract value [@CL-CMP-6813]. The $150,000 test is about the contract value [@CL-CMP-6815].
