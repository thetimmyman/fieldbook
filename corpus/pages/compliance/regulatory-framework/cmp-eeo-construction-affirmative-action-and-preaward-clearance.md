## Purpose

Shows how affirmative action goals are set for construction and when the contracting officer must ask the Office of Federal Contract Compliance Programs for a preaward clearance.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-3056] |
| Codified citation | FAR 22.804-1, FAR 22.804-2 and FAR 22.805 [@CL-CMP-3056; @CL-CMP-3058; @CL-CMP-7789; @CL-CMP-8061; @CL-CMP-8062] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-3056] |
| Applies to | Covered geographical areas and construction trades for the goals listing, and contracts and subcontracts of $10 million or more, excluding construction, for preaward clearance [@CL-CMP-3056; @CL-CMP-3057; @CL-CMP-3058] |

Each agency shall maintain a listing of covered geographical areas that are subject to affirmative action requirements that specify goals for minorities and women in covered construction trades [@CL-CMP-3056]. The printed text of that section states no goal figure. Any contracting officer contemplating a construction project in excess of $10,000 within a geographic area not known to be covered by specific affirmative action goals shall request instructions on the most current information from the regional office of the Office of Federal Contract Compliance Programs (OFCCP), or as otherwise specified in agency regulations [@CL-CMP-3126]. Contracting officers shall give written notice to the OFCCP regional office within 10 working days of award of a construction contract subject to these affirmative action requirements [@CL-CMP-3127].

The paragraph on preaward clearances is headed as covering contracts and subcontracts of $10 million or more, excluding construction [@CL-CMP-3057]. Except as provided in the paragraphs it names, if the estimated amount of the contract or subcontract is $10 million or more, the contracting officer shall request clearance from the appropriate OFCCP regional office before the events listed next [@CL-CMP-3058]. The listed events include award of any contract, including any indefinite delivery contract or letter contract, and modification of an existing contract for new effort that would constitute a contract award [@CL-CMP-3059].

The contracting officer does not need to request a preaward clearance if all the conditions the paragraph lists are met [@CL-CMP-3060; @CL-CMP-3061]. One listed condition is that the specific proposed contractor is listed in OFCCP's National Preaward Registry [@CL-CMP-3060]. The list also includes that the projected award date is within 24 months of the contractor's Notice of Compliance completion date in the Registry, and that the contracting officer documents the Registry review in the contract file [@CL-CMP-3061].

In processing a preaward clearance request, the contracting officer shall assure, if possible, that the request is submitted to the OFCCP regional office at least 30 days before the proposed award date [@CL-CMP-3128]. OFCCP will inform the awarding agency within 15 days of the clearance request of its intention to conduct a preaward compliance evaluation, and if it does not inform the agency within that period, clearance shall be presumed [@CL-CMP-3062]. If OFCCP informs the awarding agency of its intention to conduct a preaward compliance evaluation, OFCCP shall be allowed an additional 20 days after the date that it so informs the awarding agency to provide its conclusions [@CL-CMP-3129].

If the procedures in the two paragraphs on time for evaluation and OFCCP response would delay award of an urgent and critical contract beyond the time necessary to make award or beyond the time specified in the offer or extension thereof, the contracting officer shall immediately inform the OFCCP regional office [@CL-CMP-3130]. If the regional office advises that a preaward evaluation cannot be completed by the required date, the contracting officer shall submit written justification for the award to the head of the contracting activity, who, after informing the OFCCP regional office, may then approve the award without the preaward clearance [@CL-CMP-3131; @CL-CMP-7088].

Except as provided in 22.807, each nonconstruction prime contractor and each subcontractor with 50 or more employees and either a contract or subcontract of $50,000 or more, or Government bills of lading that in any 12-month period total, or can reasonably be expected to total, $50,000 or more, is required to develop a written affirmative action program for each of its establishments [@CL-CMP-7789; @CL-CMP-8061]. Each contractor and subcontractor shall develop its written affirmative action programs within 120 days from the commencement of its first such Government contract, subcontract, or Government bill of lading [@CL-CMP-8062].

## How it works

The goals mechanism is an agency listing of covered areas and trades, and the printed text gives no goal figure [@CL-CMP-3056]. The preaward check is a request to the regional office before award, or before a modification that would constitute a contract award [@CL-CMP-3058; @CL-CMP-3059]. The window for OFCCP to respond is short, and silence within the window leads to a presumed clearance [@CL-CMP-3062]. An urgent and critical contract has its own path through the head of the contracting activity [@CL-CMP-3130; @CL-CMP-3131]. The section also sets out what the request must contain, where to send it when the work is performed abroad, the use of enforcement procedures after a postaward finding of noncompliance, and a duty to furnish a poster, and this card does not set those out.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contracting officer plans to award a supply contract estimated at 12 million, which is not construction. The proposed contractor is not in the registry. The contracting officer sends the clearance request 45 days before the planned award, and OFCCP says nothing within 15 days of the request. The answer: clearance is presumed. Had the estimate been 8 million, this clearance request would not apply.

## Never confuse

The clearance for contracts of $10 million or more excludes construction, and the construction goals listing is a separate mechanism [@CL-CMP-3056; @CL-CMP-3057]. Wage rules for construction contracts are a different subject. See [Construction wage rate requirements: withholding from and suspension of contract payments](cmp-construction-wage-withholding-and-suspension.md). The terms used here are defined on [Equal employment opportunity: definitions and what the Executive Order requires](cmp-eeo-definitions-and-general-rules.md), and the exemptions that decide which contracts are covered are on [Equal employment opportunity exemptions and the related provisions and clauses](cmp-eeo-exemptions-and-clauses.md).
