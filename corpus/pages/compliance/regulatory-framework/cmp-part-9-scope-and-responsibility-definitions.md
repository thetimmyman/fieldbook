## Purpose

Explains what the contractor qualifications part covers, the two definitions used in the responsibility subpart, and which proposed contracts the subpart does and does not reach.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-7966] |
| Codified citation | FAR 9.000 [@CL-CMP-7966; @CL-CMP-8443], FAR 9.101 [@CL-CMP-7967; @CL-CMP-7970] and FAR 9.102 [@CL-CMP-7971; @CL-CMP-7972] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-7966] |
| Applies to | Proposed contracts with prospective contractors, subject to the location rules and exclusions [@CL-CMP-7971; @CL-CMP-7972] |

The part prescribes policies, standards and procedures pertaining to prospective contractors' responsibility [@CL-CMP-7966]. It also prescribes policies, standards and procedures pertaining to debarment, suspension and ineligibility; qualified products; first article testing and approval; contractor team arrangements; defense production pools and research and development pools; and organizational conflicts of interest [@CL-CMP-8443].

An administrative proceeding is a non-judicial process that is adjudicatory in nature in order to make a determination of fault or liability [@CL-CMP-7967]. It includes administrative proceedings at the Federal and state level, but only in connection with performance of a Federal contract or grant [@CL-CMP-7968]. Examples are Securities and Exchange Commission Administrative Proceedings, Civilian Board of Contract Appeals Proceedings and Armed Services Board of Contract Appeals Proceedings [@CL-CMP-8444]. It does not include agency actions such as contract audits, site visits, corrective plans or inspection of deliverables [@CL-CMP-7969].

As used in the subpart that holds these definitions, a surveying activity is the cognizant contract administration office or, if there is no such office, another organization designated by the agency to conduct preaward surveys [@CL-CMP-7970].

The responsibility subpart applies to all proposed contracts with any prospective contractor located in the United States or its outlying areas, or elsewhere unless application of the subpart would be inconsistent with the laws or customs where the contractor is located [@CL-CMP-7971]. It does not apply to proposed contracts with foreign, State or local governments, other United States Government agencies or their instrumentalities, or agencies for people who are blind or severely disabled [@CL-CMP-7972].

## How it works

The part has a wider scope than the subpart that holds the definitions and the applicability rule. The part covers the responsibility of prospective contractors and also the other subjects listed above, while the applicability section governs only the subpart [@CL-CMP-7966; @CL-CMP-8443; @CL-CMP-7971].

Under the applicability rule the contractor's location decides whether the subpart reaches a proposed contract: in the United States or its outlying areas it applies, and elsewhere it applies unless it would be inconsistent with the laws or customs where the contractor is located [@CL-CMP-7971]. The text does not say who judges that inconsistency [@CL-CMP-7971].

Separately, the subpart does not apply to proposed contracts with the listed kinds of counterparties [@CL-CMP-7972].

An administrative proceeding must be a non-judicial adjudicatory process that determines fault or liability, and a Federal or state proceeding counts only when it relates to performance of a Federal contract or grant [@CL-CMP-7967; @CL-CMP-7968]. An agency audit, site visit, corrective plan or inspection of deliverables is outside the definition [@CL-CMP-7969].

The agency designates the organization that conducts preaward surveys only when there is no cognizant contract administration office [@CL-CMP-7970].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A prospective contractor is located in the United States, so the subpart applies to the proposed contract with it [@CL-CMP-7971]. A second prospective contractor is located abroad, where applying the subpart would be inconsistent with the local laws or customs, so the subpart does not apply to that proposed contract [@CL-CMP-7971]. A proposed contract with a State government is outside the subpart [@CL-CMP-7972].

The first contractor has a cognizant contract administration office, so that office is the surveying activity. If it had none, the surveying activity would be another organization that the agency designates to conduct preaward surveys [@CL-CMP-7970].

An agency site visit is an agency action and is not within the definition of an administrative proceeding [@CL-CMP-7969].

## Never confuse

An administrative proceeding is not an agency action [@CL-CMP-7967; @CL-CMP-7969]. Contract audits, site visits, corrective plans and inspection of deliverables are outside the definition [@CL-CMP-7969].

The part is not the subpart: the part scope lists several subjects, while the applicability rule and the exclusions apply to proposed contracts under the responsibility subpart [@CL-CMP-8443; @CL-CMP-7971; @CL-CMP-7972].
