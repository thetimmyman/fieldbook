## Purpose

Shows the performance standards that reach contractors, including early exchanges with industry, and how the Government uses voluntary survey feedback.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-6872] |
| Codified citation | FAR 1.102-2 and 1.102-3 [@CL-CMP-6872; @CL-CMP-6875] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-6872] |
| Applies to | Contractors and prospective contractors, and the Government acquisition personnel who deal with them [@CL-CMP-6873; @CL-CMP-6874] |

When selecting contractors to provide products or perform services, the Government will use contractors who have a track record of successful past performance or who demonstrate a current superior ability to perform [@CL-CMP-6872]. The Government will maximize its use of commercial products and commercial services in meeting Government requirements [@CL-CMP-7168]. It is the policy of the System to promote competition in the acquisition process [@CL-CMP-7169]. All members of the Team are required to employ planning as an integral part of the overall process of acquiring products or services, and although advance planning is required, each member must be flexible in order to accommodate changing or unforeseen mission needs [@CL-CMP-7170]. The System must support the attainment of public policy goals adopted by the Congress and the President [@CL-CMP-7174].

## How it works

The Government must not hesitate to communicate with industry as early as possible in the acquisition cycle to help determine the capabilities available in the marketplace [@CL-CMP-7167]. Government acquisition personnel are permitted and encouraged to engage in responsible and constructive exchanges with industry [@CL-CMP-6873], so long as those exchanges are consistent with existing laws and regulations and do not promote an unfair competitive advantage to particular firms [@CL-CMP-7104].

Not only must the System have integrity, but the actions of each member of the Team must reflect integrity, fairness and openness [@CL-CMP-7171]. Fairness and openness require open communication among team members, internal and external customers, and the public [@CL-CMP-7172]. All contractors and prospective contractors must be treated fairly and impartially but need not be treated the same [@CL-CMP-6874]. The Government must exercise discretion, use sound business judgment, and comply with applicable laws and regulations in dealing with them [@CL-CMP-7173].

Agencies are encouraged to develop internal procedures seeking voluntary feedback from interested parties in an acquisition, to assess process strengths and weaknesses and improve the effectiveness and efficiency of the process [@CL-CMP-7175]. One of the things agencies may do is utilize a variety of feedback mechanisms available to the public, such as surveys, in-person exchanges and group exchanges [@CL-CMP-7176]. Contracting officers are encouraged to insert the voluntary survey provision in accordance with agency procedures, and contracting officers shall not review information until after contract award and shall not consider it in the award decision [@CL-CMP-6875].

For a contractor, exchanges with Government acquisition personnel are permitted and encouraged so long as they are consistent with existing laws and regulations and do not promote an unfair competitive advantage to particular firms [@CL-CMP-6873; @CL-CMP-7104].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

Before a solicitation issues, an agency holds an industry day, which is an early exchange with industry permitted and encouraged by the guiding principles [@CL-CMP-6873]. A supplier asks whether a 5-year term is contemplated. The agency answers publicly and shares the answer with every interested firm, which fits the condition that exchanges not promote an unfair competitive advantage to particular firms [@CL-CMP-7104]. The solicitation includes the voluntary survey provision. The supplier later loses the award and answers the survey. The contracting officer reads the survey only after contract award and does not consider it in the award decision [@CL-CMP-6875].

## Never confuse

Fair and impartial treatment is not identical treatment: contractors and prospective contractors must be treated fairly and impartially but need not be treated the same [@CL-CMP-6874]. Contracting officers shall not review information until after contract award and shall not consider it in the award decision [@CL-CMP-6875].
