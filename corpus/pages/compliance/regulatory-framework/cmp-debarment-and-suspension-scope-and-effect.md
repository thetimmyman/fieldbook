## Purpose

Shows what the debarment and suspension subpart covers, and how a debarment, suspension or other Governmentwide exclusion is recognized across the Executive Branch and the nonprocurement system.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-8230] |
| Codified citation | FAR 9.400 and 9.401 [@CL-CMP-8230; @CL-CMP-8235] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-8230] |
| Applies to | Debarments, suspensions, proposed debarments and other Governmentwide exclusions initiated on or after August 25, 1995 under the Nonprocurement Common Rule or the subpart [@CL-CMP-8235; @CL-CMP-8236] |

The subpart prescribes policies and procedures governing the debarment and suspension of contractors by agencies for the causes given in 9.406-2 and 9.407-2 [@CL-CMP-8230]. It provides for the listing of contractors debarred, suspended, proposed for debarment and declared ineligible, and it sets forth the consequences of that listing [@CL-CMP-8231; @CL-CMP-8232]. The subpart does cover the listing of ineligible contractors (9.404) and the effect of that listing (9.405) [@CL-CMP-8510]. It does not prescribe policies and procedures governing declarations of ineligibility, except for contractors declared ineligible under section 983 of title 10 of the United States Code [@CL-CMP-8233]. For Federal Acquisition Supply Chain Security Act orders, the section directs the reader to subpart 4.23 [@CL-CMP-8234].

Two recognition rules apply. A debarment, suspension or other Governmentwide exclusion initiated under the Nonprocurement Common Rule implementing Executive Order 12549 on or after August 25, 1995 shall be recognized by and effective for Executive Branch agencies as a debarment or suspension under this subpart [@CL-CMP-8235]. A debarment, suspension, proposed debarment or other Governmentwide exclusion initiated on or after August 25, 1995 under this subpart shall also be recognized by and effective for those agencies and participants as an exclusion under the Nonprocurement Common Rule [@CL-CMP-8236].

## How it works

The causes for debarment and for suspension are on the [causes for debarment](../../supply-chain/supplier-identity/sup-causes-for-debarment.md) and [causes for suspension](../../supply-chain/supplier-identity/sup-causes-for-suspension.md) cards, and what a listing does is on the [exclusion records and effect of listing](../../supply-chain/supplier-identity/sup-sam-exclusion-records-and-effect-of-listing.md) card. The recognition rules are duties of Executive Branch agencies, and of participants in the second rule, and they run in both directions [@CL-CMP-8235; @CL-CMP-8236]. The first rule covers an exclusion initiated under the Nonprocurement Common Rule, and the second covers an exclusion initiated under this subpart, and the second also names a proposed debarment [@CL-CMP-8235; @CL-CMP-8236]. Both are keyed to the date the exclusion was initiated [@CL-CMP-8235; @CL-CMP-8236]. These two rules do not say who issues an exclusion; the [decision process](../../supply-chain/supplier-identity/sup-debarment-decision-process.md) card covers that.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An Executive Branch agency suspends a supplier under this subpart, and the suspension is initiated on or after August 25, 1995. A second Executive Branch agency asks whether the suspension reaches it. Answer: the suspension shall be recognized by and effective for those agencies and participants as an exclusion under the Nonprocurement Common Rule [@CL-CMP-8236]. In the other direction, a Governmentwide exclusion that a nonprocurement participant initiates under the Nonprocurement Common Rule on or after August 25, 1995 is effective for the second agency as a debarment or suspension under this subpart [@CL-CMP-8235]. What the second agency must then do about the supplier is set by the effect-of-listing card, not by these recognition rules.

## Never confuse

A declaration of ineligibility is not a debarment. The subpart covers the listing of ineligible contractors and the effect of that listing, but it does not prescribe the procedures for declaring a contractor ineligible, apart from the statutory exception [@CL-CMP-8510; @CL-CMP-8233]. Recognition is also not the same as the consequences of a listing: the consequences are a separate part of the subpart [@CL-CMP-8232].
