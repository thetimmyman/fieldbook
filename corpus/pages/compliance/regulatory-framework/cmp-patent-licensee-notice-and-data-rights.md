## Purpose

Explains the notice a solicitation gives about a Government royalty and how an offer is evaluated, the minimum rights the Government keeps in major system technical data, and the rights it takes in proposal technical data [@CL-CMP-5606; @CL-CMP-5749; @CL-CMP-5750].

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-5606; @CL-CMP-5749; @CL-CMP-5750] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.227-7, FAR 52.227-22 and FAR 52.227-23 [@CL-CMP-5606; @CL-CMP-5749; @CL-CMP-5750] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.227-7 is reserved [@CL-CMP-1823]; RFO FAR 52.227-23 is reserved [@CL-CMP-1824]; RFO FAR 52.227-22 [@CL-CMP-1825] |
| Edition date | FAR 52.227-7: APR 1984 [@CL-CMP-10254]; FAR 52.227-22: Jun 1987 [@CL-CMP-1825]; FAR 52.227-23: JUN 1987 [@CL-CMP-10255] |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-5606; @CL-CMP-5749; @CL-CMP-5750] |
| Applies to | Solicitations and contracts in which a provision or clause is inserted as prescribed [@CL-CMP-5606; @CL-CMP-5749; @CL-CMP-5750] |

The provision tells offerors that the Government is obligated to pay a royalty applicable to the proposed acquisition because of a license agreement between the Government and the patent owner, and the contracting officer fills in the patent number and the royalty rate [@CL-CMP-5607]. An offeror that is the owner of, or a licensee under, the patent indicates which, and if an offeror does not indicate that it is the owner or a licensee, its offer will be evaluated by adding an amount equal to the royalty [@CL-CMP-5748].

The minimum rights clause gives the Government unlimited rights in technical data, other than computer software, developed in the performance of the contract and relating to a major system or supplies for a major system procured or to be procured by the Government, to the extent that delivery of such data is required as an element of performance under the contract [@CL-CMP-5608; @CL-CMP-5609]. Notwithstanding any other provision of the contract, the Government has those rights [@CL-CMP-5751]. The rights of the Government under the clause are in addition to and not in lieu of its rights under the other provisions of the contract [@CL-CMP-5609].

## How it works

Under the proposal data clause, except for data contained on the identified pages, the Government has unlimited rights in the technical data as a condition of award, notwithstanding any notice appearing on the proposal [@CL-CMP-5610]. Those rights run to the technical data contained in the identified proposal on which the contract is based [@CL-CMP-5611]. Unlimited rights have the meaning given in the Rights in Data—General clause contained in the contract [@CL-CMP-5752]. The identified pages and the proposal date are blanks that differ by contract.

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text from February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. The RFO text prints 52.227-7 as "[Reserved]", so the codified provision on the Government licensee notice has no text under that number [@CL-CMP-5606] [@CL-CMP-1823]. It likewise prints 52.227-23 as "[Reserved]", so the codified clause on rights to proposal data has no text under that number [@CL-CMP-5750] [@CL-CMP-1824]. The RFO minimum rights clause, 52.227-22, says the same as the codified clause: unlimited rights in technical data, other than computer software, developed in the performance of the contract and relating to a major system or supplies for a major system procured or to be procured by the Government [@CL-CMP-5608] [@CL-CMP-1826], notwithstanding any other provision of the contract [@CL-CMP-5751] [@CL-CMP-1827], to the extent delivery is required as an element of performance and in addition to the Government's other rights [@CL-CMP-5609] [@CL-CMP-1828].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A solicitation tells offerors that the Government pays a royalty of 3 percent under a license, and the contracting officer has filled in the patent number and rate. An offeror proposes 2,000,000 and does not mark owner or licensee. The royalty applicable to the proposed acquisition comes to 60,000, so the offer is evaluated at 2,060,000.

A proposal for a 6,000,000 award has technical data on all 40 of its pages, and the clause identifies 5 pages as excluded. The Government takes unlimited rights in the technical data on the other 35 pages as a condition of award, whatever notice the proposal carries.

On a major system contract, drawings developed in performing the contract that the contract requires to be delivered come within the minimum rights clause, so the Government has unlimited rights in them. Working notes that the contract does not require the contractor to deliver do not come within that clause, although other provisions of the contract may still apply.

## Never confuse

Unlimited rights and no rights are not the only two outcomes. The minimum rights clause sets a floor for major system data that the contract requires to be delivered, and it adds to the Government's other rights rather than replacing them [@CL-CMP-5608; @CL-CMP-5609]. Proposal data rights attach as a condition of award and leave only the identified pages outside [@CL-CMP-5610]. The royalty notice and the data rights clauses deal with different subjects: how an offer is evaluated, and the Government's rights in technical data [@CL-CMP-5607; @CL-CMP-5748; @CL-CMP-5608; @CL-CMP-5610].

Of the three numbers on this card, the RFO text gives text only to the minimum rights clause; the royalty notice provision and the proposal data rights clause are printed as reserved under their numbers, where the codified text gives each its own rule [@CL-CMP-5607; @CL-CMP-5610] [@CL-CMP-1823] [@CL-CMP-1824] [@CL-CMP-1825].

The royalty information that an offeror gives with its offer is covered in [Royalty information in offers](cmp-royalty-information-provision.md), and the Rights in Data—General clause is covered in [Rights in data, general: definitions and allocation of rights](cmp-rights-in-data-general-definitions-and-allocation.md).
