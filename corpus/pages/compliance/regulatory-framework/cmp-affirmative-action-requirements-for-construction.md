## Purpose

Shows how the construction affirmative action clause turns solicitation goals into documented steps, records and consequences.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-5229] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.222-27 [@CL-CMP-5229] |
| RFO citation | Reserved in the RFO text: the RFO text lists RFO FAR 52.222-27 as reserved [@CL-CMP-0372] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-5229] |
| Applies to | Construction contracts that carry the clause, and construction-trade subcontracts over $10,000 at any tier [@CL-CMP-5229] |

The solicitation states goals as percentages of the total hours of employment and training of minority and female utilization that the contractor should reasonably be able to achieve in each construction trade in which it has employees in the covered area [@CL-CMP-5231]. The covered area is the geographical area described in the solicitation for the contract, and the Deputy Assistant Secretary is the Deputy Assistant Secretary for the Office of Federal Contract Compliance Programs at the Department of Labor, or a designee [@CL-CMP-5780]. The first group within the definition of minority is American Indian or Alaskan Native, meaning all persons having origins in any of the original peoples of North America and maintaining identifiable tribal affiliations through membership and participation or community identification [@CL-CMP-5810]. The second and third groups are Asian and Pacific Islander, meaning all persons having origins in any of the original peoples of the Far East, Southeast Asia, the Indian Subcontinent or the Pacific Islands, and Black, meaning all persons having origins in any of the black African racial groups not of Hispanic origin [@CL-CMP-5811]. The fourth group is Hispanic, meaning all persons of Mexican, Puerto Rican, Cuban, Central or South American, or other Spanish culture or origin, regardless of race [@CL-CMP-5812]. If the contractor performs construction work in a geographical area outside the covered area, it applies the goals established for the area where that work is actually performed [@CL-CMP-5232]. The contractor is expected to make substantially uniform progress toward its goals in each craft [@CL-CMP-5791]. A single goal for minorities and a separate single goal for women must be established, and the contractor must provide equal employment opportunity and take affirmative action for all minority groups, both male and female, and all women, both minority and nonminority [@CL-CMP-5368]. The contractor may violate Executive Order 11246, as amended, if a particular group is employed in a substantially disparate manner [@CL-CMP-5369].

The contractor must take affirmative action to ensure equal employment opportunity, and its compliance with the clause is evaluated on its effort to achieve maximum results from its actions [@CL-CMP-5340]. It must document its efforts fully and implement affirmative action steps at least as extensive as the listed steps [@CL-CMP-5341], and it must implement the procedures in the clause's listed steps (g)(1) through (16) [@CL-CMP-5230].

## How it works

Each subcontract over $10,000, at any tier, for work involving a construction trade must include the clause and the notice that states the minority and female participation goals [@CL-CMP-5229]. The contractor must not enter into a subcontract with any person or firm debarred from Government contracts under Executive Order 11246, as amended [@CL-CMP-5371]. It must not use goals or affirmative action standards to discriminate against any person because of race, color, religion, sex, sexual orientation, gender identity or national origin [@CL-CMP-5370]. Gender identity has the meaning given by the Department of Labor's Office of Federal Contract Compliance Programs, and is found at www.dol.gov/ofccp/LGBT/LGBT_FAQs.html [@CL-CMP-5813]. Sexual orientation has the meaning given by the Department of Labor's Office of Federal Contract Compliance Programs, and is found at www.dol.gov/ofccp/LGBT/LGBT_FAQs.html [@CL-CMP-5814].

Neither the terms of a collective bargaining agreement nor a union's failure to refer minorities or women excuses the contractor's obligations under this clause [@CL-CMP-5337], and a union's failure to refer also does not excuse its obligations under Executive Order 11246, as amended, or the regulations thereunder [@CL-CMP-6016]. Nonworking training hours of apprentices and trainees count toward the goals only if the apprentices and trainees are employed by the contractor during the training period [@CL-CMP-5338] and the contractor has committed to employ them when training ends, subject to the availability of employment opportunities, with trainees trained in programs the Department of Labor has approved [@CL-CMP-5339].

The listed steps are these.

- Step 1: the contractor must ensure a working environment free of harassment, intimidation and coercion at all sites and in all facilities where its employees are assigned to work, and if possible will assign two or more women to each construction project [@CL-CMP-5342]. Foremen, superintendents and other onsite supervisors must be aware of and carry out that obligation, with specific attention to minority or female individuals working at those sites [@CL-CMP-5343].
- Step 2: the contractor must establish and maintain a current list of sources for minority and female recruitment [@CL-CMP-5344]. It must give those sources and community organizations written notification when it or its unions have employment opportunities, and keep a record of their responses [@CL-CMP-5345].
- Step 3: the contractor must keep a current file of the names, addresses and telephone numbers of each minority and female off-the-street applicant, referrals of minorities or females from unions, recruitment sources or community organizations, and the action taken on each person [@CL-CMP-5233]. If an individual sent to the union hiring hall is not referred back by the union or, if referred back, is not employed, the file must record that along with the additional actions taken [@CL-CMP-5346].
- Step 4: the contractor must immediately notify the Deputy Assistant Secretary when a union with which it has a collective bargaining agreement has not referred back a minority or woman whom the contractor sent [@CL-CMP-5347]. The same immediate notice is due when it has other information that the union referral process has impeded its efforts to meet its obligations [@CL-CMP-5348].
- Step 5: the contractor must develop on-the-job training opportunities or take part in area training programs that expressly include minorities and women, including upgrading, apprenticeship and trainee programs relevant to its employment needs [@CL-CMP-5349]. It should especially use programs funded or approved by the Department of Labor, and it must give notice of these programs to the sources compiled under step 2 [@CL-CMP-5350].
- Step 6: the contractor must disseminate its equal employment policy, including by giving notice of it to unions and to training, recruitment and outreach programs and requesting their cooperation [@CL-CMP-5351], by putting it in any policy manual and in collective bargaining agreements and publicizing it in the company newspaper and annual report [@CL-CMP-5352], by reviewing it with all management personnel and all minority and female employees at least once a year [@CL-CMP-5353], and by posting it on bulletin boards at each location where construction work is performed [@CL-CMP-5354].
- Step 7: the contractor must review its equal employment policy and affirmative action obligations, at least annually, with all employees who have responsibility for hiring, assignment, layoff, termination or other employment decisions [@CL-CMP-5355]. It must also review the policy with all on-site supervisory personnel before starting construction work at a job site, and must keep a written record of the time and place of these meetings, the persons attending, the subject matter discussed and its disposition [@CL-CMP-5356].
- Step 8: the contractor must include its equal employment policy in any advertising in the news media, specifically including minority and female news media [@CL-CMP-5357]. It must also give written notification of the policy to, and discuss it with, other contractors and subcontractors with which it does or anticipates doing business [@CL-CMP-5358].
- Step 9: the contractor must direct oral and written recruitment efforts to minority, female and community organizations, to schools with minority and female students, and to minority and female recruitment and training organizations serving its recruitment area and employment needs [@CL-CMP-5359]. Not later than 1 month before the date for acceptance of applications for apprenticeship or training by any recruitment source, it must send those organizations written notification describing the openings, screening procedures and tests to be used [@CL-CMP-5360].
- Step 10: the contractor must encourage present minority and female employees to recruit minority persons and women, and where reasonable provide after-school, summer and vacation employment to minority and female youth both on the site and in other areas of its workforce [@CL-CMP-5361].
- Step 11: the contractor must validate all tests and other selection requirements where 41 CFR 60-3 requires it [@CL-CMP-5362].
- Step 12: the contractor must conduct, at least annually, an inventory and evaluation of at least all minority and female personnel for promotional opportunities, and must encourage these employees to seek or prepare for promotion through appropriate training [@CL-CMP-5363].
- Step 13: the contractor must ensure that seniority practices, job classifications, work assignments and other personnel practices have no discriminatory effect, by continually monitoring all personnel and employment-related activities to ensure that its obligations under the contract are being carried out [@CL-CMP-5364].
- Step 14: the contractor must ensure that all facilities and company activities are nonsegregated, except that separate or single-user rest rooms and necessary dressing or sleeping areas must be provided to assure privacy between the sexes [@CL-CMP-5365].
- Step 15: the contractor must maintain a record of solicitations for subcontracts for minority and female construction contractors and suppliers, including circulation of solicitations to minority and female contractor associations and other business associations [@CL-CMP-5366].
- Step 16: the contractor must conduct a review, at least annually, of all supervisors' adherence to and performance under its equal employment policy and affirmative action obligations [@CL-CMP-5367].

The contractor must designate a responsible official to monitor all employment-related activity so that its equal employment policy is carried out and to submit reports the Government may require [@CL-CMP-5234]. That official must keep records that include, for each employee, at least the name, address, telephone number, construction trade, union affiliation if any, employee identification number, social security number, race and sex [@CL-CMP-5234; @CL-CMP-5373], and also status, dates of changes in status, hours worked per week in the indicated trade, rate of pay and the locations at which the work was performed [@CL-CMP-5374]. The records must be maintained in an easily understandable and retrievable form, but separate records are not required to the degree that existing records satisfy the requirement [@CL-CMP-5375].

The contractor must carry out such sanctions and penalties for violation of the clause and the Equal Opportunity clause, including suspension, termination and cancellation of existing subcontracts [@CL-CMP-5235]. Those sanctions are imposed or ordered under the Executive Order and its implementing regulations by the OFCCP, and a failure to carry them out as ordered is a violation of the clause and the Executive Order [@CL-CMP-5372]. The contractor, in fulfilling its obligations under the clause, must implement affirmative action procedures at least as extensive as those prescribed in paragraph (g), so as to achieve maximum results from its efforts to ensure equal employment opportunity [@CL-CMP-5789]. If the contractor fails to comply with Executive Order 11246, its implementing regulations or the clause, the Deputy Assistant Secretary must take the action that 41 CFR 60-4.8 prescribes [@CL-CMP-5236].

A contractor participating, individually or through an association, in a Hometown Plan (41 CFR 60-4) approved by the Department of Labor in a covered area must make its affirmative action obligations on all work in the plan area, including goals, comply with the plan, but that duty applies to those trades that have unions participating in the plan [@CL-CMP-5781; @CL-CMP-5782]. Contractors must be able to demonstrate participation in, and compliance with, the provisions of the plan [@CL-CMP-5782]. Each contractor or subcontractor participating in an approved plan must also comply with its obligations under the Equal Opportunity clause and make a good faith effort to achieve each goal under the plan in each trade in which it has employees [@CL-CMP-5783]. The overall good-faith performance by other contractors or subcontractors toward a goal in an approved plan does not excuse any contractor's or subcontractor's failure to make good-faith efforts to achieve the plan's goals [@CL-CMP-5784].

The contractor is encouraged to participate in voluntary associations that may assist in fulfilling one or more of the affirmative action obligations contained in steps (g)(1) through (16) of the clause [@CL-CMP-5809]. The efforts of a contractor association, joint contractor-union, contractor-community or similar group of which the contractor is a member and participant may be asserted as fulfilling one or more of its obligations under steps (g)(1) through (16) of the clause, provided the contractor meets the conditions listed next [@CL-CMP-5785]. The first three conditions are that the contractor actively participates in the group, makes every effort to ensure that the group has a positive impact on the employment of minorities and women in the industry, and ensures that concrete benefits of the program are reflected in its minority and female workforce participation [@CL-CMP-5786]. The fourth and fifth conditions are that the contractor makes a good-faith effort to meet its individual goals and timetables and can provide access to documentation that demonstrates the effectiveness of actions taken on its behalf [@CL-CMP-5787]. The obligation to comply is the contractor's, and failure of such a group to fulfill an obligation is not a defense for the contractor's noncompliance [@CL-CMP-5788].

Nothing in the clause is to be construed as a limitation upon the application of other laws that establish different standards of compliance or upon the requirements for the hiring of local or other area residents [@CL-CMP-5790].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text under Class Deviation 2026-O0038, effective February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. The RFO text lists FAR 52.222-27 as reserved [@CL-CMP-0372].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A solicitation states a goal of 20 percent female utilization in the carpentry trade for the covered area. By the end of a quarter the contractor has logged 2,000 hours of carpentry employment and training, 300 of them worked by women. That is 15 percent, 100 hours short of the 400 hours the goal implies. The shortfall alone is not the test: the clause evaluates the contractor on its documented effort, so the contractor keeps its list of recruitment sources and its applicant file, sends written notice of openings to those sources and opens training places. When a union with which the contractor has a collective bargaining agreement does not refer back a woman whom the contractor sent to the hiring hall, the contractor records that in its file and immediately notifies the Deputy Assistant Secretary. Two apprentices' nonworking classroom hours count toward the goal because the contractor employs them during training and has committed to employ them when training ends, subject to the availability of employment opportunities. The contractor is expected to make substantially uniform progress toward its goals in each craft, so it tracks carpentry and each other craft separately. The contractor also belongs to a contractor-union group; if the group fails to fulfill an obligation, that failure is not a defense for the contractor's noncompliance. A construction subcontract of 9,000 dollars is below the 10,000 dollar flowdown test, and one of 12,000 dollars exceeds it.

## Never confuse

The solicitation states the goals as percentages of the total hours of employment and training [@CL-CMP-5231]. Compliance is evaluated on the contractor's effort to achieve maximum results, and the contractor must document that effort fully [@CL-CMP-5340; @CL-CMP-5341]. A union's failure to refer minorities or women does not excuse the contractor's obligations under the clause [@CL-CMP-5337].

The codified FAR 52.222-27 carries the construction affirmative action clause, while the RFO text prints that section number as reserved [@CL-CMP-5229; @CL-CMP-0372].

The segregated facilities prohibition, the construction notice of goals and the preaward evaluation provision are covered in [Segregated facilities, construction goals and preaward equal opportunity evaluation](cmp-segregated-facilities-construction-affirmative-action.md).
