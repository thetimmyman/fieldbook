## Purpose

Shows the warranty against contingent fees, the clause that carries it, the Government's remedy, and who decides what to do about a suspected breach.

## The rule

| Field | Value |

| Codified citation | FAR 3.400 [@CL-CMP-9701]; FAR 3.402, FAR 3.404 and FAR 3.405 [@CL-CMP-2031; @CL-CMP-2033; @CL-CMP-2034]; FAR 3.406 [@CL-CMP-9702; @CL-CMP-9703] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-2033] |
| Applies to | Solicitations and contracts exceeding the simplified acquisition threshold, other than those for commercial products or commercial services [@CL-CMP-2033] |

Contractors' arrangements to pay contingent fees for soliciting or obtaining Government contracts have long been considered contrary to public policy, because such arrangements may lead to attempted or actual exercise of improper influence [@CL-CMP-2030]. The statutes cited in the section require in every negotiated contract a warranty by the contractor against contingent fees, and permit, as an exception to the warranty, contingent fee arrangements between contractors and bona fide employees or bona fide agencies [@CL-CMP-2031]. The same statutes also provide that, for breach or violation of the warranty by the contractor, the Government may annul the contract without liability, or deduct from the contract price or consideration, or otherwise recover, the full amount of the contingent fee [@CL-CMP-2031; @CL-CMP-2032]. A contingent fee means any commission, percentage, brokerage, or other fee that is contingent upon the success that a person or concern has in securing a Government contract [@CL-CMP-2105].

The contracting officer shall insert the Covenant Against Contingent Fees clause in all solicitations and contracts exceeding the simplified acquisition threshold, other than those for commercial products or commercial services [@CL-CMP-2033]. For the current threshold value, see [Simplified acquisition and micro-purchase thresholds](cmp-acquisition-thresholds.md).

## How it works

The chief of the contracting office decides: when there is specific evidence or other reasonable basis to suspect a violation the section describes, that officer shall review the facts and, if appropriate, take or direct one or more of the listed or other actions [@CL-CMP-2034]. One listed action, if before award, is to reject the bid or proposal [@CL-CMP-2034; @CL-CMP-2035]. One listed action, if after award, is to enforce the Government's right to annul the contract or to recover the fee [@CL-CMP-2034; @CL-CMP-2036]. A finance reader checks whether the contract carries the clause [@CL-CMP-2033], whether any fee depends on winning the contract, and whether the payee is a bona fide employee or bona fide agency [@CL-CMP-2031]. The definitions section of the subpart defines a bona fide employee and a bona fide agency, but this card does not set out their full wording.

The subpart prescribes policies and procedures that restrict contingent fee arrangements for soliciting or obtaining Government contracts to those permitted by section 3321(b)(1) of title 10 and section 3901 of title 41 of the United States Code [@CL-CMP-9701]. For enforcement purposes, agencies shall preserve any specific evidence of one or more of the violations in FAR 3.405(a), together with all other pertinent data, including a record of actions taken [@CL-CMP-9702]. Contracting offices shall not retire or destroy these records until it is certain that they are no longer needed for enforcement purposes, and if the original record is maintained in a central file, a copy must be retained in the contract file [@CL-CMP-9703].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor agrees to pay a consultant a success fee of 100,000 if it wins a contract worth 5 million that carries the covenant clause. Suppose the consultant is neither a bona fide employee nor a bona fide agency. After award, the Government may annul the contract without liability or recover the full 100,000 fee. The chief of the contracting office reviews the facts and decides which action to take.

## Never confuse

The statutes require the warranty in every negotiated contract [@CL-CMP-2031], and the subpart applies to all contracts, with the statutory requirements for negotiated contracts extended to sealed bid contracts as a matter of policy [@CL-CMP-2106; @CL-CMP-2107]. The clause prescription is narrower: it covers solicitations and contracts exceeding the simplified acquisition threshold, other than those for commercial products or commercial services [@CL-CMP-2033]. For breach or violation of the warranty by the contractor, the Government may annul the contract without liability, or deduct from the contract price or consideration, or otherwise recover, the full amount of the contingent fee [@CL-CMP-2032].
