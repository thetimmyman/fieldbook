## Purpose

Shows who the deployed-forces clause covers, what emergency medical care and other support the Government gives, what the contractor must supply and comply with, and what must happen before personnel deploy. It does not set out every duty in the clause, and each section below says what it leaves out.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-CMP-4149] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 252.225-7040 [@CL-CMP-4149] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-4149] |
| Applies to | Contracts that include the clause, for CAAF and non-CAAF performing in a designated operational area outside the United States in one of the listed operations [@CL-CMP-4149; @CL-CMP-4150] |

The clause applies to both CAAF and non-CAAF when they perform in a designated operational area outside the United States to support United States Armed Forces deployed outside the United States [@CL-CMP-4149]. The operations are alternatives: contingency operations [@CL-CMP-4149]; peace operations consistent with Joint Publication 3-07.3 [@CL-CMP-4150]; or other military operations or military exercises when designated by the Combatant Commander or as directed by the Secretary of Defense [@CL-CMP-4150].

CAAF are contractor personnel, including all tiers of subcontractor personnel, who are authorized to accompany the forces in applicable operations and have CAAF status through a letter of authorization [@CL-CMP-4151]. Non-CAAF are personnel not designated as CAAF, such as local national employees and non-local-national employees who are permanent residents in the operational area or third-country nationals not routinely residing with the forces, and the definition also names third-country national expatriates who are permanent residents in the operational area; non-CAAF personnel perform support functions away from the close proximity of, and do not reside with, the forces [@CL-CMP-4152; @CL-CMP-8540].

Unless the contract provides otherwise, the Contractor accepts the risks of required contract performance in these operations, which may involve dangerous or austere conditions [@CL-CMP-4153]. Unless specified elsewhere in the contract, the Contractor is responsible for all other support required for its personnel engaged in the designated operational area [@CL-CMP-4156].

Generally, CAAF will be afforded emergency medical and dental care if injured while supporting applicable operations, and non-CAAF employees injured while in the vicinity of United States Armed Forces will normally receive emergency medical and dental care [@CL-CMP-4199]. Medical or dental care beyond the emergency care standard in that paragraph is not authorized [@CL-CMP-4199; @CL-CMP-4200]. When the Government provides medical treatment or transportation of Contractor personnel to a selected civilian facility, the Contractor must ensure that the Government is reimbursed for any costs associated with the treatment or transportation [@CL-CMP-4154].

The Contractor must comply with, and ensure that its personnel supporting United States Armed Forces deployed outside the United States as specified in the clause's scope paragraph are familiar with and comply with, all applicable items in the list that follows, the first of which is United States, host country, and third country national laws [@CL-CMP-4157]. The list also includes United States regulations, directives, instructions, policies, and procedures, and orders, directives, and instructions issued by the Combatant Commander, including those relating to force protection, security, health, safety, or relations and interaction with local nationals [@CL-CMP-4209]. The Contractor must institute and implement an effective program to prevent violations of the law of war by its employees and subcontractors, including law of war training [@CL-CMP-4158].

The Contractor must report to the appropriate investigative authorities identified in the clause any alleged offenses under the listed laws, the first of which is the Uniform Code of Military Justice [@CL-CMP-4159]. The clause describes that code as applicable to contractors serving with or accompanying an armed force in the field during a declared war or contingency operations, and the other listed law is the Military Extraterritorial Jurisdiction Act [@CL-CMP-4210].

The Contractor must ensure its employees are aware of their listed rights, which include holding their own identity or immigration documents and receiving agreed upon wages on time [@CL-CMP-4160]. It must post these rights in employee work spaces in English and in any foreign language spoken by a significant portion of the workforce, and it must enforce the rights of Contractor personnel supporting the forces [@CL-CMP-4208]. For the Contractor's duty to inform its employees in writing, in the predominant native language of the workforce, of contractor employee whistleblower rights and protections [@CL-CMP-4191], see [Informing employees of whistleblower rights and the agency Inspector General](cmp-whistleblower-rights-notice-and-agency-inspector-general.md).

## How it works

The clause defines two groups: CAAF, who have CAAF status through a letter of authorization, and non-CAAF, who are personnel not designated as CAAF [@CL-CMP-4151; @CL-CMP-4152].

Personnel must have a letter of authorization generated by the personnel tracking system and signed by the Contracting Officer in order to process through a deployment center or to travel to, from, or within the designated operational area [@CL-CMP-4155].

Before CAAF deploy, the Contractor ensures the listed requirements are met, with specific requirements for each category set in the statement of work or elsewhere in the contract [@CL-CMP-4161]. The first is that all required security and background checks are complete and acceptable [@CL-CMP-4161]. The list also includes that all CAAF deploying in support of an applicable operation are medically, dentally, and psychologically fit for deployment and performance of their contracted duties [@CL-CMP-4205]; that all deploying personnel have received personal security training that, at a minimum, covers safety and security issues facing employees overseas and identifies safety and security contingency planning activities, among other items the clause lists [@CL-CMP-4206]; that all personnel have received isolated personnel training, if specified in the contract [@CL-CMP-4207]; and that personnel have received law of war training, for which basic training is required for all CAAF deployed outside the United States [@CL-CMP-4204].

The Contractor must identify all personnel who occupy a position designated as mission essential [@CL-CMP-4201]. It must also ensure the continuity of essential Contractor services during designated operations, unless, after consultation with the Contracting Officer, Contracting Officer's representative, or local commander, the Contracting Officer directs withdrawal due to security conditions [@CL-CMP-4213].

This card does not set out every duty in the clause. It leaves out, among other things, the advanced law of war training, how basic training is provided, the security plan and other support terms, the notices to personnel, the processing and departure points, the removal of personnel, and the vehicle licence rule. The clause text controls. For weapons, clothing, evacuation and flowdown see [the companion card on weapons, evacuation and flowdown](cmp-deployed-personnel-weapons-evacuation-and-flowdown.md). Private security work has its own clause, covered on [Defense contractors performing private security functions outside the United States](cmp-private-security-functions-outside-the-united-states.md).

The Contractor must use the tracking system to enter and maintain the data for all CAAF and, as designated by the Under Secretary of Defense (Acquisition and Sustainment) or the Combatant Commander, for non-CAAF [@CL-CMP-4162]. It must enter the required information about its contractor personnel prior to deployment and continue to use the system to maintain accurate, up-to-date information throughout the deployment for all Contractor personnel [@CL-CMP-4163].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor sends ten engineers to a designated operational area. Eight hold letters of authorization signed by the Contracting Officer. Two do not.

The two without letters may not process through the deployment center or travel to, from, or within the area until they have one. If the Government later provides medical treatment or transportation of one of the engineers to a selected civilian facility, the contractor must ensure that the Government is reimbursed for the costs.

## Never confuse

The letter of authorization is needed to process through a deployment center or to travel to, from, or within the designated operational area [@CL-CMP-4155]. Before deploying CAAF, the Contractor must also ensure that the listed requirements, beginning with complete and acceptable security and background checks, are met [@CL-CMP-4161].

The clause applies to both CAAF and non-CAAF [@CL-CMP-4149]. The lead-in to the pre-deployment requirements speaks of deploying CAAF [@CL-CMP-4161].

Medical or dental care beyond the emergency care standard is not authorized [@CL-CMP-4199; @CL-CMP-4200], and the Contractor must ensure reimbursement of the Government for treatment or transportation it provides to a selected civilian facility [@CL-CMP-4154].
