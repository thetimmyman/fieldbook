## Purpose

Shows who may not serve in which roles on a DoD contract after a fraud or felony conviction, for how long, and what the Contractor risks if it lets them.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-CMP-4069] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 252.203-7001 [@CL-CMP-4069] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-4069] |
| Applies to | Contracts that include the clause, used as prescribed in DFARS 203.570-3 [@CL-CMP-4069] |

An individual convicted after September 29, 1988 of fraud or any other felony arising out of a contract with the DoD is prohibited from serving in a management or supervisory capacity on this contract and on the board of directors of the Contractor [@CL-CMP-4070]. Such an individual is also prohibited from serving as a consultant, agent, or representative for the Contractor, and from serving in any other capacity with the authority to influence, advise, or control the Contractor's decisions on this contract [@CL-CMP-4070; @CL-CMP-4071].

The clause defines arising out of a contract with the DoD as any act in connection with attempting to obtain, obtaining, or performing a contract or first-tier subcontract of any agency, department, or component of the Department of Defense [@CL-CMP-4117]. It defines conviction of fraud or any other felony as any conviction for fraud or a felony in violation of state or Federal criminal statutes, whether entered on a verdict or plea, including a plea of nolo contendere, for which sentence has been imposed [@CL-CMP-4118].

Unless waived, the prohibition applies for not less than 5 years from the date of conviction [@CL-CMP-4072]. The Contractor may submit written requests for waiver of the prohibition in paragraph (b) of the clause to the Contracting Officer [@CL-CMP-4119].

The Contractor shall be subject to a criminal penalty of not more than $500,000 if convicted of knowingly employing a person under a prohibition specified in paragraph (b) of the clause, or of allowing such a person to serve on the board of directors of the contractor or first-tier subcontractor [@CL-CMP-4073]. In addition to the criminal penalties contained in title 10 of the United States Code, section 4656, the Government may consider other available remedies, such as suspension or debarment, cancellation of the contract at no cost to the Government, or termination of the contract for default [@CL-CMP-4074].

The Contractor agrees to include the substance of this clause, appropriately modified, in all first-tier subcontracts exceeding the simplified acquisition threshold in part 2 of the Federal Acquisition Regulation, except those for commercial products, commercial services, or commercial components [@CL-CMP-4075; @CL-CMP-4076]. The dollar amount of that threshold is on [Acquisition thresholds](cmp-acquisition-thresholds.md).

## How it works

A qualifying conviction bars the person from the listed roles on the contract [@CL-CMP-4070]. The bar runs for not less than 5 years from the date of conviction, unless it is waived [@CL-CMP-4072], and the Contractor may ask the Contracting Officer for a waiver in writing [@CL-CMP-4119]. The clause definitions say what a qualifying conviction is: one tied to an act connected with attempting to obtain, obtaining, or performing a contract or first-tier subcontract of a DoD component, and one for which sentence has been imposed [@CL-CMP-4117; @CL-CMP-4118]. The Contractor faces a criminal penalty if it is convicted of knowingly employing a barred person, and the Government may also consider suspension or debarment, cancellation or default termination [@CL-CMP-4073; @CL-CMP-4074]. The Contractor passes the substance of the clause to first-tier subcontracts above the simplified acquisition threshold, other than commercial ones [@CL-CMP-4075; @CL-CMP-4076].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A person was convicted of fraud committed while performing a first-tier subcontract of a DoD component, and a sentence was imposed. The Contractor wants that person to act as a consultant on the contract 3 years after the date of judgment.

Answer: a consultant role is a listed capacity, and 3 years is less than the 5-year minimum, so the person may not serve unless a waiver has been granted.

## Never confuse

The role bar is not the criminal penalty: the bar applies to an individual convicted after September 29, 1988, of fraud or any other felony arising out of a contract with the DoD [@CL-CMP-4070], and suspension or debarment, cancellation of the contract at no cost to the Government, and termination of the contract for default are other remedies the Government may consider [@CL-CMP-4074]. The compensation rule for former DoD officials is on [the former DoD official compensation card](cmp-former-dod-official-compensation.md).
