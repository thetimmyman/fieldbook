## Purpose

Explains what a contracting officer must do before award where the prospective contractor is one to which award is otherwise prohibited, and before award to an organization owned or controlled by Government employees.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-7653] |
| Codified citation | FAR 3.603 [@CL-CMP-7653] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-7653] |
| Applies to | Contracting officers, before they award a contract [@CL-CMP-7653; @CL-CMP-7655] |

Paragraph (a): before awarding a contract, the contracting officer shall obtain an authorization under 3.602 if two conditions both hold [@CL-CMP-7653; @CL-CMP-7654]. The first is that the contracting officer knows, or has reason to believe, that a prospective contractor is one to which award is otherwise prohibited under 3.601 [@CL-CMP-7653]. The second is that there is a most compelling reason to make an award to that prospective contractor [@CL-CMP-7654].

Paragraph (b): the contracting officer shall comply with the requirements and guidance in subpart 9.5 before awarding a contract to an organization owned or substantially owned or controlled by Government employees [@CL-CMP-7655].

## How it works

Both duties fall on the contracting officer and come before award [@CL-CMP-7653; @CL-CMP-7655].

The first duty applies when both conditions in paragraph (a) hold. If the contracting officer lacks the knowledge or reason to believe, or there is no most compelling reason, paragraph (a) does not itself require an authorization [@CL-CMP-7653; @CL-CMP-7654]. Section 3.602 names who may authorize the exception: the agency head, or a designee not below the level of the head of the contracting activity, may authorize an exception to the 3.601 policy only if there is a most compelling reason to do so, such as when the Government's needs cannot reasonably be otherwise met [@CL-CMP-7152].

The prohibition itself is in 3.601 [@CL-CMP-6862]. Except as specified in 3.602, a contracting officer must not knowingly award a contract to a Government employee or to a business concern or other organization owned or substantially owned or controlled by one or more Government employees [@CL-CMP-6862].

The second duty applies to an award to an organization owned or substantially owned or controlled by Government employees [@CL-CMP-7655]. Subpart 9.5 is the subpart on organizational conflicts of interest [@CL-CMP-8238]. The section does not say what the requirements and guidance of that subpart contain; see [Where the organizational conflicts of interest rules apply](cmp-organizational-conflicts-applicability.md). The section sets no deadline or dollar threshold and does not say how the contracting officer records either step.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contracting officer learns before award that the apparent successful offeror is wholly owned by two agency employees. The contracting officer must not knowingly award to that organization unless an exception is authorized under 3.602 [@CL-CMP-6862]. If the contracting officer concludes that there is a most compelling reason to award to it, the contracting officer shall obtain an authorization under 3.602 before award, and the agency head or a designee not below the level of the head of the contracting activity decides whether to authorize the exception [@CL-CMP-7653; @CL-CMP-7654; @CL-CMP-7152]. If the contracting officer awards to the organization after any authorization, the contracting officer shall also comply with the requirements and guidance in subpart 9.5 before the award [@CL-CMP-7655].

## Never confuse

An authorization under 3.602 and the subpart 9.5 step are two separate duties with two separate triggers [@CL-CMP-7653; @CL-CMP-7655]. The authorization responds to knowledge or reason to believe that award is otherwise prohibited, together with a most compelling reason to award [@CL-CMP-7653; @CL-CMP-7654]. The subpart 9.5 step responds to an organization being owned or substantially owned or controlled by Government employees [@CL-CMP-7655].

The contracting officer's duty is to obtain the authorization. The decision to authorize belongs to the agency head or the designee [@CL-CMP-7152]. For this subpart, special Government employees performing services as experts, advisors or consultants, or as members of advisory committees, are not considered Government employees unless one of three listed alternatives applies; see [Contracts with Government employees and mitigation of personal conflicts of interest](cmp-government-employee-contracts-and-personal-conflicts.md) [@CL-CMP-6864; @CL-CMP-7153].
