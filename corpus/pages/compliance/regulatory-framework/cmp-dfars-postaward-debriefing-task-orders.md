## Purpose

Explains what the DoD clause on postaward debriefings for task orders and delivery orders requires, who must act, the timetable for follow-up questions, and the protest windows in which the Government may suspend or terminate an order.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-CMP-9060] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 252.216-7010 [@CL-CMP-9060] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-9060] |
| Applies to | Task orders and delivery orders valued at $15 million or more, when the contractor makes a timely request [@CL-CMP-9060; @CL-CMP-9061; @CL-CMP-9270] |

Upon a timely request, the Government will provide a written or oral postaward debriefing to the contractor for a task order or delivery order valued at $15 million or more, whether the contractor's offer for the order was successful or unsuccessful [@CL-CMP-9060]. The Government provides it while protecting the confidential and proprietary information of other contractors [@CL-CMP-9062]. The duty to debrief falls on the Government, and the contractor must make a timely request to trigger it. The clause does not say who chooses between a written and an oral debriefing.

A request is considered timely if it is received within 3 days of notification of task order or delivery order award [@CL-CMP-9061]. The clause is used as prescribed at DFARS 216.506-70(b) [@CL-CMP-9270].

If a required postaward debriefing is provided, the rules on additional questions, the agency's written response and the conclusion of the debriefing apply [@CL-CMP-9271; @CL-CMP-9064; @CL-CMP-9065]. The debriefed contractor may submit additional written questions related to the debriefing within 2 business days after the date of the debriefing [@CL-CMP-9063]. The agency will respond in writing to timely submitted questions within 5 business days after receipt [@CL-CMP-9064]. The debriefing is not considered concluded until the later of two dates: the date it is delivered, orally or in writing, or, if additional written questions are timely received, the date the agency delivers its written response [@CL-CMP-9065].

The Government may suspend performance of, or terminate, the awarded task order or delivery order upon notice from the Government Accountability Office of a protest filed within the time periods listed in the clause, whichever is later [@CL-CMP-9066]. The first period is 10 days after the date the order is issued, where the value exceeds $25 million [@CL-CMP-9067]. The second is 5 days after a debriefing date offered to the protestor under a timely debriefing request in accordance with FAR 15.506, unless an earlier debriefing date is negotiated as a result [@CL-CMP-9068]. The third is 5 days after a postaward debriefing under FAR 15.506 is concluded in accordance with DFARS 215.506-70(b) [@CL-CMP-9069].

## How it works

The contractor starts the process by requesting a debriefing inside the window that runs from notification of award [@CL-CMP-9061]. The Government then debriefs, in writing or orally, and the contractor may follow up with written questions that the agency will answer in writing if they are timely submitted [@CL-CMP-9060; @CL-CMP-9063; @CL-CMP-9064]. The questions are the contractor's option; the written response to timely submitted questions is the agency's duty [@CL-CMP-9063; @CL-CMP-9064].

It is the Government that decides whether to suspend or terminate: the clause says it "may" do so on notice from the Government Accountability Office, and it names no approving official [@CL-CMP-9066]. Of the three protest periods, only the first depends on the value of the order, and it uses a higher threshold than the debriefing duty [@CL-CMP-9067; @CL-CMP-9060]. The other two periods carry no value test in the clause [@CL-CMP-9068; @CL-CMP-9069].

The third period is tied to a debriefing concluded in accordance with DFARS 215.506-70(b), and this card does not establish how that section defines conclusion [@CL-CMP-9069]. The clause's own rule on when its debriefing is concluded appears in the debriefing paragraph [@CL-CMP-9065].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An order valued at 12,000,000 is awarded to another offeror. The debriefing paragraph of this clause does not require a debriefing for it, because the duty applies to orders valued at $15 million or more [@CL-CMP-9060]. Other debriefing rules are covered in the linked cards.

A second order, valued at 30,000,000, is issued on day 0 and the contractor is notified of the award that day, and the contractor's request is received on day 2. The request is timely because it arrived within 3 days of the award notification [@CL-CMP-9061]. The Government must debrief in writing or orally, even if the contractor's offer was unsuccessful [@CL-CMP-9060]. The debriefing is delivered on day 8, and the contractor's additional written questions are received on day 9, within 2 business days after the date of the debriefing [@CL-CMP-9063]. The agency delivers its written response on day 14, within 5 business days after receipt [@CL-CMP-9064]. The debriefing is not concluded until that response date [@CL-CMP-9065].

For protest timing, the order value exceeds $25 million, so the first period ends on day 10 [@CL-CMP-9067]. If day 8 was the debriefing date offered, the second period ends on day 13 [@CL-CMP-9068]. Assume the debriefing is concluded on day 14 under DFARS 215.506-70(b); the third period then ends on day 19 [@CL-CMP-9069]. A protest filed on day 18 falls inside the third period, so the Government may suspend or terminate the order on notice from the Government Accountability Office [@CL-CMP-9066]. The clause leaves that choice to the Government [@CL-CMP-9066].

## Never confuse

This clause is not the general debriefing rule: it sets its own duty for task orders and delivery orders valued at $15 million or more and its own timetable [@CL-CMP-9060; @CL-CMP-9061]. The general rules are in [Postaward debriefing](../../estimating/pricing/est-postaward-debriefing.md) and [DFARS postaward debriefing at 15 million dollars and above](../../estimating/pricing/est-dfars-postaward-debriefing-15-million-and-above.md) [@CL-EST-2464]. The prescription and the ordering rules are in [DoD ordering under multiple award contracts](../../estimating/pricing/est-dod-ordering-multiple-award-contracts.md) and [DFARS indefinite delivery solicitation provisions and clause](../../market/programs/mkt-dfars-indefinite-delivery-solicitation-provisions-clause.md). The clause ties the Government's right to suspend or terminate to notice from the Government Accountability Office of a protest filed within the listed periods [@CL-CMP-9066]. The $15 million debriefing threshold [@CL-CMP-9060] and the $25 million threshold for the first protest period [@CL-CMP-9067] apply to different paragraphs of the clause.

## Company calibration

Whether this company holds task orders or delivery orders at or above these thresholds, and who tracks the award notice date, the request window and the protest periods. [@Q-004]
