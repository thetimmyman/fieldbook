## Purpose

Shows the contractor's duty to justify asserted restrictions on technical data, how the Government challenges them, the time limits, and what follows when the restriction is sustained or not justified.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-CMP-3793] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 252.227-7037 [@CL-CMP-3793] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-3793] |
| Applies to | Contractors and subcontractors at any tier that assert restrictions on technical data [@CL-CMP-3793] |

A contractor or subcontractor at any tier is responsible for maintaining records sufficient to justify the validity of its asserted restrictions on the rights of the Government and others to use, duplicate, release, or disclose technical data [@CL-CMP-3793]. If the Contracting Officer determines that a challenge to the asserted restriction is warranted, the Contracting Officer will send a written challenge notice to the Contractor or subcontractor making the asserted restriction [@CL-CMP-3794]. A challenge notice will state the specific grounds for challenging the asserted restriction, including, for commercial products or commercial services, sufficient information to reasonably demonstrate that the item was not developed exclusively at private expense [@CL-CMP-3826]. It will also require a response within 60 days justifying and providing sufficient evidence as to the current validity of the asserted restriction [@CL-CMP-3794; @CL-CMP-3826; @CL-CMP-3795]. The written response of the Contractor or subcontractor shall be considered a claim within the meaning of the Contract Disputes statute and shall be certified in the form prescribed in the Federal Acquisition Regulation, regardless of dollar amount [@CL-CMP-3801].

If the Contracting Officer determines that the Contractor or subcontractor has justified the validity of the asserted restriction, the Contracting Officer will issue a final decision that sustains the validity of the asserted restriction [@CL-CMP-3796]. If the Contracting Officer determines that the validity of the asserted restriction is not justified, the Contracting Officer will issue a final decision in accordance with the Disputes clause of the contract [@CL-CMP-3797]. The Government agrees to remain bound by the asserted restriction for 90 days from the issuance of that final decision [@CL-CMP-3797; @CL-CMP-3798]. If the Contractor or subcontractor fails to appeal, file suit, or provide a notice of intent to file suit within the 90-day period, the Government may cancel or ignore the restrictive markings that are based on the asserted restrictions [@CL-CMP-3799]. During the period within 6 years of final payment on a contract or within 6 years of delivery of the technical data, whichever is later, the Contracting Officer may review and make a written determination to challenge the restriction [@CL-CMP-3800]. The Government may, however, challenge a restriction on the release, disclosure, or use of technical data at any time if the data are publicly available or have been furnished to the United States without restriction [@CL-CMP-3827]. The same list of data that may be challenged at any time also includes technical data otherwise made available without restriction and technical data that are the subject of a fraudulently asserted use or release restriction [@CL-CMP-3827; @CL-CMP-3828].

## How it works

The contractor keeps the justifying records, and the Contracting Officer starts the process with a written challenge notice that requires a response within 60 days [@CL-CMP-3793; @CL-CMP-3794; @CL-CMP-3826; @CL-CMP-3795]. If the contractor justifies the restriction, a final decision sustains it [@CL-CMP-3796]. If the Contracting Officer finds the restriction not justified, a final decision follows under the Disputes clause, and the Government stays bound for 90 days after it [@CL-CMP-3797; @CL-CMP-3798]. If the contractor then does nothing within that period, the Government may cancel or ignore the markings [@CL-CMP-3799]. The Contracting Officer may review and challenge a restriction within 6 years of final payment or of delivery, whichever is later, with exceptions that allow a challenge at any time for listed kinds of data [@CL-CMP-3800; @CL-CMP-3827; @CL-CMP-3828].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor has marked a set of drawings with a restriction. The Contracting Officer sends a challenge notice, and the contractor responds with its funding records and the required certification. The Contracting Officer later finds the restriction not justified and issues a final decision. The Government stays bound by the restriction for 90 days after that decision. If the contractor appeals, sues, or gives notice of intent to sue within that time, the Government stays bound for a further period that the clause sets. If it does nothing, the Government may cancel or ignore the markings. Separately, if the drawings had been posted publicly without restriction, the Government could challenge the restriction even after the 6-year period.

## Never confuse

Assertion, marking and validation are different steps. The offeror lists restrictions with its offer [@CL-CMP-3790], as the card on [identification and assertion of restrictions](cmp-dod-assertion-of-data-restrictions.md) explains. The Contractor asserts a restriction on delivered data by marking it [@CL-CMP-3809], as the card on [markings](cmp-dod-technical-data-markings.md) explains. The Government challenges a restriction later under this clause [@CL-CMP-3794]. Validation of computer software restrictions has its own clause and procedure, covered on the card for [validation of asserted restrictions on computer software](cmp-dod-software-restriction-validation.md). A nonconforming marking is a different matter, because correction of nonconforming markings is not subject to this clause [@CL-CMP-3821]. The final decision is made under the Disputes clause; see [Disputes clause, claims and certification](../../close-revrec/revenue/cls-disputes-clause.md).
