## Purpose

Shows when the Government must let a telecommunications contractor into its own furnished facilities, and how the contractor acknowledges, charges for and is paid for ordered facilities and services.

## The rule

| Codified citation | DFARS 252.239-7002 and DFARS 252.239-7004 [@CL-CMP-4641; @CL-CMP-4645] |

Subject to military security regulations, the Government shall permit the Contractor access at all reasonable times to Contractor furnished facilities [@CL-CMP-4641]. If the Government is unable to permit access, it shall at its own risk and expense maintain these facilities [@CL-CMP-4642], and the Contractor shall not be responsible for the service involving any of them during the period of nonaccess unless the service failure results from the Contractor's fault or negligence [@CL-CMP-4643].

During periods when the Government does not permit access, the Government will reimburse the Contractor at mutually acceptable rates for the loss of or damage to the equipment due to the fault or negligence of the Government [@CL-CMP-4644]. Failure to agree on those rates shall be a dispute concerning a question of fact within the meaning of the Disputes clause [@CL-CMP-4708].

## How it works

The Contractor acknowledges a communication service authorization or other type order for supplies and facilities either by commencing performance after receipt of an order or by written acceptance by a duly authorized representative [@CL-CMP-4645]. The Contractor shall furnish the services and facilities in accordance with all applicable tariffs, rates, charges, regulations, requirements, terms, and conditions of service and facilities furnished or offered by the Contractor to the general public or the Contractor's subscribers, or of service as lawfully established by a governmental regulatory body [@CL-CMP-4698; @CL-CMP-4747]. The Government will not prepay for services [@CL-CMP-4646]. Recurring charges start with the satisfactory beginning of service or provision of facilities or equipment and are payable monthly in arrears [@CL-CMP-4647]. For nontariffed services, the Contractor shall charge the Government at the lowest rate and under the most favorable terms and conditions for similar service and facilities offered to any other customer [@CL-CMP-4648].

The Government shall pay expediting charges only when they are provided for in the tariff established by a governmental regulatory body or are authorized in a communication service authorization or other contractual document [@CL-CMP-4699]. Expediting charges are costs necessary to get services earlier than normal, such as overtime pay or special shipment, and when authorized they are the additional costs incurred by the Contractor and the subcontractor [@CL-CMP-4745]. Regulatory bodies whose decisions are not subject to judicial appeal, and regulatory bodies which regulate a company owned by the same entity that creates the regulatory body, are not governmental regulatory bodies [@CL-CMP-4748]. When services normally provided are technically unacceptable and special equipment must be developed, fabricated or manufactured, the Government may provide the equipment or direct the Contractor to acquire the equipment or facilities [@CL-CMP-4700]. If the Contractor acquires them, the acquisition shall be competitive, if practicable [@CL-CMP-4746]. If the Government defers or changes its orders for any of the services but does not cancel or terminate them, the amount paid or payable for the services deferred or modified shall be equitably adjusted [@CL-CMP-4701]. The adjustment is made under applicable tariffs filed by the Contractor with the regulatory commission in effect at the time of deferral or change, and if no tariffs are in effect the Government and the Contractor shall equitably adjust the rates by mutual agreement [@CL-CMP-4743]. Failure to agree on any adjustment is a dispute concerning a question of fact within the meaning of the Disputes clause [@CL-CMP-4744].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A carrier receives a written order for a leased circuit and starts work the same week. Starting work after receipt of the order is one way to acknowledge it. Service begins satisfactorily on the 20th of the month, so the monthly charge of 1,000 starts on that day. The carrier bills it at the end of each month, not in advance. Separately, the carrier asks to add 300 of overtime to get the circuit in earlier than normal. The Government pays that only if the tariff of a governmental regulatory body or the authorization provides for expediting charges, and then only the additional costs incurred. Separately, the Government defers one ordered circuit but does not cancel it. A tariff the carrier filed is in effect at the time of the deferral, so the amount payable for the deferred service is adjusted under that tariff; if no tariff were in effect, the carrier and the Government would equitably adjust the rates by mutual agreement.

## Never confuse

Under the Access clause, subject to military security regulations, the Government shall permit the Contractor access at all reasonable times to Contractor furnished facilities, and if it is unable to permit access it shall at its own risk and expense maintain these facilities [@CL-CMP-4641; @CL-CMP-4642]. Under the Orders for Facilities and Services clause, the Contractor shall acknowledge an order either by commencing performance after receipt of an order or by written acceptance by a duly authorized representative [@CL-CMP-4645], and recurring charges are payable monthly in arrears [@CL-CMP-4647]. For what the Government owes when it cancels or terminates an order, see [Cancellation or termination of orders](cmp-dfars-telecom-cancellation-or-termination-of-orders.md). For special construction charges, title and the term of the basic agreement, see [Special construction, title and term](cmp-dfars-telecom-special-construction-title-and-term.md). For the Government-wide ban on buying covered telecommunications equipment, see [Covered telecommunications equipment or services](../trade-cyber/cmp-covered-telecommunications-definitions-and-prohibition.md). Under the Access clause, failure to agree on the reimbursement rates is a dispute concerning a question of fact within the meaning of the Disputes clause [@CL-CMP-4708]; see [Disputes clause, claims and certification](../../close-revrec/revenue/cls-disputes-clause.md).
