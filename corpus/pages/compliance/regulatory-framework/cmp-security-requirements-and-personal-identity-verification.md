## Purpose

Shows when the Security Requirements clause and its alternates go into a contract, and the identity verification duties that attach to contractor personnel with routine access.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-7214] |
| Codified citation | FAR 4.404, 4.1301 and 4.1302 [@CL-CMP-7214; @CL-CMP-7218; @CL-CMP-7315] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-7214] |
| Applies to | Contracts that may require access to classified information, and contracts that require routine physical access to a Federally-controlled facility or routine access to a Federally-controlled information system [@CL-CMP-7214; @CL-CMP-7219] |

The contracting officer inserts the Security Requirements clause in solicitations and contracts when the contract may require access to classified information, unless the conditions specified in paragraph (d) of that section apply [@CL-CMP-7214]. For a contemplated cost contract for research and development with an educational institution, the contracting officer uses the clause with Alternate I [@CL-CMP-7215]. For a contemplated construction or architect-engineer contract where employee identification is required for security reasons, the contracting officer uses it with Alternate II [@CL-CMP-7216]. If the contracting agency is not covered by the National Industrial Security Program (NISP) and has prescribed a clause and alternates substantially the same as the standard clause, the contracting officer uses the agency-prescribed clause as required by agency procedures [@CL-CMP-7217].

Agencies must follow FIPS publication 201 and the associated OMB implementation guidance for personal identity verification for all affected contractor and subcontractor personnel [@CL-CMP-7218]. That duty applies when contract performance requires contractors to have routine physical access to a Federally-controlled facility, routine access to a Federally-controlled information system, or both [@CL-CMP-7311]. Agencies must include their implementation of that guidance in solicitations and contracts that require such access [@CL-CMP-7219]. Each agency must designate an official responsible for verifying contractor employee personal identity [@CL-CMP-7312].

## How it works

Agency procedures for the return of personal identity verification products must ensure that contractors account for all Government-provided identification issued to their employees under a contract, that is, the PIV cards or other similar badges [@CL-CMP-7313]. The procedures must also ensure that contractors return such identification to the issuing agency as soon as any of the listed events occurs, unless the agency determines otherwise [@CL-CMP-7220]. The events are when the identification is no longer needed for contract performance, upon completion of a contractor employee's employment, and upon contract completion or termination [@CL-CMP-7328].

The contracting officer may delay final payment under a contract if the contractor fails to comply with those requirements [@CL-CMP-7314].

To comply with FIPS publication 201, agencies must purchase only approved personal identity verification products and services [@CL-CMP-7315]. Agencies may acquire the approved products and services from the General Services Administration Federal Supply Schedule 70, Special Item Number 132-62 (HSPD-12 Product and Service Components), in accordance with the ordering procedures in FAR Subpart 8.4 [@CL-CMP-7316]. When an agency acquires them without using that process, it must ensure that they are approved as compliant with FIPS publication 201 [@CL-CMP-7317]. That includes certifying that the products and services procured meet all applicable Federal standards and requirements [@CL-CMP-7331]. It also includes ensuring interoperability and conformance to those standards for the lifecycle of the components, and maintaining a written plan for ongoing conformance for that lifecycle [@CL-CMP-7318].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An agency covered by the NISP solicits a cost contract for research and development with a university, and the contract may require access to classified information. The contracting officer inserts the Security Requirements clause with Alternate I [@CL-CMP-7214; @CL-CMP-7215]. The contract also requires the contractor's employees to have routine physical access to a Federally-controlled laboratory, so the agency includes its implementation of the identity verification guidance in the solicitation and contract [@CL-CMP-7219].

A contractor employee leaves the company. Under the agency's procedures the contractor returns the employee's badge to the issuing agency, because the employee's employment has ended, unless the agency determines otherwise [@CL-CMP-7220; @CL-CMP-7328]. If the contractor fails to comply with the return requirements, the contracting officer may delay final payment [@CL-CMP-7314].

## Never confuse

Access to a Federally-controlled facility is not the same as access to classified information. The Security Requirements clause is inserted when the contract may require access to classified information (unless the conditions in paragraph (d) apply), while the identity verification rules apply when performance requires routine physical access to a facility or routine access to an information system [@CL-CMP-7214; @CL-CMP-7311].
