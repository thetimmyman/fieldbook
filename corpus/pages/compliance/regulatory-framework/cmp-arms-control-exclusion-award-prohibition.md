## Purpose

Explains the award prohibition for entities excluded over arms control treaty violations, its route of supply exception, and the solicitation provision that the contracting officer includes unless the exception applies.

## The rule

Contracting officers shall not award, renew or extend a contract for the procurement of products or services with an entity identified as excluded in the System for Award Management specifically for this subpart [@CL-CMP-7973]. The exclusion is on the basis of involvement in activities that violate arms control treaties or agreements with the United States [@CL-CMP-7974].

The prohibition does not apply to contracts for the procurement of products or services along a major route of supply to a zone of active combat or major contingency operation, as specified in statute or by the cognizant Combatant Commander in consultation with the Chief of Mission [@CL-CMP-7975]. As of May 10, 2018, the countries along the major route of supply to support operations in Afghanistan are Afghanistan, Georgia, the Kyrgyz Republic, Pakistan, the Republic of Armenia, the Republic of Azerbaijan, the Republic of Kazakhstan, the Republic of Tajikistan, the Republic of Uzbekistan and Turkmenistan [@CL-CMP-8451; @CL-CMP-8445].

Unless the exception applies, the contracting officer shall include the provision at 52.209-13 [@CL-CMP-7976]. The provision goes in each solicitation for the acquisition of products or services, including construction, that exceeds the simplified acquisition threshold [@CL-CMP-7977]. Solicitations for the acquisition of commercial products or commercial services are excluded from that requirement [@CL-CMP-7978].

## How it works

The prohibition binds the contracting officer, who may not award, renew or extend the contract; the contractor's consequence is that an entity with the listing cannot receive any of those three actions [@CL-CMP-7973]. The prohibition turns on an entity identified as excluded in the System for Award Management specifically for this subpart, on the arms control ground [@CL-CMP-7973; @CL-CMP-7974].

The exception is stated for contracts along a major route of supply, and the route is specified in statute or by the cognizant Combatant Commander in consultation with the Chief of Mission [@CL-CMP-7975]. The listed countries are as of the stated date and relate to operations in Afghanistan [@CL-CMP-8445; @CL-CMP-8451].

The exception also removes the solicitation provision, because the contracting officer includes the provision unless the exception applies [@CL-CMP-7975; @CL-CMP-7976].

The prohibition is stated with no dollar threshold and no commercial carve-out; the threshold and the commercial carve-out appear in the provision requirement [@CL-CMP-7973; @CL-CMP-7977; @CL-CMP-7978].

The duty to include the provision falls on the contracting officer [@CL-CMP-7976]. The offeror's certification is covered in the linked card.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A supplier is identified in the System for Award Management as excluded specifically for this subpart, on the basis of involvement in activities that violate arms control treaties. Assume the contract is not along a major route of supply to a zone of active combat or major contingency operation. The contracting officer may not award it a new contract for products or services, renew its existing contract or extend it [@CL-CMP-7973; @CL-CMP-7974]. No dollar threshold limits that prohibition [@CL-CMP-7973].

Separately, the contracting officer issues a solicitation for non-commercial supplies expected to exceed the simplified acquisition threshold, and includes the provision at 52.209-13 [@CL-CMP-7976; @CL-CMP-7977; @CL-CMP-7978]. A solicitation for commercial services does not need the provision [@CL-CMP-7978].

If the contract is for services along a major route of supply to a zone of active combat, as specified by the cognizant Combatant Commander in consultation with the Chief of Mission, the prohibition does not apply and the contracting officer is not required to include the provision [@CL-CMP-7975; @CL-CMP-7976].

## Never confuse

The prohibition applies to an entity identified as excluded in the System for Award Management, specifically for this subpart, on the basis of involvement in activities that violate arms control treaties or agreements with the United States [@CL-CMP-7973; @CL-CMP-7974].

The provision is to be included in each solicitation that exceeds the simplified acquisition threshold, other than solicitations for the acquisition of commercial products or commercial services [@CL-CMP-7977; @CL-CMP-7978], while the prohibition is stated for any contract for the procurement of products or services with an entity identified as excluded [@CL-CMP-7973].
