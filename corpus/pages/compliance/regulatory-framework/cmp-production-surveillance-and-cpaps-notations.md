## Purpose

Shows when the cognizant contract administration office performs production surveillance, how it handles the contractor's report, and what the past performance evaluation in the Contractor Performance Assessment Reporting System (CPARS) must include. The duties in the first two parts fall on Government staff, and the contractor feels them through surveillance, reporting and the evaluation.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-CMP-7257] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 242.1104 [@CL-CMP-7257]; DFARS 242.1106 [@CL-CMP-7259]; DFARS 242.1502 [@CL-CMP-7261] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-7257] |
| Applies to | Contractors with Criticality Designator A or B contracts, and contractors with only designator C contracts when the contracting officer requests surveillance [@CL-CMP-7257; @CL-CMP-7439]; past performance evaluations in CPARS [@CL-CMP-7261] |

The cognizant contract administration office must perform production surveillance on all contractors that have Criticality Designator A or B contracts [@CL-CMP-7257]. It must not perform production surveillance on contractors that have only Criticality Designator C contracts, unless the contracting officer specifically requests it [@CL-CMP-7439]. Contracts with Criticality Designator C are exempt from the duty to monitor contract progress and identify potential contract delinquencies under the surveillance plan, unless the contracting officer specifically requests it [@CL-CMP-7497].

## How it works

When production surveillance is required, the office has three duties.

- It must conduct a periodic risk assessment of the contractor to determine the degree of surveillance needed for all contracts awarded to that contractor, and the assessment must consider information provided by the contractor and the contracting officer [@CL-CMP-7258].
- It must develop a production surveillance plan based on the risk level the assessment determined, and modify the plan to incorporate any special surveillance requirements for individual contracts, including requirements the contracting officer identified [@CL-CMP-7441].
- It must monitor contract progress and identify potential contract delinquencies in accordance with the plan [@CL-CMP-7443].

Within four working days after receipt of the contractor's report, the office must provide the report and any required comments to the contracting officer and, unless the contract says otherwise, the inventory control manager [@CL-CMP-7259]. If the report indicates that the contract is on schedule and the office agrees, the office does not need to add further comments; in all other cases it must add comments and recommend a course of action [@CL-CMP-7260].

Past performance evaluations in CPARS have three required contents:

- an assessment of the contractor's performance against, and efforts to achieve, the goals in its comprehensive small business subcontracting plan, when the contract contains the clause at DFARS 252.219-7004 [@CL-CMP-7445];
- unless the head of the contracting activity exempts it, a notation on a contractor that denied multiple requests for submission of data other than certified cost or pricing data over the preceding 3-year period but nevertheless received an award [@CL-CMP-7261];
- information on the contractor's compliance with the requirements of the clause at DFARS 252.247-7023, Transportation of Supplies by Sea [@CL-CMP-7446].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor holds one Criticality Designator A contract and two designator C contracts. The contract administration office must perform production surveillance on the contractor because of the designator A contract [@CL-CMP-7257]. It assesses risk for all contracts awarded to the contractor, using information from the contractor and the contracting officer, builds a plan from the risk level, and adds a special requirement that the contracting officer identified for the designator A contract [@CL-CMP-7258; @CL-CMP-7441].

The contractor's report arrives on a Monday and shows the contract behind schedule. The office provides the report to the contracting officer and the inventory control manager by the fourth working day after receipt, with comments and a recommended course of action, because the contract is not on schedule [@CL-CMP-7259; @CL-CMP-7260].

The risk assessment still covers the two designator C contracts, because it extends to all contracts awarded to the contractor [@CL-CMP-7258], but they are exempt from the monitoring of contract progress and potential delinquencies unless the contracting officer specifically requests it [@CL-CMP-7497]. A second contractor holds only designator C contracts, so the office does not perform surveillance unless the contracting officer asks for it [@CL-CMP-7439].

## Never confuse

Production surveillance rests on the Criticality Designator of the contractor's contracts: designator A or B requires it, and a contractor with only designator C contracts gets it only on the contracting officer's request [@CL-CMP-7257; @CL-CMP-7439]. Once surveillance is required, a designator C contract is exempt only from progress monitoring, not from the risk assessment that covers all of the contractor's contracts [@CL-CMP-7258; @CL-CMP-7497].

The CPARS notation concerns a contractor that denied multiple requests for data other than certified cost or pricing data yet received an award, and the head of the contracting activity may exempt it [@CL-CMP-7261]. The linked cards cover the production surveillance rules and designators; see [Production surveillance applicability and progress reports](cmp-production-surveillance-applicability-and-progress-reports.md).
