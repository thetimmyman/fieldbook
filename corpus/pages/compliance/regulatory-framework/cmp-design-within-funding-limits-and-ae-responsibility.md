## Purpose

Shows the two design clauses that hold an architect-engineer design inside the construction funding limitation and that keep design responsibility with the architect-engineer after Government review.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-4913; @CL-CMP-4919] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.236-22 and FAR 52.236-23 [@CL-CMP-4913; @CL-CMP-4919] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.236-22 [@CL-CMP-1052]; RFO FAR 52.236-23 [@CL-CMP-1063] |
| Edition date | FAR 52.236-22: APR 1984 [@CL-CMP-10118]; FAR 52.236-23: APR 1984 [@CL-CMP-10119] |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-4913] |
| Applies to | Contracts where the prescriptions in FAR 36.609-1(c) and FAR 36.609-2(b) direct insertion of the clauses [@CL-CMP-4913; @CL-CMP-4919] |

The funding limitation clause is inserted as prescribed in FAR 36.609-1(c) [@CL-CMP-4913], and the responsibility clause as prescribed in FAR 36.609-2(b) [@CL-CMP-4919].

The contractor must accomplish the design services so as to permit award, using standard Federal Acquisition Regulation procedures, of a contract for constructing the facilities designed at a price that does not exceed the estimated construction contract price [@CL-CMP-4914]. Paragraph (c) of the clause states that estimated price as a dollar amount left blank in the printed text [@CL-CMP-5118].

## How it works

Where bids or proposals for the construction contract exceed the estimated price, the contractor performs the redesign and other services necessary to permit award within the funding limitation [@CL-CMP-4915]. Those services come at no increase in the contract price, unless the unfavorable bids or proposals result from conditions beyond the contractor's reasonable control, in which case the contractor is not required to perform them at no cost to the Government [@CL-CMP-4916].

The contractor must promptly advise the Contracting Officer when it finds that the project will exceed or is likely to exceed the funding limitations and it cannot design a usable facility within them [@CL-CMP-4917]. The Contracting Officer then reviews the contractor's revised estimate of construction cost [@CL-CMP-5114]. The Government may, if it determines that the estimated price in the contract is so low that award of a construction contract not in excess of it is improbable, authorize a change in scope or materials [@CL-CMP-5115]. That change is as required to reduce the estimated construction cost to an amount within the estimated construction contract price stated in paragraph (c) [@CL-CMP-5116]. The Government may alternatively adjust the estimated price [@CL-CMP-5117].

Where bids or proposals are not solicited or are unreasonably delayed, a Government estimate of constructing the design submitted is used in place of bids or proposals to determine compliance with the funding limitation [@CL-CMP-4918].

The contractor is responsible for the professional quality, technical accuracy and coordination of all designs, drawings, specifications and other services it furnishes under the contract [@CL-CMP-4920]. It corrects or revises any errors or deficiencies in them without additional compensation [@CL-CMP-4921]. Government review, approval, acceptance and payment are not a waiver of rights under the contract or of a cause of action arising out of performance [@CL-CMP-4922]. The contractor remains liable to the Government, in accordance with applicable law, for all damages caused by its negligent performance of any of the services [@CL-CMP-4923]. The Government's rights and remedies under the contract are in addition to any other rights and remedies provided by law [@CL-CMP-5119]. If the contractor is comprised of more than one legal entity, each entity is jointly and severally liable [@CL-CMP-5120].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text under Class Deviation 2026-O0038, effective February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. The RFO prescriptions cite FAR 36.102-4(a) for the funding limitation clause [@CL-CMP-1051] and FAR 36.102-4(b) for the responsibility clause [@CL-CMP-1062], where the codified text cites FAR 36.609-1(c) and FAR 36.609-2(b) [@CL-CMP-4913; @CL-CMP-4919]. RFO FAR 52.236-22 keeps the rule that the contractor must accomplish the design services required under the contract to permit the award of a contract, using standard Federal Acquisition Regulation procedures for the construction of the facilities designed, at a price that does not exceed the estimated construction contract price as set forth in paragraph (d) of the clause [@CL-CMP-1052; @CL-CMP-1053; @CL-CMP-4914]. It prints the estimated construction contract price as paragraph (d) of the clause [@CL-CMP-1061], where the codified clause prints it as paragraph (c) [@CL-CMP-5118]. The RFO references to that price in paragraph (a) and in paragraph (b)(2) point to paragraph (d) [@CL-CMP-1053; @CL-CMP-1059], where the codified reference in the sentence on a change in scope or materials points to paragraph (c) [@CL-CMP-5116]. Paragraph (a)(1) keeps the redesign duty where bids or proposals exceed the estimated price [@CL-CMP-1054; @CL-CMP-4915], and paragraphs (a)(2) and (a)(3) keep the rule that the additional services are performed at no increase in the price of the contract, with the exception where the unfavorable bids or proposals are the result of conditions beyond the contractor's reasonable control [@CL-CMP-1055; @CL-CMP-4916]. Paragraph (b) says the contractor will promptly provide written notice to the Contracting Officer if it finds that the project will exceed or is likely to exceed the funding limitations and it is unable to design a usable facility within them [@CL-CMP-1056], where the codified clause says the contractor will promptly advise the Contracting Officer [@CL-CMP-4917], and the Contracting Officer's review of the revised estimate of construction cost follows receipt of that written notice [@CL-CMP-1057], where the codified review follows receipt of such information [@CL-CMP-5114]. Paragraph (b)(2) keeps the condition that the Government determines that the estimated price is so low that award of a construction contract not in excess of it is improbable [@CL-CMP-1058; @CL-CMP-5115], and says the Government may then authorize a change in scope or materials as required to reduce the estimated construction cost to an amount within the price set forth in paragraph (d), or adjust the estimated price [@CL-CMP-1059; @CL-CMP-5116; @CL-CMP-5117]. Paragraph (c) keeps the Government estimate used in place of bids or proposals where they are not solicited or are unreasonably delayed [@CL-CMP-1060; @CL-CMP-4918]. RFO FAR 52.236-23 prints its rules as paragraphs (a) through (f) [@CL-CMP-1063; @CL-CMP-1067]. It keeps the contractor's responsibility for the professional quality, technical accuracy and coordination of designs, drawings, specifications and other services [@CL-CMP-1063; @CL-CMP-4920], the duty to correct or revise errors or deficiencies without additional compensation, now paragraph (b) [@CL-CMP-1064; @CL-CMP-4921], the rule that Government review, approval, acceptance and payment are not a waiver, now paragraph (c) [@CL-CMP-1065; @CL-CMP-4922], the liability for damages caused by negligent performance, now paragraph (d) [@CL-CMP-1066; @CL-CMP-4923], and the rights and remedies and joint and several liability rules, now paragraphs (e) and (f) [@CL-CMP-1067; @CL-CMP-5119; @CL-CMP-5120].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

The estimated construction contract price is 4,000,000. The low bid arrives at 4,600,000, and the overrun does not come from conditions beyond the contractor's reasonable control. The contractor redesigns to a smaller mechanical plant at no increase in its design price. If the overrun had come from conditions that were beyond its reasonable control, it would not have to perform that redesign at no cost to the Government.

If no bids were solicited, the Government would prepare its own estimate of constructing the design and compare that estimate with 4,000,000 instead. Later a design error is found that cost the Government 60,000 to fix, and it came from negligent performance. The contractor corrects the design without added fee. The Government's earlier approval and payment waive nothing, so the contractor also stays liable for the 60,000 of damages under applicable law. If the contractor is a joint venture of two firms, each firm is liable for all of it.

## Never confuse

The no-cost redesign is not a waiver of the contractor's liability: where bids or proposals for the construction contract exceed the estimated price, the contractor performs the redesign and other services necessary to permit award within the funding limitation [@CL-CMP-4915], and those additional services are performed at no increase in the price of the contract, unless the unfavorable bids or proposals result from conditions beyond the contractor's reasonable control [@CL-CMP-4916]; yet neither the Government's review, approval or acceptance of the services nor payment for them is a waiver of rights under the contract or of a cause of action arising out of performance, and the contractor remains liable to the Government, in accordance with applicable law, for all damages to the Government caused by its negligent performance of any of the services [@CL-CMP-4922; @CL-CMP-4923]. The contractor corrects or revises any errors or deficiencies in its designs, drawings, specifications and other services without additional compensation [@CL-CMP-4921]. The estimated construction contract price is in paragraph (c) of the codified clause [@CL-CMP-5118] and in paragraph (d) of the RFO clause [@CL-CMP-1061], and the RFO clause asks for written notice where the codified clause asks the contractor to advise [@CL-CMP-1056; @CL-CMP-4917]. The clauses on work oversight and designer registration are covered in [Work oversight, designer registration and the preconstruction conference](cmp-construction-work-oversight-and-designer-registration.md), and the construction specifications and drawings clause is covered in [Specifications and drawings for construction](cmp-specifications-and-drawings-construction.md).
