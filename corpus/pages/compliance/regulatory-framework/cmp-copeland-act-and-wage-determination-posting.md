## Purpose

Explains what the Copeland (Anti-Kickback) Act makes unlawful, the weekly compliance statement that each contractor and subcontractor furnishes, and the determination and notice that the contractor posts at a construction site.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-6733] |
| Codified citation | FAR 22.403-2 and 22.404-10 [@CL-CMP-6733; @CL-CMP-6736] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-6733] |
| Applies to | Contracts subject to the Copeland Act, and the site of the work under a wage determination [@CL-CMP-6735; @CL-CMP-6736] |

The Copeland (Anti-Kickback) Act makes it unlawful to induce, by force, intimidation, threat of procuring dismissal from employment, or otherwise, any person employed in the construction or repair of public buildings or public works, financed in whole or in part by the United States, to give up any part of the compensation [@CL-CMP-6733]. The compensation covered is the compensation to which that person is entitled under a contract of employment [@CL-CMP-6901]. The Act also requires each contractor and subcontractor to furnish weekly a statement of compliance with respect to the wages paid each employee during the preceding week [@CL-CMP-6734].

Contracts subject to the Act must contain a clause, referenced as 52.222-10, requiring contractors and subcontractors to comply with the regulations issued by the Secretary of Labor under the Act [@CL-CMP-6735].

## How it works

The contractor must keep a copy of the applicable wage determination, and any approved additional classifications, posted at the site of the work in a prominent place where the workers can easily see it [@CL-CMP-6736]. The contracting officer must furnish the contractor Department of Labor Form WH-1321, Notice to Employees Working on Federal and Federally Financed Construction Projects, for posting with the wage rates [@CL-CMP-6737]. The poster must show the name, address and telephone number of the Government officer responsible for the administration of the contract, so that workers know to whom they may submit complaints or raise questions concerning labor standards [@CL-CMP-6738].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A site employs twelve laborers and four carpenters on a building financed in part by the United States. The contractor posts the applicable determination and any approved additional classifications in the site trailer, where workers can easily see them [@CL-CMP-6736]. The contracting officer furnishes the contractor Form WH-1321 for posting with the rates, and the poster shows the name, address and telephone number of the Government officer responsible for administering the contract [@CL-CMP-6737; @CL-CMP-6738]. Each week the contractor and each subcontractor furnish a statement of compliance covering the wages paid each employee in the preceding week [@CL-CMP-6734]. When a foreman threatens to dismiss a laborer unless the laborer hands back part of the wages that the employment contract entitles the laborer to, that is the inducement the Act makes unlawful [@CL-CMP-6733; @CL-CMP-6901].

## Never confuse

The contractor must keep a copy of the applicable wage determination posted at the site of the work [@CL-CMP-6736], and the contracting officer furnishes Form WH-1321 for posting with the wage rates [@CL-CMP-6737]. Each contractor and subcontractor furnishes weekly a statement of compliance with respect to the wages paid each employee during the preceding week [@CL-CMP-6734]. The clause itself, apprentices and trainees are covered on [Apprentices, trainees and the Copeland Act](../regulatory-framework/cmp-apprentices-trainees-and-copeland-act.md).
