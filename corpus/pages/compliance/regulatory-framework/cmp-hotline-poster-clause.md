## Purpose

Shows when the fraud hotline poster must be displayed, where it goes, and when an existing awareness program removes the duty for agency posters.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-2637] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.203-14 [@CL-CMP-2637] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.203-14 [@CL-CMP-1280; @CL-CMP-1281] |
| Edition date | Nov 2021 [@CL-CMP-1286] |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-2637] |
| Applies to | Contracts that exceed $7.5 million or a lesser amount established by the agency, not for commercial products or commercial services and not performed entirely outside the United States, where the agency has a fraud hotline poster or the contract is funded with disaster assistance funds, subject to the exception for an existing awareness program [@CL-CMP-2676; @CL-CMP-2677; @CL-CMP-2639] |

The clause is inserted as prescribed in FAR 3.1004(b) [@CL-CMP-2637]. Unless the contract is for the acquisition of a commercial product or commercial service or will be performed entirely outside the United States, one condition for inserting it is that the contract exceeds $7.5 million or a lesser amount established by the agency [@CL-CMP-2676]. A second condition is that the agency has a fraud hotline poster or the contract is funded with disaster assistance funds [@CL-CMP-2677]. Except as provided in paragraph (c), during contract performance in the United States, the Contractor shall prominently display in common work areas within business segments performing work under the contract and at contract work sites any agency fraud hotline poster or Department of Homeland Security (DHS) fraud hotline poster identified in paragraph (b)(3) of the clause, and any DHS fraud hotline poster subsequently identified by the Contracting Officer [@CL-CMP-2638; @CL-CMP-7774]. The Contracting Officer shall insert the appropriate agency name(s) and/or title of the applicable DHS fraud hotline poster and the website(s) or other contact information for obtaining the poster(s) [@CL-CMP-7775]. The exception below applies to the agency posters [@CL-CMP-2639].

If the Contractor has implemented a business ethics and conduct awareness program, including a reporting mechanism, such as a hotline poster, the Contractor need not display any agency fraud hotline posters, other than any required DHS posters [@CL-CMP-2639]. The Contractor shall include the substance of the clause, including paragraph (d), in all subcontracts that exceed the threshold specified in Federal Acquisition Regulation 3.1004(b)(1) on the date of subcontract award, except when the subcontract is for the acquisition of a commercial product or commercial service or is performed entirely outside the United States [@CL-CMP-7776; @CL-CMP-2678]. The insertion conditions are also covered in [Contractor code of business ethics and conduct](cmp-contractor-code-of-business-ethics.md).

## How it works

The poster duty is a display duty tied to performance in the United States and to specific places, the common work areas and the contract work sites [@CL-CMP-2638]. The flowdown does not apply when the subcontract is for the acquisition of a commercial product or commercial service or is performed entirely outside the United States [@CL-CMP-2678]. A contractor that has implemented a business ethics and conduct awareness program, including a reporting mechanism, need not display any agency fraud hotline posters as required in paragraph (b), other than any required DHS posters [@CL-CMP-2639].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text because Class Deviation 2026-O0038 directs it, effective February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. The RFO clause keeps the prescription cross-reference to FAR 3.1004(b) [@CL-CMP-1280; @CL-CMP-2637]. It keeps the duty, except as provided in paragraph (c), during contract performance in the United States to prominently display, in common work areas within business segments performing work under the contract and at contract work sites, the listed posters, the first being any agency fraud hotline poster [@CL-CMP-1281; @CL-CMP-1282; @CL-CMP-2638]. It keeps the paragraph (c) exception under which a contractor with a business ethics and conduct awareness program, including a reporting mechanism such as a hotline poster, need not display agency fraud hotline posters other than any required DHS posters [@CL-CMP-1283; @CL-CMP-2639]. It keeps the flowdown of the substance of the clause in all subcontracts that exceed the threshold specified in FAR 3.1004(b)(1) on the date of subcontract award, except a subcontract for the acquisition of a commercial product or commercial service or one performed entirely outside the United States [@CL-CMP-1284; @CL-CMP-1285; @CL-CMP-2678]. The RFO clause also has a paragraph requiring an electronic version of the poster(s) at the company website if the contractor maintains one as a method of providing information to employees [@CL-CMP-1286].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor performs a contract worth 9 million, not for commercial products or services, in the United States at two sites with 40 staff each, and the agency has a fraud hotline poster. It posts the agency fraud hotline poster in the common work areas at both sites. If it already ran an awareness program with a reporting mechanism, it would not need the agency poster, but it would still post any required DHS poster.

## Never confuse

The displayed poster is not the awareness program's reporting mechanism: a hotline poster is named as one example of a reporting mechanism in an awareness program [@CL-CMP-2639]. For the code of business ethics clause and its internal control system, see [Code of business ethics clause: timely disclosure and the internal control system](cmp-code-of-ethics-clause-disclosure-and-controls.md).

## Company calibration

Which contracts carry the poster clause, where the company performs work on them in the United States, and whether its awareness program already includes a reporting mechanism. [@Q-004]
