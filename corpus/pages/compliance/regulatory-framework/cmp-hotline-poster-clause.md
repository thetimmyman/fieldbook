## Purpose

Shows when the fraud hotline poster must be displayed, where it goes, and when an existing awareness program removes the duty for agency posters.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-2637] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.203-14 [@CL-CMP-2637] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.203-14 [@CL-CMP-1280; @CL-CMP-1281] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-2637] |
| Applies to | Contracts that exceed $7.5 million or a lesser amount established by the agency, not for commercial products or commercial services and not performed entirely outside the United States, where the agency has a fraud hotline poster or the contract is funded with disaster assistance funds, subject to the exception for an existing awareness program [@CL-CMP-2676; @CL-CMP-2677; @CL-CMP-2639] |

The clause is inserted as prescribed in FAR 3.1004(b) [@CL-CMP-2637]. Unless the contract is for the acquisition of a commercial product or commercial service or will be performed entirely outside the United States, one condition for inserting it is that the contract exceeds $7.5 million or a lesser amount established by the agency [@CL-CMP-2676]. A second condition is that the agency has a fraud hotline poster or the contract is funded with disaster assistance funds [@CL-CMP-2677]. During contract performance in the United States, the Contractor shall prominently display in common work areas within business segments performing work under the contract and at contract work sites the fraud hotline posters listed in the clause, the first of which is any agency fraud hotline poster [@CL-CMP-2638]. The clause points to its own paragraph that identifies the agency and Department of Homeland Security posters, and that paragraph and the later posters the Contracting Officer may identify are not set out on this card. The exception below limits the duty.

If the Contractor has implemented a business ethics and conduct awareness program, including a reporting mechanism, such as a hotline poster, the Contractor need not display any agency fraud hotline posters, other than any required DHS posters [@CL-CMP-2639]. The clause also requires its substance in all subcontracts that exceed the threshold specified in FAR 3.1004(b)(1) on the date of subcontract award, except a subcontract for the acquisition of a commercial product or commercial service or one performed entirely outside the United States [@CL-CMP-2678]. The insertion conditions are also covered in [Contractor code of business ethics and conduct](cmp-contractor-code-of-business-ethics.md).

## How it works

The poster duty is a display duty tied to performance in the United States and to specific places, the common work areas and the contract work sites [@CL-CMP-2638]. The clause leaves a blank for the Contracting Officer to fill with where the posters can be obtained. The clause also has a paragraph on an electronic version at a company website, and this card does not set it out. Its flowdown paragraph is limited by a threshold and by exceptions for commercial and wholly foreign subcontracts [@CL-CMP-2678]. An existing awareness program with a reporting mechanism removes the duty for agency posters, but not for required DHS posters [@CL-CMP-2639].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text because Class Deviation 2026-O0038 directs it, effective February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. The RFO clause keeps the prescription cross-reference to FAR 3.1004(b) [@CL-CMP-1280; @CL-CMP-2637]. It keeps the duty, except as provided in paragraph (c), during contract performance in the United States to prominently display, in common work areas within business segments performing work under the contract and at contract work sites, the listed posters, the first being any agency fraud hotline poster [@CL-CMP-1281; @CL-CMP-1282; @CL-CMP-2638]. It keeps the paragraph (c) exception under which a contractor with a business ethics and conduct awareness program, including a reporting mechanism such as a hotline poster, need not display agency fraud hotline posters other than any required DHS posters [@CL-CMP-1283; @CL-CMP-2639]. It keeps the flowdown of the substance of the clause in all subcontracts that exceed the threshold specified in FAR 3.1004(b)(1) on the date of subcontract award, except a subcontract for the acquisition of a commercial product or commercial service or one performed entirely outside the United States [@CL-CMP-1284; @CL-CMP-1285; @CL-CMP-2678]. The RFO clause also has a paragraph requiring an electronic version of the poster(s) at the company website if the contractor maintains one as a method of providing information to employees [@CL-CMP-1286].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor performs a contract worth 9 million, not for commercial products or services, in the United States at two sites with 40 staff each, and the agency has a fraud hotline poster. It posts the agency fraud hotline poster in the common work areas at both sites. If it already ran an awareness program with a reporting mechanism, it would not need the agency poster, but it would still post any required DHS poster.

## Never confuse

The poster is a display duty. The internal reporting mechanism that the code of business ethics clause requires inside its internal control system is a separate duty, and it is on [Code of business ethics clause: timely disclosure and the internal control system](cmp-code-of-ethics-clause-disclosure-and-controls.md). The two meet in one place, because a hotline poster is named as one example of a reporting mechanism in an awareness program that can trigger the exception [@CL-CMP-2639].
