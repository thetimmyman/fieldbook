## Purpose

Shows what an offeror discloses about foreign government ownership or control, and when a contractor may not subcontract with a firm owned or controlled by a state sponsor of terrorism.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-CMP-4083; @CL-CMP-4087] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 252.209-7002 and DFARS 252.209-7004 [@CL-CMP-4083; @CL-CMP-4087] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-4083; @CL-CMP-4087] |
| Applies to | Solicitations with the disclosure provision, as prescribed in DFARS 209.104-70 [@CL-CMP-4083], and contracts with the subcontracting clause, as prescribed in DFARS 209.409 [@CL-CMP-4087] |

No contract under a national security program may be awarded to an entity controlled by a foreign government if that entity requires access to proscribed information to perform the contract, unless the Secretary of Defense or a designee has waived application of the named statute [@CL-CMP-4084]. The Offeror shall disclose any interest a foreign government has in the Offeror when that interest constitutes control by a foreign government as defined in the provision [@CL-CMP-4085]. If the Offeror is a subsidiary, it shall also disclose any reportable interest a foreign government has in any entity that owns or controls the subsidiary, including interest concerning the Offeror's immediate parent, intermediate parents, and the ultimate parent [@CL-CMP-4086].

Unless the Government determines that there is a compelling reason to do so, the Contractor shall not enter into any subcontract in excess of the threshold specified in FAR 9.405-2(b) on the date of subcontract award with a firm, or a subsidiary of a firm, that is identified in SAM Exclusions as being ineligible for the award of Defense contracts or subcontracts because it is owned or controlled by the government of a country that is a state sponsor of terrorism [@CL-CMP-4088; @CL-CMP-4089].

A corporate officer or a designee of the Contractor shall notify the Contracting Officer, in writing, before entering into a subcontract with a party that is identified in SAM Exclusions as being ineligible for the award of Defense contracts or subcontracts because it is owned or controlled by the government of a country that is a state sponsor of terrorism [@CL-CMP-4090; @CL-CMP-4138]. The notice must include the name of the proposed subcontractor and the compelling reasons for doing business with it [@CL-CMP-4138].

## How it works

The Offeror discloses a foreign government interest when it constitutes control as the provision defines it, and a subsidiary also looks up the chain to its parents [@CL-CMP-4085; @CL-CMP-4086]. The award prohibition reaches an entity controlled by a foreign government that requires access to proscribed information to perform the contract, and a waiver by the Secretary of Defense or a designee lifts it [@CL-CMP-4084]. The subcontracting clause bars listed subcontracts above a threshold unless the Government determines there is a compelling reason, and it requires written notice to the Contracting Officer, with the proposed subcontractor's name and the compelling reasons, before the Contractor enters the subcontract [@CL-CMP-4088; @CL-CMP-4090; @CL-CMP-4138].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An offeror is a subsidiary. Its ultimate parent is controlled by a foreign government. After award, the Contractor wants to place a subcontract worth 5 million with a firm that SAM Exclusions lists as owned by a state sponsor of terrorism. Assume the subcontract threshold is 1 million, a made-up figure.

Answer: the offeror disclosed the parent interest in its offer. The subcontract is above the assumed threshold, so the Contractor may not enter it unless the Government determines there is a compelling reason, and an officer or designee must notify the Contracting Officer in writing first.

## Never confuse

The disclosure provision is used in solicitations as prescribed in DFARS 209.104-70 [@CL-CMP-4083], and the subcontracting clause is used as prescribed in DFARS 209.409 [@CL-CMP-4087]. The disclosure duty reaches an interest that constitutes control as the provision defines it [@CL-CMP-4085]. A separate provision on consulting services lists, among its covered foreign entities, the government of any country if the Secretary of State determines that such government has repeatedly provided support for acts of international terrorism, pursuant to any of the provisions of law that the provision then lists [@CL-CMP-4123]; see [the consulting services card](cmp-consulting-services-conflicts-of-interest.md).
