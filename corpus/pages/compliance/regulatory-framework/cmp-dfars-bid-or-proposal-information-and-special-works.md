## Purpose

Shows how the DoD clause on bid or proposal information limits what the Government does with an offer, and what rights the Government takes in special works.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-CMP-3950; @CL-CMP-3955] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 252.227-7016 and 252.227-7020 [@CL-CMP-3950; @CL-CMP-3955] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-3950; @CL-CMP-3955] |
| Applies to | Offers, by their submission, and works first produced, created or generated and required to be delivered under the contract [@CL-CMP-3950; @CL-CMP-3955] |

By submission of its offer, the Offeror agrees that the Government may reproduce the bid or proposal, or any portions, to the extent necessary to evaluate the offer [@CL-CMP-3950]. One listed agreement is that, except as provided in paragraph (d) of the clause, the Government shall use information in the bid or proposal only for evaluational purposes and shall not disclose it, directly or indirectly, to any person including potential evaluators, unless that person has been authorized [@CL-CMP-3951]. The authorization that permits disclosure is authorization by the head of the agency, his or her designee, or the Contracting Officer to receive such information [@CL-CMP-3980]. After award, except as provided in the listed paragraphs, the Government shall have the rights to use, modify, reproduce, release, perform, display or disclose information in the Contractor's bid or proposal within the Government [@CL-CMP-3952]. The same paragraph says the Government shall not release, perform, display or disclose that information outside the Government without the Contractor's written permission [@CL-CMP-3953]. The Contractor shall include the clause in all subcontracts or similar contractual instruments and require subcontractors or suppliers to do so without alteration, except to identify the parties [@CL-CMP-3954].

Under the special works clause, the Government shall have unlimited rights in works first produced, created or generated and required to be delivered under the contract [@CL-CMP-3955]. When such a work is first produced under the contract and is required to be delivered under it, the Contractor shall assign copyright in those works to the Government [@CL-CMP-3956].

## How it works

By submission of its offer, the Offeror agrees that the Government may reproduce the bid or proposal, or any portions, to the extent necessary to evaluate the offer [@CL-CMP-3950]. Except as provided in paragraph (d) of the clause, the Government shall use the information only for evaluational purposes and shall not disclose it to any person unless that person has been authorized by the head of the agency, his or her designee, or the Contracting Officer to receive it [@CL-CMP-3951; @CL-CMP-3980]. After award, except as provided in the listed paragraphs, the Government has the rights to use, modify, reproduce, release, perform, display, or disclose the information within the Government, and it shall not release, perform, display, or disclose it outside the Government without the Contractor's written permission [@CL-CMP-3952; @CL-CMP-3953]. The first sentence of that paragraph is subject to exceptions in the listed paragraphs [@CL-CMP-3952]. One of them provides that the Government's rights in information that is technical data or computer software required to be delivered under the contract are determined by one of three alternative clauses of the contract: DFARS 252.227-7013, DFARS 252.227-7014, or DFARS 252.227-7018 [@CL-CMP-3981; @CL-CMP-8621]. This card does not set out the other exceptions. For works first produced, created, or generated and required to be delivered under the contract, the Government shall have unlimited rights [@CL-CMP-3955], and the Contractor shall assign copyright in those works to the Government [@CL-CMP-3956].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A company submits an offer that includes a technical approach. Before award the Government copies the offer only for evaluation and shows it only to authorized evaluators. After the company wins, the Government shares the approach with other Government offices but asks the company's written permission before sending it to another company. Separately, a training video made and delivered under the contract is a special work, and the company assigns the copyright in it to the Government.

## Never confuse

The bid or proposal information clause is not the special works clause: the clause provides that the Government's rights in information that is technical data or computer software required to be delivered under the contract are determined by one of three alternative clauses of the contract: DFARS 252.227-7013, DFARS 252.227-7014, or DFARS 252.227-7018 [@CL-CMP-3981; @CL-CMP-8621]. Data rights are covered on [Rights in data, general: definitions and allocation](cmp-rights-in-data-general-definitions-and-allocation.md), and the clause for SBIR and STTR data is covered on [DoD SBIR and STTR data rights: definitions, protection period and markings](cmp-dfars-sbir-sttr-data-rights-definitions-and-period.md). Under the special works clause, the Contractor shall assign copyright in the covered works to the Government [@CL-CMP-3956]. The FAR special works clause is covered on [Additional data requirements and rights in data for special works](cmp-additional-data-requirements-and-special-works.md).
