## Purpose

Names the contract types that the Secretary of Labor has exempted from the Service Contract Labor Standards statute and the conditions and exceptions this card drafts.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-2359] |
| Codified citation | FAR 22.1003-4 [@CL-CMP-2359; @CL-CMP-2365; @CL-CMP-2369; @CL-CMP-2481] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-2359] |
| Applies to | Contracts and subcontracts of the listed types, subject to the stated conditions and, for the services exemption, the listed exceptions [@CL-CMP-2359; @CL-CMP-2365; @CL-CMP-2369; @CL-CMP-2481; @CL-CMP-2376; @CL-CMP-2377] |

In addition to the statutory exemptions, the Secretary of Labor has exempted the listed types of contracts from all provisions of the Service Contract Labor Standards statute [@CL-CMP-2359]. The first listed type is contracts entered into by the United States with common carriers for the carriage of mail by rail, air (except air star routes), bus, and ocean vessel, where the carriage is performed on regularly scheduled runs over regularly established routes [@CL-CMP-2360] and accounts for an insubstantial portion of the revenue therefrom [@CL-CMP-2361]. The second is a contract entered into by the United States Postal Service with an individual owner-operator for mail service [@CL-CMP-2362], where it is not contemplated at the time the contract is made that the owner-operator will hire any service employee to perform the services, except for short periods of vacation time or for unexpected contingencies or emergency situations such as illness or accident [@CL-CMP-2363]. The third is contracts for the carriage of freight or personnel if the carriage is subject to rates covered by section 10721 of the Interstate Commerce Act [@CL-CMP-2364].

A second group covers contracts and subcontracts whose primary purpose is to furnish maintenance, calibration, or repair of listed equipment, if the equipment conditions are met [@CL-CMP-2365]. The listed equipment is automated data processing equipment and office information or word processing systems, and scientific equipment and medical apparatus or equipment where micro-electronic circuitry or other technology of at least similar sophistication is an essential element [@CL-CMP-2366]. It also includes office and business machines not otherwise exempt, if the services are performed by the manufacturer or supplier of the equipment [@CL-CMP-2367]. The equipment exemption applies if all the listed conditions are met for a contract or subcontract [@CL-CMP-2368]. One condition is that the items of equipment to be serviced are used regularly for other than Government purposes and are sold or traded by the contractor in substantial quantities to the general public in the course of normal business operations [@CL-CMP-2478]. Another is that the services will be furnished at prices which are, or are based on, established catalog or market prices for the maintenance, calibration, or repair of such equipment [@CL-CMP-2479]. A third is that the contractor will use the same compensation plan for all service employees performing work under the contract as the contractor uses for these employees and equivalent employees servicing the same equipment of commercial customers [@CL-CMP-2480]. The printed section also lists a certification condition and the steps for awarding the contract and for a Department of Labor determination after award, and this card does not draft them.

A third group covers contracts and subcontracts whose primary purpose is to provide the listed services, if the services conditions are met, and subject to the listed exceptions that follow [@CL-CMP-2369; @CL-CMP-2481; @CL-CMP-2376; @CL-CMP-2377]. The listed services are automobile or other vehicle maintenance, other than contracts or subcontracts to operate a Government motor pool or similar facility, and financial services involving the issuance and servicing of cards [@CL-CMP-2370]. They also include hotel or motel services for conferences, which must not include ongoing contracts for lodging on an as needed or continuing basis [@CL-CMP-2371]. One listed category is services for all types of equipment where the services are obtained from the manufacturer or supplier under a contract awarded on a sole source basis, with a parenthetical about installation that is not subject to the Construction Wage Rate Requirements statute [@CL-CMP-2372]. The printed item also names the kinds of service covered, and this card does not draft that wording. They include transportation by common carrier of persons on regularly scheduled routes or via standard commercial services, not including charter services [@CL-CMP-2373]. They include real estate services related to housing Federal agencies or disposing of real property owned by the Government [@CL-CMP-2374]. The last is relocation services to assist Federal employees or military personnel in buying and selling homes, not including actual moving or storage of household goods and related services [@CL-CMP-2375].

The services exemption applies if all the listed conditions are met for a contract or for a subcontract [@CL-CMP-2498; @CL-CMP-2481]. One condition is that each service employee who will perform the services will spend only a small portion of his or her time, a monthly average of less than 20 percent of the available hours on an annualized basis, or less than 20 percent of available hours during the contract period if the contract period is less than a month, servicing the Government contract [@CL-CMP-2482]. The printed section lists the other conditions and the steps for award, resolicitation and a Department of Labor determination after award, and this card does not draft them, so that section must be read before any contract is treated as exempt.

The services exemption does not apply to solicitations and contracts (subcontracts) awarded under the Committee for Purchase from People Who Are Blind or Severely Disabled statute [@CL-CMP-2376]. Two further listed exceptions are solicitations and contracts for the operation of a Government facility or part of one, though the exemption may be applicable to subcontracts for services, and those subject to the successor wage provision described on [obtaining wage determinations and successor pay](cmp-scls-wage-determinations-and-successorship.md) [@CL-CMP-2377; @CL-CMP-2379].

## How it works

Each exemption takes a type of contract out of the statute [@CL-CMP-2359]. The first group of types carries conditions in its own items, and the equipment and services exemptions also depend on stated conditions, with the services exemption subject to listed exceptions [@CL-CMP-2360; @CL-CMP-2361; @CL-CMP-2365; @CL-CMP-2369; @CL-CMP-2481]. The printed section adds award procedures and a Department of Labor determination after award, and this card does not draft them.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A company offers to repair automated data processing equipment for an agency. It sells the same equipment to the public in volume, say 2,000 units a year, and the services are priced from its catalog. The answer: the equipment category fits, but the exemption applies only if every listed condition is met, so the company must check each condition in the printed section.

## Never confuse

An exemption takes a contract out of the statute. It is not the same as the rules on when the clause goes into a contract and when wage determinations are obtained, which are on [Service contract labor standards clause and wage determinations](../../operating-model/talent/opm-service-contract-labor-standards.md) and are not restated here. The exemptions made by the Secretary of Labor are also separate from the statutory exemptions [@CL-CMP-2359].
