## Purpose

Shows what the Government may do with commercial computer software delivered under a contract, and what notice the contractor must put on it.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-3711] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.227-19 [@CL-CMP-3711; @CL-CMP-3712] |
| RFO citation | No RFO equivalent in part 52: RFO FAR 52.227-19 is [Reserved] [@CL-CMP-0645] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-3711] |
| Applies to | Commercial computer software delivered under the contract [@CL-CMP-3712] |

Notwithstanding contrary provisions in the Contractor's standard commercial license or lease agreement, the Contractor agrees that the Government will have the rights set out in the clause's rights paragraph to use, duplicate or disclose any commercial computer software [@CL-CMP-3711]. Commercial computer software delivered under the contract may not be used, reproduced, or disclosed by the Government except as provided in the listed uses or as expressly stated otherwise in the contract [@CL-CMP-3712]. The listed uses include use or copying for use with the computer or computers for which it was acquired, including use at any Government installation to which those computers may be transferred [@CL-CMP-3713].

The Contractor shall affix a notice substantially as the clause sets out to any commercial computer software delivered under the contract [@CL-CMP-3714]. The notice states that, notwithstanding any other lease or license agreement that may pertain to or accompany delivery of the software, the rights of the Government regarding its use, reproduction and disclosure are as set forth in a stated Government contract number [@CL-CMP-3715].

## How it works

The clause overrides the contractor's own license terms for the Government. The Government's rights come from the clause, and use outside the listed uses is allowed only if the contract says so [@CL-CMP-3711; @CL-CMP-3712]. The notice on the delivered software points anyone who reads it back to the contract, instead of to the contractor's licence [@CL-CMP-3715]. If the commercial computer software is otherwise available without disclosure restrictions, the Contractor licenses it to the Government without disclosure restrictions [@CL-CMP-3753]. The clause lists further permitted uses that this card does not set out. The clause text used here does not mention commercial computer software documentation, so it establishes no Government right in it and this card states none.

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text from February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. RFO FAR 52.227-19 is [Reserved], so the codified rights paragraph and notice paragraph have no text under that section number in the RFO text [@CL-CMP-0645; @CL-CMP-3711; @CL-CMP-3714].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor delivers a commercial analysis program with its standard licence, which limits use to one machine. The contract includes this clause. The standard licence's terms do not override the clause, and the contractor affixes the clause's notice to the delivered software. The notice points to the contract number as the source of the Government's rights.

## Never confuse

This clause covers commercial computer software and sets a short list of permitted uses. The [Rights in Data—General clause](cmp-rights-in-data-general-definitions-and-allocation.md) instead gives the Government unlimited rights in listed data and lets the contractor withhold restricted computer software [@CL-CMP-3684; @CL-CMP-3697]. The withholding rules are on [Rights in data, general: copyright, withholding and markings](cmp-rights-in-data-general-copyright-withholding-and-markings.md). The codified commercial computer software license clause is not RFO text under the same number, because the RFO text lists FAR 52.227-19 as [Reserved] [@CL-CMP-3711; @CL-CMP-0645].
