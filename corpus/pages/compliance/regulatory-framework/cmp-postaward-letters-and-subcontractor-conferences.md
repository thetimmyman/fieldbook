## Purpose

Shows how a postaward letter can stand in for a conference, and who conducts a postaward conference with subcontractors and what Government representatives must and must not do at it.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-7806] |
| Codified citation | FAR 42.504 and FAR 42.505 [@CL-CMP-7806; @CL-CMP-7809] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 42.302(a)(3) lists conducting post-award orientation conferences among the functions the contracting officer normally delegates to a CAO [@CL-CMP-0026; @CL-CMP-0091]. The RFO sections numbered 42.504, 42.505 and 42.503-2 cover other subjects [@CL-CMP-0132; @CL-CMP-0133; @CL-CMP-0134]. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-7806] |
| Applies to | Postaward orientation of the contractor in writing, and postaward conferences with subcontractors [@CL-CMP-7806; @CL-CMP-7809] |

In some circumstances, a letter or other written form of communication to the contractor may be adequate postaward orientation in lieu of a conference [@CL-CMP-7806]. The letter should identify the Government representative responsible for administering the contract and cite any unusual or significant contract requirements [@CL-CMP-7807]. The rules on changes to the contract in FAR 42.503-2 also apply to a postaward letter [@CL-CMP-7808]. Those rules include that the contracting officer may make commitments or give directions within the scope of that officer's authority and shall put in writing and sign any commitment or direction, whether or not it changes the contract [@CL-OPM-0036]. They also require that any change to the contract that results from the postaward conference be made only by a contract modification referencing the applicable terms of the contract, and that participants without authority to bind the Government not take action that in any way alters it [@CL-OPM-0037]. The section does not say who decides whether a letter is enough; the contracting officer's decision on orientation is on [Postaward orientation](../../operating-model/cadence/opm-postaward-orientation.md).

The prime contractor is generally responsible for conducting postaward conferences with subcontractors [@CL-CMP-7809]. However, the prime contractor may invite Government representatives to a conference with subcontractors, or the Government may request that the prime contractor initiate a conference with subcontractors [@CL-CMP-7810]. The prime contractor should ensure that representatives from involved contract administration offices are invited [@CL-CMP-7811].

## How it works

Government representatives at a subcontractor conference must recognize the lack of privity of contract between the Government and subcontractors, and shall not take action that is inconsistent with or alters subcontracts [@CL-CMP-7812]. They shall also ensure that any changes in direction or commitment affecting the prime contract or contractor that result from the conference are made by written direction of the contracting officer to the prime contractor, in the same manner as described in FAR 42.503-2 [@CL-CMP-7813]. That manner includes writing and signing any commitment or direction, and a contract modification for any change to the contract that results from the postaward conference [@CL-OPM-0036; @CL-OPM-0037].

The prime contractor is generally responsible for conducting the conference and should ensure that the involved administration offices are invited [@CL-CMP-7809; @CL-CMP-7811]. The Government representatives carry the limits on what they may do at it [@CL-CMP-7812; @CL-CMP-7813]. A postaward letter and a conference with subcontractors are different tools. The letter goes to the contractor [@CL-CMP-7806]. The conference is held with subcontractors [@CL-CMP-7809]. For the conference between the Government and the prime contractor, see [Contract correspondence and the postaward conference](cmp-contract-correspondence-and-postaward-conference.md).

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 42 text in place of the codified text under Class Deviation 2026-O0050, effective March 16, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9759] [@CL-CMP-9761]. The RFO section numbered 42.504 is headed Billing rates [@CL-CMP-0132], where codified FAR 42.504 covers postaward letters [@CL-CMP-7806], and the RFO section numbered 42.505 is headed Final indirect cost rates [@CL-CMP-0133], where codified FAR 42.505 covers postaward subcontractor conferences [@CL-CMP-7809]. The RFO section numbered 42.503-2 is headed Certificate of indirect costs [@CL-CMP-0134], where codified FAR 42.503-2 holds the rules on changes to the contract that codified FAR 42.504 applies to a postaward letter [@CL-CMP-7808; @CL-OPM-0036; @CL-OPM-0037]. RFO FAR 42.302(a) says the contracting officer normally delegates the functions it lists to a CAO [@CL-CMP-0026], and item (3) of that list is conducting post-award orientation conferences [@CL-CMP-0091]. This page cites no RFO text on postaward letters or on postaward subcontractor conferences, and it does not treat that as a repeal of the codified rules.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contracting officer decides that a letter is enough orientation for a 900,000 contract. The letter names the Government representative who administers the contract and cites the one unusual delivery requirement [@CL-CMP-7806; @CL-CMP-7807]. The letter also says that no change in the contract results from it, and a later change in delivery dates is made only by a modification [@CL-CMP-7808; @CL-OPM-0037].

On a different contract, the prime contractor holds a conference with a subcontractor whose subcontract is worth 1,200,000 and invites the contract administration office [@CL-CMP-7809; @CL-CMP-7811]. A Government representative who attends raises a delivery change but takes no action on the subcontract itself [@CL-CMP-7812]. The change affects the prime contract, so the contracting officer gives written direction to the prime contractor, and does not direct the subcontractor [@CL-CMP-7813]. The answer: the prime contractor generally runs the conference, a Government representative who attends recognizes the lack of privity and does not alter the subcontract, and any change reaches the prime contractor in writing from the contracting officer. This example follows the codified sections; under the RFO text, conducting a postaward orientation conference is a function the contracting officer normally delegates to a CAO [@CL-CMP-0026; @CL-CMP-0091].

## Never confuse

A postaward letter is written orientation to the contractor that may replace a conference rather than supplement it [@CL-CMP-7806]. A subcontractor conference is a meeting the prime contractor generally runs [@CL-CMP-7809], and the Government attends without privity of contract with the subcontractors [@CL-CMP-7812]. The section numbers 42.504 and 42.505 are postaward letters and postaward subcontractor conferences in the codified text [@CL-CMP-7806; @CL-CMP-7809] but billing rates and final indirect cost rates in the RFO text [@CL-CMP-0132; @CL-CMP-0133].
