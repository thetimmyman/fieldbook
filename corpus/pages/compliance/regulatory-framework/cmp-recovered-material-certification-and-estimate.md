## Purpose

Explains the recovered material content certification an offeror gives when it signs its offer, the estimate of recovered material content a contractor submits when the contract ends, and the officer certification that Alternate I adds.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-5176] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.223-4 and FAR 52.223-9 [@CL-CMP-0707; @CL-CMP-5176; @CL-CMP-5177] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.223-4 and RFO FAR 52.223-9 [@CL-CMP-0708; @CL-CMP-2158; @CL-CMP-2159] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-5176] |
| Applies to | Offerors whose offers carry the recovered material certification provision, and contractors whose contracts carry the estimate clause [@CL-CMP-0707; @CL-CMP-5176; @CL-CMP-5177] |

As required by the Resource Conservation and Recovery Act of 1976 (section 6962(c)(3)(A)(i) of title 42 of the United States Code), the offeror certifies, by signing its offer, that the percentage of recovered materials content for EPA-designated items to be delivered or used in the performance of the contract will be at least the amount required by the applicable contract specifications or other contractual requirements [@CL-CMP-0707; @CL-CMP-5176].

The Contractor, on completion of the contract, shall (1) estimate the percentage of the total recovered material content for EPA-designated item(s) delivered and/or used in contract performance, including, if applicable, the percentage of post-consumer material content, and (2) submit this estimate to the person designated in the clause blank, which the Contracting Officer completes in accordance with agency procedures [@CL-CMP-5177; @CL-CMP-5178].

## How it works

Recovered material means waste materials and by-products recovered or diverted from solid waste, but the term does not include those materials and by-products generated from, and commonly reused within, an original manufacturing process [@CL-CMP-5179]. Postconsumer material means a material or finished product that has served its intended use and has been discarded for disposal or recovery, having completed its life as a consumer item [@CL-CMP-5283]. Postconsumer material is a part of the broader category of recovered material [@CL-CMP-5284].

The certification at offer concerns the percentage of recovered materials content for EPA-designated items to be delivered or used in the performance of the contract, while the estimate on completion of the contract concerns the percentage of the total recovered material content for EPA-designated item(s) delivered and/or used in contract performance [@CL-CMP-0707; @CL-CMP-5176; @CL-CMP-5177].

Alternate I redesignates paragraph (b) of the basic clause as paragraph (c) and adds a new paragraph (b) [@CL-CMP-5285]. Under the new paragraph (b), the Contractor shall execute the certification required by the Resource Conservation and Recovery Act of 1976 (section 6962(i)(2)(C) of title 42 of the United States Code) [@CL-CMP-5286]. An officer or employee responsible for the performance of the contract certifies that the percentage of recovered material content for EPA-designated items met the applicable contract specifications or other contractual requirements [@CL-CMP-5180]. The certification form provides lines for the signature, typed name and title of the officer or employee, the name of the company, firm or organization, and the date [@CL-CMP-5287].

Class Deviation 2026-O0038 directs DoD contracting officers to use the revised FAR part 52 published on the Revolutionary FAR Overhaul web page in place of the codified part 52 text, effective February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760; @CL-CMP-9762]. Under RFO FAR 52.223-4, as required by the Resource Conservation and Recovery Act of 1976 (section 6962(c)(3)(A)(i) of title 42 of the United States Code), the offeror certifies, by signing the offer, that the percentage of recovered materials content for EPA-designated items to be delivered or used in the performance of the contract will be at least the amount required by the applicable contract specifications or other contractual requirements [@CL-CMP-0708; @CL-CMP-2158]. Under RFO FAR 52.223-9, the Contractor, on completion of the contract, shall estimate the percentage of the total recovered material content for EPA-designated item(s) delivered and/or used in contract performance, including, if applicable, the percentage of post-consumer material content, and submit this estimate to the person designated in the clause blank, which the Contracting Officer completes in accordance with agency procedures [@CL-CMP-2159; @CL-CMP-2160]. RFO FAR 52.223-9 defines recovered material and postconsumer material and states that postconsumer material is a part of the broader category of recovered material [@CL-CMP-2161; @CL-CMP-2162]. Its Alternate I redesignates paragraph (b) of the basic clause as paragraph (c), adds a new paragraph (b) requiring the Contractor to execute the certification required by section 6962(i)(2)(C) of title 42 of the United States Code, and includes the certification by an officer or employee responsible for the performance of the contract that the percentage of recovered material content for EPA-designated items met the applicable contract specifications or other contractual requirements [@CL-CMP-2163; @CL-CMP-2164]. The RFO certification form provides lines for the signature, typed name and title of the officer or employee, the name of the company, firm or organization, and the date [@CL-CMP-9763; @CL-CMP-9764].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contract for EPA-designated items requires at least 30 percent recovered material content. By signing its offer the offeror certifies that the delivered items will have at least that percentage. At completion the contractor estimates that the delivered items had 35 percent total recovered material content, of which 10 percentage points were postconsumer material, and it submits that estimate to the official named in the blank the contracting officer filled in. The 10 points sit inside the 35 and are not added to it. If the contract includes Alternate I, an officer responsible for performance also signs the certification that the percentage met the contract specifications, and 35 percent is above the required 30 percent.

## Never confuse

A postconsumer percentage is reported inside the recovered material total, not added to it, because postconsumer material is a part of the broader category of recovered material [@CL-CMP-5284; @CL-CMP-5177].

The estimate at completion does not replace the certification at offer, and the Alternate I certification is a further statement given by an officer or employee [@CL-CMP-0707; @CL-CMP-5176; @CL-CMP-5177; @CL-CMP-5180].

Recovered material means waste materials and by-products recovered or diverted from solid waste, excluding materials and by-products generated from, and commonly reused within, an original manufacturing process [@CL-CMP-5179], whereas postconsumer material has served its intended use and has been discarded for disposal or recovery, having completed its life as a consumer item [@CL-CMP-5283; @CL-CMP-5284]. See [Biobased product certification and reporting](cmp-biobased-product-certification-and-reporting.md).

## Company calibration

Whether item records can produce the recovered and postconsumer percentages for each EPA-designated item at contract completion, who completes the submission blank under agency procedures, and whether the contract uses Alternate I. [@Q-004]
