## Purpose

Shows when a new contractor certification may enter the FAR, how a dollar threshold is measured, and how a FAR change reaches solicitations and existing contracts.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-2008] |
| Codified citation | FAR 1.107 and FAR 1.108 [@CL-CMP-2008; @CL-CMP-2010] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-2008] |
| Applies to | New contractor or offeror certification requirements for the FAR chapter, and the thresholds and FAR changes the conventions govern [@CL-CMP-2008; @CL-CMP-2010; @CL-CMP-2012] |

A new requirement for a certification by a contractor or offeror may not be included in the FAR chapter unless one of two conditions is met [@CL-CMP-2008; @CL-CMP-2009]. The first condition is that the certification requirement is specifically imposed by statute [@CL-CMP-2008]. The second condition is that the FAR Council provides written justification to the Administrator for Federal Procurement Policy and the Administrator approves its inclusion in writing [@CL-CMP-2008; @CL-CMP-2009].

Unless otherwise specified, a specific dollar threshold for the purpose of applicability is the final anticipated dollar value of the action, including the dollar value of all options [@CL-CMP-2010]. If the action establishes a maximum quantity, a ceiling price or a final price based on future events, the final anticipated dollar value must be the highest final priced alternative to the Government, and that alternative includes the dollar value of all options [@CL-CMP-2011; @CL-CMP-2100]. Unless otherwise specified, FAR changes apply to solicitations issued on or after the effective date of the change [@CL-CMP-2012]. Unless otherwise specified, contracting officers may, at their discretion, include a change in solicitations issued before the effective date, provided award occurs on or after the effective date [@CL-CMP-2012; @CL-CMP-2013], and may include it in any existing contract with appropriate consideration [@CL-CMP-2012; @CL-CMP-2014].

## How it works

A new requirement for a certification by a contractor or offeror may not be included in the FAR chapter unless the certification requirement is specifically imposed by statute, or written justification for it is provided to the Administrator for Federal Procurement Policy by the Federal Acquisition Regulatory Council and the Administrator approves in writing its inclusion [@CL-CMP-2008; @CL-CMP-2009]. Unless otherwise specified, a specific dollar threshold for the purpose of applicability is the final anticipated value of the action including all options, or, where the action sets a maximum quantity or a ceiling price or bases the final price on future events, the highest final priced alternative including all options [@CL-CMP-2010; @CL-CMP-2011; @CL-CMP-2100]. Unless otherwise specified, FAR changes apply to solicitations issued on or after the effective date of the change [@CL-CMP-2012]. Unless otherwise specified, the contracting officer may, at their discretion, include a change in an earlier solicitation if award of the resulting contract or contracts occurs on or after the effective date, or in an existing contract with appropriate consideration [@CL-CMP-2012; @CL-CMP-2013; @CL-CMP-2014].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A planned contract has a base price of 6 million and priced options of 2 million. A clause applies above 7 million. The final anticipated value including all options is 8 million, so the clause threshold is met even though the base price alone is below it. Separately, a FAR change takes effect after a solicitation was issued. The change does not apply to that solicitation automatically. The contracting officer may include it, if award comes on or after the effective date.

## Never confuse

Unless otherwise specified, a specific dollar threshold for the purpose of applicability is the final anticipated dollar value of the action, including the dollar value of all options [@CL-CMP-2010]. A new certification requirement may be included in the FAR chapter if it is specifically imposed by statute, or if written justification is provided to the Administrator for Federal Procurement Policy by the Federal Acquisition Regulatory Council and the Administrator approves its inclusion in writing [@CL-CMP-2008; @CL-CMP-2009].
