## Purpose

Shows which drawings and other works become Government property, what the Government may do with shop drawings, and the duty to get approval and give notice before using a sole source design.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-CMP-4278] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 252.227-7023, 252.227-7033 and 252.227-7024 [@CL-CMP-4278; @CL-CMP-4281; @CL-CMP-4282] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-4278] |
| Applies to | Contracts using the property clause where DFARS 227.7107-1(b) prescribes it [@CL-CMP-4376]; the prescriptions for the shop drawings and restricted design clauses are not restated on this card |

Under the Drawings and Other Data to Become Property of Government clause, all designs, drawings, specifications, notes and other works developed in the performance of the contract become the sole property of the Government and may be used on any other design or construction without additional compensation to the Contractor [@CL-CMP-4278]. The Government is considered the person for whom the work was prepared for the purpose of authorship in any copyrightable work under section 201(b) of title 17 of the United States Code [@CL-CMP-4377], and the Contractor agrees, with respect to that authorship, not to assert or authorize others to assert any rights nor establish any claim under the design patent or copyright laws [@CL-CMP-4379]. The Contractor also agrees, for three years after completion of the project, to furnish all retained works on the request of the Contracting Officer, and unless the contract provides otherwise the Contractor has the right to retain copies of all works beyond that period [@CL-CMP-4279].

In the Rights in Shop Drawings clause, shop drawings for construction are drawings submitted to the Government by the Construction Contractor, subcontractor or any lower-tier subcontractor pursuant to a construction contract, showing in detail first the proposed fabrication and assembly of structural elements [@CL-CMP-4280]. Under that clause the Government may duplicate, use and disclose in any manner and for any purpose shop drawings delivered under the contract [@CL-CMP-4281]. The clause, including its subcontract paragraph, is to be included in all subcontracts under the contract at any tier [@CL-CMP-4378].

Under the Notice and Approval of Restricted Designs clause, the Contractor shall, to the extent practicable, make maximum use of structures, machines, products, materials, construction methods and equipment that are readily available through Government or competitive commercial channels, or through standard or proven production techniques, methods and processes [@CL-CMP-4380]. Unless approved by the Contracting Officer, the Contractor shall not produce a design or specification that requires in the construction work the use of structures, products, materials, construction equipment or processes that are known by the Contractor to be available only from a sole source [@CL-CMP-4282]. The Contractor shall promptly report any such design or specification to the Contracting Officer and give the reason why it is considered necessary to so restrict the design or specification [@CL-CMP-4283].

## How it works

Works developed in performance become the sole property of the Government, which may reuse them without extra payment, and the Contractor agrees not to assert design patent or copyright claims with respect to the authorship the clause describes [@CL-CMP-4278; @CL-CMP-4377; @CL-CMP-4379]. The Contractor must hand over retained works on request for a set period but may keep copies afterwards unless the contract says otherwise [@CL-CMP-4279]. Shop drawings are a separate class with a broad Government right to copy, use and disclose, and the shop drawings clause must flow down to subcontracts at any tier [@CL-CMP-4280; @CL-CMP-4281; @CL-CMP-4378]. The restricted design clause asks for maximum practicable use of readily available items, then works through approval and prompt reporting for sole source designs [@CL-CMP-4380; @CL-CMP-4282; @CL-CMP-4283].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A construction contractor develops structural drawings under the contract. The drawings become Government property and the Government reuses them on a second building without paying more. A steel fabricator submits shop drawings under the construction contract, which the Government may copy, use and disclose for any purpose, and the contractor includes the shop drawings clause in the fabricator's subcontract. The contractor's design calls for a fitting sold by one supplier only. The contractor needs the Contracting Officer's approval and must promptly report the design with its reason.

## Never confuse

Ownership of the works under the property clause is different from a license in technical data; see [Technical data rights categories](cmp-dod-technical-data-rights-categories.md) and [Rights in data, general](cmp-rights-in-data-general-definitions-and-allocation.md). Shop drawings under the shop drawings clause carry their own Government right to duplicate, use and disclose [@CL-CMP-4281]; the other works developed in performing the contract become Government property under the property clause [@CL-CMP-4278]. The existing works and unlimited rights clauses are in [Rights in data for existing works and Government unlimited rights](cmp-dfars-existing-works-and-unlimited-rights-in-data.md). The restricted design clause concerns sole source designs known to the Contractor, not every design choice [@CL-CMP-4282].
