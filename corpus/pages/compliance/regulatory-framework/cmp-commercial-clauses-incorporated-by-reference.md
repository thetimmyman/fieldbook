## Purpose

Shows the seven FAR clauses that paragraph (a) of the clause on contract terms and conditions required to implement statutes or Executive orders incorporates by reference in a commercial contract, and what the paragraph says about each.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-9039] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.212-5(a) [@CL-CMP-9039] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.212-5 is [Reserved] [@CL-CMP-0644] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-9039] |
| Applies to | Contracts in which the clause is inserted as prescribed in FAR 12.301(b)(4) [@CL-CMP-9201] |

The Contractor shall comply with the FAR clauses listed in paragraph (a), which are incorporated in the contract by reference, to implement provisions of law or Executive orders applicable to acquisitions of commercial products and commercial services [@CL-CMP-9039]. The duty falls on the Contractor, and the paragraph has no contracting officer indication step for these entries [@CL-CMP-9039].

For each entry the paragraph gives the clause number and title and the statute that the entry cites. Paragraph (a)(1) lists 52.203-19, Prohibition on Requiring Certain Internal Confidentiality Agreements or Statements, and cites section 743 of Division E, Title VII, of the Consolidated and Further Continuing Appropriations Act, 2015 [@CL-CMP-9040]. That citation extends to the successor provisions of the section in subsequent appropriations acts, as extended in continuing resolutions [@CL-CMP-9202]. Paragraph (a)(2) lists 52.204-23, Prohibition on Contracting for Hardware, Software, and Services Developed or Provided by Kaspersky Lab Covered Entities, and cites section 1634 of Public Law 115-91 [@CL-CMP-9041]. Paragraph (a)(3) lists 52.204-25, Prohibition on Contracting for Certain Telecommunications and Video Surveillance Services or Equipment, and cites section 889(a)(1)(A) of Public Law 115-232 [@CL-CMP-9042]. Paragraph (a)(4) lists 52.209-10, Prohibition on Contracting with Inverted Domestic Corporations [@CL-CMP-9043]. Paragraph (a)(5) lists 52.232-40, Providing Accelerated Payments to Small Business Subcontractors, and cites 31 United States Code section 3903 and 10 United States Code section 3801 [@CL-CMP-9203]. Paragraph (a)(6) lists 52.233-3, Protest After Award, and cites 31 United States Code section 3553 [@CL-CMP-9044]. Paragraph (a)(7) lists 52.233-4, Applicable Law for Breach of Contract Claim, and cites Public Laws 108-77 and 108-78 [@CL-CMP-9204].

## How it works

The Contractor's duty comes from the list itself, so it begins when the contract includes the clause and needs no check mark [@CL-CMP-9201; @CL-CMP-9039]. The paragraph gives each entry's title and authority, which lets a reader trace the duty to its source [@CL-CMP-9040; @CL-CMP-9041; @CL-CMP-9042]. It does not restate what each listed clause requires, so the scope, thresholds, deadlines and reports of each clause are read in that clause, and the linked cards cover several of them [@CL-CMP-9039].

Which clauses must be flowed down to a commercial subcontract is a separate question for a later paragraph of the clause, and the linked flowdown card covers it.

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text from February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. RFO FAR 52.212-5 is [Reserved], so the codified paragraph (a) list of clauses incorporated by reference has no text under that section number in the RFO text [@CL-CMP-0644; @CL-CMP-9039].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A commercial products contract includes the clause. The contractor's compliance register lists the seven entries in paragraph (a): the confidentiality agreements prohibition [@CL-CMP-9040], the Kaspersky Lab prohibition [@CL-CMP-9041], the telecommunications and video surveillance prohibition [@CL-CMP-9042], the inverted domestic corporation prohibition [@CL-CMP-9043], accelerated payments to small business subcontractors [@CL-CMP-9203], protest after award [@CL-CMP-9044], and the law for a breach of contract claim [@CL-CMP-9204]. No box was checked for any of them, because paragraph (a) incorporates them by reference, unlike the selection lists that depend on the Contracting Officer's indication [@CL-CMP-9039; @CL-CMP-9045]. The register records the clause that each entry names and assigns an owner for each duty, and it keeps a separate section for the entries the contracting officer checks in the selection paragraphs [@CL-CMP-9045].

## Never confuse

The by-reference clauses in paragraph (a) are not the selected clauses that follow, because those are the clauses the contracting officer has indicated [@CL-CMP-9039; @CL-CMP-9045]. A listed clause and the statute beside it are different things: the clause is the contract term, and the statute is the authority the entry cites [@CL-CMP-9040]. The inverted domestic corporation clause in paragraph (a)(4) is a contract clause, which is not the offeror's representation that the offeror provision asks for at the offer stage [@CL-CMP-9043]. The paragraph (a) list is not RFO FAR 52.212-5 text, because the RFO text lists that section as [Reserved] [@CL-CMP-9039; @CL-CMP-0644].

## Company calibration

The company must confirm that its contract register records these by-reference clauses and the owner of each compliance duty [@Q-004].
