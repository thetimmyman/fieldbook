## Purpose

Shows how an alteration to a contract is introduced, and how a provision or clause used with an authorized deviation is marked in the solicitation or contract.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-6005] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.252-4; FAR 52.252-5; FAR 52.252-6 [@CL-CMP-6005; @CL-CMP-6007; @CL-CMP-6010] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-6005] |
| Applies to | Solicitations and contracts that carry an alteration, and solicitations and contracts that use a provision or clause with an authorized deviation [@CL-CMP-6005; @CL-CMP-6702; @CL-CMP-6703] |

The alterations clause is inserted in solicitations and contracts to revise or supplement other parts of the contract, or parts of the solicitation that apply after contract award, except for any clause authorized for use with a deviation [@CL-CMP-6005]. It must include clear identification of what is being altered [@CL-CMP-6006]. The clause states that portions of the contract are altered as follows [@CL-CMP-6701].

When a FAR or supplemental provision or clause is used with an authorized deviation, the contracting officer identifies it by the same number, title, and date assigned to it when it is used without deviation, includes the regulation name for any supplemental provision or clause, and inserts (DEVIATION) after the date [@CL-CMP-6007; @CL-CMP-6010].

## How it works

### Alterations

The standard text of the alterations clause ends after the statement that portions of the contract are altered as follows, and it does not list any alteration [@CL-CMP-6701]. The clause must include clear identification of what is being altered [@CL-CMP-6006].

### Deviations in provisions

The authorized deviations provision is inserted in solicitations that include any FAR or supplemental provision with an authorized deviation [@CL-CMP-6702]. The use in the solicitation of any FAR provision with an authorized deviation is indicated by the addition of (DEVIATION) after the date of the provision [@CL-CMP-6008]. The use of a supplemental provision with an authorized deviation is indicated by the addition of (DEVIATION) after the name of the regulation [@CL-CMP-6009].

### Deviations in clauses

The authorized deviations clause is inserted in solicitations and contracts that include any FAR or supplemental clause with an authorized deviation [@CL-CMP-6703]. The use of any FAR clause with an authorized deviation is indicated by the addition of (DEVIATION) after the date of the clause [@CL-CMP-6012]. The use of a supplemental clause with an authorized deviation is indicated by the addition of (DEVIATION) after the name of the regulation [@CL-CMP-6011].

### Blanks

In paragraph (b) of both texts, the regulation name and the 48 CFR reference are blanks, with a bracketed instruction to insert the regulation name, to be completed for the regulation concerned [@CL-CMP-6009; @CL-CMP-6011].

### Two statements about the marker

The opening sentence of each text says the contracting officer inserts (DEVIATION) after the date [@CL-CMP-6007; @CL-CMP-6010]. Paragraph (b) of each text says the marker for a supplemental provision or clause comes after the name of the regulation [@CL-CMP-6009; @CL-CMP-6011]. This card reports both statements and does not resolve how they fit together.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contract carries the alterations clause, which says that portions of the contract are altered as follows. The contracting officer then writes under it that the delivery paragraph for one line item is changed, and identifies the paragraph clearly [@CL-CMP-6701; @CL-CMP-6006].

A FAR clause is used in the same contract with an authorized deviation. It keeps the number, title and date it has without the deviation, so a clause dated JAN 2020 appears with its usual number and title, and the contracting officer inserts (DEVIATION) after the date [@CL-CMP-6010; @CL-CMP-6012]. The contract therefore also includes the authorized deviations clause [@CL-CMP-6703]. A clause from a departmental supplement carries the name of that regulation [@CL-CMP-6010]. Paragraph (b) shows its use with an authorized deviation by (DEVIATION) after the name of the regulation, while the opening sentence puts the marker after the date, and this card does not resolve how the two fit together [@CL-CMP-6011; @CL-CMP-6010].

## Never confuse

An alteration and a deviation are different. The alterations clause revises or supplements other parts of the contract and is not used for a clause authorized for use with a deviation [@CL-CMP-6005]. A provision or clause used with an authorized deviation keeps its number, title and date and is marked with (DEVIATION) [@CL-CMP-6007; @CL-CMP-6010].

The solicitation-phase alterations provision is on [Provisions and clauses incorporated by reference, and alterations in a solicitation](cmp-incorporation-by-reference-and-solicitation-alterations.md). The authorized deviations provision applies to solicitations, whereas the authorized deviations clause applies to solicitations and contracts [@CL-CMP-6702; @CL-CMP-6703].
