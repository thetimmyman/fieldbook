## Purpose

Shows what the construction wage rate terms mean and which contracts the requirements reach, so a company can tell whether work on a contract carries a prevailing wage duty.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-3000] |
| Codified citation | FAR 22.400 [@CL-CMP-10003; @CL-CMP-10004]; FAR 22.401, FAR 22.402 and FAR 22.403-1 [@CL-CMP-3000; @CL-CMP-3008; @CL-CMP-3100] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-3000] |
| Applies to | Contracts for construction work, and construction work inside nonconstruction contracts that meets the listed conditions [@CL-CMP-3008; @CL-CMP-3009; @CL-CMP-3010] |

The subpart implements the statutes which prescribe labor standards requirements for contracts in excess of $2,000 for construction, alteration, or repair, including painting and decorating, of public buildings and public works [@CL-CMP-10003]. Labor relations requirements prescribed in other subparts of part 22 may also apply [@CL-CMP-10004]. The definitions fix who and what is covered. The apprentice definition includes a person employed and individually registered in a bona fide apprenticeship program registered with the Department of Labor's Office of Apprenticeship Training, Employer, and Labor Services (OATELS) or with a State Apprenticeship Agency recognized by OATELS [@CL-CMP-3000]. Another listed alternative covers a person in the first 90 days of probationary employment as an apprentice in an apprenticeship program who is not individually registered in the program but has been certified by OATELS or a State Apprenticeship Agency (where appropriate) to be eligible [@CL-CMP-3001; @CL-CMP-3000]. A trainee is a person registered and receiving on-the-job training in a construction occupation under a program approved in advance by the Department of Labor [@CL-CMP-3006], approved by OATELS as meeting its standards for on-the-job training programs, and so certified by that Administration [@CL-CMP-3601]. Construction, alteration, or repair means all types of work done by laborers and mechanics employed by the construction contractor or subcontractor on a particular building or work at the site thereof, including without limitation the types of work the section lists [@CL-CMP-3002]. The laborers or mechanics definition includes workers, used by a contractor or subcontractor at any tier, whose duties are manual or physical in nature, as distinguished from mental or managerial [@CL-CMP-3003]. A public building or public work is building or work whose construction, prosecution, completion, or repair is carried on directly by authority of, or with funds of, a Federal agency to serve the interest of the general public [@CL-CMP-3004], and that definition applies regardless of whether title to the building or work is in a Federal agency [@CL-CMP-3105]. The site of the work includes the primary site, which is the physical place or places where the construction called for in the contract will remain when work on it is completed [@CL-CMP-3005]. The site of the work does not include permanent home offices, branch plant establishments, fabrication plants, or tool yards of a contractor or subcontractor whose locations and continuance in operation are determined wholly without regard to a particular Federal contract or project [@CL-CMP-3104]. The wages definition includes the basic hourly rate of pay and any contribution irrevocably made by a contractor or subcontractor to a trustee or third person under a bona fide fringe benefit fund, plan, or program [@CL-CMP-3007].

For construction work, the requirements apply only if the work is, or reasonably can be foreseen to be, performed at a particular site so that wage rates can be determined for the locality, and only to construction work performed by laborers and mechanics at the site of the work [@CL-CMP-3008]. For a nonconstruction contract, such as a supply or service contract, the requirements apply to construction work in it if listed conditions are met, the first being that the work is to be performed on a public building or public work [@CL-CMP-3009]. Another listed condition is that the contract contains specific requirements for a substantial amount of construction work exceeding the monetary threshold for application of the Construction Wage Rate Requirements statute, and the word substantial relates to the type and quantity of construction work to be performed and not merely to the total value of construction work as compared to the total value of the contract [@CL-CMP-3010; @CL-CMP-3009; @CL-CMP-8526]. The last listed condition is that the construction work is physically or functionally separate from, and capable of being performed on a segregated basis from, the other work required by the contract [@CL-CMP-3101; @CL-CMP-3009]. The applicability paragraph refers to the monetary threshold without stating an amount, but the section on the statute states it: the statute provides that contracts in excess of $2,000 to which the United States or the District of Columbia is a party for construction, alteration, or repair of public buildings or public works within the United States shall contain a clause [@CL-CMP-3100; @CL-CMP-3010]. The clause section also uses a contract value of $2,000 for inserting the construction labor clauses in solicitations and contracts for construction within the United States [@CL-CMP-3043].

The requirements of the subpart do not apply if the construction work is incidental to the furnishing of supplies, equipment, or services [@CL-CMP-3102]. Under that exclusion, the requirements still apply if a substantial and segregable amount of construction, alteration, or repair is required, such as for installation of heavy generators or large refrigerator systems or for plant modification or rearrangement [@CL-CMP-3103; @CL-CMP-3102]. The sections list further exclusions, such as off-site manufacturing by a commercial supplier and work merged with nonconstruction work, that this card does not restate.

## How it works

Coverage is decided from the printed definitions and applicability text. The work must be construction, alteration, or repair at the site of the work, done by laborers and mechanics [@CL-CMP-3002; @CL-CMP-3008]. For work inside a nonconstruction contract, the listed conditions are checked, and this card drafts three of them: a public building or public work, a substantial amount of construction work above the statute's monetary threshold, and work that is physically or functionally separate [@CL-CMP-3009; @CL-CMP-3010; @CL-CMP-3101]. Work that is merely incidental to supplying equipment is excluded unless a substantial and segregable amount of construction is required [@CL-CMP-3102; @CL-CMP-3103]. Pay is then measured against the wages definition, which counts both the basic hourly rate and irrevocable fringe contributions [@CL-CMP-3007]. How the matching wage determination is chosen and requested is on [Construction wage determinations: general and project types and how each is requested](cmp-construction-wage-determination-types-and-requests.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A supply contract worth 800,000 includes installing heavy generators at a federal building. The installation is a substantial amount of construction work that is physically separate from the rest of the contract and can be performed on its own, and it is far above the statute's monetary threshold. It is not merely incidental to supplying the equipment. The same contract also has parts built at the contractor's permanent plant far from the site, a plant whose location and operation are decided without regard to this project. The installation work done by laborers and mechanics at the federal building is covered. The plant work away from the site is not covered.

## Never confuse

Construction wage rates and service contract wage rates are different regimes: this card covers the Construction Wage Rate Requirements statute, while the service contract rules are on [Service contract labor standards clause and wage determinations](../../operating-model/talent/opm-service-contract-labor-standards.md). The money rules for withholding and suspension are on [Construction wage rate requirements: withholding from and suspension of contract payments](cmp-construction-wage-withholding-and-suspension.md), and the clauses that carry these requirements into a contract are listed on [Construction labor standards disputes and the contract clauses and provision to insert](cmp-construction-labor-disputes-and-contract-clauses.md). Wages as defined here are not the basic cash rate alone: the definition includes the basic hourly rate of pay and any contribution irrevocably made by a contractor or subcontractor to a trustee or third person under a bona fide fringe benefit fund, plan, or program [@CL-CMP-3007].
