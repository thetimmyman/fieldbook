## Purpose

Shows the conditions an offeror certifies to exempt certain services from Service Contract Labor Standards, and what follows when the exemption fails.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-4840] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.222-52 and FAR 52.222-53 [@CL-CMP-4988; @CL-CMP-4846] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.222-52 [@CL-CMP-5316; @CL-CMP-5317; @CL-CMP-9802; @CL-CMP-9803; @CL-CMP-9804; @CL-CMP-5318; @CL-CMP-5319; @CL-CMP-5320; @CL-CMP-5321; @CL-CMP-5322; @CL-CMP-9805; @CL-CMP-9806; @CL-CMP-9807; @CL-CMP-5323]; RFO FAR 52.222-53 [@CL-CMP-5324; @CL-CMP-5325; @CL-CMP-5326; @CL-CMP-5327; @CL-CMP-5328; @CL-CMP-5329; @CL-CMP-5330; @CL-CMP-5331] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-4840] |
| Applies to | Solicitations and contracts for services that meet the certified exemption conditions [@CL-CMP-4840; @CL-CMP-4988] |

The offeror checks a certification that it does or does not certify to the listed conditions [@CL-CMP-4988]. The first condition is that the services are offered and sold regularly to non-Governmental customers and are provided by the offeror, or by the subcontractor in the case of an exempt subcontract, to the general public in substantial quantities in the course of normal business operations [@CL-CMP-4840]. The second is that the services are furnished at prices that are, or are based on, established catalog or market prices [@CL-CMP-4841]. The third is that each service employee who will perform the services spends only a small portion of his or her time, measured as a monthly average of less than 20 percent of the available hours on an annualized basis [@CL-CMP-4842]. If the contract period is less than a month, the limit is less than 20 percent of the available hours during the contract period [@CL-CMP-4989]. The fourth is that the offeror uses the same compensation plan for all service employees performing work under the contract as it uses for these employees and for equivalent employees servicing commercial customers [@CL-CMP-4843].

An established catalog price is a price included in a catalog, price list, schedule or other form that is regularly maintained by the manufacturer or the offeror and is published or otherwise available for inspection by customers [@CL-CMP-4990]. It also states prices at which sales currently, or were last, made to a significant number of buyers constituting the general public [@CL-CMP-4991]. An established market price is a current price, established in the usual course of ordinary and usual trade between buyers and sellers free to bargain, which can be substantiated from sources independent of the manufacturer or offeror [@CL-CMP-4992]. Certification by the offeror about its compliance with respect to the contract also certifies the compliance of a subcontractor if the offeror subcontracts out the exempt services [@CL-CMP-4844].

## How it works

If the offeror certifies to the conditions, the contracting officer determines under the FAR whether the Service Contract Labor Standards statute applies to the offeror [@CL-CMP-4993]. If the contracting officer determines that the statute will not apply, the Service Contract Labor Standards clause in the solicitation is not included in any resultant contract to that offeror [@CL-CMP-4994]. If the contracting officer determines that the statute will apply, the requirements clause for the services exemption is not included in any resultant contract awarded to that offeror [@CL-CMP-4995], and the offeror may be provided an opportunity to submit a new offer on that basis [@CL-CMP-4996].

If the offeror does not certify to the conditions [@CL-CMP-4997], the requirements clause is not included in any resultant contract to that offeror [@CL-CMP-4998]. The offeror must notify the contracting officer as soon as possible if the contracting officer did not attach a Service Contract Labor Standards wage determination to the solicitation [@CL-CMP-4999]. The contracting officer may not make an award to the offeror if the offeror fails to execute the certification or to contact the contracting officer as the provision requires [@CL-CMP-4845].

Under the requirements clause the contractor is responsible for compliance with all the conditions of the exemption by its subcontractors [@CL-CMP-5000]. The clause gives two alternatives for selecting an exempt services subcontractor. Except for services identified in the FAR exclusion, one is selection on other factors in addition to price or cost, with the combination of other factors at least as important as price or cost [@CL-CMP-4846]. The other is an award on a sole source basis [@CL-CMP-5001].

The contractor must determine in advance, from the nature of the subcontract requirements and knowledge of the practices of likely subcontractors, that all or nearly all likely subcontractors will meet the conditions in paragraphs (a) through (d) of the requirements clause [@CL-CMP-4847]. If the services are currently being performed under a subcontract, the contractor must consider the practices of the existing subcontractor in that determination [@CL-CMP-5002]. If the contractor has reason to doubt the validity of the certification, the requirements of the Service Contract Labor Standards statute must be included in the subcontract [@CL-CMP-4848].

If the Department of Labor determines that any condition for the exemption has not been met, the exemption is deemed inapplicable and the contract becomes subject to the Service Contract Labor Standards statute [@CL-CMP-4849]. The procedures in the Department of Labor regulations that the clause names will then be followed [@CL-CMP-5003]. The contractor must include the substance of the requirements clause, including its flowdown paragraph, in subcontracts for exempt services under the contract [@CL-CMP-4850].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text under Class Deviation 2026-O0038, effective February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. The RFO certification provision keeps the four certified conditions: regular sale to non-Governmental customers, catalog or market prices, a small portion of each service employee's time with the 20 percent thresholds, and the same compensation plan [@CL-CMP-5316; @CL-CMP-5317; @CL-CMP-5318; @CL-CMP-5319; @CL-CMP-5320; @CL-CMP-4840; @CL-CMP-4841; @CL-CMP-4842; @CL-CMP-4989; @CL-CMP-4843]. It keeps the rule that certification also certifies a subcontractor's compliance [@CL-CMP-5321; @CL-CMP-4844] and the bar on award where the offeror fails to execute the certification or to contact the contracting officer [@CL-CMP-5323; @CL-CMP-4845]. It says the contracting officer's determination whether the statute applies is made in accordance with FAR 22.1002-1(f)(3), where the codified provision cites FAR 22.1003-4(d)(3) [@CL-CMP-5322; @CL-CMP-4993]. It keeps the definitions of an established catalog price and an established market price [@CL-CMP-9802; @CL-CMP-9803; @CL-CMP-9804; @CL-CMP-4990; @CL-CMP-4991; @CL-CMP-4992]. It keeps the outcomes of that determination: if the statute will not apply, the Service Contract Labor Standards clause in the solicitation will not be included in any resultant contract to the offeror [@CL-CMP-9805; @CL-CMP-4994], and if it will apply, the clause at FAR 52.222-53 will not be included in any resultant contract awarded to the offeror, which may be provided an opportunity to submit a new offer on that basis [@CL-CMP-9806; @CL-CMP-9807; @CL-CMP-4995; @CL-CMP-4996]. The RFO requirements clause says the subcontractor for exempt services must be selected for award on other factors in addition to price or cost, with the combination of other factors at least as important as price or cost, except for services identified in FAR 22.1002-1(f)(1)(iv), where the codified clause says "shall" and cites FAR 22.1003-4(d)(1)(iv) [@CL-CMP-5324; @CL-CMP-4846], and keeps the alternative of a sole source award [@CL-CMP-5325; @CL-CMP-5001]. It keeps the contractor's responsibility for its subcontractors' compliance [@CL-CMP-5326; @CL-CMP-5000], its duty to determine in advance that all or nearly all likely subcontractors will meet the conditions [@CL-CMP-5327; @CL-CMP-4847], to consider the practices of an existing subcontractor [@CL-CMP-5328; @CL-CMP-5002], and to include the Service Contract Labor Standards requirements in the subcontract where it has reason to doubt the certification [@CL-CMP-5329; @CL-CMP-4848]. It keeps the rule that the exemption is deemed inapplicable if the Department of Labor determines that a condition has not been met [@CL-CMP-5330; @CL-CMP-4849], and the flowdown of the substance of the clause [@CL-CMP-5331; @CL-CMP-4850].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A firm sells vehicle maintenance to commercial customers from a published price list. Assume the service is one of the listed exempt services on the administrative exemptions card. Its crews spend about 10 percent of their annual hours on one government contract, and pay follows the same plan as for commercial work. The offeror can certify all four conditions. If one crew member instead spent a quarter of the available hours on the government contract, the offeror could not certify the time condition. On a contract that lasts only two weeks, the same test compares the time spent with the available hours in those two weeks.

## Never confuse

This services exemption is not the equipment exemption on [exemption for equipment maintenance](cmp-scls-equipment-exemption.md): it adds a limit on the share of each employee's time spent on the government contract [@CL-CMP-4842] and rules on how exempt subcontracts are awarded [@CL-CMP-4846; @CL-CMP-5001]. The listed exempt services and the administrative exemptions by contract type are on [administrative exemptions](cmp-scls-exemptions-and-variations.md).
