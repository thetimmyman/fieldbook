## Purpose

Shows what an offeror certifies about biobased products by signing its offer, what counts as a biobased product, and what a contractor reports about the biobased products it buys during performance.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-5164] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.223-1 and FAR 52.223-2 [@CL-CMP-5164; @CL-CMP-5166] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.223-1 [@CL-CMP-0528] and RFO FAR 52.223-2 [@CL-CMP-0534] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-5164] |
| Applies to | Offerors whose offers carry the biobased product certification provision, and contractors whose contracts carry the biobased reporting clause [@CL-CMP-5164; @CL-CMP-5166] |

The offeror's signature on its offer is itself the certification, and it covers the biobased products in the categories the United States Department of Agriculture lists that the offeror will use or deliver in performing the contract [@CL-CMP-5164].

Biobased products that the offeror does not purchase as a direct result of the contract fall outside the certification, and for the products that stay inside it the offeror certifies that they will comply with the applicable specifications or other contractual requirements [@CL-CMP-5165].

In the reporting clause a biobased product is a product that USDA determines to be a commercial or industrial product, other than food or feed, that is either composed in whole or in significant part of biological products, including renewable domestic agricultural materials and forestry materials, or is an intermediate ingredient or feedstock [@CL-CMP-5264; @CL-CMP-5265]. The biological products include renewable domestic agricultural materials and forestry materials [@CL-CMP-5265]. For forestry materials the term includes forest products that meet biobased content requirements, whatever market share the product holds, its age, or whether its market is new or emerging [@CL-CMP-5266]. A USDA-designated product category is a generic grouping of products that are or can be made with biobased materials, that USDA lists in a procurement guideline, and for which USDA has provided purchasing recommendations [@CL-CMP-5267].

## How it works

The reporting duty is separate from the certification and falls on the contractor [@CL-CMP-5166].

The contractor reports to the website the clause names, with a copy to the contracting officer, on the product types and dollar value of biobased products in USDA-designated product categories that it purchased during the previous Government fiscal year, which runs between October 1 and September 30 [@CL-CMP-5166], and it submits the report no later than October 31 of each year during contract performance and at the end of contract performance [@CL-CMP-5167].

The report is due no later than October 31 of each year during contract performance, and again at the end of contract performance [@CL-CMP-5167].

Under the RFO text, the DoD class deviation directs DoD contracting officers to use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text, effective February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. The RFO certification provision says the offeror certifies, by signing the offer, for biobased products within categories the Department of Agriculture lists at a web address instead of in 7 CFR part 3201, subpart B [@CL-CMP-0528; @CL-CMP-5164]. It keeps the exclusion for biobased products not purchased as a direct result of the contract and the compliance statement for the rest [@CL-CMP-0529; @CL-CMP-5165]. The RFO reporting clause keeps the definition of a biobased product, including the biological products limb, the intermediate ingredient or feedstock limb, and the forestry materials sentence [@CL-CMP-0530; @CL-CMP-0531; @CL-CMP-0532; @CL-CMP-5264; @CL-CMP-5265; @CL-CMP-5266]. It describes a USDA-designated product category differently [@CL-CMP-0533; @CL-CMP-5267]. It keeps the Contractor's duty under paragraph (b) to report on the product types and dollar value of biobased products purchased during the previous Government fiscal year, to the website the clause names with a copy to the contracting officer [@CL-CMP-0622; @CL-CMP-0534; @CL-CMP-5166], and keeps the deadlines of October 31 each year and the end of contract performance [@CL-CMP-0622; @CL-CMP-0535; @CL-CMP-5167].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A service contractor signs its offer, which makes the biobased certification: the biobased floor cleaners it will use under the contract will comply with the contract's specifications. A biobased product it would buy anyway, not as a direct result of the contract, is outside that certification. During the Government fiscal year that ends on September 30 the contractor buys 40,000 dollars of cleaning products in one USDA-designated category and 12,500 dollars in another. By October 31 it reports both product types and the combined value of 52,500 dollars to the website the clause names, with a copy to the contracting officer, and it files another report at the end of contract performance.

## Never confuse

The certification and the report are different duties: the offeror gives the certification by signing its offer, while the contractor makes the report to the website the clause names, with a copy to the contracting officer [@CL-CMP-5164; @CL-CMP-5166].

A later purchase report does not replace the certification, because the signature on the offer is the certification event [@CL-CMP-5164].

Biobased content is not recovered material content: recovered material content is certified separately for EPA-designated items [@CL-CMP-5176].

A USDA-designated product category is described with USDA purchasing recommendations in the codified clause [@CL-CMP-5267], but with minimum biobased content standards in the RFO clause [@CL-CMP-0533].

## Company calibration

Whether the buying records can produce the product types and dollar value purchased in each USDA-designated product category for the Government fiscal year, and which contracts carry the reporting clause. [@Q-004]
