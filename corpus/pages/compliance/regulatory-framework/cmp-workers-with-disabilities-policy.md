## Purpose

Sets out the three duties the policy section places on contractors and subcontractors for qualified individuals with disabilities, names who each duty falls on, and states what the section leaves to other texts.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-8158] |
| Codified citation | FAR 22.1400 and FAR 22.1401(a), (b) and (c) [@CL-CMP-8823; @CL-CMP-8824; @CL-CMP-8159; @CL-CMP-8160; @CL-CMP-8161] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-8158] |
| Applies to | Contractors and subcontractors, when entering into contracts and subcontracts subject to the Act [@CL-CMP-8158] |

The section states what contractors and subcontractors are required to do when they enter into contracts and subcontracts subject to the Act [@CL-CMP-8158]. The text printed in this section does not name the Act. The scope section of the subpart does: the subpart prescribes policies and procedures for implementing section 503 of the Rehabilitation Act of 1973, as amended (section 793 of title 29 of the United States Code), called the Act, Executive Order 11758 of January 15, 1974, and the regulations of the Secretary of Labor at 41 CFR part 60-741 [@CL-CMP-8823]. In the subpart, the terms contract and contractor include subcontract and subcontractor [@CL-CMP-8824].

Paragraph (a): they must take affirmative action to employ, and advance in employment, qualified individuals with disabilities, and must otherwise treat qualified individuals without discrimination based on their physical or mental disability [@CL-CMP-8159].

Paragraph (b): they must undertake appropriate outreach and positive recruitment activities that are reasonably designed to effectively recruit qualified individuals with disabilities [@CL-CMP-8160].

Paragraph (c): they must compare the utilization of individuals with disabilities in their workforces to the utilization goal, as prescribed in the regulations of the Secretary of Labor, on an annual basis [@CL-CMP-8161].

## How it works

All three duties fall on the contractor or subcontractor. The section states no duty for a contracting officer or an agency [@CL-CMP-8158].

The duties attach when a contractor or subcontractor enters into a contract or subcontract subject to the Act [@CL-CMP-8158].

Two duties concern conduct. Paragraph (a) covers affirmative action in employment and advancement, and nondiscrimination based on physical or mental disability. Paragraph (b) covers outreach and positive recruitment that is reasonably designed to be effective [@CL-CMP-8159; @CL-CMP-8160].

The third duty is a measurement. The contractor compares the utilization of individuals with disabilities in its workforce to the utilization goal [@CL-CMP-8161].

The Secretary of Labor's regulations prescribe the goal. The section does not state the goal, so its figure is Not established here [@CL-CMP-8161].

The comparison is made on an annual basis. The section gives no date in the year and no filing, and it does not say who in the company decides or signs off on the comparison [@CL-CMP-8161].

The section states no threshold. It does not say what follows if utilization is below the goal, and the result of such a comparison is Not established here [@CL-CMP-8161].

The disability equal opportunity clause is on [Workers with disabilities and the statement of equivalent rates for Federal hires](cmp-workers-with-disabilities-and-equivalent-rates.md). Waivers are on [Forced or indentured child labor list and waivers for workers with disabilities](cmp-forced-child-labor-list-and-disability-waivers.md). Neither is restated here.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor enters into a contract subject to the Act and has a workforce of 400 employees. It counts 16 individuals with disabilities in that workforce, and the goal in the Secretary of Labor's regulations is assumed to be 7 percent. Sixteen of 400 is 4 percent, so utilization is below the assumed goal.

The contractor makes the comparison every year, as paragraph (c) requires [@CL-CMP-8161]. The section does not say what follows from the shortfall [@CL-CMP-8161].

Running the comparison does not satisfy the other two duties. The contractor must still take affirmative action under paragraph (a) and undertake outreach under paragraph (b) [@CL-CMP-8159; @CL-CMP-8160].

## Never confuse

Never confuse the outreach duty with the affirmative action duty. Outreach and positive recruitment are a separate requirement from taking affirmative action to employ and advance, and the utilization comparison is a third requirement, so completing one does not discharge the others [@CL-CMP-8159; @CL-CMP-8160; @CL-CMP-8161].

Never confuse the duty to compare with a duty to meet the goal. The section requires the comparison; it states no duty to reach the goal, and the Secretary of Labor's regulations prescribe the goal [@CL-CMP-8161].
