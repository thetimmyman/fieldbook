## Purpose

Shows when a corporate administrative contracting officer (CACO) is assigned, how the location and responsible agency are chosen, and how Government visits to a contractor facility are coordinated.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-2207] |
| Codified citation | FAR 42.602, FAR 42.603 and FAR 42.402 [@CL-CMP-2207; @CL-CMP-2259; @CL-CMP-2213] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-2207] |
| Applies to | Contractors with at least two locations with resident ACOs, or where the agency head or designee approves the need [@CL-CMP-2207] |

A CACO may be assigned only when the contractor has at least two locations with resident administrative contracting officers, or when the agency head or designee approves the need [@CL-CMP-2207]. For the rule on when a CACO may be assigned, a nonresident officer counts as resident if at least 75 percent of that officer's effort is devoted to a single contractor [@CL-CMP-2208].

In choosing where to put the CACO, the responsible agency considers such factors as the location of corporate records, the corporate office, the major plant, the cognizant government auditor and overall cost effectiveness [@CL-CMP-2209]. A decision to start or end the assignment should rest on such factors as the benefits of coordination and liaison at the corporate level and the volume of Government sales [@CL-CMP-2210]. When all locations of a corporate entity sit under a single agency, that agency is responsible for assigning the CACO [@CL-CMP-2211]. When they sit under more than one agency, the agencies agree on the responsible agency, normally the one with the largest dollar balance, including options, of affected contracts [@CL-CMP-2212].

The CACO shall perform, on a corporate-wide basis, the contract administration functions as designated by the responsible agency [@CL-CMP-2259]. Typical functions include the determination of final indirect cost rates for cost-reimbursement contracts [@CL-CMP-2260].

Government personnel planning to visit a contractor facility on one or more Government contracts shall give prior notification to the cognizant contract administration office, sufficiently in advance for it to make arrangements [@CL-CMP-2213]. Where a visit will result in reviewing, auditing or obtaining information from the contractor relating to contract administration functions, the prospective visitor shall identify the information in sufficient detail [@CL-CMP-2258]. If the contract administration office determines that adequate information has recently been reviewed by or is available within the Government, it will discourage the visit and refer the visitor to the office that holds the information [@CL-CMP-2215].

## How it works

The decision to assign a CACO looks to the benefits of coordination and liaison at the corporate level [@CL-CMP-2210]. Which agency assigns the CACO depends on how many agencies administer the contractor's locations [@CL-CMP-2211; @CL-CMP-2212]. Notice of a planned visit goes to the cognizant contract administration office so that it can make arrangements [@CL-CMP-2213]. For a visit that involves information about contract administration functions, that office can steer visitors to information the Government already holds [@CL-CMP-2258; @CL-CMP-2215]. The responsible agency designates the functions a CACO performs [@CL-CMP-2259]. Which functions a given CACO has been designated to perform is not printed in this source. For a corporate ACO's role on IR&D and B&P costs, see [DoD IR&D and B&P responsibilities](../cas-allowability/cmp-dod-ird-bp-responsibilities.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor has plants in two states, each with a resident administrative contracting officer. A CACO may be assigned because there are two such locations. A buying office then plans to visit one plant to ask for rate data that the contract administration office reviewed last month. The office finds the data is already available within the Government, discourages the visit and refers the buying office to where the data is held.

## Never confuse

Visits are not routed through the CACO by this rule. The visit rule sends notice to the cognizant contract administration office, and it does not mention the CACO [@CL-CMP-2213]. For the related cognizant Federal agency role, see [Cognizant Federal agency](../cas-allowability/cmp-cas-cognizant-agency.md). For the wider set of oversight roles, see [Oversight roles](../audit-retention/cmp-oversight-roles.md).
