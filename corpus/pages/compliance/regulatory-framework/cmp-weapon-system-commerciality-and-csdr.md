## Purpose

Shows when a DoD major weapon system, subsystem, component or spare part may be treated as a commercial product, what an offeror must supply for that treatment, how cost and software data reporting (CSDR) is applied before award, and what limits apply to a follow-on item under a broad agency announcement award.

A DoD major weapon system may be treated as a commercial product, or acquired under procedures established for the acquisition of commercial products, only if two conditions are met [@CL-CMP-7400]. The Secretary of Defense determines that the major weapon system is a commercial product as defined in FAR 2.101 and that such treatment is necessary to meet national security objectives [@CL-CMP-7274]. The congressional defense committees are notified at least 30 days before such treatment or acquisition occurs [@CL-CMP-7275]. The Secretary's authority to make the determination may not be delegated below the level of the Deputy Secretary of Defense [@CL-CMP-7276].

## How it works

A subsystem of a major weapon system, other than a commercially available off-the-shelf item, must be treated as a commercial product and acquired under procedures established for the acquisition of commercial products if one of two conditions is met [@CL-CMP-7409]. One is that the subsystem is intended for a major weapon system that is being acquired, or has been acquired, under those procedures in accordance with paragraph (a) of the section [@CL-CMP-7413]. The other is that the contracting officer determines in writing that the subsystem is a commercial product in accordance with DFARS 212.102(a)(iii) [@CL-CMP-7417].

A component or spare part for a major weapon system, other than a commercially available off-the-shelf item, may be treated as a commercial product if one of the conditions below is met [@CL-CMP-7440]. It qualifies if it is intended for a major weapon system acquired, or being acquired, as a commercial product under paragraph (a) [@CL-CMP-7442], or for a subsystem acquired, or being acquired, as a commercial product under paragraph (b) [@CL-CMP-7444]. It also qualifies if the contracting officer determines in writing that it is a commercial product in accordance with DFARS 212.102(a)(iii) [@CL-CMP-7452].

The subsystem rule applies only to subsystems that DoD acquires through a prime contract, a modification to a prime contract, or a subcontract under a prime contract for a subsystem proposed as a commercial product [@CL-CMP-7424]. For a subcontract, the subsystem must be one that has not previously been determined to be a commercial product [@CL-CMP-7618]. The component and spare part rule has the same scope for components and spare parts [@CL-CMP-7425; @CL-CMP-7619]. For a subsystem, component or spare part proposed as a commercial product that has not previously been determined to be one, the offeror requirements in paragraph (d) are followed [@CL-CMP-7620; @CL-CMP-7621].

To the extent necessary to make a commercial product determination in accordance with DFARS 212.102(a)(iii) that relies on paragraph (1), (2), (3), (4) or (5) of the commercial product definition at FAR 2.101 for a subsystem, component or spare part described in paragraphs (b) and (c) [@CL-CMP-7612], the provision at DFARS 252.215-7010 requires the offeror to identify the comparable commercial product the offeror sells to the general public or nongovernmental entities for other than governmental purposes [@CL-CMP-7453]. The offeror must also provide a comparison between the physical characteristics and functionality of that product and the subsystem, component or spare part, including the matters that the paragraph then lists [@CL-CMP-7454]. For products under paragraph (3)(i) of that definition the comparison must include a description of the modification and documentation to support that the modification is customarily available in the marketplace [@CL-CMP-7613], and for products under paragraph (3)(ii) a detailed description of the modification and detailed technical data to demonstrate that the modification is minor [@CL-CMP-7614]. It must also provide the national stock numbers for the comparable commercial product and for the subsystem, component or spare part, if assigned [@CL-CMP-7469]. The paragraph offers those three requirements as an alternative to the next one [@CL-CMP-7469]. If the offeror does not sell a comparable commercial product to the general public or nongovernmental entities for other than governmental purposes, it is required to notify the contracting officer in writing that it does not [@CL-CMP-7470], and also to provide a comparison with the most comparable commercial product in the commercial market, if available [@CL-CMP-7426].

Unless an exception at FAR 15.403-1(b)(1) or (2) applies [@CL-CMP-7615], and to the extent necessary to determine price reasonableness, the contracting officer must require the offeror to submit, or provide access to, a representative sample, as determined by the contracting officer, of prices paid for the same or similar commercial products under comparable terms and conditions [@CL-CMP-7471]. If the officer determines that the offeror cannot provide or give access to sufficient information of that kind to determine the reasonableness of price [@CL-CMP-7616], the officer must require the offeror to submit or provide access to a representative sample, as determined by the contracting officer, of the prices paid for the same or similar commercial products sold under different terms and conditions and the terms and conditions of such sales [@CL-CMP-7617]. The officer must allow the offeror to redact only information under that paragraph that identifies the customer, if the offeror certifies in writing for each sale that the customer is one of the kinds the paragraph lists [@CL-CMP-7472]. If the officer determines that the information submitted is not sufficient because the comparable commercial product is not a valid basis for price analysis or the proposed price is not reasonable after evaluating sales data [@CL-CMP-7473], the officer must obtain approval from an official one level above the contracting officer, without power of delegation, and require the offeror to submit other relevant information regarding the basis for price or cost [@CL-CMP-7474].

An offeror may submit information or analysis relating to the value of a commercial product to aid the determination of price reasonableness [@CL-CMP-7427]. An offeror must not be required to submit the sales information for a commercially available off-the-shelf item [@CL-CMP-7475]. For any other item developed exclusively at private expense, the offeror may be required to submit it only after the head of the contracting activity determines in writing that the information is not sufficient [@CL-CMP-7476].

CSDR is mandatory for major defense acquisition programs as specified in the DoD instruction and the CSDR Manual [@CL-CMP-7277]. Before contract award, the contracting officer must consult with the Defense Cost and Resource Center to determine that the offeror selected for award has proposed a standard CSDR system, as described in its proposal in response to the provision at DFARS 252.234-7003 [@CL-CMP-7278]. That system must be in compliance with the DoD instruction and the CSDR manual that the section names [@CL-CMP-7477].

A contract initially awarded from the competitive selection of a proposal resulting from a broad agency announcement may contain a contract line item or option using funds not limited to those identified in DFARS 235.016 [@CL-CMP-7478], for the development and demonstration or initial production of technology developed under the contract, or the delivery of initial or additional items if the item or a prototype is created as the result of work performed under the contract, only when it adheres to the three limits below [@CL-CMP-7622]. The item or option must be limited to the minimal amount of initial or additional items or prototypes that will allow timely competitive solicitation and award of a follow-on development or production contract [@CL-CMP-7479]. Its term must be not more than 2 years, and its work value must not exceed $100 million in fiscal year 2017 constant dollars [@CL-CMP-7279].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A program office wants to buy a major weapon system under commercial procedures. The Secretary of Defense determines that it is a commercial product and that the treatment is necessary to meet national security objectives, and the congressional defense committees are notified on day 0 for treatment that begins on day 45, which is at least 30 days later [@CL-CMP-7274; @CL-CMP-7275]. The Secretary could not delegate the determination to an official below the Deputy Secretary of Defense [@CL-CMP-7276].

A spare part that is not a commercially available off-the-shelf item is intended for a subsystem that is acquired under commercial procedures under paragraph (b), so it may be treated as a commercial product [@CL-CMP-7440; @CL-CMP-7444]. For another part, the offeror sells no comparable commercial product, notifies the contracting officer in writing, and provides a comparison with the most comparable commercial product in the commercial market, if available [@CL-CMP-7470; @CL-CMP-7426]. For a third part, the contracting officer finds after evaluating sales data that the proposed price is not reasonable, so the officer obtains approval from an official one level above, without power of delegation, and asks for information on the basis for price or cost [@CL-CMP-7473; @CL-CMP-7474].

A follow-on contract line item under a broad agency announcement award has a term of 2 years and work worth 80 million dollars in fiscal year 2017 constant dollars, and it is limited to the minimal amount of items or prototypes that will allow timely competitive solicitation and award of a follow-on contract. It is within the limits [@CL-CMP-7279; @CL-CMP-7479]. A second item worth 120 million dollars exceeds the $100 million limit [@CL-CMP-7279].

## Never confuse

Commercial treatment of a major weapon system needs a determination by the Secretary of Defense that cannot be delegated below the Deputy Secretary [@CL-CMP-7274; @CL-CMP-7276], while treatment of a subsystem can follow the weapon system, and treatment of a component or spare part can follow the weapon system or a subsystem, or either can follow a written determination by the contracting officer [@CL-CMP-7413; @CL-CMP-7417; @CL-CMP-7442; @CL-CMP-7444; @CL-CMP-7452].

The approval above the contracting officer for insufficient sales data is a different route from the head of the contracting activity determination needed to require information about an item developed exclusively at private expense [@CL-CMP-7474; @CL-CMP-7476]. The CSDR card and the pricing cards cover the reporting clause and the analysis methods; see [Cost and software data reporting](../business-systems/cmp-dfars-cost-and-software-data-reporting.md), [DoD proposal analysis techniques](../../estimating/pricing/est-dod-proposal-analysis-techniques.md) and [Commercial offer evaluation](../../estimating/pricing/est-commercial-offer-evaluation.md).
