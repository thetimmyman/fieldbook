## Purpose

Shows which employee claims the contractor may not force into arbitration, the subcontract value that triggers the certification, the exceptions, and how the restriction reaches subcontractors.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-CMP-4185] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 252.222-7006 [@CL-CMP-4185] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-4185] |
| Applies to | Contracts that include the clause, with a certification covering covered subcontractors [@CL-CMP-4185; @CL-CMP-4188] |

The Contractor agrees not to enter into any agreement with its employees or independent contractors that requires, as a condition of employment, that they agree to resolve through arbitration, among listed claims, any claim under title VII of the Civil Rights Act [@CL-CMP-4185]. The list also includes one category of tort, any tort related to or arising out of sexual assault or harassment, including assault and battery, intentional infliction of emotional distress, false imprisonment, or negligent hiring, supervision, or retention [@CL-CMP-4186]. The Contractor also agrees not to take any action to enforce any provision of an existing agreement that mandates arbitration of listed claims, among them any claim under title VII of the Civil Rights Act [@CL-CMP-4187].

A covered subcontractor is any entity that has a subcontract valued in excess of $1 million, except a subcontract for the acquisition of commercial products or commercial services, including commercially available off-the-shelf items [@CL-CMP-4184]. The Contractor certifies, by signature of the contract, that it requires each covered subcontractor to agree not to enter into, and not to take any action to enforce, any provision of any existing agreements, as described in paragraph (b)(1) of the clause [@CL-CMP-4188]. The certification is with respect to any employee or independent contractor performing work related to the subcontract [@CL-CMP-4223].

The prohibitions of the clause do not apply with respect to a contractor's or subcontractor's agreements with employees or independent contractors that may not be enforced in a court of the United States [@CL-CMP-4189]. The Secretary of Defense may waive the applicability of the restrictions of paragraph (b) of the clause in accordance with DFARS 222.7404 [@CL-CMP-4190].

## How it works

The clause restricts both the making and the enforcement of arbitration agreements for the listed claims [@CL-CMP-4185; @CL-CMP-4186; @CL-CMP-4187]. It reaches subcontractors through a certification that the Contractor gives by signing the contract [@CL-CMP-4188].

A covered subcontractor is any entity that has a subcontract valued in excess of $1 million, except a subcontract for the acquisition of commercial products or commercial services, including commercially available off-the-shelf items [@CL-CMP-4184]. The prohibitions of the clause do not apply with respect to a contractor's or subcontractor's agreements with employees or independent contractors that may not be enforced in a court of the United States [@CL-CMP-4189], and the Secretary of Defense may waive the applicability of the restrictions of paragraph (b) [@CL-CMP-4190].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor has two subcontracts. One is worth three million for non-commercial engineering services. The other is worth eight hundred thousand for the same kind of work.

The first subcontractor is a covered subcontractor, so the contractor must require it to agree to the restriction, for people performing work related to that subcontract, and must certify that by signing the contract. The second is below the one million threshold and is not covered.

## Never confuse

The clause restricts agreements requiring arbitration of any claim under title VII of the Civil Rights Act of 1964 or any tort related to or arising out of sexual assault or harassment [@CL-CMP-4185; @CL-CMP-4186].

A covered subcontractor is an entity that has a subcontract valued in excess of $1 million [@CL-CMP-4184].

For the notice of whistleblower rights, see [Informing employees of whistleblower rights and the agency Inspector General](cmp-whistleblower-rights-notice-and-agency-inspector-general.md).
