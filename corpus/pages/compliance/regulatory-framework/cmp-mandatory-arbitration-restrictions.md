## Purpose

Shows which employee claims the contractor may not force into arbitration, the subcontract value that triggers the certification, the exceptions, and how the restriction reaches subcontractors.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-CMP-4185] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 252.222-7006 [@CL-CMP-4185] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-4185] |
| Applies to | Contracts that include the clause, with a certification covering covered subcontractors [@CL-CMP-4185; @CL-CMP-4188] |

The Contractor agrees not to enter into any agreement with its employees or independent contractors that requires, as a condition of employment, that they agree to resolve through arbitration, among listed claims, any claim under title VII of the Civil Rights Act [@CL-CMP-4185]. The list also includes one category of tort, any tort related to or arising out of sexual assault or harassment, including assault and battery, intentional infliction of emotional distress, false imprisonment, or negligent hiring, supervision, or retention [@CL-CMP-4186]. The Contractor also agrees not to take any action to enforce any provision of an existing agreement that mandates arbitration of listed claims, among them any claim under title VII of the Civil Rights Act [@CL-CMP-4187].

A covered subcontractor is any entity that has a subcontract valued in excess of $1 million, except a subcontract for the acquisition of commercial products or commercial services, including commercially available off-the-shelf items [@CL-CMP-4184]. The Contractor certifies, by signature of the contract, that it requires each covered subcontractor to agree not to enter into, and not to take any action to enforce, any provision of any existing agreements, as described in the paragraph that sets the Contractor's own restrictions [@CL-CMP-4188]. The certification is with respect to any employee or independent contractor performing work related to the subcontract [@CL-CMP-4223].

The prohibitions do not apply with respect to agreements with employees or independent contractors that may not be enforced in a court of the United States [@CL-CMP-4189]. The Secretary of Defense may waive the clause's restrictions in accordance with the supplement section the clause names [@CL-CMP-4190].

## How it works

The clause restricts both the making and the enforcement of arbitration agreements for the listed claims [@CL-CMP-4185; @CL-CMP-4186; @CL-CMP-4187]. It reaches subcontractors through a certification that the Contractor gives by signing the contract [@CL-CMP-4188]. The clause as printed has no separate paragraph requiring the substance of the clause in subcontracts; its reach to subcontractors is the certification [@CL-CMP-4188].

Only covered subcontractors count: those above the stated dollar value, other than commercial ones [@CL-CMP-4184]. Agreements that cannot be enforced in a United States court are outside the prohibitions [@CL-CMP-4189], and the Secretary of Defense may waive the restrictions [@CL-CMP-4190].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor has two subcontracts. One is worth three million for non-commercial engineering services. The other is worth eight hundred thousand for the same kind of work.

The first subcontractor is a covered subcontractor, so the contractor must require it to agree to the restriction, for people performing work related to that subcontract, and must certify that by signing the contract. The second is below the one million threshold and is not covered.

## Never confuse

The employee arbitration restriction is not a ban on arbitration of every dispute. It names listed claims [@CL-CMP-4185; @CL-CMP-4186].

The dollar threshold is not the prime contract value. It is the value of the subcontract [@CL-CMP-4184].

The restriction on arbitration is not the notice of whistleblower rights. That notice is a separate clause covered on [Informing employees of whistleblower rights and the agency Inspector General](cmp-whistleblower-rights-notice-and-agency-inspector-general.md).
