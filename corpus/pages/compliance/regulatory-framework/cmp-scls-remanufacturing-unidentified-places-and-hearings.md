## Purpose

Shows the printed test that separates remanufacturing from repair, the first steps when places of performance are not identified, and how a hearing request reaches the Department of Labor.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-3266] |
| Codified citation | FAR 22.1003-6, FAR 22.1009-1, FAR 22.1009-2, FAR 22.1009-4 and FAR 22.1021 [@CL-CMP-3266; @CL-CMP-8505; @CL-CMP-8506; @CL-CMP-3272; @CL-CMP-3275] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-3266] |
| Applies to | Contracts principally for remanufacturing or repair of equipment, solicitations with unidentified places of performance, and agency hearing requests [@CL-CMP-3266; @CL-CMP-3272; @CL-CMP-3275] |

Contracts principally for remanufacturing of equipment so extensive as to be equivalent to manufacturing are subject to chapter 65 of title 41 of the United States Code rather than to the Service Contract Labor Standards statute [@CL-CMP-3266]. Remanufacturing is deemed to be manufacturing when the criteria in either of two listed paragraphs are met [@CL-CMP-3267]. For a major overhaul of a degraded or inoperable item, all listed conditions must exist, the first being complete or substantial teardown into individual component parts [@CL-CMP-3268]. For a major modification of a wholly or partially obsolete item, all listed conditions must exist, the first being complete or substantial teardown [@CL-CMP-3269]. Remanufacturing does not include repair of damaged or broken equipment that does not require a complete teardown, overhaul and rebuild as described in those paragraphs [@CL-CMP-3270], or the periodic and routine maintenance, preservation, care, adjustment, upkeep or servicing of equipment to keep it in usable, serviceable, working order [@CL-CMP-8207], and a contract principally for the repair and routine maintenance work that the paragraph describes as not remanufacturing is subject to the Service Contract Labor Standards statute [@CL-CMP-3271].

The contracting officer should first attempt to identify the specific places or geographical areas where the services might be performed (see 22.1009-2) and then may follow the procedures either in 22.1009-3 or in 22.1009-4 [@CL-CMP-8505]. The printed section says the following may indicate possible places of performance: locations of previous contractors and their competitors, databases available via the Internet for lists of prospective offerors and contractors, and responses to a presolicitation notice (see 5.204) [@CL-CMP-8506]. If the contracting officer believes offerors may want to perform in unidentified places, the contracting officer may state in the synopsis and solicitation that the place of performance is unknown [@CL-CMP-3272]. If the successful offeror made no timely request for a wage determination and will perform where none was requested, the contracting officer shall award the contract [@CL-CMP-3273]. The contracting officer shall also obtain a wage determination and incorporate it in the contract retroactive to the date of award, with no adjustment in contract price [@CL-CMP-3273; @CL-CMP-3274].

To obtain a hearing for the agency, the contracting officer shall submit a written request through appropriate channels, ordinarily the agency labor advisor, to the Administrator of the Wage and Hour Division [@CL-CMP-3275]. A request for a substantial variance hearing shall include sufficient data to show that the rates at issue vary substantially from those prevailing for similar services in the locality, and the listed items that begin with the number of the wage determinations at issue [@CL-CMP-3362]. A request for an arm's length hearing shall include, as its first listed item, a statement of the applicant's case setting forth in detail why the applicant believes the wages and fringe benefits in the collective bargaining agreement were not reached as a result of arm's length negotiations [@CL-CMP-3363]. Unless the Administrator determines that extraordinary circumstances exist, the Administrator will not consider a hearing request for a sealed bid contract unless it is received more than 10 days before award [@CL-CMP-3276]. For negotiated contracts and for contracts with provisions exceeding the initial term by option, the Administrator will not consider a request unless it is received before the commencement date of the contract or the follow-up option period [@CL-CMP-3276; @CL-CMP-3361].

## How it works

The remanufacturing test is a gate: when the listed criteria are met, remanufacturing is deemed to be manufacturing, and a contract principally for remanufacturing that is so extensive as to be equivalent to manufacturing is subject to chapter 65 of title 41 of the United States Code rather than to the Service Contract Labor Standards statute [@CL-CMP-3266; @CL-CMP-3267]. The printed overhaul and modification tests list more conditions than this card drafts, so the full test is in the source [@CL-CMP-3268; @CL-CMP-3269]. For unidentified places, the card drafts the first step of identifying possible places and the listed signs of them, the notice step and the award steps, and the section also lists further information for the synopsis, steps for timely requests and a closing date. For hearings, the section also lists the further items each kind of request must contain, and this card does not draft them.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A shop is awarded a contract to repair 50 broken pumps, billed by the hour, with no complete teardown. The answer: this is repair, so the contract is subject to the Service Contract Labor Standards statute. A second contract strips 20 obsolete engines to their parts, replaces outmoded parts and rebuilds them in the contractor's own plant. The answer: if all the listed modification conditions exist, the work is manufacturing.

## Never confuse

Repair that does not need a complete teardown, overhaul and rebuild is not remanufacturing, and neither is periodic and routine maintenance to keep equipment in usable, serviceable, working order; a contract principally for that work stays under the Service Contract Labor Standards statute [@CL-CMP-3270; @CL-CMP-8207; @CL-CMP-3271]. A missed request for a wage determination does not stop award in the case described: when the successful offeror made no timely request, the contracting officer awards, then obtains and incorporates the wage determination [@CL-CMP-3273; @CL-CMP-3274]. When the clause goes into a contract and when wage determinations are obtained are on [Service contract labor standards clause and wage determinations](../../operating-model/talent/opm-service-contract-labor-standards.md) and are not restated here. The successor pay duty is on [Obtaining wage determinations and successor pay](cmp-scls-wage-determinations-and-successorship.md), and its exceptions to a collective bargaining agreement are on [Service Contract Labor Standards: successor pay under a collective bargaining agreement](cmp-scls-collective-bargaining-successor-wages.md).
