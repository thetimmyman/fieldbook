## Purpose

Shows that the Government takes title to subject inventions under this clause, what rights the contractor keeps or may request, the Government's minimum license, what the contractor must disclose and report, and when payment can be withheld.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-3642] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.227-13 [@CL-CMP-3642] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-3642] |
| Applies to | Each subject invention under the clause [@CL-CMP-3642] |

The Contractor shall assign to the Government title throughout the world to each subject invention, except to the extent that rights are retained under paragraphs (b)(2) and (d) of the clause [@CL-CMP-3642]. The Contractor, or an employee-inventor after consultation with the Contractor, may request greater rights than the nonexclusive license provided in paragraph (d) of the clause [@CL-CMP-3674]. The request is to be submitted to the Contracting Officer at the time of the first disclosure of the subject invention, or not later than 8 months thereafter, unless the Contracting Officer authorizes a longer period in writing for good cause shown in writing by the Contractor [@CL-CMP-4227]. The Contractor is granted a revocable, nonexclusive, paid-up license in each patent application filed in any country on a subject invention, and any resulting patent, in which the Government obtains title [@CL-CMP-3643]. The license is granted unless the Contractor fails to disclose the subject invention within the times specified in the disclosure paragraph [@CL-CMP-3643; @CL-CMP-3644].

Regarding each subject invention to which the Contractor retains ownership, the Government will have a nonexclusive, nontransferable, irrevocable, paid-up license to practice, or have practiced for or on its behalf, the subject invention throughout the world [@CL-CMP-3670]. One listed right is the agency's right to require licensing under the statute and procedures that the clause names [@CL-CMP-3670; @CL-CMP-3671].

The clause sets the times for disclosing each subject invention in writing to the Contracting Officer as within 2 months after the inventor discloses it in writing to Contractor personnel responsible for patent matters or, if earlier, within 6 months after the Contractor becomes aware that a subject invention has been made [@CL-CMP-3645]. The same disclosure is in any event due before any on sale, public use, or publication of the subject invention known to the Contractor [@CL-CMP-3646]. The Contractor shall furnish the Contracting Officer interim reports every 12 months, or a longer period the Contracting Officer specifies, listing subject inventions during that period [@CL-CMP-3647]. Each interim report must also state that all subject inventions have been disclosed, or that there are none, and that the procedures required by the invention identification paragraph have been followed [@CL-CMP-4229]. A final report is also listed, due within 3 months after completion of the contracted work [@CL-CMP-3647; @CL-CMP-3648]. The final report must also list all subcontracts at any tier containing a patent rights clause, or state that there were none [@CL-CMP-4228].

At any time before final payment, the Contracting Officer may, in the Government's interest, withhold payment until a reserve not exceeding $50,000 or 5 percent of the amount of the contract, whichever is less, has been set aside, if in the Contracting Officer's opinion the Contractor fails to take one of the actions that the paragraph then lists [@CL-CMP-3649; @CL-CMP-3650]. The first two listed actions are to establish, maintain, and follow effective procedures for identifying and disclosing subject inventions, and to disclose any subject invention [@CL-CMP-3650; @CL-CMP-3672]. The last two are to deliver acceptable interim reports and to provide the information regarding subcontracts [@CL-CMP-3650; @CL-CMP-3673]. The Contracting Officer will not withhold any amount under that paragraph while the amount it specifies is being withheld under other provisions of the contract [@CL-CMP-4230]. The withholding paragraph does not apply to subcontracts [@CL-CMP-3675].

## How it works

Title starts with the Government, and the Contractor's rights are the exceptions the clause allows, including the license in inventions to which the Government has title and any greater rights the Contractor requests, which must be requested by the stated deadline [@CL-CMP-3642; @CL-CMP-3643; @CL-CMP-3674; @CL-CMP-4227]. Where the Contractor does retain ownership, the rights the clause lists for the Government include a license and the agency's right to require licensing [@CL-CMP-3670; @CL-CMP-3671]. The disclosure and the interim and final reports tell the Contracting Officer which subject inventions exist [@CL-CMP-3645; @CL-CMP-3647; @CL-CMP-3648]. Withholding is the Contracting Officer's remedy where, in the Contracting Officer's opinion, the Contractor fails to take a listed action, and it is capped and does not stack on amounts already withheld under other provisions [@CL-CMP-3649; @CL-CMP-3650; @CL-CMP-4230].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contract worth 400,000 contains this clause. The Contractor does not deliver acceptable interim reports, and the Contracting Officer decides to withhold payment. The cap is the lesser of 50,000 and 5 percent of 400,000. Five percent of 400,000 is 20,000, which is less than 50,000, so the reserve can be at most 20,000.

## Never confuse

Under this clause the Contractor assigns title to the Government [@CL-CMP-3642]. Under the contractor-ownership clause the Contractor may retain ownership instead [@CL-CMP-3629]. The two clauses are separate, and which one a contract carries decides who holds title; see the [contractor ownership card](cmp-patent-rights-contractor-ownership-definitions-and-licenses.md) and its [companion on deadlines and title](cmp-patent-rights-contractor-ownership-deadlines-and-title.md). The Government's license in an invention is also separate from its rights in data, which have their own card on [rights in data, definitions and allocation](cmp-rights-in-data-general-definitions-and-allocation.md).
