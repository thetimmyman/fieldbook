## Purpose

Sets out the identity verification procedures a contractor follows, the duty to account for and return Government-provided identification, the payment consequence and the subcontract flowdown.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-5570] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.204-9 [@CL-CMP-5570] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-5570] |
| Applies to | Contracts that include the clause prescribed by FAR 4.1303 [@CL-CMP-5658] |

The contractor must comply with the agency personal identity verification procedures identified in the contract, which implement Homeland Security Presidential Directive-12, Office of Management and Budget guidance M-05-24 and Federal Information Processing Standards Publication 201 [@CL-CMP-5570]. The procedures themselves are specific to each contract.

The contractor must account for all forms of Government-provided identification issued to its employees in connection with performance under the contract [@CL-CMP-5659]. It must return such identification to the issuing agency at the earliest of the listed events, unless the Government determines otherwise [@CL-CMP-5571].

## How it works

The events are when the identification is no longer needed for contract performance, upon completion of the contractor employee's employment, and upon contract completion or termination [@CL-CMP-5571].

The Contracting Officer may delay final payment under a contract if the contractor fails to comply with these requirements [@CL-CMP-5572].

The contractor must insert the substance of the clause, including its flowdown paragraph, in all subcontracts when the subcontractor's employees are required to have routine physical access to a Federally-controlled facility, routine access to a Federally-controlled information system, or both [@CL-CMP-5573]. It is the responsibility of the prime contractor to return such identification to the issuing agency in accordance with paragraph (b), unless the Contracting Officer approves otherwise in writing [@CL-CMP-5660].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A subcontractor's 12 employees are required to have routine physical access to a Government site, and the agency issues each of them a badge. The prime puts the clause substance, including its flowdown paragraph, in the subcontract and, as the prime contractor responsible for the return, takes back the 12 badges. When one employee's employment ends, when another employee no longer needs access for performance, and when the contract is completed, the prime returns the matching badges to the issuing agency at the earliest of those events for each badge, unless the Government determines otherwise or the Contracting Officer approves otherwise in writing. If the prime failed to do so, the Contracting Officer could delay final payment.

## Never confuse

Personal identity verification is not a background investigation the contractor designs for itself. The clause points the contractor to the agency procedures identified in the contract, and it adds the duty to account for and return Government-provided identification [@CL-CMP-5570; @CL-CMP-5659; @CL-CMP-5571].

## Company calibration

Confirm which employees hold Government identification and who returns it when employment or the contract ends. [@Q-004]
