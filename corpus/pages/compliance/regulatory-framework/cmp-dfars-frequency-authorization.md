## Purpose

Shows when a contractor must obtain radio frequency authorization, what it must give the contracting officer, how the two versions of the clause differ, and the flowdown.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-CMP-4575] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 252.235-7003 [@CL-CMP-4575] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-4575] |
| Applies to | Contracts with the frequency authorization clause, in the basic version [@CL-CMP-4578] or Alternate I [@CL-CMP-4579], and subcontracts requiring the development, production, construction, testing, or operation of a device for which a radio frequency authorization is required [@CL-CMP-4580] |

The contractor must obtain authorization for the radio frequencies required in support of the contract [@CL-CMP-4575]. For experimental, developmental or operational equipment for which the appropriate frequency allocation has not been made, it must provide the technical operating characteristics of the proposed electromagnetic radiating device to the contracting officer [@CL-CMP-4576]. Those characteristics are due during the initial planning, experimental, or developmental phase of contract performance [@CL-CMP-4577].

The clause has two versions. Under the basic clause, the contracting officer furnishes the procedures for obtaining radio frequency authorization [@CL-CMP-4578]. Under Alternate I, the contractor must use DD Form 1494, Application for Equipment Frequency Allocation, to obtain radio frequency authorization [@CL-CMP-4579].

## How it works

The duty to obtain authorization sits with the contractor [@CL-CMP-4575]. Equipment with no frequency allocation triggers an early report of its technical operating characteristics to the contracting officer [@CL-CMP-4576; @CL-CMP-4577]. The route to authorization depends on the version: the contracting officer supplies the procedures under the basic clause [@CL-CMP-4578], and the contractor files the application form under Alternate I [@CL-CMP-4579]. The clause, including its flowdown paragraph, must also be included in subcontracts requiring the development, production, construction, testing, or operation of a device for which a radio frequency authorization is required [@CL-CMP-4580].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

Assume a contract worth 2,000,000 under which a contractor develops a new radio transmitter. No frequency allocation exists for it yet. In the planning phase the contractor sends the contracting officer the technical operating characteristics. Under the basic clause it then waits for the procedures the officer furnishes. Under Alternate I it files the application form itself. Assume the antenna subcontract requires the development of a device for which a radio frequency authorization is required, so the contractor adds the clause to that subcontract.

## Never confuse

The basic clause and Alternate I differ in who supplies the route to authorization [@CL-CMP-4578; @CL-CMP-4579]. Giving the technical operating characteristics is also not the same as obtaining authorization. The characteristics go to the contracting officer for equipment without an allocation [@CL-CMP-4576], while the contractor still has to obtain the authorization itself [@CL-CMP-4575]. Other clause cards pass their clause down with a flowdown paragraph in a similar way, for example [Animal welfare in research](cmp-dfars-animal-welfare-in-research.md) [@CL-CMP-4574] and [Protection of human subjects](cmp-dfars-protection-of-human-subjects.md) [@CL-CMP-4589].
