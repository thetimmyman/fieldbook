## Purpose

Defines the two terms the subpart on forced or indentured child labor uses: the prohibited labor itself and the Department of Labor product list that drives the certification.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-8162] |
| Codified citation | FAR 22.1500, FAR 22.1501 and FAR 22.1502 [@CL-CMP-8829; @CL-CMP-8162; @CL-CMP-8164; @CL-CMP-8956; @CL-CMP-8957] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-8162] |
| Applies to | The terms as the subpart uses them; the section states no contract threshold of its own [@CL-CMP-8162] |

The section defines the labor in two alternatives, joined by "or" [@CL-CMP-8162].

Under the first, forced or indentured child labor means all work or service exacted from any person under the age of 18 under the menace of any penalty for its nonperformance and for which the worker does not offer himself voluntarily [@CL-CMP-8162].

Under the second, it means all work or service performed by any person under the age of 18 pursuant to a contract the enforcement of which can be accomplished by process or penalties [@CL-CMP-8163].

The section also defines the List of Products Requiring Contractor Certification as to Forced or Indentured Child Labor. It means the list published by the Department of Labor in accordance with Executive Order 13126 of June 12, 1999, Prohibition of Acquisition of Products Produced by Forced or Indentured Child Labor [@CL-CMP-8164].

The list identifies products, by their country of origin, that the Departments of Labor, Treasury, and State have a reasonable basis to believe might have been mined, produced, or manufactured by forced or indentured child labor [@CL-CMP-8165].

## How it works

Both alternatives set the same age line, a person under the age of 18 [@CL-CMP-8162; @CL-CMP-8163]. Work that meets either alternative is forced or indentured child labor [@CL-CMP-8162; @CL-CMP-8163].

Under the first alternative, the work or service is exacted under the menace of a penalty for its nonperformance, and the worker does not offer himself voluntarily [@CL-CMP-8162].

Under the second alternative, the work is performed by any person under the age of 18 pursuant to a contract the enforcement of which can be accomplished by process or penalties [@CL-CMP-8163].

For the certification, the check of the list and the remedies, see [Forced or indentured child labor list and waivers for workers with disabilities](cmp-forced-child-labor-list-and-disability-waivers.md), [Child labor certification, cooperation with authorities and remedies](cmp-child-labor-certification-and-remedies.md) and [Forced or indentured child labor: violations, remedies, provision and clause](cmp-forced-child-labor-violations-remedies-and-clauses.md).

The scope section of the subpart says the subpart applies to acquisitions of supplies that exceed the micro-purchase threshold [@CL-CMP-8829]. The policy section says agencies must take appropriate action to enforce the laws prohibiting the manufacture or importation of products that have been mined, produced, or manufactured wholly or in part by forced or indentured child labor, consistent with section 1307 of title 19, section 201 and following of title 29, and chapter 65 of title 41 of the United States Code [@CL-CMP-8956]. It adds that agencies should make every effort to avoid acquiring such products [@CL-CMP-8957].

The Department of Labor publishes the list. The list identifies products, by their country of origin, that the Departments of Labor, Treasury, and State have a reasonable basis to believe might have been mined, produced, or manufactured by forced or indentured child labor [@CL-CMP-8164; @CL-CMP-8165].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An audit of a component line finds a 16 year old worker who works under a contract that the recruiter can enforce by penalties. The file shows no threat and the worker agreed to the job. The first alternative is not met on these facts [@CL-CMP-8162]. The second alternative is met because the work is performed by a person under 18 under a contract enforceable by process or penalties, so the work is forced or indentured child labor [@CL-CMP-8163].

A 19 year old worker in the same audit is outside both alternatives, because each applies to a person under the age of 18 [@CL-CMP-8162; @CL-CMP-8163].

Whether the component appears on the Department of Labor list is a separate question. The section says the Department of Labor publishes the list and states no way for an audit finding to put a product on it [@CL-CMP-8164].

## Never confuse

Forced or indentured child labor means all work or service exacted from any person under the age of 18 under the menace of any penalty for its nonperformance and for which the worker does not offer himself voluntarily, or performed by any person under the age of 18 pursuant to a contract the enforcement of which can be accomplished by process or penalties [@CL-CMP-8162; @CL-CMP-8163].

The labor is defined by the two alternatives [@CL-CMP-8162; @CL-CMP-8163]. The List of Products Requiring Contractor Certification as to Forced or Indentured Child Labor is the list published by the Department of Labor, and it identifies products by their country of origin [@CL-CMP-8164; @CL-CMP-8165].
