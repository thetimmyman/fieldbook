## Purpose

Shows what the Rights in Data—General clause means by data and by unlimited rights, which data the Government gets unlimited rights in, and what the contractor keeps.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-3679] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.227-14(a) and (b) [@CL-CMP-3679; @CL-CMP-3684] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |

| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-3679] |

Unless the copyright paragraph provides otherwise, the Government shall have unlimited rights in data first produced in the performance of the contract and in form, fit, and function data delivered under the contract [@CL-CMP-3684]. Under the same allocation paragraph, the Government also has unlimited rights in one listed category of manuals and instructional and training material delivered under the contract, other than restricted computer software [@CL-CMP-3684; @CL-CMP-3685]. A last listed category is all other data delivered under the contract, unless provided otherwise for limited rights data or restricted computer software under the withholding paragraph [@CL-CMP-3684; @CL-CMP-3686].

Unlimited rights are the Government's rights to use, disclose, reproduce, prepare derivative works, distribute copies to the public, and perform and display publicly, in any manner and for any purpose, and to have or permit others to do so [@CL-CMP-3679].

The Contractor keeps the right to assert copyright in data first produced in the performance of the contract, to the extent the copyright paragraph provides [@CL-CMP-3687]. It also keeps the right to protect limited rights data and restricted computer software from unauthorized disclosure and use, to the extent the withholding paragraph provides [@CL-CMP-3688].

## How it works

The clause starts from a default of unlimited rights for the Government, and then lets some privately funded data stay protected. Data means recorded information in any form, including technical data and computer software, and it leaves out information incidental to contract administration [@CL-CMP-3680]. Two protected classes are defined: limited rights data, which is data other than computer software [@CL-CMP-3681], and restricted computer software, which is software [@CL-CMP-3682]. Form, fit, and function data includes data relating to items, components, or processes that is sufficient to enable physical and functional interchangeability, and the Government has unlimited rights in it when it is delivered [@CL-CMP-3683; @CL-CMP-3684]. For computer software, it means data identifying source, functional characteristics, and performance requirements, and it specifically excludes the source code, algorithms, processes, formulas, and flow charts of the software [@CL-CMP-3745]. How the copyright, marking and withholding paragraphs work is set out on the separate card [Rights in data, general: copyright, withholding and markings](cmp-rights-in-data-general-copyright-withholding-and-markings.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contract calls for a test report first produced in the work and a manual for operating the delivered machine. The Government has unlimited rights in both, so it may reproduce them and give copies to the public. The contractor may assert copyright in the test report, to the extent the copyright paragraph allows. The contractor also owns a program built at its own expense that qualifies as restricted computer software. The first three listed categories do not cover it, so the contractor may withhold it, if it identifies the program and furnishes form, fit, and function data instead.

## Never confuse

Limited rights data is data other than computer software, while restricted computer software is software [@CL-CMP-3681; @CL-CMP-3682]. Neither is the same as form, fit, and function data, which the contractor delivers in their place when it withholds them [@CL-CMP-3683; @CL-CMP-3698]. Information incidental to contract administration, such as financial, administrative, or cost or pricing information, is not data under this clause [@CL-CMP-3680]. Commercial computer software delivered under a contract may not be used, reproduced, or disclosed by the Government except as listed or as the contract expressly states otherwise [@CL-CMP-3712]; see [Commercial computer software license clause](cmp-commercial-computer-software-license.md).
