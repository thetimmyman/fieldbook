## Purpose

Shows what the contracting officer does about contractor labor policies and strike costs when allowing costs or pricing a contract.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-2350] |
| Codified citation | FAR 22.101-2 [@CL-CMP-2350; @CL-CMP-2351] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-2350] |
| Applies to | Cost-reimbursement contracts when allowing costs, and fixed-price contracts when recognizing costs in pricing, where labor policies or compensation practices result in unreasonable costs to the Government [@CL-CMP-2350] |

Labor policies and compensation practices, whether or not included in labor-management agreements, are not acceptable bases for allowing costs in cost-reimbursement contracts or for recognition of costs in pricing fixed-price contracts if they result in unreasonable costs to the Government [@CL-CMP-2350].

All costs incurred during strikes shall be carefully examined to ensure recognition of only those costs necessary for performing the contract in accordance with the Government's essential interest [@CL-CMP-2351]. The section sets no formula or dollar limit for strike costs.

## How it works

The rule works on the cost, not on the dispute. For cost-reimbursement contracts it limits the costs that are allowed, and for fixed-price contracts it limits the costs recognized in pricing [@CL-CMP-2350]. All costs incurred during a strike are carefully examined, and only those necessary for performing the contract in the Government's essential interest are recognized [@CL-CMP-2351]. The same section also deals with excused delay from strikes and with plant inspection during a dispute, and this card does not cover those parts.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor's pay policy sets a labor rate of 120 per hour for work that comparable firms price at 90, and the policy results in unreasonable costs to the Government. The contractor includes 120 in a fixed-price proposal. The answer: the policy is not an acceptable basis for recognizing the 120 in pricing, and it makes no difference whether a labor-management agreement contains the policy.

## Never confuse

Cost treatment and delay are separate questions. The section handles excused delay from a strike in its own paragraph, so this card says nothing about when a delay is excused. The general idea of a reasonable cost is on [Reasonableness of costs](../cas-allowability/cmp-reasonableness.md) and is not restated here.
