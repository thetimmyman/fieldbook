## Purpose

Shows what the contracting officer does about contractor labor policies and strike costs when allowing costs or pricing a contract.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-2350] |
| Codified citation | FAR 22.101-2 and FAR 22.101-3 [@CL-CMP-2350; @CL-CMP-2351; @CL-CMP-7099; @CL-CMP-7163] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-2350] |
| Applies to | Cost-reimbursement contracts when allowing costs, and fixed-price contracts when recognizing costs in pricing, where labor policies or compensation practices result in unreasonable costs to the Government [@CL-CMP-2350] |

Labor policies and compensation practices, whether or not included in labor-management agreements, are not acceptable bases for allowing costs in cost-reimbursement contracts or for recognition of costs in pricing fixed-price contracts if they result in unreasonable costs to the Government [@CL-CMP-2350].

All costs incurred during strikes shall be carefully examined to ensure recognition of only those costs necessary for performing the contract in accordance with the Government's essential interest [@CL-CMP-2351].

## How it works

Labor policies and compensation practices are not acceptable bases for allowing costs in cost-reimbursement contracts, or for recognition of costs in pricing fixed-price contracts, if they result in unreasonable costs to the Government [@CL-CMP-2350]. All costs incurred during strikes shall be carefully examined to ensure recognition of only those costs necessary for performing the contract in accordance with the Government's essential interest [@CL-CMP-2351]. The same section also deals with excused delay from strikes and with plant inspection during a dispute, and this card does not cover those parts.

The office administering the contract shall report, in accordance with agency procedures, any potential or actual labor disputes that may interfere with performing any contracts under its cognizance [@CL-CMP-7099]. If a contract contains the clause at 52.222-1, Notice to the Government of Labor Disputes, the contractor also must report any actual or potential dispute that may delay contract performance [@CL-CMP-7163].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor's pay policy sets a labor rate of 120 per hour for work that comparable firms price at 90, and the policy results in unreasonable costs to the Government. The contractor includes 120 in a fixed-price proposal. The answer: the policy is not an acceptable basis for recognizing the 120 in pricing, and it makes no difference whether a labor-management agreement contains the policy.

## Never confuse

This card does not cover when a delay from a strike is excused. For the general idea of a reasonable cost, see [Reasonableness of costs](../cas-allowability/cmp-reasonableness.md).
