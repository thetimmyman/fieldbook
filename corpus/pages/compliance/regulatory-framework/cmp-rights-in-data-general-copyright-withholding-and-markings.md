## Purpose

Shows how the later paragraphs of the Rights in Data—General clause treat copyright, unauthorized or omitted markings, and the withholding of limited rights data and restricted computer software.

## The rule

Unless the clause's paragraph on release, publication and use of data provides otherwise, the Contractor may, without prior approval of the Contracting Officer, assert copyright in scientific and technical articles [@CL-CMP-3729] that are based on or contain data first produced in the performance of the contract and are published in academic, technical or professional journals, symposia proceedings, or similar works [@CL-CMP-3730]. The prior, express written permission of the Contracting Officer is required to assert copyright in all other data first produced in the performance of the contract [@CL-CMP-3689]. For data other than computer software, the Contractor grants the Government and others acting on its behalf a paid-up, nonexclusive, irrevocable, worldwide license in such copyrighted data [@CL-CMP-3734] to reproduce, prepare derivative works, distribute copies to the public, and perform and display publicly [@CL-CMP-3690]. For copyrighted computer software, the grant is similar but does not include distributing copies to the public [@CL-CMP-3691].

The Contractor shall not, without the prior written permission of the Contracting Officer, incorporate in delivered data any data not first produced in the performance of the contract unless it meets the listed conditions [@CL-CMP-3731]. The listed conditions are that the Contractor identifies the data and grants to the Government, or acquires on its behalf, a license of the same scope as the first-produced-data copyright paragraph sets out or, if such data are restricted computer software, the Government shall acquire a copyright license [@CL-CMP-3732]. This card does not set out the terms of that copyright license for restricted computer software.

For restrictive or limiting markings not authorized by the contract, the Contracting Officer may at any time return the data or cancel or ignore the markings, but the listed procedures apply before the markings are cancelled or ignored [@CL-CMP-3692]. One listed step is a written inquiry that gives the Contractor 60 days from receipt to justify the markings in writing [@CL-CMP-3693]. If the Contractor fails to provide that justification within the period, or a longer time approved in writing for good cause, the Government has the right to cancel or ignore the markings at any time after the period [@CL-CMP-3694]. If the Contractor provides written justification within the period set in the written inquiry, the Contracting Officer will consider it and determine whether or not the markings are to be cancelled or ignored [@CL-CMP-3693; @CL-CMP-3741]. If the Contracting Officer determines, with the concurrence of the head of the contracting activity, that the markings are not authorized, the Contracting Officer will furnish the Contractor a written determination [@CL-CMP-3742]. That written determination becomes the final agency decision on the markings unless the Contractor files suit in a court of competent jurisdiction within 90 days of receipt of the Contracting Officer's decision [@CL-CMP-3743].

Data delivered without any restrictive markings shall be deemed furnished with unlimited rights, and the Government is not liable for its disclosure, use, or reproduction [@CL-CMP-3695]. If unmarked data has not been disclosed without restriction outside the Government, the Contractor may request, within 6 months or a longer time approved in writing for good cause, permission to have authorized notices put on the data [@CL-CMP-3696]. The Contracting Officer may agree to place the omitted notice if the Contractor, among other listed things, identifies the data, demonstrates that the omission of the notice was inadvertent, and establishes that the proposed notice is authorized [@CL-CMP-3744].

The Contractor may withhold from delivery qualifying limited rights data or restricted computer software that are not data in the three listed unlimited-rights categories of the allocation paragraph [@CL-CMP-3697]. As a condition, the Contractor shall identify the data being withheld and furnish form, fit, and function data instead [@CL-CMP-3698]. Nothing in the clause implies a license to the Government under any patent or is to be construed as affecting the scope of any license or other right otherwise granted to the Government [@CL-CMP-3733].

Alternate II inserts a paragraph under which, notwithstanding the withholding paragraph, the contract may identify and specify the delivery of limited rights data [@CL-CMP-3699], and the Contracting Officer may require by written request the delivery of limited rights data that has been withheld or would otherwise be entitled to be withheld [@CL-CMP-3735]. Alternate III inserts a paragraph under which, notwithstanding the withholding paragraph, the contract may identify and specify the delivery of restricted computer software [@CL-CMP-3736], and the Contracting Officer may require by written request the delivery of restricted computer software that has been withheld or would otherwise be entitled to be withheld [@CL-CMP-3737]. Alternate IV substitutes a copyright paragraph on data first produced in the performance of the contract under which, except as otherwise specifically provided in the contract, the Contractor may assert copyright in any data [@CL-CMP-3738]. Alternate V adds a paragraph, and the Contractor's agreement in it is subject to an exception for specific data deliverables that the contract lists as not subject to the paragraph [@CL-CMP-3739]. Under that paragraph, the Contracting Officer may, up to three years after acceptance of all deliverables under the contract, inspect at the Contractor's facility any data withheld under the withholding paragraph [@CL-CMP-3740].

## How it works

Withholding happens before delivery: the contractor names what it holds back and supplies form, fit, and function data in its place [@CL-CMP-3698]. Markings matter after delivery, because data without restrictive markings is treated as delivered with unlimited rights [@CL-CMP-3695]. A marking the contract does not authorize can be cancelled or ignored, but only after the clause's procedures are followed [@CL-CMP-3692]. The alternates add ways to obtain delivery of withheld data or to inspect it, and one of them changes the copyright rule [@CL-CMP-3735; @CL-CMP-3737; @CL-CMP-3738; @CL-CMP-3740]. This card does not set out Alternate I, the notice texts that the alternates prescribe, or the other paragraphs of the clause. The definitions and the allocation of rights are on [Rights in data, general: definitions and allocation of rights](cmp-rights-in-data-general-definitions-and-allocation.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor delivers a design report with no restrictive marking. Two months later it wants the authorized notice placed on the report. The report has not been disclosed outside the Government. The contractor asks in writing for permission to place the notice. The request is inside the six-month window, so the contractor may make it, and the Contracting Officer may agree if the contractor meets the listed conditions, such as showing that the omission was inadvertent. Until the notice is placed, the Government treats the report as delivered with unlimited rights.

## Never confuse

Withholding and marking are different tools. Withholding keeps qualifying data from being delivered at all and substitutes form, fit, and function data [@CL-CMP-3697; @CL-CMP-3698]. Marking labels data that has been delivered, and an unauthorized label can be cancelled or ignored [@CL-CMP-3692]. Data under the small business innovation research clause has its own omitted-notice rule; see [Rights in data under the SBIR and STTR program](cmp-rights-in-data-sbir-and-sttr-program.md) [@CL-CMP-3721]. Marking disputes over contractor bid or proposal information under the procurement integrity rules are a different process; see [Procurement integrity: marking disputes and violations](cmp-procurement-integrity-marking-and-violations.md).
