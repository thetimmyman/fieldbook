## Purpose

Shows how the later paragraphs of the Rights in Data—General clause treat copyright, unauthorized or omitted markings, and the withholding of limited rights data and restricted computer software.

Unless provided otherwise in paragraph (d) of the clause, the Contractor may, without prior approval of the Contracting Officer, assert copyright in scientific and technical articles [@CL-CMP-3729] based on or containing data first produced in the performance of the contract and published in academic, technical or professional journals, symposia proceedings, or similar works [@CL-CMP-3730]. The prior, express written permission of the Contracting Officer is required to assert copyright in all other data first produced in the performance of the contract [@CL-CMP-3689]. For data other than computer software, the Contractor grants to the Government, and others acting on its behalf, a paid-up, nonexclusive, irrevocable, worldwide license in such copyrighted data [@CL-CMP-3734] to reproduce, prepare derivative works, distribute copies to the public, and perform publicly and display publicly by or on behalf of the Government [@CL-CMP-3690; @CL-CMP-0343]. For copyrighted computer software, the Contractor grants to the Government, and others acting on its behalf, a paid-up, nonexclusive, irrevocable, worldwide license in such copyrighted computer software to reproduce, prepare derivative works, and perform publicly and display publicly (but not to distribute copies to the public) by or on behalf of the Government [@CL-CMP-3691; @CL-CMP-0344].

The Contractor shall not, without the prior written permission of the Contracting Officer, incorporate in data delivered under the contract any data not first produced in the performance of the contract unless the Contractor identifies the data and grants to the Government, or acquires on its behalf, a license of the same scope as set forth in paragraph (c)(1) of the clause or, if such data are restricted computer software, the Government shall acquire a copyright license as set forth in paragraph (g)(4) of the clause (if included in the contract) or as otherwise provided in a collateral agreement incorporated in or made part of the contract [@CL-CMP-3731; @CL-CMP-3732; @CL-CMP-0345].

Notwithstanding any other provisions of the contract concerning inspection or acceptance, if any data delivered under the contract are marked with the notices specified in paragraph (g)(3) or (g)(4) if included in the clause and use of the notices is not authorized by the clause, or if the data bears any other restrictive or limiting markings not authorized by the contract, the Contracting Officer may at any time either return the data to the Contractor, or cancel or ignore the markings, although pursuant to section 4703 of title 41 of the United States Code, the clause's procedures shall apply prior to canceling or ignoring the markings [@CL-CMP-0346; @CL-CMP-3692]. First, the Contracting Officer will make written inquiry to the Contractor affording the Contractor 60 days from receipt of the inquiry to provide written justification to substantiate the propriety of the markings [@CL-CMP-3693]. Second, if the Contractor fails to respond or fails to provide written justification to substantiate the propriety of the markings within the 60-day period (or a longer time approved in writing by the Contracting Officer for good cause shown), the Government shall have the right to cancel or ignore the markings at any time after said period and the data will no longer be made subject to any disclosure prohibitions [@CL-CMP-0347; @CL-CMP-3694; @CL-CMP-0348]. Third, if the Contractor provides written justification to substantiate the propriety of the markings within the period set in paragraph (e)(1)(i) of the clause, the Contracting Officer will consider such written justification and determine whether or not the markings are to be cancelled or ignored [@CL-CMP-3693; @CL-CMP-3741]. If the Contracting Officer determines, with concurrence of the head of the contracting activity, that the markings are not authorized, the Contracting Officer will furnish the Contractor a written determination [@CL-CMP-3742], which determination will become the final agency decision regarding the appropriateness of the markings unless the Contractor files suit in a court of competent jurisdiction within 90 days of receipt of the Contracting Officer's decision [@CL-CMP-3743].

Data delivered to the Government without any restrictive markings shall be deemed to have been furnished with unlimited rights, and the Government is not liable for the disclosure, use, or reproduction of such data [@CL-CMP-3695]. If the unmarked data has not been disclosed without restriction outside the Government, the Contractor may request, within 6 months (or a longer time approved by the Contracting Officer in writing for good cause shown) after delivery of the data, permission to have authorized notices placed on the data at the Contractor's expense [@CL-CMP-3696; @CL-CMP-7693]. The Contracting Officer may agree to do so if the Contractor identifies the data to which the omitted notice is to be applied, demonstrates that the omission of the notice was inadvertent, establishes that the proposed notice is authorized, and acknowledges that the Government has no liability for the disclosure, use, or reproduction of any data made prior to the addition of the notice or resulting from the omission of the notice [@CL-CMP-3744; @CL-CMP-0425].

The Contractor may withhold from delivery qualifying limited rights data or restricted computer software that are not data identified in paragraphs (b)(1)(i), (ii), and (iii) of the clause [@CL-CMP-3697]. As a condition to this withholding, the Contractor shall identify the data being withheld and furnish form, fit, and function data instead [@CL-CMP-3698]. Nothing contained in the clause shall imply a license to the Government under any patent or be construed as affecting the scope of any license or other right otherwise granted to the Government [@CL-CMP-3733].

Alternate II inserts paragraph (g)(3), under which, notwithstanding paragraph (g)(1) of the clause, the contract may identify and specify the delivery of limited rights data [@CL-CMP-3699], or the Contracting Officer may require by written request the delivery of limited rights data that has been withheld or would otherwise be entitled to be withheld [@CL-CMP-3735]. Alternate III inserts paragraph (g)(4), under which, notwithstanding paragraph (g)(1) of the clause, the contract may identify and specify the delivery of restricted computer software [@CL-CMP-3736], or the Contracting Officer may require by written request the delivery of restricted computer software that has been withheld or would otherwise be entitled to be withheld [@CL-CMP-3737]. Alternate IV substitutes paragraph (c)(1) on data first produced in the performance of the contract, under which, except as otherwise specifically provided in the contract, the Contractor may assert copyright in any data first produced in the performance of the contract [@CL-CMP-3738; @CL-CMP-0427]. Alternate V adds paragraph (j), under which the Contractor agrees, except as may be otherwise specified in the contract for specific data deliverables listed as not subject to that paragraph, that the Contracting Officer may, up to three years after acceptance of all deliverables under the contract, inspect at the Contractor's facility any data withheld pursuant to paragraph (g)(1) of the clause, for purposes of verifying the Contractor's assertion of limited rights or restricted rights status of the data or for evaluating work performance [@CL-CMP-3739; @CL-CMP-3740; @CL-CMP-7694].

## How it works

As a condition to withholding qualifying limited rights data or restricted computer software that are not data identified in paragraphs (b)(1)(i), (ii), and (iii) of the clause, the Contractor shall identify the data being withheld and furnish form, fit, and function data instead [@CL-CMP-3697; @CL-CMP-3698]. Data delivered to the Government without any restrictive markings shall be deemed to have been furnished with unlimited rights, and the Government is not liable for the disclosure, use, or reproduction of such data [@CL-CMP-3695]. For unauthorized notices under paragraph (g)(3) or (g)(4) or other restrictive or limiting markings not authorized by the contract, the Contracting Officer may at any time return the data to the Contractor or cancel or ignore the markings, subject to the procedures in paragraph (e)(1) prior to canceling or ignoring the markings [@CL-CMP-0346; @CL-CMP-3692]. Alternates II, III, and V provide for delivery or inspection of limited rights data or restricted computer software, and Alternate IV substitutes paragraph (c)(1) on copyright in data first produced in the performance of the contract [@CL-CMP-3699; @CL-CMP-3735; @CL-CMP-3736; @CL-CMP-3737; @CL-CMP-3738; @CL-CMP-0427; @CL-CMP-3739; @CL-CMP-3740; @CL-CMP-7694]. The definitions and the allocation of rights are on [Rights in data, general: definitions and allocation of rights](cmp-rights-in-data-general-definitions-and-allocation.md).

Class Deviation 2026-O0038 directs DoD contracting officers to use the revised FAR part 52 published on the Revolutionary FAR Overhaul web page in lieu of the text codified at 48 CFR chapter 1, effective February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760; @CL-CMP-9762]. Under RFO FAR 52.227-14(c)(1)(i), the Contracting Officer's prior, express written permission is required to assert copyright in all other data first produced in the performance of the contract [@CL-CMP-2174], and, unless provided otherwise in paragraph (d) of the clause, the Contractor may, without prior approval of the Contracting Officer, assert copyright in scientific and technical articles based on or containing data first produced in the performance of the contract and published in academic, technical or professional journals, symposia proceedings, or similar works [@CL-CMP-2175; @CL-CMP-2176]. Under RFO FAR 52.227-14(c)(1)(iii), for data other than computer software, the Contractor grants to the Government, and others acting on its behalf, a paid-up, nonexclusive, irrevocable, worldwide license in such copyrighted data to reproduce, prepare derivative works, distribute copies to the public, and perform publicly and display publicly by or on behalf of the Government [@CL-CMP-2177; @CL-CMP-4483], and for copyrighted computer software grants a paid-up, nonexclusive, irrevocable, worldwide license to reproduce, prepare derivative works, and perform publicly and display publicly (but not to distribute copies to the public) by or on behalf of the Government [@CL-CMP-2178; @CL-CMP-4484]. Under RFO FAR 52.227-14(c)(2), the Contractor shall not, without the prior written permission of the Contracting Officer, incorporate in data delivered under the contract any data not first produced in the performance of the contract unless the Contractor identifies the data and grants to the Government, or acquires on its behalf, a license of the same scope as set forth in paragraph (c)(1) of the clause or, if such data are restricted computer software, the Government shall acquire a copyright license as set forth in paragraph (g)(4) of the clause (if included in the contract) or as otherwise provided in a collateral agreement incorporated in or made part of the contract [@CL-CMP-2179; @CL-CMP-2180; @CL-CMP-4485]. Under RFO FAR 52.227-14(e)(1), notwithstanding any other provisions of the contract concerning inspection or acceptance, if any data delivered under the contract are marked with the notices specified in paragraph (g)(3) or (g)(4) if included in the clause and use of the notices is not authorized by the clause, or if the data bears any other restrictive or limiting markings not authorized by the contract, the Contracting Officer may at any time either return the data to the Contractor, or cancel or ignore the markings, but pursuant to section 4703 of title 41 of the United States Code the listed procedures apply prior to canceling or ignoring the markings [@CL-CMP-4486; @CL-CMP-2181], namely a written inquiry affording the Contractor 60 days from receipt to provide written justification to substantiate the propriety of the markings [@CL-CMP-2182], the Government's right to cancel or ignore the markings at any time after the 60-day period (or a longer time approved in writing by the Contracting Officer for good cause shown), with the data no longer made subject to any disclosure prohibitions, if the Contractor fails to respond or fails to provide written justification within that period [@CL-CMP-2183; @CL-CMP-4233], the Contracting Officer's consideration of written justification provided within the period set in paragraph (e)(1)(i) to determine whether or not the markings are to be cancelled or ignored [@CL-CMP-2184], and a written determination furnished by the Contracting Officer, with concurrence of the head of the contracting activity, when the markings are not authorized, which becomes the final agency decision regarding the appropriateness of the markings unless the Contractor files suit in a court of competent jurisdiction within 90 days of receipt of the Contracting Officer's decision [@CL-CMP-2185; @CL-CMP-2186]. Under RFO FAR 52.227-14(f)(1), data delivered to the Government without any restrictive markings shall be deemed to have been furnished with unlimited rights, and the Government is not liable for the disclosure, use, or reproduction of such data [@CL-CMP-2187]. Under RFO FAR 52.227-14(f)(2), if unmarked data has not been disclosed without restriction outside the Government, the Contractor may request, within 6 months (or a longer time approved by the Contracting Officer in writing for good cause shown) after delivery of the data, permission to have authorized notices placed on the data at the Contractor's expense [@CL-CMP-2188; @CL-CMP-9774], and the Contracting Officer may agree to do so if the Contractor identifies the data to which the omitted notice is to be applied, demonstrates that the omission of the notice was inadvertent, establishes that the proposed notice is authorized, and acknowledges that the Government has no liability for the disclosure, use, or reproduction of any data made prior to the addition of the notice or resulting from the omission of the notice [@CL-CMP-2189; @CL-CMP-0426]. Under RFO FAR 52.227-14(g)(1), the Contractor may withhold from delivery qualifying limited rights data or restricted computer software that are not data identified in paragraphs (b)(1)(i), (ii), and (iii) of the clause [@CL-CMP-2190], on the condition that the Contractor identifies the data being withheld and furnishes form, fit, and function data instead [@CL-CMP-2191]. Under RFO FAR 52.227-14(i), nothing contained in the clause shall imply a license to the Government under any patent or be construed as affecting the scope of any license or other right otherwise granted to the Government [@CL-CMP-2192]. Under RFO FAR 52.227-14 Alternate II, paragraph (g)(3) provides that, notwithstanding paragraph (g)(1) of the clause, the contract may identify and specify the delivery of limited rights data, or the Contracting Officer may require by written request the delivery of limited rights data that has been withheld or would otherwise be entitled to be withheld [@CL-CMP-2193]. Under RFO FAR 52.227-14 Alternate III, paragraph (g)(4)(i) provides the same rule for restricted computer software [@CL-CMP-2194; @CL-CMP-2195]. Under RFO FAR 52.227-14 Alternate IV, the substitute paragraph (c)(1) on data first produced in the performance of the contract provides that, except as otherwise specifically provided in the contract, the Contractor may assert copyright in any data first produced in the performance of the contract [@CL-CMP-2196; @CL-CMP-9775]. Under RFO FAR 52.227-14 Alternate V, the added paragraph (j) provides that the Contractor agrees, except as may be otherwise specified in the contract for specific data deliverables listed as not subject to that paragraph, that the Contracting Officer may, up to three years after acceptance of all deliverables under the contract, inspect at the Contractor's facility any data withheld pursuant to paragraph (g)(1) of the clause, for purposes of verifying the Contractor's assertion of limited rights or restricted rights status of the data or for evaluating work performance [@CL-CMP-2197; @CL-CMP-2198; @CL-CMP-9776].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor delivers a design report with no restrictive marking. Two months later it wants the authorized notice placed on the report. The report has not been disclosed outside the Government. The contractor asks in writing for permission to place the notice. The request is inside the six-month window, so the contractor may make it, and the Contracting Officer may agree if the contractor meets the four conditions in paragraph (f)(2), including showing that the omission was inadvertent and acknowledging that the Government has no liability for prior disclosure, use, or reproduction. Until the notice is placed, the Government treats the report as delivered with unlimited rights.

## Never confuse

Withholding under paragraph (g)(1) lets the Contractor withhold from delivery qualifying limited rights data or restricted computer software that are not data identified in paragraphs (b)(1)(i), (ii), and (iii) of the clause, on the condition that the Contractor identifies the data being withheld and furnishes form, fit, and function data instead [@CL-CMP-3697; @CL-CMP-3698]. Under paragraph (e)(1), for unauthorized notices under paragraph (g)(3) or (g)(4) or other restrictive or limiting markings not authorized by the contract on delivered data, the Contracting Officer may return the data to the Contractor or, after the paragraph (e)(1) procedures, cancel or ignore the markings [@CL-CMP-0346; @CL-CMP-3692]. Under the SBIR data rights clause at FAR 52.227-20(e)(1), data delivered to the Government without any notice authorized by paragraph (d) of that clause shall be deemed to have been furnished with unlimited rights, and the Government assumes no liability for the disclosure, use, or reproduction of such data [@CL-CMP-3721], as covered on [Rights in data under the SBIR and STTR program](cmp-rights-in-data-sbir-and-sttr-program.md). For marking disputes over contractor bid or proposal information under the procurement integrity rules, see [Procurement integrity: marking disputes and violations](cmp-procurement-integrity-marking-and-violations.md).
