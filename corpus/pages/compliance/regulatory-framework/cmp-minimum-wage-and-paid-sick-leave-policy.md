## Purpose

Sets out the minimum wage the subpart requires, who sets later rates, how the minimum wage relates to other wage laws and agreements, and how the paid sick leave requirements relate to other laws, agreements and voluntary paid time off policies. Who is covered is on the linked coverage page.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-6817] |
| Codified citation | FAR 22.1902, FAR 22.2100, FAR 22.2102 and FAR 22.2108 [@CL-CMP-6817; @CL-CMP-9544; @CL-CMP-6996; @CL-CMP-9546] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-6817] |
| Applies to | Workers performing on, or in connection with, contracts and subcontracts subject to the minimum wage subpart, and employees performing work on or in connection with a contract covered by Executive Order 13706 [@CL-CMP-6817; @CL-CMP-6996] |

Under FAR 22.1902, pursuant to Executive Order 14026, the minimum hourly wage rate required to be paid to workers performing on, or in connection with, contracts and subcontracts subject to the subpart is at least $15.00 per hour beginning January 30, 2022 [@CL-CMP-6817]. Beginning January 1, 2023, and annually thereafter, the required rate is an amount determined by the Secretary of Labor [@CL-CMP-6995]. The Administrator of the Wage and Hour Division will notify the public of the new E.O. minimum wage rate at least 90 days before it is to take effect [@CL-CMP-6818].

Nothing in the subpart excuses noncompliance with any applicable Federal or State prevailing wage law, any applicable law or municipal ordinance, or any applicable contract establishing a minimum wage higher than the E.O. minimum wage [@CL-CMP-6819]. Wage increases under such other laws or municipal ordinances are not subject to price adjustment under the subpart [@CL-CMP-6820]. The E.O. minimum wage rate applies whenever it is higher than any applicable collective bargaining agreement wage rate [@CL-CMP-6821]. Department of Labor regulations address the relationship between the E.O. minimum wage and the wages of workers engaged in an occupation in which they customarily and regularly receive more than $30 a month in tips [@CL-CMP-6822].

The paid sick leave subpart prescribes policies and procedures to implement Executive Order 13706, Establishing Paid Sick Leave for Federal Contractors, dated September 7, 2015, and Department of Labor implementing regulations at 29 CFR part 13 [@CL-CMP-9544].

Under FAR 22.2102, the Government must require contractors to allow employees performing work on or in connection with a contract covered by Executive Order 13706 to accrue and use paid sick leave in accordance with the order and 29 CFR part 13 [@CL-CMP-6996]. Nothing in the order or its regulations excuses noncompliance with or supersedes any applicable Federal or State law, any applicable law or municipal ordinance, or a collective bargaining agreement requiring greater paid sick leave or leave rights than those established under the order and 29 CFR part 13 [@CL-CMP-6823]. For additional details on interaction with the Service Contract Labor Standards statute, the Wage Rate Requirements (Construction) statute, the Family and Medical Leave Act and State and local paid sick time laws, the section refers to 29 CFR 13.5(f)(2) through (4) [@CL-CMP-6997].

In accordance with 29 CFR 13.5(f)(5)(i), the paid sick leave requirements may be satisfied by a contractor's voluntary paid time off policy, whether provided pursuant to a collective bargaining agreement or otherwise, where that policy meets or exceeds the requirements [@CL-CMP-8786] [@CL-CMP-6824]. For additional details on paid time off policies, the section refers to 29 CFR 13.5(f)(5)(ii) and (iii) [@CL-CMP-6998]. Under FAR 22.2108, contractors may fulfill their obligations under the order and 29 CFR part 13 jointly with other contractors through a multiemployer plan, or may fulfill their obligations through an individual fund, plan, or program (see 29 CFR 13.8) [@CL-CMP-9546]. Unless the subpart provides otherwise, compliance is the responsibility of the contractor and enforcement is the responsibility of the Department of Labor [@CL-CMP-6825].

## How it works

The Secretary of Labor determines each annual rate from January 1, 2023 on, and the Administrator of the Wage and Hour Division gives the public at least 90 days of notice before a new rate takes effect [@CL-CMP-6995; @CL-CMP-6818]. Nothing in the subpart excuses noncompliance with any applicable Federal or State prevailing wage law, any applicable law or municipal ordinance, or any applicable contract establishing a minimum wage higher than the E.O. minimum wage [@CL-CMP-6819], and the E.O. minimum wage rate applies whenever it is higher than any applicable collective bargaining agreement wage rate [@CL-CMP-6821]. Wage increases under such other laws or municipal ordinances are not subject to price adjustment under the subpart [@CL-CMP-6820]. Department of Labor regulations address the relationship between the E.O. minimum wage and wages of workers engaged in an occupation in which they customarily and regularly receive more than $30 a month in tips, in the policies and procedures at 29 CFR 23.240(b) and 23.280 [@CL-CMP-6822].

On paid sick leave, the Government shall require contractors to allow employees performing work on or in connection with a covered contract to accrue and use paid sick leave in accordance with the order and 29 CFR part 13 [@CL-CMP-6996]. A voluntary paid time off policy may satisfy the paid sick leave requirements where it meets or exceeds the requirements [@CL-CMP-6824]; for additional details regarding paid time off policies, see 29 CFR 13.5(f)(5)(ii) and (iii) [@CL-CMP-6998]. Unless otherwise provided in the subpart, compliance is the responsibility of the contractor, and enforcement is the responsibility of the Department of Labor [@CL-CMP-6825].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A services contract is subject to the minimum wage subpart. The E.O. minimum wage rate for the year is 17.00 an hour, and the collective bargaining agreement rate for one job category is 16.00. The E.O. minimum wage rate applies, because it is higher [@CL-CMP-6821]. A city ordinance sets 18.50 for the same work. The ordinance still has to be met, because nothing in the subpart excuses noncompliance with a municipal ordinance that sets a higher rate [@CL-CMP-6819]. The wage increase that comes from the ordinance is not subject to price adjustment under the subpart [@CL-CMP-6820].

The contractor operates a voluntary paid time off policy that gives its employees on the contract at least as much leave as the paid sick leave requirements. That policy may satisfy those requirements [@CL-CMP-6824]. A state law gives greater paid sick leave than the order. The order does not excuse noncompliance with it [@CL-CMP-6823]. Unless the subpart provides otherwise, the contractor is responsible for compliance, and the Department of Labor enforces [@CL-CMP-6825].

## Never confuse

Nothing in the subpart excuses noncompliance with any applicable Federal or State prevailing wage law, any applicable law or municipal ordinance, or any applicable contract establishing a minimum wage higher than the E.O. minimum wage [@CL-CMP-6819], and wage increases under such other laws or municipal ordinances are not subject to price adjustment under the subpart [@CL-CMP-6820].

A voluntary paid time off policy may satisfy the paid sick leave requirements where it meets or exceeds them [@CL-CMP-6824]. Nothing in E.O. 13706 or 29 CFR part 13 shall excuse noncompliance with or supersede a collective bargaining agreement requiring greater paid sick leave or leave rights than those established under the order and 29 CFR part 13 [@CL-CMP-6823].
