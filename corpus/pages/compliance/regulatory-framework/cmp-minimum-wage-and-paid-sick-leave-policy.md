## Purpose

Sets out the minimum wage the subpart requires, who sets later rates, how the minimum wage relates to other wage laws and agreements, and how the paid sick leave requirements relate to other laws, agreements and voluntary paid time off policies. Who is covered is on the linked coverage page.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-6817] |
| Codified citation | FAR 22.1902, FAR 22.2100, FAR 22.2102 and FAR 22.2108 [@CL-CMP-6817; @CL-CMP-9544; @CL-CMP-6996; @CL-CMP-9546] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-6817] |
| Applies to | Workers performing on, or in connection with, contracts and subcontracts subject to the minimum wage subpart, and employees performing work on or in connection with a contract covered by Executive Order 13706 [@CL-CMP-6817; @CL-CMP-6996] |

Under FAR 22.1902, pursuant to Executive Order 14026, the minimum hourly wage rate required to be paid to workers performing on, or in connection with, contracts and subcontracts subject to the subpart is at least $15.00 per hour beginning January 30, 2022 [@CL-CMP-6817]. Beginning January 1, 2023, and annually thereafter, the required rate is an amount determined by the Secretary of Labor [@CL-CMP-6995]. The Administrator of the Wage and Hour Division will notify the public of the new E.O. minimum wage rate at least 90 days before it is to take effect [@CL-CMP-6818].

Nothing in the subpart excuses noncompliance with any applicable Federal or State prevailing wage law, any applicable law or municipal ordinance, or any applicable contract establishing a minimum wage higher than the E.O. minimum wage [@CL-CMP-6819]. Wage increases under such other laws or municipal ordinances are not subject to price adjustment under the subpart [@CL-CMP-6820]. The E.O. minimum wage rate applies whenever it is higher than any applicable collective bargaining agreement wage rate [@CL-CMP-6821]. Department of Labor regulations address the relationship between the E.O. minimum wage and the wages of workers engaged in an occupation in which they customarily and regularly receive more than $30 a month in tips [@CL-CMP-6822].

The paid sick leave subpart prescribes policies and procedures to implement Executive Order 13706, Establishing Paid Sick Leave for Federal Contractors, dated September 7, 2015, and Department of Labor implementing regulations at 29 CFR part 13 [@CL-CMP-9544].

Under FAR 22.2102, the Government must require contractors to allow employees performing work on or in connection with a contract covered by Executive Order 13706 to accrue and use paid sick leave in accordance with the order and 29 CFR part 13 [@CL-CMP-6996]. Nothing in the order or its regulations excuses noncompliance with or supersedes any applicable Federal or State law, any applicable law or municipal ordinance, or a collective bargaining agreement requiring greater paid sick leave or leave rights than those established under the order [@CL-CMP-6823]. For additional details on interaction with the Service Contract Labor Standards statute, the Wage Rate Requirements (Construction) statute, the Family and Medical Leave Act and State and local paid sick time laws, the section refers to 29 CFR 13.5(f)(2) through (4) [@CL-CMP-6997].

The paid sick leave requirements may be satisfied by a contractor's voluntary paid time off policy, whether provided pursuant to a collective bargaining agreement or otherwise, where that policy meets or exceeds the requirements [@CL-CMP-6824]. For additional details on paid time off policies, the section refers to 29 CFR 13.5(f)(5)(ii) and (iii) [@CL-CMP-6998]. Under FAR 22.2108, contractors may fulfill their obligations under the order and 29 CFR part 13 jointly with other contractors through a multiemployer plan, or may fulfill their obligations through an individual fund, plan, or program (see 29 CFR 13.8) [@CL-CMP-9546]. Unless the subpart provides otherwise, compliance is the responsibility of the contractor and enforcement is the responsibility of the Department of Labor [@CL-CMP-6825].

## How it works

The Secretary of Labor determines each annual rate from January 1, 2023 on, and the Administrator of the Wage and Hour Division gives the public at least 90 days of notice before a new rate takes effect [@CL-CMP-6995; @CL-CMP-6818]. The contractor pays the higher of the E.O. minimum wage and any higher rate required by another law, ordinance or contract, and the rate applies whenever it is higher than the collective bargaining agreement rate [@CL-CMP-6819; @CL-CMP-6821]. A contractor cannot claim a price adjustment under the subpart for increases that come from those other laws or ordinances [@CL-CMP-6820]. For tipped workers the FAR points to Department of Labor regulations rather than stating the rule [@CL-CMP-6822].

On paid sick leave, the contractor must allow accrual and use as the order and its regulations require, and a law or collective bargaining agreement that gives greater leave still has to be met [@CL-CMP-6996; @CL-CMP-6823]. A voluntary paid time off policy can satisfy the requirement where it meets or exceeds the requirements, and the FAR points to the Department of Labor regulations for the details [@CL-CMP-6824; @CL-CMP-6998]. The contractor is responsible for compliance and the Department of Labor for enforcement, unless the subpart says otherwise [@CL-CMP-6825]. The printed sections do not establish whether the orders are in effect today, so this card states the mechanism only.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A services contract is subject to the minimum wage subpart. The E.O. minimum wage rate for the year is 17.00 an hour, and the collective bargaining agreement rate for one job category is 16.00. The E.O. minimum wage rate applies, because it is higher [@CL-CMP-6821]. A city ordinance sets 18.50 for the same work. The ordinance still has to be met, because nothing in the subpart excuses noncompliance with a municipal ordinance that sets a higher rate [@CL-CMP-6819]. The wage increase that comes from the ordinance is not subject to price adjustment under the subpart [@CL-CMP-6820].

The contractor operates a voluntary paid time off policy that gives its employees on the contract at least as much leave as the paid sick leave requirements. That policy may satisfy those requirements [@CL-CMP-6824]. A state law gives greater paid sick leave than the order. The order does not excuse noncompliance with it [@CL-CMP-6823]. Unless the subpart provides otherwise, the contractor is responsible for compliance, and the Department of Labor enforces [@CL-CMP-6825].

## Never confuse

The E.O. minimum wage is a floor. It does not replace a higher rate from a prevailing wage law, a municipal ordinance, or a contract, and price adjustment under the subpart does not cover the wage increases from those other laws and ordinances [@CL-CMP-6819; @CL-CMP-6820; @CL-CMP-6821].

A voluntary paid time off policy can satisfy the paid sick leave requirements where it meets or exceeds them. It does not override a law or agreement that gives greater leave [@CL-CMP-6824; @CL-CMP-6823].
