## Purpose

Shows what an offeror certifies about forced or indentured child labor in listed end products, the cooperation duty that follows, the exceptions to the cooperation and remedies clause, and the remedies for a violation.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-5896] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.222-18 and 52.222-19 [@CL-CMP-5896; @CL-CMP-5900] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.222-18 [@CL-CMP-0584] and RFO FAR 52.222-19 [@CL-CMP-0585] |
| Edition date | Feb 2021 [@CL-CMP-0584] |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-5896] |
| Applies to | Solicitations that list end products subject to the certification, and contracts carrying the cooperation and remedies clause [@CL-CMP-5897; @CL-CMP-5901] |

Forced or indentured child labor means all work or service exacted from any person under the age of 18 under the menace of any penalty for its nonperformance and for which the worker does not offer himself voluntarily [@CL-CMP-5896], or performed by any person under the age of 18 pursuant to a contract the enforcement of which can be accomplished by process or penalties [@CL-CMP-6390].

The certification provision gates award: the Government will not make award to an offeror unless the offeror certifies, by checking the appropriate block, to one of the two certification paragraphs [@CL-CMP-5897]. In the first the offeror states that it will not supply any listed end product that was mined, produced or manufactured in a corresponding listed country [@CL-CMP-5898]. In the second the offeror states that it has made a good faith effort to determine whether forced or indentured child labor was used, and that on the basis of those efforts it is not aware of any such use [@CL-CMP-5899].

## How it works

The provision lists the end products being acquired under the solicitation that are included in the List of Products Requiring Contractor Certification as to Forced or Indentured Child Labor, identified by their country of origin [@CL-CMP-7664]. There is a reasonable basis to believe that listed end products from the listed countries of origin may have been mined, produced or manufactured by forced or indentured child labor [@CL-CMP-6391].

The contractor's agreement to cooperate applies if the solicitation includes the provision 52.222-18, Certification Regarding Knowledge of Child Labor for Listed End Products, or the equivalent at 52.212-3(i) [@CL-CMP-5901]. Authorized officials may need to conduct investigations to determine whether forced or indentured child labor was used to mine, produce or manufacture any product furnished under the contract [@CL-CMP-6634]. Where that duty applies, the contractor agrees to cooperate fully with authorized officials of the contracting agency, the Department of the Treasury or the Department of Justice by providing reasonable access to records, documents, persons or premises on reasonable request [@CL-CMP-5902].

The clause does not apply to the extent that the Contractor is supplying end products mined, produced, or manufactured in Israel when the anticipated value of the acquisition is $50,000 or more, or in Mexico when it is $105,767 or more [@CL-CMP-5900]. It also does not apply to end products from the countries listed in paragraph (a)(3), ending with Ukraine and the United Kingdom, when the anticipated value of the acquisition is $174,000 or more [@CL-CMP-6392].

The Government may impose the remedies where the contractor has submitted a false certification regarding knowledge of the use of forced or indentured child labor for listed end products [@CL-CMP-6393]. It may also impose them where the contractor has failed to cooperate, if required in accordance with paragraph (b) of the clause, with an investigation of the use of forced or indentured child labor by an Inspector General, Attorney General or the Secretary of the Treasury [@CL-CMP-6394]. The remedies are also available where the contractor uses forced or indentured child labor in its mining, production or manufacturing processes, or has furnished under the contract end products or components mined, produced or manufactured wholly or in part by forced or indentured child labor [@CL-CMP-6395].

The Contracting Officer may terminate the contract [@CL-CMP-5903]. The suspending and debarring official may suspend the Contractor in accordance with procedures in FAR subpart 9.4, and may debar the Contractor for a period not to exceed 3 years in accordance with the procedures in FAR subpart 9.4 [@CL-CMP-6635]. The Government does not pursue the suspension and debarment remedies unless sufficient evidence indicates that the contractor knew of the violation [@CL-CMP-5904].

Under the RFO text, the DoD class deviation directs DoD contracting officers to use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text, effective February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. The RFO certification provision keeps the definition of forced or indentured child labor [@CL-CMP-0582; @CL-CMP-0583; @CL-CMP-5896; @CL-CMP-6390] and keeps the rule that the Government will not make award unless the offeror certifies to one of the two paragraphs [@CL-CMP-0584; @CL-CMP-5897]. The RFO cooperation and remedies clause keeps the exclusions for Israel at $50,000 or more and Mexico at $105,767 or more [@CL-CMP-0585; @CL-CMP-5900] and for the paragraph (a)(3) countries at $174,000 or more [@CL-CMP-0585; @CL-CMP-0586; @CL-CMP-6392]. It states the cooperation condition as the solicitation including the provision 52.222-18 [@CL-CMP-0587; @CL-CMP-5901], and keeps the duty to give reasonable access to records, documents, persons or premises on reasonable request [@CL-CMP-0588; @CL-CMP-5902]. It keeps the false-certification violation [@CL-CMP-0589; @CL-CMP-6393], the rule that remedies at paragraphs (d)(2) and (d)(3) need sufficient evidence that the Contractor knew of the violation [@CL-CMP-0590; @CL-CMP-5904], and the remedies of termination, suspension and debarment for a period not to exceed 3 years [@CL-CMP-0591; @CL-CMP-0592; @CL-CMP-5903; @CL-CMP-6635].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A solicitation lists an end product from a listed country, and the offeror checks the second block, certifying a good faith effort and no awareness of forced or indentured child labor, although it in fact knew that such labor was used [@CL-CMP-5899]. That is a false certification regarding knowledge of the use of forced or indentured child labor, so the Government may impose remedies [@CL-CMP-6393]. The Contracting Officer may terminate the contract [@CL-CMP-5903]; the Government will not pursue suspension or debarment unless sufficient evidence indicates that the Contractor knew of the violation, and the evidence here shows that it did [@CL-CMP-5904]. Separately, the clause does not apply to the extent that the Contractor is supplying end products mined, produced, or manufactured in Israel on an acquisition anticipated at 60,000, because 60,000 is at least 50,000 [@CL-CMP-5900]. For end products manufactured in Mexico on an acquisition anticipated at 100,000, the exclusion does not apply, because 100,000 is below 105,767 [@CL-CMP-5900].

## Never confuse

The definition covers work or service exacted from any person under the age of 18 under the menace of any penalty for its nonperformance, or performed by any person under the age of 18 pursuant to a contract the enforcement of which can be accomplished by process or penalties [@CL-CMP-5896; @CL-CMP-6390]. The first certification paragraph covers any end product listed in paragraph (b) of the provision that was mined, produced, or manufactured in a corresponding country as listed for that end product [@CL-CMP-5898]. The codified cooperation condition differs from the RFO condition: the codified condition covers a solicitation that includes the certification provision or the equivalent at 52.212-3(i) [@CL-CMP-5901], while the RFO condition is that the solicitation includes the provision 52.222-18, without the equivalent at 52.212-3(i) [@CL-CMP-0587].
