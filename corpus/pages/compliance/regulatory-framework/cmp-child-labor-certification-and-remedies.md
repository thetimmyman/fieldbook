## Purpose

Shows what an offeror certifies about forced or indentured child labor in listed end products, the cooperation duty that follows, the exceptions to the cooperation and remedies clause, and the remedies for a violation.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-5896] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.222-18 and 52.222-19 [@CL-CMP-5896; @CL-CMP-5900] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.222-18 [@CL-CMP-0584] and RFO FAR 52.222-19 [@CL-CMP-0585] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-5896] |
| Applies to | Solicitations that list end products subject to the certification, and contracts carrying the cooperation and remedies clause [@CL-CMP-5897; @CL-CMP-5901] |

Forced or indentured child labor includes work or service exacted from a person under the age of 18 under the menace of a penalty for nonperformance, where the worker does not offer himself voluntarily [@CL-CMP-5896]. It also includes all work or service performed by any person under the age of 18 pursuant to a contract the enforcement of which can be accomplished by process or penalties [@CL-CMP-6390].

The certification provision gates award: the Government will not make award to an offeror unless the offeror certifies, by checking the appropriate block, to one of the two certification paragraphs [@CL-CMP-5897]. In the first the offeror states that it will not supply any listed end product that was mined, produced or manufactured in a corresponding listed country [@CL-CMP-5898]. In the second the offeror states that it has made a good faith effort to determine whether forced or indentured child labor was used, and that on the basis of those efforts it is not aware of any such use [@CL-CMP-5899].

## How it works

The provision lists the end products by country of origin, and there is a reasonable basis to believe that listed end products from the listed countries of origin may have been mined, produced or manufactured by forced or indentured child labor [@CL-CMP-6391]. The listed end products and countries are filled in for each solicitation, so they are contract-specific.

The contractor's agreement to cooperate applies where the solicitation includes the child labor certification provision or its equivalent in the offeror representations [@CL-CMP-5901]. Authorized officials may need to conduct investigations to determine whether forced or indentured child labor was used to mine, produce or manufacture any product furnished under the contract [@CL-CMP-6634]. Where that duty applies, the contractor agrees to cooperate fully with authorized officials of the contracting agency, the Department of the Treasury or the Department of Justice by providing reasonable access to records, documents, persons or premises on reasonable request [@CL-CMP-5902].

The clause does not apply to the extent the contractor is supplying end products from Israel when the anticipated acquisition value is $50,000 or more, or from Mexico when it is $105,767 or more [@CL-CMP-5900]. It also does not apply to end products from the countries listed in paragraph (a)(3), ending with Ukraine and the United Kingdom, when the anticipated value of the acquisition is $174,000 or more [@CL-CMP-6392].

The Government may impose the remedies where the contractor has submitted a false certification regarding knowledge of the use of forced or indentured child labor for listed end products [@CL-CMP-6393]. It may also impose them where the contractor has failed to cooperate, if required, with an investigation of the use of forced or indentured child labor by an Inspector General, Attorney General or the Secretary of the Treasury [@CL-CMP-6394]. The remedies are also available where the contractor uses forced or indentured child labor in its mining, production or manufacturing processes, or has furnished under the contract end products or components mined, produced or manufactured wholly or in part by forced or indentured child labor [@CL-CMP-6395].

The remedies are termination by the contracting officer, suspension by the suspending and debarring official, and debarment by that official for a period not to exceed 3 years [@CL-CMP-5903]. The suspending and debarring official may suspend the contractor, and may debar it for a period not to exceed 3 years, in each case in accordance with the procedures in FAR subpart 9.4 [@CL-CMP-6635]. The Government does not pursue the suspension and debarment remedies unless sufficient evidence indicates that the contractor knew of the violation [@CL-CMP-5904].

Under the RFO text, the DoD class deviation directs DoD contracting officers to use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text, effective February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. The RFO certification provision keeps the definition of forced or indentured child labor [@CL-CMP-0582; @CL-CMP-0583; @CL-CMP-5896; @CL-CMP-6390] and keeps the rule that the Government will not make award unless the offeror certifies to one of the two paragraphs [@CL-CMP-0584; @CL-CMP-5897]. The RFO cooperation and remedies clause keeps the exclusions for Israel at $50,000 or more and Mexico at $105,767 or more [@CL-CMP-0585; @CL-CMP-5900] and for the paragraph (a)(3) countries at $174,000 or more [@CL-CMP-0585; @CL-CMP-0586; @CL-CMP-6392]. It states the cooperation condition as the solicitation including the provision 52.222-18 [@CL-CMP-0587; @CL-CMP-5901], and keeps the duty to give reasonable access to records, documents, persons or premises on reasonable request [@CL-CMP-0588; @CL-CMP-5902]. It keeps the false-certification violation [@CL-CMP-0589; @CL-CMP-6393], the rule that remedies at paragraphs (d)(2) and (d)(3) need sufficient evidence that the Contractor knew of the violation [@CL-CMP-0590; @CL-CMP-5904], and the remedies of termination, suspension and debarment for a period not to exceed 3 years [@CL-CMP-0591; @CL-CMP-0592; @CL-CMP-5903; @CL-CMP-6635].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A solicitation lists an end product from a listed country, and the offeror checks the second block, certifying a good faith effort and no awareness of forced or indentured child labor, although it in fact knew that such labor was used. That is a false certification regarding knowledge of the use of forced or indentured child labor, so the Government may impose remedies. The contracting officer may terminate the contract; suspension or debarment for a period not to exceed 3 years is pursued only if sufficient evidence indicates that the firm knew of the violation, and the evidence here shows that it did. Separately, a contractor supplying end products made in Israel on an acquisition anticipated at 60,000 is outside the clause to that extent, because 60,000 is at least 50,000. A contractor supplying end products made in Mexico on an acquisition anticipated at 100,000 is inside it, because 100,000 is below 105,767.

## Never confuse

Forced or indentured child labor is not child labor in general. The definition turns on compulsion: work or service exacted under the menace of a penalty that the worker does not volunteer for, or work performed under a contract enforceable by process or penalties, by a person under the age of 18 [@CL-CMP-5896; @CL-CMP-6390]. The certification is narrower still, covering listed end products from listed countries rather than every product in the supply chain [@CL-CMP-5898].

The codified cooperation condition covers a solicitation that includes the certification provision or the equivalent at 52.212-3(i) [@CL-CMP-5901], while the RFO condition names only the provision 52.222-18 [@CL-CMP-0587].
