## Purpose

Shows when the private security clause applies, what the contractor must do, which incidents it reports, how it must cooperate, and how the clause passes to subcontractors. It does not set out every requirement, and it says where it stops.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-CMP-4175] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 252.225-7039 [@CL-CMP-4175] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-4175] |
| Applies to | Contracts that include the clause, for performance in a designated area outside the United States [@CL-CMP-4175] |

If the contract is performed both in a designated area and in an area that is not designated, the clause only applies to performance in the designated area [@CL-CMP-4175]. Designated areas are areas outside the United States of the listed operations, the first of which is contingency operations [@CL-CMP-4175]. Private security functions are the listed activities engaged in by a contractor, the first of which is guarding of personnel, facilities, designated sites or property of a Federal agency, the contractor or subcontractor, or a third party [@CL-CMP-4176]. They also include any other activity for which personnel are required to carry weapons in the performance of their duties in accordance with the terms of the contract [@CL-CMP-4221].

The Contractor must ensure that all Contractor personnel who are responsible for performing private security functions under the contract comply with 32 CFR part 159 [@CL-CMP-4177]. It must also ensure that those personnel comply with any orders, directives, or instructions to contractors performing private security functions that are identified in the contract for registering, processing, accounting for, managing, overseeing and keeping appropriate records of personnel performing private security functions [@CL-CMP-4214]. The orders identified in the contract also cover authorizing, accounting for and registering in the Synchronized Predeployment and Operational Tracker the weapons to be carried by or available to be used by those personnel [@CL-CMP-4215], and identifying and registering in that tracker armored vehicles, helicopters and other military vehicles operated by Contractors performing private security functions [@CL-CMP-4216].

The orders identified in the contract also cover reporting incidents, in accordance with orders and instructions established by the applicable Combatant Commander [@CL-CMP-4178]. The listed incidents, any one of which is reportable, are ones in which a weapon is discharged by personnel performing private security functions [@CL-CMP-4178]; personnel performing private security functions are attacked, killed, or injured [@CL-CMP-4217]; persons are killed or injured or property is destroyed as a result of conduct by Contractor personnel [@CL-CMP-4218]; a weapon is discharged against such personnel or they believe a weapon was so discharged [@CL-CMP-4219]; or active, non-lethal countermeasures, other than the discharge of a weapon, are employed in response to a perceived immediate threat [@CL-CMP-4220]. The Contractor must also provide full cooperation with any Government-authorized investigation of reported incidents and of alleged misconduct by personnel performing private security functions, by providing the listed access and information [@CL-CMP-4179].

The Contracting Officer may direct the Contractor, at its own expense, to remove and replace any Contractor or subcontractor personnel performing private security functions who fail to comply with or violate applicable requirements of the clause or 32 CFR part 159 [@CL-CMP-4180]. The Contractor's duty to comply with the clause is not reduced or diminished by the failure of a higher- or lower-tier contractor or subcontractor to comply or by a failure of the contracting activity to provide required oversight [@CL-CMP-4222]. The Contractor must include the substance of the clause, including its flowdown paragraph, in subcontracts, including subcontracts for commercial products or commercial services, when private security functions will be performed outside the United States in areas of the listed operations [@CL-CMP-4181].

## How it works

The clause reaches only the designated-area part of a mixed contract [@CL-CMP-4175]. Within that scope the Contractor ensures its security personnel comply with the cited regulation and the orders identified in the contract [@CL-CMP-4177; @CL-CMP-4214], including the orders on reporting the listed incidents [@CL-CMP-4178; @CL-CMP-4217], and cooperates with Government-authorized investigations [@CL-CMP-4179].

Personnel who fail to comply with or violate applicable requirements can be removed and replaced at the Contractor's expense [@CL-CMP-4180]. The Contractor includes the substance of the clause in subcontracts, including subcontracts for commercial products or commercial services, when private security functions will be performed outside the United States in areas of the listed operations [@CL-CMP-4181].

This card does not set out every requirement. It leaves out the briefing duties for personnel, the management system standards the clause names, the definition of full cooperation, and the remedies other than removal and replacement. The clause text controls. Weapons authorization for deployed contractor personnel generally is covered on [the card on weapons, evacuation and flowdown](cmp-deployed-contractor-personnel-weapons-evacuation-and-flowdown.md), and the wider deployed-forces clause is on [the card on definitions and pre-deployment](cmp-deployed-contractor-personnel-definitions-and-predeployment.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A services contract has guard work at a site in a designated area and office work at a site that is not designated. Only the guard work falls under the clause.

A guard discharges a weapon during an incident. The contractor reports the incident as the Combatant Commander's orders and instructions require and gives investigators access to the guard and to the relevant information it holds. The same reporting duty also reaches other listed incidents, such as an attack on its guards.

## Never confuse

A contract that is only partly performed in a designated area is not wholly covered. Only the performance in the designated area is [@CL-CMP-4175].

Removal and replacement at the Contractor's expense is one remedy that the clause lists [@CL-CMP-4180]. The clause lists further remedies, which this card does not set out.

Reporting a weapon discharge is not the whole reporting duty. It is one of several listed incidents [@CL-CMP-4178; @CL-CMP-4217].
