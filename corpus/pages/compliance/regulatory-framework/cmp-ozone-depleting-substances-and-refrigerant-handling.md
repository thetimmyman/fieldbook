## Purpose

Explains the ozone-depleting substance labeling duty, the direction to EPA's alternatives program, and the reduction and handling duties for maintenance, service, repair or disposal of refrigeration and air conditioning equipment.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-5197] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.223-11 and FAR 52.223-12 [@CL-CMP-5197; @CL-CMP-5201] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-5197] |
| Applies to | Contractors whose contracts carry the ozone-depleting substances clause or the refrigeration and air conditioning clause [@CL-CMP-5197; @CL-CMP-5201] |

An ozone-depleting substance is any substance the Environmental Protection Agency designates in 40 CFR part 82 as Class I, including but not limited to chlorofluorocarbons, halons, carbon tetrachloride and methyl chloroform, or as Class II, including but not limited to hydrochlorofluorocarbons [@CL-CMP-5296].

The contractor must label products that contain or are manufactured with ozone-depleting substances in the manner and to the extent required by the statutory provisions the clause cites and 40 CFR part 82, subpart E [@CL-CMP-5197]. The label warns that the product contains, or is manufactured with, the named substance, which harms public health and environment by destroying ozone in the upper atmosphere, and the contractor inserts the name of the substance in the blank [@CL-CMP-5198].

For refrigeration and air conditioning work the contractor must comply with the applicable requirements of sections 608 and 609 of the Clean Air Act as each or both apply to the contract [@CL-CMP-5201].

## How it works

The contractor must refer to EPA's Significant New Alternatives Policy program to identify alternatives, whose list is found at 40 CFR part 82, subpart G, with supplemental tables available on the EPA website [@CL-CMP-5199]. The equipment clause gives the contractor the same direction [@CL-CMP-5206].

Global warming potential is how much a given mass of a chemical contributes to global warming over a given time period compared to the same mass of carbon dioxide, and the potential of carbon dioxide is defined as 1.0 [@CL-CMP-5297]. Hydrofluorocarbons are compounds that contain only hydrogen, fluorine, and carbon [@CL-CMP-5298; @CL-CMP-5300]. High global warming potential hydrofluorocarbons are any hydrofluorocarbons in a particular end use for which EPA's alternatives program has identified other acceptable alternatives that have lower global warming potential [@CL-CMP-5200; @CL-CMP-5299].

Unless the contract specifies otherwise, the equipment clause requires the contractor to reduce the use, release or emissions of high global warming potential hydrofluorocarbons under the contract by the routes it lists [@CL-CMP-5202]. One route is transitioning over time to another acceptable alternative in a particular end use where the alternatives program has identified acceptable alternatives with lower global warming potential [@CL-CMP-5203]. Two more are preventing and repairing refrigerant leaks through service and maintenance during contract performance, and implementing recovery, recycling and responsible disposal programs that avoid release or emissions during equipment service and as the equipment reaches the end of its useful life [@CL-CMP-5204]. The last is using reclaimed hydrofluorocarbons to service and repair refrigeration and air conditioning equipment, where feasible [@CL-CMP-5205].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor services 20 rooftop units under a contract that does not specify otherwise. It follows the applicable Clean Air Act requirements, repairs two refrigerant leaks during maintenance, and recovers refrigerant when a unit reaches the end of its useful life so that it is not released. It uses reclaimed hydrofluorocarbons for top-ups where that is feasible, and it plans to move one end use over time to an acceptable alternative that EPA's alternatives program lists with lower global warming potential. Separately it supplies a product manufactured with a Class II substance, so it labels the product with the required warning, which says the product was manufactured with the substance and names it.

## Never confuse

Labeling a product is not the same as reducing refrigerant emissions: the labeling duty attaches to products that contain or are manufactured with ozone-depleting substances, while the equipment clause governs the reduction of high global warming potential hydrofluorocarbons through the listed routes [@CL-CMP-5197; @CL-CMP-5202].

The definition of an ozone-depleting substance and the definition of a high global warming potential hydrofluorocarbon are different tests: the first turns on EPA's Class I and Class II designations in the regulation the clause cites, the second on whether EPA's alternatives program has identified other acceptable alternatives with lower global warming potential [@CL-CMP-5296; @CL-CMP-5200].

The reduction duty applies unless the contract specifies otherwise, and only the reclaimed hydrofluorocarbon route carries the words where feasible [@CL-CMP-5202; @CL-CMP-5205].

## Company calibration

Whether the company tracks which supplied products contain or are manufactured with ozone-depleting substances and which equipment holds high global warming potential refrigerant. [@Q-004]
