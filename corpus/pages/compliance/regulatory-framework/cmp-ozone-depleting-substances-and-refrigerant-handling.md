## Purpose

Explains the ozone-depleting substance labeling duty, the direction to EPA's alternatives program, and the reduction and handling duties for maintenance, service, repair or disposal of refrigeration and air conditioning equipment.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-5197] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.223-11 and FAR 52.223-12 [@CL-CMP-5197; @CL-CMP-5201] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.223-11 [@CL-CMP-1648] [@CL-CMP-1649] [@CL-CMP-1690] [@CL-CMP-1691] [@CL-CMP-1692] and RFO FAR 52.223-12 [@CL-CMP-1693] [@CL-CMP-1694] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-5197] |
| Applies to | Contractors whose contracts carry the ozone-depleting substances clause or the refrigeration and air conditioning clause [@CL-CMP-5197; @CL-CMP-5201] |

An ozone-depleting substance is any substance the Environmental Protection Agency designates in 40 CFR part 82 as Class I, including but not limited to chlorofluorocarbons, halons, carbon tetrachloride and methyl chloroform, or as Class II, including but not limited to hydrochlorofluorocarbons [@CL-CMP-5296].

The contractor must label products that contain or are manufactured with ozone-depleting substances in the manner and to the extent required by title 42 of the United States Code, section 7671j (b), (c), (d), and (e) and 40 CFR part 82, subpart E [@CL-CMP-5197]. The label warns that the product contains (or is manufactured with, if applicable) the named substance, which harms public health and environment by destroying ozone in the upper atmosphere, and the contractor inserts the name of the substance in the blank [@CL-CMP-5198].

Under FAR 52.223-12 the contractor must comply with the applicable requirements of sections 608 and 609 of the Clean Air Act as each or both apply to the contract [@CL-CMP-5201].

## How it works

The contractor must refer to EPA's Significant New Alternatives Policy program to identify alternatives, whose list is found at 40 CFR part 82, subpart G, with supplemental tables available on the EPA website [@CL-CMP-5199]. The equipment clause gives the contractor the same direction [@CL-CMP-5206].

Global warming potential is how much a given mass of a chemical contributes to global warming over a given time period compared to the same mass of carbon dioxide, and the potential of carbon dioxide is defined as 1.0 [@CL-CMP-5297]. Hydrofluorocarbons are compounds that contain only hydrogen, fluorine, and carbon [@CL-CMP-5298; @CL-CMP-5300]. High global warming potential hydrofluorocarbons are any hydrofluorocarbons in a particular end use for which EPA's alternatives program has identified other acceptable alternatives that have lower global warming potential [@CL-CMP-5200; @CL-CMP-5299].

Unless otherwise specified in the contract, the Contractor shall reduce the use, release, or emissions of high global warming potential hydrofluorocarbons under the contract by the following [@CL-CMP-5202]: transitioning over time to the use of another acceptable alternative in lieu of high global warming potential hydrofluorocarbons in a particular end use for which EPA's alternatives program has identified other acceptable alternatives that have lower global warming potential [@CL-CMP-5203]; preventing and repairing refrigerant leaks through service and maintenance during contract performance; implementing recovery, recycling, and responsible disposal programs that avoid release or emissions during equipment service and as the equipment reaches the end of its useful life [@CL-CMP-5204]; and using reclaimed hydrofluorocarbons to service and repair refrigeration and air conditioning equipment, where feasible [@CL-CMP-5205].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text from February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. RFO FAR 52.223-11 defines an ozone-depleting substance in the same terms as the codified clause [@CL-CMP-5296] [@CL-CMP-1648]. It says that, in accordance with 40 CFR 82.84(a)(5), the contractor shall label products that contain or are manufactured with ozone-depleting substances [@CL-CMP-5197] [@CL-CMP-1690], with the same warning wording and duty to insert the name of the substance [@CL-CMP-5198] [@CL-CMP-1691]. Its paragraph (a) ends with the ozone-depleting substance definition, where the codified paragraph (a) also defined global warming potential, hydrofluorocarbons and high global warming potential hydrofluorocarbons [@CL-CMP-5297] [@CL-CMP-5298] [@CL-CMP-5200] [@CL-CMP-1649]. The RFO clause also ends after the warning, where the codified clause continued with the direction to EPA's alternatives program [@CL-CMP-5199] [@CL-CMP-1692]. RFO FAR 52.223-12 says that, in accordance with 40 CFR 82.84(a)(3), the contractor shall comply with the applicable requirements of sections 608 and 609 of the Clean Air Act as each or both apply [@CL-CMP-5201] [@CL-CMP-1693]. That sentence is the whole of the RFO clause text, where the codified clause also had a paragraph (a) defining hydrofluorocarbons and high global warming potential hydrofluorocarbons, listed the routes for reducing high global warming potential hydrofluorocarbons, and gave the same direction to EPA's alternatives program [@CL-CMP-5300] [@CL-CMP-5299] [@CL-CMP-5202] [@CL-CMP-5206] [@CL-CMP-1694].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor services 20 rooftop units under a contract that does not specify otherwise. It follows the applicable Clean Air Act requirements, repairs two refrigerant leaks during maintenance, and recovers refrigerant when a unit reaches the end of its useful life so that it is not released. It uses reclaimed hydrofluorocarbons for top-ups where that is feasible, and it plans to move one end use over time to an acceptable alternative that EPA's alternatives program lists with lower global warming potential. Separately it supplies a product manufactured with a Class II substance, so it labels the product with the required warning, which says the product was manufactured with the substance and names it. The reduction steps in that example come from the codified equipment clause [@CL-CMP-5202]; RFO FAR 52.223-12 prints only the Clean Air Act compliance sentence [@CL-CMP-1694].

## Never confuse

The labeling duty in FAR 52.223-11(b) applies to products that contain or are manufactured with ozone-depleting substances [@CL-CMP-5197]. Under FAR 52.223-12(c), unless otherwise specified in the contract, the Contractor shall reduce the use, release, or emissions of high global warming potential hydrofluorocarbons under the contract [@CL-CMP-5202].

An ozone-depleting substance is any substance the Environmental Protection Agency designates in 40 CFR part 82 as Class I or Class II [@CL-CMP-5296]. High global warming potential hydrofluorocarbons are any hydrofluorocarbons in a particular end use for which EPA's alternatives program has identified other acceptable alternatives that have lower global warming potential [@CL-CMP-5200].

The reduction duty applies unless otherwise specified in the contract [@CL-CMP-5202], and the use of reclaimed hydrofluorocarbons to service and repair refrigeration and air conditioning equipment is to be done where feasible [@CL-CMP-5205].

The codified equipment clause requires the contractor, unless the contract specifies otherwise, to reduce the use, release or emissions of high global warming potential hydrofluorocarbons by the listed routes, but RFO FAR 52.223-12 is a single sentence on Clean Air Act sections 608 and 609 [@CL-CMP-5202] [@CL-CMP-1694].

The clauses on aerosols and foams that use high global warming potential hydrofluorocarbons are covered in [Aerosols and foams with high global warming potential hydrofluorocarbons](cmp-aerosols-and-foams-hydrofluorocarbon-reduction.md).

## Company calibration

Whether the company tracks which supplied products contain or are manufactured with ozone-depleting substances and which equipment holds high global warming potential refrigerant. [@Q-004]
