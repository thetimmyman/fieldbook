## Purpose

Shows which contractor personnel must hold information assurance certifications, what the contractor must document, and what the supply chain risk notice tells offerors.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-CMP-4602; @CL-CMP-4606] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 252.239-7001 [@CL-CMP-4602]; DFARS 252.239-7017 [@CL-CMP-4606] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-4602; @CL-CMP-4606] |
| Applies to | Contractor personnel accessing information systems to perform information assurance functions [@CL-CMP-4602], and offerors and their supply chains under the notice provision [@CL-CMP-4606] |

The contractor must ensure that personnel accessing information systems have the proper and current information assurance certification to perform information assurance functions in accordance with DoD 8570.01-M, Information Assurance Workforce Improvement Program [@CL-CMP-4602]. Two listed certification requirements are DoD-approved information assurance workforce certifications appropriate for each category and level in the current version of DoD 8570.01-M, and appropriate operating system certification for information assurance technical positions as DoD 8570.01-M requires [@CL-CMP-4603]. Upon request by the Government, the contractor must provide documentation supporting the certification status of personnel performing information assurance functions [@CL-CMP-4604]. Contractor personnel who do not have proper and current certifications shall be denied access to DoD information systems for the purpose of performing information assurance functions [@CL-CMP-4605].

Supply chain risk means the risk that an adversary may sabotage, maliciously introduce unwanted function, or otherwise subvert the design, integrity, manufacturing, production, distribution, installation, operation, or maintenance of a covered system [@CL-CMP-4734]. To manage supply chain risk, the Government may use the authorities provided by the statute the provision cites, and in exercising those authorities it may consider public and non-public information, including all-source intelligence, relating to an offeror and its supply chain [@CL-CMP-4606]. If the Government exercises its statutory authority to limit disclosure of information, no action it undertakes under that authority is subject to review in a bid protest before the Government Accountability Office or in any Federal court [@CL-CMP-4607].

## How it works

The training clause works through the people who do the work. Only personnel with proper and current certification may perform information assurance functions on DoD information systems [@CL-CMP-4602; @CL-CMP-4605]. The contractor keeps documentation of their certification status ready for a Government request [@CL-CMP-4604]. The notice provision works on the offer side: it defines supply chain risk [@CL-CMP-4734], tells offerors that the Government may weigh information about them and their supply chain [@CL-CMP-4606], and states that a decision to limit disclosure cannot be reviewed in a bid protest or in any Federal court [@CL-CMP-4607].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

Assume a contractor has 40 staff on a help desk, and 12 of them perform information assurance functions. Two of the 12 have lapsed certifications. Those two are denied access to DoD information systems for that work until their certifications are current. When the Government asks, the contractor sends documentation of the certification status of all 12.

## Never confuse

The notice provision is not the clause that puts a mitigation duty on the contractor. The provision tells offerors what the Government may consider and that disclosure limits are not reviewable [@CL-CMP-4606; @CL-CMP-4607]. The mitigation clause is covered in [Supply chain risk and telecommunications security equipment](../trade-cyber/cmp-supply-chain-risk-and-telecommunications-security-equipment.md). The training clause is also about certified people, not about the offeror's supply chain [@CL-CMP-4602], and not about the safeguarding of contractor information systems, which is covered in [Basic safeguarding clause controls](cmp-basic-safeguarding-clause-controls.md).
