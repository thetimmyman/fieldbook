## Purpose

Sets out the written notice a contractor gives before delivering or servicing items that contain radioactive material, the marking duty, the waiver the contractor may request when nothing has changed, and the flowdown to subcontracts.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-5189] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.223-7 [@CL-CMP-5189] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.223-7 [@CL-CMP-1473; @CL-CMP-1474; @CL-CMP-1475] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-5189] |
| Applies to | Contractors delivering or servicing items that contain radioactive material of either kind the clause describes [@CL-CMP-5189; @CL-CMP-5190; @CL-CMP-5191] |

The contractor must notify the contracting officer or designee in writing, the number of days that the contract inserts before delivery of items, or before completion of any servicing the contract requires of items, that contain either of two kinds of radioactive material [@CL-CMP-5189]. The number of days is a blank that the contracting officer fills in, so it is specific to the contract: the contracting officer inserts the number of days required in advance to assure that required licenses are obtained and appropriate personnel are notified to institute any necessary safety and health precautions [@CL-CMP-5292].

The first kind is radioactive material requiring specific licensing under the regulations issued pursuant to the Atomic Energy Act of 1954, as set forth in title 10 of the Code of Federal Regulations in effect on the date of the contract [@CL-CMP-5190]. The second kind is other radioactive material not requiring specific licensing in which the specific activity is greater than 0.002 microcuries per gram or the activity per item equals or exceeds 0.01 microcuries [@CL-CMP-5191].

## How it works

The notice must specify the part or parts of the items that contain radioactive materials, a description of the materials, the name and activity of the isotope, and the manufacturer of the materials [@CL-CMP-5192]. It must also include any other information known to the contractor that will put users of the items on notice as to the hazards involved [@CL-CMP-5293].

If there has been no change affecting the quantity of activity, or the characteristics and composition of the radioactive material, from deliveries under this contract or prior contracts, the contractor may request that the contracting officer or designee waive the notice requirement in paragraph (a) of that clause [@CL-CMP-5193; @CL-CMP-8219]. The request must be submitted in writing and must state that the quantity of activity, characteristics and composition have not changed [@CL-CMP-5294]. It must also cite the contract number on which the prior notification was submitted and the contracting office to which it was submitted [@CL-CMP-5295].

The marking requirement covers all items, parts or subassemblies that contain radioactive materials in which the specific activity is greater than 0.002 microcuries per gram or the activity per item equals or exceeds 0.01 microcuries [@CL-CMP-5194]. Those items, parts or subassemblies, and all containers in which they are delivered to the Government, must be clearly marked and labeled as required by the latest revision of MIL-STD 129 in effect on the date of the contract [@CL-CMP-5195].

The clause, including its flowdown paragraph, must be inserted in all subcontracts for radioactive materials meeting the criteria in the notice paragraph [@CL-CMP-5196].

Under the RFO text, the DoD class deviation directs DoD contracting officers to use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text, effective February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. The RFO text of FAR 52.223-7 keeps the written notice before delivery or servicing for the two kinds of radioactive material, with the number of days a blank [@CL-CMP-1473] [@CL-CMP-1474] [@CL-CMP-1475] [@CL-CMP-5189] [@CL-CMP-5190] [@CL-CMP-5191], and the list of what the notice must specify [@CL-CMP-1476] [@CL-CMP-5192]. Its notice sentence also requires any other information known to the contractor that will put users of the items on notice as to the hazards involved [@CL-CMP-1477] [@CL-CMP-5293], and its footnote has the contracting officer insert the number of days required in advance of delivery or completion of the servicing [@CL-CMP-1478] [@CL-CMP-5292]. Two references differ. The codified sentence on the contents of the notice ends with the reference "OMB No. 9000-0107" [@CL-CMP-1529], and the RFO text prints no such reference after that sentence [@CL-CMP-1499]. The codified footnote ends with a cross-reference to FAR 23.601(d) [@CL-CMP-9249], and the RFO footnote prints no such cross-reference [@CL-CMP-1530]. The RFO text also keeps the waiver request when nothing has changed, with its three requirements [@CL-CMP-1479] [@CL-CMP-8218] [@CL-CMP-1480] [@CL-CMP-1481] [@CL-CMP-5193] [@CL-CMP-8219] [@CL-CMP-5294] [@CL-CMP-5295]. It keeps the marking duty for the items and their containers [@CL-CMP-1482] [@CL-CMP-1483] [@CL-CMP-5194] [@CL-CMP-5195] and the insertion of the clause, including paragraph (d), in subcontracts [@CL-CMP-1484] [@CL-CMP-5196]. The RFO text keeps "shall" in this clause [@CL-CMP-1473] [@CL-CMP-5189].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

The contract inserts 45 days. A contractor will deliver a gauge holding a source that requires specific licensing under the regulations, and a small assembly that needs no specific licensing but carries 0.03 microcuries per item, which is above the per-item limit of 0.01. It sends written notice to the contracting officer 45 days before delivery. The notice names the parts that hold the material, describes the materials, gives the isotope name and activity, names the manufacturer, and adds what else the contractor knows about the hazards. The gauge carries 0.5 microcuries per item, so the gauge, the assembly and the shipping container are marked and labeled to the version of the military standard in effect on the contract date. The contractor inserts the clause, with its flowdown paragraph, in its subcontract for the source. On a later delivery of an identical gauge with the same activity, characteristics and composition, it may request a waiver. The request is written, says nothing has changed, and cites the earlier contract number and the contracting office that received the first notice.

## Never confuse

The waiver is a request the contractor may make, not a general exemption: it depends on there being no change affecting the quantity of activity, characteristics or composition, and the request must be written, state that nothing has changed, and cite the earlier notification [@CL-CMP-5193; @CL-CMP-5294; @CL-CMP-5295].

Marking is a separate duty from the notice. The notice has two triggers, licensing and the activity limits, while the marking paragraph states only the activity limits, and it attaches to the items, parts and subassemblies above them and to their containers [@CL-CMP-5190; @CL-CMP-5191; @CL-CMP-5194; @CL-CMP-5195].

The clause does not fix the notice period: the number of days is a blank that the contracting officer fills in for the contract [@CL-CMP-5292].

## Company calibration

Whether shipping records identify every item that meets the licensing or activity triggers and can produce the notice before each delivery or servicing, and which subcontracts for radioactive materials need the clause. [@Q-004]
