## Purpose

Sets out what an agency must do before it acts against a contractor for a gratuities violation: the notice and hearing, the determination, and the remedies that follow.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-7646] |
| Codified citation | FAR 3.203 [@CL-CMP-9631]; FAR 3.204 [@CL-CMP-7646] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-7646] |
| Applies to | Action by an agency against a contractor, where the question is whether the contractor, its agent or another representative, under a contract containing the Gratuities clause, offered or gave the gratuity [@CL-CMP-7646] |

Before taking any action against a contractor, the agency head or a designee shall determine, after notice and hearing under agency procedures, whether the contractor, its agent, or another representative, under a contract containing the Gratuities clause, did two things [@CL-CMP-7646]. The first is that it offered or gave a gratuity (for example an entertainment or gift) to an officer, official, or employee of the Government [@CL-CMP-7647]. The second is that it intended by the gratuity to obtain a contract or favorable treatment under a contract; intent generally must be inferred [@CL-CMP-7648].

Agency procedures shall afford the contractor an opportunity to appear with counsel, submit documentary evidence, present witnesses, and confront any person the agency presents [@CL-CMP-7649]. The procedures should be as informal as practicable, consistent with principles of fundamental fairness [@CL-CMP-7650].

When the agency head or designee determines that a violation has occurred, the Government may terminate the contractor's right to proceed and may initiate debarment or suspension measures as set forth in subpart 9.4 [@CL-CMP-7651]. It may also assess exemplary damages, if the contract uses money appropriated to the Department of Defense [@CL-CMP-7652].

## How it works

The order is fixed. Notice and a hearing come first, under agency procedures. The agency head or a designee then makes the determination. Only after a determination that a violation has occurred may the Government use the listed remedies [@CL-CMP-7646; @CL-CMP-7651]. The determination comes before any action against the contractor, not only before the three listed remedies [@CL-CMP-7646].

The section does not say who gives the notice, when, or what it must contain. It sets no deadline and no dollar threshold. Those points fall to agency procedures [@CL-CMP-7646].

The duty to provide the hearing rights falls on the agency, whose procedures shall afford them, and the rights run to the contractor [@CL-CMP-7649]. The procedures should be informal where practicable, within the limit of fundamental fairness [@CL-CMP-7650].

The determination has two parts, and the agency head or designee decides both. The first is the offer or gift [@CL-CMP-7647]. The second is intent, which generally must be inferred [@CL-CMP-7648]. The section applies to a contract containing the Gratuities clause [@CL-CMP-7646].

The listed remedies are each something the Government "may" do, not something it must do. The section does not say who in the Government chooses among them [@CL-CMP-7651]. The paragraph states exemplary damages for a contract that uses money appropriated to the Department of Defense [@CL-CMP-7652]. For the clause terms, including termination by written notice and the amount of exemplary damages, see [Gratuities, termination and exemplary damages](cmp-gratuities.md). For debarment and suspension, see [Debarment and suspension: subpart scope and Governmentwide effect](cmp-debarment-and-suspension-scope-and-effect.md).

A separate section covers reports of suspected violations: agency personnel shall report suspected violations of the Gratuities clause to the contracting officer or other designated official in accordance with agency procedures [@CL-CMP-9631].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contract contains the Gratuities clause and uses money appropriated to the Department of Defense [@CL-CMP-7646; @CL-CMP-7652]. During a competition, the contractor's sales agent gives a buyer in a Government office an entertainment package that costs 400. The agency gives the contractor notice and holds a hearing under agency procedures [@CL-CMP-7646]. The contractor appears with counsel, submits documents, presents witnesses and confronts the person the agency presents [@CL-CMP-7649].

The agency head determines that the package was given to the buyer and that the sales agent intended it to obtain favorable treatment under a contract. Intent generally must be inferred [@CL-CMP-7646; @CL-CMP-7647; @CL-CMP-7648]. Only now may the Government terminate the contractor's right to proceed and initiate debarment or suspension measures under subpart 9.4 [@CL-CMP-7651]. It may also assess exemplary damages, because the contract uses Department of Defense money [@CL-CMP-7652].

If the contract used no such money, the paragraph would not list exemplary damages for it, and the Government could still use the other two actions after the same determination [@CL-CMP-7651; @CL-CMP-7652].

## Never confuse

Showing that a gift was offered or given is not enough. The determination also covers the intent behind it [@CL-CMP-7647; @CL-CMP-7648].

Termination of the right to proceed, debarment or suspension, and exemplary damages are three separate listed actions. Only the third carries a condition about Department of Defense money in this paragraph [@CL-CMP-7651; @CL-CMP-7652].

## Company calibration

The company must confirm which of its contracts contain the Gratuities clause and who confirms clause applicability [@Q-004].
