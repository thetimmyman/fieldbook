## Purpose

Shows what the contractor indemnifies under the patent indemnity clause, where the indemnity stops, and how royalties reported before final payment are tested and refunded.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-3611; @CL-CMP-3619] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.227-3 and FAR 52.227-9 [@CL-CMP-3611; @CL-CMP-3619] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-3611; @CL-CMP-3619] |
| Applies to | Work under the contract for the indemnity, and royalties included in the contract price for the refund [@CL-CMP-3613; @CL-CMP-3620] |

The patent indemnity clause provides that the Contractor shall indemnify the Government and its officers, agents, and employees against liability, including costs, for infringement of any United States patent [@CL-CMP-3611], other than a patent issued upon an application that is now or may hereafter be withheld from issue under a Secrecy Order [@CL-CMP-3612]. It covers liability arising out of the manufacture or delivery of supplies, the performance of services, or construction work under the contract [@CL-CMP-3613], and liability arising out of the use or disposal, by or for the account of the Government, of those supplies or that construction work [@CL-CMP-3614]. The indemnity does not apply unless the Contractor was informed as soon as practicable by the Government of the suit or action and was given the opportunity the law affords to take part in its defense [@CL-CMP-3615]. The clause then lists exceptions [@CL-CMP-3616]. The first covers an infringement resulting from compliance with specific written instructions of the Contracting Officer directing a change in the supplies to be delivered or in the materials or equipment to be used [@CL-CMP-3616], and the same exception also reaches instructions directing a manner of performance of the contract not normally used by the Contractor [@CL-CMP-3677]. The next covers an infringement resulting from addition to or change in supplies or components furnished, or construction work performed, that was made after delivery or performance [@CL-CMP-3616; @CL-CMP-3617]. The last covers a claimed infringement that is unreasonably settled without the consent of the Contractor, unless required by final decree of a court of competent jurisdiction [@CL-CMP-3616; @CL-CMP-3618].

Alternate I adds a paragraph to the basic clause stating that the patent indemnification shall not apply to the items that the Contracting Officer lists or identifies as excluded [@CL-CMP-3658]. Alternate II adds a paragraph stating that the patent indemnification shall cover the items that are listed or identified for inclusion [@CL-CMP-3659]. Alternate III provides that, as to subcontracts at any tier for communication service, the clause shall apply only to individual communication service authorizations over the simplified acquisition threshold that are issued under the contract [@CL-CMP-4226].

Under the refund of royalties clause, the Contractor shall furnish the Contracting Officer, before final payment, a statement of royalties paid or required to be paid in connection with the contract and its subcontracts, with the reasons [@CL-CMP-3619]. The Contractor is compensated for reported royalties only to the extent that they were included in the contract price and the Contracting Officer determines they are properly chargeable to the Government and allocable to the contract [@CL-CMP-3620]. To the extent that royalties included in the contract price are not in fact paid, or are determined not to be properly chargeable and allocable, the contract price shall be reduced [@CL-CMP-3621]. Repayment or credit to the Government shall be made as the Contracting Officer directs [@CL-CMP-3660]. The duty to notify and reimburse arises if, at any time within 3 years after final payment, the Contractor is for any reason relieved in whole or in part from paying royalties included in the final contract price as adjusted [@CL-CMP-3622]. In that case the Contractor shall promptly notify the Contracting Officer and reimburse the Government in a corresponding amount [@CL-CMP-3622; @CL-CMP-3623]. The substance of the refund clause shall be included in any subcontract in which the amount of royalties reported during negotiation of the subcontract exceeds $250 [@CL-CMP-3661].

## How it works

The indemnity clause is a risk allocation: it makes the Contractor answer to the Government for infringement liability arising from the covered work, subject to the notice condition and the listed exceptions [@CL-CMP-3611; @CL-CMP-3613; @CL-CMP-3615; @CL-CMP-3616]. The refund clause is a price mechanism: the royalties the Contractor reports are tested against the contract price, and the price is reduced for royalties not paid or not properly chargeable and allocable [@CL-CMP-3620; @CL-CMP-3621]. Whether a royalty can be charged as a cost at all is a different test, covered in [Royalties and other costs for use of patents](../cas-allowability/cmp-patent-royalties.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

The contract price includes 20,000 for royalties. The final statement shows that only 15,000 was in fact paid, and the Contracting Officer finds all of the 15,000 properly chargeable and allocable. The price is reduced by 5,000. Two years after final payment, the Contractor is relieved of a further 3,000 of the royalties it did pay. It notifies the Contracting Officer promptly and reimburses 3,000.

## Never confuse

The refund clause is not the allowability test for royalty cost; that test sits in the royalties cost principle covered on its own card. The patent indemnity clause is also not the Government's authorization and consent to use patented inventions, which is a separate clause covered on the [authorization and consent card](cmp-authorization-and-consent-clause.md). The refund clause is likewise not the royalty disclosure that an offeror makes with its offer, which has its own [royalty information card](cmp-royalty-information-provision.md). The indemnity concerns infringement liability, while the refund clause concerns royalties already in the contract price [@CL-CMP-3611; @CL-CMP-3620].
