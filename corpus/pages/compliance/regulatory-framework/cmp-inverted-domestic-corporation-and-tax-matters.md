## Purpose

Shows what an offeror represents about inverted domestic corporations, what the tax matters certification requires above a stated price, and where the certification carve-outs sit.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-5566] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.209-2 and 52.209-12 [@CL-CMP-5566; @CL-CMP-5568] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-5566] |
| Applies to | Offerors that receive the inverted domestic corporations representation provision prescribed by FAR 9.108-5(a) and the tax matters certification provision prescribed by FAR 9.104-7(e) [@CL-CMP-5651; @CL-CMP-5661] |

Government agencies are not permitted to use appropriated (or otherwise made available) funds for contracts with either an inverted domestic corporation or a subsidiary of an inverted domestic corporation, unless the exception at FAR 9.108-2(b) applies or the requirement is waived [@CL-CMP-5566]. A waiver must follow the procedures at FAR 9.108-4 [@CL-CMP-5653]. The terms inverted domestic corporation and subsidiary have the meaning given in the clause at FAR 52.209-10 [@CL-CMP-5652].

In the representation the offeror marks whether it is, or is not, an inverted domestic corporation and whether it is, or is not, a subsidiary of an inverted domestic corporation [@CL-CMP-5567].

The tax matters provision implements section 523 of Division B of the Consolidated and Further Continuing Appropriations Act, 2015, and similar provisions in later appropriations acts [@CL-CMP-5654]. Where the offeror proposes a total contract price that will exceed $7 million including options, it must certify, to the best of its knowledge and belief, three matters [@CL-CMP-5568]. The provision prints a bracketed box beside each matter, and the offeror's answer is specific to its offer [@CL-CMP-5655; @CL-CMP-5656; @CL-CMP-5657].

- It has filed all Federal tax returns required during the three years preceding the certification [@CL-CMP-5655].
- It has not been convicted of a criminal offense under the Internal Revenue Code of 1986 [@CL-CMP-5656].
- It has not, more than 90 days before the certification, been notified of any unpaid Federal tax assessment for which the liability remains unsatisfied, subject to the carve-outs below [@CL-CMP-5657; @CL-CMP-5569].

## How it works

The representation asks two questions about status, and the prohibition on using funds is subject to the exception and waiver routes named above [@CL-CMP-5566; @CL-CMP-5567; @CL-CMP-5653].

The third tax matter does not apply to an assessment that is the subject of an installment agreement or offer in compromise that the Internal Revenue Service has approved and that is not in default, or that is the subject of a non-frivolous administrative or judicial proceeding [@CL-CMP-5569].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An offeror proposes a total price of 9,000,000 including options, which is over the 7,000,000 threshold, so it makes the tax certification. It was notified 120 days before the certification of an unpaid assessment that remains unsatisfied, but the assessment is the subject of an approved installment agreement that is not in default, so it does not count against the third matter. The offeror also marks that it is not an inverted domestic corporation but is a subsidiary of one, and the agency may not use appropriated funds for the award unless the exception or a waiver applies.

## Never confuse

The representation and the certification are separate provisions. The representation concerns status as an inverted domestic corporation or a subsidiary of one, and the certification concerns tax matters and is made where the proposed total price will exceed $7 million including options [@CL-CMP-5567; @CL-CMP-5568].

## Company calibration

Confirm which legal entity signs the representation and the tax certification, and which entities answer for subsidiaries. The bracketed boxes and the answers are contract-specific. [@Q-003]
