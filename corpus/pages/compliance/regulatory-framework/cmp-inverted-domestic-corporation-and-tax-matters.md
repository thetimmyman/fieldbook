## Purpose

Shows what an offeror represents about inverted domestic corporations, what the tax matters certification requires above a stated price, and where the certification carve-outs sit.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-5566] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.209-2 and 52.209-12 [@CL-CMP-5566; @CL-CMP-5568] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.209-2 [@CL-CMP-1358; @CL-CMP-1359] and RFO FAR 52.209-12 [@CL-CMP-1364; @CL-CMP-1365] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-5566] |
| Applies to | Offerors that receive the inverted domestic corporations representation provision prescribed by FAR 9.108-5(a) and the tax matters certification provision prescribed by FAR 9.104-7(e) [@CL-CMP-5651; @CL-CMP-5661] |

Government agencies are not permitted to use appropriated (or otherwise made available) funds for contracts with either an inverted domestic corporation or a subsidiary of an inverted domestic corporation, unless the exception at FAR 9.108-2(b) applies or the requirement is waived [@CL-CMP-5566]. A waiver must follow the procedures at FAR 9.108-4 [@CL-CMP-5653]. The terms inverted domestic corporation and subsidiary have the meaning given in the clause at FAR 52.209-10 [@CL-CMP-5652].

In the representation the offeror marks whether it is, or is not, an inverted domestic corporation and whether it is, or is not, a subsidiary of an inverted domestic corporation [@CL-CMP-5567].

The tax matters provision implements section 523 of Division B of the Consolidated and Further Continuing Appropriations Act, 2015, and similar provisions in later appropriations acts [@CL-CMP-5654]. Where the offeror proposes a total contract price that will exceed $7 million including options, it must certify, to the best of its knowledge and belief, three matters [@CL-CMP-5568]. The provision prints a bracketed box beside each matter [@CL-CMP-5655; @CL-CMP-5656; @CL-CMP-5657].

- It has filed all Federal tax returns required during the three years preceding the certification [@CL-CMP-5655].
- It has not been convicted of a criminal offense under the Internal Revenue Code of 1986 [@CL-CMP-5656].
- It has not, more than 90 days before the certification, been notified of any unpaid Federal tax assessment for which the liability remains unsatisfied, subject to the carve-outs below [@CL-CMP-5657; @CL-CMP-5569].

## How it works

Government agencies' use of funds for contracts with an inverted domestic corporation or a subsidiary of one is subject to the exception at FAR 9.108-2(b) or a waiver in accordance with the procedures at FAR 9.108-4 [@CL-CMP-5566; @CL-CMP-5653].

The third tax matter does not apply to an assessment that is the subject of an installment agreement or offer in compromise that the Internal Revenue Service has approved and that is not in default, or that is the subject of a non-frivolous administrative or judicial proceeding [@CL-CMP-5569].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text from February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. RFO FAR 52.209-2 says the provision is inserted as prescribed in 9.108-6(a), where the codified text says 9.108-5(a) [@CL-CMP-1358; @CL-CMP-5651]. It prints the meanings of the two terms inside the provision: an inverted domestic corporation is a foreign incorporated entity that meets the definition under a statute section the provision cites, applied in accordance with the rules and definitions of another section it cites [@CL-CMP-1359], and a subsidiary is an entity in which more than 50 percent of the entity is owned directly by a parent corporation or through another subsidiary of a parent corporation [@CL-CMP-1360]. The codified text instead takes both meanings from the clause at FAR 52.209-10 [@CL-CMP-5652]. RFO FAR 52.209-2 makes the funds prohibition subject to the exception at 9.108-3(b) [@CL-CMP-1361] and says a waiver follows the procedures at 9.108-5 [@CL-CMP-1362], where the codified text points to 9.108-2(b) and 9.108-4 [@CL-CMP-5566; @CL-CMP-5653]. It keeps the representation of whether the offeror is, or is not, an inverted domestic corporation and whether it is, or is not, a subsidiary of one [@CL-CMP-1363; @CL-CMP-5567].

RFO FAR 52.209-12 keeps the insertion of the tax matters provision as prescribed in FAR 9.104-7(e) [@CL-CMP-1364; @CL-CMP-5661] and its implementation of section 523 of Division B of the Consolidated and Further Continuing Appropriations Act, 2015, and similar provisions in subsequent appropriations acts [@CL-CMP-1365; @CL-CMP-5654]. It keeps the certification, to the best of the offeror's knowledge and belief, where the proposed total contract price will exceed $7 million including options [@CL-CMP-1366; @CL-CMP-5568]. It keeps the three matters: the filing of all Federal tax returns required during the three years preceding the certification [@CL-CMP-1367; @CL-CMP-5655], no conviction of a criminal offense under the Internal Revenue Code of 1986 [@CL-CMP-1367; @CL-CMP-5656], and no notification of an unpaid Federal tax assessment more than 90 days before the certification for which the liability remains unsatisfied [@CL-CMP-1368; @CL-CMP-5657]. It keeps the carve-outs for an assessment that is the subject of an Internal Revenue Service-approved installment agreement or offer in compromise that is not in default, or of a non-frivolous administrative or judicial proceeding [@CL-CMP-1369; @CL-CMP-5569].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An offeror proposes a total price of 9,000,000 including options, which is over the 7,000,000 threshold, so it makes the tax certification. It was notified 120 days before the certification of an unpaid assessment that remains unsatisfied, but the assessment is the subject of an approved installment agreement that is not in default, so it does not count against the third matter. The offeror also marks that it is not an inverted domestic corporation but is a subsidiary of one, and the agency may not use appropriated funds for the award unless the exception or a waiver applies.

## Never confuse

The representation covers whether the offeror is, or is not, an inverted domestic corporation or a subsidiary of one [@CL-CMP-5567]. The tax matters certification is made where the proposed total contract price will exceed $7 million including options [@CL-CMP-5568].

The codified provision takes the meanings of inverted domestic corporation and subsidiary from the clause at FAR 52.209-10 [@CL-CMP-5652], while the RFO provision prints the two definitions inside itself [@CL-CMP-1359; @CL-CMP-1360].
