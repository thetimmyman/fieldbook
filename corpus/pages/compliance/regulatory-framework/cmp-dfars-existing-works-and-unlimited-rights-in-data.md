## Purpose

Shows the license the Government receives in existing works, which items are not works, the Contractor's indemnity, and what the Government may do with drawings, designs and other works under the unlimited rights clause.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-CMP-4275] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 252.227-7021 and 252.227-7022 [@CL-CMP-4275; @CL-CMP-4277] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | MAR 1979 [@CL-CMP-10137] |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-4275] |
| Applies to | Contracts using the existing works clause where DFARS 227.7105-2(a) prescribes it [@CL-CMP-4369]; the Government Rights (Unlimited) clause as prescribed at 227.7107-1(a) [@CL-CMP-8632] |

Except as otherwise provided in the contract, the existing works clause has the Contractor grant the Government a nonexclusive, paid-up license throughout the world to distribute, perform publicly and display publicly the works called for under the contract [@CL-CMP-4275]. The license also lets the Government authorize others to distribute, perform publicly and display publicly those works for Government purposes [@CL-CMP-4276]. As used in the existing works clause, the term works includes literary, musical and dramatic works; pantomimes and choreographic works; pictorial, graphic and sculptural works; motion pictures and other audiovisual works; sound recordings; and works of a similar nature [@CL-CMP-4370]. In the existing works clause the term does not include financial reports, cost analyses and other information incidental to contract administration [@CL-CMP-4274].

The existing works clause also requires the Contractor to indemnify and hold harmless the Government, and its officers, agents and employees acting for the Government, against any liability, including costs and expenses [@CL-CMP-4371], either for violation of proprietary rights, copyrights, or rights of privacy or publicity arising out of the creation, delivery or use of any works furnished under the contract, or based upon any libelous or other unlawful matter contained in the same works [@CL-CMP-4372].

Under the Government Rights (Unlimited) clause the Government has unlimited rights in all drawings, designs, specifications, notes and other works developed in the performance of the contract, including the right to use them on any other Government design or construction without additional compensation to the Contractor [@CL-CMP-4277]. The Contractor also grants the Government a paid-up license throughout the world to all such drawings, designs, specifications, notes and other works to which the Contractor may assert or establish any claim under design patent or copyright laws [@CL-CMP-4374]. For three years after completion of the project, the Contractor agrees to furnish the original or copies of all such works on the request of the Contracting Officer [@CL-CMP-4375].

## How it works

Under the existing works clause the Government has a license and may distribute, perform and display the works, and let others do so for Government purposes [@CL-CMP-4275; @CL-CMP-4276]. The license is subject to anything the contract provides otherwise [@CL-CMP-4275]. Items such as financial reports and cost analyses are outside the term works [@CL-CMP-4274]. The Contractor shall indemnify and hold harmless the Government against liability either for violation of proprietary rights, copyrights, or rights of privacy or publicity arising out of the creation, delivery or use of any works furnished under the contract, or based upon any libelous or other unlawful matter contained in the same works [@CL-CMP-4371; @CL-CMP-4372]. The unlimited rights clause applies to works developed in performing the contract and lets the Government reuse them on other designs and construction without paying more, and it adds a paid-up license under design patent or copyright claims and a duty to furnish the works on request [@CL-CMP-4277; @CL-CMP-4374; @CL-CMP-4375].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contract calls for a training video and for a set of building drawings developed during performance. The Government may display the video and let another agency show it for Government purposes, unless the contract says otherwise. Under the unlimited rights clause it may reuse the drawings on a second building without paying the contractor extra. The contractor's cost analysis submitted for pricing is not a work under the existing works clause. If the video is later found to infringe a copyright, the contractor must indemnify the Government against the resulting liability.

## Never confuse

The existing works license is not the unlimited rights grant: under the existing works clause the Contractor grants the Government a nonexclusive, paid-up license [@CL-CMP-4275], while the Government has unlimited rights under the unlimited rights clause in all drawings, designs, specifications, notes and other works developed in the performance of the contract [@CL-CMP-4277]. Unlimited rights in technical data are covered separately; see [Technical data rights categories](cmp-dod-technical-data-rights-categories.md), and for computer software see [Noncommercial computer software rights](cmp-dod-noncommercial-software-rights.md). Drawings that become Government property under a separate clause are in [Drawings and other data as Government property](cmp-dfars-drawings-property-shop-drawings-and-restricted-designs.md). The FAR existing works clause is covered in [Existing works and technical data declaration](cmp-existing-works-and-technical-data-declaration-major-systems.md), and the general clause is in [Rights in data, general](cmp-rights-in-data-general-definitions-and-allocation.md).
