## Purpose

Explains the certification an offeror gives about restricted business operations in Sudan, and the duties on a contractor whose personnel perform private security functions outside the United States.

Sudan certification. By submission of its offer, the offeror certifies that it does not conduct any restricted business operations in Sudan [@CL-CMP-5427]. Restricted business operations are business operations in Sudan that include power production activities, mineral extraction activities, oil-related activities, or the production of military equipment, as those terms are defined in the Sudan Accountability and Divestment Act of 2007 [@CL-CMP-5428].

Restricted business operations do not include business operations that the person conducting the business can demonstrate fall within the listed categories [@CL-CMP-5429]. Those categories are:

- Operations conducted under contract directly and exclusively with the regional government of southern Sudan [@CL-CMP-5514].
- Operations conducted pursuant to specific authorization from the Office of Foreign Assets Control in the Department of the Treasury, or expressly exempted under Federal law from the requirement to be conducted under such authorization [@CL-CMP-5515].
- Operations that consist of providing goods or services to marginalized populations of Sudan, to an internationally recognized peacekeeping force or humanitarian organization, or that are used only to promote health or education [@CL-CMP-5516].
- Operations that have been voluntarily suspended [@CL-CMP-5427].

The list joins its categories with "or" [@CL-CMP-5516]. Marginalized populations of Sudan means adversely affected groups in regions authorized to receive assistance under section 8(c) of the Darfur Peace and Accountability Act, and marginalized areas in Northern Sudan described in section 4(9) of that Act [@CL-CMP-6071].

Private security functions. The clause defines private security functions as activities engaged in by a Contractor, and the first kind is the guarding of personnel, facilities, designated sites, or property of a Federal agency, the Contractor or subcontractor, or a third party [@CL-CMP-5489]. The second kind is any other activity for which personnel are required to carry weapons in the performance of their duties in accordance with the terms of the contract [@CL-CMP-5490].

Definitions. Area of combat operations means an area of operations designated as such by the Secretary of Defense when enhanced coordination of contractors performing private security functions working for Government agencies is required [@CL-CMP-6062]. Other significant military operations means activities, other than combat operations, as part of a contingency operation outside the United States that is carried out by United States Armed Forces in an uncontrolled or unpredictable high-threat environment where personnel performing security functions may be called upon to use deadly force [@CL-CMP-6068]. Full cooperation means disclosure to the Government of the information sufficient to identify the nature and extent of the incident and the individuals responsible for the conduct [@CL-CMP-6063], and it includes providing timely and complete responses to Government auditors' and investigators' requests for documents and access to employees with information [@CL-CMP-6064]. Full cooperation does not foreclose any Contractor rights arising in law, the FAR, or the terms of the contract, and it does not require the matters listed after that sentence [@CL-CMP-6065]. It does not require the Contractor to waive its attorney-client privilege or the protections of the attorney work product doctrine, or any officer, director, owner or employee of the Contractor, including a sole proprietor, to waive his or her attorney-client privilege or Fifth Amendment rights [@CL-CMP-6066]. It does not restrict the Contractor from conducting an internal investigation or from defending a proceeding or dispute arising under the contract or related to a potential or disclosed violation [@CL-CMP-6067].

Where the clause applies. If the contract is performed both in a designated area and in an area that is not designated, the clause applies only to performance in the following designated areas. One is combat operations, as designated by the Secretary of Defense [@CL-CMP-5491]. The other is other significant military operations, as designated by the Secretary of Defense and only upon agreement of the Secretary of Defense and the Secretary of State [@CL-CMP-5492].

## How it works

Compliance and orders. The Contractor must ensure that all of its employees who are responsible for performing private security functions under the contract comply with 32 CFR part 159 and with any orders, directives and instructions to Contractors performing private security functions that are identified in the contract [@CL-CMP-5430]. Those orders, directives and instructions cover registering, processing, accounting for, managing, overseeing and keeping appropriate records of personnel performing private security functions, and authorizing and accounting for weapons to be carried by or available to be used by those personnel [@CL-CMP-5493]. They also cover registering and identifying armored vehicles, helicopters and other military vehicles operated by Contractors performing private security functions, and reporting incidents in which a weapon is discharged by personnel performing private security functions [@CL-CMP-5494].

Reportable incidents. Reportable incidents also include incidents in which personnel performing private security functions are attacked, killed or injured, and incidents in which persons are killed or injured or property is destroyed as a result of conduct by Contractor personnel [@CL-CMP-5495]. They include incidents in which a weapon is discharged against personnel performing private security functions or those personnel believe a weapon was so discharged [@CL-CMP-5496]. They also include incidents in which active, non-lethal countermeasures other than the discharge of a weapon are employed by personnel performing private security functions in response to a perceived immediate threat [@CL-CMP-5497].

Briefing. The Contractor must ensure that it and all of its employees who are responsible for performing private security functions under the contract are briefed on and understand their obligation to comply with the matters listed in paragraph (c)(2) [@CL-CMP-5498]. The first matter is the qualification, training, screening (including, if applicable, thorough background checks), and security requirements established by 32 CFR part 159, Private Security Contractors Operating in Contingency Operations [@CL-CMP-6069]. The second is applicable laws and regulations of the United States and the host country and applicable treaties and international agreements regarding performance of private security functions [@CL-CMP-6070]. The third is orders, directives and instructions issued by the applicable commander of a combatant command or relevant Chief of Mission relating to weapons, equipment, force protection, security, health, safety, or relations and interaction with locals [@CL-CMP-5499]. The fourth is rules on the use of force issued by the applicable commander of a combatant command or relevant Chief of Mission for personnel performing private security functions [@CL-CMP-5500].

Cooperation. The Contractor must provide full cooperation with any Government-authorized investigation of incidents reported pursuant to paragraph (c)(1)(iv) and incidents of alleged misconduct by personnel performing private security functions under the contract [@CL-CMP-5431]. It provides that cooperation by providing access to employees performing private security functions and relevant information in the possession of the Contractor regarding the incident concerned [@CL-CMP-5501].

Remedies. The remedies in the clause are in addition to other remedies available to the Government [@CL-CMP-6322]. The Contracting Officer may direct the Contractor, at its own expense, to remove and replace any Contractor or subcontractor personnel performing private security functions who fail to comply with or violate applicable requirements of the clause or 32 CFR part 159 [@CL-CMP-5502]. The Government may take that action at its discretion without prejudice to its rights under any other provision of the contract [@CL-CMP-6323]. The Contractor's failure to comply with the requirements of the clause will be included in appropriate databases of past performance and considered in any responsibility determination or evaluation of past performance [@CL-CMP-5503]. If the contract is an award-fee contract, the Contractor's failure to comply with the requirements of the clause shall be considered in the evaluation of the Contractor's performance during the relevant evaluation period [@CL-CMP-5504]. The Contracting Officer may treat such a failure to comply as a basis for reducing or denying award fees for such period or for recovering all or part of award fees previously paid for such period [@CL-CMP-5505].

No reduction of the duty. The duty of the Contractor to comply with the requirements of the clause shall not be reduced or diminished by the failure of a higher- or lower-tier Contractor or subcontractor to comply with the clause requirements or by a failure of the contracting activity to provide required oversight [@CL-CMP-5432].

Subcontracts. The Contractor must include the substance of the clause, including its subcontract paragraph, in all subcontracts that will be performed outside the United States in the areas listed in paragraph (f) [@CL-CMP-5433]. Those areas are combat operations as designated by the Secretary of Defense, or other significant military operations upon agreement of the Secretaries of Defense and State that the clause applies in that area [@CL-CMP-5506].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text from February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. The RFO text prints FAR 52.225-20 as reserved [@CL-CMP-6299], and the Sudan certification appears in RFO FAR 52.240-90(e) instead [@CL-CMP-6300]. There the offeror certifies, by submission of its offer and after conducting a reasonable inquiry (that looks at any information in the offeror's possession but does not need to include an internal or third-party audit), that it does not conduct any restricted business operations in Sudan [@CL-CMP-6300]. The codified certification has no reasonable-inquiry wording [@CL-CMP-5427]. The RFO definition of restricted business operations keeps the same activities, as those terms are defined in the Sudan Accountability and Divestment Act of 2007 [@CL-CMP-6301; @CL-CMP-5428]. Its exclusions keep the lead-in that the person conducting the business must be able to demonstrate the exclusion [@CL-CMP-6302; @CL-CMP-5429], and keep the exclusion for operations conducted under contract directly and exclusively with the regional government of southern Sudan [@CL-CMP-6303; @CL-CMP-5514]. The RFO definition of marginalized populations of Sudan keeps its two parts [@CL-CMP-6307; @CL-CMP-6071]. ## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An offeror with 400,000 of oil-related activity in Sudan can show that the work runs under a specific authorization from the Office of Foreign Assets Control, so that work is not a restricted business operation and, if it has no other restricted business operations, the offeror can certify. A second offeror with 250,000 of power production activity in Sudan and no showing that it falls in any listed category cannot certify.

A contractor performs a contract at one site in a designated combat operations area and at another site that is not designated. The clause applies to the work at the designated site only. A guard at the designated site discharges a weapon, and the contractor reports it as an incident under the orders identified in the contract. It gives the investigators access to the guard and to the relevant records it holds. The Contracting Officer directs the contractor, at the contractor's expense, to remove and replace a guard who violated 32 CFR part 159. A subcontractor fails to brief its guards, and that failure does not reduce the prime contractor's own duty to comply.

## Never confuse

The Sudan certification is a certification about business operations, and the private security clause is a performance duty. One is answered when the offer is submitted, and the other governs compliance, reporting and cooperation during performance [@CL-CMP-5427; @CL-CMP-5430]. An operation that falls within one of the listed categories is not a restricted business operation when the person conducting the business can demonstrate it [@CL-CMP-5429]. Under the codified text the Sudan certification is made by submission of the offer [@CL-CMP-5427]; under the RFO text it is made after conducting a reasonable inquiry that looks at any information in the offeror's possession but does not need to include an internal or third-party audit [@CL-CMP-6300].

The clause on contractor personnel in a designated operational area or supporting a diplomatic or consular mission is covered in [Contractor personnel in a designated operational area or supporting a diplomatic or consular mission](cmp-overseas-designated-area-personnel.md).
