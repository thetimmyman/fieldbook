## Purpose

Shows which offerors DoD cannot award a consulting-related contract to, what the offeror certifies, and what a conflict-of-interest mitigation plan must contain.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-CMP-4091] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 252.209-7012 [@CL-CMP-4091] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-4091] |
| Applies to | Offerors for a contract assigned a code beginning with 5416, under the provision as prescribed in DFARS 209.572(e) [@CL-CMP-4093; @CL-CMP-4091] |

DoD cannot award to the Offeror a contract assigned a North American Industry Classification System code beginning with 5416 if the Offeror meets the listed conditions that follow the lead-in [@CL-CMP-4093]. The listed conditions, joined by the word and, are that the Offeror cannot certify that neither it nor any of its subsidiaries or affiliates hold a contract or subcontract that involves consulting services with one or more covered foreign entities, and that it does not maintain a conflict-of-interest mitigation plan as described in paragraph (d) of the provision [@CL-CMP-4094].

A covered foreign entity is any of a listed set of entities. The first category is the government of the People's Republic of China, the Chinese Communist Party, the People's Liberation Army, the Ministry of State Security, or another security service or intelligence agency of the People's Republic of China [@CL-CMP-4092]. The second is the government of the Russian Federation or any entity sanctioned by the Secretary of the Treasury under the executive order named in the provision [@CL-CMP-4122]. The third is the government of any country, if the Secretary of State determines that such government has repeatedly provided support for acts of international terrorism, pursuant to any of the provisions of law the provision then lists [@CL-CMP-4123]. The fourth is any entity included on any of the listed lists maintained by the Department of Commerce, which include the Entity List and the Denied Persons List [@CL-CMP-4124], and the Unverified List and the Military End User List [@CL-CMP-4125]. The fifth is any entity identified by the Secretary of Defense pursuant to the section of the National Defense Authorization Act for Fiscal Year 1999 that the provision names [@CL-CMP-4126]. The sixth is any entity on the Non-Specially Designated Nationals Chinese Military-Industrial Complex Companies List maintained by the Office of Foreign Assets Control of the Department of the Treasury [@CL-CMP-4127].

The provision defines consulting services as advisory and assistance services, except that the term does not include the provision of products or services related to compliance with legal, audit, accounting, tax, reporting, or other requirements of the laws and standards of countries, or to participation in a judicial, legal, or equitable dispute resolution proceeding [@CL-CMP-4128; @CL-CMP-4129].

In the certification, the Offeror states whether it does or does not hold a contract or subcontract that involves consulting services with one or more covered foreign entities [@CL-CMP-4095]. The Offeror also states whether its subsidiaries or affiliates do or do not hold such a contract or subcontract, and whether it does or does not maintain a conflict-of-interest mitigation plan [@CL-CMP-4130]. If the Offeror answered in the affirmative in both certification paragraphs, the Offeror shall submit its conflict-of-interest mitigation plan to the Contracting Officer for approval [@CL-CMP-4096]. The plan shall be auditable by a contract oversight entity and shall include the elements listed after the lead-in [@CL-CMP-4097]. One listed element is a description of the procedures for submitting to the contract oversight entities a notice of an unmitigated conflict of interest with respect to a covered contract within 15 days of determining that such a conflict has arisen [@CL-CMP-4098].

## How it works

The provision is a screen at award. The Offeror certifies its position, and its subsidiaries' and affiliates' position, on covered foreign entity contracts and on whether it keeps a mitigation plan [@CL-CMP-4095; @CL-CMP-4130]. The prohibition is stated for a contract in the stated code class, and it applies when both listed conditions are met [@CL-CMP-4093; @CL-CMP-4094]. An Offeror that answered in the affirmative in both certification paragraphs sends its plan to the Contracting Officer for approval [@CL-CMP-4096]. The plan must be auditable and must include listed elements, among them the procedures for notifying the oversight entities of an unmitigated conflict [@CL-CMP-4097; @CL-CMP-4098].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An offeror bids on a DoD contract assigned code 541611. Its affiliate holds a consulting subcontract with a listed foreign entity. The offeror also maintains a mitigation plan.

Answer: the offeror cannot certify that none of its affiliates holds such a contract, but it does maintain a plan, so the listed conditions are not both met. It submits the plan to the Contracting Officer for approval. Had it kept no plan, DoD could not make the award.

## Never confuse

Holding a contract that involves consulting services with a covered foreign entity does not by itself trigger the prohibition. The prohibition is stated for a contract assigned a code beginning with 5416, and it needs both listed conditions, which are joined by the word and [@CL-CMP-4093; @CL-CMP-4094]. An offeror that maintains a plan does not meet the second condition, even if it cannot make the first certification [@CL-CMP-4094]. Maintaining a plan does not end the matter, because an Offeror that answered in the affirmative in both certification paragraphs submits the plan to the Contracting Officer for approval [@CL-CMP-4096]. For the foreign-government side of the covered foreign entity list, see [the foreign government control card](cmp-foreign-government-control-and-terrorist-state-subcontracting.md).
