## Purpose

Defines covered defense telecommunications equipment or services, covered foreign countries and covered missions, states the prohibition on the contracting officer, and lists the provisions and clause that the supplement prescribes for solicitations and awards.

Covered defense telecommunications equipment or services includes telecommunications equipment produced by Huawei Technologies Company or ZTE Corporation, or any subsidiary or affiliate of such entities, and telecommunications services provided by such entities or using such equipment [@CL-CMP-7915]. It also includes telecommunications equipment or services produced or provided by an entity that the Secretary of Defense reasonably believes to be an entity owned or controlled by, or otherwise connected to, the government of a covered foreign country [@CL-CMP-7916]. The Secretary of Defense's reasonable belief is what puts such an entity in the definition. A covered foreign country is the People's Republic of China or the Russian Federation [@CL-CMP-7917].

Covered missions are the nuclear deterrence mission of DoD, including nuclear command, control and communications, integrated tactical warning and attack assessment, and continuity of Government [@CL-CMP-7918], and the homeland defense mission of DoD, including ballistic missile defense [@CL-CMP-7919].

The prohibition is addressed to the contracting officer. In addition to the prohibition at FAR 4.2102(a), and unless the covered equipment or services are subject to a waiver described in DFARS 204.2104, the contracting officer shall not procure or obtain, or extend or renew a contract (for example by exercising an option) to procure or obtain, the equipment, systems or services the section describes [@CL-CMP-7920]. Those are equipment, systems or services to carry out covered missions that use covered defense telecommunications equipment or services as a substantial or essential component of any system [@CL-CMP-7921], or as critical technology as part of any system [@CL-CMP-7922].

Three prescriptions follow. The provision at 252.204-7016, Covered Defense Telecommunications Equipment or Services - Representation, and the provision at 252.204-7017, Prohibition on the Acquisition of Covered Defense Telecommunications Equipment or Services - Representation, are each to be used in all solicitations [@CL-CMP-7923; @CL-CMP-7925]. The clause at 252.204-7018, Prohibition on the Acquisition of Covered Defense Telecommunications Equipment or Services, is to be used in all solicitations and resultant awards [@CL-CMP-7926]. Each prescription includes solicitations using FAR part 12 procedures for commercial products and commercial services, and solicitations (and for the clause, awards) for task orders and delivery orders, basic ordering agreements, orders against those agreements, blanket purchase agreements and calls against those agreements [@CL-CMP-7924; @CL-CMP-8150; @CL-CMP-8151].

## How it works

The prohibition sits on the buying decision. A new procurement and an extension or renewal such as an option exercise are both barred when the conditions are met [@CL-CMP-7920]. The conditions are that the equipment, system or service is to carry out covered missions and uses covered equipment or services as a substantial or essential component of any system, or as critical technology as part of any system [@CL-CMP-7921; @CL-CMP-7922]. Equipment or services bought for other purposes are outside this section's prohibition, although the FAR prohibition it adds to applies separately [@CL-CMP-7920; @CL-CMP-7921]. A waiver described in DFARS 204.2104 takes the covered equipment or services out of the prohibition [@CL-CMP-7920]. The conditions for such a waiver are in that section and not on this card.

The section states no duty on the contractor. The contractor-facing duties come from the representation provisions and the clause once they are in the solicitation or award; see [the DFARS prohibition and reporting card](../trade-cyber/cmp-covered-defense-telecommunications-equipment-prohibition.md) and [the FAR definitions and prohibition card](../trade-cyber/cmp-covered-telecommunications-definitions-and-prohibition.md). The prescriptions direct the drafter of the solicitation or contract; none of them states a dollar threshold, a deadline, or an exception [@CL-CMP-7923; @CL-CMP-7925; @CL-CMP-7926].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An office plans to exercise an option on a contract for a ballistic missile defense ground system, which is a covered mission [@CL-CMP-7919]. The system uses telecommunications equipment produced by an affiliate of Huawei Technologies Company as a substantial component [@CL-CMP-7915; @CL-CMP-7921]. No waiver described in DFARS 204.2104 applies, so the contracting officer shall not exercise the option [@CL-CMP-7920]. If a waiver did cover that equipment, the prohibition in this section would not apply [@CL-CMP-7920]. An office buying the same kind of equipment for an administrative network that does not carry out a covered mission is outside this section's prohibition, though the FAR prohibition is separate [@CL-CMP-7921]. A solicitation for a delivery order under a blanket purchase agreement still carries the representation provisions and the clause, because the prescriptions reach orders and agreements [@CL-CMP-7924; @CL-CMP-8150; @CL-CMP-8151].

## Never confuse

The definition is not limited to equipment produced by Huawei Technologies Company or ZTE Corporation [@CL-CMP-7915]. It also includes equipment or services from an entity the Secretary of Defense reasonably believes to be owned or controlled by, or connected to, the government of a covered foreign country [@CL-CMP-7916]. The prohibition in this section is also not a duty stated on the contractor; it is addressed to the contracting officer [@CL-CMP-7920].

## Company calibration

Where the executed contracts and modifications sit, and who confirms whether any delivered system for a covered mission uses covered equipment or services. [@Q-004]
