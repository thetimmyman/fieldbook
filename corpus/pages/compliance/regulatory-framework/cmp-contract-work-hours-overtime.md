## Purpose

Shows the overtime rate and threshold for laborers and mechanics, the liability, liquidated damages and withholding that follow a shortfall, and when overtime premiums are authorized.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-5852] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.222-4 and FAR 52.222-2 [@CL-CMP-5852; @CL-CMP-5858] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.222-4 [@CL-CMP-1025]; RFO FAR 52.222-2 [@CL-CMP-1036] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-5852] |
| Applies to | Contracts that carry the overtime compensation clause as prescribed at FAR 22.305, and the payment for overtime premiums clause as prescribed at FAR 22.103-5(b) [@CL-CMP-5852; @CL-CMP-5858] |

A contractor or subcontractor employing laborers or mechanics may not require or permit them to work over 40 hours in a workweek unless they are paid at least one and one-half times the basic rate of pay for each hour worked over 40 hours [@CL-CMP-5853]. Payrolls and basic payroll records are kept during the contract and made available to the Government until 3 years after contract completion [@CL-CMP-5856]. The records contain the name and address of each employee, social security number, labor classifications, hourly rates of wages paid, daily and weekly number of hours worked, deductions made and actual wages paid [@CL-CMP-6456]. They need not duplicate the records that Department of Labor regulations at 29 CFR 5.5(a)(3) require for construction work [@CL-CMP-6457]. The contractor and its subcontractors allow authorized representatives of the Contracting Officer or the Department of Labor to inspect, copy or transcribe those records [@CL-CMP-6458] and to interview employees in the workplace during working hours [@CL-CMP-6459].

## How it works

If the contractor or subcontractor violates the overtime requirement, it is liable for the unpaid wages and, in addition, for liquidated damages payable to the Government [@CL-CMP-5865]. The Contracting Officer assesses the damages at the rate specified in 29 CFR 5.5(b)(2) per affected employee for each calendar day on which the employer required or permitted the employee to work in excess of the standard workweek of 40 hours without paying the required overtime wages [@CL-CMP-5854]. The clause points to that regulation for the rate and states no dollar figure. The Department of Labor adjusts the civil monetary penalty for inflation no later than January 15 each year [@CL-CMP-6186].

The Contracting Officer withholds from payments due under the contract sufficient funds to satisfy any contractor or subcontractor liabilities for unpaid wages and liquidated damages [@CL-CMP-5855]. If amounts withheld under the contract are insufficient, the Contracting Officer will withhold payments from other Federal or Federally assisted contracts held by the same contractor [@CL-CMP-6454], limited to those that are subject to the Contract Work Hours and Safety Standards statute [@CL-CMP-6455]. The overtime provisions flow into subcontracts that may require or involve the employment of laborers and mechanics, and subcontractors include them in any such lower tier subcontracts [@CL-CMP-5857]. The contractor is responsible for compliance by any subcontractor or lower-tier subcontractor with those provisions [@CL-CMP-6187].

Under the payment for overtime premiums clause, overtime is authorized if the overtime premium cost does not exceed the amount inserted in the clause, which is a contract-specific fill-in that the clause text leaves blank, or if the premium is paid for work of a listed kind [@CL-CMP-5859]. The blank is completed by inserting either zero or the dollar amount agreed to during negotiations [@CL-CMP-6465]. One listed kind is work necessary to cope with emergencies such as those resulting from accidents, natural disasters, breakdowns of production equipment, or occasional production bottlenecks of a sporadic nature [@CL-CMP-6188]. Another is work by indirect-labor employees such as those performing duties in connection with administration, protection, transportation, maintenance, standby plant protection, operation of utilities, or accounting [@CL-CMP-6189]. A third is work to perform tests, industrial processes, laboratory procedures, loading or unloading of transportation conveyances, and operations in flight or afloat that are continuous in nature and cannot reasonably be interrupted or completed otherwise [@CL-CMP-6190]. The last is work that will result in lower overall costs to the Government [@CL-CMP-6191].

A request for estimated overtime premiums that exceeds the amount specified in the clause must include all estimated overtime for contract completion and the items the clause lists [@CL-CMP-6460]. One item is to identify the work unit, such as the department or section in which the requested overtime will be used, with present workload, staffing and other data of the affected unit sufficient to permit the Contracting Officer to evaluate the necessity for the overtime [@CL-CMP-6461]. Another is to demonstrate the effect that denial of the request will have on the contract delivery or performance schedule [@CL-CMP-6462]. Another is to identify the extent to which approval of overtime would affect the performance or payments in connection with other Government contracts, with identification of each affected contract [@CL-CMP-6463]. The last is to provide reasons why the required work cannot be performed by using multishift operations or by employing additional personnel [@CL-CMP-6464].

During contract performance, such a request goes to the office administering the contract, submitted as paragraph (b) of the clause states [@CL-CMP-6469]. That office reviews it and, if it approves, sends it to the contracting officer [@CL-CMP-6470]. If the contracting officer determines that the overtime should be approved in whole or in part, the contracting officer requests the approval of the agency's designated approving official and modifies the amount in paragraph (a) of the clause to reflect any approval [@CL-CMP-6471].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text under Class Deviation 2026-O0038, effective February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. RFO FAR 52.222-4(a) says that no contractor or subcontractor employing laborers or mechanics must require or permit them to work over 40 hours in any workweek unless they are paid at least one and one-half times the basic rate of pay for each hour worked over 40 hours [@CL-CMP-1025; @CL-CMP-5853]. Paragraph (b) keeps the liability for unpaid wages and, in addition, for liquidated damages payable to the Government [@CL-CMP-1026; @CL-CMP-5865], the Contracting Officer's assessment of liquidated damages at the rate specified in 29 CFR 5.5(b)(2) per affected employee for each calendar day on which the employer required or permitted the employee to work in excess of the standard workweek of 40 hours without paying overtime wages required by the Contract Work Hours and Safety Standards statute [@CL-CMP-1027; @CL-CMP-9099; @CL-CMP-5854], and the Department of Labor's inflation adjustment of the penalty no later than January 15 each year [@CL-CMP-1028; @CL-CMP-6186]. Paragraph (c) keeps the withholding of sufficient funds from payments due under the contract [@CL-CMP-1029; @CL-CMP-5855] and, if those amounts are insufficient, the withholding from other Federal or federally assisted contracts held by the same contractor that are subject to the Contract Work Hours and Safety Standards statute [@CL-CMP-1030; @CL-CMP-1031; @CL-CMP-6454; @CL-CMP-6455]. Paragraph (d)(1) keeps the duty to maintain payrolls and basic payroll records for all laborers and mechanics working on the contract during the contract and to make them available to the Government until 3 years after contract completion [@CL-CMP-1032; @CL-CMP-5856], with the same record contents [@CL-CMP-1033; @CL-CMP-6456]. The records need not duplicate those required for construction work by Department of Labor regulations at 29 CFR 5.5(a)(3) implementing the Construction Wage Rate Requirements statute [@CL-CMP-9100; @CL-CMP-6457]. Paragraph (d)(2) keeps the duty of the contractor and its subcontractors to allow authorized representatives of the Contracting Officer or the Department of Labor to inspect, copy, or transcribe those records [@CL-CMP-9101; @CL-CMP-6458] and to interview employees in the workplace during working hours [@CL-CMP-9102; @CL-CMP-6459]. Paragraph (e) keeps the duty to insert the provisions in subcontracts that may require or involve the employment of laborers and mechanics and to require subcontractors to include them in any such lower tier subcontracts [@CL-CMP-1034; @CL-CMP-5857], and the contractor's responsibility for compliance by any subcontractor or lower-tier subcontractor with paragraphs (a) through (d) [@CL-CMP-9103; @CL-CMP-6187]. RFO FAR 52.222-2(a) keeps the authorization of overtime where the overtime premium cost does not exceed the amount in the blank or the overtime premium is paid for work of the kinds listed [@CL-CMP-1036; @CL-CMP-5859]: emergency work [@CL-CMP-1037; @CL-CMP-6188], work by indirect-labor employees [@CL-CMP-1038; @CL-CMP-6189], continuous tests and processes [@CL-CMP-1039; @CL-CMP-6190] and work that will result in lower overall costs to the Government [@CL-CMP-1040; @CL-CMP-6191]. Its footnote keeps the instruction to insert either zero or the dollar amount agreed to during negotiations [@CL-CMP-1041; @CL-CMP-6465]. Paragraph (b) keeps the rule that a request for estimated overtime premiums that exceeds the amount specified must include all estimated overtime for contract completion and the items listed [@CL-CMP-9104; @CL-CMP-6460]: identifying the work unit, with present workload, staffing and other data sufficient to permit the Contracting Officer to evaluate the necessity for the overtime [@CL-CMP-9105; @CL-CMP-6461], demonstrating the effect that denial of the request will have on the contract delivery or performance schedule [@CL-CMP-9106; @CL-CMP-6462], identifying the extent to which approval of overtime would affect the performance or payments in connection with other Government contracts, with identification of each affected contract [@CL-CMP-9107; @CL-CMP-6463], and providing reasons why the required work cannot be performed by using multishift operations or by employing additional personnel [@CL-CMP-9108; @CL-CMP-6464]. The RFO prescriptions cite FAR 22.301-2 for the overtime compensation clause [@CL-CMP-1024] and FAR 22.102-2(b) for the overtime premiums clause [@CL-CMP-1035], where the codified prescriptions cite FAR 22.305 and FAR 22.103-5(b) [@CL-CMP-5852; @CL-CMP-5858].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A laborer works 46 hours in a week at a basic rate of 20 per hour. The hours over 40 must be paid at least one and one-half times that rate, which is 30 per hour, so the 6 overtime hours require at least 180. If the employer pays all 46 hours at 20 per hour, the overtime hours are paid 120 and the unpaid wages are 60. If the employer instead pays the 6 hours at 30 per hour, there is no violation.

In the case where all 46 hours are paid at 20 per hour, suppose the 6 overtime hours fall on 2 calendar days and the rate per affected employee per calendar day in the regulation is 25 for this example. The real rate is the one in 29 CFR 5.5(b)(2), adjusted each year. Liquidated damages are 1 employee times 2 days times 25, which is 50, payable to the Government, in addition to the 60 in unpaid wages. The Contracting Officer withholds enough from payments due to cover both, so at least 110.

Separately, a contract inserts 1,000 as the overtime premium amount. A premium cost of 800 is authorized. A premium cost of 1,500 is authorized if the premium is paid for a listed kind of work, such as emergency work after an accident. Otherwise the contractor submits a request with the listed items to the office administering the contract, and the 1,500 is authorized only after the designated official approves it and the contracting officer raises the amount in the clause to at least 1,500.

## Never confuse

Liquidated damages are not the unpaid wages. The contractor is liable for both, the damages are payable to the Government, and the officer withholds enough to cover both [@CL-CMP-5865; @CL-CMP-5855]. Authorization of overtime under the premiums clause is also separate from the duty to pay overtime wages, because the premiums clause states when the use of overtime is authorized and what a request above the stated amount must contain [@CL-CMP-5859; @CL-CMP-6460], while the overtime compensation clause states what must be paid [@CL-CMP-5853]. A Government refusal to authorize overtime does not release the contractor from paying the required overtime rates for overtime actually worked [@CL-CMP-6195].

The prescriptions named in the Applies to row, FAR 22.305 and FAR 22.103-5(b), are the codified ones [@CL-CMP-5852; @CL-CMP-5858], and the RFO text prescribes the two clauses at FAR 22.301-2 and FAR 22.102-2(b) [@CL-CMP-1024; @CL-CMP-1035].
