## Purpose

Shows how the construction specifications and drawings clause treats gaps, conflicts, defined words and shop drawings, and who carries the risk at each step.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-4904] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.236-21 [@CL-CMP-4904] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.236-21 [@CL-CMP-6040] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-4904] |
| Applies to | Solicitations and contracts where the prescription directs insertion of the clause [@CL-CMP-4904] |

The clause is titled Specifications and Drawings for Construction and is inserted as prescribed in FAR 36.521 [@CL-CMP-4904].

The contractor keeps a copy of the drawings and specifications on the work site and gives the Contracting Officer access to it at all times [@CL-CMP-5094]. Unless the contract provides otherwise, the Contracting Officer furnishes from time to time the detailed drawings and other information considered necessary [@CL-CMP-5095].

Material mentioned in the specifications but not shown on the drawings, or shown on the drawings but not mentioned in the specifications, has the same effect as if it appeared in both [@CL-CMP-4905]. Where the drawings and the specifications differ, the specifications govern [@CL-CMP-4906].

Words such as directed, required, ordered, designated and prescribed mean the direction, requirement, order, designation or prescription of the Contracting Officer [@CL-CMP-5096]. Words such as approved, acceptable and satisfactory mean approved by, acceptable to or satisfactory to the Contracting Officer, unless otherwise expressly stated [@CL-CMP-5097]. A reference to what is shown, indicated or detailed points to the drawings accompanying the contract unless stated otherwise [@CL-CMP-5098]. The word provided means provide complete in place, that is furnished and installed [@CL-CMP-5099].

## How it works

A discrepancy in the figures, the drawings or the specifications goes promptly to the Contracting Officer, who promptly makes a determination in writing [@CL-CMP-4907]. An adjustment the contractor makes without that determination is at its own risk and expense [@CL-CMP-4908].

Shop drawings are drawings submitted to the Government by the contractor, a subcontractor or any lower tier subcontractor under a construction contract [@CL-CMP-5100]. They show in detail the proposed fabrication and assembly of structural elements and the installation of materials or equipment, meaning form, fit and attachment details [@CL-CMP-5101]. The term also covers diagrams, layouts, schematics, descriptive literature, illustrations, schedules, performance and test data, and similar materials furnished by the contractor to explain specific portions of the work in detail [@CL-CMP-5102]. The Government may duplicate, use and disclose delivered shop drawings in any manner and for any purpose [@CL-CMP-5103].

Where the contract requires shop drawings, the contractor coordinates them, reviews them for accuracy, completeness and compliance with contract requirements, and indicates its approval on them as evidence of that review [@CL-CMP-4909]. Shop drawings submitted without evidence of the contractor's approval may be returned for resubmission [@CL-CMP-5104]. The contractor submits four copies of all shop drawings called for in the specifications for approval, unless otherwise indicated [@CL-CMP-5107]. The Contracting Officer retains three sets, unless otherwise indicated, and returns one set to the contractor [@CL-CMP-5108].

The Contracting Officer indicates approval or disapproval of the shop drawings and, if they are not approved as submitted, gives the Government's reasons [@CL-CMP-5105]. Work done before that approval is at the contractor's risk [@CL-CMP-4910]. Approval does not relieve the contractor of responsibility for errors or omissions in the drawings, or of responsibility for complying with the contract, except for variations described and approved under the clause [@CL-CMP-4911].

Where shop drawings show variations from the contract requirements, the contractor describes them in writing, separate from the drawings, at the time of submission [@CL-CMP-4912]. If the Contracting Officer approves a variation, the Contracting Officer issues an appropriate contract modification, but no modification is needed if the variation is minor or does not involve a change in price or in time of performance [@CL-CMP-5106].

Two alternates add sentences to paragraph (g) of the codified clause on record shop drawings. Alternate I applies when record shop drawings are required and reproducible shop drawings are needed [@CL-CMP-5109]. On completing the work, the contractor then furnishes a complete set of all shop drawings as finally approved, showing all changes and revisions made up to the time the equipment is completed and accepted [@CL-CMP-5110]. Alternate II applies when record shop drawings are required and reproducible shop drawings are not needed [@CL-CMP-5111]. On completing the work, the contractor then furnishes the number of sets of prints that the Contracting Officer inserts, of all shop drawings as finally approved [@CL-CMP-5112]. Those drawings show changes and revisions made up to the time the equipment is completed and accepted [@CL-CMP-5113]. The number of sets is a bracketed fill-in that is specific to each contract [@CL-CMP-5112].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text from February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. The RFO clause is inserted as prescribed in 36.101-7(k), where the codified clause names FAR 36.521 [@CL-CMP-6040; @CL-CMP-4904]. It says the contractor keeps a copy of the drawings and specifications at the site, where the codified clause says on the work site [@CL-CMP-6041; @CL-CMP-5094]. It keeps the rules that material in only one of the two has the same effect as if in both and that the specifications govern a difference [@CL-CMP-6042; @CL-CMP-4905; @CL-CMP-4906], now in paragraph (b) and (b)(1), and the written determination of a discrepancy and the contractor's risk for an unapproved adjustment [@CL-CMP-6043; @CL-CMP-4907] [@CL-CMP-6044; @CL-CMP-4908], now in paragraphs (b)(2) and (b)(3). It says the Contracting Officer will furnish from time to time the detailed drawings and other information considered necessary, unless otherwise provided, where the codified clause says shall furnish [@CL-CMP-6045; @CL-CMP-5095], and will issue an appropriate contract modification for an approved variation, in paragraph (i)(2), except that a modification need not be issued if the variation is minor or does not involve a change in price or in time of performance, where the codified clause says shall issue in paragraph (f) [@CL-CMP-6196; @CL-CMP-5106]. Alternates I and II add their sentences to paragraph (j) of the basic clause [@CL-CMP-6197; @CL-CMP-6199], and they keep the record shop drawing sentences [@CL-CMP-6198; @CL-CMP-5110] [@CL-CMP-6200; @CL-CMP-5112] [@CL-CMP-6201; @CL-CMP-5113].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

The specifications call for a 12-inch base plate and the drawings show a 10-inch plate. That is a difference between drawings and specifications, so the specifications govern and the contractor uses the 12-inch plate [@CL-CMP-4906]. If instead two drawing sheets showed different plate sizes, that discrepancy would go promptly to the Contracting Officer for a written determination, and resizing the plate without it would be at the contractor's own risk and expense [@CL-CMP-4907] [@CL-CMP-4908].

The contractor then prepares a shop drawing for the plate and, after its own review, marks its approval on the sheet. The drawing shows a 14-inch plate, so the contractor sends a separate written note describing that variation with the submission. It submits four copies, and the Contracting Officer keeps three and returns one. If the Contracting Officer approves the variation, a modification follows, unless the variation is minor or changes neither price nor time. If the note had been left out, the approval would not cover the 14-inch plate, and the contractor would stay responsible for complying with the contract. Fabrication started before approval would also be at the contractor's risk.

## Never confuse

A shop drawing approval is not an acceptance of design responsibility, because the contractor stays responsible for errors and omissions in the drawings except for variations described and approved under the clause [@CL-CMP-4911]. A variation is not a discrepancy report either, because the variation is described in writing, separate from the drawings, at the time of submission [@CL-CMP-4912]. The contractor's approval marked on a shop drawing is evidence of its own coordination and review [@CL-CMP-4909], and it is separate from the approval of the Contracting Officer [@CL-CMP-5105].

The codified Alternates I and II add sentences to paragraph (g) [@CL-CMP-5109; @CL-CMP-5111], while the RFO Alternates add them to paragraph (j) [@CL-CMP-6197; @CL-CMP-6199].
