## Purpose

Shows where labor standards disputes are routed, what the eligibility certification states, and when a wage rate above the determination needs approval before it can be recovered.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-5845] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.222-14, FAR 52.222-15 and FAR 52.222-16 [@CL-CMP-5845; @CL-CMP-5847; @CL-CMP-5851] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-5845] |
| Applies to | Contracts that carry the disputes clause as prescribed at FAR 22.407(a), and the approval of wage rates clause as prescribed at FAR 22.407(b) [@CL-CMP-5845; @CL-CMP-6194] |

Labor standards disputes go to the Department of Labor procedures in 29 CFR parts 5, 6 and 7 and not to the Disputes clause of the contract [@CL-CMP-5846]. Disputes within the meaning of the clause include disputes between the contractor or any of its subcontractors and the contracting agency, the Department of Labor, or the employees or their representatives [@CL-CMP-6192].

By entering the contract the contractor certifies that neither it nor any person or firm with an interest in its firm is ineligible to be awarded Government contracts under section 3144(b)(2) of title 40 of the United States Code or 29 CFR 5.12(a)(1) [@CL-CMP-5847]. The penalty for making false statements is prescribed in the United States Criminal Code at section 1001 of title 18 [@CL-CMP-6193].

## How it works

The same bar governs subcontracting, because no part of the contract may be subcontracted to a person or firm ineligible for award on those grounds [@CL-CMP-5848].

All straight time wage rates, and the overtime rates based on them, for laborers and mechanics engaged in work under the contract must be submitted for written approval by the head of the contracting activity or a representative expressly designated for that purpose [@CL-CMP-5849]. The approval requirement applies where the straight time wages exceed the rates for corresponding classifications in the applicable minimum wage determination included in the contract [@CL-CMP-5850]. Any amount paid to a laborer or mechanic above the agency approved wage rate is at the contractor's expense and is not reimbursed by the Government [@CL-CMP-5851]. If the Government refuses to authorize the use of the overtime, the contractor is not released from the obligation to pay employees at the required overtime rates for any overtime actually worked [@CL-CMP-6195].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A mechanic is paid 48 per hour against a determination rate of 42. Because the straight time wage exceeds the determination rate, the contractor submits the rate, and the overtime rates based on it, to the head of the contracting activity or a designated representative for written approval. The agency approves 45. The 3 per hour above the approved rate stays at the contractor's expense, so over 100 hours the contractor bears 300 that is never reimbursed. A second mechanic paid exactly the determination rate of 42 does not trigger the approval requirement, because that straight time wage does not exceed the determination.

Later the Government refuses to authorize overtime for a week, but the first mechanic works 5 overtime hours anyway. The contractor still owes the required overtime rates for those 5 hours.

## Never confuse

A labor standards dispute is not an ordinary contract dispute. The clause routes it to the Department of Labor procedures and states that the Disputes clause does not govern it [@CL-CMP-5846]. Approval of a rate is also not approval of the cost, because the excess over the approved rate is never reimbursed [@CL-CMP-5851]. A refusal to authorize overtime is not a release from paying overtime actually worked [@CL-CMP-6195].
