## Purpose

Explains how the contracting officer's indications turn the selection lists in paragraphs (b) and (c) of the clause on contract terms and conditions required to implement statutes or Executive orders into contractor duties, and where individual entries carry their own limits.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-9045] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.212-5(b) and (c) [@CL-CMP-9045; @CL-CMP-9046] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.212-5 is [Reserved] [@CL-CMP-0643] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-9045] |
| Applies to | Contracts in which the clause is inserted as prescribed in FAR 12.301(b)(4) [@CL-CMP-9201] |

Paragraph (b) is a list for commercial products and commercial services [@CL-CMP-9045]. The Contractor shall comply with the FAR clauses in paragraph (b) that the Contracting Officer has indicated as being incorporated in the contract by reference to implement provisions of law or Executive orders applicable to acquisitions of commercial products and commercial services [@CL-CMP-9045]. The list carries the instruction that the Contracting Officer check as appropriate [@CL-CMP-9205].

Paragraph (c) is a second list, for commercial services. The Contractor shall comply with the FAR clauses in paragraph (c), applicable to commercial services, that the Contracting Officer has indicated as being incorporated in the contract by reference [@CL-CMP-9046]. It carries the same instruction to check as appropriate [@CL-CMP-9206].

Entries can carry their own limits, and these appear in the entry text itself. The entry for 52.203-15 in paragraph (b)(3) says it applies to contracts funded by the American Recovery and Reinvestment Act of 2009 [@CL-CMP-9047]. The entry for 52.203-17 in paragraph (b)(4) says the clause does not apply to contracts of DoD, NASA, the Coast Guard, or applicable elements of the intelligence community, and it refers to FAR 3.900(a) [@CL-CMP-9048]. The entry for 52.219-4 in paragraph (b)(16) says that an offeror that elects to waive the HUBZone price evaluation preference shall so indicate in its offer [@CL-CMP-9049]. The entry for 52.222-54 in paragraph (b)(40) says it is not applicable to the acquisition of commercially available off-the-shelf items or certain other types of commercial products or commercial services as prescribed in FAR 22.1803 [@CL-CMP-9050]. The entry for 52.223-9 in paragraph (b)(41) says it is not applicable to the acquisition of commercially available off-the-shelf items [@CL-CMP-9207].

A listed clause can appear with its alternate as a separate sub-entry. Paragraph (b)(18) lists 52.219-6 at (i) and Alternate I of 52.219-6 at (ii) [@CL-CMP-9208].

## How it works

The Contracting Officer decides which entries apply to the contract and records that decision by indicating them. The Contractor's duty under paragraph (b) or (c) reaches the entries so indicated [@CL-CMP-9045; @CL-CMP-9046; @CL-CMP-9205; @CL-CMP-9206]. Paragraph (c) is selected separately from paragraph (b), so a commercial services contract can carry entries from both lists and a commercial products contract only from paragraph (b) [@CL-CMP-9045; @CL-CMP-9046].

An entry's own limit is written into that entry, so a reader checks both the indication and the limit. The funding source, the agency and the type of item are the limits that appear in the entries above [@CL-CMP-9047; @CL-CMP-9048; @CL-CMP-9050; @CL-CMP-9207]. The entry text for paragraph (b)(40) points to FAR 22.1803 for the excluded types [@CL-CMP-9050]. The HUBZone entry works differently, because the offeror's own election decides whether the preference is waived, and the offeror makes it in its offer [@CL-CMP-9049].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text from February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. RFO FAR 52.212-5 is [Reserved], so the codified paragraph (b) and (c) selection lists have no text under that section number in the RFO text [@CL-CMP-0643; @CL-CMP-9045; @CL-CMP-9046].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A commercial products contract is awarded by a civilian agency that is not DoD, NASA, the Coast Guard or an intelligence community element. The Contracting Officer checks the entry for 52.203-17 in paragraph (b)(4), so the Contractor shall comply with it [@CL-CMP-9045; @CL-CMP-9205]. The agency is not one of the excluded agencies in the entry, so that exclusion does not apply [@CL-CMP-9048]. The acquisition is for commercially available off-the-shelf items, so the entry for 52.222-54 in paragraph (b)(40) states that it is not applicable to the acquisition [@CL-CMP-9050]. The offeror does not elect to waive the HUBZone preference, so it has nothing to indicate in its offer on that point [@CL-CMP-9049].

A separate commercial services contract carries the Contracting Officer's checks in paragraph (c) as well, and the Contractor shall comply with the paragraph (c) entries indicated [@CL-CMP-9046; @CL-CMP-9206].

## Never confuse

An indicated entry is not the same as an automatic clause, because the Contractor's duty under paragraph (b) or (c) reaches the entries the Contracting Officer has indicated [@CL-CMP-9045; @CL-CMP-9046]. The by-reference clauses in paragraph (a) need no indication and are covered on the linked card. An entry's own limit is not the Contracting Officer's selection decision, since the limit sits in the entry text and applies alongside the indication [@CL-CMP-9047; @CL-CMP-9050]. The paragraph (c) list is not something that follows automatically from paragraph (b), because each list is selected separately [@CL-CMP-9045; @CL-CMP-9046]. The paragraph (b) and (c) lists are not RFO FAR 52.212-5 text, because the RFO text lists that section as [Reserved] [@CL-CMP-9045; @CL-CMP-9046; @CL-CMP-0643].

## Company calibration

The company must confirm who reads the Contracting Officer's indications on each award and who maps them to contract duties [@Q-004].
