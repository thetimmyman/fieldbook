## Purpose

Shows what the Government receives and pays under a DoD patent license and release contract, how later license terms can readjust payments, and the duties to report and pay royalties.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-CMP-3958] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 252.227-7012, 252.227-7002 and 252.227-7009 [@CL-CMP-3958; @CL-CMP-3959; @CL-CMP-3961] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-3958] |
| Applies to | Patent releases, license agreements and assignments [@CL-CMP-3958] |

The patent license and release contract clause is inserted in patent releases, license agreements and assignments [@CL-CMP-3958]. Payment under its payment article is in full compensation for the rights granted and agreed to be granted, and for a license on a running royalty basis the article directs insertion of the clause at 252.227-7006 and also the clauses at 252.227-7002, 252.227-7009 and 252.227-7010 [@CL-CMP-3957].

The readjustment clause applies if any license, under substantially the same patents and authorizing substantially the same acts as the contract, has been or shall hereafter be granted within the United States on royalty terms more favorable to the licensee than those in the contract [@CL-CMP-3959]. The Government shall be entitled to the benefit of those more favorable terms for royalties accruing after the date the more favorable terms become effective [@CL-CMP-3960]. The clause also requires the Contractor to promptly notify the Contracting Officer in writing of the granting of such more favorable terms [@CL-CMP-3986].

Under the reporting clause, a report in writing is to be delivered to the Contractor, subject to military security regulations, on or before the sixtieth day following the end of each yearly period during which royalties have accrued [@CL-CMP-3961]. Royalties that have accrued during the yearly period shall be paid to the Contractor, if appropriations are or become available, within sixty days following receipt of a voucher from the Contractor [@CL-CMP-3962], a voucher submitted in accordance with the report referred to in paragraph (a) of the clause [@CL-CMP-3988]. The clause also has a proviso on a maximum yearly obligation: if combined royalties exceed the said maximum yearly obligation, each department or agency shall pay a pro rata share of the maximum as determined by the proportion its accrued royalties bear to the combined total [@CL-CMP-3963].

## How it works

The license and release contract is the form, and for a license on a running royalty basis it directs insertion of further clauses, including the readjustment and reporting clauses covered here [@CL-CMP-3957]. The readjustment clause lets the Government take more favorable royalty terms granted elsewhere in the United States under substantially the same patents and for substantially the same acts [@CL-CMP-3959; @CL-CMP-3960]. The reporting clause then sets the yearly report, the payment on a voucher and the pro rata sharing when the maximum yearly obligation is exceeded [@CL-CMP-3961; @CL-CMP-3962; @CL-CMP-3963]. The printed clauses leave blanks for the reporting office, the period and the maximum amount, so each executed contract must be read for its entries. This card does not set out the license grant, term, release, non-estoppel or administrative articles, or the paragraph of the readjustment clause on court decisions about invalid patent claims.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A license pays royalties yearly. Royalties accrue during a year ending on a set date. The designated office sends the contractor a written report within 60 days of that date. The contractor sends a voucher and is paid within 60 days of receiving it, if funds are available. Later, the contractor grants another license for the same patents in the United States at a lower rate, and the Government receives the lower rate for royalties accruing after that date.

## Never confuse

These clauses cover royalties the Government pays a contractor under a license. They are not the royalty disclosure an offeror makes in an offer, which is covered on [Royalty information in offers](cmp-royalty-information-provision.md), and they do not decide whether a royalty cost is allowable, which is covered on [Royalties and other costs for use of patents](../cas-allowability/cmp-patent-royalties.md). The refund of royalties is also separate and is covered on [Patent indemnity and refund of royalties](cmp-patent-indemnity-and-refund-of-royalties.md).
