## Purpose

Shows what an offeror submits about professional employee pay and how the government evaluates it in a service contract competition.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-4823] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.222-46 [@CL-CMP-4823] |
| RFO citation | No RFO equivalent: the RFO FAR part 52 text lists section 52.222-46 as reserved [@CL-CMP-2118] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-4823] |
| Applies to | Offerors that submit a total compensation plan for professional employees with their proposals [@CL-CMP-4823] |

Recompetition of service contracts may in some cases lower the compensation paid to professional employees, and the provision says this can be detrimental to obtaining the quality of professional services needed for adequate contract performance [@CL-CMP-4968]. It states that it is in the government's best interest that professional employees, as defined in 29 CFR 541, be properly and fairly compensated [@CL-CMP-4969].

As part of their proposals, offerors submit a total compensation plan setting forth the salaries and fringe benefits proposed for the professional employees who will work under the contract [@CL-CMP-4823]. The government evaluates the plan to assure that it reflects a sound management approach and understanding of the contract requirements, and the evaluation includes an assessment of the offeror's ability to provide uninterrupted high-quality work [@CL-CMP-4824]. The proposed compensation is considered in terms of its impact on recruiting and retention, its realism, and its consistency with a total plan for compensation [@CL-CMP-4825]. Supporting information includes data such as recognized national and regional compensation surveys and studies of professional, public and private organizations, used in establishing the total compensation structure [@CL-CMP-4826].

## How it works

The compensation levels proposed should reflect a clear understanding of the work to be performed and should indicate the capability of the proposed compensation structure to obtain and keep suitably qualified personnel to meet mission objectives [@CL-CMP-4970]. Salary rates or ranges must take into account differences in skills, the complexity of various disciplines and professional job difficulty [@CL-CMP-4827].

A proposal that envisions compensation lower than that of predecessor contractors for the same work is evaluated on the basis of maintaining program continuity, uninterrupted high-quality work and the availability of required competent professional service employees [@CL-CMP-4828]. Offerors are cautioned that lowered compensation for essentially the same professional work may indicate a lack of sound management judgment and a lack of understanding of the requirement [@CL-CMP-4829]. The government is concerned with the quality and stability of the work force to be employed on the contract [@CL-CMP-4971]. Professional compensation that is unrealistically low or not in reasonable relationship to the various job categories may be viewed as evidence of failure to comprehend the complexity of the contract requirements, since it may impair the contractor's ability to attract and retain competent professional service employees [@CL-CMP-4830]. Failure to comply with these provisions may constitute sufficient cause to justify rejection of a proposal [@CL-CMP-4831].

Class Deviation 2026-O0038 directs DoD contracting officers to use the revised FAR part 52 published on the Revolutionary FAR Overhaul web page (the RFO text) in place of the codified part 52 text, effective February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. The RFO text lists section 52.222-46 as reserved [@CL-CMP-2118].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An offeror proposes 90,000 a year for a senior analyst, while the predecessor contractor paid 110,000 for the same work. The proposal includes data from two regional compensation surveys. The evaluator weighs the lower figure on program continuity, uninterrupted high-quality work and the availability of competent professional employees, and reads it against the survey data. If the offeror cannot show that the lower pay still recruits and retains qualified analysts, the evaluator may take the lowered compensation as a sign of weak management judgment. Failure to comply with the provision may be enough to reject the proposal. This example follows the codified provision, not the RFO text, which lists section 52.222-46 as reserved [@CL-CMP-2118].

## Never confuse

This provision looks at the salaries and fringe benefits proposed for professional employees and at whether that pay can obtain and keep suitably qualified personnel [@CL-CMP-4823; @CL-CMP-4970]. The wage determination rules for service contracts are on [service contract labor standards](../../operating-model/talent/opm-service-contract-labor-standards.md).

The codified section prints a provision on the evaluation of compensation for professional employees [@CL-CMP-4823], while the RFO text lists the section number as reserved [@CL-CMP-2118].
