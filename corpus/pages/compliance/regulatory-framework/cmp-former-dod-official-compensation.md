## Purpose

Shows what a contractor must check before it pays a former DoD official, and what can happen if it does not.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-CMP-4064] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 252.203-7000 [@CL-CMP-4064] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-4064] |
| Applies to | Contracts that include the clause, used as prescribed in DFARS 203.171-4(a) [@CL-CMP-4064] |

The Contractor shall not knowingly provide compensation to a covered DoD official within 2 years after the official leaves DoD service, without first determining that the official has sought and received, or has not received after 30 days of seeking, a written opinion from the appropriate DoD ethics counselor [@CL-CMP-4067].

A covered DoD official is an individual who leaves or left DoD service on or after January 28, 2008 and who also meets a further test in the definition [@CL-CMP-4065]. The first alternative test applies to an individual who participated personally and substantially in an acquisition with a value in excess of $10 million and who serves or served in an Executive Schedule position, among other listed positions [@CL-CMP-4114]. Two more positions listed under that test are a position in the Senior Executive Service and a general or flag officer position compensated at a rate of pay for grade O-7 or above [@CL-CMP-4115]. The second alternative test lists service in DoD as program manager, deputy program manager, procuring contracting officer, or administrative contracting officer, among other listed positions [@CL-CMP-4066]. That list ends with source selection authority, member of the source selection evaluation board, or chief of a financial or technical evaluation team for a contract in an amount in excess of $10 million [@CL-CMP-4116].

Failure to comply with the prohibition may subject the Contractor to rescission of the contract, suspension, or debarment [@CL-CMP-4068].

## How it works

The clause turns on two facts: whether the person is a covered DoD official [@CL-CMP-4065], and whether the compensation would fall within 2 years after the person left DoD service [@CL-CMP-4067]. If both are true, the Contractor must first determine that the official has sought and received the written ethics counselor opinion, or has not received it after 30 days of seeking it [@CL-CMP-4067]. The clause names rescission of the contract, suspension and debarment as possible results of failing to comply [@CL-CMP-4068]. The second element of the definition has two alternative tests. One turns on personal and substantial participation in an acquisition above a stated value together with a listed senior position [@CL-CMP-4114; @CL-CMP-4115], and the other turns on a listed acquisition position [@CL-CMP-4066; @CL-CMP-4116].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A former program manager left DoD service 18 months ago, after 2008, and the program was for a contract worth 40 million. The Contractor wants to pay the person as a consultant. The person asked the DoD ethics counselor for a written opinion 45 days ago and has had no answer.

Answer: the person is within 2 years of leaving, so the Contractor must make the determination first. No opinion arrived after more than 30 days of seeking one, so the Contractor can record that finding and then pay.

## Never confuse

This rule is about compensation to a person who recently left DoD service. It is not [the prohibition on persons convicted of fraud or other felonies](cmp-felony-convicted-persons-prohibition.md), which bars listed roles on a contract [@CL-CMP-4070]. It also does not bar the compensation outright, because the Contractor may pay once it has made the required determination [@CL-CMP-4067].
