## Purpose

Defines the systems and information the basic safeguarding clause reaches, and states when the contracting officer inserts the clause into a solicitation or contract [@CL-CMP-7575; @CL-CMP-7579].

| Codified citation | FAR 4.1901 and 4.1903 [@CL-CMP-7575; @CL-CMP-7579] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |

| Applies to | Solicitations and contracts where the contractor or a subcontractor at any tier may have Federal contract information residing in or transiting through its information system [@CL-CMP-7579] |

A covered contractor information system is an information system that is owned or operated by a contractor and that processes, stores or transmits Federal contract information [@CL-CMP-7575].

Federal contract information is information, not intended for public release, that is provided by or generated for the Government under a contract to develop or deliver a product or service to the Government, but not including information provided by the Government to the public [@CL-CMP-7576]. Information provided by the Government to the public, such as that on public Web sites, and simple transactional information, such as that necessary to process payments, are not Federal contract information [@CL-CMP-7577].

Information means any communication or representation of knowledge, such as facts, data or opinions, in any medium or form, including textual, numerical, graphic, cartographic, narrative, or audiovisual [@CL-CMP-7720]. An information system is a discrete set of information resources organized for the collection, processing, maintenance, use, sharing, dissemination or disposition of information [@CL-CMP-7721]. Safeguarding means measures or controls that are prescribed to protect information systems [@CL-CMP-7578].

The contracting officer must insert the clause at FAR 52.204-21, Basic Safeguarding of Covered Contractor Information Systems, in solicitations and contracts when the contractor or a subcontractor at any tier may have Federal contract information residing in or transiting through its information system [@CL-CMP-7579]. The insertion duty falls on the contracting officer. The contractor's safeguarding duties come from the clause, which is on the linked controls card, and not from this prescription.

## How it works

The trigger is factual. It turns on whether Federal contract information may reside in or pass through an information system of the contractor or of a subcontractor at any tier, and the prescription states no dollar threshold [@CL-CMP-7579].

The definitions fix the scope. The system must be owned or operated by a contractor, and the information must be provided by or generated for the Government under a contract to develop or deliver a product or service and not intended for public release [@CL-CMP-7575; @CL-CMP-7576]. The exclusions take out information the Government gives to the public and simple transactional information, so a system that holds only payment-processing information does not hold Federal contract information [@CL-CMP-7577]. Safeguarding itself is a set of prescribed measures and controls for information systems [@CL-CMP-7578].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor runs a billing portal that holds only records needed to process payments, and a separate engineering server that stores technical drawings generated for the Government under a contract to deliver a product and not intended for public release. The payment records are simple transactional information, so the billing portal does not process Federal contract information on that basis [@CL-CMP-7577]. The engineering server stores information generated for the Government under a contract to deliver a product and not intended for public release, so it is a covered contractor information system [@CL-CMP-7575; @CL-CMP-7576]. Because the contractor may have Federal contract information in that system, the contracting officer inserts the basic safeguarding clause [@CL-CMP-7579].

## Never confuse

Federal contract information is not any Government information, because information given to the public and simple transactional information are carved out [@CL-CMP-7576; @CL-CMP-7577]. A covered contractor information system is also not identified by who owns the data. It is identified by who owns or operates the system and by what the system handles [@CL-CMP-7575]. This prescription is also not the clause itself, whose controls and flowdown are on [Basic safeguarding of covered contractor information systems: controls and flowdown](cmp-basic-safeguarding-clause-controls.md).
