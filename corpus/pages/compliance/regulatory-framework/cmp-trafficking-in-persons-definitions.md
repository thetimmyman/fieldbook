## Purpose

Gives the printed definitions of severe forms of trafficking in persons, sex trafficking, agent and employee, which the trafficking rules use.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-3284] |
| Codified citation | FAR 22.1702 [@CL-CMP-3284] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-3284] |

Severe forms of trafficking in persons has two alternative parts [@CL-CMP-3284; @CL-CMP-3285]. One alternative is sex trafficking in which a commercial sex act is induced by force, fraud, or coercion, or in which the person induced to perform the act has not attained 18 years of age [@CL-CMP-3284]. The other alternative is the recruitment, harboring, transportation, provision, or obtaining of a person for labor or services, through the use of force, fraud, or coercion for the purpose of subjection to involuntary servitude, peonage, debt bondage, or slavery [@CL-CMP-3285]. Sex trafficking is the recruitment, harboring, transportation, provision, or obtaining of a person for the purpose of a commercial sex act [@CL-CMP-3367].

An agent is any individual, including a director, an officer, an employee, or an independent contractor, authorized to act on behalf of the organization [@CL-CMP-3286]. An employee is an employee of the Contractor directly engaged in the performance of work under the contract who has other than a minimal impact or involvement in contract performance [@CL-CMP-3287].

## How it works

The definitions are for use in the trafficking rules, which are on the trafficking card and are not restated here. The agent definition names who can act for the organization, including an independent contractor [@CL-CMP-3286]. The employee definition limits the term to those directly engaged in contract work with more than a minimal impact [@CL-CMP-3287]. The same section defines further terms, among them coercion, commercial sex act, debt bondage, forced labor, involuntary servitude, recruitment fees, subcontract, subcontractor and United States, and this card does not draft them.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor uses a staffing firm to place 30 workers. A person at the firm is authorized to act for the contractor in hiring. The answer: that person can be an agent under the definition, even if not an employee of the contractor. A back-office clerk who spends a few hours a month on the contract must be tested against the minimal impact language before being treated as an employee.

## Never confuse

An agent is not limited to employees: the definition lists directors, officers, employees and independent contractors [@CL-CMP-3286], while the defined term employee means an employee of the Contractor directly engaged in the performance of work under the contract who has other than a minimal impact or involvement in contract performance [@CL-CMP-3287]. The definitions are not the prohibition or the certification rules, which are on [Combating trafficking in persons](cmp-combating-trafficking-in-persons.md).
