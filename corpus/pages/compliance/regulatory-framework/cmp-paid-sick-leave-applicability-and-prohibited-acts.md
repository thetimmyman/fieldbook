## Purpose

Explains which contracts and employees the paid sick leave subpart covers, and which acts the section bars a contractor from taking.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-8180; @CL-CMP-8194] |
| Codified citation | FAR 22.2103(a) and (b), FAR 22.2106(a), (b) and (c), and FAR 22.2107 [@CL-CMP-8180; @CL-CMP-8194; @CL-CMP-8183; @CL-CMP-8185; @CL-CMP-8186; @CL-CMP-8189; @CL-CMP-9545] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-8180; @CL-CMP-8194] |
| Applies to | Contracts covered by the Service Contract Labor Standards statute or the Wage Rate Requirements (Construction) statute that require performance in whole or in part within the United States, and the employees described in paragraph (b) [@CL-CMP-8195; @CL-CMP-8180; @CL-CMP-8194; @CL-CMP-8181; @CL-CMP-8183] |

Section 22.2103(a) covers contracts that meet two conditions [@CL-CMP-8195]. The contract is covered by the Service Contract Labor Standards statute (title 41 of the United States Code, chapter 67) or the Wage Rate Requirements (Construction) statute (title 40, chapter 31, subchapter IV) [@CL-CMP-8180; @CL-CMP-8194]. The contract also requires performance in whole or in part within the United States [@CL-CMP-8181].

When performance is in part within and in part outside the United States, the subpart applies to the part of the contract that is performed within the United States [@CL-CMP-8182].

Section 22.2103(b) covers employees performing on or in connection with such contracts whose wages are governed by the Service Contract Labor Standards statute, the Wage Rate Requirements (Construction) statute, or the Fair Labor Standards Act. It includes employees who qualify for an exemption from the Fair Labor Standards Act's minimum wage and overtime provisions [@CL-CMP-8195; @CL-CMP-8183; @CL-CMP-8184].

Section 22.2106 begins that, in accordance with 29 CFR 13.6 and by operation of the clause at 52.222-62, a contractor may not do the following [@CL-CMP-8185].

Paragraph (a): interfere with an employee's accrual or use of paid sick leave as required by Executive Order 13706 or 29 CFR part 13 [@CL-CMP-8185].

Paragraph (b): discharge or in any other manner discriminate against any employee for using, or attempting to use, paid sick leave as provided for under Executive Order 13706 and 29 CFR part 13 [@CL-CMP-8186]; for filing any complaint, initiating any proceeding, or otherwise asserting any right or claim under them [@CL-CMP-8187]; for cooperating in any investigation or testifying in any proceeding under them [@CL-CMP-8188]; or for informing any other person about his or her rights under them [@CL-CMP-8188].

Paragraph (c): fail to make and maintain, or to make available to authorized representatives of the Wage and Hour Division, records for inspection, copying, and transcription as required by 29 CFR 13.25, or otherwise fail to comply with the requirements of 29 CFR 13.25 [@CL-CMP-8189].

Section 22.2107 is a separate rule on waiver of rights: employees cannot waive, nor may contractors induce employees to waive, their rights under Executive Order 13706 or 29 CFR part 13 (see 29 CFR 13.7) [@CL-CMP-9545].

## How it works

Coverage is tested twice. The contract must be covered by one of the two statutes and must require performance at least in part within the United States, and the employee's wages must be governed by one of the three statutes named in paragraph (b) [@CL-CMP-8180; @CL-CMP-8194; @CL-CMP-8181; @CL-CMP-8183].

Both contract conditions must be met. A contract covered by one of the statutes but not requiring performance within the United States is outside paragraph (a) [@CL-CMP-8180; @CL-CMP-8194; @CL-CMP-8181].

For a contract performed partly abroad, the subpart applies to the part performed within the United States [@CL-CMP-8182].

The employee test keeps people who qualify for an exemption from the minimum wage and overtime provisions of the Fair Labor Standards Act inside paragraph (b) [@CL-CMP-8184]. Any other limits on coverage are on [Executive order minimum wage coverage and paid sick leave exclusions](cmp-eo-wage-and-sick-leave-coverage-and-exclusions.md). This card does not restate them.

Every prohibition in section 22.2106 falls on the contractor [@CL-CMP-8185]. The section does not state a decision for a contracting officer or an agency [@CL-CMP-8185].

Paragraph (a) protects accrual and use of the leave. Paragraph (b) protects the employee from discharge or other discrimination for using or attempting to use the leave, for filing a complaint, starting a proceeding or asserting a right or claim, for cooperating in an investigation or testifying, and for telling others about the employee's rights [@CL-CMP-8185; @CL-CMP-8186; @CL-CMP-8187; @CL-CMP-8188].

Paragraph (c) concerns records. The contractor must not fail to make and maintain them, or to make them available to authorized representatives of the Wage and Hour Division, as 29 CFR 13.25 requires, or otherwise fail to comply with that regulation [@CL-CMP-8189]. What the records must contain is in 29 CFR 13.25 and is Not established here [@CL-CMP-8189].

The section prints no deadline, dollar threshold or penalty. The clause and the other rules are on [Paid sick leave under Executive Order 13706](cmp-paid-sick-leave-eo-13706.md) and [Minimum wage and paid sick leave policy on covered contracts](cmp-minimum-wage-and-paid-sick-leave-policy.md) [@CL-CMP-8185].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A services contract is covered by the Service Contract Labor Standards statute and is performed in three states and in one foreign province. Both coverage conditions in paragraph (a) are met, because the contract is covered by that statute and requires performance in part within the United States. The subpart applies to the part performed within the United States [@CL-CMP-8180; @CL-CMP-8194; @CL-CMP-8181; @CL-CMP-8182].

An employee on the part performed in the United States has wages governed by that statute. The employee takes 3 days of paid sick leave as provided for under Executive Order 13706 and 29 CFR part 13, and the contractor docks a bonus for it. Docking the bonus is discrimination against the employee for using that leave, which paragraph (b) bars [@CL-CMP-8183; @CL-CMP-8186]. The example assumes that no other section of the subpart excludes the employee.

Separately, the contractor fails to make its records available to authorized representatives of the Wage and Hour Division for inspection as 29 CFR 13.25 requires. That failure is a prohibited act under paragraph (c) [@CL-CMP-8189].

## Never confuse

Never confuse a partial place of performance with full coverage. A contract performed partly outside the United States is not outside the subpart, because the subpart applies to the part performed within the United States [@CL-CMP-8182].

Never confuse the exemption from the minimum wage and overtime provisions of the Fair Labor Standards Act with an exemption from the subpart. The section includes employees who qualify for the first [@CL-CMP-8184].

Never confuse the coverage section with the prohibited acts. Section 22.2103 says what the subpart covers [@CL-CMP-8195; @CL-CMP-8180; @CL-CMP-8194]. Section 22.2106 says what a contractor may not do [@CL-CMP-8185].
