## Purpose

Shows the violations and remedies for forced or indentured child labor and which solicitations and contracts the certification provision and the cooperation clause go into.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-3277] |
| Codified citation | FAR 22.1504 and FAR 22.1505 [@CL-CMP-3277; @CL-CMP-3282; @CL-CMP-3283] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-3277] |
| Applies to | Solicitations and contracts expected to exceed the micro-purchase threshold, for the listed end products or supplies [@CL-CMP-3282; @CL-CMP-3283] |

The Government may impose the remedies set forth in the remedies paragraph for the listed violations [@CL-CMP-3277]. One listed violation is that the contractor uses forced or indentured child labor in its mining, production, or manufacturing processes [@CL-CMP-3278]. Another is that the contractor has furnished an end product or component mined, produced, or manufactured, wholly or in part, by forced or indentured child labor [@CL-CMP-3366]. Two listed remedies are that the contracting officer may terminate the contract and that the suspending and debarring official may suspend the contractor [@CL-CMP-3280]. As a listed remedy, the suspending and debarring official may debar the contractor for a period not to exceed 3 years [@CL-CMP-3281]. The second and third listed remedies, suspension and debarment, are inappropriate unless the contractor knew of the violation [@CL-CMP-3279; @CL-CMP-3280; @CL-CMP-3281].

One condition for inserting the provision Certification Regarding Knowledge of Child Labor for Listed End Products is that the solicitation is expected to exceed the micro-purchase threshold and is for the acquisition of end products [@CL-CMP-3282]. The prescription for the provision covers end products of a type identified by country of origin on the List of Products Requiring Contractor Certification as to Forced or Indentured Child Labor, except solicitations for commercial products or commercial services that include the provision at 52.212-3 [@CL-CMP-3364]. For solicitations estimated to equal or exceed $50,000, the contracting officer must exclude from the List in the solicitation end products from any countries identified in the thresholds section, in accordance with the specified thresholds [@CL-CMP-3365]. The provision paragraph also requires the contracting officer to identify applicable end products and countries of origin from the List and refers to an exception in another section, and this card does not draft them. The contracting officer must insert the clause Child Labor-Cooperation with Authorities and Remedies in all solicitations and contracts for the acquisition of supplies that are expected to exceed the micro-purchase threshold [@CL-CMP-3283].

## How it works

A violation can lead to termination by the contracting officer, or to suspension or debarment by the suspending and debarring official [@CL-CMP-3280; @CL-CMP-3281]. Suspension and debarment, as the second and third listed remedies, are inappropriate unless the contractor knew of the violation [@CL-CMP-3279]. The provision and the clause are both tied to the micro-purchase threshold [@CL-CMP-3282; @CL-CMP-3283], and its figure is on [Simplified acquisition and micro-purchase thresholds](cmp-acquisition-thresholds.md). The section also lists violations for a false certification and for failure to cooperate with an investigation, and this card does not draft them.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor on a 500,000 supply contract is found to use forced child labor in its production process, and it knew of the violation. The answer: the contracting officer may terminate the contract, and the suspending and debarring official may suspend the contractor or debar it for up to 3 years. If the contractor had not known, suspension and debarment would be inappropriate, and the printed text does not make termination depend on that knowledge.

## Never confuse

The certification provision is not the cooperation clause: the provision depends on end products and the clause on supplies, and each has its own prescription [@CL-CMP-3282; @CL-CMP-3283]. The knowledge condition applies to the second and third listed remedies, which are suspension and debarment [@CL-CMP-3279; @CL-CMP-3280; @CL-CMP-3281], and the printed text does not state it for termination. The list of products, the certification and the waivers are on [Forced or indentured child labor list and waivers](cmp-forced-child-labor-list-and-disability-waivers.md) and are not restated here.
