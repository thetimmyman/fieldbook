## Purpose

Sets out the tax and entity information an offeror supplies, how an offeror without a unique entity identifier obtains one, and the contractor's duty to keep that identifier maintained and to report a change.

## The rule

| Codified citation | FAR 52.204-3; FAR 52.204-6; FAR 52.204-12 [@CL-CMP-5978; @CL-CMP-5981; @CL-CMP-5984] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.204-90 [@CL-CMP-6318; @CL-CMP-6326; @CL-CMP-6328]; RFO FAR 52.204-91 [@CL-CMP-6331; @CL-CMP-6332]; the RFO text prints FAR 52.204-3, 52.204-6 and 52.204-12 as reserved [@CL-CMP-6315; @CL-CMP-6316; @CL-CMP-6317] |

A taxpayer identification number is the number the Internal Revenue Service requires an offeror to use in reporting income tax and other returns, and it may be either a Social Security Number or an Employer Identification Number [@CL-CMP-5978]. A common parent is the corporate entity that owns or controls an affiliated group of corporations that files its Federal income tax returns on a consolidated basis, and of which the offeror is a member [@CL-CMP-6670]. A unique entity identifier is a number or other identifier used to identify one specific commercial, nonprofit, or Government entity [@CL-CMP-5981].

## How it works

### Taxpayer identification

All offerors must submit the information required in paragraphs (d) through (f) of the provision to comply with debt collection requirements, reporting requirements and the implementing regulations issued by the Internal Revenue Service [@CL-CMP-5979]. Where the resulting contract is subject to the payment reporting requirements, a failure or refusal by the offeror to furnish the information may result in a 31 percent reduction of payments otherwise due under the contract [@CL-CMP-5980].

The Government may use the taxpayer identification number to collect and report on any delinquent amounts arising out of the offeror's relationship with the Government [@CL-CMP-6671]. Where the resulting contract is subject to the payment reporting requirements, the number provided may be matched with Internal Revenue Service records to verify its accuracy [@CL-CMP-6672].

Paragraph (d) gives the offeror three options: to state its number, to say that the number has been applied for, or to say that the number is not required [@CL-CMP-6673]. The provision lists three reasons that a number is not required [@CL-CMP-6674; @CL-CMP-6675; @CL-CMP-6676]. The offeror is a nonresident alien, foreign corporation, or foreign partnership that has no income effectively connected with a trade or business in the United States and no office or place of business or fiscal paying agent in the United States [@CL-CMP-6674]. The offeror is an agency or instrumentality of a foreign government [@CL-CMP-6675]. The offeror is an agency or instrumentality of the Federal Government [@CL-CMP-6676].

Paragraph (e) lists these types of organization: sole proprietorship, partnership, corporate entity (not tax-exempt), corporate entity (tax-exempt), government entity (Federal, State, or local), foreign government, international organization per 26 CFR 1.6049-4, and other [@CL-CMP-6677]. Paragraph (f) gives the offeror two options: to state that it is not owned or controlled by a common parent as defined in paragraph (a), or to give the name and taxpayer identification number of its common parent [@CL-CMP-6678].

### Unique entity identifier in the offer

The offeror must enter, in the block with its name and address on the cover page of its offer, the annotation "Unique Entity Identifier" followed by the unique entity identifier that identifies its name and address exactly as stated in the offer [@CL-CMP-5982]. The offeror must also enter its Electronic Funds Transfer indicator, if applicable [@CL-CMP-6681]. That indicator is a four-character suffix to the unique entity identifier [@CL-CMP-6679]. The suffix is assigned at the discretion of the entity to establish additional System for Award Management records for identifying alternative Electronic Funds Transfer accounts for the same entity [@CL-CMP-6680].

### Obtaining an identifier

An offeror that does not have a unique entity identifier should contact the entity designated for establishing identifiers directly to obtain one [@CL-CMP-5983]. It should be prepared to provide its company legal business name, its tradestyle, doing business, or other name by which the entity is commonly recognized, and its company physical street address, city, state and zip code [@CL-CMP-6682]. The same list also includes the company mailing address if separate from the physical address, the company telephone number, the date the company was started, and the number of employees at the location [@CL-CMP-6683]. It also includes the chief executive officer or key manager, the line of business, and the company headquarters name and address with its reporting relationship within the entity [@CL-CMP-6684].

### Maintenance during the contract

The maintenance clause defines a unique entity identifier as a number or other identifier used to identify a specific commercial, nonprofit, or Government entity [@CL-CMP-6685]. The contractor must ensure that the identifier is maintained with the entity designated at the System for Award Management for establishment of identifiers, throughout the life of the contract [@CL-CMP-5984]. The contractor must communicate any change to the identifier to the contracting officer within 30 days after the change, so that an appropriate modification can be issued to update the data on the contract [@CL-CMP-5985]. A change in the identifier does not necessarily require a novation [@CL-CMP-5986].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text from February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. The RFO text prints FAR 52.204-3, 52.204-6 and 52.204-12 as reserved [@CL-CMP-6315; @CL-CMP-6316; @CL-CMP-6317], and the taxpayer and unique entity identifier paragraphs appear instead in RFO FAR 52.204-90 [@CL-CMP-6318] and RFO FAR 52.204-91 [@CL-CMP-6330]. Under RFO FAR 52.204-90, if the offeror will not have an active Federal Government contracts registration in the System for Award Management when submitting its offer, it shall complete paragraphs (c) and (d) of the provision and include its responses with its offer [@CL-CMP-6318]; the codified provision says all offerors must submit the taxpayer information [@CL-CMP-5979]. Paragraph (c) says the offeror shall provide with its offer the information necessary to comply with the debt collection requirements, the reporting requirements and the implementing IRS regulations, and it prints no sentence reducing payments for a failure or refusal to furnish it, where the codified provision has the 31 percent sentence [@CL-CMP-6328; @CL-CMP-5980]. Paragraph (c)(4) says the number may be matched with IRS records and used to collect and report delinquent amounts, and states no condition about payment reporting requirements, where the codified text conditions the matching on the contract being subject to them [@CL-CMP-6329; @CL-CMP-6671; @CL-CMP-6672]. The taxpayer information is in paragraph (c) of the RFO provision rather than in paragraphs (d) through (f) [@CL-CMP-6328; @CL-CMP-5979]. Paragraph (c)(1) gives the offeror the same three options: to state its number, to say that the number has been applied for, or to say that the number is not required [@CL-CMP-9861; @CL-CMP-6673], with the same three listed reasons [@CL-CMP-9862; @CL-CMP-6674] [@CL-CMP-9863; @CL-CMP-6675; @CL-CMP-6676]. Paragraph (c)(2) lists the same types of organization [@CL-CMP-9864; @CL-CMP-6677], and paragraph (c)(3) gives the same two options about a common parent [@CL-CMP-9865; @CL-CMP-6678]. The RFO definitions of the taxpayer identification number and the common parent keep the codified meanings [@CL-CMP-6319; @CL-CMP-5978] [@CL-CMP-6320; @CL-CMP-6670]. The rule on the unique entity identifier annotation is kept: the offeror shall enter, in the block with its name and address on the cover page of its offer, the annotation "Unique Entity Identifier" followed by the identifier that identifies its name and address exactly as stated in the offer, and shall also enter its EFT indicator, if applicable [@CL-CMP-6326; @CL-CMP-5982; @CL-CMP-6681]. An offeror without a unique entity identifier shall go to the System for Award Management web site to obtain one, and the Government will independently validate the existence and uniqueness of the offeror before assigning it, with no list of company information to be provided [@CL-CMP-6327; @CL-CMP-5983; @CL-CMP-6682]. The RFO definition of the EFT indicator is a bank account identifier to establish additional System for Award Management records for identifying alternative EFT accounts for the same entity [@CL-CMP-6325; @CL-CMP-6680]. In RFO FAR 52.204-91, the Contractor shall ensure that its identifier is maintained throughout the life of the contract, without the codified words naming the entity designated at the System for Award Management [@CL-CMP-6331; @CL-CMP-5984]. The RFO definition says "an identifier" where the codified definition says "a number or other identifier" [@CL-CMP-6330; @CL-CMP-6685]. The Contractor shall communicate any change to its identifier or its CAGE code to the Contracting Officer within 30 days after the change, so a modification can be issued to update the identifier or CAGE code on the contract, and a change in the identifier does not necessarily require a novation [@CL-CMP-6332; @CL-CMP-5985; @CL-CMP-5986].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An offeror that is a corporate entity, not tax-exempt, has an Employer Identification Number and states it in paragraph (d) (paragraph (c)(1) under the RFO text [@CL-CMP-9861]). It marks that it is not owned or controlled by a common parent. It has no unique entity identifier, so it contacts the designated entity [@CL-CMP-5983] (under the RFO text it goes to the System for Award Management web site instead [@CL-CMP-6327]) and prepares its legal business name, physical address, mailing address, telephone number and the other listed items. It then writes the annotation "Unique Entity Identifier" and the assigned identifier in the name and address block on the offer cover, and the identifier matches the name and address stated in the offer exactly [@CL-CMP-5982].

The resulting contract is subject to the payment reporting requirements. Had the offeror refused to furnish the tax information, a payment of 200,000 otherwise due could have been reduced by 31 percent, which is 62,000 [@CL-CMP-5980]. Under the RFO text, the taxpayer paragraph prints no such reduction [@CL-CMP-6328].

During performance the identifier changes on day 0. The contractor must tell the contracting officer by day 30 so that a modification can update the data on the contract [@CL-CMP-5985]. A change in the identifier alone does not necessarily require a novation [@CL-CMP-5986].

## Never confuse

The taxpayer identification number and the unique entity identifier are different things. The first is the number the Internal Revenue Service requires for reporting returns [@CL-CMP-5978], and the second identifies a specific entity [@CL-CMP-5981].

The clauses on SAM registration and its maintenance are separate clauses; they are covered on [SAM registration and maintenance clauses](cmp-sam-registration-and-maintenance-clauses.md), including their own notice rules, and this card does not restate them. Under the codified text all offerors must submit the taxpayer information and, where the resulting contract is subject to the payment reporting requirements, a failure or refusal to furnish it may result in a 31 percent reduction of payments otherwise due [@CL-CMP-5979; @CL-CMP-5980]; under the RFO text, the duty to complete the taxpayer paragraph is stated for an offeror that will not have an active registration when submitting its offer, and that paragraph prints no payment reduction [@CL-CMP-6318; @CL-CMP-6328]. A changed identifier does not necessarily require a novation [@CL-CMP-5986]; novation agreements are covered on [Novation and change-of-name agreements](../../mna/diligence/mna-novation-agreements.md).

The Electronic Funds Transfer indicator is a suffix assigned to the same entity's identifier to establish additional records for alternative accounts [@CL-CMP-6679; @CL-CMP-6680]. It does not identify a separate entity. Under the codified text the indicator is a four-character suffix to the identifier [@CL-CMP-6679], and the RFO definition calls it a bank account identifier and does not describe it as a suffix [@CL-CMP-6325].
