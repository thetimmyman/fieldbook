## Purpose

Shows what the essential-services clause makes a contractor do in a crisis, how the mission-essential services plan fits in, and how the extra or lower costs of continued performance are noticed and adjusted.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-CMP-4389] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 252.237-7023 [@CL-CMP-4389] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-4389] |
| Applies to | Contracts that include the clause, which is used as prescribed in DFARS 237.7603(a), where the Government has identified contractor services as essential contractor services [@CL-CMP-4389; @CL-CMP-4314] |

The Government has identified all or a portion of the contractor services performed under the contract as essential contractor services in support of mission-essential functions, and it lists them in an attachment to the contract [@CL-CMP-4314]. The contractor's Mission-Essential Contractor Services Plan is incorporated in the contract, and the contractor shall maintain and update the plan as necessary and give all updates to the contracting officer for approval [@CL-CMP-4315].

Notwithstanding any other clause, the contractor is responsible for performing the services identified as essential during crisis situations, as directed by the contracting officer and in accordance with its plan [@CL-CMP-4316]. If the contractor anticipates not being able to perform any of those services during a crisis situation, it shall notify the contracting officer or other designated representative as expeditiously as possible and use its best efforts to cooperate with the Government in the Government's efforts to maintain the continuity of operations [@CL-CMP-4317; @CL-CMP-8053]. In such crisis situations the Government reserves the right to use Federal employees, military personnel, or contract support from other contractors, or to enter into new contracts for essential contractor services [@CL-CMP-4404]. The clause is used as prescribed in DFARS 237.7603(a) [@CL-CMP-4389].

## How it works

The contractor performs the essential services in crisis situations as the contracting officer directs, under its plan [@CL-CMP-4316]. It shall segregate and separately identify all costs incurred in continuing performance of essential services in a crisis situation [@CL-CMP-4318]. It shall notify the contracting officer of an increase or decrease in costs within ninety days after continued performance has been directed, or within any additional period the contracting officer approves in writing [@CL-CMP-4319], but not later than the date of final payment under the contract [@CL-CMP-4320]. The notice includes the contractor's proposal for an equitable adjustment and any supporting data in the form the contracting officer prescribes, and the parties shall negotiate an equitable price adjustment to the contract price, delivery schedule, or both as soon as is practicable after receipt of the proposal [@CL-CMP-4321; @CL-CMP-4322]. The contractor shall include the substance of the clause, including its flowdown paragraph, in subcontracts for the essential services [@CL-CMP-4405].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contract lists a base-support service as essential [@CL-CMP-4314]. The contracting officer directs continued performance during a crisis [@CL-CMP-4316]. The contractor charges the extra overtime to a separate cost line [@CL-CMP-4318] and finds it comes to 40,000 above what the price assumed. It sends a notice with an equitable adjustment proposal and its supporting data 60 days after the direction [@CL-CMP-4321]. The answer: the notice is inside the ninety day period [@CL-CMP-4319], and the parties shall negotiate an equitable price adjustment to the contract price, delivery schedule, or both as soon as is practicable after receipt of the proposal [@CL-CMP-4322].

## Never confuse

Contractor personnel supporting deployed forces are covered in [Contractor personnel supporting deployed forces](cmp-deployed-contractor-personnel-definitions-and-predeployment.md). The cost notice is due within ninety days after continued performance has been directed by the contracting officer, or within any additional period that the contracting officer approves in writing [@CL-CMP-4319].

The solicitation provision, covered in [Notice of continuation of essential services and services at installations being closed](cmp-essential-services-notice-and-closing-installations.md), says the offeror shall provide with its offer a written plan describing how it will continue to perform the essential contractor services during periods of crisis [@CL-CMP-4323]. The clause says the Mission-Essential Contractor Services Plan submitted by the Contractor is incorporated in the contract [@CL-CMP-4315].
