## Purpose

Shows how validation of asserted software restrictions works, how it differs from the technical data validation clause, the justification the contractor must give, and the Contracting Officer's decision.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-CMP-3802] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 252.227-7019 [@CL-CMP-3802] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-3802] |
| Applies to | Restrictions asserted on the Government's rights in computer software delivered, required to be delivered, or otherwise provided to the Government under the contract [@CL-CMP-3802; @CL-CMP-8511] |

The Contractor shall maintain records sufficient to justify the validity of any asserted restrictions on the Government's rights to use, modify, reproduce, perform, display, release, or disclose computer software delivered, required to be delivered, or otherwise provided to the Government under the contract, and shall be prepared to furnish to the Contracting Officer a written justification for such asserted restrictions in response to a request for information under paragraph (d) of the clause or a challenge under paragraph (f) of the clause [@CL-CMP-3802; @CL-CMP-8511; @CL-CMP-0626]. A challenge must be in writing, must state the specific grounds for challenging the asserted restriction, and shall require the Contractor to respond in writing within 60 days [@CL-CMP-3803]. A challenge shall also require the Contractor to provide justification for the assertion, based upon records kept in accordance with paragraph (b) of the clause and such other documentation that are reasonably available to the Contractor, in sufficient detail to enable the Contracting Officer to determine the validity of the asserted restrictions [@CL-CMP-3803; @CL-CMP-3804; @CL-CMP-0336]. The Contractor's failure to provide a timely response to a Contracting Officer's request for information or failure to provide sufficient information to enable the Contracting Officer to evaluate an asserted restriction shall constitute reasonable grounds for questioning the validity of an asserted restriction [@CL-CMP-0337; @CL-CMP-3808].

If the Contracting Officer, after reviewing any available information pertaining to the validity of an asserted restriction, determines that the asserted restriction has not been justified, the Contracting Officer shall issue promptly a final decision, in accordance with the Disputes clause of the contract, denying the validity of the asserted restriction [@CL-CMP-3805; @CL-CMP-0338]. If the Contracting Officer determines that the asserted restriction has been justified, the Contracting Officer shall issue promptly a final decision, in accordance with the Disputes clause of the contract, validating the asserted restriction [@CL-CMP-3805; @CL-CMP-3806]. Notwithstanding a Contracting Officer's final decision denying the validity of an asserted restriction and except as provided in paragraph (g)(3) of the clause, the Government agrees to honor the asserted restriction for one listed period of 90 days from the date of the Contracting Officer's final decision to allow the Contractor to appeal to the appropriate Board of Contract Appeals or to file suit in an appropriate court [@CL-CMP-3807; @CL-CMP-8513].

## How it works

The Contractor keeps records sufficient to justify the validity of any asserted restrictions on the Government's rights in computer software delivered, required to be delivered, or otherwise provided to the Government under the contract, and must be prepared to furnish a written justification to the Contracting Officer in response to a request for information under paragraph (d) or a challenge under paragraph (f) of the clause [@CL-CMP-3802; @CL-CMP-8511; @CL-CMP-0626]. A written challenge states the specific grounds for challenging the asserted restriction and requires the Contractor to respond in writing within 60 days and provide justification based upon records kept under paragraph (b) and other reasonably available documentation in sufficient detail to enable the Contracting Officer to determine the validity of the asserted restrictions [@CL-CMP-3803; @CL-CMP-3804; @CL-CMP-0336]. The Contractor's failure to provide a timely response to a Contracting Officer's request for information or failure to provide sufficient information to enable the Contracting Officer to evaluate an asserted restriction constitutes reasonable grounds for questioning the validity of an asserted restriction [@CL-CMP-0337; @CL-CMP-3808]. After reviewing any available information pertaining to the validity of an asserted restriction, the Contracting Officer issues a final decision promptly in accordance with the Disputes clause of the contract, either denying the validity of the asserted restriction if it has not been justified [@CL-CMP-3805; @CL-CMP-0338] or validating the asserted restriction if it has been justified [@CL-CMP-3805; @CL-CMP-3806]. Notwithstanding a Contracting Officer's final decision denying validity and except as provided in paragraph (g)(3) of the clause, the Government agrees to honor the asserted restriction for one listed period of 90 days from the date of the final decision to allow the Contractor to appeal to the appropriate Board of Contract Appeals or to file suit in an appropriate court [@CL-CMP-3807; @CL-CMP-8513].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor delivered a flight-control program marked with restrictions. A written challenge arrives that states its grounds and requires a written response within 60 days. The contractor answers with its records and the documentation it has. The Contracting Officer finds the restriction not justified and issues a final decision. The Government honors the restriction for 90 days after that decision, which gives the contractor time to appeal or sue.

## Never confuse

Under the technical data validation clause, the Contractor's or subcontractor's written response shall be considered a claim within the meaning of section 7101 of title 41 of the United States Code, Contract Disputes, and shall be certified in the form prescribed at FAR 33.207, regardless of dollar amount [@CL-CMP-3801]. Under the software validation clause, the Contracting Officer's final decisions denying or validating an asserted restriction are issued in accordance with the Disputes clause of the contract [@CL-CMP-0338; @CL-CMP-3806]. The technical data clause is covered on the card for [validation of asserted restrictions on technical data](cmp-dod-technical-data-restriction-validation.md). The technical data clause requires a response within 60 days justifying and providing sufficient evidence as to the current validity of the asserted restriction, while the software clause requires the Contractor to respond in writing within 60 days [@CL-CMP-3795; @CL-CMP-3803]. Under the technical data clause, the Government agrees that it will continue to be bound by the asserted restriction for a period of 90 days from the issuance of the Contracting Officer's final decision under paragraph (g)(2)(i) of that clause, while under the software clause, except as provided in paragraph (g)(3), the Government agrees to honor the asserted restriction for one listed period of 90 days from the date of the Contracting Officer's final decision to allow the Contractor to appeal to the appropriate Board of Contract Appeals or to file suit in an appropriate court [@CL-CMP-3798; @CL-CMP-3807; @CL-CMP-8513]. Under the software clause, the Contractor's failure to provide a timely response to a Contracting Officer's request for information or failure to provide sufficient information to enable the Contracting Officer to evaluate an asserted restriction constitutes reasonable grounds for questioning the validity of an asserted restriction [@CL-CMP-0337; @CL-CMP-3808]. For the Disputes clause, see [Disputes clause, claims and certification](../../close-revrec/revenue/cls-disputes-clause.md).
