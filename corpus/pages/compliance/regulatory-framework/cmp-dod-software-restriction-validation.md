## Purpose

Shows how validation of asserted software restrictions works, how it differs from the technical data validation clause, the justification the contractor must give, and the Contracting Officer's decision.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-CMP-3802] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 252.227-7019 [@CL-CMP-3802] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-3802] |
| Applies to | Restrictions asserted on the Government's rights in computer software [@CL-CMP-3802] |

The Contractor shall maintain records sufficient to justify the validity of any asserted restrictions on the Government's rights to use, modify, reproduce, perform, display, release, or disclose computer software [@CL-CMP-3802]. A challenge must be in writing, must state the specific grounds for challenging the asserted restriction, and shall require the Contractor to respond in writing within 60 days [@CL-CMP-3803]. One listed element of a challenge is a requirement that the Contractor provide justification for the assertion, based upon records kept under the justification paragraph and other reasonably available documentation, in sufficient detail to enable the Contracting Officer to determine the validity [@CL-CMP-3803; @CL-CMP-3804]. A failure to provide a timely response to a request for information, or to provide sufficient information to enable evaluation, shall constitute reasonable grounds for questioning the validity of an asserted restriction [@CL-CMP-3808].

Where the Contracting Officer, after reviewing any available information, determines that the asserted restriction has not been justified, the Contracting Officer shall issue promptly a final decision [@CL-CMP-3805]. One listed outcome is that, where the Contracting Officer determines that the asserted restriction has been justified, the Contracting Officer shall issue promptly a final decision, in accordance with the Disputes clause, validating the asserted restriction [@CL-CMP-3805; @CL-CMP-3806]. Notwithstanding a final decision denying the validity of an asserted restriction, and except as provided in paragraph (g)(3) of the clause, the Government agrees to honor the asserted restriction for one listed period of 90 days [@CL-CMP-3807].

## How it works

The Contractor keeps records that justify each asserted restriction [@CL-CMP-3802]. A written challenge states the specific grounds and requires a written response within 60 days, with justification in sufficient detail [@CL-CMP-3803; @CL-CMP-3804]. A late or insufficient response is itself reasonable grounds to question the restriction [@CL-CMP-3808]. The Contracting Officer then issues a final decision promptly, either on a restriction found not justified [@CL-CMP-3805] or on one found justified, which validates it [@CL-CMP-3806]. After a decision denying validity, the Government agrees to honor the restriction for one listed period of 90 days, with the exception the clause states [@CL-CMP-3807].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor delivered a flight-control program marked with restrictions. A written challenge arrives that states its grounds and requires a written response within 60 days. The contractor answers with its records and the documentation it has. The Contracting Officer finds the restriction not justified and issues a final decision. The Government honors the restriction for 90 days after that decision, which gives the contractor time to appeal or sue.

## Never confuse

The technical data validation clause says the contractor's response to a challenge is a claim that must be certified [@CL-CMP-3801]. In the full printed text of the software clause, no paragraph calls the response a claim or requires it to be certified. Its final decisions are issued under the Disputes clause [@CL-CMP-3806]. The technical data clause is covered on the card for [validation of asserted restrictions on technical data](cmp-dod-technical-data-restriction-validation.md). Both clauses require a response within 60 days [@CL-CMP-3795; @CL-CMP-3803], and both give the contractor 90 days of continued protection after a final decision denying the restriction [@CL-CMP-3798; @CL-CMP-3807]. The software clause also treats a failure to respond in time or with enough information as reasonable grounds for questioning the restriction [@CL-CMP-3808]. The final decision is made under the Disputes clause; see [Disputes clause, claims and certification](../../close-revrec/revenue/cls-disputes-clause.md).
