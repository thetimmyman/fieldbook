## Purpose

Covers the duty to keep the Commercial and Government entity code current at each location of performance, how a code change is requested, and what an offeror represents and reviews under the covered telecommunications representation provision.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-5987] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.204-18; FAR 52.204-26 [@CL-CMP-5988; @CL-CMP-5993] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.204-91 [@CL-CMP-0538]; RFO FAR 52.240-90 [@CL-CMP-0550]; RFO FAR 52.240-92 [@CL-CMP-0545] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-5987] |
| Applies to | Contractors under contracts with the code maintenance clause, and offerors on solicitations with the representation provision [@CL-CMP-5988; @CL-CMP-5993] |

For entities located in the United States or its outlying areas, a Commercial and Government Entity code is an identifier assigned by the Defense Logistics Agency code branch to identify a commercial or government entity by unique location [@CL-CMP-5987]. For entities located outside the United States and its outlying areas, it is an identifier assigned by a member of the North Atlantic Treaty Organization or by the NATO Support and Procurement Agency [@CL-CMP-6686]. The Defense Logistics Agency code branch records and maintains that identifier in the CAGE master file, and this type of code is known as a NATO CAGE code [@CL-CMP-6687].

The contractor must ensure that the code is maintained throughout the life of the contract for each location of contract performance, including subcontract performance [@CL-CMP-5988].

The telecommunications provision asks the offeror to represent whether it does or does not provide covered telecommunications equipment or services as a part of its offered products or services to the Government in the performance of any contract, subcontract, or other contractual instrument [@CL-CMP-5993]. After a reasonable inquiry, it also asks whether the offeror does or does not use covered telecommunications equipment or services, or any equipment, system, or service that uses them [@CL-CMP-5994].

## How it works

### Code maintenance

A contractor registered in the System for Award Management can have data that the code branch received from that registration changed in the CAGE master file only if the contractor initiates the change by updating its registration [@CL-CMP-5989]. A contractor undergoing a novation or change-of-name agreement must notify the contracting officer in accordance with the subpart that governs those agreements [@CL-CMP-5990]. The contractor must communicate any change to the code to the contracting officer within 30 days after the change, so that a modification can be issued to update the code on the contract [@CL-CMP-5991].

A contractor located in the United States or its outlying areas that is not registered in the System for Award Management must submit written change requests to the code branch [@CL-CMP-6688]. The requests must be provided at the address that the clause names [@CL-CMP-6689]. Change requests to the master file are accepted from the entity identified by the code [@CL-CMP-6690]. A contractor located outside the United States and its outlying areas that is not registered in the System for Award Management must contact the appropriate National Codification Bureau [@CL-CMP-6691]. It may instead contact the NATO Support and Procurement Agency at the address that the clause names to request code changes [@CL-CMP-6692]. Additional guidance for maintaining codes is available at the address the clause names [@CL-CMP-6693].

If the contract includes the security requirements clause, the contractor must ensure that subcontractors maintain their codes throughout the life of the contract [@CL-CMP-5992]; see [Security requirements for access to classified information](cmp-security-requirements-classified-information.md).

### Telecommunications representation

In the provision, covered telecommunications equipment or services and reasonable inquiry have the meaning provided in the clause on the prohibition on contracting for certain telecommunications and video surveillance services or equipment [@CL-CMP-6694]. The offeror must review the list of excluded parties in the System for Award Management for entities excluded from receiving federal awards for covered telecommunications equipment or services [@CL-CMP-6695]. The bracketed boxes in each representation are the offeror's answers to the two statements [@CL-CMP-5993; @CL-CMP-5994].

Under the RFO text, the DoD class deviation directs DoD contracting officers to use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text, effective February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. The RFO text lists FAR 52.204-18 and FAR 52.204-26 as reserved [@CL-CMP-0536; @CL-CMP-0537]. The RFO clause at FAR 52.204-91 keeps the definition of the code for entities located in the United States or its outlying areas and for entities located outside them [@CL-CMP-0538; @CL-CMP-0539; @CL-CMP-5987; @CL-CMP-6686; @CL-CMP-6687]. Its paragraph (c) says the Contractor shall ensure that the code is maintained throughout the life of the contract, without the codified words about each location of contract performance [@CL-CMP-0540; @CL-CMP-5988]. A table in that paragraph says a Contractor registered in the System for Award Management initiates a change by updating its registration [@CL-CMP-0541; @CL-CMP-5989], a Contractor in the United States or its outlying areas that is not registered submits a change request to the code branch [@CL-CMP-0542; @CL-CMP-6688; @CL-CMP-6689], and a Contractor outside them that is not registered contacts the appropriate National Codification Bureau or the NATO Support and Procurement Agency [@CL-CMP-0543; @CL-CMP-6691; @CL-CMP-6692]. Paragraph (d) says the Contractor communicates a change to its unique entity identifier or code to the contracting officer within 30 days, and adds that a change in the unique entity identifier does not necessarily require a novation [@CL-CMP-0544; @CL-CMP-5991]. The RFO paragraphs (c) and (d) set out the routes for changing a code in a rule that the Contractor shall request changes as indicated in the table [@CL-CMP-0623], and they do not repeat the codified sentences on notifying the contracting officer of a novation or change-of-name agreement [@CL-CMP-0623; @CL-CMP-0544; @CL-CMP-5990], on the CAGE Branch modifying data received from SAM only if the contractor initiates the change [@CL-CMP-0623; @CL-CMP-0541; @CL-CMP-5989], on accepting change requests from the entity identified by the code [@CL-CMP-0623; @CL-CMP-0542; @CL-CMP-6690], or on additional guidance for maintaining codes [@CL-CMP-0623; @CL-CMP-0543; @CL-CMP-6693]. The security requirements clause at FAR 52.240-92 ends its paragraph (e) with the rule that the contractor shall ensure subcontractors maintain their codes throughout the life of the contract, without the codified condition about the contract including the clause [@CL-CMP-0545; @CL-CMP-5992]. For the telecommunications representation, the RFO provision at FAR 52.240-90 takes its defined terms from the clause at FAR 52.240-91, in a list that does not include reasonable inquiry [@CL-CMP-0546; @CL-CMP-0547; @CL-CMP-6694], describes a reasonable inquiry in its paragraph (c) as one that looks at any information in the Offeror's possession but does not need to include an internal or third-party audit [@CL-CMP-0549], keeps the review of the list of excluded parties [@CL-CMP-0548; @CL-CMP-6695], and has the offeror represent that, after a reasonable inquiry, it will not provide and does not use covered equipment or services, except as waived by the solicitation or disclosed in its paragraph (g) [@CL-CMP-0549; @CL-CMP-0550; @CL-CMP-0581; @CL-CMP-5993; @CL-CMP-5994].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor performs at three locations and must ensure that the code is maintained throughout the life of the contract for each location of contract performance [@CL-CMP-5988]. The code for one registered location is changed on day 0. The contractor updates its System for Award Management registration, which is the route for changing data the code branch received from that registration [@CL-CMP-5989]. It tells the contracting officer by day 30 so that a modification can update the code on the contract [@CL-CMP-5991]. The prime contract includes the security requirements clause, so the contractor also ensures that its subcontractors maintain their own codes for the life of the contract [@CL-CMP-5992].

Another contractor is located in the United States and is not registered in the System for Award Management. It submits a written change request to the code branch [@CL-CMP-6688], and the request comes from the entity identified by the code [@CL-CMP-6690].

A separate offeror answers the telecommunications provision. It reviews the list of excluded parties in the System for Award Management [@CL-CMP-6695]. It states that it does not provide covered telecommunications equipment or services as a part of its offered products or services to the Government in the performance of any contract, subcontract, or other contractual instrument [@CL-CMP-5993]. After a reasonable inquiry it states that it does not use covered equipment or services, or any equipment, system or service that uses them [@CL-CMP-5994].

## Never confuse

The code identifies an entity by unique location [@CL-CMP-5987]. The unique entity identifier is a different identifier, described on [Taxpayer identification, the unique entity identifier and its maintenance](cmp-taxpayer-identifier-and-entity-identifier.md). A change of code is communicated within 30 days [@CL-CMP-5991], whereas a novation or change-of-name agreement is notified under the subpart that governs those agreements [@CL-CMP-5990]; see [Novation and change-of-name agreements](../../mna/diligence/mna-novation-agreements.md).

The other covered telecommunications representation provision asks whether the offeror will or will not provide covered telecommunications equipment or services to the Government in the performance of any contract, subcontract or other contractual instrument resulting from this solicitation [@CL-CMP-3558], while this one asks whether the offeror does or does not provide covered telecommunications equipment or services as a part of its offered products or services to the Government in the performance of any contract, subcontract, or other contractual instrument [@CL-CMP-5993]. The disclosure and reporting duties are on [Covered telecommunications representation and reporting](cmp-covered-telecommunications-representation-and-reporting.md) and are not restated here. The meanings of the defined terms are on [Covered telecommunications equipment or services: definitions and prohibition](../trade-cyber/cmp-covered-telecommunications-definitions-and-prohibition.md).

The codified provision asks whether the offeror does or does not provide covered telecommunications equipment or services as a part of its offered products or services to the Government in the performance of any contract, subcontract, or other contractual instrument [@CL-CMP-5993], whereas the RFO provision has the offeror represent that it will not provide covered telecommunications equipment or services to the Government in the performance of any contract, subcontract or other contractual instrument resulting from this solicitation, except as waived by the solicitation, or as disclosed in paragraph (g) [@CL-CMP-0550].
