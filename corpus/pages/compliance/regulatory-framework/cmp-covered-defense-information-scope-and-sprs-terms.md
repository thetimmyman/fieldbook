## Purpose

Sets out which contracts the safeguarding subpart covers, what else it requires and leaves in place, and the three risk definitions that the Supplier Performance Risk System subpart uses.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-CMP-7960] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 204.7300 [@CL-CMP-7960; @CL-CMP-7961; @CL-CMP-7962; @CL-CMP-8441] and DFARS 204.7601 [@CL-CMP-7963; @CL-CMP-7964; @CL-CMP-7965; @CL-CMP-8442] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-7960] |
| Applies to | Contracts and subcontracts requiring contractors and subcontractors to safeguard covered defense information [@CL-CMP-7960] |

The safeguarding subpart applies to contracts and subcontracts requiring contractors and subcontractors to safeguard covered defense information that resides in or transits through covered contractor information systems by applying specified network security requirements [@CL-CMP-7960]. It also requires reporting of cyber incidents [@CL-CMP-7961].

The subpart does not abrogate any other requirements regarding contractor physical, personnel, information, technical or general administrative security operations governing the protection of unclassified information [@CL-CMP-7962]. It also does not affect requirements of the National Industrial Security Program [@CL-CMP-8441].

The risk definitions belong to a different subpart, and each term is defined as it is used in the subpart that holds section 204.7601 [@CL-CMP-8442]. Item risk means the probability that a product, based on intended use, will introduce performance risk resulting in safety issues, mission degradation or monetary loss [@CL-CMP-7963]. Price risk means the measure of whether a proposed price for a product or service is consistent with historical prices paid for that item or service [@CL-CMP-7964]. Supplier risk means the probability that an award may subject the procurement to the risk of unsuccessful performance or to supply chain risk, and the definition refers to section 239.7301 for supply chain risk [@CL-CMP-7965].

## How it works

The safeguarding scope follows the requirement to safeguard, not the contract type: it reaches contracts and subcontracts that require safeguarding of covered defense information residing in or transiting through a covered contractor information system [@CL-CMP-7960].

The scope text names the contractors and subcontractors on whom the safeguarding requirement falls, and it says the subpart also requires reporting of cyber incidents; it does not say in this sentence who reports or when [@CL-CMP-7960; @CL-CMP-7961].

Other security requirements stay in force: the subpart neither abrogates the other requirements for unclassified information nor affects National Industrial Security Program requirements [@CL-CMP-7962; @CL-CMP-8441].

The three definitions only define terms. They state no duty, no threshold and no decision-maker, and they keep product, price and supplier risk separate [@CL-CMP-7963; @CL-CMP-7964; @CL-CMP-7965].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A subcontract requires the subcontractor to protect covered defense information that sits on its own covered contractor information system by applying specified network security requirements. The subpart applies to that subcontract [@CL-CMP-7960], and the subpart also requires reporting of cyber incidents [@CL-CMP-7961]. The other requirements governing the protection of unclassified information, such as those for physical and personnel security, are not abrogated [@CL-CMP-7962].

An evaluator compares a proposed price of 120 with historical prices paid of about 100 for the same item. Price risk is the measure of whether the proposed price is consistent with those historical prices [@CL-CMP-7964]. The definition does not set a pass mark.

## Never confuse

Covered defense information is not the same as unclassified information generally [@CL-CMP-7960; @CL-CMP-7962]. The safeguarding requirements attach to covered defense information that resides in or transits through covered contractor information systems, and other requirements for unclassified information stay in place [@CL-CMP-7960; @CL-CMP-7962].

The safeguarding scope and the risk definitions come from different subparts, and the risk definitions apply only as the terms are used in their own subpart [@CL-CMP-7960; @CL-CMP-8442].
