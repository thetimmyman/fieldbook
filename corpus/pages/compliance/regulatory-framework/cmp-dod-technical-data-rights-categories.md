## Purpose

Shows what unlimited rights, government purpose rights and limited rights allow in technical data under the Defense clause for items that are not commercial, and how the funding of development decides which category applies.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-CMP-3759] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 252.227-7013 [@CL-CMP-3759] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Aug 2025 [@CL-CMP-10190] |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-3759] |
| Applies to | Technical data on other than commercial products or services, and on parts of commercial products or services developed in any part at Government expense, except as paragraph (b)(2) of the clause provides [@CL-CMP-3759] |

Except as provided in paragraph (b)(2) of the clause, the clause governs all technical data pertaining to other than commercial products or commercial services, or to any portion of a commercial product or commercial service that was developed in any part at Government expense [@CL-CMP-3759]. The Contractor grants or shall obtain for the Government the clause's listed royalty free, worldwide, nonexclusive, irrevocable license rights in technical data other than computer software documentation [@CL-CMP-3768]. Unlimited rights are rights to use, modify, reproduce, perform, display, release, or disclose technical data in whole or in part, in any manner and for any purpose whatsoever, and to have or authorize others to do so [@CL-CMP-3760]. Government purpose rights are the rights to use, modify, reproduce, release, perform, display, or disclose technical data within the Government without restriction [@CL-CMP-3761], and to release or disclose technical data outside the Government and authorize persons to whom release or disclosure has been made to use, modify, reproduce, release, perform, display, or disclose that data for United States Government purposes [@CL-CMP-3762]. Government purposes include competitive procurement, but do not include the rights to use the data for commercial purposes or to authorize others to do so [@CL-CMP-3763].

Limited rights means the rights to use, modify, reproduce, release, perform, display, or disclose technical data, in whole or in part, within the Government [@CL-CMP-3764]. The Government may not, without the written permission of the party asserting limited rights, release or disclose the technical data outside the Government, use the technical data for manufacture, or authorize the technical data to be used by another party, except that the Government may reproduce, release, or disclose such data or authorize the use or reproduction of the data by persons outside the Government if the reproduction, release, disclosure, or use is necessary for emergency repair and overhaul or is a release or disclosure under paragraph (1)(ii), the recipient of the technical data is subject to a prohibition on further reproduction, release, disclosure, or use, and the contractor or subcontractor asserting the restriction is notified [@CL-CMP-3765; @CL-CMP-0627; @CL-CMP-0628].

## How it works

Development exclusively at private expense means development accomplished entirely with costs charged to indirect cost pools, costs not allocated to a Government contract, or any combination of those [@CL-CMP-3766], while development with mixed funding is accomplished partly with those costs and partly with costs charged directly to a government contract [@CL-CMP-3767]. The Government shall have unlimited rights in technical data that fall in any of the alternatives in paragraph (c)(1), which include data pertaining to an item, component, or process which has been or will be developed exclusively with Government funds [@CL-CMP-3769], data in which the Government has obtained unlimited rights under another Government contract or as a result of negotiations, or data furnished to the Government under the contract or any other Government contract or subcontract thereunder with government purpose license rights or limited rights where the restrictive conditions have expired, or with government purpose rights where the Contractor's exclusive right to use such data for commercial purposes has expired [@CL-CMP-0473; @CL-CMP-0474]. The Government shall have government purpose rights for a 5-year period, or such other period as may be negotiated, in technical data that pertain to items, components, or processes developed with mixed funding except when the Government is entitled to unlimited rights in such data as provided in paragraphs (c)(1)(ii) and (c)(1)(iv) through (c)(1)(ix) of the clause, or created with mixed funding in the performance of a contract that does not require the development, manufacture, construction, or production of items, components, or processes [@CL-CMP-3770; @CL-CMP-0475]. Upon expiration of the 5-year or other negotiated period, the Government shall have unlimited rights in the technical data [@CL-CMP-3771]. Except as provided in paragraphs (c)(1)(ii) and (c)(1)(iv) through (c)(1)(ix) of the clause, the Government shall have limited rights in technical data pertaining to items, components, or processes developed exclusively at private expense and marked with the limited rights legend prescribed in paragraph (g) of the clause, or created exclusively at private expense in the performance of a contract that does not require the development, manufacture, construction, or production of items, components, or processes [@CL-CMP-3772; @CL-CMP-0476].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor develops a bracket design that costs 100. In case one, all 100 is charged to overhead and the data is marked with the limited rights legend, so the Government has limited rights. In case two, 60 is charged to overhead and 40 directly to a contract, so the funding is mixed and the Government has government purpose rights for the five-year period and then unlimited rights. In case three, a contract pays all 100, so the Government has unlimited rights.

## Never confuse

Government purposes include competitive procurement, but do not include the rights to use, modify, reproduce, release, perform, display, or disclose technical data for commercial purposes or to authorize others to do so [@CL-CMP-3763]. The general FAR rights in data clause has its own definitions and allocation; see [Rights in data, general: definitions and allocation](cmp-rights-in-data-general-definitions-and-allocation.md). The grant covers technical data other than computer software documentation [@CL-CMP-3768]; for rights in computer software documentation, see the card on the [noncommercial software clause](cmp-dod-noncommercial-software-rights.md). The legends that assert limited rights and government purpose rights, and the procedure for correcting them, are on the card for [markings](cmp-dod-technical-data-markings.md). For technical data on commercial products developed exclusively at private expense, see [DoD technical data for commercial products and services](cmp-dod-commercial-technical-data-license.md).
