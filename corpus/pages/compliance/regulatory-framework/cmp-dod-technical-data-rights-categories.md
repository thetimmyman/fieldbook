## Purpose

Shows what unlimited rights, government purpose rights and limited rights allow in technical data under the Defense clause for items that are not commercial, and how the funding of development decides which category applies.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-CMP-3759] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 252.227-7013 [@CL-CMP-3759] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-3759] |
| Applies to | Technical data on other than commercial products or services, and on parts of commercial products or services developed in any part at Government expense, except as a later paragraph provides [@CL-CMP-3759] |

Except as provided in a later paragraph of the clause, the clause governs all technical data pertaining to other than commercial products or commercial services, or to any portion of a commercial product or commercial service that was developed in any part at Government expense [@CL-CMP-3759]. The Contractor grants or shall obtain for the Government the clause's listed royalty free, worldwide, nonexclusive, irrevocable license rights in technical data other than computer software documentation [@CL-CMP-3768]. Unlimited rights are rights to use, modify, reproduce, perform, display, release, or disclose technical data in whole or in part, in any manner and for any purpose whatsoever, and to have or authorize others to do so [@CL-CMP-3760]. Government purpose rights are, first, the rights to use, modify, reproduce, release, perform, display, or disclose technical data within the Government without restriction [@CL-CMP-3761]. One listed further right is to release or disclose the data outside the Government and authorize recipients to use it for United States Government purposes [@CL-CMP-3761; @CL-CMP-3762]. Government purposes include competitive procurement, but do not include the rights to use the data for commercial purposes or to authorize others to do so [@CL-CMP-3763].

Limited rights are the rights to use, modify, reproduce, release, perform, display, or disclose technical data, in whole or in part, within the Government [@CL-CMP-3764]. The Government may not, without the written permission of the party asserting limited rights, release or disclose the data outside the Government, use it for manufacture, or authorize another party to use it, subject to an exception stated in the definition [@CL-CMP-3765]. This card does not set out that exception or the other limits on release.

## How it works

The funding test turns on who paid for development. Development exclusively at private expense means development accomplished entirely with costs charged to indirect cost pools, costs not allocated to a Government contract, or any combination of those [@CL-CMP-3766], while development with mixed funding is accomplished partly with those costs and partly with costs charged directly to a government contract [@CL-CMP-3767]. Technical data pertaining to an item, component, or process developed exclusively with Government funds carries unlimited rights [@CL-CMP-3769]. Mixed funding gives the Government government purpose rights for five years or another negotiated period, except when the Government is entitled to unlimited rights in the data, with unlimited rights after that period ends [@CL-CMP-3770; @CL-CMP-3771]. Data pertaining to items developed exclusively at private expense and marked with the limited rights legend carry limited rights, subject to listed exceptions [@CL-CMP-3772].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor develops a bracket design that costs 100. In case one, all 100 is charged to overhead and the data is marked with the limited rights legend, so the Government has limited rights. In case two, 60 is charged to overhead and 40 directly to a contract, so the funding is mixed and the Government has government purpose rights for the five-year period and then unlimited rights. In case three, a contract pays all 100, so the Government has unlimited rights.

## Never confuse

Government purpose rights are not commercial rights: the Government may share the data for its own purposes, including competitive procurement, but not for commercial purposes [@CL-CMP-3763]. The general FAR rights in data clause has its own definitions and allocation; see [Rights in data, general: definitions and allocation](cmp-rights-in-data-general-definitions-and-allocation.md). Rights in computer software documentation are outside this grant [@CL-CMP-3768], and the card on the [noncommercial software clause](cmp-dod-noncommercial-software-rights.md) covers them. The legends that assert limited rights and government purpose rights, and the procedure for correcting them, are on the card for [markings](cmp-dod-technical-data-markings.md). For technical data on commercial products developed exclusively at private expense, see [DoD technical data for commercial products and services](cmp-dod-commercial-technical-data-license.md).
