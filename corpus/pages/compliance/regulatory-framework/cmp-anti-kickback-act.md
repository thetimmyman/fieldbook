## Purpose

Shows what a kickback is, when the Anti-Kickback Procedures clause goes into a contract, what procedures and reporting the contractor owes, and the Government's offset and withholding powers.

## The rule

| Codified citation | FAR 3.502-1, FAR 3.502-2 and FAR 3.502-3 [@CL-CMP-2037; @CL-CMP-2039; @CL-CMP-2043] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-2043] |
| Applies to | For the clause, solicitations and contracts exceeding $200,000, other than those for commercial products or commercial services [@CL-CMP-2043] |

A kickback is any money, fee, commission, credit, gift, gratuity, thing of value, or compensation of any kind provided to any prime contractor, prime contractor employee, subcontractor, or subcontractor employee for the purpose of improperly obtaining or rewarding favorable treatment [@CL-CMP-2037; @CL-CMP-2038]. The favorable treatment is treatment in connection with a prime contract or in connection with a subcontract relating to a prime contract [@CL-CMP-2038]. The Kickbacks statute prohibits any person from providing, attempting to provide, or offering to provide any kickback, and from soliciting, accepting, or attempting to accept any kickbacks [@CL-CMP-2039]. One listed prohibition is including, directly or indirectly, the amount of any kickback in the contract price charged by a subcontractor to a prime contractor or a higher tier subcontractor or in the contract price charged by a prime contractor to the United States [@CL-CMP-2039; @CL-CMP-2040]. The contracting officer shall insert the Anti-Kickback Procedures clause in solicitations and contracts exceeding $200,000, other than those for commercial products or commercial services [@CL-CMP-2043].

The statute requires each contracting agency to include in each prime contract, other than for commercial products or commercial services, exceeding $200,000, a requirement that the prime contractor shall have in place and follow reasonable procedures designed to prevent and detect violations, among other listed requirements [@CL-CMP-2044]. The procedures are designed to prevent and detect violations of the Kickbacks statute in its own operations and direct business relationships, and the section gives company ethics rules prohibiting kickbacks by employees, agents, or subcontractors as one example [@CL-CMP-2044; @CL-CMP-2103]. One listed requirement is that the prime contractor cooperate fully with any Federal agency investigating a possible violation of the Kickbacks statute [@CL-CMP-2044; @CL-CMP-2045]. The duty to report any possible violation of the Kickbacks statute applies when the prime contractor or subcontractor has reasonable grounds to believe such violation may have occurred [@CL-CMP-2047]. The written report goes to the inspector general of the contracting agency, to the head of the contracting agency if the agency does not have an inspector general, or to the Attorney General [@CL-CMP-2046; @CL-CMP-2047].

## How it works

The contracting officer may offset the amount of a kickback against monies owed by the United States to the prime contractor under the prime contract to which the kickback relates [@CL-CMP-2041]. The contracting officer may also direct a prime contractor to withhold from any sums owed to a subcontractor under a subcontract of the prime contract the amount of any kickback which was or may be offset against the prime contractor [@CL-CMP-2041; @CL-CMP-2042]. The contractor's side is procedures, cooperation and written reporting [@CL-CMP-2044; @CL-CMP-2045; @CL-CMP-2046]. A kickback that is priced into a subcontract or prime contract is itself a prohibited act [@CL-CMP-2039; @CL-CMP-2040]. The section gives examples of reasonable procedures, but the requirement itself is to have and follow reasonable ones [@CL-CMP-2044; @CL-CMP-2103].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A prime contractor pays a subcontractor 500,000 under a subcontract, and 20,000 of the price is a kickback to a purchasing employee. The Government may offset 20,000 against money it owes the prime contractor under that prime contract, and may direct the prime contractor to withhold 20,000 from what it owes the subcontractor. The prime contractor and the subcontractor must each report the possible violation in writing if they have reasonable grounds to believe it occurred.

## Never confuse

The procedures requirement belongs to the contractor and is to be included in each prime contract exceeding $200,000, other than for commercial products or commercial services [@CL-CMP-2044]. The contracting officer may offset the amount of a kickback against monies owed to the prime contractor under the prime contract to which it relates [@CL-CMP-2041], and may direct a prime contractor to withhold the amount of any kickback which was or may be offset from sums owed to a subcontractor [@CL-CMP-2042]. The reporting duty applies to any possible violation once the prime contractor or subcontractor has reasonable grounds to believe it may have occurred [@CL-CMP-2047].
