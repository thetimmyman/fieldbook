## Purpose

Shows what the contractor must send the Contracting Officer before filing a patent application that discloses classified subject matter, when the Contractor may file if no instructions are given within the period stated in the clause, and when foreign filing needs approval.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-3624] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.227-10 [@CL-CMP-3624] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.227-10 [@CL-CMP-0593] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-3624] |
| Applies to | Contract subject matter classified Secret or higher, or Confidential [@CL-CMP-3624; @CL-CMP-3626] |

Before filing or causing to be filed a United States patent application disclosing any subject matter of the contract classified Secret or higher, the Contractor shall, citing the 30-day provision, transmit the proposed application to the Contracting Officer [@CL-CMP-3624]. The Contractor shall observe any instructions of the Contracting Officer regarding the manner of delivery of the application to the United States Patent Office, but the Contractor shall not be denied the right to file the application [@CL-CMP-3662]. The Contractor may file the application if the Contracting Officer has not given, within 30 days from the date of mailing or other transmittal of the proposed application, any instructions of the kind the clause describes [@CL-CMP-3625]. Before filing a patent application in the United States disclosing any subject matter of the contract classified Confidential, the Contractor shall furnish the Contracting Officer a copy of the application [@CL-CMP-3626] for Government determination whether, for reasons of national security, the application should be placed under an order of secrecy or the issuance of a patent should be otherwise delayed under pertinent United States statutes or regulations [@CL-CMP-7665].

Where the subject matter of the contract is classified for reasons of security, the foreign filing paragraph applies [@CL-CMP-3627]. Under it, the Contractor shall not file, or cause to be filed, in any country other than in the United States as provided in the two earlier paragraphs of the clause, an application or registration for a patent containing any of the subject matter of the contract without first obtaining written approval of the Contracting Officer [@CL-CMP-3628]. This card does not detail the later paragraphs on security regulations, on furnishing the filing details of each application, or on subcontracts.

## How it works

The clause sorts the filing step by classification level. Secret or higher subject matter goes to the Contracting Officer first, and the Contractor may file if the Contracting Officer has given no instructions within 30 days of transmittal, while observing any instructions on the manner of delivery and keeping the right to file [@CL-CMP-3624; @CL-CMP-3625; @CL-CMP-3662]. Confidential subject matter goes to the Contracting Officer as a copy before filing [@CL-CMP-3626]. Where the subject matter is classified for reasons of security, filing outside the United States needs the Contracting Officer's written approval first [@CL-CMP-3627; @CL-CMP-3628].

Under the RFO text, the DoD class deviation directs DoD contracting officers to use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text, effective February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. The RFO text keeps the Secret or higher rule: the Contractor transmits the proposed application to the Contracting Officer, citing the 30-day provision [@CL-CMP-0593; @CL-CMP-3624], observes the Contracting Officer's instructions on delivery without losing the right to file [@CL-CMP-0594; @CL-CMP-3662], and may file if no instructions come within 30 days [@CL-CMP-0595; @CL-CMP-3625]. It keeps the rule that, for Confidential subject matter, the Contractor furnishes the Contracting Officer a copy of the application before filing, for Government determination whether, for reasons of national security, the application should be placed under an order of secrecy or the issuance of a patent should be otherwise delayed [@CL-CMP-0596; @CL-CMP-0624; @CL-CMP-3626]. It keeps the rule on filing outside the United States where the subject matter is classified for reasons of security, which needs the Contracting Officer's written approval first [@CL-CMP-0597; @CL-CMP-0598; @CL-CMP-3627; @CL-CMP-3628].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor has an invention whose description includes subject matter classified Secret. On Day 0 it transmits the proposed application to the Contracting Officer, citing the 30-day provision [@CL-CMP-3624]. No instructions come back within 30 days, so the contractor may file in the United States [@CL-CMP-3625]. The subject matter is classified for reasons of security, and the contractor also wants to file in another country. It must first obtain the written approval of the Contracting Officer [@CL-CMP-3627; @CL-CMP-3628].

## Never confuse

The clause addresses patent applications that disclose subject matter of the contract classified Secret or higher or classified Confidential, and patent filing in any country other than the United States where the subject matter is classified for reasons of security [@CL-CMP-3624; @CL-CMP-3626; @CL-CMP-3627; @CL-CMP-3628]. For who holds title to an invention, see [ownership by the contractor](cmp-patent-rights-contractor-ownership-definitions-and-licenses.md) and [ownership by the Government](cmp-patent-rights-government-ownership.md). For the Contracting Officer's wider duties on classified acquisitions, see [Safeguarding classified information within industry](../audit-retention/cmp-classified-information-safeguarding-duties.md).
