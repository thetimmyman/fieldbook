## Purpose

Shows what the FAR says about employing convict labor, which rulings the Secretary of Labor has issued on contract value under the statute in the rulings section, and who may waive the veterans equal opportunity clause.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-3250] |
| Codified citation | FAR 22.201, FAR 22.605 and FAR 22.1305 [@CL-CMP-3250; @CL-CMP-3253; @CL-CMP-3256] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-3250] |
| Applies to | Contractors that employ prisoners, contracts under the statute in the rulings section, and contracts with the veterans clause [@CL-CMP-3250; @CL-CMP-3254; @CL-CMP-3256] |

The convict labor Executive order quoted in the FAR does not prohibit a contractor from employing persons on parole or probation, pardoned persons, persons who have served their terms, or Federal prisoners [@CL-CMP-3250]. It also does not prohibit employing nonfederal prisoners authorized to work at paid employment in the community under the laws of a listed jurisdiction, if the listed conditions are met [@CL-CMP-3251]. The first listed condition is that the worker is paid or is in an approved work training program on a voluntary basis [@CL-CMP-3251]. Paid employment must not displace employed workers, be applied in skills, crafts, or trades with a surplus of available gainful labor in the locality, or impair existing contracts for services [@CL-CMP-3251; @CL-CMP-3350]. The rates of pay and other conditions of employment must not be less than those for work of a similar nature in the locality [@CL-CMP-3251; @CL-CMP-3351]. The Attorney General must have certified that the work-release laws or regulations of the jurisdiction involved are in conformity with the Executive order [@CL-CMP-3251; @CL-CMP-3352]. Department of Justice regulations authorize the Director of the Bureau of Justice Assistance to certify, and to revoke the certification of, work-release laws or regulations [@CL-CMP-3252].

The Secretary of Labor has issued rulings and interpretations on the administration of the statute, and the section lists their substance [@CL-CMP-3253]. A contract for $20,000 or less that is later modified to exceed $20,000 becomes subject to the statute for work performed after the date of the modification [@CL-CMP-3254]. A contract for more than $20,000 that is later modified by mutual agreement to $20,000 or less is not subject to the statute for work performed after the date of the modification [@CL-CMP-3353]. Basic ordering agreements and blanket purchase agreements are subject to the statute unless it can be determined in advance that the aggregate amount of all orders estimated to be placed under them for 1 year after the effective date of the agreement will not exceed $20,000 [@CL-CMP-3255].

The Director of the Office of Federal Contract Compliance Programs may waive any or all of the terms of the Equal Opportunity for Veterans clause for any contract if a waiver is in the national interest [@CL-CMP-3256]. A waiver is also a listed ground for groups or categories of contracts, if a waiver is in the national interest and it is impracticable to act on each request individually and the waiver is determined to contribute substantially to convenience in administering the Act [@CL-CMP-3256; @CL-CMP-3354]. The head of the agency may waive any requirement of the veterans subpart when the contract is essential to the national security and its award without complying is necessary to the national security [@CL-CMP-3257], and must then notify the Deputy Assistant Secretary of Labor in writing within 30 days [@CL-CMP-3258]. The Deputy Assistant Secretary of Labor may withdraw an approved waiver for a specific contract or group of contracts to be awarded when in the Deputy's judgment such action is necessary to achieve the purposes of the Act, and the withdrawal does not apply to awarded contracts [@CL-CMP-3355]. For procurements entered into by sealed bidding, a withdrawal does not apply unless it is made more than 10 calendar days before the date set for the opening of bids [@CL-CMP-3355; @CL-CMP-3356].

## How it works

Each rule names its own decider. The Director of the Bureau of Justice Assistance certifies work-release laws [@CL-CMP-3252], the Secretary of Labor issues the rulings [@CL-CMP-3253], the Director of the Office of Federal Contract Compliance Programs waives for the national interest [@CL-CMP-3256], and the head of the agency waives for national security [@CL-CMP-3257]. The head of the agency then has 30 days to notify the Deputy Assistant Secretary of Labor in writing [@CL-CMP-3258]. The printed sections also list a condition on consulting local labor union organizations, further rulings on agent contracts, Government-owned facilities and annual determinations, and the submission of waiver requests, and this card does not draft them.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A supply contract is awarded at 15,000 and later modified to 25,000. The answer: the contract becomes subject to the statute for work performed after the modification date, because it was modified to exceed the 20,000 line. Separately, an agency head determines that a veterans-clause contract is essential to the national security and that its award without complying with the requirements is necessary to the national security, and waives the requirements. The answer: the agency head must notify the Deputy Assistant Secretary of Labor in writing within 30 days.

## Never confuse

The convict labor order does not prohibit employing the groups it lists, but employing nonfederal prisoners is subject to the listed conditions [@CL-CMP-3250; @CL-CMP-3251]. A ruling is not a waiver. The rulings say when a contract falls under the statute [@CL-CMP-3254; @CL-CMP-3255], while a waiver is a decision by a named official to set aside veterans clause terms or requirements [@CL-CMP-3256; @CL-CMP-3257]. A waiver by the Director or the agency head is also not a withdrawal: the Deputy Assistant Secretary of Labor may withdraw approved waivers, and a withdrawal does not apply to awarded contracts [@CL-CMP-3256; @CL-CMP-3257; @CL-CMP-3355]. The veterans clause thresholds and definitions are on [Equal opportunity for veterans](cmp-veterans-equal-opportunity.md) and are not restated here.
