## Purpose

Maps the policy on releasing acquisition information: the business security the policy requires, what a contracting officer may make available to the public, the categories that permission leaves out, and who the policy covers.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-8610] |
| Codified citation | FAR 5.401 [@CL-CMP-8610] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-8610] |
| Applies to | All Government personnel who participate directly or indirectly in any stage of the acquisition cycle [@CL-CMP-8614] |

A high level of business security must be maintained in order to preserve the integrity of the acquisition process [@CL-CMP-8610]. The text does not name who maintains it; the policy applies to all Government personnel who participate directly or indirectly in any stage of the acquisition cycle [@CL-CMP-8614].

When it is necessary to obtain information from potential contractors and others outside the Government for use in preparing Government estimates, contracting officers shall ensure that the information is not publicized or discussed with potential contractors [@CL-CMP-8611].

Contracting officers may make available maximum information to the public, except information on plans that would provide undue or discriminatory advantage to private or personal interests and information received in confidence from an offeror [@CL-CMP-8612]. The exception also covers information otherwise requiring protection under the Freedom of Information Act (see subpart 24.2) or the Privacy Act (see subpart 24.1) and information pertaining to internal agency communications, for example technical reviews, contracting authority or other reasons, or recommendations referring thereto [@CL-CMP-8613].

## How it works

The policy has two operative parts. The first is a duty on contracting officers to keep information obtained from outside the Government for Government estimates from being publicized or discussed with potential contractors [@CL-CMP-8611]. The second is a permission for contracting officers to make maximum information available to the public, with the exceptions listed above [@CL-CMP-8612; @CL-CMP-8613]. Because the text says "may", the permission to release is given to contracting officers [@CL-CMP-8612]; the text does not say who rules on whether an item falls within an exception. The policy applies to Government personnel and the text does not say that it applies to contractors [@CL-CMP-8614].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contracting officer asks three prospective offerors for cost breakdowns to prepare a Government estimate. The officer must ensure that the breakdowns are not publicized or discussed with potential contractors [@CL-CMP-8611]. Separately, one offeror sends a paper in confidence. The paper falls within the exception for information received in confidence from an offeror, so the permission to make maximum information available does not cover it [@CL-CMP-8612]. An internal technical review of the offers is likewise outside that permission, as information pertaining to internal agency communications [@CL-CMP-8613]. Other information that no exception covers is within the officer's permission to release [@CL-CMP-8612].

## Never confuse

Business security is not secrecy about everything: the policy permits release of maximum information to the public, and the exceptions are the listed categories [@CL-CMP-8612; @CL-CMP-8613]. The duty to keep information from being publicized or discussed is narrower. It covers information obtained from potential contractors and others outside the Government for use in preparing Government estimates [@CL-CMP-8611]. The text applies the policy to Government personnel and does not say that it applies to contractors [@CL-CMP-8614].
