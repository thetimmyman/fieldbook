## Purpose

Maps the policy on releasing acquisition information: the business security the policy requires, what a contracting officer may make available to the public, the categories that permission leaves out, and who the policy covers.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-8610] |
| Codified citation | FAR 5.401 [@CL-CMP-8610] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-8610] |
| Applies to | All Government personnel who participate directly or indirectly in any stage of the acquisition cycle [@CL-CMP-8614] |

A high level of business security must be maintained in order to preserve the integrity of the acquisition process [@CL-CMP-8610]. This policy applies to all Government personnel who participate directly or indirectly in any stage of the acquisition cycle [@CL-CMP-8614].

When it is necessary to obtain information from potential contractors and others outside the Government for use in preparing Government estimates, contracting officers shall ensure that the information is not publicized or discussed with potential contractors [@CL-CMP-8611].

Contracting officers may make available maximum information to the public, except information in one of the four alternatives in FAR 5.401(b): information on plans that would provide undue or discriminatory advantage to private or personal interests, information received in confidence from an offeror, information otherwise requiring protection under the Freedom of Information Act (see subpart 24.2) or the Privacy Act (see subpart 24.1), or information pertaining to internal agency communications (for example, technical reviews, contracting authority or other reasons, or recommendations referring thereto) [@CL-CMP-8612; @CL-CMP-8613].

## How it works

First, when it is necessary to obtain information from potential contractors and others outside the Government for use in preparing Government estimates, contracting officers shall ensure that the information is not publicized or discussed with potential contractors [@CL-CMP-8611]. Second, contracting officers may make available maximum information to the public, except information falling under one of the four alternatives in FAR 5.401(b) [@CL-CMP-8612; @CL-CMP-8613]. The policy applies to all Government personnel who participate directly or indirectly in any stage of the acquisition cycle [@CL-CMP-8614].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contracting officer asks three prospective offerors for cost breakdowns to prepare a Government estimate. The officer must ensure that the breakdowns are not publicized or discussed with potential contractors [@CL-CMP-8611]. Separately, one offeror sends a paper in confidence. The paper falls within the exception for information received in confidence from an offeror, so the permission to make maximum information available does not cover it [@CL-CMP-8612]. An internal technical review of the offers is likewise outside that permission, as information pertaining to internal agency communications [@CL-CMP-8613]. Other information that no exception covers is within the officer's permission to release [@CL-CMP-8612].

## Never confuse

Maintaining a high level of business security in order to preserve the integrity of the acquisition process [@CL-CMP-8610] operates alongside the rule that contracting officers may make available maximum information to the public, except information in one of the four alternatives in FAR 5.401(b) [@CL-CMP-8612; @CL-CMP-8613]. The duty to ensure that information is not publicized or discussed with potential contractors applies when it is necessary to obtain information from potential contractors and others outside the Government for use in preparing Government estimates [@CL-CMP-8611]. This policy applies to all Government personnel who participate directly or indirectly in any stage of the acquisition cycle [@CL-CMP-8614].
