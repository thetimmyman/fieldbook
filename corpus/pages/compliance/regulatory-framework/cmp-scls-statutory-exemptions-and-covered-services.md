## Purpose

Lists the contract categories to which the Service Contract Labor Standards statute does not apply, and the examples of services that have been found to be covered. The examples are illustrative and do not settle coverage.

FAR 22.1003-3 says the statute does not apply to the following [@CL-CMP-8092].

|---|---|---|
| (a) | Any contract for construction, alteration, or repair of public buildings or public works, including painting and decorating | [@CL-CMP-8092] |
| (b) | Any work required to be done in accordance with the provisions of title 41 of the United States Code, chapter 65 | [@CL-CMP-8104] |
| (c) | Any contract for transporting freight or personnel by vessel, aircraft, bus, truck, express, railroad, or oil or gas pipeline where published tariff rates are in effect | [@CL-CMP-8093] |

| (e) | Any contract for public utility services | [@CL-CMP-8094] |
| (f) | Any employment contract providing for direct services to a Federal agency by an individual or individuals | [@CL-CMP-8106] |
| (g) | Any contract for operating postal contract stations for the United States Postal Service | [@CL-CMP-8107] |

FAR 22.1003-5 lists examples [@CL-CMP-8095]. They are not definitive or exclusive and illustrate some of the types of services that have been found to be covered by the statute. The section points to 29 CFR 4.130 for additional examples [@CL-CMP-8095].

| Paragraph | Example | Claim |
|---|---|---|
| (a) to (c) | Motor pool operation, parking, taxicab, and ambulance services; packing, crating, and storage; custodial, janitorial, housekeeping, and guard services | [@CL-CMP-8096] |
| (d) to (f) | Food service and lodging; laundry, dry-cleaning, linen-supply, and clothing alteration and repair services; snow, trash, and garbage removal | [@CL-CMP-8108] |
| (g), (h) | Aerial spraying and aerial reconnaissance for fire detection; some support services at installations, including grounds maintenance and landscaping | [@CL-CMP-8109] |
| (i), (j) | Certain specialized services requiring specific skills, such as drafting, illustrating, graphic arts, stenographic reporting, or mortuary services; electronic equipment maintenance and operation and engineering support services | [@CL-CMP-8110] |
| (k) | Maintenance and repair of all types of equipment, for example aircraft, engines, electrical motors, vehicles, and electronic, office and related business and construction equipment, with a cross-reference "But see 22.1003-4(c)(1) and (d)(1)(iv)" | [@CL-CMP-8111] |
| (l) | Operation, maintenance, or logistics support of a Federal facility | [@CL-CMP-8097] |
| (m) | Data collection, processing and analysis services | [@CL-CMP-8098] |

Subpart 22.10 applies to all Government contracts, the principal purpose of which is to furnish services in the United States through the use of service employees, except as exempted in 22.1003-3 and 22.1003-4, or any subcontract at any tier thereunder [@CL-CMP-8228]. The subpart does not apply to individual contract requirements for services in contracts not having as their principal purpose the furnishing of services, and the nomenclature, type, or particular form of contract used by contracting agencies is not determinative of coverage [@CL-CMP-8229]. The Service Contract Labor Standards statute applies to service contracts performed in the United States (see 22.1001) and does not apply to contracts performed outside the United States [@CL-CMP-8502].

If the contracting officer questions the applicability of the Service Contract Labor Standards statute to an acquisition, the contracting officer shall request the advice of the agency labor advisor [@CL-CMP-8503]. Unresolved questions shall be submitted in a timely manner to the Administrator, Wage and Hour Division, for determination [@CL-CMP-8504].

## How it works

Both sections state coverage by category or example. They create no duty on a contractor, no threshold, no deadline, and no filing. They name no one who decides coverage.

The exemptions are stated by contract category. Paragraph (c) covers any contract for transporting freight or personnel by vessel, aircraft, bus, truck, express, railroad, or oil or gas pipeline where published tariff rates are in effect [@CL-CMP-8093]. Paragraph (d) covers a contract for furnishing services by radio, telephone, or cable companies subject to the Communications Act of 1934 [@CL-CMP-8105]. Paragraph (f) covers an employment contract providing for direct services to a Federal agency by an individual or individuals [@CL-CMP-8106].

The examples are illustrative. A service that matches an example is of a type that has been found to be covered, but the list is not definitive or exclusive [@CL-CMP-8095]. Paragraph (k) carries a cross-reference to FAR 22.1003-4(c)(1) and (d)(1)(iv) [@CL-CMP-8111]. Exemptions that the Secretary of Labor has added by administrative action are on [the administrative exemptions card](cmp-scls-exemptions-and-variations.md), and the certification for certain services is on [the services exemption card](cmp-scls-services-exemption.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A company holds three orders. Order 1 is for repair of a public building, including painting. It falls in paragraph (a), and the statute does not apply to it [@CL-CMP-8092]. Order 2 is for public utility services. It falls in paragraph (e), and the statute does not apply to it [@CL-CMP-8094].

Order 3 is for guard services. Guard services appear among the examples of services found to be covered [@CL-CMP-8096]. The examples are not definitive or exclusive [@CL-CMP-8095]. The sections here do not say that every guard services order is covered, so the company also checks the other exemptions listed above and the administrative exemptions on the card linked above.

## Never confuse

A statutory exemption in FAR 22.1003-3 removes a stated category of contract or work from the statute [@CL-CMP-8092; @CL-CMP-8104; @CL-CMP-8093; @CL-CMP-8105; @CL-CMP-8094; @CL-CMP-8106; @CL-CMP-8107]. An example in FAR 22.1003-5 only illustrates a type of service found to be covered, and the list is not definitive or exclusive [@CL-CMP-8095].

Paragraph (b) of FAR 22.1003-3 reaches work required to be done in accordance with title 41 of the United States Code, chapter 65, not every contract [@CL-CMP-8104]. Paragraph (c) reaches transport where published tariff rates are in effect [@CL-CMP-8093].
