## Purpose

Shows the contractor's duties to check drawings and to clear navigable waterways, and how additive or deductive items decide the low offeror and the award.

The Government will provide to the Contractor, without charge, one set of contract drawings and specifications, except publications incorporated into the technical provisions by reference, in electronic or paper media as chosen by the Contracting Officer [@CL-CMP-4710]. The Contractor shall check all drawings furnished immediately upon receipt, compare all drawings and verify the figures before laying out the work, and promptly notify the Contracting Officer of any discrepancies [@CL-CMP-4671]. Among the duties the paragraph lists is to be responsible for any errors that might have been avoided by complying with that paragraph [@CL-CMP-4672]. The Contractor shall also reproduce and print the contract drawings and specifications as needed [@CL-CMP-4773].

In general, large-scale drawings shall govern small-scale drawings, and the Contractor shall follow figures marked on drawings in preference to scale measurements [@CL-CMP-4711]. Omissions from the drawings or specifications, or the misdescription of details of work, that are manifestly necessary to carry out the intent of the drawings and specifications or that are customarily performed shall not relieve the Contractor from performing such omitted or misdescribed details [@CL-CMP-4712], and the Contractor shall perform them as if fully and correctly set forth and described in the drawings and specifications [@CL-CMP-4795]. The work shall conform to the specifications and the contract drawings identified on the index of drawings in the clause [@CL-CMP-4796].

## How it works

For navigable waterways, the Contractor shall promptly recover and remove any material, plant, machinery, or appliance which the contractor loses, dumps, throws overboard, sinks, or misplaces and which, in the opinion of the Contracting Officer, may be dangerous to or obstruct navigation [@CL-CMP-4673]. It shall also give immediate notice, with description and locations of any such obstructions, to the Contracting Officer and, when required by the Contracting Officer, mark or buoy such obstructions until they are removed [@CL-CMP-4674]. The Contracting Officer may remove the obstructions by contract or otherwise should the Contractor refuse, neglect, or delay compliance with the duties in the first paragraph of the clause, and may deduct the cost of removal from any monies due or to become due to the Contractor [@CL-CMP-4713], or recover the cost of removal under the Contractor's bond [@CL-CMP-8623; @CL-CMP-4774]. The Contractor's liability for the removal of a vessel wrecked or sunk without fault or negligence is limited to that provided in sections 15, 19, and 20 of the River and Harbor Act of March 3, 1899 [@CL-CMP-4714].

For additive or deductive items, the Government determines the amount of funds available for the project prior to the opening of bids [@CL-CMP-4715]. The low offeror shall be the Offeror that is otherwise eligible for award and offers the lowest aggregate amount for the first or base bid item, plus or minus, in the order stated in the list of priorities in the bid schedule, those additive or deductive items that provide the most features within the funds determined available [@CL-CMP-8622; @CL-CMP-4675]. If adding another item from the list of priorities would make the award exceed the available funds for all offerors, the Contracting Officer will skip that item and go to the next item from the bid schedule of priorities [@CL-CMP-4676], and it will add that next item if an award may be made that includes it and is within the available funds [@CL-CMP-4775]. All bids are evaluated on the basis of the same additive or deductive items [@CL-CMP-4776]. The Contracting Officer will use the list of priorities only to determine the low offeror, and after determining the low offeror an award may be made on any combination of items if the listed conditions are met [@CL-CMP-4677]. Those conditions are that it is in the best interest of the Government, funds are available at the time of award, and the low offeror's price for the combination to be awarded is less than the price offered by any other responsive, responsible offeror [@CL-CMP-4678].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A drawing shows a dimension that disagrees with the specification. The contractor notifies the Contracting Officer promptly, before laying out the work. A detail that the drawings omit but that is customarily performed still has to be built as if the drawings showed it, and the finished work has to match the drawings on the index. In a bid, the Government determined before opening that 50,000 is available. Both bids are evaluated on the same additive items. Bidder A's base bid is 40,000 with additive items of 6,000, 8,000 and 3,000 in priority order; bidder B's base bid is 38,000 with additive items of 9,000, 7,000 and 2,500. With the first item, A totals 46,000 and B totals 47,000, both within 50,000. Adding the second item would bring A to 54,000 and B to 54,000, which exceeds 50,000 for all offerors, so the second item is skipped for both. The third item fits for both, bringing A to 49,000 and B to 49,500, so A is the low offeror. Once A is the low offeror, the Contracting Officer may still award a different combination if it is in the best interest of the Government, funds are available at award, and the price for that combination is lower than any other responsive, responsible offeror's.

## Never confuse

The list of priorities in the bid schedule is not the award rule: the Contracting Officer will use the list of priorities in the bid schedule only to determine the low offeror [@CL-CMP-4677].
