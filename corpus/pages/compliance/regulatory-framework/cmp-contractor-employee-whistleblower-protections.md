## Purpose

Shows the reprisal a contractor or subcontractor may not take against an employee who makes a protected disclosure, and the remedies and exposure that follow.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-2331] |
| Codified citation | FAR 3.903 and FAR 3.905-1 [@CL-CMP-2331; @CL-CMP-2332] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-2331] |
| Applies to | Contractors and subcontractors and their employees, except under contracts of DoD, NASA, the Coast Guard or an element of the intelligence community [@CL-CMP-2331; @CL-CMP-2526] |

Contractors and subcontractors are prohibited from discharging, demoting, or otherwise discriminating against an employee as a reprisal for disclosing, to any of the entities listed in the section, information of the listed kinds that the employee reasonably believes is within those kinds [@CL-CMP-2331]. The section lists the entities and the kinds of information; this card does not restate them. These FAR sections, 3.900 through 3.906, do not apply to DoD, NASA, the Coast Guard, or any element of the intelligence community [@CL-CMP-2526], nor to the listed disclosures by employees of intelligence community contractors [@CL-CMP-2528; @CL-CMP-2529]. DoD, NASA and Coast Guard contractor employees fall under a separate statutory program that this subpart does not implement, so this card's agency process and remedies are not the ones for those contracts [@CL-CMP-2527]. The rights and remedies provided for in section 4712 of title 41 of the United States Code may not be waived by any agreement, policy, form, or condition of employment [@CL-CMP-2337].

## How it works

Not later than 30 days after receiving a report under the cited reporting section, the head of the agency shall determine whether sufficient basis exists to conclude that the contractor or subcontractor has subjected the employee who submitted the complaint to a reprisal as prohibited by the policy section [@CL-CMP-2332]. The head of the agency then either issues an order denying relief or takes one or more listed actions, the first being an order to take affirmative action to abate the reprisal [@CL-CMP-2333]. Another listed action is reinstatement, together with compensatory damages (including back pay), employment benefits, and other terms and conditions of employment [@CL-CMP-2334]. Another is payment of the complainant's costs and expenses, including attorneys' fees and expert witnesses' fees, reasonably incurred in bringing the complaint [@CL-CMP-2335]. If either of two alternative conditions in the paragraph on the right to go to court is met, one being an order denying relief by the head of the agency [@CL-CMP-2464], the complainant is deemed to have exhausted all administrative remedies and may bring a de novo action at law or equity against the contractor [@CL-CMP-2336]. Whether payments made under these remedies are allowable costs is a cost-principle question that this card does not address.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor demotes an employee soon after the employee reports a suspected billing problem to the agency's Inspector General. The agency head finds sufficient basis for reprisal and orders reinstatement with back pay and payment of the employee's legal fees. The employment agreement cannot waive these rights.

## Never confuse

The reprisal ban protects an employee who discloses to the listed entities. A contractor principal's duty to disclose credible evidence of violations to the Government is a separate rule; see [Contractor code of business ethics](cmp-contractor-code-of-business-ethics.md). The Recovery Act protections are tied to the covered funds they define; see [Whistleblower protections under the Recovery Act: coverage, file access and relief](cmp-recovery-act-whistleblower-protections.md).
