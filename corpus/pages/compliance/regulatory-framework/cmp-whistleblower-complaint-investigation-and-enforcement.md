## Purpose

Shows how a contractor employee reprisal complaint is investigated, enforced and reviewed, and how a Recovery Act complaint is filed.

## The rule

| Codified citation | FAR 3.904-2, 3.905-2, 3.907-3 and 3.907-4 [@CL-CMP-6855; @CL-CMP-6858; @CL-CMP-6860; @CL-CMP-9718] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |

| Applies to | Contractors and subcontractors, except that the investigation and enforcement sections do not apply to DoD, NASA, the Coast Guard or an element of the intelligence community; and, for the Recovery Act complaint procedure, contracts funded in whole or in part by that Act [@CL-CMP-7140; @CL-CMP-7143] |

The investigation and enforcement sections are among the sections that implement the contractor employee whistleblower statute, and those sections do not apply to DoD, NASA, the Coast Guard, or any element of the intelligence community [@CL-CMP-7140]. They also do not apply to a disclosure by an employee of an intelligence community contractor or subcontractor if the disclosure meets either of two listed conditions [@CL-CMP-7141]. The conditions are that the disclosure relates to an activity of an element of the intelligence community, or was discovered during contract or subcontract services provided to such an element [@CL-CMP-7142]. The Recovery Act complaint section applies to all contracts funded in whole or in part by the Recovery Act [@CL-CMP-7143].

Investigation of complaints will be in accordance with the whistleblower statute [@CL-CMP-6855]. Upon completion of the investigation, the head of the agency must ensure that the Inspector General has provided the report of findings to the head of the agency [@CL-CMP-7144], to the complainant and any person acting on the complainant's behalf, and to the contractor and/or subcontractor alleged to have committed the violation [@CL-CMP-6856].

## How it works

### Response and agency action

The complainant, contractor, and/or subcontractor must be afforded the opportunity to submit a written response to the report of findings to the head of the agency and the Office of Inspector General, in a time and manner that permits the agency head to take action not later than 30 days after receiving the report [@CL-CMP-6857]. Not later than 30 days after receiving the report, the head of the agency must determine whether sufficient basis exists to conclude that the contractor or subcontractor has subjected the employee who submitted the complaint to a reprisal as prohibited by 3.903 [@CL-CMP-7145]. The head must also either issue an order denying relief or take one or more listed actions, the first being an order to take affirmative action to abate the reprisal [@CL-CMP-7146]. The linked cards cover the other listed remedies.

### Enforcement and review

Whenever a contractor or subcontractor fails to comply with an order issued under the remedies section, the head of the agency concerned must file an action for enforcement of the order in the United States district court for a district in which the reprisal was found to have occurred [@CL-CMP-7147]. In an action brought under that authority, the court may grant appropriate relief, including injunctive relief, compensatory and exemplary damages, and attorney fees and costs [@CL-CMP-6858]. The complainant employee upon whose behalf the order was issued may also file such an action or join in the agency head's action [@CL-CMP-7148]. Any person adversely affected or aggrieved by an order issued under the remedies section may obtain review of the order's conformance with the statute and its regulations in the United States court of appeals for a circuit in which the reprisal is alleged in the order to have occurred [@CL-CMP-7149]. No petition may be filed more than 60 days after the agency head issues the order, and filing such an appeal shall not act to stay the enforcement of the order of the head of an agency, unless a stay is specifically entered by the court [@CL-CMP-6859].

### Recovery Act complaints

An employee who believes that he or she has been subjected to a reprisal prohibited by the Recovery Act may submit a complaint to the Inspector General of the agency that awarded the contract [@CL-CMP-6860]. The complaint must be signed and must contain the contractor's name and the contract number, if known; if not, a description reasonably sufficient to identify the contract(s) involved [@CL-CMP-6861]. It must also contain the covered information giving rise to the disclosure, the nature of the disclosure, and the specific nature and date of the reprisal [@CL-CMP-7150]. A contracting officer who receives such a complaint must forward it to the Office of Inspector General and to other designated officials in accordance with agency procedures [@CL-CMP-7151].

Investigation of complaints under the Recovery Act whistleblower subpart will be in accordance with section 1553 of the Recovery Act [@CL-CMP-9718].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An employee of a contractor on a civilian-agency contract reports a compliance concern and is later demoted. The Inspector General's report of findings goes to the agency head, the employee and the contractor [@CL-CMP-7144; @CL-CMP-6856], and each may respond in writing in time for the agency head to act within 30 days [@CL-CMP-6857]. The agency head orders the contractor to abate the reprisal [@CL-CMP-7146]. The contractor does not comply, so the agency head files an enforcement action in the district court for the district where the reprisal was found to have occurred [@CL-CMP-7147]. The contractor, as a person aggrieved by the order, files a petition in the court of appeals [@CL-CMP-7149] 45 days after the order issues, within the 60-day limit [@CL-CMP-6859], and the order stays enforceable unless the court enters a stay [@CL-CMP-6859].

In a separate matter, an employee at a firm whose contract is funded in whole or in part by the Recovery Act [@CL-CMP-7143] signs a complaint that names the contractor, gives the contract number, and describes the disclosure and the nature and date of the reprisal [@CL-CMP-6861; @CL-CMP-7150]. The employee may submit it to the Inspector General of the awarding agency [@CL-CMP-6860]. If it reaches a contracting officer instead, the officer forwards it to the Office of Inspector General and other designated officials as agency procedures require [@CL-CMP-7151].

## Never confuse

A reprisal complaint is not a disclosure of wrongdoing: it alleges retaliation for a protected disclosure. A Recovery Act complaint goes to the Inspector General of the awarding agency, and a contracting officer who receives one forwards it [@CL-CMP-6860; @CL-CMP-7151]. The agency head's action period runs from receipt of the Inspector General's report, not from the filing of the complaint [@CL-CMP-7145]. The protections themselves are in [Whistleblower protections](../regulatory-framework/cmp-whistleblower-protections.md), [Contractor employee whistleblower protections](../regulatory-framework/cmp-contractor-employee-whistleblower-protections.md) and [Recovery Act whistleblower protections](../regulatory-framework/cmp-recovery-act-whistleblower-protections.md).
