## Purpose

Sets out what the service contracts inventory subpart implements, which agencies it applies to, and how it defines the subcontracts that count as first-tier [@CL-CMP-8308; @CL-CMP-8309; @CL-CMP-8312].

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-8308] |
| Codified citation | FAR 4.1700 and 4.1701 [@CL-CMP-8308; @CL-CMP-8311] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-8308] |
| Applies to | Executive agencies covered by the Federal Activities Inventory Reform Act, other than the Department of Defense [@CL-CMP-8309] |

The subpart implements section 743(a) of Division C of the Consolidated Appropriations Act, 2010, which requires agencies to report annually to the Office of Management and Budget on activities performed by service contractors [@CL-CMP-8308]. That section applies to executive agencies covered by the Federal Activities Inventory Reform Act, other than the Department of Defense [@CL-CMP-8309]. The information reported in the inventory will be publicly accessible [@CL-CMP-8310].

The definitions in section 4.1701 apply as used in this subpart [@CL-CMP-8594]. FAIR Act agencies are the agencies required under the FAIR Act to submit inventories annually of the activities performed by Government personnel [@CL-CMP-8311]. A first-tier subcontract is a subcontract awarded directly by the contractor for the purpose of acquiring supplies or services, including construction, for performance of a prime contract [@CL-CMP-8312]. The definition does not include the contractor's supplier agreements with vendors, such as long-term arrangements for materials or supplies that benefit multiple contracts and/or the costs of which are normally applied to general and administrative expenses or indirect costs [@CL-CMP-8313].

## How it works

The reporting duty in the scope section falls on agencies: they report annually to the Office of Management and Budget on activities performed by service contractors [@CL-CMP-8308]. Neither section places a duty on a contractor, and neither names a threshold, deadline or approving official. The contractor's own reporting is in [Service contract reporting requirements](cmp-service-contract-reporting.md), [Service contract reporting on indefinite-delivery contracts](cmp-service-contract-reporting-indefinite-delivery.md) and [Service contracts inventory definition and the reporting clause prescriptions](cmp-dfars-service-contracts-inventory-clauses.md). The scope section reaches executive agencies covered by the FAIR Act, and it excludes the Department of Defense [@CL-CMP-8309]. The inventory information will be publicly accessible [@CL-CMP-8310].

For counting subcontracts, a subcontract awarded directly by the contractor to acquire supplies or services for performance of a prime contract is a first-tier subcontract [@CL-CMP-8312]. A supplier agreement with a vendor, such as a long-term arrangement for materials or supplies that benefit multiple contracts and/or the costs of which are normally applied to general and administrative expenses or indirect costs, is not [@CL-CMP-8313].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An executive agency other than the Department of Defense that is covered by the FAIR Act reports annually to the Office of Management and Budget on the activities its service contractors performed, and the information reported in the inventory will be publicly accessible [@CL-CMP-8308; @CL-CMP-8309; @CL-CMP-8310]. A contractor under one of that agency's contracts awards a subcontract directly to a firm to staff a help desk for performance of the prime contract, and that award is a first-tier subcontract [@CL-CMP-8312]. The same contractor holds a long-term supply agreement for materials with one vendor, covering 3 of its contracts. That agreement is a supplier agreement and is not counted as a first-tier subcontract [@CL-CMP-8313].

## Never confuse

A first-tier subcontract is not a supplier agreement: the first is a direct award to perform a prime contract, while the second is a vendor arrangement, such as a long-term arrangement for materials or supplies that benefit multiple contracts and/or the costs of which are normally applied to general and administrative expenses or indirect costs [@CL-CMP-8312; @CL-CMP-8313].
