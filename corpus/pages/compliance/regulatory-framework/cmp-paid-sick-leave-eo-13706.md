## Purpose

Shows who is covered by the paid sick leave clause, the accrual rate and the further duties it adds, the records it requires, the protections against interference and retaliation, and the consequences of noncompliance.

## The rule

| Codified citation | FAR 52.222-62 [@CL-CMP-5910] |

The contract is subject to Executive Order 13706 and the regulations the Secretary of Labor issued in 29 CFR part 13 under it [@CL-CMP-6398]. If the contract is not performed wholly within the United States, the clause applies only to the part of the contract performed within the United States [@CL-CMP-6399]. In this clause, United States means the 50 States and the District of Columbia [@CL-CMP-6573].

The contractor must permit each employee engaged in performing work on or in connection with the contract to earn not less than 1 hour of paid sick leave for every 30 hours worked [@CL-CMP-5910]. An employee under the clause is any person engaged in performing work on or in connection with a contract covered by Executive Order 13706 and whose wages under the contract are governed by the Service Contract Labor Standards statute, the Wage Rate Requirements (Construction) statute, or the Fair Labor Standards Act [@CL-CMP-6396; @CL-CMP-6397]. The definition includes employees who qualify for an exemption from the Fair Labor Standards Act's minimum wage and overtime provisions [@CL-CMP-6397]. The definition applies regardless of the contractual relationship alleged to exist between the individual and the employer [@CL-CMP-6576]. It also includes any person performing work on or in connection with the contract and individually registered in a bona fide apprenticeship or training program registered with the Department of Labor's Employment and Training Administration, Office of Apprenticeship, or with a State Apprenticeship Agency recognized by the Office of Apprenticeship [@CL-CMP-6574; @CL-CMP-6575]. An employee performs on a contract if the employee directly performs the specific services called for by the contract [@CL-CMP-6577]. An employee performs in connection with a contract if the employee's work activities are necessary to the performance of the contract but are not the specific services called for by the contract [@CL-CMP-6578]. Paid sick leave means compensated absence from employment that is required by Executive Order 13706 and 29 CFR part 13 [@CL-CMP-6580]. Child, domestic partner and domestic violence have the meaning given in 29 CFR 13.2 [@CL-CMP-6581], as do parent, sexual assault, spouse and stalking [@CL-CMP-6582]. An individual related by blood or affinity whose close association with the employee is the equivalent of a family relationship also has the meaning given in 29 CFR 13.2 [@CL-CMP-6583].

The contractor must allow accrual and use of paid sick leave as required by Executive Order 13706 and 29 CFR part 13 [@CL-CMP-5911]. It must also comply with the accrual, use and other requirements in 29 CFR 13.5 and 13.6, which the contract incorporates by reference [@CL-CMP-6400]. The recordkeeping paragraph lists any financial payment made for unused paid sick leave upon a separation from employment intended, pursuant to 29 CFR 13.5(b)(5), to relieve the Contractor from the obligation to reinstate such paid sick leave as otherwise required by 29 CFR 13.5(b)(4) [@CL-CMP-6587].

## How it works

The contractor must provide paid sick leave to all employees when due free and clear and without subsequent deduction (except as otherwise provided by 29 CFR 13.24), rebate, or kickback on any account [@CL-CMP-6401]. Pay and benefits for paid sick leave used are provided no later than one pay period following the end of the regular pay period in which the leave was taken [@CL-CMP-5912]. The paid sick leave required by Executive Order 13706, 29 CFR part 13 and the clause is in addition to the contractor's obligations under the Service Contract Labor Standards statute and the Wage Rate Requirements (Construction) statute [@CL-CMP-6584]. The contractor may not receive credit toward its prevailing wage or fringe benefit obligations under the Service Contract Labor Standards and Wage Rate Requirements (Construction) Acts for any paid sick leave provided in satisfaction of Executive Order 13706 and 29 CFR part 13 [@CL-CMP-6410].

The contractor is responsible for the compliance by any subcontractor with the requirements of Executive Order 13706, 29 CFR part 13 and the clause [@CL-CMP-6402]. It must insert the substance of the clause, including the subcontracts paragraph, in all subcontracts, regardless of dollar value, that are subject to the Service Contract Labor Standards statute or the Wage Rate Requirements (Construction) statute and are to be performed in whole or in part in the United States [@CL-CMP-6435]. Contractors may fulfill their paid sick leave obligations jointly with other contractors through a multiemployer plan, or through an individual fund, plan or program [@CL-CMP-6403]. A multiemployer plan is a plan to which more than one employer is required to contribute and which is maintained pursuant to one or more collective bargaining agreements between one or more employee organizations and more than one employer [@CL-CMP-6579].

The contractor must notify all employees performing work on or in connection with a contract covered by the order of the paid sick leave requirements by posting a notice provided by the Department of Labor in a prominent and accessible place at the worksite [@CL-CMP-6433]. Contractors that customarily post notices to employees electronically may post the notice electronically, provided the electronic posting is displayed prominently on any Web site maintained by the contractor, whether external or internal [@CL-CMP-6599]. The Web site on which the electronic notice is posted is one customarily used for notices to employees about terms and conditions of employment [@CL-CMP-6600]. Disputes related to the application of Executive Order 13706 to the contract are not subject to the general disputes clause and are resolved under the Department of Labor procedures in 29 CFR part 13 [@CL-CMP-6434]. Disputes within the meaning of the clause include disputes between the contractor, or any of its subcontractors, and the contracting agency, the Department of Labor, or the employees or their representatives [@CL-CMP-6601]. Nothing in Executive Order 13706 or 29 CFR part 13 excuses noncompliance with or supersedes any applicable Federal or State law, any applicable law or municipal ordinance, or a collective bargaining agreement requiring greater paid sick leave or leave rights [@CL-CMP-6585], greater meaning greater than those established under the order and the regulation [@CL-CMP-6586].

The contractor must not in any manner interfere with an employee's accrual or use of paid sick leave as required by Executive Order 13706 or 29 CFR part 13, and interference includes, but is not limited to, miscalculating the amount of paid sick leave an employee has accrued [@CL-CMP-6428]. Interference also includes denying or unreasonably delaying a response to a proper request to use paid sick leave, discouraging an employee from using paid sick leave, and reducing an employee's accrued paid sick leave by more than the amount of such leave used [@CL-CMP-6429]. Interference also includes transferring an employee to work on contracts not covered by the order to prevent the accrual or use of paid sick leave [@CL-CMP-6596], disclosing confidential information contained in certification or other documentation provided to verify the need to use paid sick leave [@CL-CMP-6597], and making the use of paid sick leave contingent on the employee's finding a replacement worker or the fulfillment of the contractor's operational needs [@CL-CMP-6598]. The contractor must not discharge or in any other manner discriminate against any employee for using, or attempting to use, paid sick leave as provided for under Executive Order 13706 and 29 CFR part 13 [@CL-CMP-6430]. It must also not do so for filing any complaint, initiating any proceeding, or otherwise asserting any right or claim under the order and the regulation [@CL-CMP-6431], or for cooperating in any investigation or testifying in any proceeding, or for informing any other person about the employee's rights, under the order and the regulation [@CL-CMP-6432].

The contractor must make and maintain, for no less than three years from completion of the work on the contract, records containing the listed information for each employee [@CL-CMP-5913]. It must make those records available upon request for inspection, copying and transcription by authorized representatives of the Administrator of the Wage and Hour Division of the Department of Labor [@CL-CMP-6411]. The records the clause lists include each employee's name, address and social security number, occupation or classification, rate or rates of wages paid including all pay and benefits provided, and number of daily and weekly hours worked [@CL-CMP-6412]. They include any deductions made, the total wages paid each pay period including all pay and benefits provided, and a copy of notifications to employees of the amount of paid sick leave accrued, as 29 CFR 13.5(a)(2) requires [@CL-CMP-6413]. They include a copy of employees' requests to use paid sick leave if in writing or, if not in writing, any other records reflecting such requests [@CL-CMP-6414]. They include the dates and amounts of paid sick leave taken, and unless the contractor's paid time off policy satisfies the requirements of Executive Order 13706 and 29 CFR part 13 as 29 CFR 13.5(f)(5) describes, that leave is designated in the records as paid sick leave [@CL-CMP-6415]. They include a copy of any written responses to employees' requests to use paid sick leave, including explanations for any denials, as 29 CFR 13.5(d)(3) requires [@CL-CMP-6416], and any records reflecting the certification and documentation the contractor may require an employee to provide under 29 CFR 13.5(e), including copies of any certification or documentation an employee provided [@CL-CMP-6417]. They also include any other records showing tracking of or calculations related to an employee's accrual or use of paid sick leave, the relevant contract, and the regular pay and benefits provided to an employee for each use of paid sick leave [@CL-CMP-6418]. They also include any financial payment made for unused paid sick leave upon a separation from employment intended, under 29 CFR 13.5(b)(5), to relieve the contractor from the obligation to reinstate such leave otherwise required by 29 CFR 13.5(b)(4) [@CL-CMP-6587].

A contractor that wishes to distinguish between an employee's covered and noncovered work must keep records or other proof reflecting such distinctions [@CL-CMP-6419]. On that route, only if the contractor adequately segregates the employee's time is time spent on noncovered work excluded from hours worked counted toward the accrual of paid sick leave [@CL-CMP-6420]. On that route, only if the contractor adequately segregates the employee's time may it properly refuse an employee's request to use paid sick leave on the ground that the employee was scheduled to perform noncovered work during the time the employee asked to use it [@CL-CMP-6588]. A contractor that estimates covered hours worked by an employee who performs work in connection with covered contracts under 29 CFR 13.5(a)(1)(i) or (iii) must keep records or other proof of the verifiable information on which the estimates are reasonably based [@CL-CMP-6421]. Only if the contractor relies on an estimate that is reasonable and based on verifiable information is an employee's time spent in connection with noncovered work excluded from hours worked counted toward the accrual of paid sick leave [@CL-CMP-6422]. If the contractor estimates the amount of time an employee spends performing in connection with covered contracts, it must permit the employee to use paid sick leave during any work time for the contractor [@CL-CMP-6423]. The exception for hours-worked records applies where the Service Contract Labor Standards statute, the Wage Rate Requirements (Construction) statute and the Fair Labor Standards Act do not obligate the contractor to keep records of an employee's hours worked and the contractor chooses to use the assumption permitted by 29 CFR 13.5(a)(1)(iii) [@CL-CMP-6589; @CL-CMP-6591]. One example the clause gives is an employee who is exempt from the Fair Labor Standards Act's minimum wage and overtime requirements [@CL-CMP-6590]. A contractor that chooses to use the assumption permitted by 29 CFR 13.5(a)(1)(iii) is excused from the requirement in paragraph (i)(1)(iv) of the clause and 29 CFR 13.25(a)(4) to keep records of the employee's number of daily and weekly hours worked [@CL-CMP-6591].

The paragraph on confidential records addresses records relating to medical histories or domestic violence, sexual assault or stalking, created for purposes of Executive Order 13706, whether of an employee or of the employee's child, parent, spouse, domestic partner or other individual related by blood or affinity whose close association with the employee is the equivalent of a family relationship [@CL-CMP-6424; @CL-CMP-6583]. Those records must be maintained as confidential records in separate files from the usual personnel files [@CL-CMP-6425]. The next confidentiality requirement applies if the confidentiality requirements of the Genetic Information Nondiscrimination Act of 2008, section 503 of the Rehabilitation Act of 1973, or the Americans with Disabilities Act apply to records or documents created to comply with the recordkeeping requirements [@CL-CMP-6592]. Those records and documents must also be maintained in compliance with the confidentiality requirements of those laws as described in 29 CFR 1635.9, 41 CFR 60-741.23(d) and 29 CFR 1630.14(c)(1), respectively [@CL-CMP-6593]. The contractor must not disclose any documentation used to verify the need to use 3 or more consecutive days of paid sick leave for the purposes listed in 29 CFR 13.5(c)(1)(iv) [@CL-CMP-5914]. It must also maintain confidentiality about any domestic abuse, sexual assault or stalking, unless the employee consents or when disclosure is required by law [@CL-CMP-6426]. It must permit authorized representatives of the Wage and Hour Division to conduct interviews with employees at the worksite during normal working hours [@CL-CMP-6427]. Nothing in the clause limits or otherwise modifies the contractor's recordkeeping obligations, if any, under the Service Contract Labor Standards statute, the Wage Rate Requirements (Construction) statute or the Fair Labor Standards Act [@CL-CMP-6594]. That no-limitation statement also names the Family and Medical Leave Act, Executive Order 14026, the respective implementing regulations and any other applicable law [@CL-CMP-6595].

On a failure to comply with Executive Order 13706, 29 CFR part 13 or the clause, the contracting agency may, on its own action or after authorization or by direction of the Department of Labor and written notification to the contractor, take action [@CL-CMP-6407]. That action is to cause suspension of any further payment, advance or guarantee of funds until the violations have ceased [@CL-CMP-6408]. A failure to comply with the requirements of the clause may be grounds for termination for default or cause [@CL-CMP-5915]. A breach of the clause may be grounds for debarment as a contractor and subcontractor as provided in 29 CFR 13.52 [@CL-CMP-6409]. The Contracting Officer will, on the officer's own action or on written request of an authorized representative of the Department of Labor, withhold or cause to be withheld from the contractor under this or any other Federal contract with the same contractor [@CL-CMP-6404]. The amount withheld is so much of the accrued payments or advances as may be considered necessary to pay employees the full amount owed to compensate for any violation of Executive Order 13706, 29 CFR part 13 or the clause, including the items the clause lists [@CL-CMP-6405]. Those items are any pay or benefits denied or lost by reason of the violation, other actual monetary losses sustained as a direct result of the violation, and liquidated damages [@CL-CMP-6406].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text from February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. RFO FAR 52.222-62 says the same in substance as the codified clause on the accrual rate, with the lead-in to the list of duties printing "must" where the codified text prints "shall" [@CL-CMP-5910] [@CL-CMP-1699]. It says the same in substance on the part of the contract performed within the United States, the meaning of United States and the part of the employee definition on wages governed by the Service Contract Labor Standards statute, the Wage Rate Requirements (Construction) statute or the Fair Labor Standards Act [@CL-CMP-6399] [@CL-CMP-1695] [@CL-CMP-6573] [@CL-CMP-1696] [@CL-CMP-6396] [@CL-CMP-1697] [@CL-CMP-6397] [@CL-CMP-1698]. It also says the same in substance on the incorporated accrual and use requirements and the one-pay-period timing of pay and benefits [@CL-CMP-6400] [@CL-CMP-1736] [@CL-CMP-5912] [@CL-CMP-1737], and on paid sick leave being in addition to, and earning no credit toward, the prevailing wage and fringe benefit obligations [@CL-CMP-6584] [@CL-CMP-1738] [@CL-CMP-6410] [@CL-CMP-1739]. The prohibition on interference with accrual or use, the prohibition on discharge or discrimination for using or attempting to use paid sick leave, and the three-year recordkeeping period are also the same in substance [@CL-CMP-6428] [@CL-CMP-1743] [@CL-CMP-6430] [@CL-CMP-1744] [@CL-CMP-5913] [@CL-CMP-1745]. The payment suspension and subcontract paragraphs match too [@CL-CMP-6407] [@CL-CMP-1741] [@CL-CMP-6408] [@CL-CMP-1742] [@CL-CMP-6435] [@CL-CMP-1746]. In the withholding paragraph, the codified text says the Contracting Officer "will" withhold and the RFO text says the Contracting Officer "must" withhold [@CL-CMP-6404] [@CL-CMP-1740], and the amount withheld and the items it includes say the same in substance [@CL-CMP-6405] [@CL-CMP-9285] [@CL-CMP-6406] [@CL-CMP-9286]. Elsewhere the RFO text prints "must" where the codified text prints "shall", as in the recordkeeping paragraph [@CL-CMP-5913] [@CL-CMP-1745].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contract is performed partly in the United States and partly abroad, so the clause applies only to the part performed in the United States. An employee works 240 hours on the covered part in a quarter. At 1 hour for every 30 hours, the employee earns at least 8 hours of paid sick leave, because 240 divided by 30 is 8. A second employee works 200 hours in the quarter, of which 90 are covered work. Because the contractor adequately segregates the employee's time with records, the 110 noncovered hours are excluded and the 90 covered hours accrue 3 hours, because 90 divided by 30 is 3. Without records that adequately segregate the time, and absent a reasonable estimate based on verifiable information, the noncovered hours would not be excluded. The first employee uses 4 hours of leave in a two-week pay period; the contractor must provide pay and benefits for that leave no later than one pay period following the end of that two-week pay period. If the contractor lowers that employee's accrued balance by 6 hours when only 4 were used, it has reduced the balance by more than the amount used, which is interference. If the contractor unreasonably delays its response to a proper request to use leave, that is interference, and if it discharges an employee for filing a complaint under the order, that is discrimination the clause prohibits. If it moves an employee onto work under contracts not covered by the order to stop the leave from accruing, that is also interference, and so is making leave depend on the employee finding a replacement worker. An employee who is individually registered in a bona fide apprenticeship program registered with the Department of Labor and works on the contract is within the employee definition. The contractor keeps the records, including written requests to use leave or other records reflecting oral requests, for three years from completion of the work on the contract. If it pays an employee for unused leave on separation in order to relieve itself of the obligation to reinstate that leave, the record includes that payment. For an employee exempt from the Fair Labor Standards Act minimum wage and overtime requirements, for whom no statute obliges it to record hours worked, a contractor that uses the assumption permitted by 29 CFR 13.5(a)(1)(iii) is excused from recording that employee's daily and weekly hours. If it fails to comply, the contracting agency may, after written notification to the contractor, suspend further payment until the violation ceases, and amounts withheld may include liquidated damages.

## Never confuse

The clause sets a floor of 1 hour of paid sick leave for every 30 hours worked [@CL-CMP-5910] and requires the contractor to comply with the accrual, use and other requirements in the incorporated regulations [@CL-CMP-6400]. Paid sick leave under the clause is in addition to the contractor's obligations under the Service Contract Labor Standards and Wage Rate Requirements (Construction) statutes, and it earns no credit toward the prevailing wage or fringe benefit obligations under those Acts [@CL-CMP-6584; @CL-CMP-6410].

The codified withholding paragraph says the Contracting Officer "will" withhold from the contractor, while RFO FAR 52.222-62(e) says the Contracting Officer "must" withhold [@CL-CMP-6404] [@CL-CMP-1740].
