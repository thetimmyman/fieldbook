## Purpose

Shows the trafficking in persons prohibition, when an offeror must certify a compliance plan and its threshold, and the remedies after a violation is substantiated.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-2394] |
| Codified citation | FAR 22.1700, FAR 22.1701, FAR 22.1703, FAR 22.1704 and FAR 52.222-50 [@CL-CMP-9471; @CL-CMP-9526; @CL-CMP-9527; @CL-CMP-9528; @CL-CMP-2394; @CL-CMP-2398; @CL-CMP-2495] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.222-50 [@CL-CMP-6725; @CL-CMP-6876; @CL-CMP-6877] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-2394] |
| Applies to | Government solicitations and contracts, with the compliance plan certification applying where a portion meets the listed conditions [@CL-CMP-2394; @CL-CMP-2395; @CL-CMP-2396] |

The subpart prescribes policy for implementing chapter 78 of title 22 of the United States Code and Executive Order 13627, Strengthening Protections Against Trafficking in Persons in Federal Contracts, dated September 25, 2012 [@CL-CMP-9471]. The subpart applies to all acquisitions [@CL-CMP-9526]. The applicability section also says the requirement at 22.1703(c) for a certification and compliance plan applies only to any portion of a contract or subcontract that is for supplies, other than commercially available off-the-shelf (COTS) items, to be acquired outside the United States, or for services to be performed outside the United States, and that has an estimated value that exceeds $700,000 [@CL-CMP-9527; @CL-CMP-9528].

Government solicitations and contracts shall prohibit contractors, contractor employees, subcontractors, subcontractor employees, and their agents from engaging in severe forms of trafficking in persons during the period of performance of the contract [@CL-CMP-2394]. The same list names further prohibited activities, and this card drafts only the first.

One listed item requires the apparent successful offeror to provide, before contract award, a certification that the offeror has a compliance plan if any portion of the contract or subcontract meets the conditions listed beneath it [@CL-CMP-2395]. The conditions are that the portion is for supplies, other than COTS items, to be acquired outside the United States, or services to be performed outside the United States, and that the estimated value exceeds $700,000 [@CL-CMP-2395; @CL-CMP-2396]. The printed section adds employee notification, annual and subcontractor certifications, a plan-content standard and disclosure and cooperation duties, and this card does not draft them.

Government solicitations and contracts shall provide suitable remedies, including termination, to be imposed on contractors that fail to comply with the requirements of the listed paragraphs on prohibitions, notification, certification and cooperation [@CL-CMP-2397]. After a final determination in accordance with paragraph (c)(2)(ii) of the section that the allegations of a trafficking in persons violation are substantiated, the contracting officer shall enter the violation in FAPIIS [@CL-CMP-2398]. A further listed step is to consider taking any of the remedies specified in paragraph (e) of the clause on combating trafficking in persons, which are in addition to any other remedies available to the United States Government [@CL-CMP-2399].

The clause provides that, in addition to other remedies available to the Government, the contractor's failure to comply with the requirements of paragraphs (c), (d), (g), (h), or (i) of the clause may result in requiring the contractor to remove a contractor employee or employees from the performance of the contract [@CL-CMP-2495]. The clause's list of remedies for that failure also includes requiring the contractor to terminate a subcontract, suspension of contract payments until the contractor has taken appropriate remedial action, and loss of award fee, consistent with the award fee plan, for the performance period in which the Government determined contractor non-compliance [@CL-CMP-2495; @CL-CMP-2496]. It also includes declining to exercise available options under the contract, termination of the contract for default or cause in accordance with the termination clause of the contract, and suspension or debarment [@CL-CMP-2495; @CL-CMP-2497].

## How it works

The policy prohibits the listed conduct and requires remedies for failure to comply [@CL-CMP-2394; @CL-CMP-2397]. A compliance plan certification is owed before award where a portion of the contract or subcontract meets the listed conditions, including the value threshold [@CL-CMP-2395; @CL-CMP-2396]. After a final determination that a violation is substantiated, the contracting officer records it in FAPIIS and considers the clause remedies [@CL-CMP-2398; @CL-CMP-2399]. Upon receipt of a report from the agency Inspector General that provides support for the allegations, the head of the executive agency, in accordance with agency procedures, must delegate to an authorized agency official, such as the agency suspending and debarring official, the responsibility to expeditiously conduct an administrative proceeding allowing the contractor the opportunity to respond to the report, to make a final determination as to whether the allegations are substantiated, and to notify the contracting officer of the determination [@CL-CMP-8714] [@CL-CMP-2494] [@CL-CMP-8715].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text under Class Deviation 2026-O0038, effective February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. RFO FAR 52.222-50(e) keeps the rule that, in addition to other remedies available to the Government, the contractor's failure to comply with the requirements of paragraphs (c), (d), (g), (h), or (i) of the clause may result in requiring the contractor to remove a contractor employee or employees from the performance of the contract [@CL-CMP-6725; @CL-CMP-2495]. It keeps the further listed remedies of requiring the contractor to terminate a subcontract, suspension of contract payments until the contractor has taken appropriate remedial action, and loss of award fee, consistent with the award fee plan, for the performance period in which the Government determined contractor non-compliance [@CL-CMP-6876; @CL-CMP-2496]. It also keeps declining to exercise available options under the contract, termination of the contract for default or cause in accordance with the termination clause of the contract, and suspension or debarment [@CL-CMP-6877; @CL-CMP-2497].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contract includes services to be performed outside the United States with an estimated value of 2,000,000. The answer: the portion is for services outside the United States and its value is above the threshold, so the offeror must certify a compliance plan before award. If the estimated value were 500,000, the value condition would not be met.

## Never confuse

The certification is not owed on every contract, because it depends on the listed conditions [@CL-CMP-2395; @CL-CMP-2396]. The prohibition applies during the period of performance of the contract [@CL-CMP-2394], while the certification is a pre-award item [@CL-CMP-2395]. Debarment and suspension have their own pages, such as [Causes for debarment](../../supply-chain/supplier-identity/sup-causes-for-debarment.md), and are not restated here.
