## Purpose

Shows the equal opportunity duties a contractor carries on a covered Federal contract, from posting and advertising through pay transparency, reporting, access to records and the sanctions that follow noncompliance.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-5866] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.222-26 [@CL-CMP-5866] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.222-26 prints as [Reserved] [@CL-CMP-1119] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-5866] |
| Applies to | Contractors holding nonexempt Federal contracts or subcontracts above the coverage threshold [@CL-CMP-5866] |

The clause reaches a contractor that, within any 12-month period including the 12 months before award, has been or is awarded nonexempt Federal contracts or subcontracts whose aggregate value exceeds $10,000 [@CL-CMP-5866]. It does not cover work performed outside the United States by employees who were not recruited within the United States [@CL-CMP-6350]. In this clause, United States means the 50 States, the District of Columbia, Puerto Rico, the Northern Mariana Islands, American Samoa, Guam, the United States Virgin Islands and Wake Island [@CL-CMP-6566]. Upon request, the contractor must provide information necessary to determine whether the clause applies [@CL-CMP-6567]. A religious corporation, association, educational institution or society is not bound by the clause with respect to employing individuals of a particular religion to perform work connected with carrying on its activities [@CL-CMP-6351].

The contractor must not discriminate against any employee or applicant for employment because of race, color, religion, sex, sexual orientation, gender identity or national origin [@CL-CMP-5867]. Gender identity has the meaning given by the Department of Labor's Office of Federal Contract Compliance Programs [@CL-CMP-6568], and so does sexual orientation [@CL-CMP-6569]. It is not a violation of the clause to extend a publicly announced preference in employment to Indians living on or near an Indian reservation, in connection with employment opportunities on or near an Indian reservation, as permitted by 41 CFR 60-1.5 [@CL-CMP-6352]. The contractor must also take affirmative action so that applicants are employed, and employees are treated during employment, without regard to those bases [@CL-CMP-5868]. That duty includes, but is not limited to, employment, upgrading, demotion, transfer, recruitment or recruitment advertising, layoff or termination, rates of pay or other forms of compensation, and selection for training, including apprenticeship [@CL-CMP-6353].

## How it works

The duties turn into operating steps. The contractor posts, in conspicuous places available to employees and applicants for employment, the notices the contracting officer provides to explain the clause [@CL-CMP-5869]. In all solicitations or advertisements for employees placed by or on behalf of the contractor, it states that all qualified applicants will receive consideration for employment without regard to race, color, religion, sex, sexual orientation, gender identity or national origin [@CL-CMP-6354]. It sends to each labor union or representative of workers with which it has a collective bargaining agreement or other contract or understanding the notice that the contracting officer provides, advising the union or representative of the contractor's commitments under the clause [@CL-CMP-6359]. It also posts copies of that notice in conspicuous places available to employees and applicants for employment [@CL-CMP-6570].

Pay transparency protects a person who asks about, discusses or discloses pay. The contractor may not discharge or otherwise discriminate against an employee or applicant because that person inquired about, discussed or disclosed that person's own compensation or the compensation of another employee or applicant [@CL-CMP-5870]. Compensation means any payments made to, or on behalf of, an employee or offered to an applicant as remuneration for employment, and the definition is not limited to the items it lists [@CL-CMP-6346]. Compensation information means the amount and type of compensation provided to employees or offered to applicants, and that definition is likewise not limited to the items it lists [@CL-CMP-6347].

One exception applies. The prohibition does not apply to instances in which an employee who has access to the compensation information of other employees or applicants as a part of the employee's essential job functions discloses the compensation of those other employees or applicants [@CL-CMP-6355], where the disclosure is to individuals who do not otherwise have access to the compensation information [@CL-CMP-6356]. The exception does not cover a disclosure made in response to a formal complaint or charge, in furtherance of an investigation, proceeding, hearing or action, including an investigation conducted by the employer, or consistent with the contractor's legal duty to furnish information [@CL-CMP-5871]. Essential job functions are the fundamental job duties of the employment position an individual holds, and a function may be considered essential on the conditions the definition lists [@CL-CMP-6348]. Those conditions are alternatives: that access to compensation information is necessary to perform the function or another routinely assigned business task, or that the duties of the position include protecting and maintaining the privacy of employee personnel records, including compensation information [@CL-CMP-6349].

The contractor must also spread the pay protection. Using language prescribed by the Director of the Office of Federal Contract Compliance Programs, it disseminates the prohibition to employees and applicants by incorporating it into existing employee manuals or handbooks [@CL-CMP-6357]. It also disseminates the prohibition by electronic posting or by posting a copy of the provision in conspicuous places available to employees and applicants for employment [@CL-CMP-6358].

The contractor must comply with Executive Order 11246, as amended, and the rules, regulations and orders of the Secretary of Labor [@CL-CMP-6360], and must furnish to the contracting agency all information that the order and those rules, regulations and orders require [@CL-CMP-6361]. It must also file Standard Form 100 (EEO-1), or any successor form, as prescribed in 41 CFR part 60-1 [@CL-CMP-6362]. Unless it filed within the 12 months before award, it must, within 30 days after award, apply to either the regional Office of Federal Contract Compliance Programs or the local office of the Equal Employment Opportunity Commission [@CL-CMP-5872], for the necessary forms [@CL-CMP-6363].

The contractor must permit the contracting agency or the Office of Federal Contract Compliance Programs to have access to its premises during normal business hours for on-site compliance evaluations and complaint investigations [@CL-CMP-5873]. It must also permit the Government to inspect and copy any books, accounts, records, including computerized records, and other material that may be relevant to the matter under investigation and pertinent to compliance with Executive Order 11246, as amended, and with the rules and regulations that implement the Executive Order [@CL-CMP-6364; @CL-CMP-7779].

If the Office of Federal Contract Compliance Programs determines that the contractor is not in compliance with the clause or any rule, regulation or order of the Secretary of Labor, the contract may be canceled, terminated or suspended in whole or in part and the contractor may be declared ineligible for further Government contracts [@CL-CMP-5874]. Those actions follow the procedures authorized in Executive Order 11246, as amended, and sanctions may also be imposed and remedies invoked as provided in that order, in the Secretary of Labor's rules, regulations and orders, or as otherwise provided by law [@CL-CMP-6365].

The contractor must include the terms and conditions of the clause in every subcontract or purchase order that is not exempted by the Secretary of Labor's rules, regulations or orders issued under Executive Order 11246, as amended [@CL-CMP-5875], so that those terms and conditions are binding upon each subcontractor or vendor [@CL-CMP-6571]. It must take such action with respect to any subcontract or purchase order [@CL-CMP-7763] as the Director of the Office of Federal Contract Compliance Programs may direct as a means of enforcing the clause's terms, including sanctions for noncompliance [@CL-CMP-6366]. If the contractor becomes involved in, or is threatened with, litigation with a subcontractor or vendor as a result of a direction, it may request the United States to enter the litigation to protect the interests of the United States [@CL-CMP-6572]. Disputes relative to the clause are governed by the procedures in 41 CFR part 60-1, notwithstanding any other clause in the contract [@CL-CMP-6367].

Alternate I adds a preamble stating that the terms of the clause which the contracting officer lists are waived for the contract, and the list is filled in per contract [@CL-CMP-6368]. Bracketed fill-ins in the clause text are contract-specific and are not established by this card.

Class Deviation 2026-O0038 directs DoD contracting officers to use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text, effective February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. The RFO text prints FAR 52.222-26 as [Reserved] [@CL-CMP-1119].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor wins a contract worth 80,000 and, in the same 12 months, holds other nonexempt Federal contracts worth 45,000. The aggregate is 125,000, above the 10,000 threshold, so the clause applies. The contractor did not file Standard Form 100 in the 12 months before award, so it must apply for the forms within 30 days after award; for an award on day 0, the application is due by day 30. It has a collective bargaining agreement, so it sends that union the contracting officer's notice and also posts copies of it where employees and applicants can see them. Upon request, the contractor provides the information needed to decide whether the clause applies. It includes the clause in a subcontract that is not exempted, so the terms bind the subcontractor, and if the Director of the Office of Federal Contract Compliance Programs directs action on that subcontract and litigation with the subcontractor follows or is threatened, the contractor may ask the United States to enter the litigation. Its payroll clerk, whose essential job functions include access to pay records, tells a coworker who has no access what another employee earns; no complaint or investigation is involved, so the pay protection does not apply to that disclosure. A different employee, who has no access to pay records, tells a coworker her own salary; the contractor may not discharge or otherwise discriminate against her for it. Work done in another country by employees recruited there is outside the clause. If the Office of Federal Contract Compliance Programs determines that the contractor is not in compliance, the contract may be canceled, terminated or suspended, and the contractor may be declared ineligible for further Government contracts.

## Never confuse

The clause bars discrimination on the listed bases [@CL-CMP-5867], and it separately requires affirmative action so that applicants are employed and employees are treated without regard to those bases [@CL-CMP-5868]. The pay transparency rule bars discharging or otherwise discriminating against an employee or applicant for inquiring about, discussing or disclosing compensation [@CL-CMP-5870], and it has an exception for an employee who has access to the compensation information of others as a part of the employee's essential job functions [@CL-CMP-6355]. If the Office of Federal Contract Compliance Programs determines that the contractor is not in compliance, the contract may be canceled, terminated or suspended in whole or in part and the contractor may be declared ineligible for further Government contracts [@CL-CMP-5874].

In the codified text, FAR 52.222-26 is the equal opportunity clause that carries the duties described on this page [@CL-CMP-5866; @CL-CMP-5867], but the RFO text prints that number as [Reserved] [@CL-CMP-1119].
