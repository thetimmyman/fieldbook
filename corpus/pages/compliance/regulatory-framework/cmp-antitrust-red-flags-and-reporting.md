## Purpose

Shows the practices and patterns that the FAR treats as possible evidence of an antitrust violation, who reports them, where reports go and what a report contains.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-6835; @CL-CMP-6837] |
| Codified citation | FAR 3.301, 3.302 and 3.303 [@CL-CMP-6835; @CL-CMP-9700; @CL-CMP-6837] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-6835] |
| Applies to | Agency personnel in acquisitions where there is evidence of suspected antitrust violations, and agencies that receive bids or proposals evidencing a violation [@CL-CMP-6836; @CL-CMP-6837] |

Practices that eliminate competition or restrain trade usually lead to excessive prices and may warrant criminal, civil, or administrative action against the participants [@CL-CMP-7103]. The given examples are collusive bidding, follow-the-leader pricing, rotated low bids, collusive price estimating systems and sharing of the business [@CL-CMP-6835].

Agency personnel must report, in accordance with agency regulations, evidence of suspected antitrust violations in acquisitions for possible referral to the Attorney General and to the agency office responsible for contractor debarment and suspension [@CL-CMP-6836]. Agencies are required to report to the Attorney General any bids or proposals that evidence a violation of the antitrust laws, and these reports are in addition to those required by the debarment and suspension subpart [@CL-CMP-6837].

## How it works

The antitrust laws are intended to ensure that markets operate competitively, and any agreement or mutual understanding among competing firms that restrains the natural operation of market forces is suspect [@CL-CMP-7105]. Activities that match the listed descriptions are not necessarily improper, but they are sufficiently questionable to warrant notifying the appropriate authorities in accordance with agency procedures [@CL-CMP-6838]. The list covers an industry price list or price agreement that contractors refer to when formulating offers, and a sudden change from competitive bidding to identical bidding [@CL-CMP-6839]; simultaneous price increases or follow-the-leader pricing, and rotation of bids or proposals so that each competitor takes a turn as low bidder or certain competitors bid low only on some sizes of contracts and high on other sizes [@CL-CMP-6840]; division of the market, so that certain competitors bid low only for certain agencies, areas or products and bid high on all other jobs [@CL-CMP-6841]; a collusive price estimating system, and a joint bid by competitors when at least one has sufficient technical capability and productive capacity to perform [@CL-CMP-6842]; incidents suggesting direct collusion, such as identical calculation or spelling errors or one firm submitting offers for other firms [@CL-CMP-6843]; and assertions by employees, former employees or competitors of offerors that an agreement to restrain trade exists [@CL-CMP-7106].

Identical bids must be reported under the section if the agency has some reason to believe that the bids resulted from collusion [@CL-CMP-6844]. For offers from foreign contractors for contracts to be performed outside the United States and its outlying areas, contracting officers may refer suspected collusive offers to the authorities of the foreign government concerned [@CL-CMP-7107]. Agency personnel report evidence of suspected violations in accordance with agency regulations [@CL-CMP-6836], and activities meeting the listed descriptions warrant notifying the appropriate authorities in accordance with agency procedures [@CL-CMP-6838].

Agency reports go to the Attorney General, Department of Justice, to the attention of the Assistant Attorney General, Antitrust Division [@CL-CMP-6845]. Each report must include a brief statement describing the suspected practice and the reason for the suspicion [@CL-CMP-7108], and the name, address and telephone number of an individual in the agency who can be contacted for further information [@CL-CMP-7109].

Identical bids, as the subpart uses the term, means bids for the same line item that are determined to be identical as to unit price or total line item amount, with or without the application of evaluation factors such as discount or transportation cost [@CL-CMP-9700].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

Three firms bid on the same line item. Two of the offers contain the same spelling error in a cost narrative, and an employee of one competitor tells the contracting officer that the firms agreed to take turns as low bidder. The identical error and the employee's assertion are both listed patterns [@CL-CMP-6843; @CL-CMP-7106], and bid rotation is also listed [@CL-CMP-6840]. The patterns are not necessarily improper, but they are questionable enough that agency personnel notify the appropriate authorities under agency procedures [@CL-CMP-6838]. The agency's report to the Attorney General is addressed to the Antitrust Division, describes the suspected practice and the reason for the suspicion, and names an agency contact [@CL-CMP-6845; @CL-CMP-7108; @CL-CMP-7109]. Agency personnel also report the evidence for possible referral to the office responsible for debarment and suspension [@CL-CMP-6836].

## Never confuse

Identical bids must be reported when the agency has some reason to believe that collusion produced them [@CL-CMP-6844], and a sudden change from competitive to identical bidding is also a listed pattern that warrants notifying the authorities [@CL-CMP-6839; @CL-CMP-6838]. Activities meeting a listed description are not necessarily improper, but they are sufficiently questionable to warrant notifying the appropriate authorities [@CL-CMP-6838]. The certificate that offerors sign is a different tool; see [Evaluating the Certificate of Independent Price Determination](../regulatory-framework/cmp-certificate-of-independent-price-evaluation.md), where the contracting officer must report the situation to the Attorney General, in accordance with FAR 3.303, whenever an offer is rejected under FAR 3.103-2(b)(1) or (2) or the certificate is suspected of being false [@CL-CMP-6834].
