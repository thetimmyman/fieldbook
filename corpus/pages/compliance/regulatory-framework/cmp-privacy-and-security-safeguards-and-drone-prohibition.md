## Purpose

Covers the clause that limits disclosure of safeguard details and gives the Government inspection access and breach notice, and the separate clause that bans delivering, operating or funding an unmanned aircraft system made or assembled by a covered foreign entity.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-5641; @CL-CMP-5642] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.239-1 and 52.240-1 [@CL-CMP-5641; @CL-CMP-5642] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-5542] |
| Applies to | Contracts that include a clause substantially the same as the safeguards clause under FAR 39.106, and contracts that include the unmanned aircraft system prohibition clause under FAR 40.202-8 [@CL-CMP-5641; @CL-CMP-5642] |

The safeguards clause bars the contractor from publishing or disclosing in any manner, without the Contracting Officer's written consent, the details of any safeguards either designed or developed by the contractor under the contract or otherwise provided by the Government [@CL-CMP-5542]. FAR 39.106 prescribes a clause substantially the same as the text, so the wording in a contract may differ [@CL-CMP-5641].

The drone clause prohibits the contractor from delivering any FASC-prohibited unmanned aircraft system, meaning one manufactured or assembled by a covered foreign entity on the Federal Acquisition Security Council (FASC) list [@CL-CMP-5548; @CL-CMP-5547]. On or after December 22, 2025 it also prohibits operating one in the performance of the contract [@CL-CMP-5549] and using Federal funds for the procurement or operation of one [@CL-CMP-5550].

## How it works

The safeguards clause gives the Government access only to the extent required to carry out a program of inspection to safeguard against threats and hazards to the security, integrity and confidentiality of Government data [@CL-CMP-5543]. To that extent the contractor must afford the Government access to its facilities, installations, technical capabilities, operations, documentation, records and databases [@CL-CMP-5544].

If new or unanticipated threats or hazards are discovered by either the Government or the contractor, or if existing safeguards have ceased to function, the discoverer must immediately bring the situation to the attention of the other party [@CL-CMP-5545].

A covered foreign entity is an entity included on a list that the Federal Acquisition Security Council (FASC) develops and maintains and publishes in the System for Award Management [@CL-CMP-5546]. A FASC-prohibited unmanned aircraft system is an unmanned aircraft system manufactured or assembled by such an entity [@CL-CMP-5547].

An unmanned aircraft is an aircraft that is operated without the possibility of direct human intervention from within or on the aircraft [@CL-CMP-5643]. An unmanned aircraft system is an unmanned aircraft and associated elements, including communication links and the components that control the aircraft, that are required for the operator to operate safely and efficiently in the national airspace system [@CL-CMP-5644]. The delivery prohibition covers unmanned aircraft (drones) and associated elements [@CL-CMP-5548].

Before proposing, or using in the performance of the contract, any unmanned aircraft system, the contractor must search the System for Award Management for the FASC-maintained list of covered foreign entities [@CL-CMP-5551]. The contractor must also ensure that any effort or expenditure associated with a FASC-prohibited unmanned aircraft system is consistent with a corresponding exemption, exception or waiver determination expressly stated in the contract [@CL-CMP-5645].

The prohibitions do not apply where the agency has determined that an exemption, exception or waiver applies and the contract indicates that such a determination has been made [@CL-CMP-5552]. The clause points to sections 1823 through 1825 and 1832 of Public Law 118-31 for the statutory requirements on exemptions, exceptions and waivers [@CL-CMP-5646].

The contractor must insert the substance of the drone clause, including its flowdown paragraph, in all subcontracts and other contractual instruments, including subcontracts for the acquisition of commercial products or commercial services [@CL-CMP-5553].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor plans to fly 6 drones on a support contract, and the contract states no exemption, exception or waiver determination. Before proposing, it searches the System for Award Management for the Federal Acquisition Security Council list and finds that the planned model is assembled by a listed covered foreign entity, so it switches to a different model. Delivering the original model would have breached the clause, and so, on or after December 22, 2025, would operating it in the performance of the contract or paying for its operation with Federal funds. The contractor also puts the clause substance, including the flowdown paragraph, in its subcontract for the flight crews. Separately, it asks the Contracting Officer for written consent before publishing details of the safeguards it designed for the contract, and it tells the Government at once when a safeguard stops working.

## Never confuse

The safeguards clause is not the drone prohibition. The first governs disclosure of safeguard details, inspection access and notice of threats, while the second governs which unmanned aircraft systems may be delivered, operated or bought with Federal funds [@CL-CMP-5542; @CL-CMP-5545; @CL-CMP-5548].
