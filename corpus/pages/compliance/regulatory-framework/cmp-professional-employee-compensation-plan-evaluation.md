## Purpose

Explains when the contracting officer inserts the provision on evaluation of compensation for professional employees, what an offeror submits, and how an unrealistically low compensation plan may count against the offeror. The provision text is on the linked page.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-6792] |
| Codified citation | FAR 22.1103 [@CL-CMP-6792] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |

| Applies to | Solicitations for negotiated contracts expected to exceed $900,000 that will require meaningful numbers of professional employees [@CL-CMP-6792; @CL-CMP-7001] |

All professional employees shall be compensated fairly and properly [@CL-CMP-6791]. The contracting officer must insert the provision at FAR 52.222-46 in solicitations for negotiated contracts when the contract amount is expected to exceed $900,000 [@CL-CMP-6792]. The provision is inserted only where, in addition, services are to be provided which will require meaningful numbers of professional employees [@CL-CMP-7001].

The provision requires offerors to submit for evaluation a total compensation plan setting forth proposed salaries and fringe benefits for professional employees working on the contract [@CL-CMP-6793]. Supporting information includes data, such as recognized national and regional compensation surveys and studies of professional, public and private organizations, used in establishing the total compensation structure [@CL-CMP-6981]. Plans indicating unrealistically low professional employee compensation may be assessed adversely as one of the factors considered in making an award [@CL-CMP-6794].

## How it works

The contracting officer decides when the provision goes into a solicitation, and both conditions must be met: the negotiated contract is expected to exceed the dollar amount, and the services need meaningful numbers of professional employees [@CL-CMP-6792; @CL-CMP-7001]. The section does not define meaningful numbers or give a count.

Offerors submit the plan with their offers, and the supporting data shows how the compensation structure was set [@CL-CMP-6793; @CL-CMP-6981]. The Government assesses the plan as part of the award decision. An unrealistically low plan may be assessed adversely as one of the factors considered, so it counts alongside the other award factors [@CL-CMP-6794]. The section does not say that a low plan is rejected.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contracting officer prepares a solicitation for a negotiated services contract expected to be 2,000,000. The contracting officer judges that the work will need meaningful numbers of professional employees, so the provision is inserted [@CL-CMP-6792; @CL-CMP-7001].

Each offeror submits a total compensation plan with proposed salaries and fringe benefits for the professional employees, supported by national and regional survey data [@CL-CMP-6793; @CL-CMP-6981]. One offeror's plan sets salaries about a third below the surveys for the same work and so indicates unrealistically low professional employee compensation. That plan may be assessed adversely as one of the factors considered in making the award, alongside the other factors [@CL-CMP-6794].

A second negotiated solicitation is expected to be 700,000. It does not meet the dollar condition for inserting the provision [@CL-CMP-6792].

## Never confuse

The policy that all professional employees be compensated fairly and properly is a statement of policy. The provision is inserted only when the dollar and professional-employee conditions are met [@CL-CMP-6791; @CL-CMP-6792; @CL-CMP-7001].

A total compensation plan states proposed salaries and fringe benefits for the professional employees on the contract, with survey data behind the structure. It is not the contract price [@CL-CMP-6793; @CL-CMP-6981]. An unrealistically low plan may count against the offeror as one factor in award. The section does not say it disqualifies the offer [@CL-CMP-6794].
