## Purpose

Explains the reporting and assistance duties that apply when performance of a contract is said to infringe a patent or copyright, and the construction patent indemnity and waiver that shift or remove liability [@CL-CMP-5598; @CL-CMP-5744; @CL-CMP-5746].

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-5598; @CL-CMP-5744; @CL-CMP-5746] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.227-2, FAR 52.227-4 and FAR 52.227-5 [@CL-CMP-5598; @CL-CMP-5744; @CL-CMP-5746] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.227-2 [@CL-CMP-1747], RFO FAR 52.227-4 [@CL-CMP-1800] and RFO FAR 52.227-5 [@CL-CMP-1804] |
| Edition date | FAR 52.227-2: Jun 2020 [@CL-CMP-1747]; FAR 52.227-4: Dec 2007 [@CL-CMP-1800]; FAR 52.227-5: Apr 1984 [@CL-CMP-1804] |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-5598; @CL-CMP-5744; @CL-CMP-5746] |
| Applies to | Contracts in which a clause is inserted as prescribed [@CL-CMP-5598; @CL-CMP-5744; @CL-CMP-5746] |

The contractor reports to the contracting officer, promptly and in reasonable written detail, each notice or claim of patent or copyright infringement based on performance of the contract of which it has knowledge [@CL-CMP-5599]. The assistance duty applies to any claim or suit against the Government on account of any alleged patent or copyright infringement arising out of performing the contract or out of using any supplies furnished or work or services performed under it [@CL-CMP-5742]. On that duty, the contractor furnishes, when the contracting officer requests, all evidence and information in its possession pertaining to the claim or suit [@CL-CMP-5600]. That evidence and information is furnished at the expense of the Government except where the contractor has agreed to indemnify the Government [@CL-CMP-5601]. The contractor must include the substance of the notice clause, including paragraph (c), in all subcontracts expected to exceed the simplified acquisition threshold, as defined in FAR 2.101 on the date of subcontract award [@CL-CMP-5743].

## How it works

In the construction clause, except as otherwise provided, the contractor indemnifies the Government and its officers, agents and employees against liability, including costs and expenses, for infringement of any United States patent [@CL-CMP-5602]. The indemnity covers infringement arising out of performing the contract or out of use or disposal, by or for the account of the Government, of supplies furnished or work performed under the contract [@CL-CMP-5604]. It does not cover a patent issued on an application that is now or may hereafter be withheld from issue under a Secrecy Order [@CL-CMP-5603]. Where 27.201-2(d)(2) applies, the first paragraph of the basic clause becomes paragraph (a) and a new paragraph (b) provides that the indemnification does not apply to the items that the contracting officer lists as excluded [@CL-CMP-5745]. The excluded items are a contract-specific fill-in [@CL-CMP-5745].

Any provision or clause of the contract to the contrary notwithstanding, the Government, under the waiver clause, authorizes and consents to the use and manufacture, solely in performing the contract, of any invention covered by the identified United States patents [@CL-CMP-5605]. The Government also waives indemnification by the contractor with respect to those patents [@CL-CMP-5747]. The patents are identified in the clause itself, so the list differs by contract [@CL-CMP-5605].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text from February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. The RFO text carries all three clauses under the same section numbers, with the same titles and the prescription cross-references the codified text prints [@CL-CMP-5598] [@CL-CMP-1747] [@CL-CMP-5744] [@CL-CMP-1800] [@CL-CMP-5746] [@CL-CMP-1804]. The RFO notice clause says the same as the codified clause on reporting each notice or claim, on furnishing evidence and information when the Contracting Officer requests it in the event of any claim or suit against the Government on account of alleged patent or copyright infringement arising out of the performance of the contract or the use of supplies furnished or work or services performed [@CL-CMP-5742] [@CL-CMP-9287], on the Government's expense unless the Contractor has agreed to indemnify, and on the subcontract threshold [@CL-CMP-5599] [@CL-CMP-1748] [@CL-CMP-5600] [@CL-CMP-1749] [@CL-CMP-5601] [@CL-CMP-1798] [@CL-CMP-5743] [@CL-CMP-1799]. The RFO construction clause says the same on the indemnity and on the Secrecy Order exception [@CL-CMP-5602] [@CL-CMP-1801] [@CL-CMP-5603] [@CL-CMP-1802], and on its scope of infringement arising out of performing the contract or out of the use or disposal of supplies furnished or work performed [@CL-CMP-5604] [@CL-CMP-9288], and its Alternate I says the same as the codified alternate on the items the Contracting Officer lists as excluded [@CL-CMP-5745] [@CL-CMP-1803]. The RFO waiver clause says the same on the Government's consent to use and its waiver of the Contractor's indemnification for the identified patents [@CL-CMP-5605] [@CL-CMP-1805] [@CL-CMP-5747] [@CL-CMP-1806].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor learns that its software deliverable is said to infringe a copyright and reports the claim to the contracting officer in writing at once. The Government is later sued, and the contracting officer asks for the contractor's records. The contractor spends 40,000 gathering them. The contractor did not agree to indemnify the Government, so the 40,000 is furnished at the Government's expense. Had it agreed to indemnify, the cost would be its own.

A construction contract uses the construction indemnity with its alternate, and the contracting officer lists a prefabricated sensor array as an excluded item. A third party sues the Government over a United States patent said to be infringed by the contractor's concrete-forming method, which is not excluded, so the contractor indemnifies the Government for that liability. The contractor gives no indemnity for the sensor array, and none for a patent issued on an application held under a Secrecy Order.

Another contract's waiver clause identifies one United States patent by number. The contractor may use the invention solely in performing that contract, and the Government waives the contractor's indemnity for that patent only.

## Never confuse

Notice is not indemnity. Reporting a claim and furnishing evidence are duties under the notice clause, while the construction clause shifts liability for infringement to the contractor [@CL-CMP-5599; @CL-CMP-5600; @CL-CMP-5602]. The cost of furnishing evidence is the Government's unless the contractor has agreed to indemnify [@CL-CMP-5601]. A waiver runs the other way: the Government consents to use of the identified patents solely in performing the contract and gives up the contractor's indemnity for them [@CL-CMP-5605; @CL-CMP-5747]. The construction indemnity and its alternate belong only to the construction clause, not to the notice clause [@CL-CMP-5598; @CL-CMP-5744; @CL-CMP-5745].

The patent indemnity and refund of royalties clauses are covered in [Patent indemnity and refund of royalties](cmp-patent-indemnity-and-refund-of-royalties.md), and the authorization and consent clause is covered in [Authorization and consent for patent use](cmp-authorization-and-consent-clause.md).
