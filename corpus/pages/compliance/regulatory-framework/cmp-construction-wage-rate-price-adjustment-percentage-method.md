## Purpose

Shows when a construction wage rate determination moves the contract price at all, and how the percentage method computes that movement from a stated share of the price and a published rate.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-4938; @CL-CMP-4941] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.222-30 and FAR 52.222-31 [@CL-CMP-4938; @CL-CMP-4941] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.222-30 [@CL-CMP-0898] and RFO FAR 52.222-31 [@CL-CMP-0917] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-4938] |
| Applies to | Construction contracts where the prescriptions direct insertion of the matching price adjustment clause [@CL-CMP-4938; @CL-CMP-4941] |

The none or separately specified method clause is inserted as prescribed in FAR 22.407(e) [@CL-CMP-4938], and the percentage method clause as prescribed in FAR 22.407(f) [@CL-CMP-4941].

Under both clauses, the wage determination that is effective for an option to extend the term applies to that option period [@CL-CMP-4940; @CL-CMP-5132].

Under the none or separately specified method, the Contracting Officer makes no adjustment in contract price, other than as provided for elsewhere in the contract, to cover increases or decreases in wages and benefits resulting from the causes the paragraph lists [@CL-CMP-4939]. Those causes are incorporation of the Department of Labor wage determination applicable at the exercise of the option [@CL-CMP-5129], incorporation of a wage determination otherwise applied to the contract by operation of law [@CL-CMP-5130], and an increase in wages and benefits resulting from any other requirement applicable to workers subject to the statute [@CL-CMP-5131].

## How it works

Under the percentage method, the Contracting Officer adjusts the portion of the contract price or unit prices that contains labor costs subject to the statute, to provide for an increase in wages and fringe benefits at the exercise of each option to extend the term [@CL-CMP-4942]. The Contracting Officer states in the clause the percentage of the price or unit prices that contains those labor costs, a bracketed fill-in [@CL-CMP-5133]. The Contracting Officer increases that portion by the percentage rate published in a publication the clause names, which is also a bracketed fill-in [@CL-CMP-4943].

The adjustment is made at the exercise of each option to extend the term, and it is the only adjustment the Contracting Officer makes to cover increases in wages and benefits resulting from the causes the paragraph lists [@CL-CMP-5134]. Those causes are the same three as under the other method, namely the Department of Labor wage determination applicable at the exercise of the option [@CL-CMP-5135], a wage determination otherwise applied by operation of law [@CL-CMP-5136], and an increase resulting from any other requirement applicable to workers subject to the statute [@CL-CMP-5137].

Option-period wage determinations are also covered on the [option wage pricing card](cmp-improper-wage-determinations-and-option-wage-pricing.md), and the [actual method card](cmp-construction-wage-rate-price-adjustment-actual-method.md) covers the third clause.

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text from February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. The RFO none or separately specified clause is inserted as prescribed in FAR 22.402-4(d), where the codified clause points to FAR 22.407(e) [@CL-CMP-0916; @CL-CMP-4938], and the RFO percentage clause as prescribed in FAR 22.402-4(e), where the codified clause points to FAR 22.407(f) [@CL-CMP-0924; @CL-CMP-4941]. Both RFO clauses keep the rule that the wage determination effective for an option to extend the term applies to that option period [@CL-CMP-0898; @CL-CMP-0917; @CL-CMP-4940; @CL-CMP-5132]. The RFO none or separately specified clause keeps the rule that the Contracting Officer makes no adjustment in contract price, other than as provided for elsewhere in the contract, to cover increases or decreases in wages and benefits resulting from its three causes [@CL-CMP-0899; @CL-CMP-0900; @CL-CMP-0915; @CL-CMP-4939; @CL-CMP-5129; @CL-CMP-5130; @CL-CMP-5131]. The RFO percentage clause keeps the adjustment of the portion of the price or unit prices containing the covered labor costs at the exercise of each option [@CL-CMP-0918; @CL-CMP-4942], the percentage the Contracting Officer states in the clause [@CL-CMP-0919; @CL-CMP-5133], and the increase by the percentage rate published in a named publication [@CL-CMP-0920; @CL-CMP-4943]. It keeps the rule that this is the only adjustment the Contracting Officer makes to cover increases in wages and benefits resulting from the three causes it lists [@CL-CMP-0921; @CL-CMP-0922; @CL-CMP-0923; @CL-CMP-5134; @CL-CMP-5135; @CL-CMP-5136; @CL-CMP-5137].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contract price is 1,000,000. Under the percentage method clause, the Contracting Officer has inserted 35 percent as the share of the price that contains covered labor costs, so the covered portion is 350,000. The named publication gives a rate of 3 percent. At the option, the covered portion rises by 3 percent, which is 10,500, so the price becomes 1,010,500.

The contractor's payroll actually rises by 14,000 because of the new wage determination. The price still moves by only 10,500, because the percentage adjustment is the only adjustment for those causes. Under the none or separately specified method, the same new wage determination would move nothing unless another provision of the contract provided for an adjustment.

## Never confuse

A wage determination for an option period is not a change order, because the percentage method produces an arithmetic adjustment to a defined share of the price at each option while the none or separately specified method produces no adjustment from the determination itself [@CL-CMP-4939; @CL-CMP-4942]. The percentage in the clause is also not the size of the wage increase, because the inserted percentage is the share of the price that carries covered labor costs and the increase comes from the rate published in the named publication [@CL-CMP-5133; @CL-CMP-4943].
