## Purpose

Shows whom the Recovery Act whistleblower sections cover, the reprisal they prohibit, who may see the Inspector General file, and the court relief available.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-2338] |
| Codified citation | FAR 3.907-1, FAR 3.907-2, FAR 3.907-5 and FAR 3.907-6 [@CL-CMP-2338; @CL-CMP-2462; @CL-CMP-2340; @CL-CMP-2341] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-2338] |
| Applies to | Non-Federal employers that receive Recovery Act funds [@CL-CMP-2339; @CL-CMP-2462] |

Non-Federal employers are prohibited from discharging, demoting, or otherwise discriminating against an employee as a reprisal for disclosing covered information to any of the listed entities or their representatives [@CL-CMP-2462]. Covered information means information that the employee reasonably believes is evidence of gross mismanagement of the contract or subcontract related to covered funds, gross waste of covered funds, or one of the further listed kinds [@CL-CMP-2463]. The section lists the entities, which this card does not restate.

Covered funds means any contract payment, grant payment, or other payment received by a contractor if the Federal Government provides any portion of the money or property that is provided, requested, or demanded and at least some of the funds are appropriated or otherwise made available by the Recovery Act [@CL-CMP-2338]. A non-Federal employer means any employer that receives Recovery Act funds, including a contractor, subcontractor, or other recipient of funds pursuant to a contract or other agreement awarded and administered in accordance with the Federal Acquisition Regulation [@CL-CMP-2339].

The employee alleging reprisal shall have access to the investigation file of the Inspector General, in accordance with the Privacy Act, section 552a of title 5 of the United States Code [@CL-CMP-2340]. The Inspector General may exclude some information from those disclosures, which this card does not restate. In any action brought under the remedies section, the court may grant appropriate relief, including injunctive relief, compensatory and exemplary damages, and attorneys fees and costs [@CL-CMP-2341].

## How it works

On the printed definitions, these sections are tied to Recovery Act funds [@CL-CMP-2338; @CL-CMP-2339]. A payment falls outside covered funds unless at least some of its funds are appropriated or otherwise made available by the Recovery Act [@CL-CMP-2338]. These sections therefore address employers that receive such funds [@CL-CMP-2339]. The prohibition reaches reprisal for disclosing covered information, whose first two listed kinds are tied to covered funds [@CL-CMP-2462; @CL-CMP-2463]. Employer access to the investigative file, the burden of proof, the agency head's order and the enforcement route are in the same sections; this card does not restate them.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A subcontractor receives a payment financed entirely from non-Recovery Act appropriations. That payment is not covered funds, so these sections do not reach it. A second payment includes some Recovery Act money, so it is covered funds. An employee of the subcontractor who is demoted for telling the Inspector General about gross waste of the second payment's funds suffers a reprisal that the section prohibits.

## Never confuse

These sections are defined by reference to Recovery Act funds [@CL-CMP-2338]. The general whistleblower protections for contractor employees are a separate rule; see [Whistleblower protections for contractor employees: policy and remedies](cmp-contractor-employee-whistleblower-protections.md).

Whether any contract or subcontract payment includes Recovery Act funds. [@Q-003]
