## Purpose

Shows when a contracting officer may issue a stop-work order, what must happen before it expires, and the length and cost effects that the basic stop-work order clause sets out.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-2240] |
| Codified citation | FAR 42.1303, FAR 42.1305 and FAR 52.242-15 [@CL-CMP-2240; @CL-CMP-2263; @CL-CMP-2264] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 42.1003 [@CL-CMP-6966; @CL-CMP-6967; @CL-CMP-6968; @CL-CMP-6969; @CL-CMP-6970], RFO FAR 42.1005 [@CL-CMP-6971; @CL-CMP-6972; @CL-CMP-6973] and RFO FAR 52.242-15 [@CL-CMP-6974; @CL-CMP-6975; @CL-CMP-6989; @CL-CMP-7008; @CL-CMP-7009; @CL-CMP-7010; @CL-CMP-7011; @CL-CMP-7012; @CL-CMP-9899; @CL-CMP-9900] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-2240] |
| Applies to | Negotiated fixed-price or cost-reimbursement supply, research and development, or service contracts [@CL-CMP-2240] |

Stop-work orders may be used, when appropriate, in any negotiated fixed-price or cost-reimbursement supply, research and development, or service contract if work stoppage may be required for reasons such as advancement in the state of the art [@CL-CMP-2240]. Generally, an order will be issued only if it is advisable to suspend work pending a Government decision and a supplemental agreement providing for the suspension is not feasible, and its issuance shall be approved at a level higher than the contracting officer [@CL-CMP-2241]. Stop-work orders shall not be used in place of a termination notice after a decision to terminate has been made [@CL-CMP-2261].

As soon as feasible after an order is issued, but before its expiration, the contracting officer shall take appropriate action, including terminating the contract or canceling the order [@CL-CMP-2242]. Any cancellation shall be subject to the same approvals as were required for the issuance [@CL-CMP-2262]. One listed action is to extend the order if it is necessary and if the contractor agrees, and any extension shall be by a supplemental agreement [@CL-CMP-2243].

The contracting officer may, when contracting by negotiation, insert the stop-work order clause in solicitations and contracts for supplies, services, or research and development [@CL-CMP-2263]. If a cost-reimbursement contract is contemplated, the officer shall use the clause with its Alternate I [@CL-CMP-2273]. Under the basic clause, the officer may, at any time, by written order, require the contractor to stop all or any part of the work for a period of 90 days after the order is delivered, and for any further period to which the parties may agree [@CL-CMP-2264]. The prescription note says the 90-day period stated in the clause may be reduced to less than 90 days [@CL-CMP-2265]. Upon receipt of the order, the contractor shall immediately comply with its terms and take all reasonable steps to minimize the incurrence of costs allocable to the work covered by the order during the period of work stoppage [@CL-CMP-2266].

Under the basic clause, when the order is canceled or its period or any extension expires, the contractor shall resume work [@CL-CMP-2278]. In that case the officer shall make an equitable adjustment in the delivery schedule or contract price, or both, and modify the contract in writing, if the listed conditions are met [@CL-CMP-2267]. One condition is that the order results in an increase in the time required for, or in the contractor's cost properly allocable to, the performance of any part of the contract [@CL-CMP-2268]. A further condition is that the contractor asserts its right to the adjustment within 30 days after the end of the period of work stoppage, although the officer may act upon a proposal submitted at any time before final payment if the officer decides the facts justify the action [@CL-CMP-2269]. If the order is not canceled and the work covered by it is terminated for the convenience of the Government, the officer shall allow reasonable costs resulting from the order in arriving at the termination settlement [@CL-CMP-2279]; if the order is not canceled and the work is terminated for default, the officer shall allow, by equitable adjustment or otherwise, reasonable costs resulting from the order [@CL-CMP-2280]. Under Alternate I, the adjustment is worded as one in the delivery schedule, the estimated cost, the fee, or a combination thereof, and in any other terms of the contract that may be affected [@CL-CMP-2272].

The printed stop-work sections and clause list no cost categories for a finance reader to segregate.

## How it works

The contracting officer issues the order after approval at a higher level, and generally only when a supplemental agreement for the suspension is not feasible [@CL-CMP-2241]. The order has an expiration, and the officer must act before it arrives [@CL-CMP-2242]. In the basic clause the stop period is 90 days after delivery, plus any further period the parties agree [@CL-CMP-2264]. The listed actions are to terminate, cancel or extend [@CL-CMP-2242; @CL-CMP-2243]. An extension needs the contractor's agreement and a supplemental agreement [@CL-CMP-2243]. A contractor that wants an equitable adjustment asserts its right within the period the clause states, although, if the officer decides the facts justify the action, the officer may receive and act upon a proposal submitted at any time before final payment [@CL-CMP-2269].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 42 text in place of the codified text from March 16, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9759] [@CL-CMP-9761], and the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text from February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. RFO FAR 42.1003 says the same as the codified section on when stop-work orders may be used and on the approval needed to issue one [@CL-CMP-2240; @CL-CMP-2241; @CL-CMP-6966; @CL-CMP-6967]. RFO FAR 42.1003(e)(3) says the contracting officer may extend the period of the order if it is necessary and the contractor agrees, and words the extension requirement as "must be done by a supplemental agreement" where the codified section says "shall be by a supplemental agreement" [@CL-CMP-2243; @CL-CMP-6970]. RFO FAR 42.1003(b) says stop-work orders will not be used in place of a termination notice after a decision to terminate has been made, where the codified section says they shall not be [@CL-CMP-2261; @CL-CMP-6968]. RFO FAR 42.1003(e) says that, before the order expires, the contracting officer must take appropriate action, which includes terminating the contract or canceling the order, with any cancellation subject to the same approvals as its issuance, and words that cancellation point as "is subject to" where the codified section says "shall be subject to" [@CL-CMP-2242; @CL-CMP-2262; @CL-CMP-6969]. RFO FAR 42.1005(b)(1) says the contracting officer may, when contracting by negotiation, insert the Stop-Work Order clause in solicitations and contracts for supplies, services, or research and development, as the codified section does [@CL-CMP-2263; @CL-CMP-6971]. It then says contracting officers may reduce the 90-day period in the clause, as appropriate, where the codified clause's prescription says the period may be reduced to less than 90 days [@CL-CMP-2265; @CL-CMP-6972]. RFO FAR 42.1005(b)(2) says to use the clause with its Alternate I if a cost-reimbursement contract is contemplated, without the words "the contracting officer shall" [@CL-CMP-2273; @CL-CMP-6973]. RFO FAR 52.242-15 says the same as the codified clause on the 90-day order [@CL-CMP-2264; @CL-CMP-6974], on the contractor's duty upon receipt of the order to comply immediately and minimize the incurrence of costs [@CL-CMP-2266; @CL-CMP-9899], on the contractor's duty to resume work when the order is canceled or its period expires [@CL-CMP-2278; @CL-CMP-9900], on the equitable adjustment and its two conditions, including the 30-day assertion and the officer's power to act on a later proposal [@CL-CMP-2267; @CL-CMP-2268; @CL-CMP-2269; @CL-CMP-6975; @CL-CMP-6989; @CL-CMP-7008; @CL-CMP-7009], on reasonable costs when the work is terminated for the convenience of the Government or for default [@CL-CMP-2279; @CL-CMP-2280; @CL-CMP-7010; @CL-CMP-7011], and in Alternate I on the wording of the adjustment [@CL-CMP-2272; @CL-CMP-7012].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A cost-reimbursement development contract worth 15,000,000 is suspended by a stop-work order after approval above the contracting officer. Before the order expires, the officer and contractor agree to extend it, and the extension is written as a supplemental agreement. Had the contractor not agreed, the officer would have had to terminate the contract or cancel the order.

## Never confuse

A stop-work order is not a termination. A stop-work order shall not be used in place of a termination notice after a decision to terminate has been made [@CL-CMP-2261], and termination is one of the actions the officer may take before the order expires [@CL-CMP-2242]. The codified section says stop-work orders shall not be used in place of a termination notice after a decision to terminate has been made [@CL-CMP-2261], while RFO FAR 42.1003(b) says they will not be used that way [@CL-CMP-6968]. A stop-work order is also not a supplemental agreement for the suspension. Generally, the order is issued only when such an agreement is not feasible [@CL-CMP-2241]. For termination accounting, see [Termination for convenience settlement](../../contract-finance/billing-payment/ctf-termination-for-convenience-settlement.md) and [Termination costs](../../close-revrec/inventory-wip/cls-termination-costs.md).
