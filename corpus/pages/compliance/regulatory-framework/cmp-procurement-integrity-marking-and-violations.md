## Purpose

Shows how contractor bid or proposal information is protected, what happens to a marking dispute, and who decides after a possible violation: the contracting officer first, then the HCA, as the section calls that official.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-2307] |
| Codified citation | FAR 3.104-4 and FAR 3.104-7 [@CL-CMP-2307; @CL-CMP-2312] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-2307] |
| Applies to | Contractor bid or proposal information and source selection information [@CL-CMP-2307] |

Except as specifically provided for in the same subsection, no person or other entity may disclose contractor bid or proposal information or source selection information to any person other than a person authorized in accordance with applicable agency regulations or procedures [@CL-CMP-2307]. The authorization that permits disclosure is authorization by the agency head or the contracting officer to receive the information [@CL-CMP-2468]. The same section does not restrict or prohibit a contractor from disclosing its own bid or proposal information or the recipient from receiving that information [@CL-CMP-2465]. The information must be protected from unauthorized disclosure in accordance with the FAR provisions the paragraph names, applicable law and agency regulations [@CL-CMP-2308].

This card does not set out a legend that a contractor must place on proposal pricing. Information that the contractor marked as bid or proposal information in accordance with applicable law or regulation, or marked under the FAR paragraph the definition names, is within the definition of contractor bid or proposal information if it was submitted with a bid or proposal and has not previously been made public [@CL-CMP-2300; @CL-CMP-2302]. Except for technical data marked as proprietary by a contractor, the contracting officer must notify the contractor in writing if the contracting officer believes that proprietary information, contractor bid or proposal information, or information marked under that paragraph has been inappropriately marked [@CL-CMP-2309; @CL-CMP-2469]. For technical data marked as proprietary by a contractor, the contracting officer must instead follow the procedures in a separate FAR section [@CL-CMP-2310].

A contracting officer who receives information of a violation or possible violation must determine if it has any impact on the pending award or selection [@CL-CMP-2312]. If the contracting officer concludes there is an impact, the information goes promptly to the HCA [@CL-CMP-2313]. A contracting officer who concludes there is no impact follows a different route through an individual designated under agency procedures, which this card does not cover.

## How it works

The contractor that affixed a marking must be given an opportunity to justify it. If the contractor agrees the marking is not justified, or does not respond within the time stated in the notice, the contracting officer may remove the marking and release the information [@CL-CMP-2311]. If, after reviewing the contractor's justification, the contracting officer determines that the marking is not justified, the contracting officer must notify the contractor in writing before releasing the information [@CL-CMP-2474]. The HCA must review all information available and take appropriate action under agency procedures, such as advising the contracting officer to continue or beginning an investigation [@CL-CMP-2314]. If the HCA concludes that the statutory chapter the section cites has been violated, the HCA may direct the contracting officer to take the steps listed [@CL-CMP-2315]. If no contract has been awarded, those steps include cancelling the procurement, disqualifying an offeror, or taking other appropriate actions in the interests of the Government [@CL-CMP-2315]. If a contract has been awarded, one listed step is to effect appropriate contractual remedies, including profit recapture under the price or fee adjustment clause [@CL-CMP-2315; @CL-CMP-2316].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A proposal carries a restrictive legend on its overhead rate sheet. The contracting officer doubts the legend and sends the company a written notice. The company writes back to justify the marking. If the contracting officer still finds the marking unjustified, the company is told in writing before the sheet is released.

## Never confuse

A dispute over a marking is not a finding of a violation. A marking dispute concerns whether a marking is justified, and the contracting officer must give the contractor the chance to justify it first [@CL-CMP-2311]. A possible violation of the statutory bans goes to the contracting officer for an impact decision and then, if there is an impact, to the HCA [@CL-CMP-2312; @CL-CMP-2313]. The bans themselves are in [Procurement integrity: bid and proposal information and the disclosure and obtaining bans](cmp-procurement-integrity-prohibitions.md). Voiding or rescinding a contract is covered in [Voiding and rescinding contracts after a conviction or proven offense](cmp-voiding-and-rescinding-contracts.md), and suspension or debarment is covered in [Causes for debarment](../../supply-chain/supplier-identity/sup-causes-for-debarment.md).
