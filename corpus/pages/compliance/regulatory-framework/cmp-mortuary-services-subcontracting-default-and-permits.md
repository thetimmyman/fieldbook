## Purpose

Shows the approval a mortuary services contractor needs before subcontracting, the grounds on which the contracting officer may terminate for default without the usual notice period, and the licensing and permit duties.

The contractor shall not subcontract any work under the contract without the contracting officer's written approval, and the clause does not apply to contracts of employment between the contractor and its personnel [@CL-CMP-4356]. The contractor shall meet all State and local licensing requirements and obtain and furnish all necessary health department and shipping permits at no additional cost to the Government [@CL-CMP-4363]. It shall also ensure that all necessary health department permits are in order for disposition of the remains [@CL-CMP-4410].

## How it works

The contracting officer may terminate the contract for default by written notice without the ten day notice required by the Default clause if one of the listed grounds applies [@CL-CMP-4357]. The first listed ground is that the contractor, through circumstances reasonably within its control or that of its employees, performs any act under or in connection with the contract, or fails in the performance of any service under the contract, and the act or failures may reasonably be considered to reflect discredit upon the Department of Defense in fulfilling its responsibility for proper care of remains [@CL-CMP-4358; @CL-CMP-4359]. A second ground is that the contractor or its employees solicit relatives or friends of the deceased to purchase supplies or services not under the contract [@CL-CMP-4360], and the clause adds that the contractor may furnish supplies or arrange for services not under the contract only if representatives of the deceased voluntarily request, select, and pay for them [@CL-CMP-4411]. A further ground is that the services or any part of the services are performed by anyone other than the contractor or the contractor's employees without the written authorization of the contracting officer [@CL-CMP-4361]. Two more are that the contractor refuses to perform the services required for any particular remains and that it mentions or otherwise uses the contract in its advertising in any way [@CL-CMP-4362].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor asks an outside firm to move remains for one job and does not tell the contracting officer. The contractor also lists the contract in a brochure. The answer: the services were performed by someone other than the contractor or its employees without written authorization, and the contract was used in advertising, so the contracting officer may terminate for default by written notice without waiting out the ten day notice period.

## Never confuse

The contracting officer may terminate the contract for default by written notice without the ten day notice required by paragraph (a)(2) of the Default clause if one of the listed grounds applies [@CL-CMP-4357]. For the default clauses used in fixed-price contracts, see [Default clauses for fixed-price contracts](../../close-revrec/revenue/cls-fixed-price-default-clauses.md).

The contractor shall not subcontract any work under the contract without the contracting officer's written approval [@CL-CMP-4356]. For the consent rule in the FAR subcontracts clause, see [Subcontracts clause: consent and advance notification](../../supply-chain/procure-to-pay/sup-subcontracts-consent-advance-notification.md). The ordering, area of performance and delivery clauses are covered on [Mortuary services: requirements, area of performance, and performance and delivery](cmp-mortuary-services-requirements-area-and-delivery.md).
