## Purpose

Shows the Executive Order minimum wage rates stated in this section, the order that supersedes the earlier one to the extent that the two are inconsistent, and what the minimum wage clause requires when its rate is higher than another minimum wage rate.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-7997] |
| Codified citation | FAR 22.403-4 [@CL-CMP-7997] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-7997] |
| Applies to | Certain workers whose minimum wage rate is set by the executive orders; the section does not define them [@CL-CMP-7999] |

Executive Order 13658 established minimum wages for certain workers at $10.10 per hour [@CL-CMP-7997]. That rate has increased each year since 2015, rising to $11.25 on January 1, 2022 [@CL-CMP-7997].

As of January 30, 2022, Executive Order 13658 is superseded by Executive Order 14026 to the extent that it is inconsistent with Executive Order 14026, and the minimum wage rate for certain workers is increased to $15.00 per hour [@CL-CMP-7998; @CL-CMP-7999].

The wage rate is subject to annual increases by an amount determined by the Secretary of Labor, and the section refers the reader to subpart 22.19 [@CL-CMP-8000].

The clause at 52.222-55, Minimum Wages for Contractor Workers under Executive Order 14026, requires the Executive Order 14026 minimum wage rate to be paid if it is higher than other minimum wage rates, such as the subpart 22.4 statutory wage determination amount [@CL-CMP-8001].

## How it works

Who decides. The Secretary of Labor determines the amount of each annual increase [@CL-CMP-8000]. The section gives no date for an increase. The linked pages on the minimum wage policy and the annual rate notice cover subpart 22.19 [@CL-CMP-8000].

Who pays. The section does not name the payer. It says the clause requires the Executive Order 14026 rate to be paid when that rate is higher than other minimum wage rates [@CL-CMP-8001].

Threshold. The test is a comparison of rates. The $15.00 figure is the rate as of January 30, 2022 [@CL-CMP-7998; @CL-CMP-7999], and annual increases follow it [@CL-CMP-8000]. It is not a standing figure.

Exception. Supersession reaches Executive Order 13658 only to the extent that it is inconsistent with Executive Order 14026 [@CL-CMP-7998].

Not stated here. The section does not say what the clause requires when the Executive Order 14026 rate is not higher, and it does not say which workers are covered [@CL-CMP-8001; @CL-CMP-7999]. The linked pages cover coverage and the statutory wage determination.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

Assume that in a made-up year the annual rate set by the Secretary of Labor under Executive Order 14026 is $17.50 per hour [@CL-CMP-8000]. A laborer classification on a construction contract has a statutory wage determination amount of $16.00 per hour. The Executive Order 14026 rate is higher, so the clause at 52.222-55 requires $17.50 to be paid [@CL-CMP-8001]. A second classification has a statutory amount of $19.00 per hour. The Executive Order 14026 rate is not higher, so the sentence on the clause in this section states no payment requirement for that classification [@CL-CMP-8001]. This section does not say what applies to that classification instead; the linked wage rate page covers the statutory wage determination.

## Never confuse

Never confuse the $15.00 rate that applied as of January 30, 2022 with the rate in force later, because annual increases follow it [@CL-CMP-7998; @CL-CMP-7999; @CL-CMP-8000].

Never confuse the executive order minimum wage with the statutory wage determination amount under subpart 22.4, because the section treats them as different rates and compares them [@CL-CMP-8001].
