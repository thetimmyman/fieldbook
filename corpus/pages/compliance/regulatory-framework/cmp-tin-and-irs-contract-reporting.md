## Purpose

Shows what the contractor must furnish, what the Government must do with the taxpayer identification number, how contract information is reported to the Internal Revenue Service, and when the contracting officer inserts the taxpayer identification provision [@CL-CMP-7558; @CL-CMP-7560; @CL-CMP-7565].

## The rule

Each contractor doing business with a Government agency must furnish its taxpayer identification number to that agency [@CL-CMP-7558]. The Government must include the number of the contractor receiving payment with each certified voucher that the Government payment office prepares and submits to a disbursing official [@CL-CMP-7559]. The Government may use the number to collect and report on any delinquent amounts arising out of the contractor's relationship with the Government [@CL-CMP-7711]. The number is also required for Government reporting of certain contract information and payment information to the Internal Revenue Service [@CL-CMP-7712]. The payment information report is in a separate section. That section says that section 6109 of title 26 of the United States Code requires a contractor to provide its taxpayer identification number if a Form 1099 is required, and that the payment office is responsible for submitting reports to the Internal Revenue Service [@CL-CMP-9753]. This card does not set out the rest of that section.

The subpart provides policies and procedures for obtaining taxpayer identification number (TIN) information that may be used for debt collection purposes, and contract information and payment information for submittal to the payment office for Internal Revenue Service (IRS) reporting purposes [@CL-SUP-9436]. As used in the subpart, a common parent is the corporate entity that owns or controls an affiliated group of corporations that files its Federal income tax returns on a consolidated basis, and of which the offeror is a member [@CL-SUP-9437].

Heads of Federal executive agencies are required to report certain contract information to the Internal Revenue Service [@CL-CMP-7560]. The required information applies to contract modifications that increase the amount of a contract awarded before January 1, 1989 by $50,000 or more and that are entered into on or after April 1, 1990 [@CL-CMP-7561]. The reporting requirement also applies to certain contracts and modifications to them in excess of $25,000 entered into on or after January 1, 1989, and the section does not list which contracts those are [@CL-CMP-7562].

The information to report is the contractor's name, address and taxpayer identification number, the name and taxpayer identification number of any common parent, the date of the contract action, the amount obligated on the action and the estimated contract completion date [@CL-CMP-7563]. The information is to be transmitted to the Internal Revenue Service through the Federal Procurement Data System [@CL-CMP-7564].

The contracting officer must insert the taxpayer identification provision in solicitations that both do not include the System for Award Management provision and are not conducted under the procedures of part 12 [@CL-CMP-7565].

## How it works

The contractor's duty is to furnish the number to the agency [@CL-CMP-7558]. The Government's duties are to put the number on each certified voucher and, through the heads of executive agencies, to report contract information [@CL-CMP-7559; @CL-CMP-7560]. The reporting duty and the transmission route are separate statements: agency heads must report, and the information is to be transmitted through the Federal Procurement Data System [@CL-CMP-7560; @CL-CMP-7564]. The defense supplement adds that DoD uses the Federal Procurement Data System (FPDS) to meet these reporting requirements [@CL-MKT-9146]. The supplement paragraph as stored does not name those requirements, and this card does not say which they are.

Which actions fall inside the report depends on the date the contract was awarded, the date the modification was entered into and the dollar amounts. The second category is limited to certain contracts and modifications [@CL-CMP-7561; @CL-CMP-7562]. The provision is a solicitation-stage step for the contracting officer, and the contractor's own answers under the provision are on the linked card [@CL-CMP-7565].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contract was awarded in 1987 for 300,000 dollars. A modification entered into in 1991 increases it by 60,000 dollars. The increase is over 50,000 dollars and the modification was entered into after April 1, 1990, so it falls within the first category and the agency head's report covers the contractor's name, address and taxpayer identification number, the common parent's name and taxpayer identification number if any, the action date, the amount obligated and the estimated completion date, transmitted through the Federal Procurement Data System [@CL-CMP-7561; @CL-CMP-7563; @CL-CMP-7564].

A modification entered into in 1988 that increases the same contract by 60,000 dollars is outside the first category because it was not entered into on or after April 1, 1990, and outside the second because it was entered into before January 1, 1989 [@CL-CMP-7561; @CL-CMP-7562].

A solicitation that includes the System for Award Management provision is outside the insertion rule of this section for the taxpayer identification provision [@CL-CMP-7565].

## Never confuse

The two reporting categories are not the same trigger: the first keys off a contract awarded before January 1, 1989 and a modification that adds $50,000 or more and is entered into on or after April 1, 1990, while the second covers certain contracts and modifications over $25,000 entered into on or after January 1, 1989 [@CL-CMP-7561; @CL-CMP-7562]. Furnishing the number to the agency is also separate from the agency's own reporting duty, which sits with the heads of executive agencies [@CL-CMP-7558; @CL-CMP-7560].
