## Purpose

Shows how the DoD part on major system acquisition defines an earned value management system, an acceptable one and production of a major defense acquisition program, and where the prohibition on a lowest price technically acceptable approach for certain development work sits.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-CMP-7859] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 234.001-70 [@CL-CMP-7859]; DFARS 234.005-2 [@CL-CMP-7863] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-7859] |
| Applies to | Terms as used in the DoD part on major system acquisition: earned value management systems and production of major defense acquisition programs [@CL-CMP-8073; @CL-CMP-7859; @CL-CMP-7861]; mission-oriented solicitations for engineering and manufacturing development [@CL-CMP-7863] |

The definitions apply as the terms are used in the part that contains the section [@CL-CMP-8073]. An acceptable earned value management system is one that generally complies with the system criteria in paragraph (b) of 252.234-7002, Earned Value Management System [@CL-CMP-7859]. An earned value management system is one that complies with the earned value management system guidelines in the ANSI/EIA-748 [@CL-CMP-7860].

Production of a major defense acquisition program means the production and deployment of a major system that is intended to achieve an operational capability that satisfies mission needs [@CL-CMP-7861]. It also means, as an alternative, an activity otherwise defined as Milestone C under Department of Defense Instruction 5000.02 or related authorities [@CL-CMP-7862].

The section on mission-oriented solicitations does not set the prohibition itself. It refers the reader to 215.101-2-70(b)(2) for the prohibition on the use of the lowest price technically acceptable source selection process for engineering and manufacturing development of a major defense acquisition program for which budgetary authority is requested beginning in fiscal year 2019 [@CL-CMP-7863]. Under that rule, contracting officers shall not use the process to acquire that development work [@CL-EST-1541].

## How it works

The section separates two ideas about earned value: a system that complies with the guidelines in the ANSI/EIA-748, and an acceptable system that generally complies with the clause criteria [@CL-CMP-7860; @CL-CMP-7859]. The section does not say who decides whether a system generally complies. Production of a major defense acquisition program is defined by what the activity is meant to achieve, or alternatively by its Milestone C status under the DoD instruction [@CL-CMP-7861; @CL-CMP-7862]. The mission-oriented solicitation section does not restate the source selection prohibition; it points to 215.101-2-70(b)(2) [@CL-CMP-7863]. The prohibition is tied to engineering and manufacturing development for which budgetary authority is requested beginning in fiscal year 2019, and contracting officers are the ones barred from using the process [@CL-CMP-7863; @CL-EST-1541].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A program office plans engineering and manufacturing development for a major defense acquisition program and requests budgetary authority for it beginning in fiscal year 2019. The prohibition referred to in the mission-oriented solicitation section applies, so the contracting officer may not use the lowest price technically acceptable process for that development work [@CL-CMP-7863; @CL-EST-1541].

One contractor system departs from the guidelines in the ANSI/EIA-748, so it is not an earned value management system under this definition [@CL-CMP-7860]. Another follows those guidelines and generally complies with the clause criteria, so it is both an earned value management system and an acceptable one [@CL-CMP-7859; @CL-CMP-7860].

A program activity that Department of Defense Instruction 5000.02 defines as Milestone C counts as production of a major defense acquisition program [@CL-CMP-7862].

## Never confuse

An acceptable earned value management system is not the same as an earned value management system: acceptance is measured by general compliance with the clause criteria, while the system definition is measured by the guidelines in the ANSI/EIA-748 [@CL-CMP-7859; @CL-CMP-7860]. Production of a major defense acquisition program is not the program's development phase, because the definition describes production and deployment, or an activity otherwise defined as Milestone C [@CL-CMP-7861; @CL-CMP-7862]. The prohibition on the lowest price technically acceptable process for that development phase is a separate rule that this section only cross-references [@CL-CMP-7863]. For the wider governance of a major system, see [Major system governance](../../program-finance/baseline/prg-far-major-system-governance.md); for the prohibition and its companions, see [DoD limits and prohibitions on lowest price technically acceptable source selection](../../estimating/pricing/est-dod-lpta-limits-and-prohibitions.md).
