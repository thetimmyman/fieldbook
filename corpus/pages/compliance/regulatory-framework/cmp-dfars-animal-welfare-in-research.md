## Purpose

Shows which registration and approval a contractor needs before animal work starts, which standards apply, who may inspect, and what follows noncompliance.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-CMP-4566] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 252.235-7002 [@CL-CMP-4566] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-4566] |
| Applies to | Contracts with the animal welfare clause [@CL-CMP-4566], and subcontracts involving research, development, test, and evaluation or training that use live vertebrate animals [@CL-CMP-4574] |

The contractor must register its research, development, test, and evaluation or training facility with the Secretary of Agriculture, unless it is otherwise exempt from that requirement [@CL-CMP-4566]. The exemption applies to a contractor that meets, for the duration of the activity, the conditions in the statute and regulations the clause cites [@CL-CMP-4716]. The contractor must also have its proposed animal use approved, in accordance with the Department of Defense instruction on the use of animals, by a DoD Component Headquarters Oversight Office [@CL-CMP-4567], and it must give the contracting officer evidence of the registration and the approval before beginning work [@CL-CMP-4568]. It must acquire animals in accordance with the Department of Defense instruction on the use of animals, in the version current at the time of award [@CL-CMP-4717].

The care and use of animals must conform with the pertinent laws of the United States, the regulations of the Department of Agriculture, and the policies and procedures of the Department of Defense [@CL-CMP-4571]. If the contract includes acquisition of training, the contractor must also comply with Department of Defense instruction 1322.24, Medical Readiness Training [@CL-CMP-4780]. The contracting officer may immediately suspend, in whole or in part, work and further payments under the contract for failure to comply with the requirements of paragraphs (a) through (c) of the clause [@CL-CMP-4572]. The suspension stays in effect until the contractor complies with the requirements [@CL-CMP-4718].

## How it works

Before work starts, the contractor registers the facility unless it is exempt (it may request registration by contacting the United States Department of Agriculture animal care office the clause names [@CL-CMP-4803]), gets approval of its proposed animal use, and gives the contracting officer evidence of the registration and the approval [@CL-CMP-4566; @CL-CMP-4567; @CL-CMP-4568]. During performance, it makes its animals, and the premises, facilities, vehicles, equipment and records that support animal care, available during business hours and at other times mutually agreeable to the contractor and the United States Department of Agriculture Office of Animal and Plant Health Inspection Service representative [@CL-CMP-4569]; the clause also names personnel representing the DoD component oversight offices and the contracting officer, to ascertain that the contractor is compliant with the animal welfare statute and regulations the clause cites [@CL-CMP-4570]. A suspension stays in effect until the contractor complies [@CL-CMP-4718], and failure to complete corrective action within the time the contracting officer specifies may result in termination and, if applicable, removal from the approved vendor list for live animals used in medical training [@CL-CMP-4573]. The contractor passes the substance of the clause, including its flowdown paragraph, down to subcontracts involving research, development, test, and evaluation or training that use live vertebrate animals [@CL-CMP-4574].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

Assume a research contract worth 200,000 that uses live animals, run in a facility that is not exempt. The contractor sends the contracting officer proof of its registration and of the Defense approval before the first day of work. Later the Department of Agriculture office finds that animal care records are missing. The contracting officer may suspend work and hold the next payment, assumed to be 50,000, until the contractor complies. If corrective action is not finished by the date the officer sets, the contract may be terminated.

## Never confuse

The animal welfare remedies are not the human subjects remedies: the contracting officer may immediately suspend, in whole or in part, work and further payments for failure to comply [@CL-CMP-4572], and failure to complete corrective action within the time the contracting officer specifies may result in termination [@CL-CMP-4573], while under the clause on protection of human subjects, failure to comply will result in the issuance of a stop-work order to immediately suspend, in whole or in part, work and further payment, or will result in other issuance of suspension of work and further payment for as long as determined necessary at the discretion of the contracting officer [@CL-CMP-4588; @CL-CMP-4726]; see [Protection of human subjects](cmp-dfars-protection-of-human-subjects.md) and [Stop-work orders](cmp-stop-work-orders.md). The contractor must register the facility with the Secretary of Agriculture unless it is otherwise exempt, and must have its proposed animal use approved by a DoD Component Headquarters Oversight Office [@CL-CMP-4566; @CL-CMP-4567], and it must furnish evidence of the registration and the approval to the contracting officer before beginning work [@CL-CMP-4568].
