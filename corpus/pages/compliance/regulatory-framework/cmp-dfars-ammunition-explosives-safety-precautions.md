## Purpose

Shows which manual applies to ammunition and explosives work, the contractor's safety duties, mishap notification, Government inspection and the subcontract flowdown.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-CMP-4500] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 252.223-7002 [@CL-CMP-4500] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-4500] |
| Applies to | Contracts that include the clause; the clause text names the DFARS section that prescribes its use [@CL-CMP-4552] |

The clause defines ammunition and explosives as liquid and solid propellants and explosives, pyrotechnics, incendiaries and smokes in the forms that follow in the definition [@CL-CMP-4498]. The listed forms are bulk, ammunition, rockets, missiles, warheads, devices, and components of the other listed forms, except for wholly inert items [@CL-CMP-4544]. The definition does not include certain listed materials unless the Contractor is using or incorporating them for initiation, propulsion, or detonation as an integral or component part of an explosive, an ammunition or explosive end item, or of a weapon system [@CL-CMP-4499]. The listed materials are alternatives: inert components containing no explosives, propellants, or pyrotechnics; flammable liquids; acids; oxidizers; powdered metals; or other materials having fire or explosive characteristics [@CL-CMP-4545].

The Contractor must comply with DoD Manual 4145.26, DoD Contractors' Safety Manual for Ammunition and Explosives, in effect on the date of the solicitation [@CL-CMP-4500]. It must allow the Government access to its facilities, personnel, and safety program documentation, and must allow authorized Government representatives to evaluate safety programs, implementation, and facilities [@CL-CMP-4501]. If a mishap involving ammunition or explosives occurs, the Contractor must notify the Contracting Officer immediately [@CL-CMP-4506]. Its other listed mishap duties are to conduct an investigation in accordance with other provisions of the contract or as the Contracting Officer requires, and to submit a written report to the Contracting Officer [@CL-CMP-4507].

The Contractor must insert the clause, including its subcontractors paragraph, in every subcontract that involves ammunition or explosives [@CL-CMP-4510]. The inserted clause must include a provision allowing authorized Government safety representatives to evaluate subcontractor safety programs, implementation, and facilities as the Government determines necessary [@CL-CMP-4511]. The Contractor and higher tier subcontractors must also include provisions to allow direction to cease performance of the subcontract if a serious uncorrected or recurring safety deficiency potentially causes an imminent hazard to DoD personnel, property, or contract performance [@CL-CMP-4550]. The Contractor must notify the Contracting Officer or authorized representative before issuing any subcontract that involves ammunition or explosives [@CL-CMP-4512]. If the proposed subcontract represents a change in the place of performance, the Contractor must request approval for the change in accordance with the clause entitled Change in Place of Performance—Ammunition and Explosives [@CL-CMP-4551]; see [Change in place of performance for ammunition and explosives (DFARS)](cmp-dfars-ammunition-explosives-place-of-performance.md).

## How it works

If the Contracting Officer notifies the Contractor of any noncompliance with the manual or schedule provisions, the Contractor must take immediate steps to correct it [@CL-CMP-4502], and it has 30 days from the notification to correct it and report the actions taken, unless the Contracting Officer directs a different period [@CL-CMP-4504]. The Contractor is not entitled to reimbursement of the cost of correction unless the contract specifies it elsewhere [@CL-CMP-4503]. If it refuses or fails to correct noncompliances within the time period specified by the Contracting Officer, the Government has the right to direct it to cease performance on all or part of the contract [@CL-CMP-4505], and it shall not resume performance until the Contracting Officer is satisfied that the corrective action was effective and informs the Contractor [@CL-CMP-4565]. The Contracting Officer may also remove Government personnel at any time the Contractor is in noncompliance with any safety requirement of the clause [@CL-CMP-4549]. If the direction to cease work or the removal of Government personnel results in increased costs to the Contractor, the Contractor shall not be entitled to an adjustment in the contract price or a change in the delivery or performance schedule unless the Contracting Officer later determines that the Contractor had in fact complied with the manual or schedule provisions [@CL-CMP-4546; @CL-CMP-4547], and if the Contractor is entitled to an equitable adjustment, it is made in accordance with the Changes clause [@CL-CMP-4548].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor makes rocket motors and receives a written notice of a noncompliance with the manual. Assumed: the Contracting Officer does not change the 30-day period, and the contract has no term that reimburses correction costs. The contractor fixes the problem in 20 days at a cost of 40,000 and reports the actions within the 30 days. The 40,000 is its own cost. Had it failed to correct in time and the Government had directed it to stop work, the cost of the stoppage would not support a price or schedule adjustment unless the Contracting Officer later determined that the contractor had in fact complied with the manual.

## Never confuse

Government surveillance is not an assumption of the safety responsibility: nothing in the clause, and no Government action or failure to act in surveillance of the contract, relieves the Contractor of its responsibility for the safety of the Contractor's personnel and property, the Government's personnel and property, or the general public [@CL-CMP-4508]. Neither the number or frequency of Government inspections nor the degree of surveillance relieves the Contractor of its responsibility for contract performance [@CL-CMP-4509]. The mishap notification duty applies if a mishap involving ammunition or explosives occurs [@CL-CMP-4506]. In the clause on sensitive arms, ammunition and explosives, arms, ammunition, and explosives means those items within the scope of DoD Manual 5100.76, Physical Security of Sensitive Conventional Arms, Ammunition, and Explosives [@CL-CMP-4527]; see [Safeguarding sensitive conventional arms, ammunition and explosives (DFARS)](cmp-dfars-sensitive-arms-ammunition-explosives-safeguarding.md). For notice of potential safety issues in systems and parts, see [Notification of potential safety issues and warranty tracking of serialized items (DFARS)](../business-systems/cmp-dfars-safety-notification-and-warranty-tracking.md), and for critical safety items see [Critical safety items clause (DFARS)](../../supply-chain/sourcing-evidence/sup-critical-safety-items.md).
