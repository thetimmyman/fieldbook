## Purpose

Shows what is withheld or suspended when a labor standards violation is believed or found, who decides, and what happens to the money.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-2352] |
| Codified citation | FAR 22.406-9 [@CL-CMP-2352; @CL-CMP-2355] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-2352] |
| Applies to | Payments to a contractor when the contracting officer believes a violation exists or the Department of Labor requests withholding, and further payments to a contractor or subcontractor that fails or refuses to comply with the labor standards clauses [@CL-CMP-2352; @CL-CMP-2355] |

If the contracting officer believes a violation exists, or upon request of the Department of Labor, the contracting officer must withhold from payments due the contractor an amount equal to the estimated wage underpayment and estimated liquidated damages due the United States under the Contract Work Hours and Safety Standards statute [@CL-CMP-2352; @CL-CMP-2477]. The withholding is not limited to one contract: in the same circumstances, the contracting officer must withhold funds from any current Federal contract or Federally assisted contract with the same prime contractor that is subject to either the Construction Wage Rate Requirements statute or the Contract Work Hours and Safety Standards statute requirements [@CL-CMP-2352; @CL-CMP-2353]. If a later investigation confirms violations, the contracting officer must adjust the withholding as necessary, but if the Department of Labor requested the withholding, the contracting officer must not reduce or release it without written approval of the Department of Labor [@CL-CMP-2475].

Separately, the suspension provision covers the case where a contractor or subcontractor fails or refuses to comply with the labor standards clauses of the Construction Wage Rate Requirements statute and related statutes [@CL-CMP-2355]. In that case, the agency, upon its own action or upon the written request of the Department of Labor, must suspend any further payment, advance, or guarantee of funds until the violations cease or until the agency has withheld sufficient funds to compensate employees for back wages and to cover any liquidated damages due [@CL-CMP-2355; @CL-CMP-2356].

Withheld funds are used, as provided in the disposition paragraph, to satisfy assessed liquidated damages and, unless the contractor makes restitution, validated wage underpayments [@CL-CMP-2354]. Upon final administrative determination, if the contractor or subcontractor has not made restitution, the contracting officer must follow the Department of Labor guidance published in All Agency Memorandum number 215 [@CL-CMP-2357]. When funds withheld exceed the amount required to satisfy validated wage underpayments and assessed liquidated damages, the funds are returned to the contractor [@CL-CMP-2476]. If the Department of Labor requested the withholding or if the findings are disputed, the contracting officer must not return the funds to the contractor without approval by the Department of Labor [@CL-CMP-2358].

## How it works

Two tools are used. Withholding takes an estimated amount out of payments due, and the contracting officer decides it on belief of a violation or on request of the Department of Labor [@CL-CMP-2352]. Suspension stops further payment, advance or guarantee of funds, and the agency decides it on its own action or on written request of the Department of Labor [@CL-CMP-2355; @CL-CMP-2356]. Money held is applied to liquidated damages and, unless the contractor makes restitution, to wage underpayments [@CL-CMP-2354]. The Department of Labor keeps a say over any release or return of funds when it requested the withholding or the findings are disputed [@CL-CMP-2475; @CL-CMP-2358]. The printed section also covers how funds withheld or collected for liquidated damages are disposed of, and this card does not draft that part.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

On a construction contract the contracting officer believes a violation exists and estimates a wage underpayment of 30,000 and liquidated damages of 5,000. The contracting officer withholds 35,000 from payments due. Later the Department of Labor, which had requested the withholding, disputes part of the findings. The answer: the contracting officer may not return any of the withheld funds to the contractor without the approval of the Department of Labor, and if the contractor makes no restitution the validated underpayment is forwarded under the Department of Labor guidance.

## Never confuse

Withholding and suspension are different. Withholding holds an amount equal to the estimated underpayment and damages out of payments due [@CL-CMP-2352]. Suspension stops further payment, advance or guarantee of funds until the violations cease or enough funds are withheld [@CL-CMP-2355; @CL-CMP-2356]. The overtime standard that produces the liquidated damages has its own card, [Contract Work Hours and Safety Standards overtime](../../operating-model/talent/opm-contract-work-hours-overtime.md), and is not restated here. The minimum wage and paid sick leave enforcement rules have their own withholding steps, which are on [Executive Order minimum wage and paid sick leave](cmp-eo-minimum-wage-and-paid-sick-leave.md).
