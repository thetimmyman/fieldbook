## Purpose

Shows the written code duty and the written disclosure duty in the clause, the internal control system the contractor must establish, and who is excused from the control system paragraph.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-2625] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.203-13 [@CL-CMP-2625] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.203-13 [@CL-CMP-0599] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-2625] |
| Applies to | Solicitations and contracts expected to exceed $7.5 million with a performance period of 120 days or more, with two exceptions for the control system paragraph [@CL-CMP-2675; @CL-CMP-2628] |

The clause is inserted as prescribed in FAR 3.1004(a) [@CL-CMP-2625]. FAR 3.1004(a) directs insertion in solicitations and contracts if the value of the contract is expected to exceed $7.5 million and the performance period is 120 days or more [@CL-CMP-2675]. Within 30 days after contract award, unless the Contracting Officer establishes a longer time period, the Contractor shall have a written code of business ethics and conduct and make a copy available to each employee engaged in performance of the contract [@CL-CMP-2695]. The Contractor shall timely disclose, in writing, to the agency Office of the Inspector General, with a copy to the Contracting Officer, whenever, in connection with the award, performance, or closeout of this contract or any subcontract under it, it has credible evidence of listed conduct [@CL-CMP-2626]. The listed conduct is that a principal, employee, agent, or subcontractor has committed a violation of Federal criminal law involving fraud, conflict of interest, bribery, or gratuity violations found in Title 18 of the United States Code, or a violation of the civil False Claims Act [@CL-CMP-2627].

Paragraph (c) of the clause does not apply if the Contractor has represented itself as a small business concern pursuant to the award, or if the contract is for the acquisition of a commercial product or commercial service as defined in the FAR [@CL-CMP-2628]. Where it applies, the Contractor shall establish the listed items within 90 days after contract award, unless the Contracting Officer establishes a longer time period [@CL-CMP-2629]. At a minimum, the internal control system shall provide for the listed items, and the first is assignment of responsibility at a sufficiently high level and adequate resources to ensure effectiveness of the business ethics awareness and compliance program and internal control system [@CL-CMP-2630]. Another is reasonable efforts not to include as a principal an individual whom due diligence would have exposed as having engaged in conduct in conflict with the code of business ethics and conduct [@CL-CMP-2631]. Another is periodic reviews of company business practices, procedures, policies, and internal controls for compliance with the code and the special requirements of Government contracting [@CL-CMP-2632]. Another is an internal reporting mechanism, such as a hotline, which allows for anonymity or confidentiality, with instructions that encourage employees to report [@CL-CMP-2633]. Another is disciplinary action for improper conduct or for failing to take reasonable steps to prevent or detect it [@CL-CMP-2634]. Another is full cooperation with any Government agencies responsible for audits, investigations, or corrective actions [@CL-CMP-2636]. The list also includes timely disclosure, and the disclosure requirement for an individual contract continues until at least 3 years after final payment on the contract [@CL-CMP-2635].

## How it works

The clause has two layers. The written code duty and the disclosure duty sit in the code paragraph, which is outside paragraph (c), so the two exceptions printed in paragraph (c) do not by their terms remove them [@CL-CMP-2628; @CL-CMP-2695]. The control system paragraph is built from listed items and has its own two exceptions [@CL-CMP-2628]. Its timely disclosure item, with the continuing period after final payment, sits inside that paragraph and so is subject to those exceptions [@CL-CMP-2628; @CL-CMP-2635]. The code duty has a day count, and the control system items have a longer one, but the printed clause sets no number of days for the timely disclosure [@CL-CMP-2695; @CL-CMP-2629]. The Contractor shall include the substance of the clause in subcontracts that exceed the threshold specified in FAR 3.1004(a) on the date of subcontract award and a performance period of more than 120 days [@CL-CMP-2691].

Under the RFO text, the DoD class deviation directs DoD contracting officers to use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text, effective February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. The RFO text keeps the written code duty within 30 days after contract award, unless the Contracting Officer establishes a longer time period [@CL-CMP-0599; @CL-CMP-2695]. It keeps the duty to timely disclose in writing to the agency Office of the Inspector General, with a copy to the Contracting Officer, when the Contractor has credible evidence of the listed conduct [@CL-CMP-0600; @CL-CMP-0616; @CL-CMP-2626; @CL-CMP-2627]. It keeps the exceptions to paragraph (c) for a small business concern and for a commercial product or commercial service [@CL-CMP-0617; @CL-CMP-2628], and keeps the 90-day period for the control system items, unless the Contracting Officer establishes a longer time period [@CL-CMP-0618; @CL-CMP-2629]. It keeps the rule that the disclosure requirement for an individual contract continues until at least 3 years after final payment [@CL-CMP-0619; @CL-CMP-2635], and keeps the flowdown to subcontracts that exceed the threshold in FAR 3.1004(a) with a performance period of more than 120 days [@CL-CMP-0620; @CL-CMP-2691].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A company holds a 10 million contract with a 12 month performance period that is not for a commercial product or service, and it did not represent itself as a small business when it won the award. Within 30 days of award it had a written code and gave a copy to each employee on the contract. Later it finds credible evidence that an employee committed a Title 18 fraud violation on the contract. It must disclose in writing to the agency Office of the Inspector General and copy the Contracting Officer. It also had to set up the awareness program and internal control system within 90 days of award, unless the Contracting Officer set a longer period. Had it represented itself as a small business, the control system paragraph would not apply, but the disclosure duty in the code paragraph would still be written in the clause.

## Never confuse

The clause's disclosure duty is a contract term. Suspension and debarment exposure for a principal's knowing failure to timely disclose is a separate FAR rule, and it is covered with the insertion rule in [Contractor code of business ethics and conduct](cmp-contractor-code-of-business-ethics.md). The internal reporting mechanism that the control system requires is not the hotline poster display duty, which is on [Display of hotline poster clause](cmp-hotline-poster-clause.md).
