## Purpose

Shows what triggers the cancellation and rescission clause, what triggers the price or fee adjustment clause, and what amounts the Government may recover under each.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-2615; @CL-CMP-2620] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.203-8 and FAR 52.203-10 [@CL-CMP-2615; @CL-CMP-2620] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.203-8 [@CL-CMP-1303; @CL-CMP-1307] and RFO FAR 52.203-10 [@CL-CMP-1308; @CL-CMP-1318] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-2615; @CL-CMP-2620] |
| Applies to | Solicitations and contracts that exceed the simplified acquisition threshold, other than those for commercial products or commercial services [@CL-CMP-2687] |

FAR 3.104-9 directs insertion of the clauses it lists in solicitations and contracts that exceed the simplified acquisition threshold, other than those for commercial products or commercial services [@CL-CMP-2687]. The cancellation clause is inserted as prescribed in FAR 3.104-9(a) [@CL-CMP-2615]. If the Government receives information that a contractor or a person has violated the statute on obtaining and disclosing certain information, the Government may cancel the solicitation if the contract has not yet been awarded or issued [@CL-CMP-2616]. The Government may instead rescind the contract with respect to which the Contractor or someone acting for the Contractor has been convicted for an offense where the conduct violates title 41 of the United States Code, section 2102 for the purpose of either exchanging the information covered by such subsections for anything of value, or obtaining or giving anyone a competitive advantage in the award of a Federal agency procurement contract [@CL-CMP-2617; @CL-CMP-7839]. Another ground for rescission is a determination by the head of the contracting activity, based upon a preponderance of the evidence, that the Contractor or someone acting for it engaged in conduct punishable under title 41 of the United States Code, section 2105(a) [@CL-CMP-2618]. If the Government rescinds, it is entitled to recover, in addition to any penalty prescribed by law, the amount expended under the contract [@CL-CMP-2619].

The price or fee adjustment clause is inserted as prescribed in FAR 3.104-9(b) [@CL-CMP-2620]. The Government, at its election, may reduce the price of a fixed-price type contract and the total cost and fee under a cost-type contract by the amount of profit or fee determined under the clause, conditional on a determination by the head of the contracting activity or designee [@CL-CMP-2621]. That determination is that there was a violation of the statute as implemented in the FAR section the clause cites [@CL-CMP-2622]. Among the listed reductions, for cost-plus-fixed-fee contracts the reduction is the amount of the fee specified in the contract at the time of award [@CL-CMP-2623]. For cost-plus-incentive-fee contracts it is the target fee specified in the contract at the time of award, notwithstanding any minimum fee or fee floor [@CL-CMP-2688]. For cost-plus-award-fee contracts it includes the base fee established in the contract at the time of award [@CL-CMP-2689]. If such a contract specifies no base fee, it is 30 percent of each award fee otherwise payable for each award fee evaluation period or at each award fee determination point [@CL-CMP-2699]. For fixed-price-incentive contracts, the Government may reduce the contract target price and contract target profit both by an amount equal to the initial target profit specified in the contract at the time of contract award [@CL-CMP-2690]. Alternatively, if an immediate adjustment to the contract target price and contract target profit would have a significant adverse impact on the incentive price revision relationship under the contract, or adversely affect the contract financing provisions [@CL-CMP-7873], the Contracting Officer may defer such adjustment until establishment of the total final price of the contract [@CL-CMP-7874]. The total final price established in accordance with the incentive price revision provisions of the contract shall be reduced by an amount equal to the initial target profit specified in the contract at the time of contract award, and that reduced price shall be the total final contract price [@CL-CMP-7875]. For firm-fixed-price contracts the reduction is 10 percent of the initial contract price, or a profit amount the Contracting Officer determines from records or documents that existed before award [@CL-CMP-2624].

The Government may, at its election, reduce a prime contractor's price or fee in accordance with the procedures of paragraph (b) of the clause for violations of the statute by its subcontractors [@CL-CMP-2692], by an amount not to exceed the amount of profit or fee reflected in the subcontract at the time the subcontract was first definitively priced [@CL-CMP-2694]. In addition to the price or fee reductions, the Government may terminate the contract for default, and its rights and remedies under the clause are not exclusive and are in addition to any other rights and remedies provided by law or under the contract [@CL-CMP-2693].

## How it works

If the Government receives information that a contractor or a person has violated title 41 of the United States Code, sections 2102 through 2104, it may cancel the solicitation if the contract has not yet been awarded or issued, or rescind the contract where the Contractor or someone acting for the Contractor has been convicted as the clause describes or the head of the contracting activity has made the determination the clause describes [@CL-CMP-2616; @CL-CMP-2617; @CL-CMP-2618]. If the Government rescinds, it is entitled to recover, in addition to any penalty prescribed by law, the amount expended under the contract [@CL-CMP-2619]. The price or fee reduction may be made if the head of the contracting activity or designee determines that there was a violation of title 41 of the United States Code, section 2102 or 2103 [@CL-CMP-2621; @CL-CMP-2622], and the reduction shall be the amount the clause sets for the contract type [@CL-CMP-2623]. The clause sets amounts for cost-plus-incentive-fee, cost-plus-award-fee, fixed-price-incentive and firm-fixed-price contracts [@CL-CMP-2688; @CL-CMP-2689; @CL-CMP-2690; @CL-CMP-2624]. It also reaches subcontractor violations and allows termination for default as an added remedy [@CL-CMP-2692; @CL-CMP-2693]. For fixed-price-incentive contracts, the Contracting Officer may defer the adjustment in the case the clause describes [@CL-CMP-7873; @CL-CMP-7874].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text from February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. RFO FAR 52.203-8 keeps the rule that the Government may cancel the solicitation, if the contract has not yet been awarded or issued [@CL-CMP-1303; @CL-CMP-2616]. It keeps rescission of a contract with respect to which the Contractor or someone acting for it has been convicted for an offense where the conduct violates a section the clause cites for one of two listed purposes [@CL-CMP-1304; @CL-CMP-1305; @CL-CMP-2617]. It keeps rescission on a determination by the head of the contracting activity, based upon a preponderance of the evidence, that the Contractor or someone acting for it engaged in conduct punishable under a section the clause cites [@CL-CMP-1306; @CL-CMP-2618]. It keeps the right to recover, in addition to any penalty prescribed by law, the amount expended under the contract [@CL-CMP-1307; @CL-CMP-2619].

RFO FAR 52.203-10 keeps the Government's election to reduce the price or fee conditional on a determination by the head of the contracting activity or designee [@CL-CMP-1308; @CL-CMP-2621], and that the determination is that there was a violation of title 41 of the United States Code, section 2102 or 2103, as implemented in section 3.104 of the Federal Acquisition Regulation [@CL-CMP-1309; @CL-CMP-2622]. It keeps the reduction for cost-plus-fixed-fee contracts [@CL-CMP-1310; @CL-CMP-2623] and for cost-plus-incentive-fee contracts, notwithstanding any minimum fee or fee floor [@CL-CMP-1311; @CL-CMP-2688]. It keeps the base fee for cost-plus-award-fee contracts [@CL-CMP-1312; @CL-CMP-2689] and the 30 percent figure where no base fee is specified [@CL-CMP-1313; @CL-CMP-2699]. It keeps the reduction of the contract target price and contract target profit for fixed-price-incentive contracts [@CL-CMP-1314; @CL-CMP-2690], which the Contracting Officer may instead defer until establishment of the total final price of the contract if an immediate adjustment would have a significant adverse impact on the incentive price revision relationship or adversely affect the contract financing provisions [@CL-CMP-7086; @CL-CMP-7087], and the 10 percent or profit-amount reduction for firm-fixed-price contracts [@CL-CMP-1315; @CL-CMP-2624]. It keeps the reduction for subcontractor violations [@CL-CMP-1316; @CL-CMP-2692], capped at the profit or fee reflected in the subcontract when it was first definitively priced [@CL-CMP-1317; @CL-CMP-2694], and the added remedy of termination for default, with rights and remedies that are not exclusive [@CL-CMP-1318; @CL-CMP-2693].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A cost-plus-fixed-fee contract above the simplified acquisition threshold, not for commercial products or commercial services, had a fee of 300,000 specified at award. After a determination of a covered violation, the Government may reduce the total cost and fee by 300,000. On a different contract, 2 million has been expended when the Government rescinds after a conviction. The Government is entitled to recover the 2 million, plus any penalty the law prescribes.

## Never confuse

The price or fee reduction is not the rescission remedy: under the price clause the Government may reduce the price or fee by the amount of profit or fee determined under the clause [@CL-CMP-2621], and on rescission the Government is entitled to recover the amount expended under the contract [@CL-CMP-2619]. The price clause refers to a violation as implemented in section 3.104 of the Federal Acquisition Regulation [@CL-CMP-2622], and that section's rules are covered in [Procurement integrity: bid and proposal information and the disclosure and obtaining bans](cmp-procurement-integrity-prohibitions.md) and [Procurement integrity: protection, marking and possible violations](cmp-procurement-integrity-marking-and-violations.md). For annulment under the covenant against contingent fees, see [Independent price certificate and covenant against contingent fees](cmp-independent-price-and-contingent-fee-clauses.md). For the agency head's power to void and rescind a contract after a final conviction, see [Voiding and rescinding contracts after a conviction or proven offense](cmp-voiding-and-rescinding-contracts.md).
