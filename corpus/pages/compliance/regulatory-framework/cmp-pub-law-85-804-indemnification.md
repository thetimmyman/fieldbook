## Purpose

Explains the clause under which the Government indemnifies a contractor against unusually hazardous or nuclear risks that the contract defines, with the limits, the contractor's duties and the cost-reimbursement alternate [@CL-CMP-5574; @CL-CMP-5575].

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-5574] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.250-1, including Alternate I [@CL-CMP-5574; @CL-CMP-5706] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.250-1, including Alternate I [@CL-CMP-2119; @CL-CMP-2152] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-5574] |
| Applies to | Contracts in which the clause is inserted as prescribed; Alternate I applies in cost-reimbursement contracts [@CL-CMP-5574; @CL-CMP-5706] |

Under Public Law 85-804 and Executive Order 10789, as amended, and regardless of any other contract provision, the Government indemnifies the contractor against the items listed in paragraph (b), subject to the limitations in the other paragraphs of the clause [@CL-CMP-5694]. Those items are third-person claims, including claims by the contractor's employees and reasonable expenses of litigation or settlement, for death, personal injury, or loss of, damage to, or loss of use of property [@CL-CMP-5576]. They also include loss of, damage to, or loss of use of contractor property and of Government property, but loss of profit is excluded [@CL-CMP-5577].

The indemnity applies only to the extent that the claim, loss or damage arises out of a risk the contract defines as unusually hazardous or nuclear and is not compensated for by insurance or otherwise [@CL-CMP-5575]. Which risks qualify therefore depends on the definition in the contract [@CL-CMP-5575]. A claim, loss or damage is not covered to the extent that it is within the deductible amounts of the contractor's insurance [@CL-CMP-5578]. If insurance coverage or other financial protection in effect on the date the approving official authorizes use of the clause is reduced, the Government's liability does not increase as a result [@CL-CMP-5697].

When willful misconduct or lack of good faith on the part of any of the contractor's principal officials causes the claim, loss or damage, the contractor is not indemnified for the categories that paragraph (d) lists [@CL-CMP-5579]. Those categories are Government claims against the contractor, other than those arising through subrogation, and loss or damage affecting the contractor's property [@CL-CMP-5698]. The clause defines the contractor's principal officials as directors, officers, managers, superintendents or other representatives supervising or directing a listed scope, the first being all or substantially all of the contractor's business [@CL-CMP-5695]. The other two scopes are all or substantially all of the contractor's operations at any one plant or separate location where the contract is performed, and a separate and complete major industrial operation in connection with performing the contract [@CL-CMP-5696].

## How it works

With the contracting officer's prior written approval, the contractor may indemnify a subcontractor under any subcontract against a risk the contract defines as unusually hazardous or nuclear [@CL-CMP-5580]. A subcontractor indemnification must provide, between the contractor and the subcontractor, the same rights and duties, and the same provisions for notice, furnishing of evidence or proof, and Government settlement or defense of claims, as the clause provides [@CL-CMP-5699]. The contracting officer may also approve indemnification of subcontractors at any lower tier, under the same terms and conditions [@CL-CMP-5700]. The Government indemnifies the contractor against liability to subcontractors incurred under subcontract provisions the contracting officer approved [@CL-CMP-5581].

The contractor must promptly notify the contracting officer of any claim or action against it, or any loss by it or a subcontractor, that may reasonably be expected to involve indemnification under the clause [@CL-CMP-5582]. It must immediately furnish the Government copies of all pertinent papers it receives, and must furnish evidence or proof of any claim, loss or damage covered by the clause in the manner and form the Government requires [@CL-CMP-5703]. It must also comply with the Government's directions and execute any authorizations required in connection with settlement or defense of claims or actions [@CL-CMP-5704]. The Government may direct, control or assist in settling or defending any claim or action that may involve indemnification under the clause [@CL-CMP-5705].

The Government makes no payment under the clause unless the agency head determines that the amount is just and reasonable [@CL-CMP-5583]. It may pay the contractor or subcontractors, or may directly pay parties to whom they may be liable [@CL-CMP-5702]. The rights and obligations of the parties under the clause survive the contract's termination, expiration or completion [@CL-CMP-5701].

Alternate I applies in cost-reimbursement contracts and adds paragraph (i) to the basic clause [@CL-CMP-5706]. Under it, the cost of insurance, including self-insurance programs, covering a risk the contract defines as unusually hazardous or nuclear is not reimbursed except to the extent that the contracting officer has required or approved that insurance [@CL-CMP-5707]. The Government's obligations under the clause are also excepted from the release required under the contract's clause relating to allowable cost, and they are not affected by its Limitation of Cost or Limitation of Funds clause [@CL-CMP-5708].

Class Deviation 2026-O0038 directs DoD contracting officers to use the revised FAR part 52 published on the Revolutionary FAR Overhaul web page (the RFO text) in place of the codified part 52 text, effective February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. The RFO section 52.250-1 keeps the Government's indemnity under Public Law 85-804 and Executive Order 10789, as amended, regardless of any other contract provision and subject to the limitations in the other paragraphs of the clause [@CL-CMP-2119; @CL-CMP-5694]. It keeps the covered items: third-person claims, including claims by the contractor's employees and reasonable expenses of litigation or settlement, for death, personal injury, or loss of, damage to, or loss of use of property, and loss of, damage to, or loss of use of contractor property and Government property, excluding loss of profit [@CL-CMP-2120; @CL-CMP-2121; @CL-CMP-5576; @CL-CMP-5577].

It keeps the limits on the indemnity. The indemnity applies only to the extent that the claim, loss or damage arises out of a risk the contract defines as unusually hazardous or nuclear and is not compensated for by insurance or otherwise [@CL-CMP-2122; @CL-CMP-5575]. Any such claim, loss or damage, to the extent that it is within the deductible amounts of the contractor's insurance, is not covered [@CL-CMP-2123; @CL-CMP-5578]. If insurance coverage or other financial protection in effect on the date the approving official authorizes use of the clause is reduced, the Government's liability does not increase as a result [@CL-CMP-2124; @CL-CMP-5697]. When willful misconduct or lack of good faith on the part of any of the contractor's principal officials causes the claim, loss or damage, the contractor is not indemnified for Government claims against it, other than those arising through subrogation, or for loss or damage affecting its property [@CL-CMP-2140; @CL-CMP-2141; @CL-CMP-5579; @CL-CMP-5698]. It keeps the definition of the contractor's principal officials and its three scopes [@CL-CMP-2142; @CL-CMP-2143; @CL-CMP-5695; @CL-CMP-5696].

It keeps the rules on subcontractors: indemnification of a subcontractor needs the contracting officer's prior written approval, must provide the same rights, duties and provisions as the clause, may be approved for lower tiers on the same terms, and the Government indemnifies the contractor against liability to subcontractors under approved subcontract provisions [@CL-CMP-2144; @CL-CMP-2145; @CL-CMP-2146; @CL-CMP-5580; @CL-CMP-5699; @CL-CMP-5700; @CL-CMP-5581]. It keeps the survival of the parties' rights and obligations, the rule that the Government makes no payment unless the agency head determines that the amount is just and reasonable, and the Government's power to pay the contractor, the subcontractors or the parties to whom they may be liable [@CL-CMP-2147; @CL-CMP-2148; @CL-CMP-5701; @CL-CMP-5583; @CL-CMP-5702]. It keeps the contractor's duties to furnish the Government copies of pertinent papers and evidence or proof and to comply with the Government's directions, and the Government's right to direct, control or assist in settling or defending a claim [@CL-CMP-2150; @CL-CMP-2151; @CL-CMP-5703; @CL-CMP-5704; @CL-CMP-5705]. The RFO text prints the duty to notify the contracting officer promptly with the words "may be reasonably be expected" where the codified text prints "may reasonably be expected" [@CL-CMP-2149; @CL-CMP-5582].

Its Alternate I keeps the application to cost-reimbursement contracts and the added paragraph (i), under which the cost of insurance covering such a risk is not reimbursed except to the extent that the contracting officer has required or approved it, and the Government's obligations are excepted from the allowable cost release and not affected by the Limitation of Cost or Limitation of Funds clause [@CL-CMP-2152; @CL-CMP-2153; @CL-CMP-2154; @CL-CMP-5706; @CL-CMP-5707; @CL-CMP-5708].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contract defines a test operation as an unusually hazardous risk, and no principal official acted willfully or in bad faith. A third party obtains a 500,000 judgment against the contractor for injury caused by that operation. The contractor's policy has a 100,000 deductible and a 300,000 limit, so the contractor bears the first 100,000 and insurance pays 300,000. The remaining 100,000 is above the policy limit and uncompensated by insurance, so the clause covers it once the agency head determines that the amount is just and reasonable. The 100,000 inside the deductible is not covered. The contractor also lost 50,000 of profit because its own damaged test equipment sat idle, and the clause excludes loss of profit from its cover for contractor property.

The contractor notified the contracting officer promptly when the claim arrived and sent the Government the court papers at once. If the contract is cost-reimbursement and Alternate I applies, the policy premium is reimbursed only to the extent that the contracting officer required or approved the policy.

## Never confuse

Insurance and this indemnity are different layers. The policy pays under its own terms, while the clause reaches only the part of a claim that insurance or other compensation does not cover, and it excludes loss of profit from the contractor-property and Government-property items [@CL-CMP-5575; @CL-CMP-5577]. A deductible gap stays with the contractor rather than becoming an indemnified loss [@CL-CMP-5578]. A right to indemnity is also not a right to payment: payment waits for the agency head's determination that the amount is just and reasonable [@CL-CMP-5583]. Alternate I belongs only to cost-reimbursement contracts and is not part of the basic clause [@CL-CMP-5706].

The DFARS clauses on indemnification for unusually hazardous risks are covered in [Indemnification for unusually hazardous risks (DFARS)](../../supply-chain/sourcing-evidence/sup-dfars-indemnification-unusually-hazardous-risks.md), and the cost principle on insurance and indemnification is covered in [Insurance and indemnification](../cas-allowability/cmp-insurance-and-indemnification.md).
