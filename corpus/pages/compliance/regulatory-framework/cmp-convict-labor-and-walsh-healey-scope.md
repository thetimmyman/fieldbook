## Purpose

Sets out where the convict labor clause is inserted, which supply contracts must carry the stipulations of the Walsh-Healey Act, which acquisitions are exempt, and how an agency head asks the Secretary of Labor for a partial exemption.

## The rule

| Codified citation | FAR 22.202, FAR 22.602, FAR 22.603, FAR 22.604-1 and FAR 22.604-2 [@CL-CMP-6783; @CL-CMP-6788; @CL-CMP-7004; @CL-CMP-7005; @CL-CMP-6786] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-6783] |

The convict labor clause at FAR 52.222-3 is inserted in solicitations and contracts above the micro-purchase threshold when the contract will be performed in the United States, Puerto Rico, the Northern Mariana Islands, American Samoa, Guam or the United States Virgin Islands, unless an exception applies [@CL-CMP-6783]. It is not inserted when the contract will be subject to chapter 65 of title 41 of the United States Code, which contains a separate prohibition against the employment of convict labor, or when the supplies or services are to be purchased from Federal Prison Industries [@CL-CMP-6784]. It is also not inserted when the acquisition involves the purchase, from any State prison, of finished supplies that may be secured in the open market or from existing stocks, as distinguished from supplies requiring special fabrication [@CL-CMP-6785].

Except for the exemptions at FAR 22.604, a contract subject to the Walsh-Healey statute must include or incorporate by reference the stipulations required by the statute [@CL-CMP-6978; @CL-CMP-6788]. The requirement covers contracts entered into by an executive department, independent establishment or other agency or instrumentality of the United States, by the District of Columbia, or by a corporation all of whose stock is beneficially owned by the United States [@CL-CMP-6978; @CL-CMP-6979]. It applies to contracts for the manufacture or furnishing of materials, supplies, articles and equipment in any amount exceeding $20,000 [@CL-CMP-6979; @CL-CMP-6788]. The stipulations pertain to such matters as minimum wages, maximum hours, child labor, convict labor, and safe and sanitary working conditions [@CL-CMP-6788]. The requirement also reaches indefinite-delivery contracts, basic ordering agreements, blanket purchase agreements and subcontracts under Section 8(a) of the Small Business Act, for the manufacture or furnishing of supplies that will be performed in the United States, Puerto Rico or the United States Virgin Islands, exceed or may exceed $20,000, and are not exempt under FAR 22.604 [@CL-CMP-7003; @CL-CMP-7004].

The regulation fully exempts four categories of acquisition from the statute. Two are public utility services and supplies manufactured outside the United States, Puerto Rico and the United States Virgin Islands [@CL-CMP-6786]. The other two are purchases against the account of a defaulting contractor where the stipulations were not included in the defaulted contract, and newspapers, magazines or periodicals contracted for with sales agents or publisher representatives and delivered by the publishers [@CL-CMP-6787]. Contracts for acquisition of supplies are also exempt from the statute for any item in those situations where the contracting officer is authorized by the express language of a statute to purchase in the open market generally, such as commercial products and commercial services [@CL-CMP-7005], a specific purchase under the conditions of FAR 6.302-2 where immediate delivery is required by the public exigency [@CL-CMP-7006], and perishables and certain agricultural products [@CL-CMP-7007].

On the agency head's request, the Secretary of Labor may exempt specific contracts or classes of contracts from the inclusion or application of one or more of the stipulations [@CL-CMP-6789]. The request must include a finding by the agency head stating the reasons why the conduct of Government business will be seriously impaired unless the exemption is granted [@CL-CMP-6980]. A request that relates solely to safety and health standards goes to the Assistant Secretary for Occupational Safety and Health at the Department of Labor, and all other requests go to the Administrator of the Wage and Hour Division [@CL-CMP-6790].

## How it works

The convict labor clause is inserted according to the place of performance and the micro-purchase threshold [@CL-CMP-6783], unless the contract will be subject to chapter 65 of title 41 of the United States Code, which contains a separate prohibition against the employment of convict labor [@CL-CMP-6784]. The stipulations come with contracts subject to the statute, entered into by an agency for the manufacture or furnishing of supplies in any amount exceeding $20,000 [@CL-CMP-6978; @CL-CMP-6979; @CL-CMP-6788], and they pertain to such matters as convict labor [@CL-CMP-6788]. Those contracts will be performed in the United States, Puerto Rico or the United States Virgin Islands, exceed or may exceed $20,000 and are not exempt under FAR 22.604 [@CL-CMP-7004].

Contracts for the four listed acquisitions are fully exempt from the statute [@CL-CMP-6786; @CL-CMP-6787]. For a partial exemption, upon the request of the agency head, the Secretary of Labor may exempt specific contracts or classes of contracts from one or more of the stipulations, provided the request includes a finding by the agency head [@CL-CMP-6789; @CL-CMP-6980]. A request that relates solely to safety and health standards is transmitted to the Assistant Secretary for Occupational Safety and Health, and all other requests are transmitted to the Administrator of the Wage and Hour Division [@CL-CMP-6790].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A services contract to be performed in Guam is well above the micro-purchase threshold [@CL-CMP-6783]. The contract will not be subject to chapter 65 of title 41 of the United States Code, the services are not to be purchased from Federal Prison Industries, and the acquisition does not involve the purchase of finished supplies from a State prison [@CL-CMP-6784; @CL-CMP-6785], so the clause at 52.222-3 is inserted [@CL-CMP-6783].

A supply contract for $30,000, to be performed in the United States, is entered into by an executive department and no exemption applies [@CL-CMP-6978; @CL-CMP-7004]. It exceeds $20,000 [@CL-CMP-6788], so it must include or incorporate by reference the statute's stipulations, including the convict labor stipulation [@CL-CMP-6978; @CL-CMP-6788; @CL-CMP-7004]. As the contract will be subject to the statute, the clause at 52.222-3 is not inserted [@CL-CMP-6783; @CL-CMP-6784].

A purchase against the account of a defaulting contractor, where the defaulted contract did not include the stipulations, is fully exempt from the statute [@CL-CMP-6787].

For a class of supply contracts, the agency head asks the Secretary of Labor to exempt the class from one stipulation [@CL-CMP-6789]. The request includes a finding by the agency head stating the reasons why the conduct of Government business will be seriously impaired unless the exemption is granted [@CL-CMP-6980]. The stipulation concerns minimum wages [@CL-CMP-6788], so the request does not relate solely to safety and health standards and is transmitted to the Administrator of the Wage and Hour Division [@CL-CMP-6790].

## Never confuse

The clause at 52.222-3 is inserted in solicitations and contracts above the micro-purchase threshold that will be performed in the listed locations, unless the contract will be subject to chapter 65 of title 41 of the United States Code [@CL-CMP-6783; @CL-CMP-6784]. The stipulations come with contracts subject to the statute for the manufacture or furnishing of supplies in any amount exceeding $20,000 [@CL-CMP-6978; @CL-CMP-6788].

Contracts for the listed acquisitions are fully exempt from the statute [@CL-CMP-6786; @CL-CMP-6787]. Separately, upon the request of the agency head, the Secretary of Labor may exempt specific contracts or classes of contracts from one or more of the stipulations, provided the request includes a finding by the agency head [@CL-CMP-6789; @CL-CMP-6980].
