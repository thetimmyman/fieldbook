## Purpose

Sets out where the convict labor clause is inserted, which supply contracts must carry the stipulations of the Walsh-Healey Act, which acquisitions are exempt, and how an agency head asks the Secretary of Labor for a partial exemption. The clause text and the stipulations are on the linked pages.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-6783] |
| Codified citation | FAR 22.202, FAR 22.602 and FAR 22.604-2 [@CL-CMP-6783; @CL-CMP-6788; @CL-CMP-6786] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-6783] |
| Applies to | Solicitations and contracts above the micro-purchase threshold performed in a listed location, and covered supply contracts above the stipulation amount [@CL-CMP-6783; @CL-CMP-7004] |

The convict labor clause at FAR 52.222-3 is inserted in solicitations and contracts above the micro-purchase threshold when the contract will be performed in the United States, Puerto Rico, the Northern Mariana Islands, American Samoa, Guam or the United States Virgin Islands, unless an exception applies [@CL-CMP-6783]. It is not inserted when the contract will be subject to chapter 65 of title 41 of the United States Code, which contains a separate prohibition against the employment of convict labor, or when the supplies or services are to be purchased from Federal Prison Industries [@CL-CMP-6784]. It is also not inserted when the acquisition involves the purchase, from any State prison, of finished supplies that may be secured in the open market or from existing stocks, as distinguished from supplies requiring special fabrication [@CL-CMP-6785].

Except for the regulatory and statutory exemptions, a contract subject to the Walsh-Healey statute must include or incorporate by reference the stipulations required by the statute [@CL-CMP-6978; @CL-CMP-6788]. The requirement covers contracts entered into by an executive department, independent establishment or other agency or instrumentality of the United States, by the District of Columbia, or by a corporation all of whose stock is beneficially owned by the United States [@CL-CMP-6978; @CL-CMP-6979]. It applies to contracts for the manufacture or furnishing of materials, supplies, articles and equipment in any amount exceeding $20,000 [@CL-CMP-6979; @CL-CMP-6788]. The stipulations pertain to such matters as minimum wages, maximum hours, child labor, convict labor, and safe and sanitary working conditions [@CL-CMP-6788]. The requirement also reaches indefinite-delivery contracts, basic ordering agreements, blanket purchase agreements and subcontracts under Section 8(a) of the Small Business Act, where they are for the manufacture or furnishing of supplies [@CL-CMP-7003]. The listed conditions are performance in the United States, Puerto Rico or the United States Virgin Islands, a value that exceeds or may exceed $20,000, and no exemption [@CL-CMP-7004].

The regulation fully exempts four categories of acquisition from the statute. Two are public utility services and supplies manufactured outside the United States, Puerto Rico and the United States Virgin Islands [@CL-CMP-6786]. The other two are purchases against the account of a defaulting contractor where the stipulations were not included in the defaulted contract, and newspapers, magazines or periodicals contracted for with sales agents or publisher representatives and delivered by the publishers [@CL-CMP-6787]. The statutory exemptions are separate. They cover any item that the express language of a statute lets the contracting officer buy in the open market generally, such as commercial products and commercial services [@CL-CMP-7005], a specific purchase under the conditions of FAR 6.302-2 where immediate delivery is required by the public exigency [@CL-CMP-7006], and perishables and certain agricultural products [@CL-CMP-7007].

On the agency head's request, the Secretary of Labor may exempt specific contracts or classes of contracts from the inclusion or application of one or more of the stipulations [@CL-CMP-6789]. The request must include a finding stating the reasons why the conduct of Government business will be seriously impaired unless the exemption is granted [@CL-CMP-6980]. A request that relates solely to safety and health standards goes to the Assistant Secretary for Occupational Safety and Health at the Department of Labor, and all other requests go to the Administrator of the Wage and Hour Division [@CL-CMP-6790].

## How it works

Two separate tests decide which rule reaches a contract. The convict labor clause follows the place of performance and the micro-purchase threshold, so it can reach a contract that is not subject to the statute [@CL-CMP-6783]. The stipulations follow coverage by the statute, the type of purchase, the place of performance and the dollar amount, and they include a convict labor stipulation [@CL-CMP-6788; @CL-CMP-7004]. A contract that will be subject to the statute does not also take the convict labor clause, because the statute has its own prohibition [@CL-CMP-6784].

The regulation itself exempts the four categories, so no one needs to request an exemption for them [@CL-CMP-6786; @CL-CMP-6787]. For a partial exemption the decision belongs to the Secretary of Labor, on a request from the agency head that includes the finding [@CL-CMP-6789; @CL-CMP-6980]. The destination of the request depends on subject: safety and health only, or anything else [@CL-CMP-6790].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A services contract to be performed in Guam is well above the micro-purchase threshold. It is not subject to the statute and is not a purchase from Federal Prison Industries or a State prison, so the convict labor clause is inserted [@CL-CMP-6783; @CL-CMP-6784; @CL-CMP-6785].

A supply contract for $30,000, to be performed in the United States, is entered into by an executive department and no exemption applies. It exceeds the stipulation amount, so it must include or incorporate by reference the statute's stipulations, including the convict labor stipulation [@CL-CMP-6978; @CL-CMP-6788; @CL-CMP-7004]. The convict labor clause is not inserted, because the contract is subject to the statute [@CL-CMP-6784].

A purchase against the account of a defaulting contractor, where the defaulted contract did not include the stipulations, is fully exempt from the statute [@CL-CMP-6787].

For a class of non-exempt supply contracts, the agency head asks the Secretary of Labor to exempt the class from one stipulation. The request includes a finding of why Government business would be seriously impaired without the exemption [@CL-CMP-6789; @CL-CMP-6980]. The stipulation concerns wages, not safety and health, so the request goes to the Administrator of the Wage and Hour Division [@CL-CMP-6790].

## Never confuse

The convict labor clause and the Walsh-Healey stipulations are different routes to a convict labor prohibition. The clause is inserted by place of performance and threshold, while the stipulations come with a covered supply contract above the stipulation amount, and a contract subject to the statute does not take the clause [@CL-CMP-6783; @CL-CMP-6784; @CL-CMP-6788].

A full exemption from the statute is also different from a partial exemption. The exempt categories need no request, while a partial exemption from one or more stipulations is granted by the Secretary of Labor on the agency head's request and finding [@CL-CMP-6786; @CL-CMP-6789; @CL-CMP-6980].
