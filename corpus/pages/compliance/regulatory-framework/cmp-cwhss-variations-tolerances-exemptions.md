## Purpose

Shows who may relax the Contract Work Hours and Safety Standards statute and the regulatory requirements of applicable parts of 29 CFR, who may ask for relief, and on what ground [@CL-CMP-7995; @CL-CMP-7996].

The statute is chapter 37 of title 40 of the United States Code, Contract Work Hours and Safety Standards [@CL-CMP-8020]. Both decisions in this section belong to the Secretary of Labor.

Paragraph (a). Under section 3706 of title 40 of the United States Code, the Secretary may provide reasonable limitations and allow variations, tolerances and exemptions to and from any or all provisions of the statute [@CL-CMP-7995]. The Secretary may act on the Secretary's own initiative or at the request of any Federal agency [@CL-CMP-7995]. The paragraph refers to 29 CFR 5.15 [@CL-CMP-7995].

Paragraph (b). The Secretary may make variations, tolerances and exemptions from the regulatory requirements of applicable parts of 29 CFR when the Secretary finds that the action is necessary and proper in the public interest or to prevent injustice and undue hardship [@CL-CMP-7996]. The paragraph refers to 29 CFR 5.14 [@CL-CMP-7996].

## How it works

Who decides. The Secretary of Labor decides under both paragraphs [@CL-CMP-7995; @CL-CMP-7996]. A Federal agency may request that the Secretary provide relief from the statute [@CL-CMP-7995]. Paragraph (b) names no requester [@CL-CMP-7996].

What each paragraph reaches. Paragraph (a) reaches provisions of the statute. Paragraph (b) reaches the regulatory requirements in applicable parts of 29 CFR [@CL-CMP-7995; @CL-CMP-7996].

Ground. Paragraph (a) allows reasonable limitations [@CL-CMP-7995]. Paragraph (b) is tied to a finding that the action is necessary and proper in the public interest or to prevent injustice and undue hardship [@CL-CMP-7996].

Duties. Both paragraphs say "may" [@CL-CMP-7995; @CL-CMP-7996].

Not stated here. The section sets no dollar threshold and no deadline. What 29 CFR 5.14 and 5.15 provide is outside this section [@CL-CMP-7995; @CL-CMP-7996].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A Federal agency asks the Secretary of Labor for a variation from a provision of the statute on a bridge project. The Secretary may grant reasonable relief on that request, and may also act without any request, because paragraph (a) allows both routes [@CL-CMP-7995]. Paragraph (a) states no finding for this route [@CL-CMP-7995]. Separately, if the relief sought is from a regulatory requirement in the applicable parts of 29 CFR, paragraph (b) ties it to a finding that the action is necessary and proper in the public interest or to prevent injustice and undue hardship [@CL-CMP-7996]. In both cases the decision is the Secretary's, and the text does not oblige the Secretary to grant the agency's request [@CL-CMP-7995; @CL-CMP-7996].

## Never confuse

Relief under paragraph (a) is not relief under paragraph (b): the first reaches the statute and may follow a Federal agency request, while the second reaches the regulatory requirements and depends on a finding [@CL-CMP-7995; @CL-CMP-7996]. Under paragraph (a), the Secretary of Labor may act upon the Secretary's initiative or at the request of any Federal agency [@CL-CMP-7995].
