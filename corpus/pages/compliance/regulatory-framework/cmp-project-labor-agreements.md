## Purpose

Shows when agencies must require project labor agreements on construction, the contract-level and order-level dollar thresholds, the exceptions and what an agreement must contain.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-3075] |
| Codified citation | FAR 22.503 and FAR 22.504 [@CL-CMP-3075; @CL-CMP-3077] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-3075] |
| Applies to | Contractors and subcontractors engaged in construction on large-scale construction projects, and orders under indefinite-delivery indefinite-quantity contracts at or above $35 million [@CL-CMP-3075; @CL-CMP-3450; @CL-CMP-3141; @CL-CMP-3076] |

When awarding a contract in connection with a large-scale construction project, agencies shall require use of project labor agreements for contractors and subcontractors engaged in construction on the project, unless an exception in paragraph (d) of section 22.504 applies [@CL-CMP-3075]. A large-scale construction project means a Federal construction project within the United States for which the total estimated cost of the construction contract to the Federal Government is $35 million or more [@CL-CMP-3450]. For indefinite-delivery indefinite-quantity contracts, the use of a project labor agreement may be required on an order-by-order basis rather than for the entire contract [@CL-CMP-3141]. For an order at or above $35 million an agency shall require the use of a project labor agreement unless an exception applies [@CL-CMP-3076].

A project labor agreement shall bind all contractors and subcontractors engaged in construction on the construction project to comply with the project labor agreement [@CL-CMP-3077]. One listed requirement is that it allow all contractors and subcontractors to compete for contracts and subcontracts without regard to whether they are otherwise parties to collective bargaining agreements, and another is that it contain guarantees against strikes, lockouts, and similar job disruptions [@CL-CMP-3078]. Another listed requirement is that it set forth effective, prompt, and mutually binding procedures for resolving labor disputes arising during its term [@CL-CMP-3079]. An agency may not require contractors or subcontractors to enter into a project labor agreement with any particular labor organization [@CL-CMP-3080]. The senior procurement executive may grant an exception from the project labor agreement requirement, providing a specific written explanation of why at least one of the listed conditions exists with respect to the particular contract [@CL-CMP-3081]. One listed condition is that market research indicates that requiring a project labor agreement on the project would substantially reduce the number of potential offerors to such a degree that adequate competition at a fair and reasonable price could not be achieved [@CL-CMP-3082]. A likely reduction in the number of potential offerors is not, by itself, sufficient to except a contract from the project labor agreement requirement unless it is coupled with the finding that the reduction would not allow for adequate competition at a fair and reasonable price [@CL-CMP-3139]. For contracts other than indefinite-delivery indefinite-quantity contracts, the exception must be granted for a particular contract by the solicitation date [@CL-CMP-3140].

## How it works

The agency sets the requirement when it awards the contract, and the agreement binds every contractor and subcontractor engaged in construction on the project [@CL-CMP-3075; @CL-CMP-3077]. An exception is granted by the senior procurement executive in writing for the particular contract [@CL-CMP-3081], and for a contract other than an indefinite-delivery indefinite-quantity contract it must be granted by the solicitation date [@CL-CMP-3140]. The order-level requirement applies to an order under an indefinite-delivery indefinite-quantity contract, where the agreement may be required order by order [@CL-CMP-3141; @CL-CMP-3076]. The list of required contents also includes other mechanisms for labor-management cooperation and any additional requirements the agency deems necessary. The section lists other conditions for an exception, including economy and efficiency factors and inconsistency with Federal law, a separate timing rule for exceptions on indefinite-delivery indefinite-quantity contracts, and a rule that lets an agency require an agreement on smaller projects, and this card does not set those out.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An agency awards a construction contract for a Federal construction project within the United States whose total estimated construction contract cost is 36 million, and no exception has been granted. The answer: the project is large-scale because the estimate is at or above 35 million, so the agency shall require project labor agreements for the contractors and subcontractors engaged in construction on the project. Separately, an order under an indefinite-delivery contract is valued at 40 million. The answer: the agency shall require a project labor agreement for that order unless an exception applies, because the order is above 35 million.

## Never confuse

A project labor agreement is a labor-management agreement for one project, with terms such as guarantees against strikes and procedures for resolving labor disputes [@CL-CMP-3078; @CL-CMP-3079]. It is not the same as the wage rules for construction contracts. The withholding and suspension rules are on [Construction wage rate requirements: withholding from and suspension of contract payments](cmp-construction-wage-withholding-and-suspension.md). The policy on agency impartiality in labor disputes, which points to project labor agreements, is on [Labor law definitions and labor relations policy: service contracts and agency impartiality](cmp-labor-law-definitions-and-labor-relations-policy.md).
