## Purpose

Shows which organizations, acquisitions and kinds of work the organizational conflicts of interest rules reach, and which acquisitions they leave alone.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-8244] |
| Codified citation | FAR 9.502 [@CL-CMP-8244] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-8244] |
| Applies to | Contracts with profit and nonprofit organizations, except acquisitions subject to unique agency conflict statutes [@CL-CMP-8244; @CL-CMP-8250] |

The subpart applies to contracts with either profit or nonprofit organizations, including nonprofit organizations created largely or wholly with Government funds [@CL-CMP-8244]. Its applicability is not limited to any particular kind of acquisition [@CL-CMP-8245].

Organizational conflicts of interest are more likely to occur in contracts involving management support services, consultant or other professional services, or contractor performance of or assistance in technical evaluations [@CL-CMP-8246]. They are also more likely in contracts involving systems engineering and technical direction work performed by a contractor that does not have overall contractual responsibility for development or production [@CL-CMP-8247].

A conflict may result when factors create an actual or potential conflict of interest on an instant contract, or when the nature of the work on the instant contract creates an actual or potential conflict on a future acquisition [@CL-CMP-8248]. Where the work on the instant contract creates a conflict on a future acquisition, some restrictions on future activities of the contractor may be required [@CL-CMP-8249]. Acquisitions subject to unique agency organizational conflict of interest statutes are excluded from the requirements of the subpart [@CL-CMP-8250].

## How it works

Applicability turns on the kind of organization that holds the contract, and it is not limited to any particular kind of acquisition [@CL-CMP-8244; @CL-CMP-8245]. The listed kinds of work say where conflicts are more likely, and the subpart is not confined to them [@CL-CMP-8245; @CL-CMP-8246; @CL-CMP-8247]. The section says that restrictions on a contractor's future activities may be required, but it does not say who imposes them; the [contracting officer procedures](../../supply-chain/sourcing-evidence/sup-oci-contracting-officer-procedures.md) card covers the contracting officer's analysis [@CL-CMP-8249]. The one exclusion in this section is for an acquisition that is subject to a unique agency conflict statute [@CL-CMP-8250]. The [general rules](../../mna/diligence/mna-organizational-conflicts-of-interest-general-rules.md) card says what the subpart treats as a conflict. The applicability section does not mention marketing consultants; that definition is on the [scope and marketing consultant](./cmp-organizational-conflicts-scope-and-marketing-consultant.md) card.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A nonprofit institute created largely with Government funds offers on a 2,000,000 dollar systems engineering and technical direction contract. The institute will not have overall contractual responsibility for development or production of the system. Answer: the subpart applies, because it covers nonprofit organizations including ones created largely or wholly with Government funds [@CL-CMP-8244]. The type of acquisition does not take it outside the subpart [@CL-CMP-8245], and this work is one of the listed kinds where conflicts are more likely [@CL-CMP-8247]. If the work creates a conflict on a future acquisition, some restrictions on the institute's future activities may be required [@CL-CMP-8249]. The answer changes if the acquisition is subject to a unique agency conflict statute, which excludes it [@CL-CMP-8250].

## Never confuse

Being a nonprofit does not take a contractor outside these rules, because the subpart reaches nonprofits, including ones created with Government funds [@CL-CMP-8244]. A conflict on a future acquisition is not the same as a conflict on the instant contract: the first can lead to restrictions on later work, and the second arises from factors on the contract itself [@CL-CMP-8248; @CL-CMP-8249]. For the systems engineering situation in detail, see the [systems engineering and technical direction](./cmp-organizational-conflicts-systems-engineering-and-technical-direction.md) card.
