## Purpose

Explains how the contracting officer handles overtime at Government expense in solicitations and negotiations, and how an agency gets items out of a contractor facility affected by a work stoppage. The approval of overtime premiums and the contract work hours rules are on the linked pages.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-6776] |
| Codified citation | FAR 22.103-3 and FAR 22.101-4 [@CL-CMP-6776; @CL-CMP-6977] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-6776] |
| Applies to | Solicitations and negotiated contracts where overtime at Government expense may arise, and contractor facilities affected by work stoppages [@CL-CMP-6776; @CL-CMP-6977] |

Solicitations normally do not specify delivery or performance schedules that may require overtime at Government expense [@CL-CMP-6776]. In negotiating contracts, contracting officers should, consistent with the Government's needs, attempt to ascertain the extent to which offers are based on the payment of overtime and shift premiums [@CL-CMP-6777]. They should also attempt to negotiate contract prices or estimated costs without those premiums, or obtain the requirement from other sources [@CL-CMP-6999].

When it becomes apparent during negotiations of applicable contracts that overtime will be required in performance, the contracting officer must secure from the contractor a request for all overtime to be used during the life of the contract [@CL-CMP-6778]. The request covers the overtime to the extent that it can be estimated with reasonable certainty [@CL-CMP-7000]. It must contain the information required by paragraph (b) of the overtime premiums clause at FAR 52.222-2 [@CL-CMP-6976].

Items must be removed from contractors' facilities affected by work stoppages in accordance with agency procedures [@CL-CMP-6977]. Those procedures should allow for deciding whether removal is in the Government's interest, normally by the critical needs of an agency program [@CL-CMP-6779]. They should also allow for attempting to arrange the approval of the contractor and the union representative involved for the shipment of urgently required items, for obtaining appropriate approvals from within the agency, and for deciding who will remove the items from the plants involved [@CL-CMP-6780]. The use or appearance of force is to be avoided, and incidents that might detrimentally affect labor-management relations are to be prevented [@CL-CMP-6781]. When two or more agencies' requirements are or may become involved in the removal of items, the contract administration office must ensure that the necessary coordination is accomplished [@CL-CMP-6782].

## How it works

The overtime procedures direct what the Government does in solicitation and negotiation. The solicitation should not build in schedules that need overtime at Government expense, and in negotiation the contracting officer asks how far an offer rests on overtime and shift premiums and tries to price without them or source the requirement elsewhere, consistent with the Government's needs [@CL-CMP-6776; @CL-CMP-6777; @CL-CMP-6999]. If overtime proves necessary, the contractor's request goes to the contracting officer and covers the overtime that can be estimated with reasonable certainty [@CL-CMP-6778; @CL-CMP-7000].

The section sends the reader to the clause prescription at FAR 22.103-5(b) for applicable contracts [@CL-CMP-6778]. That prescription covers contemplated cost-reimbursement contracts expected to exceed the simplified acquisition threshold, with two stated exceptions [@CL-OPM-0086; @CL-OPM-0087]. Paragraph (b) of the clause applies to a request for estimated overtime premiums that exceeds the amount specified in the clause, and requires it to include all estimated overtime for contract completion [@CL-CMP-6460]. Who approves the overtime is on the linked premium approval card.

For a work stoppage the agency decides, through its procedures, whether removal serves the Government's interest and who will remove the items [@CL-CMP-6779; @CL-CMP-6780]. The agency also tries to arrange the approval of the contractor and the union representative for urgent shipments [@CL-CMP-6780]. The contract administration office coordinates when more than one agency has requirements involved [@CL-CMP-6782].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An agency drafts a solicitation for a cost-reimbursement contract that is expected to exceed the simplified acquisition threshold, is not for operation of vessels and is not a qualifying cost-plus-incentive-fee contract, so it is an applicable contract [@CL-OPM-0086; @CL-OPM-0087]. The solicitation has a delivery schedule that does not require overtime at Government expense [@CL-CMP-6776]. One offer includes a weekend overtime premium. In negotiation the contracting officer asks how much of the offer rests on that premium and tries to agree an estimated cost without it [@CL-CMP-6777; @CL-CMP-6999]. Later it becomes apparent during negotiations that some overtime is unavoidable. The contracting officer secures from the contractor a request for all overtime it can estimate with reasonable certainty, carrying the information that paragraph (b) of the overtime premiums clause requires [@CL-CMP-6778; @CL-CMP-7000; @CL-CMP-6976].

In a separate case, a strike halts work at a plant that holds items an agency program needs urgently. Under agency procedures the agency decides that removal serves the Government's interest because of the program's critical needs. It tries to arrange the approval of the contractor and the union representative for the shipment, obtains its internal approvals and decides who will remove the items [@CL-CMP-6779; @CL-CMP-6780]. The items are removed without force or its appearance [@CL-CMP-6781]. A second agency also has items in the plant, so the contract administration office ensures the coordination is done [@CL-CMP-6782].

## Never confuse

A contractor's request for overtime is not an approval of overtime. The cited paragraph says the contracting officer shall secure the request [@CL-CMP-6778]. Approval before overtime is used is covered on [Overtime premium approval](../../operating-model/talent/opm-overtime-premium-approval.md), and the separate contract work hours rule is on [Contract work hours overtime](cmp-contract-work-hours-overtime.md).

Removal of items during a stoppage is an agency procedure for getting items out of a plant, not a price negotiation. It runs through agency procedures and must avoid force or its appearance [@CL-CMP-6977; @CL-CMP-6781].
