## Purpose

Shows what happens when a solicitation carries the wrong wage determination, how a new determination comes in at each option, and where questions about wage determinations go.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-3024] |
| Codified citation | FAR 22.404-8, FAR 22.404-12 and FAR 22.403-6 [@CL-CMP-3024; @CL-CMP-3026; @CL-CMP-3029] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-3024] |
| Applies to | Solicitations before award that carry a wrong determination, and construction contracts with options to extend the term [@CL-CMP-3024; @CL-CMP-3026; @CL-CMP-3027] |

A written notification by the Department of Labor that a solicitation includes the wrong wage determination or the wrong rate schedule is effective immediately, without regard to the section on modifications, if the contracting officer receives it before award [@CL-CMP-3024]. In negotiated acquisitions, the contracting officer shall delay award if necessary and process the notification of an improper wage determination for the primary site of the work in the manner prescribed for a new wage determination [@CL-CMP-3025]. In sealed bidding, if the notification reaches the contracting officer before bid opening, the contracting officer shall postpone the bid opening date if necessary to allow a reasonable time to obtain the appropriate determination if a new wage determination is required, amend the solicitation to incorporate the determination or rate schedule, and permit bidders to amend their bids [@CL-CMP-3115; @CL-CMP-8528]. If it reaches the contracting officer after bid opening but before award, the contracting officer shall delay awarding the contract if necessary and, if required, obtain the appropriate wage determination [@CL-CMP-3116]. The rest of the sealed-bidding steps, such as how the appropriate determination is then processed, are not drafted on this card.

Each time the contracting officer exercises an option to extend the term of a construction contract, or of a contract that includes substantial and segregable construction work, the contracting officer must modify the contract to incorporate the most current wage determination [@CL-CMP-3026]. The contracting officer must include in fixed-price contracts a clause that specifies one of the listed methods, suitable to the interest of the Government, to provide an allowance for increases or decreases in labor costs that result from the inclusion of the current wage determination at the exercise of an option to extend the term of the contract [@CL-CMP-3027; @CL-CMP-3114]. One listed price adjustment method lets the contracting officer provide for a contract price adjustment based solely on a percentage rate determined by the contracting officer using a published economic indicator incorporated into the solicitation and resulting contract [@CL-CMP-3117]. Under that method, the contracting officer must insert 50 percent as the estimated portion of the contract price that is labor, unless the contracting officer determines before issuing the solicitation that a different percentage is more appropriate for a particular contract or requirement [@CL-CMP-3028; @CL-CMP-3117]. The percentage adjustment to the designated labor costs must be the only adjustment made to cover increases in wages or benefits resulting from the incorporation of a new or revised wage determination at the exercise of the option [@CL-CMP-3118; @CL-CMP-3117]. All questions about the application and interpretation of wage determinations, including their classifications, and about the interpretation of the Department of Labor regulations in the subsection that lists them are referred to the Administrator, Wage and Hour Division [@CL-CMP-3029]. The other listed price adjustment methods and the list of Department of Labor regulation parts are not drafted on this card.

## How it works

Before award, a notice from the Department of Labor that a solicitation carries the wrong determination or rate schedule takes effect immediately once the contracting officer has it [@CL-CMP-3024]. In a negotiated acquisition the contracting officer processes it as a new determination, delaying award if necessary [@CL-CMP-3025]. After award, each exercise of an option to extend the term brings in the most current determination [@CL-CMP-3026]. For fixed-price contracts, the pricing clause chosen at solicitation says how the change in labor cost is allowed for [@CL-CMP-3027; @CL-CMP-3114]. The ordinary timing rules for modifications of determinations are on [Project wage determination expiration and modifications before and after bid opening or award](cmp-project-wage-determination-expiration-and-modifications.md). Questions on applying a determination or the Department of Labor regulations go to the Administrator [@CL-CMP-3029].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A fixed-price construction contract uses the percentage method with the default 50 percent labor share. The contract price is 1,000,000, so 500,000 is treated as labor. At option exercise the published economic indicator shows a rise of 4 percent. The 4 percent is applied to the 500,000 labor portion, which gives 20,000. The price rises by 20,000, and no other wage adjustment is made.

## Never confuse

The notice rule and the option rule are different. The notice rule applies before award when the solicitation carries the wrong determination [@CL-CMP-3024], while the option rule applies at each exercise of an option and brings in the most current determination [@CL-CMP-3026]. The option pricing clause follows whichever method the contracting officer chose and specified [@CL-CMP-3027]. Revised determinations on service contracts follow another rule, covered on [Service Contract Labor Standards: revised wage determinations and additional classes of service employees](cmp-scls-revised-wage-determinations-and-conformance.md).
