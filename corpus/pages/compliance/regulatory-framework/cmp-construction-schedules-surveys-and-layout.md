## Purpose

States what schedule a construction contractor must submit and update, what follows when it falls behind or does not comply, how quantity surveys are made under the basic clause and its alternate, and what the layout clause requires.

### Schedules for construction contracts

Within five days after the work commences on the contract, or another period of time determined by the contracting officer, the contractor must prepare and submit to the contracting officer for approval three copies of a practicable schedule [@CL-CMP-4896]. The schedule shows the order in which the contractor proposes to perform the work and the dates on which it contemplates starting and completing the several salient features of the work, including acquiring materials, plant and equipment [@CL-CMP-5073]. It must be in the form of a progress chart of suitable scale to indicate appropriately the percentage of work scheduled for completion by any given date during the period [@CL-CMP-5074]. If the contractor fails to submit a schedule within the time prescribed, the contracting officer may withhold approval of progress payments until the contractor submits the required schedule [@CL-CMP-4897].

The contractor must enter the actual progress on the chart as directed by the contracting officer and, upon doing so, immediately deliver three copies of the annotated schedule to the officer [@CL-CMP-4898]. If, in the opinion of the officer, the contractor falls behind the approved schedule, it must take the steps necessary to improve its progress, including those that may be required by the officer, without additional cost to the Government [@CL-CMP-4899]. In that circumstance the officer may require the contractor to increase the number of shifts, overtime operations, days of work and/or the amount of construction plant [@CL-CMP-5076]. The officer's requirements may also include submission for approval of any supplementary schedule or schedules in chart form that the officer deems necessary to demonstrate how the approved rate of progress will be regained [@CL-CMP-5077].

Failure of the contractor to comply with the requirements of the contracting officer under the clause is grounds for a determination by the officer [@CL-CMP-4900] that the contractor is not prosecuting the work with sufficient diligence to ensure completion within the time specified in the contract [@CL-CMP-5075]. Upon making that determination, the officer may terminate the contractor's right to proceed with the work, or any separable part of it, in accordance with the default terms of the contract [@CL-CMP-4901].

### Quantity surveys

The contracting officer may insert the quantity surveys clause in solicitations and contracts when a fixed-price construction contract providing for unit pricing of items and for payment based on quantity surveys is contemplated [@CL-CMP-5078]. Quantity surveys must be conducted, and the data derived from them must be used in computing the quantities of work performed and the actual construction completed and in place [@CL-CMP-5079].

Under the basic clause, the Government conducts the original and final surveys and makes the computations based on them, while the contractor conducts the surveys for any periods for which progress payments are requested and makes the computations based on those surveys [@CL-CMP-4902]. All surveys conducted by the contractor must be conducted under the direction of a representative of the contracting officer, unless the contracting officer waives this requirement in a specific instance [@CL-CMP-5080]. Promptly upon completing a survey, the contractor must furnish the originals of all field notes and all other records relating to the survey or to the layout of the work to the contracting officer, who uses them as necessary to determine the amount of progress payments [@CL-CMP-5081]. The contractor must retain copies of all such material furnished to the contracting officer [@CL-CMP-5082].

Alternate I applies if it is determined at a level above that of the contracting officer that it is impracticable for Government personnel to perform the original and final surveys and the Government wishes the contractor to perform them, and it substitutes a new paragraph (b) for paragraph (b) of the basic clause [@CL-CMP-5083]. Under it the contractor conducts the original and final surveys and the surveys for any periods for which progress payments are requested [@CL-CMP-5084]. All of these surveys are conducted under the direction of a representative of the contracting officer, unless the officer waives this requirement in a specific instance [@CL-CMP-5085]. The Government makes such computations as are necessary to determine the quantities of work performed or finally in place, and the contractor makes the computations based on the surveys for any periods for which progress payments are requested [@CL-CMP-5086].

### Layout of work

The layout clause is prescribed for solicitations and contracts when a fixed-price construction contract is contemplated and use of the clause is appropriate due to a need for accurate work layout and for siting verification during work performance [@CL-CMP-5087]. The contractor must lay out its work from Government-established base lines and bench marks indicated on the drawings, and is responsible for all measurements in connection with the layout [@CL-CMP-4903]. It must furnish, at its own expense, all stakes, templates, platforms, equipment, tools, materials and labor required to lay out any part of the work [@CL-CMP-5088]. It is responsible for executing the work to the lines and grades that may be established or indicated by the contracting officer [@CL-CMP-5089].

The contractor is also responsible for maintaining and preserving all stakes and other marks established by the contracting officer until authorized to remove them [@CL-CMP-5090]. If such marks are destroyed by the contractor or through its negligence before their removal is authorized, the officer may replace them and deduct the expense of the replacement from any amounts due or to become due to the contractor [@CL-CMP-5091].

## How it works

The contractor submits the schedule within five days after the work commences or another period the contracting officer determines [@CL-CMP-4896], and if it fails to submit within the time prescribed the officer may withhold approval of progress payments until it does [@CL-CMP-4897]. The contractor enters actual progress on the chart as directed and delivers three copies of the annotated schedule [@CL-CMP-4898]. If, in the officer's opinion, the contractor falls behind, it must take steps necessary to improve its progress without additional cost to the Government [@CL-CMP-4899]. Failure to comply with the requirements of the officer under the clause is grounds for a determination of insufficient diligence [@CL-CMP-4900; @CL-CMP-5075], and upon making that determination the officer may terminate the right to proceed under the default terms [@CL-CMP-4901]. Under the basic quantity surveys clause the Government conducts the original and final surveys and the contractor conducts the surveys for progress payment periods [@CL-CMP-4902], while under Alternate I the contractor conducts the original and final surveys as well [@CL-CMP-5084]. The contractor lays out its work from Government-established base lines and bench marks, is responsible for all measurements in connection with the layout [@CL-CMP-4903], furnishes the layout stakes, templates, platforms, equipment, tools, materials and labor at its own expense [@CL-CMP-5088], and maintains and preserves the stakes and other marks the officer establishes until authorized to remove them [@CL-CMP-5090].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text from February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. In the schedule clause, the RFO text says that within five days after the work commences, or another period the contracting officer determines, the contractor must prepare and submit a practicable schedule for approval, where the codified clause requires three copies of the schedule [@CL-CMP-0742; @CL-CMP-4896]. It says the schedule must show the order of the work and the dates for starting and completing the work, where the codified clause says the several salient features of the work [@CL-CMP-0743; @CL-CMP-5073]. It says the contractor must continually update the actual progress in the schedule and submit it by the means the contract prescribes or as directed, where the codified clause says to enter progress on the chart and immediately deliver three copies of the annotated schedule [@CL-CMP-0744; @CL-CMP-4898]. A contractor that falls behind the approved schedule must take steps necessary to recover lost time and execute in accordance with the approved schedule, without additional cost to the Government, where the codified sentence applies if, in the opinion of the Contracting Officer, the contractor falls behind and refers to steps necessary to improve its progress [@CL-CMP-0745; @CL-CMP-4899]. The steps may include more shifts, overtime operations, days of work or construction plant, and the contractor must submit supplementary schedules showing how the lost time will be recovered, where the codified clause says the officer may require those steps and schedules [@CL-CMP-0746; @CL-CMP-5076; @CL-CMP-5077]. The contracting officer may determine that the contractor is not prosecuting the work with sufficient diligence if the contractor does not recover the lost time [@CL-CMP-0747; @CL-CMP-4900; @CL-CMP-5075]. The termination sentence that follows is worded the same as the codified sentence [@CL-CMP-0748; @CL-CMP-4901].

In the quantity surveys clause, the RFO text keeps the rule that quantity surveys are conducted and used in computing the quantities of work performed and the construction completed and in place, and that the Government conducts the original and final surveys and makes the computations based on them [@CL-CMP-0749; @CL-CMP-5079; @CL-CMP-4902]. It keeps the contractor's surveys for progress payment periods and its computations from them [@CL-CMP-0750; @CL-CMP-4902], and the direction of a representative of the contracting officer unless the officer waives the requirement in a specific instance [@CL-CMP-0789; @CL-CMP-5080]. For the records, it says the originals of all field notes and other records go to the contracting officer, which may be used by the officer to determine the amount of progress payments, where the codified clause says the officer shall use them as necessary [@CL-CMP-0790; @CL-CMP-5081]. Alternate I keeps its condition that it is determined at a level above the contracting officer that it is impracticable for Government personnel to perform the original and final surveys and the Government wishes the contractor to perform them [@CL-CMP-0791; @CL-CMP-0792; @CL-CMP-5083]. It keeps the contractor's original, final and progress payment surveys under the direction of the officer's representative unless waived [@CL-CMP-0793; @CL-CMP-0794; @CL-CMP-5084; @CL-CMP-5085], and the Government's and the contractor's computations [@CL-CMP-0795; @CL-CMP-5086]. Alternate I says to substitute its paragraph (b) for paragraph (b) of the basic clause [@CL-CMP-0792; @CL-CMP-7669], and in the RFO basic clause paragraph (b) is the paragraph on providing field notes and other records [@CL-CMP-7670]. The RFO prescription line for the clause points to FAR 36.101-7(h) and says to insert the following clause [@CL-CMP-0796], where the codified line says the contracting officer may insert the clause when a fixed-price construction contract providing for unit pricing of items and for payment based on quantity surveys is contemplated [@CL-CMP-5078].

In the layout clause, the RFO text says the contractor must lay out its work from Government-established base lines and benchmarks provided on the drawings, where the codified clause says indicated on the drawings, and must be responsible for all measurements in connection with the layout [@CL-CMP-0797; @CL-CMP-4903]. It says the contractor must furnish at its own expense the stakes, templates, platforms, equipment, tools, materials and labor required for the layout, where the codified clause says required to lay out any part of the work, and must be responsible for executing the work to the lines and grades the officer establishes or indicates [@CL-CMP-0798; @CL-CMP-5088; @CL-CMP-5089]. It keeps the contractor's responsibility for maintaining and preserving the stakes and other marks the officer establishes until authorized to remove them [@CL-CMP-0799; @CL-CMP-5090]. If the marks are destroyed by the contractor, the officer may replace them and deduct the expense from amounts due, where the RFO sentence reads "destroyed by the Contractor" [@CL-CMP-0800] and the codified sentence reads "destroyed by the Contractor or through its negligence before their removal is authorized" [@CL-CMP-5091]. Its prescription line points to FAR 36.101-7(i) and says to insert the following clause [@CL-CMP-0845], where the codified line says to insert the clause when a fixed-price construction contract is contemplated and use of the clause is appropriate due to a need for accurate work layout and for siting verification during work performance [@CL-CMP-5087].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

The contract uses the five-day period, and the contractor submits three copies of its schedule on day 8 [@CL-CMP-4896]. The contracting officer may withhold approval of progress payments until the schedule arrives [@CL-CMP-4897]. The contractor enters actual progress on the chart as directed and immediately delivers three copies of the annotated schedule [@CL-CMP-4898]. Two months later the officer considers the contractor 3 weeks behind. The officer requires a second shift [@CL-CMP-5076] and a supplementary schedule in chart form [@CL-CMP-5077], and the contractor takes these steps without additional cost to the Government [@CL-CMP-4899]. If the contractor instead refused the requirements, the officer could determine that the work is not being prosecuted with sufficient diligence [@CL-CMP-4900; @CL-CMP-5075] and could terminate the right to proceed under the default terms [@CL-CMP-4901]. Under the RFO text, the steps may include a second shift and the contractor must submit supplementary schedules showing how the lost time will be recovered [@CL-CMP-0746], and the determination follows if the contractor does not recover the lost time [@CL-CMP-0747].

On a unit-priced contract under the basic quantity surveys clause, the Government conducts the original and final surveys [@CL-CMP-4902]. For a progress payment period the contractor conducts the survey under the direction of the officer's representative [@CL-CMP-4902; @CL-CMP-5080] and computes 1,000 cubic metres in place from it [@CL-CMP-4902]. It promptly furnishes the original field notes to the officer [@CL-CMP-5081] and keeps copies [@CL-CMP-5082]. Alternate I applies if it is determined at a level above the contracting officer that it is impracticable for Government personnel to perform the original and final surveys and the Government wishes the contractor to perform them [@CL-CMP-5083]. Under it the contractor also conducts the original and final surveys [@CL-CMP-5084], and the Government makes such computations as are necessary to determine the quantities of work performed or finally in place [@CL-CMP-5086]. The contractor's negligent crew destroys stakes established by the contracting officer before their removal is authorized [@CL-CMP-5091]. The officer replaces them for 500 and deducts 500 from an amount due to the contractor [@CL-CMP-5091]. Under the RFO text, the sentence on destroyed stakes says destroyed by the contractor, without the words about negligence [@CL-CMP-0800].

## Never confuse

The schedule shows the order in which the contractor proposes to perform the work and the dates on which it contemplates starting and completing the work [@CL-CMP-5073], while the diligence determination looks to completion within the time specified in the contract [@CL-CMP-5075]. The contractor takes the steps necessary to improve its progress without additional cost to the Government [@CL-CMP-4899]. If the contractor fails to submit a schedule within the time prescribed, the officer may withhold approval of progress payments [@CL-CMP-4897], while failure to comply with the requirements of the officer under the clause is grounds for a diligence determination [@CL-CMP-4900]. Termination under the default terms is covered in [Construction default and excusable delays](../../close-revrec/revenue/cls-construction-default-and-excusable-delays.md). Under the basic clause the Government conducts the original and final surveys and the contractor conducts the surveys for progress payment periods [@CL-CMP-4902]. The contractor is responsible for all measurements in connection with the layout [@CL-CMP-4903], and the originals of field notes and other records relating to the survey or to the layout of the work go to the contracting officer [@CL-CMP-5081]. Under the codified clause the diligence determination follows from failing to comply with the requirements of the contracting officer under the clause [@CL-CMP-4900], while under the RFO text it follows from not recovering the lost time [@CL-CMP-0747]. The codified Alternate I replaces paragraph (b) of the basic clause [@CL-CMP-7669], which is the paragraph on Government and contractor surveys [@CL-CMP-7668], while in the RFO basic clause paragraph (b) is the paragraph on providing field notes and other records [@CL-CMP-7670].
