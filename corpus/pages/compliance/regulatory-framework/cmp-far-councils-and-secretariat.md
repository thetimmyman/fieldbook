## Purpose

Explains which two councils prepare and issue revisions to the FAR, who sits on and leads them, what each council must do, and what the FAR Secretariat does for them.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-8408] |
| Codified citation | FAR 1.201-1 and 1.201-2 [@CL-CMP-8408; @CL-CMP-8417] |

Subject to the authorities discussed in 1.103, revisions to the FAR will be prepared and issued through the coordinated action of two councils, the Defense Acquisition Regulations Council (DAR Council) and the Civilian Agency Acquisition Council (CAA Council) [@CL-CMP-8408]. The authorities are on [FAR System authority and positive law codification](cmp-far-system-authority-and-positive-law-codification.md).

The General Services Administration is responsible for establishing and operating the FAR Secretariat to publish and distribute the FAR through the Code of Federal Regulations system, including a separate online edition with periodic updates [@CL-CMP-8417].

## How it works

Members of the two councils shall represent their agencies on a full-time basis, be selected for their superior qualifications in acquisition experience and demonstrated professional expertise, and be funded by their respective agencies [@CL-CMP-8409].

The chairperson of the CAA Council shall be the representative of the Administrator of General Services [@CL-CMP-8410]. The other members shall be one representative each from the Departments of Agriculture, Commerce, Education, Energy, Health and Human Services, Homeland Security, Housing and Urban Development, Interior, Justice, Labor, State, Transportation, Treasury, and Veterans Affairs [@CL-CMP-8758]. They also include one representative each from the Environmental Protection Agency, the National Aeronautics and Space Administration, the Social Security Administration, the Small Business Administration and the United States Agency for International Development [@CL-CMP-8759].

The Director of the DAR Council shall be the representative of the Secretary of Defense, and the operation of the DAR Council will be as prescribed by the Secretary of Defense [@CL-CMP-8411]. Its membership shall include representatives of the military departments, the Defense Logistics Agency and the Defense Contract Management Agency [@CL-CMP-8412].

The two councils apportion responsibility for processing revisions so that each council has cognizance over specified parts or subparts [@CL-CMP-8413]. Each council shall agree on all revisions with the other council [@CL-CMP-8414], so neither revises the FAR alone.

Each council shall submit to the FAR Secretariat the information required under 1.501-2(b) and (e) for publication in the Federal Register of a notice soliciting comments on a proposed revision [@CL-CMP-8415]. Each council is also responsible for considering all comments received, arranging for public meetings, preparing any final revision in the appropriate FAR format and language, and submitting the final revision to the FAR Secretariat for publication in the Federal Register [@CL-CMP-8416]. What the notice must contain and how long comment runs are on [Publication of significant FAR revisions for public comment](cmp-significant-far-revisions-public-comment.md).

The FAR Secretariat shall provide the two councils with centralized services for keeping a synopsis of current FAR cases and their status and for maintaining official files [@CL-CMP-8418]. Those services also include assisting parties interested in reviewing the files on completed cases and performing miscellaneous administrative tasks pertaining to the maintenance of the FAR [@CL-CMP-8419].

These sections set no price, payment or compliance duty for a contractor, and no deadline or threshold. The duties fall on the councils, their member agencies and the General Services Administration.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor asks which body changed a cost principle last quarter. The trail shows that the revision was prepared and issued through the coordinated action of the two councils [@CL-CMP-8408], that each council agreed on it with the other [@CL-CMP-8414], and that the final revision went to the FAR Secretariat for publication in the Federal Register [@CL-CMP-8416]. The Secretariat keeps the official files and a synopsis of current cases [@CL-CMP-8418] and assists parties who want to review the files on completed cases [@CL-CMP-8419]. The answer: the analyst reads the final revision and the case file behind it, and does not attribute the change to a single agency.

## Never confuse

The councils are not the agencies that issue supplements. The councils prepare and issue revisions to the FAR itself [@CL-CMP-8408], while agency acquisition regulations implement or supplement it [@CL-CMP-8396]; see [The FAR System and agency supplements](cmp-far-system-and-agency-supplements.md).

The Secretariat is also not a third council. The General Services Administration operates it to publish and distribute the FAR [@CL-CMP-8417], and it serves the councils with centralized services [@CL-CMP-8418], while the two councils prepare and issue the revisions [@CL-CMP-8408].
