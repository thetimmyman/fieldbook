## Purpose

Explains which two councils prepare and issue revisions to the FAR, who sits on and leads them, what each council must do, and what the FAR Secretariat does for them.

Subject to the authorities discussed in 1.103, revisions to the FAR will be prepared and issued through the coordinated action of two councils, the Defense Acquisition Regulations Council (DAR Council) and the Civilian Agency Acquisition Council (CAA Council) [@CL-CMP-8408]. The authorities are on [FAR System authority and positive law codification](cmp-far-system-authority-and-positive-law-codification.md).

The General Services Administration is responsible for establishing and operating the FAR Secretariat to publish and distribute the FAR through the Code of Federal Regulations system, including a separate online edition with periodic updates [@CL-CMP-8417].

Agency compliance with the FAR (see FAR 1.304) is the responsibility of the Secretary of Defense (for the military departments and defense agencies), the Administrator of General Services (for civilian agencies other than NASA), and the Administrator of NASA (for NASA activities) [@CL-CMP-9729].

## How it works

Members of the two councils shall represent their agencies on a full-time basis, be selected for their superior qualifications in acquisition experience and demonstrated professional expertise, and be funded by their respective agencies [@CL-CMP-8409].

The chairperson of the CAA Council shall be the representative of the Administrator of General Services [@CL-CMP-8410]. The other members shall be one representative each from (1) the Departments of Agriculture, Commerce, Education, Energy, Health and Human Services, Homeland Security, Housing and Urban Development, Interior, Justice, Labor, State, Transportation, Treasury, and Veterans Affairs [@CL-CMP-8758]. The other members shall also be one representative each from (2) the Environmental Protection Agency, the National Aeronautics and Space Administration, the Social Security Administration, the Small Business Administration and the U.S. Agency for International Development [@CL-CMP-8759].

The Director of the DAR Council shall be the representative of the Secretary of Defense, and the operation of the DAR Council will be as prescribed by the Secretary of Defense [@CL-CMP-8411]. Its membership shall include representatives of the military departments, the Defense Logistics Agency and the Defense Contract Management Agency [@CL-CMP-8412].

The two councils apportion responsibility for processing revisions so that each council has cognizance over specified parts or subparts [@CL-CMP-8413]. Each council shall be responsible for agreeing on all revisions with the other council [@CL-CMP-8414].

Each council shall also be responsible for submitting to the FAR Secretariat the information required under 1.501-2(b) and (e) for publication in the Federal Register of a notice soliciting comments on a proposed revision [@CL-CMP-8415]. Each council shall also be responsible for considering all comments received in response to notice of proposed revisions, arranging for public meetings, preparing any final revision in the appropriate FAR format and language, and submitting the final revision to the FAR Secretariat for publication in the Federal Register [@CL-CMP-8416]. What the notice must contain and how long comment runs are on [Publication of significant FAR revisions for public comment](cmp-significant-far-revisions-public-comment.md).

The FAR Secretariat shall provide the two councils with centralized services for keeping a synopsis of current FAR cases and their status and for maintaining official files [@CL-CMP-8418]. Those services also include assisting parties interested in reviewing the files on completed cases and performing miscellaneous administrative tasks pertaining to the maintenance of the FAR [@CL-CMP-8419].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor asks which body changed a cost principle last quarter. The trail shows that the revision was prepared and issued through the coordinated action of the two councils [@CL-CMP-8408], that each council agreed on it with the other [@CL-CMP-8414], and that the final revision went to the FAR Secretariat for publication in the Federal Register [@CL-CMP-8416]. The Secretariat keeps the official files and a synopsis of current cases [@CL-CMP-8418] and assists parties who want to review the files on completed cases [@CL-CMP-8419]. The answer: the analyst reads the final revision and the case file behind it, and does not attribute the change to a single agency.

## Never confuse

The councils write the FAR rather than the Secretariat: subject to the authorities discussed in 1.103, revisions to the FAR will be prepared and issued through the coordinated action of the DAR Council and the CAA Council [@CL-CMP-8408], while the General Services Administration is responsible for establishing and operating the FAR Secretariat to publish and distribute the FAR [@CL-CMP-8417], and the FAR Secretariat shall provide the two councils with centralized services for keeping a synopsis of current FAR cases and their status and for maintaining official files [@CL-CMP-8418]. Subsequent chapters are reserved for agency acquisition regulations that implement or supplement the FAR [@CL-CMP-8396]; see [The FAR System and agency supplements](cmp-far-system-and-agency-supplements.md).
