## Purpose

Shows the DoD postaward conference record form and the cost and software data reporting discussion, which contracting officers assign criticality designator A or C and who may change a designator, and what the contract schedule must say when the production progress reports clause is used.

## The rule

|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-CMP-7829] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 242.503-2, DFARS 242.1105 and DFARS 242.1107 [@CL-CMP-7829; @CL-CMP-7831; @CL-CMP-7834] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-7829] |
| Applies to | DoD postaward conferences [@CL-CMP-7829]; conferences on contracts that include the clause at DFARS 252.234-7004 [@CL-CMP-7830]; items and unilateral purchase orders that receive a criticality designator [@CL-CMP-7831; @CL-CMP-7832]; contracts that use the clause at FAR 52.242-2 [@CL-CMP-7834] |

DD Form 1484, Post-Award Conference Record, may be used in conducting the postaward conference and in preparing the conference report [@CL-CMP-7829]. For contracts that include the clause at DFARS 252.234-7004, Cost and Software Data Reporting, postaward conferences shall include a discussion of the contractor's standard cost and software data reporting (CSDR) process [@CL-CMP-7830]. The process discussed is one that satisfies the guidelines contained in the DoD 5000.04-M-1, CSDR Manual, and the requirements in the Government-approved CSDR plan for the contract, DD Form 2794, and related Resource Distribution Table [@CL-CMP-7993]. The section does not name who must hold the discussion; the chairperson and the contracting officer roles are on [Contract correspondence and the postaward conference](cmp-contract-correspondence-and-postaward-conference.md).

Contracting officers shall assign criticality designator A to items with a priority 01, 02, 03, or 06 (if emergency supply of clothing) under DoD Manual 4140.01, Volume 5 [@CL-CMP-7831]. They shall ordinarily assign criticality designator C to unilateral purchase orders [@CL-CMP-7832]. Only the contracting officer shall change the assigned designator [@CL-CMP-7833].

## How it works

When using the clause at FAR 52.242-2, the instructions in the contract schedule must include the frequency and timing of reporting, normally 5 working days after each reporting period [@CL-CMP-7834]. They must also include the contract line items, exhibits or exhibit line items requiring reports [@CL-CMP-7994], and the offices, with addressees or codes, where reports should be sent, always including the contracting office and the contract administration office [@CL-CMP-7835]. Finally, they must include requirements for report content: the problem, actual or potential, and its cause, the items and quantities affected, when the delinquency started or will start, the actions taken to overcome the delinquency, the estimated recovery date, and/or a proposed schedule revision [@CL-CMP-7836]. Because of "and/or", the schedule may require the estimated recovery date, a proposed schedule revision, or both [@CL-CMP-7836].

The FAR says the contracting officer shall insert the clause in solicitations and contracts when production progress reporting is required, unless a construction contract or a Federal Supply Schedule contract is contemplated [@CL-CMP-2572]. The DFARS section does not name who writes these instructions, but the FAR says the contracting officer shall specify appropriate reporting instructions in the Schedule when the clause is used [@CL-CMP-2573]. The contractor then prepares and submits the production progress reports specified in the Schedule [@CL-CMP-2654], as the progress reports page explains: [Production surveillance: applicability and the progress reports clause](cmp-production-surveillance-applicability-and-progress-reports.md). The criticality designators A, B and C and the delay report are on [Criticality designators and production reporting](../../operating-model/corrective-action/opm-criticality-designators-and-production-reporting.md). Surveillance duties tied to designators are on [Production surveillance, progress reports and past performance notations](cmp-production-surveillance-and-cpaps-notations.md). The decision whether to hold a postaward orientation is on [Postaward orientation](../../operating-model/cadence/opm-postaward-orientation.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contract for an item with priority 02 includes the cost and software data reporting clause [@CL-CMP-7830]. The contracting officer assigns criticality designator A [@CL-CMP-7831]. The postaward conference uses DD Form 1484 for its record and report [@CL-CMP-7829], and it includes a discussion of the contractor's standard CSDR process against the Government-approved CSDR plan for the contract [@CL-CMP-7830; @CL-CMP-7993]. A unilateral purchase order for a second item, whose priority is not one of those that take designator A, ordinarily receives designator C [@CL-CMP-7831; @CL-CMP-7832]. A program office asks to raise it to B, and only the contracting officer may change the designator, so the request goes to that officer [@CL-CMP-7833].

The same contract uses the clause at FAR 52.242-2, so its schedule gives reporting 5 working days after each reporting period, lists the contract line items that need reports, names the contracting office and the contract administration office as recipients, and calls for the problem and its cause, the items and quantities affected, when the delinquency started, the actions taken, and the estimated recovery date [@CL-CMP-7834; @CL-CMP-7994; @CL-CMP-7835; @CL-CMP-7836]. The answer: the designator follows the priority or the purchase order type, only the contracting officer changes it, and the schedule carries the reporting instructions.

## Never confuse

The criticality designator is assigned and changed by the contracting officer [@CL-CMP-7831; @CL-CMP-7833]. The reporting frequency, report recipients and content are instructions in the contract schedule [@CL-CMP-7834; @CL-CMP-7835; @CL-CMP-7836].
