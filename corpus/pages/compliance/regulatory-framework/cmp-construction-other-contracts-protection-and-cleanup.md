## Purpose

States the duty to cooperate with other contractors at the site, what existing property must be protected and repaired, and what cleaning up the contract requires.

## The rule

|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-4877] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.236-8, FAR 52.236-9 and FAR 52.236-12 [@CL-CMP-4877; @CL-CMP-4878; @CL-CMP-4881] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.236-8 [@CL-CMP-0728], RFO FAR 52.236-9 [@CL-CMP-0731] and RFO FAR 52.236-12 [@CL-CMP-0737] |
| Edition date | Not established. |

### Other contracts

The Government may undertake or award other contracts for additional work at or near the site of the work under the contract [@CL-CMP-5043]. The contractor must fully cooperate with the other contractors and with Government employees and carefully adapt scheduling and performing the work to accommodate the additional work, heeding any direction that may be provided by the contracting officer [@CL-CMP-4877]. It must not commit or permit any act that will interfere with the performance of work by any other contractor or by Government employees [@CL-CMP-5044].

### Protection of existing vegetation, structures, equipment, utilities and improvements

The contractor must preserve and protect all structures, equipment and vegetation, such as trees, shrubs and grass, on or adjacent to the work site that are not to be removed and that do not unreasonably interfere with the work required under the contract [@CL-CMP-4878]. It must remove trees only when specifically authorized to do so and must avoid damaging vegetation that will remain in place [@CL-CMP-5045]. If any limbs or branches of trees are broken during contract performance, or by the careless operation of equipment or by workmen, it must trim them with a clean cut and paint the cut with a tree-pruning compound as directed by the contracting officer [@CL-CMP-5046].

The contractor must protect from damage all existing improvements and utilities at or near the work site and on adjacent property of a third party, the locations of which are made known to or should be known by the contractor [@CL-CMP-4879]. It must repair any damage to those facilities, including those that are the property of a third party, resulting from failure to comply with the requirements of the contract or failure to exercise reasonable care in performing the work [@CL-CMP-5047]. If it fails or refuses to repair the damage promptly, the contracting officer may have the necessary work performed and charge the cost to the contractor [@CL-CMP-4880].

### Cleaning up

The contractor must at all times keep the work area, including storage areas, free from accumulations of waste materials [@CL-CMP-5048]. Before completing the work, it must remove from the work and premises any rubbish, tools, scaffolding, equipment and materials that are not the property of the Government [@CL-CMP-4881]. Upon completing the work, it must leave the work area in a clean, neat and orderly condition satisfactory to the contracting officer [@CL-CMP-5049].

## How it works

The Government may place other work at or near the site, and the contractor must cooperate, adapt its scheduling to that work and not interfere with it [@CL-CMP-5043; @CL-CMP-4877; @CL-CMP-5044]. The protection duties cover structures, equipment and vegetation that stay in place and improvements and utilities whose locations are made known or should be known, and the repair duty attaches to damage from failure to comply with the contract or to use reasonable care [@CL-CMP-4878; @CL-CMP-4879; @CL-CMP-5047]. If the contractor does not repair promptly, the contracting officer may have the work done and charge the cost to the contractor [@CL-CMP-4880]. Cleaning up has three parts: keeping the work area free from waste during the work, removing non-Government items before completing, and leaving the work area clean, neat and orderly on completion [@CL-CMP-5048; @CL-CMP-4881; @CL-CMP-5049].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text from February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. In the other contracts clause, the RFO text says the Government may award other contracts for work at or near the site, where the codified clause says the Government may undertake or award other contracts for additional work [@CL-CMP-0728; @CL-CMP-5043]. It says the contractor must cooperate and coordinate with other contractors and with Government employees, where the codified clause says fully cooperate [@CL-CMP-0729; @CL-CMP-4877]. Its paragraph (b) says the contractor's scheduling and performance must not delay or interfere with the performance of work by other contractors or Government employees, and it prints no words about heeding the direction of the contracting officer or about committing or permitting an act [@CL-CMP-0730; @CL-CMP-4877; @CL-CMP-5044].

In the protection clause, the RFO text says the contractor must preserve and protect structures, equipment and vegetation on or adjacent to the work site that are not to be removed and that do not unreasonably interfere with the work, without the codified word all [@CL-CMP-0731; @CL-CMP-4878]. Its paragraph (a) prints no sentence about avoiding damage to vegetation that will remain in place, and it names only breakage during contract performance as the cause of broken limbs, not careless operation of equipment or workmen [@CL-CMP-0731; @CL-CMP-0732; @CL-CMP-5045; @CL-CMP-5046]. Its paragraph (b) makes the duty to repair promptly depend on damage resulting from non-compliance with the contract or failure to exercise reasonable care, and it covers improvements and utilities on or near transportation paths and routes as well as those at or near the work site and on adjacent property of a third party [@CL-CMP-0733; @CL-CMP-0734; @CL-CMP-4879; @CL-CMP-5047]. It prints no qualifier that their locations are made known to or should be known by the contractor, which the codified paragraph prints [@CL-CMP-0733; @CL-CMP-0734; @CL-CMP-0735; @CL-CMP-4879]. It then says the contractor must repair any damage, including those that are the property of a third party [@CL-CMP-0735; @CL-CMP-5047]. The RFO text keeps the contracting officer's right to have the necessary work performed and charge the cost to the contractor if the contractor fails or refuses to repair the damage promptly, now in paragraph (c) [@CL-CMP-0736; @CL-CMP-4880].

In the cleaning up clause, paragraph (a) says the contractor must keep the work area, including storage areas, in a clean, neat, orderly condition and free from accumulations of waste materials, where the codified clause says to keep it free from accumulations of waste materials at all times [@CL-CMP-0737; @CL-CMP-5048]. Paragraph (b) says the contractor must remove rubbish, tools, scaffolding, equipment and materials that are not the property of the Government from the site before completing the work, where the codified clause says from the work and premises [@CL-CMP-0738; @CL-CMP-4881]. Paragraphs (a) and (b) do not print the codified words about leaving the work area clean, neat and orderly in a condition satisfactory to the contracting officer upon completing the work [@CL-CMP-0737; @CL-CMP-0738; @CL-CMP-5049].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

The Government awards a second contract for piling at the same site. The prime contractor reorders its crews to fit the piling work and does nothing that interferes with the piling crew [@CL-CMP-4877; @CL-CMP-5044]. While excavating, its crew breaks a limb of a tree that is to stay. The crew trims the limb with a clean cut and paints the cut with tree-pruning compound as the contracting officer directs [@CL-CMP-5046].

Later its excavator, operated without reasonable care, cracks a third-party water main whose location was shown on the utility plan. The contractor must repair the damage [@CL-CMP-4879; @CL-CMP-5047]. It does not repair promptly, so the contracting officer has the repair done for 12,000 and charges 12,000 to the contractor [@CL-CMP-4880]. At the end of the work, the contractor leaves scaffolding and rubble behind. It must remove them from the work and premises before completing the work [@CL-CMP-4881] and leave the work area clean, neat and orderly in a condition satisfactory to the contracting officer [@CL-CMP-5049]. Under the RFO text, the removal is from the site [@CL-CMP-0738], and the clean, neat, orderly condition is part of the duty to keep the work area, with no words about the contracting officer's satisfaction [@CL-CMP-0737; @CL-CMP-0738; @CL-CMP-5049].

## Never confuse

Adapting to other contracts is not the same duty as protecting what is already there: the contractor shall carefully adapt scheduling and performing the work under the contract to accommodate the additional work [@CL-CMP-4877], while protection and repair concern existing improvements and utilities, and the contractor shall repair any damage to the existing improvements and utilities resulting from failure to comply with the requirements of the contract or failure to exercise reasonable care in performing the work [@CL-CMP-5047]. Suspension and Government delay of work is covered in [Suspension and Government delay of work](cmp-suspension-and-government-delay-of-work.md). Cleaning up is the duty to keep the work area free from accumulations of waste materials and to remove non-Government items before completing the work [@CL-CMP-5048; @CL-CMP-4881]. The codified protection duty differs from the RFO sentence: the codified duty covers improvements and utilities whose locations are made known to or should be known by the contractor [@CL-CMP-4879], while the RFO sentence covers existing improvements and utilities at or near the work site, on adjacent property of a third party, and on or near transportation paths and routes, without that qualifier [@CL-CMP-0733; @CL-CMP-0734]. The codified clause ends with leaving the work area clean, neat and orderly in a condition satisfactory to the contracting officer [@CL-CMP-5049], while the RFO clause places the clean, neat, orderly condition in the duty to keep the work area [@CL-CMP-0737]. The corresponding provision and clause for services on Government installations are covered in [Site visit and protection of Government buildings, equipment and vegetation](../../supply-chain/procure-to-pay/sup-site-visit-and-protection-of-government-property.md).
