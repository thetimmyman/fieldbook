## Purpose

States the DoD policy on antiterrorism awareness training for contractor personnel who need routine physical access, the permitted ways to complete it, and when the training clause is included.

The subpart provides policy and guidance related to antiterrorism awareness training for contractor personnel who require routine physical access to a Federally-controlled facility or military installation [@CL-CMP-10018]. As used in the subpart, military installation means a base, camp, post, station, yard, center, or other activity under the jurisdiction of the Secretary of a military department or, in the case of an activity in a foreign country, under the operational control of the Secretary of a military department or the Secretary of Defense (see title 10 of the United States Code, section 2801(c)(4)) [@CL-CMP-10019; @CL-CMP-10020].

Under the DoD policy stated in the section, contractor personnel who, as a condition of contract performance, require routine physical access to a Federally-controlled facility or military installation are required to complete Level I antiterrorism awareness training within 30 days of requiring access and annually thereafter [@CL-CMP-7936]. The duty falls on those personnel. The section names no official who approves the training or grants an exception.

In accordance with Department of Defense Instruction O-2000.16, Volume 1, DoD Antiterrorism (AT) Program Implementation: DoD AT Standards, Level I antiterrorism awareness training may be completed [@CL-CMP-8152]. It may be completed through a DoD-sponsored and certified computer or web-based distance learning instruction for Level I antiterrorism awareness, or under the instruction of a qualified Level I antiterrorism awareness instructor [@CL-CMP-7937].

The clause at 252.204-7004, DoD Antiterrorism Awareness Training for Contractors, is to be included in solicitations and contracts, including those using FAR part 12 procedures for the acquisition of commercial products and commercial services [@CL-CMP-7938]. It is to be included when contractor personnel require routine physical access to a Federally-controlled facility or military installation [@CL-CMP-7939].

## How it works

The policy attaches to the person who needs access as a condition of performance. The first completion is due within the stated window counted from when access is required, and the training repeats annually thereafter [@CL-CMP-7936]. Two routes are permitted for completing the training: a DoD-sponsored and certified distance learning course, or instruction by a qualified instructor [@CL-CMP-7937].

The clause prescription is separate from the policy statement. The drafter of the solicitation or contract includes the clause when contractor personnel require routine physical access, and the section sets no dollar threshold [@CL-CMP-7939]. The prescription reaches commercial products and commercial services acquisitions that use FAR part 12 procedures [@CL-CMP-7938]. The clause's own duties are in the clause text and in [the antiterrorism training and force protection card](../trade-cyber/cmp-antiterrorism-training-and-force-protection-policy.md), not in these two sections.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A technician is assigned, as a condition of contract performance, to work that requires routine physical access to a military installation. The access requirement begins on a Monday, and the technician completes a DoD-sponsored and certified web-based Level I course 20 days later [@CL-CMP-7936; @CL-CMP-7937]. That is within 30 days of requiring access, so the first completion is on time, and the technician repeats the training annually thereafter [@CL-CMP-7936]. A different employee of the same contractor who only works at the contractor's own plant does not require routine physical access to the installation, so this section's training requirement does not cover that employee [@CL-CMP-7936]. The contract carries the training clause because contractor personnel require that access [@CL-CMP-7939].

## Never confuse

The policy and the clause prescription are different statements. The policy sets the 30-day and annual training requirement for the personnel [@CL-CMP-7936], and the prescription tells the drafter when to include the training clause [@CL-CMP-7939]. The 30 days run from requiring access, not from contract award [@CL-CMP-7936].
