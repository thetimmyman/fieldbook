## Purpose

Shows how a contracting officer treats covered telecommunications answers and the risk assessments held in the Supplier Performance Risk System (SPRS) when deciding a DoD award, and what an offeror must do after each answer.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-CMP-7241] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 204.2103 [@CL-CMP-7241]; DFARS 204.2104 [@CL-CMP-7489]; DFARS 204.7602 [@CL-CMP-7495]; DFARS 204.7603 [@CL-CMP-7249] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-7241] |
| Applies to | Offers that answer the provisions at DFARS 252.204-7016 and 252.204-7017 [@CL-CMP-7241; @CL-CMP-7242]; DoD award decisions where price risk and supplier risk are available in SPRS [@CL-CMP-7249], for solicitations for supplies and services other than those that DoD Instruction 5000.79 exempts [@CL-CMP-7495; @CL-CMP-7496] |

If the offeror selects "does not" in response to the provision at DFARS 252.204-7016, the contracting officer may rely on the representation unless the officer has an independent reason to question it [@CL-CMP-7241]. If the officer has a reason to question the "does not" representation in FAR 52.204-26, FAR 52.212-3(v) or DFARS 252.204-7016, the officer must consult the requiring activity and legal counsel [@CL-CMP-7243].

If the offeror selects "will not" in paragraph (d) of the provision at DFARS 252.204-7017, the officer may rely on the representation unless the officer has an independent reason to question it [@CL-CMP-7242]. A reason to question the "will not" representation in FAR 52.204-24 or DFARS 252.204-7017 likewise requires consultation with the requiring activity and legal counsel [@CL-CMP-7410].

An offeror that selects "does" in paragraph (c) of the provision at DFARS 252.204-7016 must complete the representation at DFARS 252.204-7017 [@CL-CMP-7244]. An offeror that selects "will" in paragraph (d) of the provision at DFARS 252.204-7017 must provide the information required by paragraph (e) of that provision [@CL-CMP-7245].

## How it works

When an offeror completes paragraph (e) of the provision at FAR 52.204-24 or DFARS 252.204-7017, the contracting officer must forward the offeror's representation and disclosure information to the requiring activity [@CL-CMP-7246; @CL-CMP-7422]. The officer must not award to the offeror unless the requiring activity gives one of two kinds of advice [@CL-CMP-7422]:

- For equipment, systems or services that use covered telecommunications equipment or services as a substantial or essential component of any system, or as critical technology as part of any system, the advice is that a waiver as described at FAR 4.2104 has been granted [@CL-CMP-7247]. Under FAR 4.2104 the head of an executive agency may, on a one-time basis, waive the prohibition for a Government entity that requests it [@CL-CMP-2965], but only for a period not extending beyond August 13, 2021 for the prohibition at FAR 4.2102(a)(1) or beyond August 13, 2022 for the prohibition at FAR 4.2102(a)(2), and only on the submissions that paragraph lists [@CL-CMP-2966]. Separately, the Director of National Intelligence may provide a waiver on determining that it is in the national security interests of the United States [@CL-CMP-2967].
- For equipment, systems or services to be used to carry out covered missions that use covered defense telecommunications equipment or services as a substantial or essential component of any system, or as critical technology as part of any system, the advice is that a waiver as described at DFARS 204.2104 has been granted [@CL-CMP-7411]. Under DFARS 204.2104 the Secretary of Defense may grant that waiver, case by case and for a single one-year period, if the Secretary determines it to be in the national security interests of the United States [@CL-CMP-7489] and certifies to the Congressional defense committees that there are sufficient mitigations in place to guarantee the ability of the Secretary to carry out the covered missions [@CL-CMP-7490] and that the Secretary is removing the use of covered defense telecommunications equipment or services in carrying out such missions [@CL-CMP-7491]. This card does not restate who grants the waiver at FAR 4.2104 [@CL-CMP-7247].

If a contractor reports information to the DIBNet site in accordance with the clause at FAR 52.204-25 or DFARS 252.204-7018, the Defense Cyber Crime Center notifies the contracting officer, who consults the requiring activity on how to proceed with the contract [@CL-CMP-7248].

On risk, the contracting officer must consider price risk and supplier risk, if available in SPRS, as part of the award decision [@CL-CMP-7249]. SPRS risk assessments are required for evaluating quotations or offers in response to solicitations for supplies and services, including solicitations that use FAR part 12 procedures [@CL-CMP-7495], other than solicitations for supplies or services that DoD Instruction 5000.79 exempts [@CL-CMP-7496]. The officer must use discretion in considering the SPRS information on item risk, price risk and supplier risk as the paragraphs that follow set out [@CL-CMP-7492]. For an end product identified by a material identifier that is available as described at PGI 204.7603, the officer must also consider item risk assessments, if available [@CL-CMP-7250]. An offeror or quoter without a risk assessment in SPRS must not be considered favorably or unfavorably [@CL-CMP-7412].

- Item risk is considered to determine whether the procurement represents a high performance risk to the Government [@CL-CMP-7414]. Before issuing a solicitation for such an end product the officer must ensure that an item risk search has been performed and must consider any warnings [@CL-CMP-7415]. If no search was performed before the solicitation, one is required when quotations or offers are evaluated [@CL-CMP-7416]. If there are item risk warnings, the officer must consider strategies to mitigate risk, such as consulting with the program office and including mitigating requirements in the statement of work, as provided by the requiring activity [@CL-CMP-7493].
- Price risk is considered when procuring a service, or an end product identified by a material identifier that is available as described at PGI 204.7603 [@CL-CMP-7418]. It informs whether a proposed price is consistent with historical prices paid for an item or otherwise creates a risk to the Government [@CL-CMP-7423]. The officer must not rely solely on it when determining prices to be fair and reasonable [@CL-CMP-7419]. The officer must consider strategies to mitigate price risk, such as not awarding to an offeror or quoter with a high risk price rating unless there is a way to justify the price through additional price or cost analysis [@CL-CMP-7420]. Other strategies include utilizing appropriate price negotiation techniques and procedures and using price reasonableness or price realism techniques at FAR 13.106 or 15.4 [@CL-CMP-7494].
- Supplier risk is considered to assess the risk of unsuccessful performance and supply chain risk, and the SPRS assessments include quality, delivery and other contractor performance information [@CL-CMP-7421].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contracting officer evaluates two offers for the same service. The first offeror selects "does not" in response to the provision at DFARS 252.204-7016, and the officer has no independent reason to question the answer, so the officer may rely on it [@CL-CMP-7241]. The second offeror selects "will" in paragraph (d) of the provision at DFARS 252.204-7017 and completes paragraph (e), so the officer forwards the representation and disclosure information to the requiring activity and may not award to that offeror unless the requiring activity advises that the applicable waiver has been granted [@CL-CMP-7245; @CL-CMP-7246; @CL-CMP-7422].

SPRS shows a high price risk rating for the first offeror and no risk assessment at all for the second. The officer considers the price risk assessment against historical prices but does not rely on it alone to find the price fair and reasonable [@CL-CMP-7423; @CL-CMP-7419]. The officer considers not awarding to the first offeror unless additional price or cost analysis justifies the price [@CL-CMP-7420]. The second offeror is not considered favorably or unfavorably for lacking an assessment [@CL-CMP-7412].

## Never confuse

A covered telecommunications representation is a statement about whether covered equipment or services are used, while an SPRS risk assessment is a rating the officer considers in the award decision [@CL-CMP-7241; @CL-CMP-7249].

The "does not" answer belongs to the provision at DFARS 252.204-7016 and the "will not" answer to the provision at DFARS 252.204-7017, and each has its own consultation route [@CL-CMP-7243; @CL-CMP-7410]. The procedures and waivers card carries the full representation flow; see [Covered telecommunications procedures and waivers](../trade-cyber/cmp-covered-telecommunications-procedures-and-waivers.md).
