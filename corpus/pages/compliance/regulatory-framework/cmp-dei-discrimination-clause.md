## Purpose

Records what a contractor agrees to under the Revolutionary FAR Overhaul clause on racially discriminatory diversity, equity and inclusion activities: the conduct, information, reporting, materiality and flow-down terms. The printed clause has no paragraph headed certification or representation.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | RFO model text [@CL-CMP-8603] |
| Codified citation | Not established. |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.222-90 [@CL-CMP-8603] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Deviation text [@CL-CMP-8603] |
| Applies to | Work performed under a contract that carries the clause [@CL-CMP-8603] |

The clause is inserted as prescribed in 22.2203 [@CL-CMP-8602]. As used in the clause, racially discriminatory DEI activities means disparate treatment based on race or ethnicity in recruitment, employment (for example hiring and promotions), contracting (for example vendor agreements), program participation, or allocation or deployment of an entity's resources [@CL-CMP-8601; @CL-CMP-8616]. Program participation means membership or participation in, or access or admission to training, mentoring or leadership development programs, educational opportunities, clubs, associations or similar opportunities that are sponsored or established by the contractor or subcontractor [@CL-CMP-8600].

In connection with the performance of work under the contract, the Contractor agrees that it will not engage in any racially discriminatory DEI activities [@CL-CMP-8603]. It will furnish all information and reports, including access to books, records and accounts, as the Contracting Officer requires for ascertaining compliance with the clause [@CL-CMP-8604]. It will report any subcontractor's known or reasonably knowable conduct that may violate the clause to the Contracting Officer, and take any appropriate remedial actions the Contracting Officer directs [@CL-CMP-8606]. It will inform the Contracting Officer if a subcontractor sues the Contractor and the suit puts at issue, in any way, the validity of the clause [@CL-CMP-8607]. It recognizes that compliance with the requirements of the clause is material to the Government's payment decisions for purposes of section 3729(b)(4) of title 31 of the United States Code [@CL-CMP-8608].

If the Contractor or a subcontractor does not comply, the contract may be canceled, terminated or suspended in whole or in part, and the Contractor or subcontractor may be declared ineligible for further Government contracts [@CL-CMP-8605]. The paragraph does not say who takes those steps.

The Contractor must include the substance of the clause, including the flow-down paragraph itself, in subcontracts at any tier, including those for commercial products and commercial services, except those where the place of delivery or performance is outside the United States [@CL-CMP-8609].

## How it works

The Contracting Officer is the decision point the clause names. The Contracting Officer requires the information and reports, directs the remedial actions, and receives the report on subcontractor conduct and the notice of a subcontractor suit [@CL-CMP-8604; @CL-CMP-8606; @CL-CMP-8607]. The conduct duty falls on the Contractor for work under its contract, and the flow-down carries the substance of the clause to subcontractors [@CL-CMP-8603; @CL-CMP-8609]. Noncompliance by the Contractor or a subcontractor opens the possible consequences in paragraph (b)(3) [@CL-CMP-8605]. The Contractor's recognition that compliance is material to payment decisions is stated in paragraph (b)(6) [@CL-CMP-8608].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor holds a 12-month service contract that carries the clause. A subcontractor performing in the United States limits access to its leadership development program by race, which is disparate treatment in program participation as the clause defines it [@CL-CMP-8600; @CL-CMP-8601]. Assume the conduct is in connection with the performance of work under the contract [@CL-CMP-8603]. The contractor learns of the conduct. It must report it to the Contracting Officer and take any appropriate remedial actions the Contracting Officer directs [@CL-CMP-8606]. If the subcontractor then sues the contractor and the suit puts the validity of the clause at issue, the contractor must also inform the Contracting Officer [@CL-CMP-8607]. The subcontract, for commercial services performed in the United States, must carry the substance of the clause [@CL-CMP-8609]. Cancellation, termination or suspension of the contract and a declaration of ineligibility are possible consequences of noncompliance, not automatic ones [@CL-CMP-8605].

## Never confuse

The Contractor's own duties are not the flow-down. The conduct, information, reporting and materiality terms are in paragraph (b), while paragraph (c) carries the substance into subcontracts at any tier, except those where the place of delivery or performance is outside the United States [@CL-CMP-8603; @CL-CMP-8609]. Recognizing materiality is not a certification of compliance: the printed clause has no certification paragraph, and paragraph (b)(6) records only that the Contractor recognizes that compliance is material to the Government's payment decisions [@CL-CMP-8608].

## Company calibration

Which contracts and subcontracts the company holds that carry the clause, who confirms the flow-down, and how conduct reports, information requests and subcontractor suits reach the Contracting Officer. [@Q-004; @Q-014]
