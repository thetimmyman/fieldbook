## Purpose

Shows how a general wage determination differs from a project wage determination, how long each lasts, and how an agency obtains each.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-3011] |
| Codified citation | FAR 22.404, FAR 22.404-1 and FAR 22.404-3 [@CL-CMP-3011; @CL-CMP-3014; @CL-CMP-7297; @CL-CMP-7298] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-3011] |
| Applies to | Construction solicitations and contracts that need a wage determination, either a general one for a geographical area or a project one [@CL-CMP-3011; @CL-CMP-3013] |

A general wage determination contains prevailing wage rates for the types of construction it designates and is used in contracts performed within a specified geographical area [@CL-CMP-3011]. General wage determinations contain no expiration date and remain valid until modified, superseded, or canceled by the Department of Labor [@CL-CMP-3106]. Once incorporated in a contract, it normally remains effective for the life of the contract unless the contracting officer exercises an option to extend the term, and general wage determinations shall be used whenever possible [@CL-CMP-3012]. A project wage determination is issued at the specific request of a contracting agency, is used only when no general wage determination applies, and is effective for 180 calendar days from the date of the determination [@CL-CMP-3013]. If a project wage determination expires before contract award, it may be possible to obtain an extension to the 180-day life of the determination [@CL-CMP-3108]. Once incorporated in a contract, a project wage determination normally remains effective for the life of the contract, unless the contracting officer exercises an option to extend the term of the contract [@CL-CMP-3107].

If a general wage determination applicable to the project is on the Wage Determinations at SAM.gov website, the agency may use it without notifying the Department of Labor, and when necessary a request for one may be made by submitting Standard Form 308 [@CL-CMP-3014]. If a general wage determination is not available on that website, a contracting agency shall submit requests for project wage determinations on Standard Form 308 to the Department of Labor [@CL-CMP-3015]. Although the time the Department of Labor needs varies with the facts and circumstances, an agency should expect the processing of a project wage determination request to take at least 30 days [@CL-CMP-3109]. Agencies should submit requests for the primary site at least 45 days, or 60 days if possible, before issuing the solicitation or exercising an option to extend the term of a contract [@CL-CMP-3016]. In the solicitation and contract, the contracting officer must incorporate only the appropriate wage determinations and must designate the work to which each determination or part thereof applies [@CL-CMP-3110]. The rest of the general requirements section, including its guidance on choosing rate schedules, is not drafted on this card.

Wage determinations apply only to those laborers and mechanics employed by a contractor upon the site of the work, including drivers who transport to or from the site materials and equipment used in the course of contract operations [@CL-CMP-7297]. Determinations are issued for different types of construction, such as building, heavy, highway, and residential (referred to as rate schedules), and apply only to the types of construction designated in the determination [@CL-CMP-7298].

## How it works

The agency first looks for a general wage determination that applies to the project. Where one exists, the agency may use it without telling the Department of Labor [@CL-CMP-3014]. Only when none is available does the agency ask for a project wage determination on the standard form [@CL-CMP-3013; @CL-CMP-3015]. Because a project determination is effective for 180 calendar days from its date and may expire before award, the lead time in the request rule matters [@CL-CMP-3013; @CL-CMP-3108; @CL-CMP-3109; @CL-CMP-3016]. What the contracting officer does when one does expire is on [Project wage determination expiration and modifications before and after bid opening or award](cmp-project-wage-determination-expiration-and-modifications.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An agency plans a building project in a county with no applicable general determination. It sends a request for a project determination on the standard form 90 days before it plans to issue the solicitation. The determination is issued and is effective for 180 days from its date. The 180 days run from the date of the determination, not from the solicitation. The contracting officer should aim to award before the determination expires, and if award cannot be made in time, the expiration rules apply.

## Never confuse

A project wage determination is not a general one with an end date added. It is issued at an agency's specific request and is used only when no general determination applies [@CL-CMP-3013], and it is effective for 180 calendar days from its date [@CL-CMP-3013], while a general determination has no expiration date [@CL-CMP-3106]. Which work and contracts need a determination at all is on [Construction wage rate requirements: definitions and which contracts are covered](cmp-construction-wage-definitions-and-applicability.md). Service contract wage determinations follow a separate regime, covered on [Service Contract Labor Standards: obtaining wage determinations and successor pay under a collective bargaining agreement](cmp-scls-wage-determinations-and-successorship.md).
