## Purpose

Shows which research the clause covers, the assurance and approvals needed before work starts, and the reporting, inspection, stop-work and flowdown duties.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-CMP-4582] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 252.235-7004 [@CL-CMP-4582] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-4582] |
| Applies to | Contracts with the human subjects clause, for research involving human subjects [@CL-CMP-4582], and subcontracts that may include such research [@CL-CMP-4589], except subcontracts that involve only the use of cadaver materials [@CL-CMP-4723] |

A human subject is a living individual about whom an investigator conducting research obtains data through intervention or interaction with the individual, or identifiable private information [@CL-CMP-4581]. Research means a systematic investigation, including research, development, testing, and evaluation, designed to develop or contribute to generalizable knowledge, and activities that meet this definition constitute research for purposes of the Federal rule at 32 CFR Part 219 [@CL-CMP-4722]. The contractor must oversee the execution of the research to ensure compliance with the clause [@CL-CMP-4720]. It must comply fully with the Federal rule and the Department of Defense directive the clause cites, applicable component policies, the statute the clause cites and, when applicable, Food and Drug Administration policies and regulations [@CL-CMP-4721]. It must not start research involving human subjects, whether covered by the Federal rule or meeting its exemption criteria, or spend funding on it, until the conditions of one of the two listed paths are met [@CL-CMP-4582].

On the first path, the contractor furnishes an assurance of compliance and institutional review board approval to the Human Research Protection Official with a copy to the contracting officer, and receives notification from the contracting officer that the official has approved the assurance as appropriate for the research under the Statement of Work [@CL-CMP-4583]. That notification must also say that the official has reviewed the protocol and accepted the board approval for compliance with the Department of Defense component policies [@CL-CMP-4584]. The contractor may furnish evidence of an existing assurance of compliance for acceptance by the official, if an appropriate assurance has been approved in connection with previous research [@CL-CMP-4783]. On the second path, the contractor furnishes a determination that the proposed human research meets the exemption criteria, with a copy to the contracting officer, and receives written notification from the contracting officer that the exemption is determined acceptable [@CL-CMP-4585]. The exemption determination must include citation of the exemption category and a rationale statement [@CL-CMP-4724]. If there is a disagreement about the contractor's exemption determination, the official retains final judgment on what research activities or classes of research are covered or are exempt under the contract [@CL-CMP-4725].

## How it works

Work and spending wait for one of the two paths [@CL-CMP-4582]. The contractor must notify the contracting officer immediately of any suspensions or terminations of the assurance [@CL-CMP-4586], and DoD staff, consultants and advisory groups may independently review and inspect the research and its procedures, with the Department able to prohibit research that presents unacceptable hazards or otherwise fails to comply with its procedures [@CL-CMP-4587]. Failure to comply results in a stop-work order under Federal Acquisition Regulation clause 52.242-15 that suspends work and further payment [@CL-CMP-4588], and the clause also provides for another suspension of work and further payment for as long as determined necessary at the discretion of the contracting officer [@CL-CMP-4726]. The clause requires that its substance, including its flowdown paragraph, be included in all subcontracts that may include such research, including research that meets the exemption criteria [@CL-CMP-4589]. It does not apply to subcontracts that involve only the use of cadaver materials [@CL-CMP-4723].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

Assume a research contract worth 1,000,000 under which a contractor plans interviews with volunteers, which is research involving human subjects. It sends the Human Research Protection Official, with a copy to the contracting officer, its assurance of compliance and board approval. The contracting officer then tells it that the official has approved the assurance and has accepted the board approval. Only then does work start and funding get spent. The contractor also awards a subcontract, assumed to be worth 100,000, that involves only the use of cadaver materials; the clause does not apply to that subcontract. Later the assurance is suspended, and the contractor tells the contracting officer at once.

## Never confuse

Exempt research is not research outside the clause. Even research that meets the exemption criteria needs a determination that the contracting officer has accepted in writing before work starts [@CL-CMP-4582; @CL-CMP-4585]. The stop-work consequence is also a suspension of work and payment for noncompliance, not a decision to end the contract; see [Stop-work orders](cmp-stop-work-orders.md). Research with live animals has its own registration and approval; see [Animal welfare in research](cmp-dfars-animal-welfare-in-research.md).
