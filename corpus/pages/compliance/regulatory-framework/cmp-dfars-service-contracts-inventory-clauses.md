## Purpose

Defines a first-tier subcontract for the service contracts inventory subpart, and states when the reporting clause is prescribed and whether its basic or alternate form is used.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-CMP-7908] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 204.1701 [@CL-CMP-7908; @CL-CMP-7909]; DFARS 204.1705 [@CL-CMP-7910; @CL-CMP-7911; @CL-CMP-7912; @CL-CMP-7913; @CL-CMP-7914] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-7908] |
| Applies to | Solicitations, contracts, agreements and orders that meet both the value criterion and the portfolio group criterion [@CL-CMP-7910; @CL-CMP-7911; @CL-CMP-7912] |

As used in the service contracts inventory subpart, a first-tier subcontract is a subcontract awarded directly by the contractor for the purpose of acquiring services for performance of a prime contract [@CL-CMP-7908]. It does not include the contractor's supplier agreements with vendors, such as long-term arrangements for materials or supplies or services that benefit multiple contracts or the costs of which are normally applied to the contractor's general and administrative expenses or indirect costs [@CL-CMP-7909].

The basic or the alternate of the clause at 252.204-7023, Reporting Requirements for Contracted Services, is to be used in solicitations, contracts, agreements and orders that meet two criteria, including solicitations and contracts using FAR part 12 procedures for commercial products and commercial services [@CL-CMP-7910]. The first is a total estimated value, including options, that exceeds 3 million dollars [@CL-CMP-7911]. The second is that the work is for services in one of four service acquisition portfolio groups: logistics management services, equipment-related services, knowledge-based services, or electronics and communications services [@CL-CMP-7912]. Both criteria must be met.

Paragraph (a)(ii) directs the basic clause for solicitations and contracts other than solicitations and resultant awards of indefinite-delivery contracts, and for orders placed under non-DoD contracts, that meet those criteria [@CL-CMP-7913]. Paragraph (a)(iii) directs Alternate I for solicitations and resultant awards of indefinite-delivery contracts, basic ordering agreements and blanket purchase agreements when one or more of the orders under the contract or agreement are expected to meet those criteria [@CL-CMP-7914].

## How it works

The value test is measured on the solicitation, contract, agreement or order itself, including options; the section sets no threshold on a subcontract [@CL-CMP-7910; @CL-CMP-7911]. A value of exactly 3 million dollars does not exceed the threshold [@CL-CMP-7911]. The portfolio group test is separate, so a large contract for services outside the four groups is not caught by this prescription [@CL-CMP-7912].

The form follows the vehicle. The basic clause is for solicitations and contracts other than indefinite-delivery solicitations and awards, and for orders placed under non-DoD contracts, that meet the criteria, while Alternate I is for solicitations and resultant awards of indefinite-delivery contracts, basic ordering agreements and blanket purchase agreements when one or more orders are expected to meet the criteria [@CL-CMP-7913; @CL-CMP-7914]. The definition of a first-tier subcontract applies in this subpart: a direct award to acquire services for the prime contract counts, while a vendor supply arrangement does not [@CL-CMP-7908; @CL-CMP-7909]. What the clause requires the contractor to report is in the clause text and the linked cards [@CL-CMP-7910].

The section assigns no decision to a named official; the prescription tells the drafter of the solicitation or contract when to use each form of the clause [@CL-CMP-7910].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A solicitation for a single contract for knowledge-based services has a total estimated value of 3 million dollars including options. The value does not exceed 3 million dollars, so the reporting clause is not prescribed on this basis [@CL-CMP-7911]. If the value including options were 3.4 million dollars, both criteria would be met and the basic clause would be used [@CL-CMP-7910; @CL-CMP-7911; @CL-CMP-7912]. For an indefinite-delivery contract for electronics and communications services where one or more orders are expected to exceed 3 million dollars including options, Alternate I would be used [@CL-CMP-7911; @CL-CMP-7912; @CL-CMP-7914]. A contractor that awards a subcontract directly to acquire services for performance of a prime contract has a first-tier subcontract, but a long-term materials arrangement that serves several contracts and is charged to general and administrative expense is a supplier agreement and not a first-tier subcontract [@CL-CMP-7908; @CL-CMP-7909].

## Never confuse

A first-tier subcontract is not a supplier agreement. The first is a direct award to acquire services for a prime contract; the second is a vendor arrangement, for example for materials, supplies or services that benefit multiple contracts or are charged to indirect costs [@CL-CMP-7908; @CL-CMP-7909]. The value test is also not enough on its own: the services must fall in a listed portfolio group [@CL-CMP-7911; @CL-CMP-7912].
