## Purpose

Shows where cognizant audit offices are listed and what the contract administration office must and may do on a contract assigned to it. The duties in both sections fall on Government offices and personnel, not on the contractor.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-7790] |
| Codified citation | FAR 42.103 and FAR 42.201 [@CL-CMP-7790; @CL-CMP-7793] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 42.103 [@CL-CMP-0074; @CL-CMP-0075]; RFO FAR 42.201 [@CL-CMP-0077] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-7790] |
| Applies to | Audit cognizance, and each contract assigned for administration [@CL-CMP-7790; @CL-CMP-7793] |

The Defense Contract Audit Agency (DCAA) maintains and distributes the Directory of Federal Contract Audit Offices, which identifies the cognizant audit offices and the contractors over which they have cognizance [@CL-CMP-7790]. Changes to audit cognizance shall be provided to DCAA so that the directory can be updated [@CL-CMP-7791]. The codified section does not say who provides a change [@CL-CMP-7791]. Agencies may obtain a copy of the directory, or information about cognizant audit offices, by contacting DCAA [@CL-CMP-7792].

For each contract assigned for administration, the contract administration office shall perform the functions listed in FAR 42.302(a) to the extent that they apply to the contract, except for the functions specifically withheld [@CL-CMP-7793]. It performs the functions listed in FAR 42.302(b) only when and to the extent the contracting officer specifically authorizes them [@CL-CMP-7794]. It shall request supporting contract administration under FAR 42.202(e) and (f) when that support is required [@CL-CMP-7795]. The section also notes that the Defense Contract Management Agency and other agencies offer a wide variety of contract administration and support services [@CL-CMP-7990].

## How it works

The contracting officer is the only decision maker the sections name, and that officer decides whether the office may perform any FAR 42.302(b) function [@CL-CMP-7794]. For the FAR 42.302(a) functions that apply to the contract the office acts by default, and the only exception the section names is a function that was specifically withheld from it [@CL-CMP-7793]. The section does not say who withholds a function or how; the assignment of administration and the delegation are on [Assignment of contract administration](cmp-assignment-of-contract-administration.md). The two lists of functions are on [Contract administration functions](../audit-retention/cmp-contract-administration-functions.md), and the DoD additions are on [DoD additions to contract administration functions](cmp-dod-contract-administration-additions.md).

The audit directory and the assignment of administration are separate. The directory is kept by DCAA and names audit offices [@CL-CMP-7790]. The administration duties belong to the contract administration office for each assigned contract [@CL-CMP-7793]. The roles of the contracting officer, the administration agency and the auditor are on [Contracting officers, DCMA and DCAA](../audit-retention/cmp-oversight-roles.md).

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 42 text in place of the codified text under Class Deviation 2026-O0050, effective March 16, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9759] [@CL-CMP-9761]. RFO FAR 42.103(a) has DCAA maintain the Directory of Federal Contract Audit Offices, which identifies cognizant audit offices and the assigned contractors they have cognizance over, where the codified sentence has DCAA maintain and distribute it [@CL-CMP-0074; @CL-CMP-7790]. The RFO text says Contract Audit Offices must provide audit cognizance changes to DCAA for directory updates, where the codified sentence says only that changes shall be provided to DCAA [@CL-CMP-0075; @CL-CMP-7791]. To obtain the directory or information about audit offices, RFO paragraph (b) says to contact the Defense Contract Audit Agency [@CL-CMP-0076; @CL-CMP-7792]. RFO FAR 42.201 keeps the three duties of the contract administration office for each contract assigned for administration: to perform the functions listed in 42.302(a) to the extent that they apply to the contract, except for the functions specifically withheld [@CL-CMP-0077; @CL-CMP-7793], to perform the functions listed in 42.302(b) only when and to the extent specifically authorized by the contracting officer [@CL-CMP-0078; @CL-CMP-7794], and to request supporting contract administration under 42.202(e) and (f) as required [@CL-CMP-0079; @CL-CMP-7795]. RFO FAR 42.201 has a lead-in sentence followed by paragraphs (a) to (c) [@CL-CMP-0077; @CL-CMP-0078; @CL-CMP-0079], where the codified section also has a paragraph (b), the note that the Defense Contract Management Agency and other agencies offer a wide variety of contract administration and support services [@CL-CMP-7990].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contract worth 4,000,000 is assigned to a contract administration office, and the assignment specifically withholds 2 of the paragraph (a) functions. The office performs the other paragraph (a) functions that apply to the contract and does not perform the 2 withheld ones [@CL-CMP-7793]. Later the contractor proposes a change to the delivery schedule. Negotiating a supplemental agreement that changes the delivery schedule is a paragraph (b) function, so the office may do it only when and to the extent the contracting officer specifically authorizes it [@CL-CMP-7794; @CL-CMP-1444]. The office also finds that it needs supporting administration at the contractor's other plant and requests it [@CL-CMP-7795]. The answer: paragraph (a) functions by default except those withheld, paragraph (b) functions only on the contracting officer's specific authorization, and supporting administration by request.

## Never confuse

The cognizant audit office, which holds audit cognizance over the contractor and appears in the directory [@CL-CMP-7790], is not the contract administration office that performs the listed functions on an assigned contract [@CL-CMP-7793].
