## Purpose

Shows the two Governmentwide funding bars that attach to particular contractors, and the clock that governs how long a suspension can last.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-7191] |
| Codified citation | FAR 9.108-2, 9.110-4 and 9.407-4 [@CL-CMP-7191; @CL-CMP-7194; @CL-CMP-7196] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-7191] |
| Applies to | Contracts using appropriated funds, and suspended contractors [@CL-CMP-7191; @CL-CMP-7196] |

Appropriations acts prohibit, on a Governmentwide basis, the use of appropriated or otherwise made available funds for contracts with either an inverted domestic corporation or a subsidiary of such a corporation, except as provided in the exception paragraph and in the waiver section [@CL-CMP-7191]. The exception is that the prohibition does not apply to a Federal Government contract entered into before the date of enactment of the Act, or to any task order issued under such a contract [@CL-CMP-7192]. To apply the prohibition and the exception correctly, contracting officers should consult legal counsel if, during performance of a contract, a contractor becomes an inverted domestic corporation or a subsidiary of one [@CL-CMP-7193].

A second bar applies where the Secretary of Defense determines, under the procedures at 32 CFR part 216, that an institution of higher education is ineligible to receive funds from a covered agency because of a policy or practice described in the preceding section of the subpart [@CL-CMP-7304]. The Secretary of Defense then creates an active exclusion record for the institution in the System for Award Management, and a covered agency may not solicit offers from, award contracts to, or consent to subcontracts with the institution [@CL-CMP-7194]. The prohibition on a covered agency soliciting offers from, awarding contracts to, or consenting to subcontracts with the institution does not apply to acquisitions at or below the simplified acquisition threshold or to acquisitions of commercial products and commercial services, including commercially available off-the-shelf items [@CL-CMP-7195].

## How it works

A suspension is for a temporary period pending the completion of an investigation and any ensuing legal proceedings, unless the suspending and debarring official terminates it sooner or it ends as otherwise provided in the section [@CL-CMP-7196].

If legal proceedings are not initiated within 12 months after the date of the suspension notice, the suspension must be terminated unless an office of a United States Assistant Attorney General, a United States Attorney or another responsible prosecuting official requests its extension [@CL-CMP-7197].

On that request the suspension may be extended for an additional 6 months, and a suspension may not extend beyond 18 months unless legal proceedings have been initiated within that period [@CL-CMP-7198].

The suspending and debarring official must notify the Department of Justice or other responsible prosecuting official of the proposed termination at least 30 days before the 12-month period expires, to give that official an opportunity to request an extension [@CL-CMP-7199].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor is suspended on a notice dated 1 March of one year, and the suspending and debarring official has not terminated the suspension earlier. At least 30 days before the 12 months run out, the official notifies the Department of Justice of the proposed termination [@CL-CMP-7199]. No legal proceedings have begun by the end of the 12 months, and no prosecuting official has requested an extension, so the suspension must be terminated [@CL-CMP-7197]. Had a prosecuting official requested an extension, the suspension could run for an additional 6 months, to 18 months in all, and would end then unless legal proceedings had been initiated within that period [@CL-CMP-7198].

In a second case, legal proceedings are initiated in month 9. The suspension is no longer subject to the 12-month termination, because the termination applies only when proceedings are not initiated within 12 months [@CL-CMP-7197]. It remains temporary, pending completion of the ensuing legal proceedings, unless the suspending and debarring official terminates it sooner [@CL-CMP-7196].

## Never confuse

The funding bars apply to contracts with an inverted domestic corporation or a subsidiary of one [@CL-CMP-7191] and to an institution for which the Secretary of Defense has created an active exclusion record [@CL-CMP-7194]. A suspension may not extend beyond 18 months, unless legal proceedings have been initiated within that period [@CL-CMP-7198].
