## Purpose

Shows which hotline posters a contractor displays on DoD contracts, where, and how the duty passes to subcontractors.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-CMP-4077] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 252.203-7004 [@CL-CMP-4077] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-4077] |
| Applies to | Contracts that include the clause, used as prescribed in DFARS 203.1004(b)(2)(ii) [@CL-CMP-4077] |

The Contractor shall display prominently the DoD fraud, waste, and abuse hotline poster prepared by the DoD Office of the Inspector General, in effect at time of contract award, in common work areas within business segments performing work under DoD contracts [@CL-CMP-4078]. For contracts performed outside the United States, when security concerns can be appropriately demonstrated, the contracting officer may provide the contractor the option to publicize the program to contractor personnel in a manner other than public display of the poster [@CL-CMP-4079]. If a significant portion of the employee workforce does not speak English, the poster is to be displayed in the foreign languages that a significant portion of the employees speak [@CL-CMP-4120]. If the Contractor maintains a company Web site as a method of providing information to employees, the Contractor shall display an electronic version of the required poster at the Web site [@CL-CMP-4121].

If the contract is funded, in whole or in part, by Department of Homeland Security disaster relief funds and the work is to be performed in the United States, the DHS fraud hotline poster shall be displayed in addition to the DoD hotline poster [@CL-CMP-4080].

The Contractor shall include the substance of this clause in all subcontracts that exceed the threshold specified in DFARS 203.1004(b)(2)(ii) on the date of subcontract award, except when the subcontract is for the acquisition of a commercial product or commercial service [@CL-CMP-4081; @CL-CMP-4082].

## How it works

The DoD poster goes in common work areas of the business segments that perform DoD work, and the poster in effect at contract award is the one to display [@CL-CMP-4078]. For contracts performed outside the United States, when security concerns can be appropriately demonstrated, the contracting officer may provide the option to publicize the program to contractor personnel in a manner other than public display of the poster [@CL-CMP-4079]. If the contract is funded, in whole or in part, by DHS disaster relief funds and the work is to be performed in the United States, the DHS fraud hotline poster shall be displayed in addition to the DoD hotline poster [@CL-CMP-4080]. If a significant portion of the employee workforce does not speak English, the poster is to be displayed in the foreign languages that a significant portion of the employees speak, and if the Contractor maintains a company Web site as a method of providing information to employees, the Contractor shall display an electronic version of the required poster at the Web site [@CL-CMP-4120; @CL-CMP-4121]. The Contractor shall include the substance of the clause in all subcontracts that exceed the threshold specified in DFARS 203.1004(b)(2)(ii) on the date of subcontract award, except when the subcontract is for the acquisition of a commercial product or commercial service [@CL-CMP-4081; @CL-CMP-4082].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor performs a DoD contract at 2 sites in the United States. Part of the funding comes from DHS disaster relief funds. Assume the subcontract threshold in the DFARS section is 2 million, a made-up figure. The contractor plans one subcontract for 3 million to buy a commercial service and another for 4 million for custom engineering.

Answer: both sites display the DoD poster in common work areas, and the DHS poster as well. The custom engineering subcontract carries the substance of the clause. The commercial service subcontract is excepted.
