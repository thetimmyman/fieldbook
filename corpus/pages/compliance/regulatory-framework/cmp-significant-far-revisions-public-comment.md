## Purpose

Explains when a FAR revision is significant, what the Federal Register notice must carry, how long comment runs, and when comments need not be solicited.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-8363] |
| Codified citation | FAR 1.501-1; FAR 1.501-2; FAR 1.501-3 [@CL-CMP-8363; @CL-CMP-8366; @CL-CMP-8370] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-8363] |
| Applies to | Proposed significant revisions to coverage in the FAR System [@CL-CMP-8363; @CL-CMP-8366] |

As used in the subpart on agency and public participation, significant revisions are revisions that alter the substantive meaning of any coverage in the FAR System and which have a significant cost or administrative impact on contractors or offerors, or a significant effect beyond the internal operating procedures of the issuing agency [@CL-CMP-8676; @CL-CMP-8363]. The expression does not include editorial, stylistic, or other revisions that have no impact on the basic meaning of the coverage being revised [@CL-CMP-8364].

Views of agencies and nongovernmental parties or organizations will be considered in formulating acquisition policies and procedures [@CL-CMP-8365]. The opportunity to submit written comments on proposed significant revisions shall be provided by placing a notice in the Federal Register [@CL-CMP-8366].

## How it works

Each notice shall include the text of the revision or, if it is impracticable to publish the full text, a summary of the proposal [@CL-CMP-8367]. It shall also include the address and telephone number of the individual from whom the full text can be requested and to whom comments should be addressed [@CL-CMP-8674]. A minimum of 30 days and, normally, at least 60 days will be given for the receipt of comments [@CL-CMP-8368; @CL-CMP-8369].

Comments need not be solicited when the proposed coverage does not constitute a significant revision [@CL-CMP-8370]. Advance comments need not be solicited when urgent and compelling circumstances make solicitation impracticable before the effective date of the coverage [@CL-CMP-8371]. An example is a new statute that must be implemented in a relatively short period of time [@CL-CMP-8675]. In that case the coverage shall be issued on a temporary basis and shall provide for at least a 30 day public comment period [@CL-CMP-8372]. The notice must then state that the revision is effective on a temporary basis pending completion of the public comment period [@CL-CMP-8373].

The text gives no authority to shorten the 30-day minimum [@CL-CMP-8368]. It does not say which official decides whether a revision is significant or whether circumstances are urgent and compelling. How the FAR and agency acquisition regulations fit together is on [The FAR, the DFARS, the overhaul and class deviations](cmp-acquisition-regulations.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An agency proposes a revision that adds a new consent requirement for subcontracts above 5,000,000, which has a significant cost impact on contractors. It is a significant revision, so the opportunity to comment is provided by a Federal Register notice with the proposal text and the contact address and telephone number, and the notice gives 60 days for comments [@CL-CMP-8363; @CL-CMP-8366; @CL-CMP-8367; @CL-CMP-8674; @CL-CMP-8368; @CL-CMP-8369]. A second change only corrects a cross-reference and has no impact on the basic meaning of the coverage, so it is editorial and comments need not be solicited [@CL-CMP-8364; @CL-CMP-8370]. A third change implements a new statute in a short time, and urgent and compelling circumstances make advance comment impracticable. It is issued on a temporary basis with at least 30 days for public comment, and its notice states that the revision is effective on a temporary basis pending completion of the comment period [@CL-CMP-8371; @CL-CMP-8372; @CL-CMP-8373].

## Never confuse

A significant revision is not any change to the FAR. The test is whether the change alters substantive meaning and has the stated cost, administrative or wider effect [@CL-CMP-8363]. A rule issued on a temporary basis for urgent circumstances is not free of comment either, because it must still provide a comment period [@CL-CMP-8372].
