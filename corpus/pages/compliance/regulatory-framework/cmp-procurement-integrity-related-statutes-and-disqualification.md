## Purpose

Shows the other statutes and rules that surround the procurement integrity prohibitions, how a conflicted official is disqualified and reinstated, and when an ethics advisory opinion protects a former official and a contractor.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-6846] |
| Codified citation | FAR 3.104-2, 3.104-5 and 3.104-6 [@CL-CMP-6846; @CL-CMP-6848; @CL-CMP-6852] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-6846] |
| Applies to | Agency officials who must disqualify themselves after an employment contact, and officials and former officials who ask for an ethics advisory opinion [@CL-CMP-6848; @CL-CMP-7133] |

Agency supplementation of the procurement integrity section, including specific definitions to identify individuals in certain positions and any clauses the section requires, must be approved by the senior procurement executive of the agency, unless a law establishes a higher level of approval for that agency [@CL-CMP-6846; @CL-CMP-7282].

The section reminds agency officials that other statutes and regulations deal with the same or related conduct. The offer or acceptance of a bribe or gratuity is prohibited by other statutes, and accepting a gift is prohibited in certain circumstances [@CL-CMP-7111]. Contacts with an offeror during an acquisition may constitute seeking employment [@CL-CMP-7114]. Officials are prohibited from participating personally and substantially in a particular matter that would affect the financial interests of a person with whom they are seeking employment [@CL-CMP-6847], and an employee who negotiates or has an arrangement for future employment with an offeror must comply with the applicable ethics disqualification requirements [@CL-CMP-7112]. The conflict statute may also require disqualification even if the employee's duties may not be considered participating personally and substantially [@CL-CMP-7281].

Post-employment restrictions prohibit certain activities by former Government employees [@CL-CMP-7115], including representation of a contractor before the Government in relation to a contract or other particular matter involving specific parties on which the former employee participated personally and substantially while employed by the Government [@CL-CMP-7116]. Additional restrictions apply to certain senior Government employees and for particular matters under an employee's official responsibility [@CL-CMP-7117]. Parts 14 and 15 of the FAR place restrictions on the release of procurement and contractor information that must be protected under a statute [@CL-CMP-7118]. Release of information before and after award may be prohibited by the Privacy Act, the Trade Secrets Act and other laws [@CL-CMP-7120], and using nonpublic information to further a private interest, or in a financial transaction, is prohibited [@CL-CMP-7121]. The prohibition on a former official accepting compensation from a contractor is set out in the statutory prohibitions section of the same subpart, which this card does not restate.

## How it works

### Disqualification and reinstatement

An agency official who must disqualify himself or herself under the employment-contact rule must, in addition to the contact report, promptly submit written notice of disqualification from further participation in the procurement [@CL-CMP-6848]. That rule covers an official participating personally and substantially in a procurement for a contract above the simplified acquisition threshold [@CL-CMP-7122] who contacts, or is contacted by, an offeror about possible non-Federal employment [@CL-CMP-7113]. The official must promptly report the contact in writing to the supervisor and the agency ethics official [@CL-CMP-7123], and must either reject the possibility of employment or disqualify until the agency authorizes resumption [@CL-CMP-7124]. Contacts conducted through agents or other intermediaries may also require disqualification, including under other statutes and regulations [@CL-CMP-7126].

The notice goes to the contracting officer, the source selection authority if other than the contracting officer, and the official's immediate supervisor [@CL-CMP-7127]. At a minimum it must identify the procurement, describe the official's participation with approximate dates, and identify the offeror and its interest [@CL-CMP-6849].

The official must remain disqualified until the agency, at its sole and exclusive discretion, authorizes resumption [@CL-CMP-7128]. The conditions for resumption are that the person is no longer an offeror, or that all employment discussions ended without an agreement or arrangement [@CL-CMP-7125]. After they are met, the head of the contracting activity, after consultation with the agency ethics official, may authorize the official to resume participation [@CL-CMP-6850], or may determine that an additional disqualification period is necessary to protect the integrity of the procurement process [@CL-CMP-7129]. In setting the period, the head must consider any factors that create an appearance that the official acted without complete impartiality [@CL-CMP-7130], and the reinstatement decision should be in writing [@CL-CMP-7131]. An official may not be reinstated into a matter affecting the financial interest of someone with whom the official is seeking employment unless the official receives a waiver under the conflict statute or an authorization under the ethics regulations [@CL-CMP-6851; @CL-CMP-7110], and must also comply with the conflict statute and the ethics regulations regarding any resumed participation [@CL-CMP-7280].

### Ethics advisory opinions

An official or former official who does not know whether the statute bars accepting compensation from a particular contractor may ask the appropriate agency ethics official for advice before accepting it [@CL-CMP-7133]. The request must be in writing, include all relevant information reasonably available to the requester, and be dated and signed [@CL-CMP-6852]. It must cover the procurements or decisions involving the contractor in which the individual was involved [@CL-CMP-7134], including contract or solicitation numbers, dates, a description of the supplies or services, and contract amount [@CL-CMP-7119]. It must also cover the individual's participation, including dates and the nature of the duties [@CL-CMP-7135], and the contractor, including the products or services of the division or affiliate that would pay the compensation [@CL-CMP-7136].

The ethics official should issue an opinion within 30 days after receiving a request with complete information, or as soon thereafter as practicable [@CL-CMP-6853]. If the request is incomplete, the ethics official may ask the requester, or other persons such as the source selection authority, the contracting officer or the requester's supervisor, for more information [@CL-CMP-7137]. The ethics official may rely on the information furnished unless he or she has reason to believe it is fraudulent, misleading, or otherwise incorrect [@CL-CMP-7138].

The protection applies where a written opinion advises the requester that compensation from a particular contractor may be accepted and the requester accepts it in good faith reliance on that opinion [@CL-CMP-6854]. In that case neither the requester nor the contractor will be found to have knowingly violated the statute [@CL-CMP-7132]. Reliance is not in good faith if the requester or the contractor has actual knowledge or reason to believe that the opinion rests on fraudulent, misleading, or otherwise incorrect information [@CL-CMP-7139].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A program manager participating personally and substantially in a competitive procurement above the simplified acquisition threshold is contacted by an offeror about a job [@CL-CMP-7122; @CL-CMP-7113]. The manager reports the contact in writing to the supervisor and the agency ethics official [@CL-CMP-7123] and disqualifies from the procurement, sending written notice to the contracting officer, the source selection authority and the supervisor [@CL-CMP-6848; @CL-CMP-7127]. The notice names the procurement, the manager's role and dates, and the offeror and its interest [@CL-CMP-6849]. When the offeror drops out of the competition, the head of the contracting activity consults the agency ethics official and may authorize the manager to resume [@CL-CMP-7125; @CL-CMP-6850], and the decision should be in writing [@CL-CMP-7131].

Separately, a former program official is offered consulting work by a contractor's division. Before accepting, the official writes to the agency ethics official with the contract number, award date, a description of the supplies, the contract amount, the dates and nature of the official's duties, and a description of the division's products [@CL-CMP-7134; @CL-CMP-7119; @CL-CMP-7135; @CL-CMP-7136]. The request is dated and signed [@CL-CMP-6852]. The ethics official answers in writing 14 days after the complete request, within the target period [@CL-CMP-6853], that the compensation may be accepted. The official accepts in good faith [@CL-CMP-6854], so neither the official nor the contractor is found to have knowingly violated the statute [@CL-CMP-7132], unless either had actual knowledge or reason to believe that the opinion rested on incorrect information [@CL-CMP-7139].

## Never confuse

A disqualification notice is not an ethics advisory opinion. The notice records that a serving official has stepped out of a procurement after an employment contact [@CL-CMP-6848]; the opinion answers whether an official or former official may accept compensation from a particular contractor [@CL-CMP-7133]. The prohibitions themselves and their marking rules are in [Procurement integrity prohibitions](../regulatory-framework/cmp-procurement-integrity-prohibitions.md) and [Procurement integrity marking and violations](../regulatory-framework/cmp-procurement-integrity-marking-and-violations.md).
