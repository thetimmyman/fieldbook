## Purpose

Shows how a provision or clause number is built, how supplemental texts are numbered, where the prescription sits, what the matrix lists and when it does not settle applicability, and that every provision, clause and alternate is dated.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-8839] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.101 [@CL-CMP-8839] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-8839] |
| Applies to | FAR provisions and clauses, and supplemental provisions and clauses published in agency acquisition regulations [@CL-CMP-8838; @CL-CMP-8841] |

Subpart 52.2 sets forth the texts of all FAR provisions and clauses, each in its own separate subsection, arranged by subject matter and keyed to the parts of the FAR [@CL-CMP-8838]. Each FAR provision or clause is uniquely identified [@CL-CMP-8880]. All FAR provision and clause numbers begin with 52.2, and the next two digits correspond to the number of the FAR subject part in which the provision or clause is prescribed [@CL-CMP-8839]. The number is completed by a hyphen and a sequential number assigned within each section of subpart 52.2 [@CL-CMP-8840].

Provisions or clauses that supplement the FAR are of three kinds. One kind is prescribed and included in authorized agency acquisition regulations issued within an agency to satisfy the specific needs of the agency as a whole [@CL-CMP-8881]. Another is prescribed and included in a regulation issued by a suborganization of an agency to satisfy the needs of that particular suborganization [@CL-CMP-8882]. The third is developed for use at a suborganizational level of an agency, not meant for repetitive use, but intended to meet the needs of an individual acquisition and impractical to include in either an agency or suborganization acquisition regulation [@CL-CMP-8883].

Supplemental provisions or clauses published in agency acquisition regulations shall be in full text, and the prescription for the use of each shall be included [@CL-CMP-8841]. They are numbered in the same manner as FAR provisions and clauses, except for two differences [@CL-CMP-8884]. If the agency acquisition regulation is published in the Federal Register and codified in 48 CFR, the number is preceded by the chapter number within 48 CFR assigned by the CFR staff [@CL-CMP-8842]. The sequential number shall be 70 or a higher number [@CL-CMP-8843]. Like the sequential number at the end of a FAR number, it indicates the subsection location of the text in subpart 52.2 of the agency acquisition regulation that contains its full text [@CL-CMP-8885].

## How it works

Each provision or clause in subpart 52.2 is prescribed at the place in the FAR text where its subject matter receives its primary treatment [@CL-CMP-8844]. The prescription includes all conditions, requirements and instructions for using the provision or clause and its alternates, if any [@CL-CMP-8887]. The provision or clause may also be referred to in other FAR locations [@CL-CMP-8888]. The introductory text of each provision or clause in subpart 52.2 includes a cross-reference to the location in the FAR subject text that prescribes its use [@CL-CMP-8889].

The matrix may be accessed via the internet [@CL-CMP-8890] and contains a column for each principal type and/or purpose of contract [@CL-CMP-8891]. It lists required, required-when-applicable and optional solicitation provisions, and required, required-when-applicable and optional contract clauses [@CL-CMP-8845]. For each provision or clause listed, it gives whether incorporation by reference is or is not authorized and the Uniform Contract Format section in which the text is to be located, if it is used in an acquisition subject to that format [@CL-CMP-8892]. It also gives the number, the citation of the FAR text that prescribes its use, and the title [@CL-CMP-8893].

Because the matrix does not provide enough information to determine the applicability of a provision or clause in the required-when-applicable and optional categories, contracting officers shall refer to the FAR text cited in the matrix that prescribes its use [@CL-CMP-8846]. The FAR matrix may be reproduced at agency levels, and at subordinate levels, to supplement it with agency-developed provisions and clauses [@CL-CMP-8894], and the resulting consolidated matrices may be included in agency acquisition regulations [@CL-CMP-8895].

All provisions, clauses and alternates are dated because they are subject to revision from time to time [@CL-CMP-8896]. To avoid questions about which version is operative in a given solicitation or contract, the date shall be included whether the text is incorporated by reference or in full text [@CL-CMP-8847]. The section does not say who inserts it.

The full-text and numbering rules apply to supplemental texts that an agency publishes in its acquisition regulations [@CL-CMP-8841; @CL-CMP-8884]. A text of the third kind is impractical to include in an agency or suborganization regulation [@CL-CMP-8883]. How it is identified is on the card for [identifying provisions and clauses](cmp-identifying-provisions-and-clauses.md). In this subpart, modification means a minor change in the details of a provision or clause that is specifically authorized by the FAR and does not alter the substance of the provision or clause [@CL-CMP-8864]. The procedure is on the card for [modifying and completing](cmp-modifying-and-completing-provisions-and-clauses.md).

The section names the contracting officer only in the rule on the matrix [@CL-CMP-8846], and names agencies in the permission to reproduce the matrix and in the rules on supplemental texts [@CL-CMP-8894; @CL-CMP-8841]. It sets no dollar threshold or deadline. How a FAR citation number is built is a different question, covered on the card for [FAR publication, arrangement and numbering](cmp-far-publication-arrangement-and-numbering.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A drafter sees a clause numbered 52.209-3. The prefix 52.2 shows that the clause text sits in subpart 52.2, the next two digits, 09, correspond to FAR part 9, the subject part in which the clause is prescribed, and 3 is the sequential number assigned within that section [@CL-CMP-8839; @CL-CMP-8840]. The drafter opens the FAR text that prescribes the clause, because the prescription holds the conditions, requirements and instructions for using the clause and its alternates [@CL-CMP-8844; @CL-CMP-8887].

The matrix lists the clause as required-when-applicable for the contract type. The matrix alone does not settle whether it belongs in this buy, so the contracting officer reads the prescribing FAR text that the matrix cites [@CL-CMP-8846]. Once the clause is used, the solicitation or contract shows its date whether it is incorporated by reference or in full text [@CL-CMP-8847].

An agency, whose regulation is published in the Federal Register and codified in 48 CFR as an invented chapter 98, issues a supplemental clause for its section 52.236. The clause is printed in full text with its prescription [@CL-CMP-8841], and its number is preceded by the chapter number [@CL-CMP-8842]. Following the FAR's own example, an agency regulation with only one provision followed by only one clause supplementing the FAR in that section numbers them 70 and 71, so the clause is 9852.236-71 [@CL-CMP-8886].

## Never confuse

For a FAR provision or clause, a prescription is not the text. The text sits in subpart 52.2 [@CL-CMP-8838], and the prescription sits in the FAR text where the subject matter receives its primary treatment [@CL-CMP-8844].

A matrix entry does not settle applicability in the required-when-applicable and optional categories. The contracting officer reads the prescribing FAR text [@CL-CMP-8846].

A supplemental clause number is not a FAR clause number. It is numbered in the same manner, but its sequential number is 70 or higher [@CL-CMP-8843], and it takes a chapter number prefix where the regulation is published in the Federal Register and codified in 48 CFR [@CL-CMP-8842].
