## Purpose

Explains the Buy American certificate in paragraph (f), the Buy American-Free Trade Agreements-Israeli Trade Act certificate and its alternates in paragraph (g)(1) to (g)(3), and the trade agreements certificate in paragraph (g)(4) of the commercial offeror representations provision, including what each offeror lists and how the Government evaluates and awards [@CL-CMP-8984; @CL-CMP-8987; @CL-CMP-8990].

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-8983] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.212-3(f) and (g) [@CL-CMP-8983; @CL-CMP-8987] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-8983] |
| Applies to | Solicitations that include FAR 52.225-1, FAR 52.225-3 or FAR 52.225-5, each for its own certificate [@CL-CMP-8983; @CL-CMP-8987; @CL-CMP-8990] |

Buy American certificate, paragraph (f). The certificate applies only if the clause at FAR 52.225-1, Buy American—Supplies, is included in the solicitation [@CL-CMP-8983]. Under the certificate, the offeror certifies that each end product, except those listed in paragraph (f)(2), is a domestic end product and that each domestic end product listed in paragraph (f)(3) contains a critical component [@CL-CMP-8984]. The offeror shall list as foreign end products those end products manufactured in the United States that do not qualify as domestic end products [@CL-CMP-8985]. For those foreign end products that do not consist wholly or predominantly of iron or steel or a combination of both, the offeror shall also indicate whether they exceed 55 percent domestic content, except for those that are commercially available off-the-shelf (COTS) items [@CL-CMP-8986]. If the percentage of the domestic content is unknown, the offeror selects no on that question [@CL-CMP-9149]. The offeror shall separately list the line item numbers of domestic end products that contain a critical component [@CL-CMP-9150]. The Government will evaluate offers in accordance with the policies and procedures of FAR part 25 [@CL-CMP-9151].

Buy American-Free Trade Agreements-Israeli Trade Act certificate, paragraph (g)(1): the certificate applies only if the clause at FAR 52.225-3 is included in the solicitation [@CL-CMP-8987]. The offeror certifies that each end product, except those listed in paragraph (g)(1)(ii) or (g)(1)(iii), is a domestic end product and that each domestic end product listed in paragraph (g)(1)(iv) contains a critical component [@CL-CMP-8988]. The offeror certifies that the supplies it lists in paragraph (g)(1)(ii) are Free Trade Agreement country end products other than Bahraini, Moroccan, Omani, Panamanian or Peruvian end products, or Israeli end products [@CL-CMP-8989]. The offeror shall list the supplies that are foreign end products, other than those listed in paragraph (g)(1)(ii), as defined in the clause [@CL-CMP-9152]. It shall list as other foreign end products those end products manufactured in the United States that do not qualify as domestic end products [@CL-CMP-9153]. For those other foreign end products that do not consist wholly or predominantly of iron or steel or a combination of both, it shall also indicate whether they exceed 55 percent domestic content, except for COTS items [@CL-CMP-9154]. If the percentage of the domestic content is unknown, it selects no [@CL-CMP-9149]. It shall list the line item numbers of domestic end products that contain a critical component [@CL-CMP-9155]. The Government will evaluate offers in accordance with the policies and procedures of FAR part 25 [@CL-CMP-9156].

Alternates, paragraphs (g)(2) and (g)(3): if Alternate II to the clause at FAR 52.225-3 is included in the solicitation, the offeror certifies in a substituted paragraph that the listed supplies are Israeli end products [@CL-CMP-9157]. If Alternate III to the clause at FAR 52.225-3 is included in the solicitation, the offeror uses substitute paragraphs (g)(1)(i)(B) and (g)(1)(ii) in place of those of the basic provision [@CL-CMP-9158]. In the Alternate III substitute paragraph, the offeror certifies that the listed supplies are Korean end products or Israeli end products [@CL-CMP-9159].

Trade agreements certificate, paragraph (g)(4): the certificate applies only if the clause at FAR 52.225-5, Trade Agreements, is included in the solicitation, and the offeror certifies that each end product, except those listed in paragraph (g)(4)(ii), is a United States-made or designated country end product [@CL-CMP-8990]. The offeror shall list as other end products those end products that are not United States-made or designated country end products [@CL-CMP-9160]. The Government will evaluate offers in accordance with FAR part 25 and, for line items covered by the WTO GPA, will evaluate offers of United States-made or designated country end products without regard to the restrictions of the Buy American statute [@CL-CMP-9161]. The Government will consider for award only offers of United States-made or designated country end products unless the Contracting Officer determines that there are no offers for such products or that the offers for such products are insufficient to fulfill the requirements of the solicitation [@CL-CMP-9162].

## How it works

Each certificate is switched on by its own clause. The paragraph (f) certificate depends on FAR 52.225-1, paragraph (g)(1) on FAR 52.225-3 and paragraph (g)(4) on FAR 52.225-5, and a solicitation can include any of them [@CL-CMP-8983; @CL-CMP-8987; @CL-CMP-8990].

Inside paragraph (f) and paragraph (g)(1) the pattern is the same [@CL-CMP-8984; @CL-CMP-8988]. The offeror certifies that its end products are domestic except those it lists, lists the foreign end products and the domestic end products with a critical component, and answers the 55 percent domestic content question for the listed foreign end products that are not wholly or predominantly iron or steel and are not COTS items [@CL-CMP-8984; @CL-CMP-8985; @CL-CMP-8986; @CL-CMP-9154; @CL-CMP-9150; @CL-CMP-9155]. An unknown percentage is answered no [@CL-CMP-9149]. Paragraph (g)(1) adds a list of Free Trade Agreement country end products and Israeli end products [@CL-CMP-8989].

Alternate II narrows the paragraph (g)(1)(ii) list to Israeli end products, and Alternate III replaces it with Korean end products or Israeli end products [@CL-CMP-9157; @CL-CMP-9159]. Paragraph (g)(4) uses a different test, United States-made or designated country end products, and it lists the end products that fail that test [@CL-CMP-8990; @CL-CMP-9160].

Under paragraph (g)(4), unless the Contracting Officer determines that there are no offers for United States-made or designated country end products or that those offers are insufficient to fulfill the requirements of the solicitation, the Government considers for award only offers of such products [@CL-CMP-9162]. Each certificate that applies states that the Government will evaluate offers in accordance with FAR part 25 [@CL-CMP-9151; @CL-CMP-9156; @CL-CMP-9161].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A solicitation includes FAR 52.225-3 without an alternate, so the offeror completes paragraph (g)(1) [@CL-CMP-8987]. It offers 4 line items. Line 1 is a domestic end product that contains a critical component, so the offeror lists line 1 among the domestic end products with a critical component [@CL-CMP-8988; @CL-CMP-9155]. Line 2 is a product from a Free Trade Agreement country that is not a Bahraini, Moroccan, Omani, Panamanian or Peruvian product, so the offeror lists it as a Free Trade Agreement country end product [@CL-CMP-8989]. Line 3 is manufactured in the United States but does not qualify as a domestic end product, so the offeror lists it as an other foreign end product [@CL-CMP-9153]. Line 3 is not wholly or predominantly iron or steel and is not a COTS item, and its domestic content is 45 percent, which does not exceed 55 percent, so the offeror answers no [@CL-CMP-9154]. If the offeror did not know the percentage, it would also answer no [@CL-CMP-9149]. Line 4 is a domestic end product with no critical component, so the offeror certifies it as domestic and lists it nowhere [@CL-CMP-8988].

If the solicitation also included Alternate II to FAR 52.225-3, the substituted paragraph would accept only Israeli end products in the list, so line 2 could not be listed there [@CL-CMP-9157].

A second solicitation includes FAR 52.225-5. The offeror offers 2 end products, one made in the United States and one from a designated country, so it certifies that each is a United States-made or designated country end product and lists no other end products [@CL-CMP-8990; @CL-CMP-9160]. The Government will consider for award only such offers, unless the Contracting Officer determines that there are no offers for such products or that those offers are insufficient to fulfill the requirements [@CL-CMP-9162].

## Never confuse

A domestic end product is not the same as a United States-made end product. The Buy American certificates use the domestic end product test, while the trade agreements certificate uses the United States-made or designated country test [@CL-CMP-8984; @CL-CMP-8988; @CL-CMP-8990].

An end product manufactured in the United States is not automatically domestic, because the offeror must list as foreign those end products manufactured in the United States that do not qualify as domestic end products [@CL-CMP-8985; @CL-CMP-9153].

Alternate II and Alternate III replace the paragraphs they name with substituted text, and the substituted lists name Israeli end products or Korean end products instead of Free Trade Agreement country end products [@CL-CMP-9157; @CL-CMP-9158; @CL-CMP-9159].
