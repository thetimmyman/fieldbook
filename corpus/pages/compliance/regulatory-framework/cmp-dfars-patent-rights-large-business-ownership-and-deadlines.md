## Purpose

Shows how the DoD clause for contractor-owned inventions of large businesses defines a subject invention and sets the deadlines to disclose it, elect title and file.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-CMP-3928] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 252.227-7038 [@CL-CMP-3928] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-3928] |
| Applies to | Subject inventions, which are inventions of the Contractor made in the performance of work under the contract [@CL-CMP-3929] |

The clause is titled Patent Rights—Ownership by the Contractor (Large Business) and is prescribed in DFARS 227.303(2) [@CL-CMP-3928]. A subject invention is any invention of the Contractor made in the performance of work under the contract, and the Contractor may elect to retain ownership of each subject invention throughout the world in accordance with the provisions of the clause [@CL-CMP-3929].

Under paragraph (c)(1)(i), the Contractor shall disclose, in writing, each subject invention to the Contracting Officer within 2 months after the inventor discloses it in writing to Contractor personnel responsible for patent matters [@CL-CMP-3987], or within 6 months after the Contractor first becomes aware that a subject invention has been made, whichever is earlier [@CL-CMP-3930]. The Contractor shall elect in writing whether or not to retain ownership of any subject invention by notifying the Contracting Officer at the time of disclosure or within 8 months of disclosure, as to those countries (including the United States) in which the Contractor will retain ownership [@CL-CMP-3931]. However, in any case where publication, on sale, or public use has initiated the 1-year statutory period during which valid patent protection can be obtained in the United States, the agency may shorten the period of election of title to a date that is no more than 60 days prior to the end of the statutory period [@CL-CMP-3932].

The Contractor shall file either a provisional or a nonprovisional patent application on an elected subject invention within 1 year after election [@CL-CMP-3933], provided that in all cases the application is filed prior to the end of any statutory period wherein valid patent protection can be obtained in the United States after a publication, on sale, or public use [@CL-CMP-3982]. The Contractor shall also file a nonprovisional application within 10 months of the filing of any provisional application [@CL-CMP-3933; @CL-CMP-3934], and file patent applications in additional countries or international patent offices within either 10 months of the first filed patent application (whether provisional or nonprovisional) or 6 months from the date the Commissioner of Patents grants permission to file foreign patent applications where such filing has been prohibited by a Secrecy Order [@CL-CMP-3933; @CL-CMP-0435; @CL-CMP-0436].

The Contractor may request extensions of time for disclosure, election, or filing under paragraphs (c)(1), (2), and (3) of the clause, and the Contracting Officer will normally grant the extension unless there is reason to believe the extension would prejudice the Government's interests [@CL-CMP-3983].

## How it works

Disclosure is due within the earlier of the two periods [@CL-CMP-3987; @CL-CMP-3930], election at the time of disclosure or within 8 months of disclosure [@CL-CMP-3931], and filing of the first application within 1 year after election, provided that in all cases the application is filed prior to the end of any statutory period wherein valid patent protection can be obtained in the United States after a publication, on sale, or public use [@CL-CMP-3933; @CL-CMP-3982]. Where publication, on sale, or public use has initiated the 1-year statutory period during which valid patent protection can be obtained in the United States, the agency may shorten the period of election of title to a date that is no more than 60 days prior to the end of the statutory period [@CL-CMP-3932]. The Contractor also files a nonprovisional application within 10 months of the filing of any provisional application and files patent applications in additional countries or international patent offices within either 10 months of the first filed patent application (whether provisional or nonprovisional) or 6 months from the date the Commissioner of Patents grants permission to file foreign patent applications where such filing has been prohibited by a Secrecy Order [@CL-CMP-3933; @CL-CMP-3934; @CL-CMP-0435; @CL-CMP-0436]. For the agency's right to request assignment of title, including where the Contractor elects not to retain title or fails to disclose or elect within the times specified in paragraph (c) and the agency requests title within 60 days after learning of the failure to report or elect within the specified times [@CL-CMP-3936; @CL-CMP-3937], see [DoD patent rights for large businesses: Government license, march-in, reports and subcontracts](cmp-dfars-patent-rights-large-business-license-and-reporting.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor reports a new alloy to its patent team. It discloses the invention to the Contracting Officer within the earlier of the two disclosure periods, elects to keep title in the United States, and files a provisional application within a year of election. It then files a nonprovisional application inside the 10-month window that follows the provisional filing. Had it failed to disclose or elect in time, the agency could have asked for title.

## Never confuse

This clause is the DoD Patent Rights—Ownership by the Contractor (Large Business) clause [@CL-CMP-3928]. For the FAR contractor-ownership clause, see [the ownership and licenses card](cmp-patent-rights-contractor-ownership-definitions-and-licenses.md) and [the deadlines and title card](cmp-patent-rights-contractor-ownership-deadlines-and-title.md); under it the Contractor elects in writing whether or not to retain ownership of any subject invention by notifying the Contracting Officer within 2 years of disclosure to the agency, subject to the agency's authority to shorten the period to no more than 60 days prior to the end of the 1-year United States statutory period initiated by publication, on sale, or public use [@CL-CMP-3633; @CL-CMP-3634]. Under this DoD clause the Contractor elects in writing whether or not to retain ownership of any subject invention at the time of disclosure or within 8 months of disclosure, as to those countries (including the United States) in which the Contractor will retain ownership, subject to the same shortening authority [@CL-CMP-3931; @CL-CMP-3932]. For the clause where the Government takes title, see [Patent rights with ownership by the Government](cmp-patent-rights-government-ownership.md).
