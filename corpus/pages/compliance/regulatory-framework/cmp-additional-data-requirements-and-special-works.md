## Purpose

Shows how long the Government may order additional data, who pays for it, and what rights the Government and the contractor have in data under the special works clause.

## The rule

| Codified citation | FAR 52.227-16 and FAR 52.227-17 [@CL-CMP-3705; @CL-CMP-3708] |

The Contracting Officer may, at any time during contract performance or within a period of 3 years after acceptance of all items to be delivered under the contract, order any data first produced or specifically used in the performance of the contract [@CL-CMP-3705]. Nothing in the Additional Data Requirements clause requires the Contractor to deliver data whose withholding is authorized by the Rights in Data—General or other equivalent clause, or data specifically identified in the contract as not subject to the clause [@CL-CMP-3706]. When data are to be delivered under that clause, the Contractor will be compensated for converting the data into the prescribed form, for reproduction, and for delivery [@CL-CMP-3707].

Under the Special Works clause, and except as provided in its copyright paragraph, the Government shall have unlimited rights in all data delivered under the contract and in all data first produced in the performance of the contract [@CL-CMP-3708]. The Contractor shall not assert or authorize others to assert any claim to copyright subsisting in any data first produced in the performance of the contract without the Contracting Officer's prior written permission [@CL-CMP-3709]. Except as otherwise specifically provided in the contract, the Contractor shall not use, release, reproduce, distribute, or publish that data, or authorize others to do so, without the Contracting Officer's written permission [@CL-CMP-3710].

## How it works

The two clauses do different jobs. The Additional Data Requirements clause lets the Contracting Officer order more data than the contract lists, within a time window, and pays the contractor for the conversion, reproduction and delivery work [@CL-CMP-3705; @CL-CMP-3707]. The Special Works clause makes the contractor get written permission both to claim copyright and to use or release the data it first produced [@CL-CMP-3709; @CL-CMP-3710]. If the Government desires to obtain copyright in data first produced in the performance of the contract and permission has not been granted under the copyright paragraph, the Contracting Officer shall direct the Contractor to assign the copyright, with or without registration [@CL-CMP-3752]. This card does not set out the indemnity paragraph of the Special Works clause, its rules on copyright notices, licenses and data not first produced in the contract, or the Contracting Officer's power to release the Contractor from the Additional Data Requirements clause.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contract ends and all items are accepted. Two years later the Contracting Officer orders a set of drawings first produced in the work. The order is inside the window, so the contractor must deliver the drawings and is paid for putting them in the prescribed form, reproducing them and delivering them. The contractor may still withhold any data that the Rights in Data—General clause lets it withhold.

## Never confuse

Additional data under the ordering clause is still governed by the [Rights in Data—General clause](cmp-rights-in-data-general-definitions-and-allocation.md) or an equivalent, so ordering data does not erase the right to withhold it [@CL-CMP-3706]. The withholding rules are on [Rights in data, general: copyright, withholding and markings](cmp-rights-in-data-general-copyright-withholding-and-markings.md). Special works data is the opposite case, where the Government has unlimited rights in all delivered data and all first-produced data [@CL-CMP-3708].
