## Purpose

Shows the rights categories the Government takes in computer software and software documentation that are not commercial, including restricted rights, and how this clause differs from the technical data clause.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-CMP-3775] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 252.227-7014 [@CL-CMP-3775] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-3775] |
| Applies to | Other than commercial computer software and other than commercial computer software documentation, subject to an exception [@CL-CMP-3775] |

The clause governs all other than commercial computer software or other than commercial computer software documentation, subject to an exception that names the clause at DFARS 252.227-7018 [@CL-CMP-3775]. The clause's definition of computer software does not include computer databases or computer software documentation [@CL-CMP-3776]. Computer software documentation means owner's manuals, user's manuals, installation instructions, operating instructions, and other similar items, regardless of storage medium, that explain the capabilities of the computer software or provide instructions for using the software [@CL-CMP-3829]. Restricted rights apply only to other than commercial computer software and mean the listed rights of the Government [@CL-CMP-3777]. One listed restricted right is to use a computer program with one computer at one time, and the program may not be accessed by more than one terminal or central processing unit or time shared unless the contract otherwise permits [@CL-CMP-3777; @CL-CMP-3778]. This card does not set out the other listed restricted rights.

Two listed categories in which the Government shall have unlimited rights are computer software developed exclusively with Government funds and computer software documentation required to be delivered under this contract [@CL-CMP-3779]. Except as the unlimited rights paragraph provides, the Government shall have government purpose rights in computer software developed with mixed funding [@CL-CMP-3780]. Government purpose rights shall remain in effect for a period of five years unless a different period has been negotiated, and upon expiration the Government shall have unlimited rights in the software or documentation [@CL-CMP-3781]. The Government shall have restricted rights in other than commercial computer software required to be delivered or otherwise provided under this contract that were developed exclusively at private expense [@CL-CMP-3782].

## How it works

The rights category follows who paid for development, as it does in the technical data clause [@CL-CMP-3769; @CL-CMP-3779]. Software developed exclusively with Government funds falls under unlimited rights, software developed with mixed funding under government purpose rights for a set period, and software developed exclusively at private expense under restricted rights [@CL-CMP-3779; @CL-CMP-3780; @CL-CMP-3781; @CL-CMP-3782]. Documentation required to be delivered under the contract is itself listed under unlimited rights in this clause [@CL-CMP-3779].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor delivers a simulation program and its user's manual. The program was written at the contractor's own expense and cost 200. The Government has restricted rights in the program, and unlimited rights in the manual if the contract requires the manual to be delivered. If the same program had been developed half at the contractor's expense and half under a contract, the Government would have government purpose rights for five years and then unlimited rights.

## Never confuse

Restricted rights belong to software, and limited rights belong to technical data. This clause names restricted rights for software developed exclusively at private expense [@CL-CMP-3782], while the technical data clause names limited rights for data developed exclusively at private expense and marked with the limited rights legend [@CL-CMP-3772]. The technical data grant leaves out computer software documentation [@CL-CMP-3768], while this clause lists documentation required to be delivered under unlimited rights [@CL-CMP-3779]. Commercial software has a different license under the FAR; see [Commercial computer software license clause](cmp-commercial-computer-software-license.md). Technical data have their own clause; see [DoD rights in technical data: definitions and rights categories](cmp-dod-technical-data-rights-categories.md). Restrictions asserted on software are validated under a separate clause; see [DoD validation of asserted restrictions on computer software](cmp-dod-software-restriction-validation.md), and the offer-time list is on the card for [identification and assertion of restrictions](cmp-dod-assertion-of-data-restrictions.md).
