## Purpose

Shows how the DoD clause for SBIR and STTR contracts defines SBIR/STTR data, what rights the Government holds in that data during the protection period, when the period ends and what follows, and how the clause treats markings, the contractor's records and the Government's release of data.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-CMP-3914] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 252.227-7018 [@CL-CMP-3914] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-3914] |
| Applies to | All SBIR/STTR data [@CL-CMP-3917] |

The clause defines SBIR/STTR data as all technical data or computer software developed or generated in the performance of a phase I, II, or III SBIR/STTR contract or subcontract [@CL-CMP-3914]. The clause states that it governs all SBIR/STTR data [@CL-CMP-3917].

The protection period begins on the date of award of the contract under which the data are developed or generated and ends 20 years after that date, unless the agency and the Contractor negotiate some other protection period after the award [@CL-CMP-3915]. During the protection period, the Government's SBIR/STTR data rights are limited rights in the covered technical data and restricted rights in the covered computer software [@CL-CMP-3916]. Except for technical data, including computer software documentation, or computer software in which the Government has unlimited rights under paragraph (c)(1) of the clause, the Government shall have SBIR/STTR data rights during the SBIR/STTR data protection period of the contract, in all SBIR/STTR data developed or generated under the contract [@CL-CMP-3964; @CL-CMP-7387].

For the SBIR/STTR data described in paragraph (c)(2)(i)(B) of the clause, the Government shall have government purpose rights perpetually, and that period commences upon the expiration of the SBIR/STTR data protection period [@CL-CMP-3965]. The protection period is not extended by any subsequent SBIR/STTR contracts under which any portion of that data is used or delivered [@CL-CMP-3966].

## How it works

### Limited and restricted rights

Limited rights let the Government use, modify, reproduce, release, perform, display or disclose technical data within the Government [@CL-CMP-3918]. Subject to listed exceptions, limited rights bar the Government from releasing or disclosing the technical data outside the Government, using it for manufacture, or authorizing another party to use it, without the written permission of the party asserting the rights [@CL-CMP-3919]. One of the conditions on which the definition lets the Government reproduce, release or disclose the technical data, or authorize its use or reproduction by persons outside the Government, is that the use is necessary for emergency repair and overhaul or is a release or disclosure to listed recipients [@CL-CMP-3977]. The definition lists, as further items, that the recipient is subject to a prohibition on further reproduction, release, disclosure or use, and that the party asserting the restriction is notified [@CL-CMP-3920]. Restricted rights apply only to other than commercial computer software, and the first listed right is to use a computer program with one computer at one time [@CL-CMP-3921].

### Markings and procedures

Restrictions on the Government's rights in data to be delivered under the contract may be asserted only by marking the deliverable data or software subject to restriction [@CL-CMP-3967]. In the paragraph on SBIR/STTR data rights markings, technical data or computer software generated under the contract shall be marked as shown, and the Contractor shall enter the expiration date for the protection period on the legend [@CL-CMP-3974]. That marking requirement excepts data in which the Government has acquired unlimited rights under paragraph (c)(1) or negotiated special license rights as provided in paragraph (c)(6) [@CL-CMP-3973].

Data delivered without restrictive markings shall be presumed to have been delivered with unlimited rights [@CL-CMP-3968]. A request to place restrictive markings on unmarked data must be received by the Contracting Officer within 6 months following furnishing or delivery, or any extension of that time approved by the Contracting Officer [@CL-CMP-3969]. If the Contracting Officer notifies the Contractor of a nonconforming marking and the Contractor fails to remove or correct it within 60 days, the Government may ignore or, at the Contractor's expense, remove or correct it [@CL-CMP-3976].

Throughout performance, the Contractor, and its subcontractors or suppliers that will deliver data with other than unlimited rights, shall do what the paragraph on contractor procedures and records lists [@CL-CMP-3970]. The first listed duty is to have, maintain and follow written procedures sufficient to assure that restrictive markings are used only when authorized [@CL-CMP-3971]. The second is to maintain records sufficient to justify the validity of any restrictive markings on delivered data [@CL-CMP-3972].

### Release of marked data

The Government shall not release or disclose data in which it has government purpose rights unless listed conditions are met, one of which is that, before release, the intended recipient is subject to the nondisclosure agreement at DFARS 227.7103-7 [@CL-CMP-3975]. The duties of a contractor that receives Government-furnished information with restrictive legends are on [Using Government-furnished information marked with restrictive legends](cmp-dfars-government-furnished-information-restrictive-legends.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A company receives a phase II SBIR contract and the award date is the start of the protection period. No other period is negotiated after award, so the period runs for the full 20 years from the award date. The company marks its deliverables with the SBIR/STTR data rights legend and enters the date the period ends. During those years the Government may use the technical data inside the Government, and it needs the company's written permission to release the data outside the Government for manufacture, except in a listed exception. A later SBIR contract that reuses some of the data does not extend the first period for that data.

## Never confuse

The protection period here runs from the date of award of the contract under which the data are developed or generated [@CL-CMP-3915]. The FAR clause for SBIR data uses a period of 4 years after acceptance of all items to be delivered, unless extended [@CL-CMP-3717]. That clause is covered on [Rights in data under the SBIR and STTR program](cmp-rights-in-data-sbir-and-sttr-program.md), so compare the two before applying either. Limited rights are also not unlimited rights, because they keep the Government's use inside the Government unless an exception or the owner's permission applies [@CL-CMP-3918; @CL-CMP-3919]. General definitions are on [Rights in data, general: definitions and allocation](cmp-rights-in-data-general-definitions-and-allocation.md). The additional agreement requirements for STTR awards are a separate topic, covered on [DoD STTR agreement requirements before and after award](cmp-dfars-sttr-preaward-and-postaward-requirements.md).
