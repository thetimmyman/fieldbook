## Purpose

Shows how the DoD clause for SBIR and STTR contracts defines SBIR/STTR data, what rights the Government holds in that data during the protection period, when the period ends and what follows, and how the clause treats markings, the contractor's records and the Government's release of data.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-CMP-3914] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 252.227-7018 [@CL-CMP-3914] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-3914] |
| Applies to | All SBIR/STTR data [@CL-CMP-3917] |

The clause defines SBIR/STTR data as all technical data or computer software developed or generated in the performance of a phase I, II, or III SBIR/STTR contract or subcontract [@CL-CMP-3914]. The clause states that it governs all SBIR/STTR data [@CL-CMP-3917].

The protection period begins on the date of award of the contract under which the SBIR/STTR data are developed or generated and ends 20 years after that date, unless, after the award, the agency and the Contractor negotiate for some other protection period for the SBIR/STTR data developed or generated under that contract [@CL-CMP-3915; @CL-CMP-0329]. During the protection period, the Government's SBIR/STTR data rights in SBIR/STTR data covered by paragraph (c)(5) of the clause are limited rights in such SBIR/STTR technical data and restricted rights in such SBIR/STTR computer software [@CL-CMP-3916]. Except for technical data, including computer software documentation, or computer software in which the Government has unlimited rights under paragraph (c)(1) of the clause, the Government shall have SBIR/STTR data rights during the SBIR/STTR data protection period of the contract, in all SBIR/STTR data developed or generated under the contract [@CL-CMP-3964; @CL-CMP-7387].

For the SBIR/STTR data described in paragraph (c)(2)(i)(B) of the clause, the Government shall have government purpose rights perpetually, and that period commences upon the expiration of the SBIR/STTR data protection period [@CL-CMP-3965]. The protection period is not extended by any subsequent SBIR/STTR contracts under which any portion of that data is used or delivered [@CL-CMP-3966].

## How it works

### Limited and restricted rights

Limited rights let the Government use, modify, reproduce, release, perform, display or disclose technical data, in whole or in part, within the Government [@CL-CMP-3918]. Without the written permission of the party asserting limited rights, the Government may not release or disclose the technical data outside the Government, use it for manufacture, or authorize it to be used by another party, except that the Government may reproduce, release or disclose such data or authorize the use or reproduction of the data by persons outside the Government if all three conditions in the definition are met [@CL-CMP-3919; @CL-CMP-3977; @CL-CMP-3920]. Under those three conditions, the reproduction, release, disclosure or use must be either necessary for emergency repair and overhaul or a release or disclosure to one of two alternatives—a covered Government support contractor in performance of its covered Government support contracts for use, modification, reproduction, performance, display, or release or disclosure to a person authorized to receive limited rights technical data, or a foreign government, of technical data other than detailed manufacturing or process data, when use of such data by the foreign government is in the interest of the Government and is required for evaluational or informational purposes [@CL-CMP-3977; @CL-CMP-0330; @CL-CMP-0331]—and the recipient of the technical data must be subject to a prohibition on the further reproduction, release, disclosure or use of the technical data, and the contractor or subcontractor asserting the restriction must be notified of such reproduction, release, disclosure or use [@CL-CMP-3920]. Restricted rights apply only to other than commercial computer software, and the first right in the definition is to use a computer program with one computer at one time; the program may not be accessed by more than one terminal or central processing unit or time shared unless otherwise permitted by the contract [@CL-CMP-3921; @CL-CMP-0332].

### Markings and procedures

The Contractor, and its subcontractors or suppliers, may only assert restrictions on the Government's rights to use, modify, reproduce, release, perform, display or disclose technical data or computer software to be delivered under the contract by marking the deliverable data or software subject to restriction [@CL-CMP-0333; @CL-CMP-3967]. In the paragraph on SBIR/STTR data rights markings, technical data or computer software generated under the contract shall be marked as shown, and the Contractor shall enter the expiration date for the protection period on the legend [@CL-CMP-3974]. That marking requirement excepts data in which the Government has acquired unlimited rights under paragraph (c)(1) or negotiated special license rights as provided in paragraph (c)(6) [@CL-CMP-3973].

Technical data, computer software or computer software documentation delivered or otherwise provided under the contract without restrictive markings shall be presumed to have been delivered with unlimited rights [@CL-CMP-3968]. The Contractor may request permission to have conforming and justified restrictive markings placed on unmarked technical data, computer software or computer software documentation at its expense, and the request must be received by the Contracting Officer within 6 months following the furnishing or delivery of such technical data, software or documentation, or any extension of that time approved by the Contracting Officer [@CL-CMP-0334; @CL-CMP-3969]. If the Contracting Officer notifies the Contractor of a nonconforming marking or markings and the Contractor fails to remove or correct such markings within 60 days, the Government may ignore or, at the Contractor's expense, remove or correct any nonconforming markings [@CL-CMP-3976].

Throughout performance of the contract, the Contractor, and its subcontractors or suppliers that will deliver technical data or computer software with other than unlimited rights, shall have, maintain and follow written procedures sufficient to assure that restrictive markings are used only when authorized by the terms of the clause, and shall maintain records sufficient to justify the validity of any restrictive markings on technical data or computer software delivered under the contract [@CL-CMP-3970; @CL-CMP-3971; @CL-CMP-3972].

### Release of marked data

The Government shall not release or disclose data in which it has government purpose rights unless one of two alternatives is met: before release or disclosure, the intended recipient is subject to the nondisclosure agreement at DFARS 227.7103-7, or the recipient is a Government contractor receiving access to the data for performance of a Government contract that contains the clause at DFARS 252.227-7025, Limitations on the Use or Disclosure of Government-Furnished Information Marked with Restrictive Legends [@CL-CMP-3975; @CL-CMP-0335]. The duties of a contractor that receives Government-furnished information with restrictive legends are on [Using Government-furnished information marked with restrictive legends](cmp-dfars-government-furnished-information-restrictive-legends.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A company receives a phase II SBIR contract and the award date is the start of the protection period. No other period is negotiated after award, so the period runs for the full 20 years from the award date. The company marks its deliverables with the SBIR/STTR data rights legend and enters the date the period ends. During those years the Government may use the technical data inside the Government, and it needs the company's written permission to release the data outside the Government for manufacture, except in a listed exception. A later SBIR contract that reuses some of the data does not extend the first period for that data.

## Never confuse

The protection period here begins on the date of award of the contract under which the SBIR/STTR data are developed or generated and ends 20 years after that date, unless, after the award, the agency and the Contractor negotiate for some other protection period for the SBIR/STTR data developed or generated under that contract [@CL-CMP-3915; @CL-CMP-0329]. Under the FAR clause for SBIR data, for a period of 4 years, unless extended in accordance with FAR 27.409(h), after acceptance of all items to be delivered under the contract, the Government will use the data for Government purposes only, and they shall not be disclosed outside the Government (including disclosure for procurement purposes) during such period without permission of the Contractor, except that, subject to the foregoing use and disclosure prohibitions, the data may be disclosed for use by support Contractors [@CL-CMP-3717; @CL-CMP-3718]. That clause is covered on [Rights in data under the SBIR and STTR program](cmp-rights-in-data-sbir-and-sttr-program.md). Limited rights let the Government use, modify, reproduce, release, perform, display or disclose technical data, in whole or in part, within the Government, and bar release or disclosure outside the Government, use for manufacture, or authorization for another party to use the data without the written permission of the party asserting limited rights, except under the conditions in the definition [@CL-CMP-3918; @CL-CMP-3919]. General definitions are on [Rights in data, general: definitions and allocation](cmp-rights-in-data-general-definitions-and-allocation.md). The additional agreement requirements for STTR awards are covered on [DoD STTR agreement requirements before and after award](cmp-dfars-sttr-preaward-and-postaward-requirements.md).
