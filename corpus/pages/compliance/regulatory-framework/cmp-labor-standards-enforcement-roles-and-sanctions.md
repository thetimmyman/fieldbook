## Purpose

Sets out who administers and enforces the equal employment opportunity requirements, how a question about applicability is routed, and how compliance evaluations, complaint investigations and sanctions work under the clause for notification of employee rights. These are two different sections: one covers the equal employment opportunity order, and the other covers the notification of employee rights clause.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-6804] |
| Codified citation | FAR 22.803 and FAR 22.1604 [@CL-CMP-6804; @CL-CMP-6807] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-6804] |
| Applies to | Contractors and subcontractors under the equal employment opportunity order, and under the clause at FAR 52.222-40 for compliance evaluations and sanctions [@CL-CMP-6804; @CL-CMP-6807] |

Under FAR 22.803, the Secretary of Labor is responsible for administration and enforcement of prescribed parts of Executive Order 11246 and for adoption of rules and regulations and the issuance of orders necessary to achieve its purposes [@CL-CMP-6804]. The Secretary has delegated authority and assigned responsibility for carrying out those responsibilities to the Deputy Assistant Secretary, except for the issuance of rules and regulations of a general nature [@CL-CMP-6983]. The Deputy Assistant Secretary is the Deputy Assistant Secretary for Federal Contract Compliance, Department of Labor, or a designee [@CL-CMP-6984]. The head of each agency is responsible for ensuring that the requirements of the equal employment opportunity subpart are carried out within the agency and for cooperating with and assisting the OFCCP in fulfilling its responsibilities [@CL-CMP-6805]. If the applicability of Executive Order 11246 and the implementing regulations is questioned, the contracting officer must forward the matter to the Deputy Assistant Secretary, through agency channels, for resolution [@CL-CMP-6806].

The notification of employee rights subpart implements Executive Order 13496, and in it Secretary means the Secretary of Labor [@CL-CMP-6985; @CL-CMP-6986]. The Secretary may conduct compliance evaluations or investigate complaints of any contractor or subcontractor to determine if any of the requirements of the clause at FAR 52.222-40 have been violated [@CL-CMP-6807]. Contracting departments and agencies must cooperate with the Secretary and provide such information and assistance as the Secretary may require [@CL-CMP-6808]. If the Secretary determines that there has been a violation, the Secretary may take the actions set forth in 29 CFR 471.14 [@CL-CMP-6809]. Procedures for enforcement by the Secretary are set out in 29 CFR 471.10 through 29 CFR 471.16 [@CL-CMP-6991].

The Secretary may not terminate or suspend a contract or suspend or debar a contractor if the agency head has provided written objections that include a statement of reasons and a finding that the contractor's performance is essential to the agency's mission [@CL-CMP-6810]. The bar applies where the agency head continues to object to the imposition of such sanctions and penalties [@CL-CMP-6990].

## How it works

Under FAR 22.803 the Secretary of Labor administers and enforces the prescribed parts of the order and adopts rules and regulations, and the Deputy Assistant Secretary carries out the other responsibilities [@CL-CMP-6804; @CL-CMP-6983]. The agency head makes sure the agency carries out the subpart and assists the OFCCP, and a compliance evaluation is any one or combination of actions that the OFCCP may take to examine a Federal contractor's compliance with one or more of the order's requirements [@CL-CMP-6805; @CL-CMP-3051]. The contracting officer does not settle whether the order applies. The contracting officer forwards the question through agency channels to the Deputy Assistant Secretary [@CL-CMP-6806].

Under FAR 22.1604 the Secretary may conduct compliance evaluations or investigate complaints on the clause at FAR 52.222-40, and agencies must supply the information and assistance the Secretary requires [@CL-CMP-6807; @CL-CMP-6808]. The clause applies in all solicitations and contracts, including commercial products, commercial services and commercially available off-the-shelf items, except those the prescription excludes [@CL-CMP-6987]. The exclusions include acquisitions under the simplified acquisition threshold and, for indefinite-quantity contracts, those where the value of orders in any calendar year is not expected to exceed that threshold [@CL-CMP-6988]. They also include work performed exclusively outside the United States and acquisitions covered in their entirety by an exemption the Secretary grants [@CL-CMP-7002].

The agency head can stop the heaviest sanctions. A written objection that gives reasons and finds the contractor's performance essential to the agency's mission, kept up while the Secretary acts, bars termination, suspension and debarment [@CL-CMP-6810; @CL-CMP-6990]. The Secretary may still determine a violation and take the other actions in the cited Department of Labor regulation [@CL-CMP-6809]. The printed sections do not establish whether the underlying executive orders are in effect today, so this card states the mechanism only.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contracting officer for a supply contract is unsure whether the equal employment opportunity order applies to the contract. The contracting officer does not decide it. The contracting officer forwards the question to the Deputy Assistant Secretary through agency channels [@CL-CMP-6806].

On another contract, a services contract to be performed in the United States is expected to exceed the simplified acquisition threshold and is not covered in its entirety by an exemption the Secretary grants, so it carries the clause at FAR 52.222-40 [@CL-CMP-6987; @CL-CMP-6988; @CL-CMP-7002]. The Secretary investigates a complaint about the contractor and determines that there has been a violation. The Secretary may take the actions set forth in the cited regulation [@CL-CMP-6807; @CL-CMP-6809].

The Secretary proposes to terminate the contract. The agency head gives written objections stating the reasons and finding that the contractor's performance is essential to the agency's mission, and continues to object. The Secretary may not terminate or suspend the contract and may not suspend or debar the contractor [@CL-CMP-6810; @CL-CMP-6990].

## Never confuse

An agency head's objection is not a waiver of the requirement. It bars termination, suspension and debarment only while the agency head continues to object, and the Secretary may still evaluate, investigate and take the other actions in the cited regulation [@CL-CMP-6990; @CL-CMP-6807; @CL-CMP-6809].

The clause at FAR 52.222-40 belongs to the subpart that implements Executive Order 13496 [@CL-CMP-6985]. Its subpart implements a different executive order from Executive Order 11246, which FAR 22.803 covers [@CL-CMP-6804].

A question about applicability goes to the Deputy Assistant Secretary through agency channels. The contracting officer does not resolve it alone [@CL-CMP-6806].
