## Purpose

Sets out who administers and enforces the equal employment opportunity requirements, how a question about applicability is routed, and how compliance evaluations, complaint investigations and sanctions work under the clause for notification of employee rights.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-6804] |
| Codified citation | FAR 22.803 and FAR 22.1604 [@CL-CMP-6804; @CL-CMP-6807] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-6804] |
| Applies to | Contractors and subcontractors under the equal employment opportunity order, and under the clause at FAR 52.222-40 for compliance evaluations and sanctions [@CL-CMP-6804; @CL-CMP-6807] |

Under FAR 22.803, the Secretary of Labor is responsible for administration and enforcement of prescribed parts of Executive Order 11246 and for adoption of rules and regulations and the issuance of orders necessary to achieve its purposes [@CL-CMP-6804]. The Secretary of Labor has delegated authority and assigned responsibility to the Deputy Assistant Secretary for carrying out the responsibilities assigned to the Secretary by Executive Order 11246, except for the issuance of rules and regulations of a general nature [@CL-CMP-6983]. The Deputy Assistant Secretary is the Deputy Assistant Secretary for Federal Contract Compliance, Department of Labor, or a designee [@CL-CMP-6984]. The head of each agency is responsible for ensuring that the requirements of the equal employment opportunity subpart are carried out within the agency and for cooperating with and assisting the OFCCP in fulfilling its responsibilities [@CL-CMP-6805]. If the applicability of Executive Order 11246 and the implementing regulations is questioned, the contracting officer must forward the matter to the Deputy Assistant Secretary, through agency channels, for resolution [@CL-CMP-6806].

The notification of employee rights subpart implements Executive Order 13496, and in it Secretary means the Secretary of Labor [@CL-CMP-6985; @CL-CMP-6986]. The Secretary may conduct compliance evaluations or investigate complaints of any contractor or subcontractor to determine if any of the requirements of the clause at FAR 52.222-40 have been violated [@CL-CMP-6807]. Contracting departments and agencies must cooperate with the Secretary and provide such information and assistance as the Secretary may require [@CL-CMP-6808]. If the Secretary determines that there has been a violation, the Secretary may take the actions set forth in 29 CFR 471.14 [@CL-CMP-6809]. Procedures for enforcement by the Secretary are set out in 29 CFR 471.10 through 29 CFR 471.16 [@CL-CMP-6991].

The Secretary may not terminate or suspend a contract or suspend or debar a contractor if the agency head has provided written objections, which must include a statement of reasons for the objection and a finding that the contractor's performance is essential to the agency's mission, and continues to object to the imposition of such sanctions and penalties [@CL-CMP-6810; @CL-CMP-6990].

## How it works

Under FAR 22.803 the Secretary of Labor administers and enforces the prescribed parts of the order and adopts rules and regulations, and the Deputy Assistant Secretary carries out the responsibilities assigned to the Secretary by the order, except the issuance of rules and regulations of a general nature [@CL-CMP-6804; @CL-CMP-6983]. The agency head makes sure the agency carries out the subpart and assists the OFCCP, and a compliance evaluation is any one or combination of actions that the OFCCP may take to examine a Federal contractor's compliance with one or more of the order's requirements [@CL-CMP-6805; @CL-CMP-3051]. If the applicability of Executive Order 11246 and implementing regulations is questioned, the contracting officer shall forward the matter to the Deputy Assistant Secretary, through agency channels, for resolution [@CL-CMP-6806].

Under FAR 22.1604 the Secretary may conduct compliance evaluations or investigate complaints on the clause at FAR 52.222-40, and agencies must supply the information and assistance the Secretary requires [@CL-CMP-6807; @CL-CMP-6808]. FAR 22.1605(a) says to insert the clause at FAR 52.222-40 in all solicitations and contracts, including acquisitions for commercial products, commercial services and commercially available off-the-shelf items, except acquisitions in the cases that follow [@CL-CMP-6987]. One exception is acquisitions under the simplified acquisition threshold; for indefinite-quantity contracts, the clause is included only if the value of orders in any calendar year of the contract is expected to exceed the simplified acquisition threshold [@CL-CMP-6988]. The other exceptions are acquisitions for work performed exclusively outside the United States and acquisitions covered in their entirety by an exemption granted by the Secretary [@CL-CMP-7002].

The Secretary may not terminate or suspend a contract or suspend or debar a contractor if the agency head has provided written objections, which must include a statement of reasons for the objection and a finding that the contractor's performance is essential to the agency's mission, and continues to object to the imposition of such sanctions and penalties [@CL-CMP-6810; @CL-CMP-6990]. If the Secretary determines that there has been a violation, the Secretary may take such actions as set forth in 29 CFR 471.14 [@CL-CMP-6809].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contracting officer for a supply contract is unsure whether the equal employment opportunity order applies to the contract. The contracting officer does not decide it. The contracting officer forwards the question to the Deputy Assistant Secretary through agency channels [@CL-CMP-6806].

On another contract, a services contract to be performed in the United States is expected to exceed the simplified acquisition threshold and is not covered in its entirety by an exemption the Secretary grants, so it carries the clause at FAR 52.222-40 [@CL-CMP-6987; @CL-CMP-6988; @CL-CMP-7002]. The Secretary investigates a complaint about the contractor and determines that there has been a violation. The Secretary may take the actions set forth in the cited regulation [@CL-CMP-6807; @CL-CMP-6809].

The Secretary proposes to terminate the contract. The agency head gives written objections stating the reasons and finding that the contractor's performance is essential to the agency's mission, and continues to object. The Secretary may not terminate or suspend the contract and may not suspend or debar the contractor [@CL-CMP-6810; @CL-CMP-6990].

## Never confuse

The E.O. 11246 enforcement differs from the E.O. 13496 employee-rights subpart: the Secretary may conduct compliance evaluations or investigate complaints to determine if any of the requirements of the clause at FAR 52.222-40 have been violated [@CL-CMP-6807]; the Secretary's bar on termination, suspension and debarment applies where the agency head has provided written objections that include a statement of reasons and a finding that the contractor's performance is essential to the agency's mission, and continues to object [@CL-CMP-6810; @CL-CMP-6990]. FAR 22.1600 prescribes policies and procedures to implement Executive Order 13496 [@CL-CMP-6985]. FAR 22.803 makes the Secretary of Labor responsible for administration and enforcement of prescribed parts of Executive Order 11246 [@CL-CMP-6804]. If the applicability of Executive Order 11246 and implementing regulations is questioned, the contracting officer shall forward the matter to the Deputy Assistant Secretary, through agency channels, for resolution [@CL-CMP-6806].
