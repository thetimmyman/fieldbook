## Purpose

States what materials may go into the work, which approvals the contracting officer controls, the workmanship standard, and who carries permits, damage and responsibility for materials and work.

### Material

All equipment, material and articles incorporated into the work must be new and of the most suitable grade for the purpose intended, unless the contract specifically provides otherwise [@CL-CMP-4871]. References in the specifications to equipment, material, articles or patented processes by trade name, make or catalog number are regarded as establishing a standard of quality and are not to be construed as limiting competition [@CL-CMP-5037]. Unless the contract specifically provides otherwise, the contractor may, at its option, use any equipment, material, article or process that, in the judgment of the contracting officer, is equal to that named in the specifications [@CL-CMP-5038].

### Approval

The contractor must obtain the contracting officer's approval of the machinery and mechanical and other equipment to be incorporated into the work [@CL-CMP-4872]. When requesting that approval, it must furnish the name of the manufacturer, the model number and other information about performance, capacity, nature and rating [@CL-CMP-5039]. When this contract or the contracting officer requires it, the contractor must also obtain approval of the material or articles that it contemplates incorporating into the work [@CL-CMP-5040]. When requesting approval of material or articles it must provide full information about them, and when directed to do so it must submit samples for approval at its own expense with all shipping charges prepaid [@CL-CMP-5041]. Machinery, equipment, material and articles that do not have the required approval are installed or used at the risk of subsequent rejection [@CL-CMP-5042].

### Workmanship

All work must be performed in a skillful and workmanlike manner, and the contracting officer may require in writing that the contractor remove from the work any employee the officer deems incompetent, careless or otherwise objectionable [@CL-CMP-4873].

### Permits and responsibilities

Without additional expense to the Government, the contractor must obtain any necessary licenses and permits and comply with the Federal, State and municipal laws, codes and regulations applicable to the performance of the work [@CL-CMP-4874]. It is also responsible for all damages to persons or property that occur as a result of its fault or negligence [@CL-CMP-4875]. It is responsible for all materials delivered and work performed until completion and acceptance of the entire work, except for any completed unit of work that may have been accepted under the contract [@CL-CMP-4876].

## How it works

The material rule sets a default that the contract may displace, and the contractor may use an item that the contracting officer judges equal to the one named in the specifications unless the contract provides otherwise [@CL-CMP-4871; @CL-CMP-5038]. Approval is a separate step: machinery and equipment need it, and material or articles need it when the contract or the contracting officer requires it [@CL-CMP-4872; @CL-CMP-5040]. Items without the required approval are installed or used at the risk of subsequent rejection [@CL-CMP-5042]. Permits, compliance with laws and damage from fault or negligence sit with the contractor [@CL-CMP-4874; @CL-CMP-4875]. Responsibility for materials delivered and work performed runs to completion and acceptance of the entire work [@CL-CMP-4876].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A specification names a pump by catalog number. The contractor proposes an equal pump, and the contracting officer judges it equal, so the contractor may use it because the contract does not provide otherwise. The contractor sends the manufacturer's name, the model number and the capacity data and obtains approval of the pump before incorporating it. The contracting officer also directs the contractor to submit a pipe sample, which the contractor sends at its own expense with shipping prepaid.

The contractor installs a boiler without the required approval. The boiler is installed at the risk of subsequent rejection. The contractor obtains the building permit without additional expense to the Government. A storm damages the finished east wing before acceptance of the entire work. The contractor remains responsible for the work performed and materials delivered, but a separate completed unit that the Government has already accepted is excepted. The contracting officer may require in writing the removal of a foreman the officer deems careless.

## Never confuse

Approval is not acceptance. Approval concerns what may be incorporated into the work [@CL-CMP-4872; @CL-CMP-5040], while the contractor's responsibility for the work continues until completion and acceptance of the entire work [@CL-CMP-4876]. A trade name in the specifications is also not a requirement to buy that brand, because such references establish a standard of quality and do not limit competition [@CL-CMP-5037]. The damages duty differs from the responsibility for materials and work: one covers damages to persons or property from the contractor's fault or negligence [@CL-CMP-4875], the other covers materials delivered and work performed until acceptance [@CL-CMP-4876]. When the Government takes possession or use before completion, the contractor is relieved of responsibility for loss or damage resulting from that possession or use [@CL-CMP-4884], as explained on [Operations and storage areas, Government possession and utility services](cmp-construction-operations-areas-and-utility-services.md).
