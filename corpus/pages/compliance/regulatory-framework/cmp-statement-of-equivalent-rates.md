## Purpose

Explains what the statement of equivalent rates must set out, how the blue collar and white collar rates are computed, and where grade and salary data can come from.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-6926] |
| Codified citation | FAR 22.1016 [@CL-CMP-6926] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-6926] |

The statement of equivalent rates for Federal hires is the statement required under the clause at FAR 52.222-42 [@CL-CMP-6926]. It must set forth the wage rates and fringe benefits that would be paid by the contracting activity to the various classes of service employees expected to be utilized under the contract if the General Schedule (white collar) and/or Wage Board (blue collar) pay statutes were applicable [@CL-CMP-6773].

## How it works

Wages paid blue collar employees shall be the basic hourly rate for each class, and that rate shall be Wage Board pay schedule step two for nonsupervisory service employees and step three for supervisory service employees [@CL-CMP-6774]. Wages paid white collar employees shall be an hourly rate for each class, obtained by dividing the general pay schedule step one biweekly rate by 80 [@CL-CMP-6925]. Local civilian personnel offices can assist in determining and providing grade and salary data [@CL-CMP-6775].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A service contract needs laborers, one working supervisor and one clerk. The laborers are nonsupervisory blue collar employees, so their basic hourly rate is the Wage Board step two rate [@CL-CMP-6774]. The supervisor's basic hourly rate is the Wage Board step three rate [@CL-CMP-6774]. The clerk is a white collar employee. If the general pay schedule step one biweekly rate for the clerk's grade is 2,400 dollars, the clerk's hourly rate is 2,400 divided by 80, which is 30 dollars [@CL-CMP-6925]. The local civilian personnel office can supply the grade and salary data behind these figures [@CL-CMP-6775].

## Never confuse

The statement of equivalent rates is for information only and is not a wage determination [@CL-CMP-5895]. It sets out what the contracting activity would pay its own hires in the same classes if the pay statutes were applicable, not what it does pay [@CL-CMP-6773]. The clause itself and its use with workers with disabilities are covered on [Workers with disabilities and equivalent rates](../regulatory-framework/cmp-workers-with-disabilities-and-equivalent-rates.md).
