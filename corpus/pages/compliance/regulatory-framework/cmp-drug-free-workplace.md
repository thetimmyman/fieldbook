## Purpose

Explains the steps a contractor other than an individual must take to keep a drug-free workplace, the deadlines that follow from a conviction, and which employees are covered.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-5434] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.226-7 [@CL-CMP-5434] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.226-7 [@CL-CMP-1083] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-5434] |
| Applies to | Contractors other than an individual [@CL-CMP-5434]; directly engaged is defined to include all direct cost employees and any other Contractor employee who has other than a minimal impact or involvement in contract performance [@CL-CMP-5440] |

A contractor other than an individual must take the steps in paragraph (b) within 30 days after award (unless a longer period is agreed to in writing for contracts of 30 days or more performance duration), or as soon as possible for contracts of less than 30 days performance duration [@CL-CMP-5434].

Directly engaged is defined to include all direct cost employees and any other Contractor employee who has other than a minimal impact or involvement in contract performance [@CL-CMP-5440].

## How it works

Statement and program. The Contractor shall publish a statement notifying its employees that the unlawful manufacture, distribution, dispensing, possession, or use of a controlled substance is prohibited in the contractor's workplace and specifying the actions that will be taken against employees for violations of the prohibition [@CL-CMP-5435]. It must establish an ongoing drug-free awareness program to inform such employees about the dangers of drug abuse in the workplace, the contractor's policy of maintaining a drug-free workplace, and any available drug counseling, rehabilitation and employee assistance programs [@CL-CMP-5436]. The program must also inform employees about the penalties that may be imposed upon employees for drug abuse violations occurring in the workplace, and the Contractor must provide all employees engaged in performance of the contract with a copy of the statement required by subparagraph (b)(1) [@CL-CMP-5517].

Condition of employment. The contractor must notify such employees in writing in the statement required by subparagraph (b)(1) that, as a condition of continued employment on this contract, the employee will abide by the terms of the statement [@CL-CMP-5518]. The statement must also say that the employee will notify the employer in writing of the employee's conviction under a criminal drug statute for a violation occurring in the workplace no later than 5 days after such conviction [@CL-CMP-5518; @CL-CMP-5437].

Notice to the Contracting Officer. The contractor must notify the Contracting Officer in writing within 10 days after receiving notice under subdivision (b)(4)(ii) of the clause, from an employee or otherwise receiving actual notice of such conviction, and the notice shall include the position title of the employee [@CL-CMP-5438].

Action. Within 30 days after receiving notice under subdivision (b)(4)(ii) of the clause of a conviction, the Contractor shall take one of two actions with respect to any employee who is convicted of a drug abuse violation occurring in the workplace [@CL-CMP-5439]. One action is taking appropriate personnel action against such employee, up to and including termination [@CL-CMP-5519]. The other is requiring such employee to satisfactorily participate in a drug abuse assistance or rehabilitation program approved for such purposes by a Federal, State, or local health, law enforcement, or other appropriate agency [@CL-CMP-5520]. The Contractor must also make a good faith effort to maintain a drug-free workplace through implementation of subparagraphs (b)(1) through (b)(6) [@CL-CMP-6073].

Individual contractors. A Contractor that is an individual agrees, by award of the contract or acceptance of a purchase order, not to engage in the unlawful manufacture, distribution, dispensing, possession, or use of a controlled substance while performing the contract [@CL-CMP-6074]. Individual means an offeror or contractor that has no more than one employee, including the offeror or contractor [@CL-CMP-6079].

Remedies. In addition to other remedies available to the Government [@CL-CMP-6324], the Contractor's failure to comply with the requirements of paragraph (b) or (c) may, pursuant to FAR 26.505, render the Contractor subject to suspension of contract payments, termination of the contract for default, and suspension or debarment [@CL-CMP-6109].

Definitions. Controlled substance means a controlled substance in schedules I through V of section 202 of the Controlled Substances Act (section 812 of title 21 of the United States Code) and as further defined in regulation at 21 CFR 1308.11-1308.15 [@CL-CMP-6075]. Conviction means a finding of guilt (including a plea of nolo contendere) or imposition of sentence, or both, by any judicial body charged with the responsibility to determine violations of the Federal or State criminal drug statutes [@CL-CMP-6076]. Criminal drug statute means a Federal or non-Federal criminal statute involving the manufacture, distribution, dispensing, possession or use of any controlled substance [@CL-CMP-6077]. A drug-free workplace is the site or sites for the performance of work done by the Contractor in connection with a specific contract where employees of the Contractor are prohibited from engaging in the unlawful manufacture, distribution, dispensing, possession, or use of a controlled substance [@CL-CMP-6078].

Class Deviation 2026-O0038 directs DoD contracting officers to use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text, effective February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. Under RFO FAR 52.226-7(b), a contractor other than an individual must take the listed steps within 30 days after award (unless a longer period is agreed to in writing for contracts of 30 days or more performance duration), or as soon as possible for contracts of less than 30 days performance duration [@CL-CMP-1083]. Under RFO FAR 52.226-7(b)(4)(ii), one item the employee will do is notify the employer in writing of the employee's conviction under a criminal drug statute for a violation occurring in the workplace no later than 5 days after the conviction [@CL-CMP-1084]. Under RFO FAR 52.226-7(b)(5), the contractor must notify the Contracting Officer in writing within 10 days after receiving notice under subdivision (b)(4)(ii) from an employee or otherwise receiving actual notice of the conviction, and the notice must include the position title of the employee [@CL-CMP-1085]. Under RFO FAR 52.226-7(b)(6), within 30 days after receiving notice under subdivision (b)(4)(ii) of a conviction, the contractor must take one of the listed actions with respect to any employee who is convicted of a drug abuse violation occurring in the workplace [@CL-CMP-1086]. The first listed action is taking appropriate personnel action against the employee, up to and including termination [@CL-CMP-1087]; the second is requiring the employee to satisfactorily participate in a drug abuse assistance or rehabilitation program approved for such purposes by a Federal, State, or local health, law enforcement, or other appropriate agency [@CL-CMP-1088]. Under RFO FAR 52.226-7(a), employee means an employee of a contractor directly engaged in the performance of work under a Government contract, and directly engaged includes all direct cost employees and any other contractor employee who has other than a minimal impact or involvement in contract performance [@CL-CMP-1090]. Under RFO FAR 52.226-7(d), the contractor's failure to comply with the requirements of paragraph (b) or (c) may, pursuant to FAR 26.505, render the contractor subject to suspension of contract payments, termination of the contract for default, and suspension or debarment [@CL-CMP-1089].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An employee is convicted on 3 March and tells the employer in writing on 6 March, 3 days after the conviction and inside the 5 day window. The contractor receives that notice on 6 March, so its 10 day and 30 day periods run from 6 March. It notifies the Contracting Officer in writing on 12 March, 6 days later, and includes the employee's position title. On 1 April, 26 days after 6 March and inside the 30 day window, it assigns the employee to an approved rehabilitation program, which is one of the two permitted actions.

## Never confuse

The employee's notice to the employer differs from the contractor's notices: the employee's notice to the employer is due no later than 5 days after the conviction [@CL-CMP-5437]; the contractor's notice to the Contracting Officer is due within 10 days after receiving notice under subdivision (b)(4)(ii), from an employee or otherwise receiving actual notice of the conviction [@CL-CMP-5438], and the contractor's action is due within 30 days after receiving notice under subdivision (b)(4)(ii) of a conviction [@CL-CMP-5439]. The DFARS clause on a drug-free work force is covered in [Drug-free work force (DFARS clause)](cmp-dfars-drug-free-work-force.md).
