## Purpose

Explains what the contracting officer obtains when every possible place of service performance is known, when the contracting officer contacts the agency labor advisor about a determination or an incumbent agreement, and what follows when the Department of Labor finds that a determination was wrongly left out.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-6748] |
| Codified citation | FAR 22.1009-3, 22.1013 and 22.1015 [@CL-CMP-6748; @CL-CMP-6750; @CL-CMP-6754] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-6754] |
| Applies to | Service contracts where the contracting officer can identify all possible places of performance, and service contracts with a determination under review, or with a determination that the Department of Labor finds was wrongly left out [@CL-CMP-6748; @CL-CMP-6753; @CL-CMP-6754] |

If the contracting officer can identify all the possible places or areas of performance, even though the actual place of performance will not be known until the successful offeror is chosen, the contracting officer must obtain a wage determination for each locality where services may be performed [@CL-CMP-6748]. If the contracting officer subsequently learns, before the closing date for submission of offers, of any potential offerors in previously unidentified places, the contracting officer must obtain wage determinations for the additional places of performance [@CL-CMP-6749]. In that situation the contracting officer must also amend the solicitation to include all wage determinations [@CL-CMP-6910], extend the time for submission of final offers if necessary [@CL-CMP-6911], and follow the procedures in FAR 22.1009-4 [@CL-CMP-6912].

If wages, fringe benefits or periodic increases provided for in a collective bargaining agreement vary substantially from those prevailing for similar services in the locality, the contracting officer must immediately contact the agency labor advisor to consider instituting the procedures in FAR 22.1021 [@CL-CMP-6750]. If the contracting officer believes that an incumbent or predecessor contractor's agreement was not the result of arm's length negotiations, the contracting officer must contact the agency labor advisor to determine appropriate action [@CL-CMP-6751]. On receiving a wage determination not predicated upon a collective bargaining agreement, the contracting officer must ascertain whether it does not conform with wages and fringe benefits prevailing for similar services in the locality [@CL-CMP-6752], and whether it contains significant errors or omissions [@CL-CMP-6913]. If either is evident, the contracting officer must contact the agency labor advisor to determine appropriate action [@CL-CMP-6753].

The section on errors found by the Department of Labor applies where the Department discovers and determines, whether before or after a contract award, that a contracting officer made an erroneous determination that the Service Contract Labor Standards statute did not apply to a particular acquisition [@CL-CMP-6914], or failed to include an appropriate wage determination in a covered contract [@CL-CMP-6915]. Within 30 days of notification by the Department, the contracting officer must include in the contract the clause at FAR 52.222-41 and any applicable wage determination issued by the Administrator [@CL-CMP-6754]. If the contract is subject to the provision of title 41 of the United States Code that the section cites, the Administrator may require retroactive application of that wage determination [@CL-CMP-6755]. The contracting officer must equitably adjust the contract price to reflect any changed cost of performance resulting from incorporating a wage determination or revision [@CL-CMP-6916].

## How it works

Where the contracting officer can identify all the possible places of performance, the officer obtains a determination for each locality where services may be performed, and the officer obtains more if, before the closing date for submission of offers, the officer learns of a potential offeror in a previously unidentified place [@CL-CMP-6748; @CL-CMP-6749]. Where an incumbent agreement varies substantially from prevailing rates or looks not to have been bargained at arm's length, or a determination not predicated on a collective bargaining agreement evidently does not conform with prevailing wages or has significant errors or omissions, the contracting officer contacts the agency labor advisor [@CL-CMP-6750; @CL-CMP-6751; @CL-CMP-6753]. Where the Department of Labor finds the error, its notification starts the contracting officer's deadline for including the clause and the determination [@CL-CMP-6754]. For a contract subject to the cited provision, the Administrator may require retroactive application [@CL-CMP-6755]. The contracting officer must equitably adjust the price for the changed cost of performance [@CL-CMP-6916].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A solicitation covers building maintenance at any of four possible duty stations, and the contracting officer can identify all of them [@CL-CMP-6748]. The contracting officer obtains a determination for each locality [@CL-CMP-6748]. Before the closing date the contracting officer learns that a firm with an office in a fifth place may offer [@CL-CMP-6749]. The contracting officer obtains a determination for that place, amends the solicitation to include all determinations, extends the time for final offers if necessary, and follows the procedures in FAR 22.1009-4 [@CL-CMP-6749; @CL-CMP-6910; @CL-CMP-6911; @CL-CMP-6912].

In another buy the contracting officer receives a determination that is not based on a collective bargaining agreement and finds that it omits a significant classification [@CL-CMP-6913]. The contracting officer contacts the agency labor advisor to determine appropriate action [@CL-CMP-6753].

On a third contract the Department of Labor determines after award that the contracting officer failed to include an appropriate wage determination in a covered contract [@CL-CMP-6915]. Within 30 days of the Department's notification, the contracting officer includes the clause and the applicable determination [@CL-CMP-6754]. The contracting officer then adjusts the contract price for the changed cost of performance [@CL-CMP-6916].

## Never confuse

A determination that is not predicated on a collective bargaining agreement and is evidently nonconforming or has significant errors or omissions is not the same as one that the Department of Labor finds missing or wrongly treated as inapplicable. For the first, the contracting officer contacts the agency labor advisor [@CL-CMP-6753]. For the second, the contracting officer must include the clause and the determination within 30 days of the Department's notification [@CL-CMP-6754]. A place of performance that is still unknown is handled on another card, see [SCLS place of performance unknown](../regulatory-framework/cmp-scls-place-of-performance-unknown.md), and the procedures that the labor advisor may consider are described in [SCLS remanufacturing, unidentified places and hearings](../regulatory-framework/cmp-scls-remanufacturing-unidentified-places-and-hearings.md).
