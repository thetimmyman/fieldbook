## Purpose

Explains when a contract that carries the approval clause becomes binding, what the voluntary preaward survey invites offerors to do, and which definition governs a word or term used in a provision or clause.

## The rule

| Codified citation | FAR 52.204-1; FAR 52.201-1; FAR 52.202-1 [@CL-CMP-5971; @CL-CMP-5972; @CL-CMP-5975] |

The contract is subject to the written approval of the designated agency official the clause identifies and is not binding until so approved [@CL-CMP-5971]. The clause text includes a bracketed instruction to identify the title of the designated agency official, so the official is contract-specific [@CL-CMP-6662].

The survey provision encourages all actual and potential offerors to provide feedback on the preaward and debriefing processes, as applicable [@CL-CMP-6663]. A word or term used in a provision or clause has the same meaning as the definition in FAR 2.101 in effect when the solicitation was issued, unless one of the exceptions listed in the definitions clause applies [@CL-CMP-5975].

## How it works

### Approval

Until the designated official approves the contract in writing, the contract is not binding [@CL-CMP-5971]. The bracketed title in the clause is a fill-in for each contract, and this card does not name any official [@CL-CMP-6662].

### Voluntary survey

Feedback on the preaward and debriefing processes may be provided to agencies up to 45 days after award [@CL-CMP-5972]. The feedback is anonymous, unless the participant identifies itself in the survey [@CL-CMP-5974]. Offerors can take part by selecting the link that the provision gives [@CL-CMP-6664].

The contracting officer will not review the information provided until after contract award and will not consider it in the award decision [@CL-CMP-5973]. The survey is voluntary and does not convey any protections, rights, or grounds for protest [@CL-CMP-6665]. It creates a way for actual and potential offerors to give the Government constructive feedback about the preaward and debriefing processes used for a specific acquisition [@CL-CMP-6666].

### Definitions

The FAR 2.101 meaning in effect when the solicitation was issued governs a word or term that the FAR defines [@CL-CMP-5975]. The FAR definition does not control in five listed cases. The first is where the solicitation, or an amended solicitation, provides a different definition [@CL-CMP-6667]. The second is where the contracting parties agree to a different definition [@CL-CMP-6668]. The third is where the part, subpart, or section of the FAR that prescribes the provision or clause provides a different meaning [@CL-CMP-5976]. The fourth is where the word or term is defined in FAR part 31 for use in the cost principles and procedures [@CL-CMP-6669]. The fifth is where the word or term defines an acquisition-related threshold that is adjusted for inflation: the changed threshold then applies throughout the remaining term of the contract, unless there is a subsequent threshold adjustment [@CL-CMP-5977].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contract is signed and the designated agency official approves it in writing 19 days later. The contract is not binding before that approval [@CL-CMP-5971]. An offeror that lost the competition gives survey feedback 30 days after award, which is inside the 45-day period [@CL-CMP-5972], and does not identify itself, so the feedback is anonymous [@CL-CMP-5974]. The offeror cannot use the survey as a protest ground [@CL-CMP-6665], and the contracting officer did not consider it in the award decision [@CL-CMP-5973].

A solicitation is issued in March. A clause in the contract uses a term that the FAR defines, and nothing in the solicitation, the contract or the prescribing part of the FAR gives a different meaning. The meaning in the FAR as it stood in March governs, even if the FAR text is later amended [@CL-CMP-5975]. A separate clause refers to a dollar threshold that the FAR set at 100 when the solicitation was issued. The FAR adjusts it for inflation to 110 in month 8 of performance. From that month the contract uses 110 for the rest of its term, and only a later adjustment would change it again [@CL-CMP-5977].

## Never confuse

The survey is not a protest route. It is voluntary, it conveys no protections, rights, or grounds for protest, and the contracting officer does not read it until after award [@CL-CMP-6665; @CL-CMP-5973].

The approval clause is not part of the survey provision or the definitions clause. It addresses when the contract is binding [@CL-CMP-5971], while the survey is about feedback on the preaward and debriefing processes and the definitions clause is about the meaning of words and terms [@CL-CMP-6663; @CL-CMP-5975].

The meaning in effect when the solicitation was issued is the default, not the only meaning. An inflation adjustment to a threshold changes the figure during performance [@CL-CMP-5977]. The cost principles use their own definitions where the word or term is defined in FAR part 31 [@CL-CMP-6669].
