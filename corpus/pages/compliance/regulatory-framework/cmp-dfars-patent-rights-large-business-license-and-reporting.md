## Purpose

Shows the license the Government holds when the contractor keeps title, the cases where the contractor must assign title, the march-in acknowledgment, the reports the contractor owes, the subcontract notices and the withholding of payment.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-CMP-3935] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 252.227-7038 [@CL-CMP-3935] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-3935] |
| Applies to | Subject inventions in which the Contractor retains ownership [@CL-CMP-3935] |

If the Contractor retains ownership of any subject invention, the Government shall have a nonexclusive, nontransferable, irrevocable, paid-up license to practice, or have practiced for or on behalf of the United States, the subject invention throughout the world [@CL-CMP-3935]. The Contractor shall assign title to the agency, upon written request, in the listed cases, one of which is where the Contractor elects not to retain title [@CL-CMP-3936]. Another listed case is where the Contractor fails to disclose or elect within the times specified and the agency requests title within 60 days after learning of the failure [@CL-CMP-3937]. With respect to any subject invention in which the Contractor has retained ownership, the agency has the right to require licensing under the statutes and regulations the clause names, which include sections 203 and 210(c) of title 35 of the United States Code and section 401.6 of title 37 of the Code of Federal Regulations [@CL-CMP-3938].

The Contractor shall furnish the Contracting Officer interim reports every 12 months, or any longer period the Contracting Officer specifies, listing subject inventions during that period [@CL-CMP-3939]. Among the items the Contractor is to furnish to the Contracting Officer is a final report, within 3 months after completion of the contracted work, listing all subject inventions or stating that there were none, and listing all subcontracts at any tier containing a patent rights clause or stating that there were none [@CL-CMP-3984]. The Contractor shall, upon request, submit periodic reports no more frequently than annually on the utilization of a subject invention or on efforts to obtain utilization that are being made by the Contractor or its licensees or assignees [@CL-CMP-3940; @CL-CMP-7386]. The Contractor shall also promptly notify the Contracting Officer in writing on the award of any subcontract at any tier containing a patent rights clause, identifying among other listed items the subcontractor, the applicable patent rights clause and the work to be performed [@CL-CMP-3941].

The Contracting Officer may, in the Government's interest, withhold payment until a reserve not exceeding $50,000 or 5 percent of the amount of the contract, whichever is less, is set aside if, in the Contracting Officer's opinion, the Contractor fails to meet any of the requirements the paragraph on withholding then lists [@CL-CMP-3985].

## How it works

The Government's license attaches when the Contractor keeps title [@CL-CMP-3935]. Title can pass to the agency on written request in listed cases [@CL-CMP-3936; @CL-CMP-3937]. The agency's march-in right lets it require licensing of an invention the Contractor owns [@CL-CMP-3938]. The interim and utilization reports tell the Contracting Officer which inventions exist and how they are used, and the subcontract notices show where lower-tier patent rights clauses apply [@CL-CMP-3939; @CL-CMP-3940; @CL-CMP-3941]. The withholding reserve lets the Contracting Officer hold back payment when listed requirements are not met [@CL-CMP-3985]. This card does not set out the preference for United States industry or the other paragraphs of the clause.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor keeps title to a new sensor design. The Government holds a paid-up license to practice it, or have it practiced for the Government, anywhere in the world. Every 12 months the contractor sends the Contracting Officer a list of inventions made in the period. It awards a subcontract containing a patent rights clause and tells the Contracting Officer in writing, naming the subcontractor and the work.

## Never confuse

The march-in right is not a license the Government already holds: it is the right to require licensing of an invention the Contractor owns [@CL-CMP-3938]. The Government's own paid-up license is the one granted when the Contractor keeps title [@CL-CMP-3935]. The clause where the Government takes title from the start is separate and is covered on [Patent rights with ownership by the Government](cmp-patent-rights-government-ownership.md), and the FAR contractor-ownership clause is covered on [the deadlines and title card](cmp-patent-rights-contractor-ownership-deadlines-and-title.md). Disclosure, election and filing deadlines for this DoD clause are on [DoD patent rights for large businesses: ownership, disclosure, election and filing](cmp-dfars-patent-rights-large-business-ownership-and-deadlines.md).
