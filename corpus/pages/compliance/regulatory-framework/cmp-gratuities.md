## Purpose

Shows what conduct lets the Government terminate a contract for gratuities, how the hearing and review work, what remedies follow and when exemplary damages are available.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-5554] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.203-3 [@CL-CMP-5554]; FAR 3.201 [@CL-CMP-9628]; FAR 3.202 [@CL-CMP-9629; @CL-CMP-9630] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.203-3 [@CL-CMP-1257; @CL-CMP-1258] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-5554] |
| Applies to | Contracts that include the gratuities clause prescribed by FAR 3.202 [@CL-CMP-5647] |

The right of the contractor to proceed may be terminated by written notice if, after notice and hearing, the agency head or a designee determines that the contractor, its agent or another representative offered or gave a gratuity, such as an entertainment or gift, to an officer, official or employee of the Government [@CL-CMP-5554; @CL-CMP-5555], and intended, by the gratuity, to obtain a contract or favorable treatment under a contract [@CL-CMP-5556].

## How it works

Both elements are part of the determination, which follows notice and a hearing [@CL-CMP-5554; @CL-CMP-5555; @CL-CMP-5556]. The facts supporting the determination may be reviewed by any court having lawful jurisdiction [@CL-CMP-5557].

If the contract is terminated on this ground, the Government is entitled to pursue the same remedies as in a breach of the contract [@CL-CMP-5558].

On the same termination the Government is also entitled, in addition to any other damages provided by law, to exemplary damages of not less than 3 nor more than 10 times the cost the contractor incurred in giving gratuities to the person concerned, as determined by the agency head or a designee [@CL-CMP-5559]. That exemplary damages entitlement is applicable only if the contract uses money appropriated to the Department of Defense [@CL-CMP-5560].

The rights and remedies of the Government in the clause are not exclusive and are in addition to any other rights and remedies provided by law or under the contract [@CL-CMP-5648].

Under Class Deviation 2026-O0038, DoD contracting officers shall use the revised FAR part 52 in place of the text codified at 48 CFR chapter 1, effective February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. The RFO clause provides that the right of the Contractor to proceed may be terminated by written notice if, after notice and hearing, the agency head or a designee determines that the Contractor, its agent, or another representative offered or gave a gratuity (e.g., an entertainment or gift) to an officer, official, or employee of the Government and intended, by the gratuity, to obtain a contract or favorable treatment under a contract [@CL-CMP-1257; @CL-CMP-1258]. The facts supporting this determination may be reviewed by any court having lawful jurisdiction [@CL-CMP-1259]. If the contract is terminated under paragraph (a) of the clause, the Government is entitled to pursue the same remedies as in a breach of the contract [@CL-CMP-1260] and, in addition to any other damages provided by law, to exemplary damages of not less than 3 nor more than 10 times the cost incurred by the Contractor in giving gratuities to the person concerned, as determined by the agency head or a designee [@CL-CMP-1261]. Paragraph (c)(2) is applicable only if the contract uses money appropriated to the Department of Defense [@CL-CMP-1262]. The rights and remedies of the Government provided in the clause shall not be exclusive and are in addition to any other rights and remedies provided by law or under the contract [@CL-CMP-1263].

The gratuities subpart applies to all executive agencies, except that its coverage concerning exemplary damages applies only to the Department of Defense (section 4651 of title 10 of the United States Code) [@CL-CMP-9628]. The contracting officer shall insert the Gratuities clause at FAR 52.203-3 in solicitations and contracts with a value exceeding the simplified acquisition threshold, except those for personal services and those between military departments or defense agencies and foreign governments that do not obligate any funds appropriated to the Department of Defense [@CL-CMP-9629; @CL-CMP-9630].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor's representative gives a Government buyer a gift that costs the contractor 2,000. After notice and hearing, the agency head determines that the representative intended the gift to win a follow-on order, so the contractor's right to proceed is terminated by written notice. The Government pursues breach remedies. If the contract uses money appropriated to the Department of Defense, exemplary damages of 6,000 to 20,000 are available, which is 3 times and 10 times the 2,000 cost, and the agency head or a designee determines the amount. If the contract uses no such money, the exemplary damages entitlement does not apply and the other remedies are unaffected.

## Never confuse

The clause reaches a gratuity given to obtain business rather than any gift to any Government employee: it reaches a gratuity (e.g., an entertainment or gift) offered or given to an officer, official, or employee of the Government and intended, by the gratuity, to obtain a contract or favorable treatment under a contract, and termination follows a determination made after notice and hearing [@CL-CMP-5554; @CL-CMP-5555; @CL-CMP-5556].
