## Purpose

Sets out the campus access clause prescription, its threshold and funding conditions, the definitions of covered agency and institution of higher education, and the two definitions used for inverted domestic corporations.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-7981] |
| Codified citation | FAR 9.110-1 [@CL-CMP-7979; @CL-CMP-8446; @CL-CMP-7980], FAR 9.110-5 [@CL-CMP-7981; @CL-CMP-7982; @CL-CMP-7983] and FAR 9.108-1 [@CL-CMP-8449; @CL-CMP-7984; @CL-CMP-7985] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-7981] |
| Applies to | Solicitations and contracts with institutions of higher education that are expected to exceed the simplified acquisition threshold, when funds from a covered agency are used, other than part 12 acquisitions of commercial products and commercial services [@CL-CMP-7981; @CL-CMP-7982; @CL-CMP-7983] |

The contracting officer shall insert the clause at FAR 52.209-14 in solicitations and contracts that are expected to exceed the simplified acquisition threshold, with institutions of higher education [@CL-CMP-7981], when using funds from a covered agency [@CL-CMP-7982]. The clause is not prescribed for solicitations and contracts using part 12 for the acquisition of commercial products and commercial services [@CL-CMP-7983].

The definitions in section 9.110-1 apply as the terms are used in that section [@CL-CMP-8446]. A covered agency is any of the following: the Department of Defense [@CL-CMP-7979]; any department or agency for which regular appropriations are made in a Department of Labor, Health and Human Services, and Education, and Related Agencies Appropriations Act [@CL-CMP-8447]; and the Department of Homeland Security, the National Nuclear Security Administration of the Department of Energy, the Department of Transportation and the Central Intelligence Agency [@CL-CMP-8448]. An institution of higher education is an institution that meets the requirements of section 1001 of title 20 of the United States Code and includes all sub-elements of such an institution [@CL-CMP-7980].

The inverted domestic corporation definitions apply as the terms are used in section 9.108-1 [@CL-CMP-8449]. An inverted domestic corporation is a foreign incorporated entity that meets the definition of an inverted domestic corporation under section 395(b) of title 6 of the United States Code, applied in accordance with the rules and definitions of section 395(c) [@CL-CMP-7984]. A subsidiary is an entity in which more than 50 percent of the entity is owned directly by a parent corporation or through another subsidiary of a parent corporation [@CL-CMP-7985].

## How it works

The duty to insert the clause falls on the contracting officer, and three conditions apply together: the other party is an institution of higher education, the solicitation or contract is expected to exceed the simplified acquisition threshold, and funds from a covered agency are used [@CL-CMP-7981; @CL-CMP-7982]. The text measures the threshold against the expected value and does not say who forms that expectation [@CL-CMP-7981].

The six kinds of covered agency are alternatives, so funds from any one of them meet the funding condition [@CL-CMP-7979; @CL-CMP-8447; @CL-CMP-8448; @CL-CMP-7982]. An institution of higher education includes all sub-elements of the institution [@CL-CMP-7980].

Because the clause is prescribed for institutions of higher education, a contract with a company that is not one falls outside the prescription [@CL-CMP-7981].

Part 12 acquisitions of commercial products and commercial services are carved out even when the other conditions are met [@CL-CMP-7983].

The inverted domestic corporation definitions state no duty, threshold or decision-maker. They define two terms, and the prohibition that uses them is covered in the linked card [@CL-CMP-7984; @CL-CMP-7985].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A covered agency funds a research support contract with a university that meets the institution of higher education definition. The contract is expected to exceed the simplified acquisition threshold and does not use part 12 for commercial products or commercial services, so the contracting officer inserts the clause at FAR 52.209-14 [@CL-CMP-7981; @CL-CMP-7982; @CL-CMP-7983]. Assume a department of that university is a sub-element of the institution, so the institution definition covers it [@CL-CMP-7980].

The same agency buys commercial products from the same university under part 12, and there the clause is not prescribed [@CL-CMP-7983].

A foreign-incorporated company is 60 percent owned by one parent corporation. It is a subsidiary of that parent, because more than 50 percent of it is owned directly by the parent [@CL-CMP-7985]. Whether it is an inverted domestic corporation depends on the statutory definition [@CL-CMP-7984].

## Never confuse

This clause is not the arms control certification provision [@CL-CMP-7981; @CL-CMP-7976]. The campus access clause turns on institutions of higher education, the threshold and covered agency funds, while the arms control provision turns on a solicitation above the threshold that is not for commercial products or services, unless the exception at FAR 9.109-3 applies [@CL-CMP-7981; @CL-CMP-7982; @CL-CMP-7976; @CL-CMP-7977; @CL-CMP-7978].
