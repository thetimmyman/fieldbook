## Purpose

Shows the bar on awarding to Government employees and to firms they own or control, who may authorize an exception, and how a contractor that cannot prevent a personal conflict of interest asks for a mitigation plan or a waiver.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-6862] |
| Codified citation | FAR 3.601, 3.602 and 3.1104 [@CL-CMP-6862; @CL-CMP-7152; @CL-CMP-6865] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-6862] |
| Applies to | Awards by contracting officers to Government employees and to organizations they own or control, and requests by contractors that cannot satisfactorily prevent a personal conflict of interest [@CL-CMP-6862; @CL-CMP-6865] |

Except as specified in the exceptions section, a contracting officer must not knowingly award a contract to a Government employee, or to a business concern or other organization owned or substantially owned or controlled by one or more Government employees [@CL-CMP-6862]. The policy is intended to avoid any conflict of interest between the employees' interests and their Government duties, and to avoid the appearance of favoritism or preferential treatment by the Government toward its employees [@CL-CMP-6863].

The agency head, or a designee not below the level of the head of the contracting activity, may authorize an exception to that policy only if there is a most compelling reason to do so, such as when the Government's needs cannot reasonably be otherwise met [@CL-CMP-7152].

## How it works

For purposes of the subpart, special Government employees performing services as experts, advisors or consultants, or as members of advisory committees, are not considered Government employees unless one of the listed conditions applies [@CL-CMP-6864]. The conditions are that the contract arises directly out of the individual's activity as a special Government employee, that in that capacity the individual is in a position to influence the award, or that another conflict of interest is determined to exist [@CL-CMP-7153].

A separate route applies to a contractor that has a personal conflict of interest. The request route applies in exceptional circumstances, when the contractor cannot satisfactorily prevent a personal conflict of interest as the clause requires [@CL-CMP-6865]. The contractor may then submit a request, through the contracting officer, for the head of the contracting activity to agree to a plan to mitigate the conflict or to waive the requirement to prevent personal conflicts of interest [@CL-CMP-7154]. The contracting officer is the channel, and the head of the contracting activity decides. If the head determines in writing that the action is in the best interest of the Government, the head may impose conditions that provide mitigation or grant a waiver [@CL-CMP-6866]. The authority to impose mitigation conditions or grant a waiver must not be redelegated [@CL-CMP-7155].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A civilian employee of an agency owns most of a small firm that bids on an agency solicitation. A contracting officer must not knowingly award the contract to that firm [@CL-CMP-6862], unless the agency head, or a designee not below the level of the head of the contracting activity, authorizes an exception for a most compelling reason, such as Government needs that cannot reasonably be met otherwise [@CL-CMP-7152].

Separately, a contractor whose contract includes the personal conflicts clause learns that a personal conflict of interest has arisen and, in exceptional circumstances, cannot satisfactorily prevent it [@CL-CMP-6865]. The contractor sends a request through the contracting officer asking the head of the contracting activity to agree to a mitigation plan [@CL-CMP-7154]. The head decides, and may impose conditions or grant a waiver if the head determines in writing that this is in the best interest of the Government [@CL-CMP-6866]. The head may not pass that authority to a designee [@CL-CMP-7155].

## Never confuse

The two routes have different decision makers and tests. An exception to the bar on awards to Government employees and their firms is authorized by the agency head or a designee not below the level of the head of the contracting activity, and only for a most compelling reason [@CL-CMP-7152]. A mitigation plan or waiver for a contractor's personal conflict of interest is decided by the head of the contracting activity on a written best-interest determination [@CL-CMP-6866]. The clause duties and the employee screening rules are in [Contractor employee personal conflicts of interest](../regulatory-framework/cmp-contractor-employee-personal-conflicts-of-interest.md).
