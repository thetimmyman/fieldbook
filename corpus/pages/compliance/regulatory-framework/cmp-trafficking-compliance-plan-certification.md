## Purpose

Shows when the trafficking compliance plan certification applies, what the offeror certifies before award, and the two alternatives the certification offers.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-5905] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.222-56 [@CL-CMP-5905] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.222-56 [@CL-CMP-6333; @CL-CMP-6334] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-5905] |
| Applies to | The apparent successful offeror, for the contract portion for supplies, other than commercially available off-the-shelf items, acquired outside the United States or services performed outside the United States above the value threshold [@CL-CMP-5906; @CL-CMP-5905] |

The certification covers the part of a contract for supplies other than commercially available off-the-shelf items to be acquired outside the United States, or services to be performed outside the United States, where the estimated value exceeds $700,000 [@CL-CMP-5905]. The apparent successful offeror submits the certification before award, covering that portion of the contract [@CL-CMP-5906].

## How it works

The certification states that the offeror has implemented a compliance plan to prevent the prohibited activities in the trafficking clause and to monitor, detect and terminate the contract with a subcontractor engaging in them [@CL-CMP-5907]. It then offers two alternatives. The certification states, after due diligence and in one alternative, that to the best of the offeror's knowledge and belief neither it nor any of its proposed agents, subcontractors or their agents is engaged in any such activities [@CL-CMP-5908]. In the other alternative, where abuses relating to the prohibited activities have been found, the certification states that the offeror or proposed subcontractor has taken the appropriate remedial and referral actions [@CL-CMP-5909].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text from February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. On the points below the RFO provision reads the same as the codified provision, with the wording change noted in the last sentence of this paragraph [@CL-CMP-6333; @CL-CMP-6334]. The apparent successful Offeror must submit, prior to award, a certification, as specified in paragraph (c), for the portion (if any) of the contract [@CL-CMP-6333; @CL-CMP-5906] that is for supplies, other than commercially available off-the-shelf items, to be acquired outside the United States, or services to be performed outside the United States, and that has an estimated value that exceeds $700,000 [@CL-CMP-6334; @CL-CMP-5905]. The certification must state that the offeror has implemented a compliance plan to prevent any prohibited activities identified in paragraph (b) of the clause at 52.222-50 [@CL-CMP-6335; @CL-CMP-5907], and to monitor, detect, and terminate the contract with a subcontractor engaging in them [@CL-CMP-6336; @CL-CMP-5907]. After due diligence it states either that to the best of the offeror's knowledge and belief neither it nor any of its proposed agents, subcontractors, or their agents is engaged in any such activities [@CL-CMP-6337; @CL-CMP-5908], or, if abuses relating to any of the prohibited activities have been found, that the offeror or proposed subcontractor has taken the appropriate remedial and referral actions [@CL-CMP-6338; @CL-CMP-5909]. The RFO text uses "must" where the codified text uses "shall" [@CL-CMP-6333; @CL-CMP-5906].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contract has two parts. Services to be performed outside the United States are estimated at 900,000, so that part is covered because 900,000 exceeds 700,000. Commercially available off-the-shelf supplies to be acquired outside the United States are estimated at 1,200,000, but they fall outside the certification because it covers supplies other than those items. A different contract has services to be performed outside the United States estimated at exactly 700,000, which does not exceed 700,000, so that portion is not covered. The offeror is the apparent successful offeror on the first contract, so before award it submits the certification for the 900,000 services portion. It states that it has implemented the compliance plan, and then states one of the two alternatives after due diligence.

## Never confuse

The certification is not the compliance plan. The plan is what the offeror implements; the certification is the statement made before award that a plan is implemented and, after due diligence, either that no prohibited activity is engaged in or that appropriate remedial and referral actions were taken on abuses found [@CL-CMP-5907; @CL-CMP-5908; @CL-CMP-5909].
