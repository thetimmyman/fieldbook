## Purpose

Shows what the FAR System is, which acquisitions the FAR covers, and how agencies may add their own regulations to it.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-2000] |
| Codified citation | FAR 1.000 [@CL-CMP-9727]; FAR 1.101, FAR 1.104 and FAR 1.301 [@CL-CMP-2000; @CL-CMP-2003; @CL-CMP-2004]; FAR 1.302 [@CL-CMP-9730] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-2000] |
| Applies to | All acquisitions as defined in part 2 of the FAR, except where expressly excluded [@CL-CMP-2003] |

The FAR System is established for the codification and publication of uniform policies and procedures for acquisition by all executive agencies [@CL-CMP-2000]. It consists of the FAR, which is the primary document, and agency acquisition regulations that implement or supplement the FAR [@CL-CMP-2001]. The FAR applies to all acquisitions as defined in part 2 of the FAR, except where expressly excluded [@CL-CMP-2003].

An agency head may issue or authorize agency acquisition regulations that implement or supplement the FAR, subject to the authorities in paragraph (c) of FAR 1.301 and other statutory authority [@CL-CMP-2004]. When adopting them, agencies shall ensure that they comply with the Paperwork Reduction Act and the Regulatory Flexibility Act [@CL-CMP-2005]. For the military departments and defense agencies, these regulations are issued subject to the authority of the Secretary of Defense [@CL-CMP-2006].

## How it works

The FAR System has two layers: the FAR itself and the agency acquisition regulations that implement or supplement it [@CL-CMP-2001]. Internal agency guidance of the type described in paragraph (a)(2) of FAR 1.301 is not part of the FAR System [@CL-CMP-2002]. Issuances under paragraph (a)(2) of FAR 1.301 need not be publicized for public comment [@CL-CMP-2007]. The sections used here do not say how a supplement's clauses reach a particular contract. For how codified text, model text and class deviations fit together, see [The FAR, the DFARS, the overhaul and class deviations](cmp-acquisition-regulations.md).

Part 1 sets forth basic policies and general information about the Federal Acquisition Regulations System including purpose, authority, applicability, issuance, arrangement, numbering, dissemination, implementation, supplementation, maintenance, administration, and deviation [@CL-CMP-9727]. Agency acquisition regulations shall be limited to those necessary to implement FAR policies and procedures within the agency and to additional policies, procedures, solicitation provisions, or contract clauses that supplement the FAR to satisfy the specific needs of the agency [@CL-CMP-9730].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A department wants to add a clause to its own supplement that asks contractors for a new report each quarter. The clause is part of the department's acquisition regulations, not part of the FAR itself. Before the department adopts it, the department must check the Paperwork Reduction Act and the Regulatory Flexibility Act. A routing memo that only tells department staff who signs which form is different: it is internal guidance, so it is not part of the FAR System.

## Never confuse

The FAR is only the primary document of the FAR System, not all of it, because agency acquisition regulations that implement or supplement the FAR are part of the System too [@CL-CMP-2001]. Internal agency guidance is outside the System [@CL-CMP-2002].
