## Purpose

Shows what the security requirements clause asks of a contractor with access to classified information, what happens when the Government changes the requirements after award, and how the two alternates differ from the basic clause.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-5966] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.204-2, with Alternates I and II [@CL-CMP-5966; @CL-CMP-6646; @CL-CMP-6658] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.240-92, with Alternates I and II; RFO FAR 52.204-2 is reserved [@CL-CMP-5665; @CL-CMP-5671; @CL-CMP-5681; @CL-CMP-5664] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-5966] |
| Applies to | Contracts to the extent they involve access to information classified Confidential, Secret, or Top Secret [@CL-CMP-5966] |

The basic clause applies to the extent that the contract involves access to information classified Confidential, Secret, or Top Secret [@CL-CMP-5966]. The contractor must comply with the Security Agreement, which is DD Form 441, including the National Industrial Security Program Operating Manual, and with any revisions to that manual of which the contractor has been given notice [@CL-CMP-5967].

Alternate I is added if a cost contract for research and development with an educational institution is contemplated, and Alternate II is added if employee identification is required for security or other reasons in a construction contract or architect-engineer contract [@CL-CMP-6646; @CL-CMP-6658]. Alternate I adds paragraphs (e), (f), and (g) to the basic clause, and Alternate II adds a paragraph (e) [@CL-CMP-6646; @CL-CMP-6658].

## How it works

### Basic clause: changes after award

Paragraph (c) applies if, after the date of the contract, the Government changes the security classification or security requirements and the changes cause an increase or decrease in security costs or otherwise affect any other term or condition of the contract [@CL-CMP-5968]. Where those conditions are met, the contract is subject to an equitable adjustment as if the changes had been directed under the Changes clause of the contract [@CL-CMP-5969].

### Basic clause: flowdown

The contractor must insert terms that conform substantially to the language of the clause in all subcontracts under the contract that involve access to classified information [@CL-CMP-5970]. The inserted terms include the flowdown paragraph itself but leave out any reference to the Changes clause [@CL-CMP-5970]. A subcontract with no classified access is outside that duty [@CL-CMP-5970].

### Alternate I: change that makes continued performance impracticable

Paragraph (e) of Alternate I applies if a change in security requirements, as provided in paragraphs (b) and (c), results in a change in the security classification of the contract or any of its elements from an unclassified status or a lower classification to a higher classification [@CL-CMP-6647]. It also applies if the change results in more restrictive area controls than previously required [@CL-CMP-6648]. The contractor must then exert every reasonable effort compatible with its established policies to continue performance in compliance with the change [@CL-CMP-6649].

If, despite reasonable efforts, the contractor determines that continuing the work is not practicable because of the change, it must notify the contracting officer in writing [@CL-CMP-6650]. Until the contracting officer resolves the problem, the contractor must continue safeguarding all classified material as the contract requires [@CL-CMP-6651].

After receiving the written notification, the contracting officer must explore the circumstances surrounding the proposed change [@CL-CMP-6652]. The contracting officer must also endeavor to work out a mutually satisfactory method whereby the contractor can continue performance [@CL-CMP-6653].

Paragraph (g) applies if, 15 days after the contracting officer receives the notification, the application of the change to the contract has not been withdrawn [@CL-CMP-6654], or if a mutually satisfactory method for continuing performance has not been agreed upon [@CL-CMP-6655]. The contractor may then request the contracting officer to terminate the contract in whole or in part [@CL-CMP-6656]. The paragraph also says that the contracting officer shall terminate the contract in whole or in part, as may be appropriate, and that the termination is deemed a termination under the Termination for the Convenience of the Government clause [@CL-CMP-6657].

### Alternate II: employee identification

Alternate II adds a paragraph (e) that is a different paragraph from the one in Alternate I [@CL-CMP-6658; @CL-CMP-6647]. The contractor is responsible for furnishing to each employee, and for requiring each employee engaged on the work to display, such identification as the contracting officer approves and directs [@CL-CMP-6659]. All prescribed identification must immediately be delivered to the contracting officer, for cancellation, upon the release of any employee [@CL-CMP-6660]. When the contracting officer requires it, the contractor must obtain and submit fingerprints of all persons employed or to be employed on the project [@CL-CMP-6661].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text from February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. The RFO text prints section 52.204-2 as reserved [@CL-CMP-5664] and prints the Security Requirements clause at section 52.240-92 instead [@CL-CMP-5665]. That clause keeps the rule that it applies to the extent that the contract involves access to information classified Confidential, Secret, or Top Secret [@CL-CMP-5665; @CL-CMP-5966], and the rule that the contractor must comply with the Security Agreement and with any revisions to the manual of which it has been given notice [@CL-CMP-5666; @CL-CMP-5967]. It keeps the conditions of paragraph (c) [@CL-CMP-5667; @CL-CMP-5968], the equitable adjustment as if the changes were directed under the Changes clause [@CL-CMP-5668; @CL-CMP-5969], and the flowdown, without any reference to the Changes clause, to subcontracts that involve access to classified information [@CL-CMP-5669; @CL-CMP-5970]. The RFO clause adds a paragraph (e) on CAGE codes. It says that a subcontractor requiring access to classified information under a contract is to be identified with a CAGE code on the DD Form 254 [@CL-CMP-6203], and that the contractor must require such a subcontractor to provide its CAGE code with its name and location address or otherwise include it prominently in the proposal [@CL-CMP-6204]. Each location of subcontractor performance listed on the DD Form 254 must reflect a corresponding unique CAGE code, unless the work is being performed at a Government facility, in which case the agency location code is used [@CL-CMP-6205]. The CAGE code must be for that name and location address, the word CAGE is inserted before the number, and the code is required prior to award [@CL-CMP-6206]. The contractor must also ensure that subcontractors maintain their CAGE codes throughout the life of the contract [@CL-CMP-5670]. Alternate I adds paragraphs (f), (g) and (h) where the codified Alternate I adds (e), (f) and (g) [@CL-CMP-5671; @CL-CMP-6646]. Its paragraph (f)(1) keeps the conditions of a change to a higher classification [@CL-CMP-5672; @CL-CMP-6647] and of more restrictive area controls than previously required, and the duty to exert every reasonable effort to continue performance [@CL-CMP-5673; @CL-CMP-6648; @CL-CMP-6649]. It prints the written notice when continuing the work is not practical, where the codified clause says not practicable [@CL-CMP-5674; @CL-CMP-6650]. It keeps the duty to keep safeguarding all classified material until the problem is resolved [@CL-CMP-5675; @CL-CMP-6651], and the duty of the contracting officer to explore the circumstances and try to work out a satisfactory method [@CL-CMP-5676; @CL-CMP-6652; @CL-CMP-6653]. Paragraph (h) keeps the 15-day conditions [@CL-CMP-5677; @CL-CMP-6654] [@CL-CMP-5678; @CL-CMP-6655], the contractor's right to request termination [@CL-CMP-5679; @CL-CMP-6656] and the termination deemed to be for the convenience of the Government [@CL-CMP-5680; @CL-CMP-6657]. Alternate II adds a paragraph (f) where the codified Alternate II adds (e) [@CL-CMP-5681; @CL-CMP-6658], and it keeps the identification and fingerprint duties [@CL-CMP-5682; @CL-CMP-6659] [@CL-CMP-5683; @CL-CMP-6660; @CL-CMP-6661].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor holds a contract with Secret access at one cleared site. After award, the Government raises one work package to Top Secret, which adds 300,000 of guarding and storage cost. This is a Government change after the contract date that increases security cost, so the contract is subject to an equitable adjustment for the added cost, worked as if the change had been directed under the Changes clause [@CL-CMP-5968; @CL-CMP-5969]. The contractor has two subcontracts. The packaging subcontract involves no classified information, so the flowdown duty does not reach it [@CL-CMP-5970]. The subcontract for the Top Secret work package involves classified access, so it must carry terms that conform substantially to the clause, without any reference to the Changes clause [@CL-CMP-5970].

A university holds a separate cost contract for research and development that carries Alternate I. A change raises the contract from unclassified to Secret, and the university decides that continuing is not practicable. It notifies the contracting officer in writing and keeps safeguarding all classified material until the contracting officer resolves the problem [@CL-CMP-6650; @CL-CMP-6651]. The contracting officer receives the notice on the 10th of the month. If the change has not been withdrawn on the 25th, which is 15 days after receipt, the university may request termination in whole or in part [@CL-CMP-6654; @CL-CMP-6656]. A termination that follows is deemed a termination for the convenience of the Government [@CL-CMP-6657].

## Never confuse

The equitable adjustment in the basic clause follows a change that the Government makes after the date of the contract [@CL-CMP-5968]. Security requirements that the contract already stated at award are not such a change [@CL-CMP-5968].

The two alternates both add a paragraph (e), but the paragraphs deal with different things: Alternate I deals with a change that makes continued performance impracticable, and Alternate II deals with employee identification [@CL-CMP-6647; @CL-CMP-6659]. The notice, exploration and termination steps come from Alternate I, and the basic clause on its own does not contain them [@CL-CMP-6646].

Where a contract includes this security requirements clause, the clause on keeping entity codes current requires the contractor to ensure that subcontractors maintain their codes [@CL-CMP-5992]; see [Commercial and Government entity code maintenance and the telecommunications representation](cmp-cage-code-and-telecommunications-representation.md). The safeguarding duties themselves are on [Classified information safeguarding duties](../audit-retention/cmp-classified-information-safeguarding-duties.md).

The codified Alternates I and II add paragraphs (e), (f) and (g) and a paragraph (e) [@CL-CMP-6646; @CL-CMP-6658], but the RFO Alternates add paragraphs (f), (g) and (h) and a paragraph (f), and the RFO basic clause has its own paragraph (e) [@CL-CMP-5671; @CL-CMP-5681; @CL-CMP-5670]. The codified text puts the duty to keep subcontractor entity codes in a separate clause that applies where the contract includes the security requirements clause [@CL-CMP-5992], while the RFO text prints section 52.204-18 as reserved [@CL-CMP-6207] and prints that duty in the security requirements clause itself [@CL-CMP-5670].
