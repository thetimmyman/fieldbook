## Purpose

Shows the E-Verify requirements that contracting officers put into solicitations and contracts, which employees the contractor must verify, and the listed exceptions.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-3288] |
| Codified citation | FAR 22.1800, FAR 22.1801 and FAR 22.1802 [@CL-CMP-9529; @CL-CMP-3292; @CL-CMP-3288] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-3288] |
| Applies to | Federal contractors under solicitations and contracts that include the E-Verify requirements [@CL-CMP-3288] |

The subpart prescribes policies and procedures requiring contractors to utilize the employment eligibility verification program (E-Verify) of the Department of Homeland Security, United States Citizenship and Immigration Service, as the means for verifying employment eligibility of certain employees [@CL-CMP-9529].

Contracting officers shall include in solicitations and contracts, as prescribed, requirements that Federal contractors must enroll as Federal contractors in E-Verify [@CL-CMP-3288]. One listed requirement is that contractors use E-Verify to verify employment eligibility of all new hires working in the United States, except that the contractor may choose to verify only new hires assigned to the contract if it is an institution of higher education [@CL-CMP-3289]. Two further listed contractors with that choice are a State or local government or the government of a Federally recognized Indian tribe, and a surety performing under a takeover agreement entered into with a Federal agency pursuant to a performance bond [@CL-CMP-3290]. Another listed requirement is that the contractor use E-Verify to verify employment eligibility of all employees assigned to the contract [@CL-CMP-3291]. A further listed requirement is that the contractor include these requirements, as required by the clause at 52.222-54, in subcontracts for services, except for commercial services that are part of the purchase of a COTS item (or an item that would be a COTS item, but for minor modifications), performed by the COTS provider, and are normally provided for that COTS item, and in subcontracts for construction [@CL-CMP-8723] [@CL-CMP-3368].

An employee assigned to the contract is an employee hired after November 6, 1986 (after November 27, 2009, in the Commonwealth of the Northern Mariana Islands) who is directly performing work, in the United States, under a contract required to include the clause prescribed at 22.1803 [@CL-CMP-3292] [@CL-CMP-8724]. An employee is not considered to be directly performing work under a contract if the employee normally performs support work, such as indirect or overhead functions, and does not perform any substantial duties applicable to the contract [@CL-CMP-3370]. The contractor is not required to verify employment eligibility of employees who hold an active security clearance of confidential, secret, or top secret [@CL-CMP-3293]. The contractor is also not required to verify employment eligibility of employees for whom background investigations have been completed and credentials issued pursuant to Homeland Security Presidential Directive (HSPD)-12 [@CL-CMP-3293; @CL-CMP-3369]. In exceptional cases, the head of the contracting activity may waive the E-Verify requirement for a contract or subcontract or a class of contracts or subcontracts, either temporarily or for the period of performance [@CL-CMP-3294].

## How it works

Contracting officers shall include the requirements in solicitations and contracts, as prescribed at 22.1803 [@CL-CMP-3288]. The requirements refer to new hires working in the United States [@CL-CMP-3289] and to employees assigned to the contract [@CL-CMP-3291], a term defined in FAR 22.1801 [@CL-CMP-3292; @CL-CMP-3370]. The contractor is not required to verify employment eligibility of employees in the two groups in FAR 22.1802(c) [@CL-CMP-3293; @CL-CMP-3369], and the head of the contracting activity may waive the E-Verify requirement in exceptional cases [@CL-CMP-3294]. This card does not draft the choice to verify existing employees, the bar on delegating the waiver authority, or termination of the memorandum of understanding.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A private company hires 40 people this year, and 10 of them are assigned to a covered contract. The answer: the company must verify all 40 new hires working in the United States and all employees assigned to the contract, unless an exception applies. A new hire who holds an active secret clearance need not be verified.

## Never confuse

One listed requirement concerns new hires working in the United States [@CL-CMP-3289], and another concerns employees assigned to the contract [@CL-CMP-3291]. The head of the contracting activity may waive the E-Verify requirement in exceptional cases [@CL-CMP-3294]. The contractor is not required to verify employment eligibility of employees who hold an active security clearance of confidential, secret, or top secret, or of employees for whom background investigations have been completed and credentials issued pursuant to HSPD-12 [@CL-CMP-3293; @CL-CMP-3369].
