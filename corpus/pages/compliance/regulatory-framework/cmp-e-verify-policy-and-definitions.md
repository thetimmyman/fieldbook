## Purpose

Shows the E-Verify requirements that contracting officers put into solicitations and contracts, which employees the contractor must verify, and the listed exceptions.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-3288] |
| Codified citation | FAR 22.1801 and FAR 22.1802 [@CL-CMP-3292; @CL-CMP-3288] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-3288] |
| Applies to | Federal contractors under solicitations and contracts that include the E-Verify requirements [@CL-CMP-3288] |

Contracting officers shall include in solicitations and contracts, as prescribed, requirements that Federal contractors must enroll as Federal contractors in E-Verify [@CL-CMP-3288]. One listed requirement is that contractors use E-Verify to verify employment eligibility of all new hires working in the United States, except that the contractor may choose to verify only new hires assigned to the contract if it is an institution of higher education [@CL-CMP-3289]. Two further listed contractors with that choice are a State or local government or the government of a Federally recognized Indian tribe, and a surety performing under a takeover agreement entered into with a Federal agency pursuant to a performance bond [@CL-CMP-3290]. Another listed requirement is that the contractor use E-Verify to verify employment eligibility of all employees assigned to the contract [@CL-CMP-3291]. The listed requirements also reach subcontracts for services, except for certain commercial services performed by the COTS provider, and subcontracts for construction [@CL-CMP-3368].

An employee assigned to the contract is an employee hired after November 6, 1986 (after November 27, 2009, in the Commonwealth of the Northern Mariana Islands) who is directly performing work, in the United States, under a contract required to include the clause [@CL-CMP-3292]. An employee is not considered to be directly performing work under a contract if the employee normally performs support work, such as indirect or overhead functions, and does not perform any substantial duties applicable to the contract [@CL-CMP-3370]. The contractor is not required to verify employment eligibility of employees who hold an active security clearance of confidential, secret, or top secret [@CL-CMP-3293]. The contractor is also not required to verify employment eligibility of employees for whom background investigations have been completed and credentials issued pursuant to Homeland Security Presidential Directive (HSPD)-12 [@CL-CMP-3293; @CL-CMP-3369]. In exceptional cases, the head of the contracting activity may waive the E-Verify requirement for a contract or subcontract or a class of contracts or subcontracts, either temporarily or for the period of performance [@CL-CMP-3294].

## How it works

The contracting officer puts the requirements into the solicitation or contract, and the contractor enrolls and then verifies the listed employees [@CL-CMP-3288; @CL-CMP-3289; @CL-CMP-3291]. Which employees are covered turns on the new hire rule and on the definition of an employee assigned to the contract [@CL-CMP-3289; @CL-CMP-3292; @CL-CMP-3370]. The exceptions drafted here are the two excepted groups of employees and a waiver by the head of the contracting activity [@CL-CMP-3293; @CL-CMP-3369; @CL-CMP-3294]. The printed sections also cover the choice to verify existing employees, the bar on delegating the waiver authority, and termination of the memorandum of understanding, and this card does not draft them.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A private company hires 40 people this year, and 10 of them are assigned to a covered contract. The answer: the company must verify all 40 new hires working in the United States and all employees assigned to the contract, unless an exception applies. A new hire who holds an active secret clearance need not be verified.

## Never confuse

The new hire rule and the assigned employee rule are two separate listed requirements: the first covers new hires working in the United States [@CL-CMP-3289], and the second covers employees assigned to the contract [@CL-CMP-3291]. A waiver is not an exception. A waiver is granted by the head of the contracting activity in exceptional cases [@CL-CMP-3294], while the clearance carve-out and the background investigation carve-out are stated in the section itself [@CL-CMP-3293; @CL-CMP-3369].

The clause text is covered in [Employment eligibility verification with E-Verify](cmp-employment-eligibility-verification.md), and the clause and provision prescriptions are covered in [Clause and provision prescriptions for trafficking in persons and employment eligibility](cmp-trafficking-and-employment-eligibility-prescriptions.md).
