## Purpose

Shows which employees the E-Verify clause reaches, when enrollment and verification start for a contractor that is not yet enrolled and for one that is, what is excused, and how the duty flows into subcontracts.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-5255] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.222-54 [@CL-CMP-5255] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.222-54 [@CL-CMP-1091] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-5255] |
| Applies to | Contracts that include the clause, and subcontracts for services (other than the COTS commercial services the clause excepts) or construction that meet the value and place-of-performance tests [@CL-CMP-5262; @CL-CMP-5263] |

A contractor not enrolled as a Federal Contractor in E-Verify at the time of contract award must enroll within 30 calendar days of contract award [@CL-CMP-5256]. Employee assigned to the contract means an employee who was hired after November 6, 1986 (after November 27, 2009, in the Commonwealth of the Northern Mariana Islands), who is directly performing work, in the United States, under a contract that is required to include the prescribed clause [@CL-CMP-5255]. An employee is not considered to be directly performing work under a contract if the employee normally performs support work, such as indirect or overhead functions, and does not perform any substantial duties applicable to the contract [@CL-CMP-5391]. The United States, as defined in section 1101(a)(38) of title 8 of the United States Code, means the 50 States, the District of Columbia, Puerto Rico, Guam, the Commonwealth of the Northern Mariana Islands and the United States Virgin Islands [@CL-CMP-5768].

## How it works

A contractor that is not enrolled at award has two verification duties after it enrolls. Within 90 calendar days of enrollment it must begin using E-Verify to initiate verification of all new hires working in the United States, whether or not assigned to the contract, within 3 business days after the date of hire (but see paragraph (b)(3) of the section) [@CL-CMP-5257; @CL-CMP-0481]. For each employee assigned to the contract, it must initiate verification within 90 calendar days after the date of enrollment or within 30 calendar days of the employee's assignment to the contract, whichever date is later (but see paragraph (b)(4)) [@CL-CMP-5258].

A contractor that is already enrolled at award must use E-Verify to initiate verification of either all new employees [@CL-CMP-5392] or the employees assigned to the contract [@CL-CMP-5395; @CL-CMP-5396]. If it has been enrolled 90 calendar days or more, it must initiate verification of all new hires working in the United States, whether or not assigned to the contract, within 3 business days after the date of hire (but see paragraph (b)(3)) [@CL-CMP-5393]. If it has been enrolled less than 90 calendar days, it must, within 90 calendar days after enrollment, initiate verification of all new hires working in the United States, whether or not assigned to the contract [@CL-CMP-5394]. Those new-hire verifications must be initiated within 3 business days after the date of hire (but see paragraph (b)(3)) [@CL-CMP-5395]. For each employee assigned to the contract, it must initiate verification within 90 calendar days after the date of contract award or within 30 days after assignment to the contract, whichever date is later (but see paragraph (b)(4)) [@CL-CMP-5396].

A contractor that is an institution of higher education, a State or local government, the government of a Federally recognized Indian tribe, or a surety performing under a takeover agreement entered into with a Federal agency pursuant to a performance bond [@CL-CMP-5397] may choose to verify only employees assigned to the contract, whether existing employees or new hires [@CL-CMP-5259]. It follows the verification requirements of paragraph (b)(1) or (b)(2), respectively, except that any requirement to verify new employees applies only to new employees assigned to the contract [@CL-CMP-5398].

The contractor may elect to verify all existing employees hired after November 6, 1986 (after November 27, 2009, in the Commonwealth of the Northern Mariana Islands), rather than just those employees assigned to the contract [@CL-CMP-5260]. A contractor that makes this election must initiate verification for each existing employee working in the United States who was hired after those dates within 180 calendar days of enrollment in the E-Verify program or of notification to E-Verify Operations of the Contractor's decision to exercise this option, using the contact information provided in the E-Verify program Memorandum of Understanding [@CL-CMP-5399; @CL-CMP-5400].

The clause does not require additional E-Verify verification of an employee whose employment eligibility the contractor previously verified through the E-Verify program [@CL-CMP-5261], who has been granted and holds an active Government security clearance for access to confidential, secret or top secret information under the National Industrial Security Program Operating Manual [@CL-CMP-5401], or who has undergone a completed background investigation and been issued credentials under Homeland Security Presidential Directive 12 [@CL-CMP-5402].

The contractor must include the requirements of the clause, including its subcontract paragraph and appropriately modified for identification of the parties, in each subcontract that is for services, other than commercial services performed by the COTS provider that are part of the purchase of a COTS item, or of an item that would be one but for minor modifications, and normally provided for that item, or for construction [@CL-CMP-5403; @CL-CMP-5262], that has a value of more than $3,500, and that includes work performed in the United States [@CL-CMP-5263].

A subcontract is any contract, as defined in FAR 2.101, entered into by a subcontractor to furnish supplies or services for performance of a prime contract or a subcontract, and it includes but is not limited to purchase orders and changes and modifications to purchase orders [@CL-CMP-5766]. A subcontractor is any supplier, distributor, vendor or firm that furnishes supplies or services to or for a prime contractor or another subcontractor [@CL-CMP-5767]. A commercially available off-the-shelf item means any item of supply that is a commercial product as defined in paragraph (1) of the FAR 2.101 definition of commercial product and is sold in substantial quantities in the commercial marketplace [@CL-CMP-5764], and is offered to the Government, without modification, in the same form in which it is sold in the commercial marketplace; it does not include bulk cargo as defined in title 46 of the United States Code, such as agricultural products and petroleum products [@CL-CMP-5765].

The contractor must comply, for the period of performance of the contract, with the requirements of the E-Verify program Memorandum of Understanding [@CL-CMP-5769]. The Department of Homeland Security or the Social Security Administration may terminate the contractor's Memorandum of Understanding and deny access to the E-Verify system in accordance with its terms, and in that case the contractor will be referred to a suspending and debarring official [@CL-CMP-5770]. During the period between termination of the Memorandum of Understanding and the suspending and debarring official's decision whether to suspend or debar, the contractor is excused from its obligations under paragraph (b) of the clause [@CL-CMP-5771]. If the contractor is not suspended, debarred or subject to a voluntary exclusion, it must reenroll in E-Verify [@CL-CMP-5772].

Information on registration for and use of the E-Verify program can be obtained through the Internet at the Department of Homeland Security Web site, https://www.e-Verify.gov [@CL-CMP-5773].

Class Deviation 2026-O0038 directs DoD contracting officers to use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text, effective February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. Under RFO FAR 52.222-54(b)(1)(i), a contractor not enrolled as a Federal Contractor in E-Verify at time of contract award must enroll as a Federal Contractor in the E-Verify program within 30 calendar days of contract award [@CL-CMP-1091]. Under RFO FAR 52.222-54(b)(1)(ii), the step listed is, within 90 calendar days of enrollment in the E-Verify program, to begin to use E-Verify to initiate verification of employment eligibility of all new hires of the contractor who are working in the United States, whether or not assigned to the contract, within 3 business days after the date of hire [@CL-CMP-1092], with the pointer "(but see paragraph (b)(3) of this section)" [@CL-CMP-1185]. Under RFO FAR 52.222-54(b)(1)(iii), the step listed for each employee assigned to the contract is to initiate verification within 90 calendar days after the date of enrollment or within 30 calendar days of the employee's assignment to the contract, whichever date is later (but see paragraph (b)(4) of this section) [@CL-CMP-1093]. Under RFO FAR 52.222-54(b)(2)(i)(A), a contractor enrolled 90 calendar days or more must initiate verification of all new hires of the contractor who are working in the United States, whether or not assigned to the contract, within 3 business days after the date of hire (but see paragraph (b)(3) of this section) [@CL-CMP-1094]. Under RFO FAR 52.222-54(b)(2)(i)(B), a contractor enrolled less than 90 calendar days must, within 90 calendar days after enrollment as a Federal Contractor in E-Verify, initiate verification of all new hires of the contractor who are working in the United States, whether or not assigned to the contract [@CL-CMP-1095], within 3 business days after the date of hire (but see paragraph (b)(3) of this section) [@CL-CMP-1186]. Under RFO FAR 52.222-54(b)(2)(ii), for each employee assigned to the contract, an enrolled contractor must initiate verification within 90 calendar days after the date of contract award or within 30 days after assignment to the contract, whichever date is later (but see paragraph (b)(4) of this section) [@CL-CMP-1096]. Under RFO FAR 52.222-54(b)(4), the contractor may elect to verify all existing employees hired after November 6, 1986 (after November 27, 2009, in the Commonwealth of the Northern Mariana Islands), rather than just those employees assigned to the contract [@CL-CMP-1187], and must initiate verification for each existing employee working in the United States who was hired after those dates [@CL-CMP-1188], within 180 calendar days of enrollment in the E-Verify program or of notification to E-Verify Operations of the contractor's decision to exercise this option, using the contact information provided in the E-Verify program Memorandum of Understanding [@CL-CMP-1097]. Under RFO FAR 52.222-54(b)(5)(ii), during the period between termination of the Memorandum of Understanding and a decision by the suspending and debarring official whether to suspend or debar, the contractor is excused from its obligations under paragraph (b) [@CL-CMP-1098]; if the contractor is not suspended, debarred, or subject to a voluntary exclusion, it must reenroll in E-Verify [@CL-CMP-1099]. Under RFO FAR 52.222-54(e), the contractor must include the requirements of the clause, including paragraph (e) appropriately modified for identification of the parties, in each subcontract that meets the conditions listed in paragraph (e) [@CL-CMP-1103]. One condition is that the subcontract is for services, except for commercial services that are part of the purchase of a COTS item (or an item that would be a COTS item, but for minor modifications), performed by the COTS provider, and normally provided for that COTS item, or is for construction [@CL-CMP-1189]. Two others are that the subcontract has a value of more than $3,500 and includes work performed in the United States [@CL-CMP-1104].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor wins a services contract and is not enrolled in E-Verify. It enrolls on day 20, within the 30 calendar days after award. It must begin verifying new hires within 90 calendar days of enrollment, so by day 110, and from then verifies each new hire within 3 business days after the date of hire. An existing employee, hired after the cut-off date, is assigned to the contract on day 100. The verification is due on the later of day 110, which is 90 calendar days after enrollment, and day 130, which is 30 calendar days after assignment, so it is due by day 130. A purchase order worth 4,000 dollars for grounds maintenance services performed in the United States is a subcontract, because the definition includes purchase orders, and it is a services subcontract above the 3,500 dollar test, so the clause goes into it; a janitorial services purchase order worth 3,000 dollars is below the value test and does not need it. Later the Department of Homeland Security terminates the contractor's Memorandum of Understanding and the contractor is referred to a suspending and debarring official. Until that official decides whether to suspend or debar, the contractor is excused from its obligations under paragraph (b). If it is then not suspended, debarred or subject to a voluntary exclusion, it must reenroll in E-Verify.

## Never confuse

A contractor that is not enrolled as a Federal Contractor in E-Verify at the time of contract award must enroll within 30 calendar days of contract award [@CL-CMP-5256], and must within 90 calendar days of enrollment begin to use E-Verify to initiate verification of all new hires working in the United States, whether or not assigned to the contract, within 3 business days after the date of hire (but see paragraph (b)(3) of the section) [@CL-CMP-5257; @CL-CMP-0481]. For each employee assigned to the contract, it must initiate verification within 90 calendar days after the date of enrollment or within 30 calendar days of the employee's assignment to the contract, whichever date is later (but see paragraph (b)(4) of the section) [@CL-CMP-5258]. The Contractor is not required by the clause to perform additional employment verification using E-Verify for any employee whose employment eligibility was previously verified by the Contractor through the E-Verify program [@CL-CMP-5261], who has been granted and holds an active United States Government security clearance for access to confidential, secret, or top secret information in accordance with the National Industrial Security Program Operating Manual [@CL-CMP-5401], or who has undergone a completed background investigation and been issued credentials pursuant to Homeland Security Presidential Directive (HSPD)-12, Policy for a Common Identification Standard for Federal Employees and Contractors [@CL-CMP-5402].

The policy sections on who must verify, which employees and the exceptions are covered in [E-Verify: who must verify, which employees, and the exceptions](cmp-e-verify-policy-and-definitions.md).
