## Purpose

Shows what an offeror represents under the FASCSA representation provision, how the offeror and the contractor search for orders, and the reporting deadlines in the prohibition clause.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-3570] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.204-28; FAR 52.204-29; FAR 52.204-30 [@CL-CMP-3599; @CL-CMP-3572; @CL-CMP-3573] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.240-90 [@CL-CMP-1232] and RFO FAR 52.240-91 [@CL-CMP-1237] carry the provision and clause topics; the RFO text lists FAR 52.204-28, FAR 52.204-29 and FAR 52.204-30 as reserved [@CL-CMP-1246; @CL-CMP-1247; @CL-CMP-1248] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-3570] |
| Applies to | Offerors on solicitations with the representation provision, and contractors under contracts with the prohibition clause [@CL-CMP-3570; @CL-CMP-3573] |

Contractors are prohibited from providing or using as part of the performance of the contract any covered article, or any products or services produced or provided by a source, if the prohibition is set out in an applicable FASCSA order [@CL-CMP-3595]. By submission of its offer, the offeror represents that it has conducted a reasonable inquiry [@CL-CMP-3570], and that it does not propose to provide or use in response to the solicitation any covered article, or any products or services produced or provided by a source [@CL-CMP-1296]. The representation covers a covered article, or products or services produced or provided by a source, if the covered article or the source is prohibited by an applicable FASCSA order in effect on the date the solicitation was issued, except as waived by the solicitation or as disclosed in the disclosure paragraph [@CL-CMP-3571]. FASCSA orders issued after the date of solicitation do not apply unless added by an amendment to the solicitation [@CL-CMP-3596].

The offeror shall search for the phrase FASCSA order in SAM for any covered article, or any products or services produced or provided by a source, if there is an applicable FASCSA order described in the prohibition paragraph of the prohibition clause [@CL-CMP-3572]. During contract performance, the contractor shall review SAM at least once every three months, or as advised by the Contracting Officer, to check for covered articles subject to FASCSA orders, or for products or services produced by a source subject to FASCSA orders, not currently identified under paragraph (b) of the clause [@CL-CMP-3573; @CL-CMP-7771]. Under the prohibition clause, a FASCSA order issued after the date of solicitation applies to the contract only if added by an amendment to the solicitation or by a modification to the contract [@CL-CMP-3600].

## How it works

The contractor's reporting duty under the prohibition clause is triggered if the contractor identifies, including through any notification by a subcontractor at any tier, that a covered article or product or service produced or provided by a source was provided to the Government or used during contract performance and is subject to a FASCSA order [@CL-CMP-3597], that is, one identified in paragraph (b) of the clause or a new FASCSA order identified in paragraph (c)(2) of the clause [@CL-CMP-7772]. One listed reporting deadline requires the contractor to report, within 3 business days from the date of identification or notification, the contract number, the order numbers if applicable, and the name of the product or service, among the listed items [@CL-CMP-3574]. Within 10 business days of submitting that first set of information, the contractor shall report the further listed items, including any further available information about mitigation actions undertaken or recommended [@CL-CMP-3575]. The contractor shall insert the substance of the prohibition clause, including its subcontracts paragraph and excluding paragraph (c)(1) of the clause, in all subcontracts and other contractual instruments, including subcontracts for the acquisition of commercial products and commercial services [@CL-CMP-3598]. The codified clause also sets the recipients of the report, the disclosure for a waiver request and the contractor's duty to notify its subcontractors of orders in the solicitation that are not in SAM, and this card does not set those out. A third clause, which the contracting officer inserts in Federal Supply Schedules, Governmentwide acquisition contracts and multi-agency contracts where FASCSA orders are applied at the order level [@CL-CMP-2979], requires the contractor during contract performance to comply with any of the following that apply: DHS FASCSA orders, DoD FASCSA orders or DNI FASCSA orders [@CL-CMP-3599]. The definitions and the order types are on [FASCSA definitions and orders](../trade-cyber/cmp-fascsa-definitions-and-orders.md). The offeror representations and report deadlines of related prohibition clauses are on [Covered telecommunications: the offeror representations and the contractor reports](cmp-covered-telecommunications-representation-and-reporting.md) and [Kaspersky and ByteDance prohibitions: what is barred, reported and flowed down](cmp-kaspersky-and-bytedance-prohibitions.md).

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text because Class Deviation 2026-O0038 directs it, effective February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. The RFO text lists FAR 52.204-28, FAR 52.204-29 and FAR 52.204-30 as reserved [@CL-CMP-1246; @CL-CMP-1247; @CL-CMP-1248]. The offeror's search for FASCSA orders and the FASCSA representation are in RFO FAR 52.240-90, where the search paragraph points to paragraph (e) of FAR 52.240-91 [@CL-CMP-1232; @CL-CMP-3572]. The RFO text keeps the rule that FASCSA orders issued after the date of solicitation do not apply unless added by an amendment to the solicitation [@CL-CMP-1233; @CL-CMP-3596]. In RFO FAR 52.240-90(d), by submission of the offer the offeror represents that it has conducted a reasonable inquiry and does not propose to provide or use in response to this solicitation any covered article, or any products or services produced or provided by a source [@CL-CMP-1234], and the codified provision has the same representation [@CL-CMP-3570; @CL-CMP-1296]. The RFO representation covers such an article or source if it is prohibited by an applicable FASCSA order in effect on the date the solicitation was issued, except as waived by the solicitation or as disclosed in paragraph (g) [@CL-CMP-1235], where the codified provision says the exception is as disclosed in paragraph (e) [@CL-CMP-3571]. The RFO provision itself says that a reasonable inquiry will look at any information in the offeror's possession but does not need to include an internal or third-party audit [@CL-CMP-1236], while the codified provision takes the meaning of reasonable inquiry from a definition in FAR 52.204-30(a), which says the definition applies when the covered article or the source is subject to an applicable FASCSA order and excludes the need to include an internal or third-party audit [@CL-CMP-1297]. In the RFO provision an offeror that is not able to represent compliance with the prohibitions in paragraphs (c) or (d) shall disclose within 72 hours to the contracting office identified in paragraph (g)(2) [@CL-CMP-1298], where the codified provision has the offeror provide the disclosure information as part of the offer [@CL-CMP-1299]. Under RFO FAR 52.240-91(e)(1), unless the Government has issued an applicable waiver, contractors shall not provide or use as part of the performance of the contract any covered article, or any products or services produced or provided by a source, that is prohibited by an applicable FASCSA order [@CL-CMP-1237]; DoD FASCSA orders apply to solicitations and contracts awarded by a Department of Defense contracting office and DHS FASCSA orders to all others [@CL-CMP-1238]. It keeps the review of SAM at least once every three months, or as advised by the Contracting Officer [@CL-CMP-1239; @CL-CMP-3573], and says the review checks for covered articles or sources subject to FASCSA orders not currently identified under paragraph (e) of the clause [@CL-CMP-1240]. It says a FASCSA order issued after the date of solicitation applies to the contract only if added by an amendment to the solicitation or modification to the contract [@CL-CMP-1241; @CL-CMP-3600]. Under RFO paragraph (i)(3) the contractor submits a report to the contracting office identified in paragraph (h)(2) if it identifies, including through any notification by a subcontractor at any tier, a covered article or product or service produced or provided by a source that was provided to the Government or used during contract performance and is subject to a FASCSA order [@CL-CMP-1242; @CL-CMP-1243; @CL-CMP-3597]. RFO paragraph (h)(1) separately gives the contractor a duty to report if it identifies or is notified by any source, including a subcontractor at any tier, that any product or service provided or used, or to be provided or used, during contract performance does not comply with any prohibition in the clause [@CL-CMP-1300]. RFO paragraph (i)(3) says the contractor reports the listed information within 72 hours [@CL-CMP-1244], and paragraph (h)(1) says the contractor then reports the listed information, or as much of it as is known, within 72 hours [@CL-CMP-1301]; the codified clause sets 3 business days from the date of identification or notification for the first information and 10 business days from submitting it for further mitigation information [@CL-CMP-3574; @CL-CMP-3575]. RFO paragraph (j) says the contractor shall insert the substance of the clause in all subcontracts and other contractual instruments, including subcontracts for acquiring commercial products or commercial services, but excluding subparagraphs (d)(1) and (i)(1), where the codified clause excludes paragraph (c)(1) [@CL-CMP-1245; @CL-CMP-3598].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An offeror searches SAM for FASCSA orders before it bids and reviews the solicitation for orders that are not in SAM. During performance, the contractor reviews SAM every three months and finds a new order. Its inquiry shows that a covered item was used on the contract. It reports the contract number, order number and product name within 3 business days, and sends the mitigation details within 10 business days after that. If the contract carried the RFO clause instead of the codified one, the report would be due within 72 hours [@CL-CMP-1244].

## Never confuse

The offeror shall search for the phrase FASCSA order in SAM [@CL-CMP-3572], and the contractor shall review SAM at least once every three months, or as advised by the Contracting Officer, during contract performance [@CL-CMP-3573]. The offeror's representation covers a covered article or source prohibited by an applicable FASCSA order in effect on the date the solicitation was issued [@CL-CMP-3571]. The first information is due within 3 business days from the date of identification or notification [@CL-CMP-3574], and further information within 10 business days of submitting it [@CL-CMP-3575].

The codified clause sets two reporting periods, 3 business days and 10 business days [@CL-CMP-3574; @CL-CMP-3575], while the RFO clause sets 72 hours for its reports [@CL-CMP-1244; @CL-CMP-1301].
