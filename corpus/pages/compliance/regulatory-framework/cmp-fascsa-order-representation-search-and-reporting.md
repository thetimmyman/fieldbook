## Purpose

Shows what an offeror represents under the FASCSA representation provision, how the offeror and the contractor search for orders, and the reporting deadlines in the prohibition clause.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-3570] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.204-28; FAR 52.204-29; FAR 52.204-30 [@CL-CMP-3599; @CL-CMP-3572; @CL-CMP-3573] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-3570] |
| Applies to | Offerors on solicitations with the representation provision, and contractors under contracts with the prohibition clause [@CL-CMP-3570; @CL-CMP-3573] |

Contractors are prohibited from providing or using as part of the performance of the contract any covered article, or any products or services produced or provided by a source, if the prohibition is set out in an applicable FASCSA order [@CL-CMP-3595]. By submission of its offer, the offeror represents that it has conducted a reasonable inquiry [@CL-CMP-3570]. The representation covers a covered article, or products or services produced or provided by a source, if the covered article or the source is prohibited by an applicable FASCSA order in effect on the date the solicitation was issued, except as waived by the solicitation or as disclosed in the disclosure paragraph [@CL-CMP-3571]. FASCSA orders issued after the date of solicitation do not apply unless added by an amendment to the solicitation [@CL-CMP-3596].

The offeror shall search for the phrase FASCSA order in SAM for any covered article, or any products or services produced or provided by a source, if there is an applicable FASCSA order described in the prohibition paragraph of the prohibition clause [@CL-CMP-3572]. During contract performance, the contractor shall review SAM at least once every three months, or as advised by the Contracting Officer, to check for covered articles or sources subject to FASCSA orders not currently identified [@CL-CMP-3573]. Under the prohibition clause, a FASCSA order issued after the date of solicitation applies to the contract only if added by an amendment to the solicitation or by a modification to the contract [@CL-CMP-3600].

## How it works

The contractor's reporting duty under the prohibition clause is triggered if the contractor identifies, including through any notification by a subcontractor at any tier, that a covered article or product or service produced or provided by a source was provided to the Government or used during contract performance and is subject to a FASCSA order [@CL-CMP-3597]. One listed reporting deadline requires the contractor to report, within 3 business days from the date of identification or notification, the contract number, the order numbers if applicable, and the name of the product or service, among the listed items [@CL-CMP-3574]. Within 10 business days of submitting that first set of information, the contractor shall report any further available information about mitigation actions undertaken or recommended [@CL-CMP-3575]. The contractor shall insert the substance of the prohibition clause, including its subcontracts paragraph and excluding paragraph (c)(1) of the clause, in all subcontracts and other contractual instruments, including subcontracts for the acquisition of commercial products and commercial services [@CL-CMP-3598]. The clause also sets the recipients of the report, the disclosure for a waiver request and the contractor's duty to notify its subcontractors of orders in the solicitation that are not in SAM, and this card does not set those out. A third clause, for Federal Supply Schedules, governmentwide acquisition contracts and multi-agency contracts, requires the contractor during performance to comply with any DHS, DoD or DNI FASCSA orders that apply [@CL-CMP-3599]; its removal rule is not set out here. The definitions and the order types are on [FASCSA definitions and orders](../trade-cyber/cmp-fascsa-definitions-and-orders.md). The offeror representations and report deadlines of related prohibition clauses are on [Covered telecommunications: the offeror representations and the contractor reports](cmp-covered-telecommunications-representation-and-reporting.md) and [Kaspersky and ByteDance prohibitions: what is barred, reported and flowed down](cmp-kaspersky-and-bytedance-prohibitions.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An offeror searches SAM for FASCSA orders before it bids and reviews the solicitation for orders that are not in SAM. During performance, the contractor reviews SAM every three months and finds a new order. Its inquiry shows that a covered item was used on the contract. It reports the contract number, order number and product name within 3 business days, and sends the mitigation details within 10 business days after that.

## Never confuse

The offeror's search belongs to the provision answered with the offer [@CL-CMP-3572], while the contractor's repeating review during performance belongs to the clause [@CL-CMP-3573]. The offeror's representation depends on orders in effect on the date the solicitation was issued [@CL-CMP-3571]. The two report deadlines differ: 3 business days and 10 business days [@CL-CMP-3574; @CL-CMP-3575].
