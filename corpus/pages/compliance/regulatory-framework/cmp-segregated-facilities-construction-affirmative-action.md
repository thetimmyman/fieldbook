## Purpose

Shows what the segregated facilities prohibition bans, how the construction affirmative action goals and the subcontract notice work, and when a preaward on-site compliance evaluation applies.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-5876] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.222-21, 52.222-23 and 52.222-24 [@CL-CMP-5876; @CL-CMP-5880; @CL-CMP-5882] |
| RFO citation | RFO part 52 prints FAR 52.222-21, 52.222-23 and 52.222-24 as reserved [@CL-CMP-5684; @CL-CMP-5685; @CL-CMP-5686] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-5876] |
| Applies to | Solicitations carrying the construction affirmative action notice or the preaward evaluation provision, and subcontracts subject to the equal opportunity clause [@CL-CMP-5880; @CL-CMP-5882; @CL-CMP-5879] |

The prohibition operates as the contractor's agreement: it does not and will not maintain or provide segregated facilities for its employees at any of its establishments, and it does not and will not permit its employees to perform services at a location under its control where segregated facilities are maintained [@CL-CMP-5876]. The contractor agrees that a breach of the clause is a violation of the equal opportunity clause in the contract [@CL-CMP-6370].

The definition covers any waiting rooms, work areas, rest rooms and wash rooms, restaurants and other eating areas, time clocks, locker rooms and other storage or dressing areas, parking lots, drinking fountains, recreation or entertainment areas, transportation, and housing facilities provided for employees, when they are segregated as the definition describes [@CL-CMP-6369]. Those facilities are segregated facilities when they are segregated by explicit directive or are in fact segregated on the basis of race, color, religion, sex, sexual orientation, gender identity or national origin because of written or oral policies or employee custom [@CL-CMP-5877]. Separate or single-user rest rooms, and necessary dressing or sleeping areas provided to assure privacy between the sexes, are not segregated facilities [@CL-CMP-5878].

## How it works

The contractor shall include the clause in every subcontract and purchase order that is subject to the equal opportunity clause of the contract [@CL-CMP-5879].

The construction notice expresses the goals for minority and female participation in percentage terms for the contractor's aggregate workforce in each trade on all construction work in the covered area [@CL-CMP-5880]. The goals for minority participation and for female participation in each trade are blanks that the contracting officer fills in, so they are contract-specific [@CL-CMP-6371]. The covered area is likewise a blank in which the contracting officer inserts a description of the geographical areas where the contract is to be performed, giving the State, county and city [@CL-CMP-6630]. The goals are applicable to all the contractor's construction work performed in the covered area [@CL-CMP-6626]. If the contractor performs construction work in a geographical area outside the covered area, it must apply the goals established for the geographical area where the work is actually performed [@CL-CMP-6627]. Goals are published periodically in the Federal Register in notice form, and the notices may be obtained from any Office of Federal Contract Compliance Programs office [@CL-CMP-6628]. Under the notice, the contractor's compliance with Executive Order 11246, as amended, and the regulations in 41 CFR 60-4 [@CL-CMP-7049] shall be based on (1) its implementation of the equal opportunity clause, (2) the specific affirmative action obligations required by the construction affirmative action clause, and (3) its efforts to meet the goals [@CL-CMP-6372]. The hours of minority and female employment and training must be substantially uniform throughout the length of the contract and in each trade, and the contractor must make a good faith effort to employ minorities and women evenly on each of its projects [@CL-CMP-6373]. Transferring minority or female employees or trainees from contractor to contractor, or from project to project, for the sole purpose of meeting the contractor's goals shall be a violation of the contract, Executive Order 11246, as amended, and 41 CFR 60-4 [@CL-CMP-6374]. Compliance with the goals is measured against the total work hours performed [@CL-CMP-6375].

Within 10 working days after awarding any construction subcontract in excess of $10,000 at any tier for construction work, the contractor must give written notification to the Deputy Assistant Secretary for Federal Contract Compliance of the Department of Labor [@CL-CMP-5881]. That duty covers a construction subcontract for construction work under the contract resulting from the solicitation [@CL-CMP-6629]. The written notification lists the subcontractor's name, address and telephone number, the subcontractor's employer identification number, and the estimated dollar amount of the subcontract [@CL-CMP-6376]. It also lists the estimated starting and completion dates of the subcontract and the geographical area in which the subcontract is to be performed [@CL-CMP-6377].

Where a contract of $10 million or more will result from a solicitation, the prospective contractor and its known first-tier subcontractors with anticipated subcontracts of $10 million or more shall be subject to a preaward compliance evaluation by the Office of Federal Contract Compliance Programs (OFCCP) [@CL-CMP-5882; @CL-CMP-7051], unless, within the preceding 24 months, the Office of Federal Contract Compliance Programs has conducted an evaluation and found the prospective contractor and subcontractors to be in compliance with Executive Order 11246 [@CL-CMP-5883].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text from February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. The RFO text prints section 52.222-21 as reserved [@CL-CMP-5684], section 52.222-23 as reserved [@CL-CMP-5685] and section 52.222-24 as reserved [@CL-CMP-5686], while the codified text prints the segregated facilities clause, the construction goals notice and the preaward evaluation provision at those sections [@CL-CMP-5876; @CL-CMP-5880; @CL-CMP-5882].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A prime contractor awards two second-tier construction subcontracts for covered work, one worth 10,000 and one worth 12,500. Only the 12,500 subcontract is in excess of 10,000, so only that award requires written notice to the Deputy Assistant Secretary within 10 working days of the award. The notice gives the subcontractor's name, address, telephone number and employer identification number, the estimated dollar amount, the estimated starting and completion dates, and the geographical area of performance. A separate solicitation that includes the preaward provision is expected to produce a 12 million contract, so the prospective contractor and its known first-tier subcontractors with anticipated subcontracts of 10 million or more would be subject to a preaward compliance evaluation. A known first-tier subcontractor with an anticipated 8 million subcontract is below the 10 million line, while one with an anticipated 10 million subcontract is at it and falls within that group. The Office of Federal Contract Compliance Programs evaluated the prospective contractor and its subcontractors 20 months earlier and found them in compliance with Executive Order 11246, which is within the preceding 24 months, so the preaward evaluation does not apply. Had that evaluation been 30 months earlier, it would fall outside the preceding 24 months and the preaward evaluation would apply.

## Never confuse

The segregated facilities clause is not the goals clause: the segregated facilities clause is about the facilities the contractor maintains or provides for its employees, and the locations under its control where employees perform services [@CL-CMP-5876], while the construction goals are percentages for the aggregate workforce in each trade, and compliance with them is measured against total work hours performed [@CL-CMP-5880; @CL-CMP-6375]. A breach of the facilities clause is a violation of the equal opportunity clause [@CL-CMP-6370]. The rules on this page come from the codified text, and the RFO text prints the same three section numbers as reserved [@CL-CMP-5684; @CL-CMP-5685; @CL-CMP-5686; @CL-CMP-5876; @CL-CMP-5880; @CL-CMP-5882]. The equal opportunity clause is covered in [Equal opportunity duties on a covered Federal contract](cmp-equal-opportunity-clause.md), the construction affirmative action compliance clause in [Affirmative action compliance requirements for construction](cmp-affirmative-action-requirements-for-construction.md), and the policy on construction goals and preaward clearance in [Equal employment opportunity: construction goals and preaward clearance](cmp-eeo-construction-affirmative-action-and-preaward-clearance.md).
