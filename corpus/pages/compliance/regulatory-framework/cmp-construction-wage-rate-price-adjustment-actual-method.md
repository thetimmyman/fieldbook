## Purpose

Shows what the actual method adjusts when a wage determination changes, what the adjustment may and may not include, and the notice and record steps that go with a claim.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-4944] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.222-32 [@CL-CMP-4944] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.222-32 [@CL-CMP-0849] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-4944] |
| Applies to | Construction contracts where the prescription directs insertion of the actual method clause [@CL-CMP-4944] |

The clause is inserted as prescribed in FAR 22.407(g) [@CL-CMP-4944]. The wage determination that is effective for an option to extend the term applies to that option period [@CL-CMP-5138].

The Contracting Officer adjusts the contract price or the contract unit price labor rates to reflect the contractor's actual increase or decrease in wages and fringe benefits, to the extent that the increase is made to comply with, or the decrease is voluntarily made by the contractor as a result of, the causes the paragraph lists [@CL-CMP-4947]. Those causes are incorporation of the Department of Labor wage determination applicable at the exercise of an option to extend the term [@CL-CMP-5139], and incorporation of a wage determination otherwise applied to the contract by operation of law [@CL-CMP-5140].

## How it works

The contractor states that, if the contract prices contain an allowance for wage or benefit increases, that allowance will not be included in any request for adjustment [@CL-CMP-4945]. Each request carries a statement that the prices do not include any allowance for the increased cost being claimed [@CL-CMP-4946]. The adjustment covers the wage and fringe benefit change plus the accompanying social security and unemployment tax and workers' compensation insurance changes [@CL-CMP-4948]. Beyond those items, it includes no amount for general and administrative costs, overhead or profit [@CL-CMP-4949].

The contractor notifies the Contracting Officer of a claimed increase within 30 days after receiving a revised wage determination, unless the Contracting Officer extends that period in writing [@CL-CMP-4950]. It notifies the Contracting Officer promptly of any decrease, and nothing in the clause precludes the Government from asserting a claim within the period permitted by law [@CL-CMP-5141]. The notice states the amount claimed and any relevant supporting data, including payroll records the Contracting Officer may reasonably require [@CL-CMP-4951]. On agreement of the parties, the Contracting Officer modifies the price in writing [@CL-CMP-5142]. The contractor continues performance pending agreement on or determination of the adjustment and its effective date [@CL-CMP-5143].

Paragraph (f) gives the computations for schedule of indefinite-quantity work, beginning with a contract unit price per single craft hour [@CL-CMP-5144]. For each labor classification, the difference between the combined actual wage and benefit rates paid and the combined rates the new wage determination requires is added to the original unit price if it is a combined increase [@CL-CMP-5145]. A combined decrease reduces the unit price by that amount if the contractor gave the required notice [@CL-CMP-5146].

For a contract unit price that contains multiple craft hours, paragraph (f)(2) applies [@CL-CMP-5147]. A difference is computed for each labor classification, as above [@CL-CMP-5148]. It is multiplied by the actual hours expended for each craft on the unit-priced item [@CL-CMP-5149]. The product is divided by the actual units ordered in the preceding contract period, and the totals for the crafts are added to the current unit price to give the new unit price [@CL-CMP-5150]. The extended amount for the line item is the new unit price times the estimated quantity [@CL-CMP-5151]. If actual hours from the preceding period are not available, the contractor and the Contracting Officer agree on an estimate of total hours per craft per unit of work [@CL-CMP-5152]. A note in paragraph (f)(2) says that adjustment for labor rate increases or decreases may be accompanied by social security and unemployment taxes and workers' compensation insurance [@CL-CMP-5153].

Wage determination coverage and option-period pricing are on the [option wage pricing card](cmp-improper-wage-determinations-and-option-wage-pricing.md), and the [percentage method card](cmp-construction-wage-rate-price-adjustment-percentage-method.md) covers the other method.

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text from February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. The RFO clause is inserted as prescribed in FAR 22.402-4(f), where the codified clause points to FAR 22.407(g) [@CL-CMP-0897; @CL-CMP-4944]. It keeps the rule that the wage determination effective for an option to extend the term applies to that option period [@CL-CMP-0849; @CL-CMP-5138], the contractor's statement about allowances for increases [@CL-CMP-0850; @CL-CMP-4945], and the statement that goes with each request for adjustment [@CL-CMP-0888; @CL-CMP-4946]. It keeps the adjustment to reflect the contractor's actual increase or decrease in wages and fringe benefits, to the extent the increase is made to comply with, or the decrease is voluntarily made as a result of, the two causes it lists [@CL-CMP-0889; @CL-CMP-0890; @CL-CMP-0891; @CL-CMP-4947; @CL-CMP-5139; @CL-CMP-5140]. It keeps the limit of the adjustment to the wage and fringe benefit change and the accompanying social security and unemployment taxes and workers' compensation insurance, with no other amount for general and administrative costs, overhead or profit [@CL-CMP-0892; @CL-CMP-0893; @CL-CMP-4948; @CL-CMP-4949]. It keeps the 30-day notice of a claimed increase unless the Contracting Officer extends the period in writing [@CL-CMP-0894; @CL-CMP-4950]. It keeps the single craft hour computation that adds a combined increase to the original unit price [@CL-CMP-0895; @CL-CMP-5145] and the multiple craft hour computation that finds a difference for each labor classification and multiplies it by the actual hours expended for each craft [@CL-CMP-0944; @CL-CMP-0896; @CL-CMP-5148; @CL-CMP-5149].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A revised wage determination raises a crew's wages by 40,000 and fringe benefits by 6,000 for the option year, with 3,000 of added payroll taxes and insurance [@CL-CMP-4947; @CL-CMP-5139; @CL-CMP-4948]. The contractor gives notice within the 30-day period [@CL-CMP-4950], states the amount, and sends the payroll records [@CL-CMP-4951]. The claim is 40,000 plus 6,000 plus 3,000, which is 49,000 [@CL-CMP-4948], with nothing for overhead, general and administrative cost or profit [@CL-CMP-4949].

For an indefinite-quantity item priced at 5.00 per unit, the new wage determination requires 20.00 per hour for one craft that was paid 19.00, and 15.50 for another that was paid 15.00 [@CL-CMP-5148]. The crafts worked 400 and 300 hours [@CL-CMP-5149], and 2,500 units were ordered in the preceding period [@CL-CMP-5150]. The first craft adds 1.00 times 400 divided by 2,500, which is 0.16, and the second adds 0.50 times 300 divided by 2,500, which is 0.06 [@CL-CMP-5149; @CL-CMP-5150]. The new unit price is 5.00 plus 0.22, which is 5.22 [@CL-CMP-5150], and 3,000 estimated units extend to 15,660 [@CL-CMP-5151].

## Never confuse

Under the percentage method the Contracting Officer increases the portion of the contract price or contract unit price(s) containing the labor costs by the percentage rate published [@CL-CMP-4943], while under the actual method the Contracting Officer adjusts to reflect the contractor's actual increase or decrease in wages and fringe benefits [@CL-CMP-4947]. The actual method adjusts for a decrease to the extent the decrease is voluntarily made by the contractor as a result of the listed causes [@CL-CMP-4947]. Any adjustment will not otherwise include any amount for general and administrative costs, overhead or profit [@CL-CMP-4949].
