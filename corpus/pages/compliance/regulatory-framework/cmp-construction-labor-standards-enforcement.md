## Purpose

Explains the agency program for enforcing labor standards on construction contracts, what the contracting officer tells the contractor before construction begins, the compliance checks made during performance, and how the contracting officer and agency work with the Department of Labor.

Contracting agencies are responsible for ensuring the full and impartial enforcement of labor standards in the administration of construction contracts [@CL-CMP-6902]. They must maintain an effective program that includes four elements [@CL-CMP-6903; @CL-CMP-6739; @CL-CMP-6740]. The first is ensuring that contractors and subcontractors are informed, before commencement of work, of their obligations under the labor standards clauses of the contract [@CL-CMP-6903]. The second is adequate payroll reviews, on-site inspections and employee interviews to determine compliance by the contractor and subcontractors, with prompt initiation of corrective action when required [@CL-CMP-6739]. The third and fourth are prompt investigation and disposition of complaints and prompt submission of all reports required by the subpart [@CL-CMP-6740].

Before construction begins, the contracting officer must inform the contractor of the labor standards clauses and wage determination requirements of the contract and of the contractor's and any subcontractor's responsibilities under the contract [@CL-CMP-6741]. Unless it is clear that the contractor is fully aware of the requirements, the contracting officer must issue an explanatory letter, arrange a conference with the contractor, or do both, promptly after award of the contract [@CL-CMP-6742].

## How it works

The contracting officer must make checks and investigations on all contracts covered by the subpart as may be necessary to ensure compliance with the labor standards requirement of the contract [@CL-CMP-6904]. Regular compliance checking includes four activities [@CL-CMP-6743; @CL-CMP-6905; @CL-CMP-6744; @CL-CMP-6745]. Employee interviews test the correctness of classifications, rates of pay, fringe benefits payments and hours worked, and the section refers to Standard Form 1445 [@CL-CMP-6743]. On-site inspections check the type of work performed, the number and classification of workers, and fulfillment of posting requirements [@CL-CMP-6905]. Payroll reviews check that payrolls of prime contractors and subcontractors have been submitted on time and are complete and in compliance with contract requirements [@CL-CMP-6744]. The information from these checks is compared with available data, including the daily inspector's report and daily logs of construction, to ensure consistency [@CL-CMP-6745].

A special compliance check may be required when inconsistencies, errors or omissions are detected during regular compliance checks, or when a complaint alleging violations is received [@CL-CMP-6746]. If a complaint is not specific enough, the complainant must be so advised and invited to submit additional information [@CL-CMP-6906].

The contracting agency must cooperate with representatives of the Department of Labor in the inspection of records, interviews with workers, and all other aspects of investigations undertaken by the Department [@CL-CMP-6907]. When requested, it must furnish to the Secretary of Labor any available information on contractors, subcontractors, current and previous contracts, and the nature of the contract work [@CL-CMP-6908]. If a Department of Labor representative undertakes an investigation at a construction project, the contracting officer must inquire into the scope of the investigation and request to be notified immediately of violations discovered under the Construction Wage Rate Requirements statute, the Contract Work Hours and Safety Standards statute, or the Copeland (Anti-Kickback) Act [@CL-CMP-6747; @CL-CMP-6909].

If a contract or subcontract is terminated for violation of the labor standards clauses, the contracting agency shall submit a report to the Administrator, Wage and Hour Division [@CL-CMP-7629]. The section also lists what the report must include, and this card does not set that out.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A covered site has six subcontractor crews. Before construction begins, the contracting officer informs the prime contractor of the labor standards clauses, the wage determination requirements and the responsibilities of the contractor and subcontractors [@CL-CMP-6741]. The contractor has not worked under these clauses before, so it is not clear that it is fully aware of the requirements, and promptly after award the contracting officer issues an explanatory letter, arranges a conference, or does both [@CL-CMP-6742].

During performance the contracting officer makes the regular checks. Four workers are interviewed about classifications, rates, fringe benefits and hours [@CL-CMP-6743]. The work is inspected on site to count and classify the workers and check the posting [@CL-CMP-6905], the payrolls are reviewed for timeliness and completeness [@CL-CMP-6744], and the findings are compared with the daily inspector's report and logs [@CL-CMP-6745]. One payroll is missing a week of entries, which is an omission found in a regular check and may call for a special check [@CL-CMP-6746].

Later a Department of Labor investigator arrives at the site. The contracting officer asks what the investigation covers and asks to be notified immediately of violations under the three named statutes [@CL-CMP-6747; @CL-CMP-6909], and the agency cooperates in the inspection of records and interviews with workers [@CL-CMP-6907].

## Never confuse

A regular compliance check is part of ordinary checking, while a special check may be required after an inconsistency, error or omission is detected in a regular check, or on receipt of a complaint alleging violations [@CL-CMP-6746]. The payroll review in a regular check is the Government's review of the payrolls that prime contractors and subcontractors submit [@CL-CMP-6744]. The payrolls and statements that the contractor submits are covered in [Construction payrolls, statements and investigations](../regulatory-framework/cmp-construction-payrolls-statements-and-investigations.md). The contracting agency must cooperate with representatives of the Department of Labor in investigations undertaken by the Department of Labor [@CL-CMP-6907].

## Company calibration

Confirm who receives the preconstruction information and letter, who attends the conference, who answers an employee interview request, and who deals with Department of Labor investigators. [@Q-004]
