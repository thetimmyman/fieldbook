## Purpose

Sets out the two kinds of differing site condition, the notice and adjustment rules that follow, the bars on a late request, what the contractor acknowledges it has investigated, and what the physical data clause says about data furnished to offerors.

### Differing site conditions

The contractor must promptly, and before the conditions are disturbed, give the contracting officer written notice [@CL-CMP-4861]. The notice covers two kinds of condition. One is subsurface or latent physical conditions at the site that differ materially from those indicated in the contract [@CL-CMP-4862]. The other is unknown physical conditions at the site, of an unusual nature, that differ materially from those ordinarily encountered and generally recognized as inhering in work of the character provided for in the contract [@CL-CMP-4863].

The contracting officer must investigate the site conditions promptly after receiving the notice [@CL-CMP-5026]. If the conditions do materially so differ and cause an increase or decrease in the contractor's cost of, or the time required for, performing any part of the work, whether or not changed as a result of the conditions, an equitable adjustment is made under the clause [@CL-CMP-4864]. Where an equitable adjustment is made, the contract is modified in writing accordingly [@CL-CMP-5027].

No request for an equitable adjustment under the clause is allowed unless the contractor has given the written notice required, although the contracting officer may extend the time for giving it [@CL-CMP-4865]. No request for an equitable adjustment for differing site conditions is allowed if it is made after final payment [@CL-CMP-4866].

### Site investigation and conditions affecting the work

The contractor acknowledges that it has taken steps reasonably necessary to ascertain the nature and location of the work, and that it has investigated and satisfied itself as to the general and local conditions that can affect the work or its cost [@CL-CMP-4867]. Those conditions include, but are not limited to, conditions bearing upon transportation, disposal, handling and storage of materials and the availability of labor, water, electric power and roads [@CL-CMP-5028]. They also include uncertainties of weather, river stages, tides or similar physical conditions at the site, the conformation and conditions of the ground, and the character of equipment and facilities needed preliminary to and during work performance [@CL-CMP-5029].

The contractor also acknowledges that it has satisfied itself as to the character, quality and quantity of surface and subsurface materials or obstacles to be encountered, so far as that information is reasonably ascertainable from an inspection of the site, including all exploratory work done by the Government [@CL-CMP-4868]. That acknowledgement extends to what is reasonably ascertainable from the drawings and specifications made a part of the contract [@CL-CMP-5030]. A failure to take the actions described and acknowledged does not relieve the contractor from responsibility for estimating properly the difficulty and cost of successfully performing the work, or for proceeding to perform it without additional expense to the Government [@CL-CMP-4869].

The Government assumes no responsibility for any conclusions or interpretations made by the contractor based on the information the Government made available [@CL-CMP-5031]. It also assumes no responsibility for any understanding reached or representation made by its officers or agents before execution of the contract concerning conditions that can affect the work, unless that understanding or representation is expressly stated in the contract [@CL-CMP-5032].

### Physical data

All information to be furnished or made available to offerors before award that pertains to the performance of the work should be identified in the physical data clause, and subparagraphs that are not applicable may be deleted [@CL-CMP-5034]. Data and information furnished or referred to in the clause is for the contractor's information, and the Government is not responsible for any interpretation of or conclusion drawn from it by the contractor [@CL-CMP-4870]. The contract fills in the clause. It states the methods of site investigation behind the indications of physical conditions on the drawings and in the specifications [@CL-CMP-5035], and it supplies summaries of weather records and warnings, of transportation facilities providing access from the site, and of other pertinent information [@CL-CMP-5036].

## How it works

Notice comes first and must be given before the conditions are disturbed [@CL-CMP-4861]. An equitable adjustment is made where the conditions materially differ and change cost or time [@CL-CMP-4864], and no request is allowed without the required written notice, subject to an extension by the contracting officer [@CL-CMP-4865], or after final payment [@CL-CMP-4866]. A failure to investigate does not relieve the contractor of responsibility for estimating properly the difficulty and cost of the work [@CL-CMP-4869]. The Government disclaims the contractor's conclusions and interpretations, and any pre-award understanding or representation that the contract does not expressly state [@CL-CMP-5031; @CL-CMP-5032]. The physical data in the contract is furnished for the contractor's information, with no Government responsibility for the contractor's interpretation [@CL-CMP-4870].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A fixed-price contract's boring log shows 6 metres of soil over bedrock. The contractor meets rock at 3 metres. It gives the contracting officer written notice the same day, before digging further, and the officer investigates promptly. Removing the rock costs 80,000 more and adds 20 days. The condition differs materially from the contract indications and the notice was timely, so an equitable adjustment of 80,000 and 20 days is made and the contract is modified in writing.

A second contractor excavates the same kind of rock before giving any notice and then gives written notice 10 days later. Its request is not allowed unless the contracting officer extends the time for notice. A third contractor gives timely notice but makes its request after final payment, and the request is not allowed.

## Never confuse

A differing site condition is not an ordered change. The clause supplies its own notice gate and its own bar at final payment [@CL-CMP-4865; @CL-CMP-4866], and an ordered change runs through the changes clause instead, on [Changes - construction](../../close-revrec/revenue/cls-changes-construction.md). Site investigation is also a different clause: it records what the contractor acknowledges it has investigated, including what is reasonably ascertainable from inspection of the site [@CL-CMP-4867; @CL-CMP-4868], while the differing site conditions clause gives notice and adjustment rights for conditions that differ materially from those indicated or ordinarily encountered [@CL-CMP-4862; @CL-CMP-4863; @CL-CMP-4864]. Physical data is furnished for information only, and the Government is not responsible for the contractor's interpretation of it [@CL-CMP-4870]. A Government suspension or delay of work is a separate mechanism, set out on [Suspension and Government delay of work](cmp-suspension-and-government-delay-of-work.md).
