## Purpose

Sets out the two kinds of differing site condition, the notice and adjustment rules that follow, the bars on a late request, what the contractor acknowledges it has investigated, and what the physical data clause says about data furnished to offerors.

### Differing site conditions

The contractor must promptly, and before the conditions are disturbed, give the contracting officer written notice [@CL-CMP-4861]. The notice is of either (1) subsurface or latent physical conditions at the site which differ materially from those indicated in the contract [@CL-CMP-4862], or (2) unknown physical conditions at the site, of an unusual nature, which differ materially from those ordinarily encountered and generally recognized as inhering in work of the character provided for in the contract [@CL-CMP-4863].

The contracting officer must investigate the site conditions promptly after receiving the notice [@CL-CMP-5026]. If the conditions do materially so differ and cause an increase or decrease in the contractor's cost of, or the time required for, performing any part of the work, whether or not changed as a result of the conditions, an equitable adjustment is made under the clause [@CL-CMP-4864]. Where an equitable adjustment is made, the contract is modified in writing accordingly [@CL-CMP-5027].

No request for an equitable adjustment under the clause is allowed unless the contractor has given the written notice required, although the contracting officer may extend the time for giving it [@CL-CMP-4865]. No request for an equitable adjustment for differing site conditions is allowed if it is made after final payment [@CL-CMP-4866].

### Site investigation and conditions affecting the work

The contractor acknowledges that it has taken steps reasonably necessary to ascertain the nature and location of the work, and that it has investigated and satisfied itself as to the general and local conditions that can affect the work or its cost [@CL-CMP-4867]. Those conditions include, but are not limited to, conditions bearing upon transportation, disposal, handling and storage of materials and the availability of labor, water, electric power and roads [@CL-CMP-5028]. They also include uncertainties of weather, river stages, tides or similar physical conditions at the site, the conformation and conditions of the ground, and the character of equipment and facilities needed preliminary to and during work performance [@CL-CMP-5029].

The contractor also acknowledges that it has satisfied itself as to the character, quality and quantity of surface and subsurface materials or obstacles to be encountered, so far as that information is reasonably ascertainable from an inspection of the site, including all exploratory work done by the Government [@CL-CMP-4868]. That acknowledgement extends to what is reasonably ascertainable from the drawings and specifications made a part of the contract [@CL-CMP-5030]. A failure to take the actions described and acknowledged does not relieve the contractor from responsibility for estimating properly the difficulty and cost of successfully performing the work, or for proceeding to perform it without additional expense to the Government [@CL-CMP-4869].

The Government assumes no responsibility for any conclusions or interpretations made by the contractor based on the information the Government made available [@CL-CMP-5031]. It also assumes no responsibility for any understanding reached or representation made by its officers or agents before execution of the contract concerning conditions that can affect the work, unless that understanding or representation is expressly stated in the contract [@CL-CMP-5032].

### Physical data

All information to be furnished or made available to offerors before award that pertains to the performance of the work should be identified in the physical data clause, and subparagraphs that are not applicable may be deleted [@CL-CMP-5034]. Data and information furnished or referred to in the clause is for the contractor's information, and the Government is not responsible for any interpretation of or conclusion drawn from it by the contractor [@CL-CMP-4870]. The contract fills in the clause. It states the methods of site investigation behind the indications of physical conditions on the drawings and in the specifications [@CL-CMP-5035], and it supplies summaries of weather records and warnings, of transportation facilities providing access from the site, and of other pertinent information [@CL-CMP-5036].

## How it works

The contractor must give the written notice promptly and before the conditions are disturbed [@CL-CMP-4861]. An equitable adjustment is made where the conditions materially differ and change cost or time [@CL-CMP-4864], and no request is allowed without the required written notice, subject to an extension by the contracting officer [@CL-CMP-4865], or after final payment [@CL-CMP-4866]. A failure to take the actions described and acknowledged in the clause will not relieve the contractor from responsibility for estimating properly the difficulty and cost of successfully performing the work [@CL-CMP-4869]. The Government assumes no responsibility for the contractor's conclusions or interpretations based on the information it made available, or for any understanding reached or representation made before execution of the contract unless expressly stated in the contract [@CL-CMP-5031; @CL-CMP-5032]. The physical data in the contract is furnished for the contractor's information, with no Government responsibility for the contractor's interpretation [@CL-CMP-4870].

Under Class Deviation 2026-O0038, DoD contracting officers use the revised FAR part 52 published on the Revolutionary FAR Overhaul web page in place of the text codified at 48 CFR chapter 1, effective February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760; @CL-CMP-9762]. Under RFO FAR 52.236-2(a), the contractor must promptly, and before the conditions are disturbed, give a written notice to the Contracting Officer of (1) latent physical conditions or subsurface conditions at the site which differ materially from those indicated in the contract [@CL-CMP-1068]; or (2) unknown physical conditions at the site, of an unusual nature, which differ materially from those ordinarily encountered and generally recognized as inherent in work of the character provided for in the contract [@CL-CMP-1069]. Under paragraph (b)(1) the Contracting Officer will investigate the site conditions promptly after receiving such a notice [@CL-CMP-1070]. Under paragraph (b)(2), if the conditions materially so differ and cause an increase or decrease in the contractor's cost of, or the time required for, performing any part of the work under the contract, an equitable adjustment must be made under the clause [@CL-CMP-1071]. Under paragraph (c), no request for an equitable adjustment to the contract under the clause will be allowed, unless the contractor has given the written notice required [@CL-CMP-1072]. Under paragraph (d), no request for an equitable adjustment to the contract for differing site conditions will be allowed if made after final payment under the contract [@CL-CMP-1073]. Under RFO FAR 52.236-3(a), the contractor acknowledges that it has taken steps reasonably necessary to ascertain the nature and location of the work, and that it has investigated and satisfied itself as to the general and local conditions which can affect the work or its cost, including the items that follow [@CL-CMP-1074]: conditions bearing upon transportation, disposal, handling, and storage of materials, and the availability of labor, water, electric power, and roads [@CL-CMP-1075]; and uncertainties of weather, river stages, tides, or similar physical conditions at the site, the conformation and conditions of the ground, and the character of equipment and facilities needed preliminary to and during work performance [@CL-CMP-1076]. Under paragraph (b), the contractor also acknowledges that it has satisfied itself as to the character, quality, and quantity of surface and subsurface materials or obstacles to be encountered insofar as this information is reasonably ascertainable from an inspection of the site [@CL-CMP-1077], including all exploratory work done by the Government, as well as from the drawings and specifications made a part of the contract [@CL-CMP-1078]. Any failure of the contractor to take the actions described and acknowledged in this paragraph will not relieve the contractor from responsibility for estimating properly the difficulty and cost of successfully performing the work [@CL-CMP-1082], or for proceeding to successfully perform the work without additional expense to the Government [@CL-CMP-9098]. Under paragraph (c), the Government assumes no responsibility for any conclusions or interpretations made by the contractor based on the information made available by the Government [@CL-CMP-1079], and does not assume responsibility for any understanding reached or representation made concerning conditions which can affect the work by any of its officers or agents before the execution of the contract, unless that understanding or representation is expressly stated in the contract [@CL-CMP-1080]. RFO FAR 52.236-4 is reserved [@CL-CMP-1081].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A fixed-price contract's boring log shows 6 metres of soil over bedrock. The contractor meets rock at 3 metres. It gives the contracting officer written notice the same day, before digging further, and the officer investigates promptly. Removing the rock costs 80,000 more and adds 20 days. The condition differs materially from the contract indications and the notice was timely, so an equitable adjustment of 80,000 and 20 days is made and the contract is modified in writing. The codified clause requires the written modification [@CL-CMP-5027], and RFO paragraph (b)(2) does not print it [@CL-CMP-1071].

A second contractor excavates the same kind of rock before giving any notice and then gives written notice 10 days later. Its request is not allowed unless the contracting officer extends the time for notice. A third contractor gives timely notice but makes its request after final payment, and the request is not allowed. The extension is the codified proviso, and RFO paragraph (c) prints no such proviso [@CL-CMP-4865; @CL-CMP-1072].

## Never confuse

For an ordered change, see [Changes - construction](../../close-revrec/revenue/cls-changes-construction.md). Under the differing site conditions clause, no request for an equitable adjustment is allowed unless the contractor has given the written notice required [@CL-CMP-4865], or if made after final payment [@CL-CMP-4866]. Under the site investigation clause, the contractor acknowledges that it has satisfied itself as to the character, quality and quantity of surface and subsurface materials or obstacles to be encountered, so far as that information is reasonably ascertainable from an inspection of the site [@CL-CMP-4867; @CL-CMP-4868]. Data and information furnished or referred to in the physical data clause is for the contractor's information, and the Government is not responsible for any interpretation of or conclusion drawn from it by the contractor [@CL-CMP-4870]. For a Government suspension or delay of work, see [Suspension and Government delay of work](cmp-suspension-and-government-delay-of-work.md).

The codified differing site conditions clause lets the Contracting Officer extend the time for the written notice [@CL-CMP-4865], but RFO paragraph (c) prints no such proviso [@CL-CMP-1072]. The codified site investigation clause says the conditions include, but are not limited to, the listed items [@CL-CMP-5028], while RFO FAR 52.236-3(a) says including and then lists them [@CL-CMP-1074].
