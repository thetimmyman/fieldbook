## Purpose

Defines the main veteran categories, states the duty to list openings, and names the veterans clause, the reporting clause and the reporting provision with their thresholds.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-3083] |
| Codified citation | FAR 22.1301, FAR 22.1302 and FAR 22.1310 [@CL-CMP-3083; @CL-CMP-3085; @CL-CMP-3086] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-3083] |
| Applies to | Solicitations and contracts with an expected value of $200,000 or more for the veterans clause, subject to the listed exceptions [@CL-CMP-3086; @CL-CMP-3087; @CL-CMP-3088] |

The definition of protected veteran specifies a veteran who may be classified as a disabled veteran, a recently separated veteran, an active duty wartime or campaign badge veteran, or an Armed Forces service medal veteran, as defined in the definitions section [@CL-CMP-3083]. A recently separated veteran is any veteran during the three-year period beginning on the date of the veteran's discharge or release from active duty in the military, ground, naval, or air service of the United States [@CL-CMP-3084]. The same section also defines the other listed terms, and this card does not set them out.

Contractors and subcontractors entering into contracts and subcontracts subject to the Act are required to list all employment openings with the appropriate employment service delivery system where the opening occurs, except for listed positions [@CL-CMP-3085]. The positions excepted from the duty to list employment openings are executive and senior management positions, positions to be filled from within the contractor's organization, and positions lasting three days or less [@CL-CMP-3142]. The contracting officer is to insert the Equal Opportunity for Veterans clause in solicitations and contracts if the expected value is $200,000 or more, except in the listed cases [@CL-CMP-3086]. One listed case in which the veterans clause is not inserted is work performed outside the United States by employees recruited outside the United States [@CL-CMP-3087]. Another is that the Director of the Office of Federal Contract Compliance Programs has waived, or the head of the agency has waived, all of the terms of the clause [@CL-CMP-3088]. If the Director of the Office of Federal Contract Compliance Programs or the head of the agency waives one or more, but not all, of the terms of the clause, the contracting officer is to use the basic clause with its Alternate I [@CL-CMP-3137]. The contracting officer is to insert the Employment Reports on Veterans clause in solicitations and contracts containing the Equal Opportunity for Veterans clause [@CL-CMP-3089]. The contracting officer is to insert the Compliance with Veterans' Employment Reporting Requirements provision in solicitations when it is anticipated the contract award will exceed the simplified acquisition threshold and the contract is not for acquisition of commercial products or commercial services [@CL-CMP-3090].

## How it works

The veterans clause goes in when the expected value reaches the threshold and neither listed exception applies [@CL-CMP-3086; @CL-CMP-3087; @CL-CMP-3088]. The reporting clause follows the veterans clause, so it goes in whenever the veterans clause is in the contract [@CL-CMP-3089]. The reporting provision depends on the simplified acquisition threshold and on the contract not being for commercial products or commercial services [@CL-CMP-3090], and the figure for that threshold is on [Simplified acquisition and micro-purchase thresholds](cmp-acquisition-thresholds.md). The policy section also covers affirmative action, outreach, a hiring benchmark and a bar on obligating funds when the annual report is missing, and this card does not set those out.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contracting officer plans a services contract with an expected value of 250,000, to be performed in the United States, and no waiver has been granted. The answer: the veterans clause goes in because the value is above 200,000 and no listed exception applies, and the reporting clause goes in with it. A second contract with an expected value of 150,000 would not trigger the veterans clause.

## Never confuse

A protected veteran is a wider group than a disabled veteran, because the definition lists several categories [@CL-CMP-3083]. The reporting clause and the reporting provision are also different. The clause goes into contracts containing the veterans clause [@CL-CMP-3089], and the provision goes into solicitations when the award is anticipated to exceed the simplified acquisition threshold and the contract is not for acquisition of commercial products or commercial services [@CL-CMP-3090].
