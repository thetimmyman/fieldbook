## Purpose

Shows what use of a patented invention the Government authorizes and consents to under the authorization and consent clause, who answers for infringement, and how the alternates change the grant.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-3604] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.227-1 [@CL-CMP-3604] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-3604] |
| Applies to | Use and manufacture in performing the contract or any subcontract at any tier [@CL-CMP-3604] |

In the basic clause, the Government authorizes and consents to all use and manufacture, in performing the contract or any subcontract at any tier, of an invention described in and covered by a United States patent that falls within one of the listed categories [@CL-CMP-3604]. One category is an invention embodied in the structure or composition of an article whose delivery the Government accepts under the contract [@CL-CMP-3604; @CL-CMP-3605]. The other is an invention used in machinery, tools, or methods whose use necessarily results from the Contractor's or a subcontractor's compliance with specifications or written provisions forming a part of the contract, or with specific written instructions of the Contracting Officer directing the manner of performance [@CL-CMP-3604; @CL-CMP-3606].

The clause also sets who answers for infringement. The entire liability to the Government for infringement of a United States patent shall be determined solely by the provisions of the indemnity clause, if any, in the contract or any subcontract, including any lower-tier subcontract [@CL-CMP-3607]. The Government assumes liability for all other infringement to the extent of the authorization and consent that the clause grants [@CL-CMP-3655].

The Contractor shall include the substance of the clause in all subcontracts that are expected to exceed the simplified acquisition threshold, as defined in the Federal Acquisition Regulation on the date of subcontract award [@CL-CMP-3656]. Omission of the clause from any subcontract, including those at or below that threshold, does not affect the authorization and consent [@CL-CMP-3657].

Alternate I substitutes its own paragraph (a) for paragraph (a) of the basic clause [@CL-CMP-3608]. The substitute authorizes and consents to all use and manufacture of any invention described in and covered by a United States patent in the performance of the contract or any subcontract at any tier, and it lists no categories of invention [@CL-CMP-3654]. Alternate II also substitutes its own paragraph (a), which begins with use and manufacture in the performance of any order at any tier [@CL-CMP-3609]. It limits the covered orders and subcontracts to those placed under the contract for communication services and facilities for which rates, charges, and tariffs are not established by a government regulatory body [@CL-CMP-3610]. Alternate II goes on to list categories of invention, which this card does not restate.

## How it works

The basic grant covers two kinds of use: use tied to the article the Government accepts, and use tied to contract specifications or Contracting Officer instructions [@CL-CMP-3604; @CL-CMP-3605; @CL-CMP-3606]. Liability to the Government for infringement is then set solely by the indemnity clause, if any, and the Government assumes liability for all other infringement to the extent of the grant [@CL-CMP-3607; @CL-CMP-3655]. The Contractor passes the clause down to subcontracts expected to exceed the simplified acquisition threshold, but leaving it out of a subcontract does not change the grant [@CL-CMP-3656; @CL-CMP-3657]. Alternate I replaces the basic paragraph with one that lists no categories [@CL-CMP-3608; @CL-CMP-3654]. Alternate II replaces it with one limited to orders and subcontracts for communication services and facilities of the kind described [@CL-CMP-3609; @CL-CMP-3610].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor builds a pump under a contract that contains the basic clause. A United States patent covers a seal built into the pump, and the Government accepts delivery of the pump. The Government has authorized and consented to the use and manufacture of that seal in performing the contract. If the contract also has an indemnity clause, that clause alone decides the contractor's liability to the Government for infringement.

## Never confuse

Authorization and consent is the Government's grant. Patent indemnity is the contractor's duty to cover infringement liability, which sits in its own clause; see the [patent indemnity and refund of royalties card](cmp-patent-indemnity-and-refund-of-royalties.md). The consent clause itself points to the indemnity clause, if any, as the sole measure of liability to the Government [@CL-CMP-3607]. Alternate II is limited to communication services and facilities of the kind described, so it is not a general grant [@CL-CMP-3610].
