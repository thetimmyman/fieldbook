## Purpose

Shows when a suspension of work, the Suspension of Work clause and the Government Delay of Work clause apply, and what claim, adjustment or settlement follows from each.

Situations may occur during contract performance that cause the Government to order a suspension of work, or a work stoppage [@CL-CMP-9674]. FAR subpart 42.13 provides clauses to meet these situations and a clause for settling contractor claims for unordered Government caused delays that are not otherwise covered in the contract [@CL-CMP-9675].

A suspension of work under a construction or architect-engineer contract may be ordered by the contracting officer for a reasonable period of time, and if the suspension is unreasonable the contractor may submit a written claim for increases in the cost of performance, excluding profit [@CL-CMP-2590]. The Government Delay of Work clause provides for the administrative settlement of contractor claims that arise from delays and interruptions in the contract work caused by the acts, or failures to act, of the contracting officer [@CL-CMP-2591]. It is not applicable if the contract otherwise specifically provides for an equitable adjustment because of the delay or interruption, for example when the Changes clause is applicable [@CL-CMP-2592].

The clause does not authorize the contracting officer to order a suspension, delay or interruption, and it shall not be used as the basis or justification of such an order [@CL-CMP-2593]. If the contracting officer has notice of an unordered delay or interruption covered by the clause, the officer shall act to end the delay or take other appropriate action as soon as practicable [@CL-CMP-2594]. The contracting officer shall retain in the file a record of all negotiations leading to any adjustment made under the clause, and related certified cost or pricing data, or data other than certified cost or pricing data [@CL-CMP-2595]. The Suspension of Work clause is inserted in solicitations and contracts when a fixed-price construction or architect-engineer contract is contemplated [@CL-CMP-2656]. Under it, the contracting officer may order the contractor, in writing, to suspend, delay or interrupt all or any part of the work for the period of time the officer determines appropriate for the convenience of the Government [@CL-CMP-2657]. The clause provides for an adjustment when all or any part of the work is, for an unreasonable period of time, suspended, delayed or interrupted by an act of the contracting officer in the administration of the contract or by the officer's failure to act within the time specified in the contract, or within a reasonable time if not specified [@CL-CMP-2658; @CL-CMP-9903]. Where it applies, an adjustment shall be made for any increase in the cost of performance, excluding profit, necessarily caused by the unreasonable suspension, delay or interruption, and the contract modified in writing [@CL-CMP-2659]. No adjustment shall be made for any suspension, delay or interruption to the extent that performance would have been so suspended, delayed or interrupted by any other cause, including the fault or negligence of the contractor [@CL-CMP-2660], or for which an equitable adjustment is provided for or excluded under any other term or condition of the contract [@CL-CMP-7046]. A claim shall not be allowed for any costs incurred more than 20 days before the contractor notified the contracting officer in writing of the act or failure to act involved, but this requirement does not apply to a claim resulting from a suspension order [@CL-CMP-2661]. A claim shall also not be allowed unless it is asserted in writing, in an amount stated, as soon as practicable after the termination of the suspension, delay or interruption, but not later than the date of final payment under the contract [@CL-CMP-2662; @CL-CMP-2661].

Among its listed circumstances, the Government Delay of Work clause provides for an adjustment when performance of all or any part of the work is delayed or interrupted by an act of the contracting officer in the administration of the contract that is not expressly or impliedly authorized by the contract [@CL-CMP-2663]. Where its adjustment provision applies, an adjustment, excluding profit, shall be made for any increase in the cost of performance caused by the delay or interruption, and the contract shall be modified in writing [@CL-CMP-2664]. No adjustment shall be made for any delay or interruption to the extent that performance would have been delayed or interrupted by any other cause, including the fault or negligence of the contractor [@CL-CMP-2665], or for which an adjustment is provided or excluded under any other term or condition of the contract [@CL-CMP-7047]. A claim shall not be allowed for any costs incurred more than 20 days before the contractor notified the contracting officer in writing of the act or failure to act involved [@CL-CMP-2666]. A claim shall also not be allowed unless it is asserted in writing, in an amount stated, as soon as practicable after the termination of the delay or interruption, but not later than the day of final payment under the contract [@CL-CMP-2667; @CL-CMP-2666].

## How it works

If an ordered suspension is unreasonable, the contractor may submit a written claim for increases in the cost of performance, excluding profit [@CL-CMP-2590]. The Government Delay of Work clause provides for the administrative settlement of contractor claims that arise from delays and interruptions caused by the acts, or failures to act, of the contracting officer [@CL-CMP-2591]. For an unordered delay covered by the clause, the officer shall act to end the delay or take other appropriate action as soon as practicable, and for any adjustment made under the clause shall keep a record of the negotiations and the related data [@CL-CMP-2594; @CL-CMP-2595]. The delay clause gives way where the contract otherwise specifically provides for an equitable adjustment because of the delay or interruption [@CL-CMP-2592]. Each clause also sets notice and timing conditions on a claim, and an adjustment excludes the part of a delay that other causes would have produced [@CL-CMP-2661; @CL-CMP-2666; @CL-CMP-2660; @CL-CMP-2665].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 42 text in place of the codified text from March 16, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9759] [@CL-CMP-9761], and the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text from February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. RFO FAR 42.1001 says situations may occur during contract performance that cause the Government to order a suspension of work or result in a work stoppage, where the codified sentence has the Government order a suspension of work, or a work stoppage [@CL-CMP-7374; @CL-CMP-9674]. RFO FAR 42.1002 says the same as the codified section about a suspension of work under a construction or architect-engineer contract [@CL-CMP-2590; @CL-CMP-7013]. RFO FAR 42.1004 says the same as the codified section on the administrative settlement of claims under the Government Delay of Work clause, on when the clause is not applicable, on not using it as the basis of an order, on acting to end an unordered delay, and on keeping a record of the negotiations leading to an adjustment [@CL-CMP-2591; @CL-CMP-2592; @CL-CMP-2593; @CL-CMP-2594; @CL-CMP-2595; @CL-CMP-7014; @CL-CMP-7015; @CL-CMP-7016; @CL-CMP-7017; @CL-CMP-9901]. RFO FAR 42.1005(a) says to insert the Suspension of Work clause in solicitations and contracts when a fixed-price construction or architect-engineer contract is contemplated, the condition that the codified clause's own prescription line states [@CL-CMP-2656; @CL-CMP-7018]. It adds that contracting officers may reduce the 90-day period in the clause, as appropriate [@CL-CMP-9910]. RFO FAR 52.242-14 says the same as the codified clause on the order to suspend, delay or interrupt the work [@CL-CMP-2657; @CL-CMP-7019], on the circumstances that give rise to an adjustment [@CL-CMP-2658; @CL-CMP-9902; @CL-CMP-9903], on the adjustment for increased cost of performance, excluding profit [@CL-CMP-2659; @CL-CMP-7020], on no adjustment to the extent that other causes, including the contractor's fault or negligence, would have produced the suspension, delay or interruption [@CL-CMP-2660; @CL-CMP-9904], and on the 20-day notice limit and the written claim in a stated amount no later than final payment [@CL-CMP-2661; @CL-CMP-2662; @CL-CMP-7021; @CL-CMP-7022]. RFO FAR 52.242-14(b) also says no adjustment is made for a suspension, delay or interruption for which an equitable adjustment is provided for or excluded under any other term or condition of the contract [@CL-CMP-9905; @CL-CMP-7046]. RFO FAR 52.242-17 says the same as the codified clause on its first circumstance, an act of the contracting officer in the administration of the contract that is not expressly or impliedly authorized by the contract [@CL-CMP-2663; @CL-CMP-9906], and lists as its second circumstance a failure of the contracting officer to act within the time specified in the contract, or within a reasonable time if not specified [@CL-CMP-9907]. It says the same on the adjustment for the delay or interruption [@CL-CMP-2664; @CL-CMP-7023], on no adjustment to the extent that other causes, including the contractor's fault or negligence, would have produced the delay or interruption [@CL-CMP-2665; @CL-CMP-9908], and on the 20-day notice limit and the written claim in a stated amount no later than the day of final payment [@CL-CMP-2666; @CL-CMP-2667; @CL-CMP-7024; @CL-CMP-7025]. RFO FAR 52.242-17(a) also says no adjustment is made for a delay or interruption for which an adjustment is provided or excluded under any other term or condition of the contract [@CL-CMP-9909; @CL-CMP-7047].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

On a construction contract, the contracting officer orders work suspended, and the contractor considers the period unreasonable. It incurs 30,000 of extra equipment cost and, as soon as practicable after the suspension ends and before final payment, submits a written claim stating the amount of that increase in the cost of performance, with no profit added. In a separate case, the contracting officer fails to act within the time the contract specifies and the contractor's work is delayed. The contractor notifies the officer in writing, and costs incurred more than 20 days before that notice are not allowed. If the contract carries the delay clause and no other equitable adjustment provision, the claim is settled administratively and the officer keeps the negotiation record.

## Never confuse

The Government Delay of Work clause does not authorize the contracting officer to order a suspension, delay or interruption, and it shall not be used as the basis or justification of such an order [@CL-CMP-2593]. The Suspension of Work clause lets the officer order a suspension in writing for the convenience of the Government [@CL-CMP-2657]. A stop-work order is a separate tool, covered on [Stop-work orders](../regulatory-framework/cmp-stop-work-orders.md), and the equitable adjustment under the Changes clause is on [Changes, Fixed-Price clause and equitable adjustment](../../contract-finance/funding/ctf-changes-fixed-price-equitable-adjustment.md).
