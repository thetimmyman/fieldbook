## Purpose

Explains what an offeror certifies or represents in paragraphs (m), (n) and (o) of the commercial offeror representations provision, which cover restricted business operations in Sudan, inverted domestic corporations, and certain activities and transactions relating to Iran, and what each paragraph says follows.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-9010] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.212-3(m), (n) and (o) [@CL-CMP-9010; @CL-CMP-9011; @CL-CMP-9013] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-9010] |
| Applies to | Offerors, who make the Sudan certification and the Iran answers by submitting an offer, and Government agencies, which the inverted domestic corporation paragraph limits [@CL-CMP-9010; @CL-CMP-9013; @CL-CMP-9011] |

An offeror that has completed the annual representations and certification electronically in the System for Award Management completes only paragraph (b) of the provision [@CL-CMP-9250]. An offeror that has not completed them electronically shall complete only paragraphs (c) through (v), which include paragraphs (m), (n) and (o) [@CL-CMP-9251].

Sudan, paragraph (m). By submission of its offer, the offeror certifies that it does not conduct any restricted business operations in Sudan [@CL-CMP-9010]. The meaning of restricted business operations and the exceptions to it are not in paragraph (m), so the linked Sudan card carries them.

Inverted domestic corporations, paragraph (n)(1): Government agencies are not permitted to use appropriated (or otherwise made available) funds for contracts with an inverted domestic corporation or a subsidiary of an inverted domestic corporation, unless the exception at FAR 9.108-2(b) applies or the requirement is waived in accordance with the procedures at FAR 9.108-4 [@CL-CMP-9011]. That limit falls on the agency, and the paragraph does not say who grants the waiver; it points to the waiver procedures [@CL-CMP-9011].

Inverted domestic corporations, paragraph (n)(2): The offeror represents whether it is, or is not, an inverted domestic corporation, and whether it is, or is not, a subsidiary of an inverted domestic corporation [@CL-CMP-9012].

Iran, paragraph (o)(1): The offeror shall email questions concerning sensitive technology to the Department of State [@CL-CMP-9190].

Iran, paragraph (o)(2): Unless a waiver is granted or an exception applies as provided in paragraph (o)(3), the offeror makes three Iran answers by submission of its offer [@CL-CMP-9013]. First, it represents, to the best of its knowledge and belief, that it does not export any sensitive technology to the government of Iran or any entities or individuals owned or controlled by, or acting on behalf or at the direction of, the government of Iran [@CL-CMP-9014]. Second, it certifies that it, or any person owned or controlled by it, does not engage in any activities for which sanctions may be imposed under section 5 of the Iran Sanctions Act [@CL-CMP-9015]. Third, it certifies that it, and any person owned or controlled by it, does not knowingly engage in any transaction that exceeds the threshold at FAR 25.703-2(a)(2) with Iran's Revolutionary Guard Corps or any of its officials, agents, or affiliates [@CL-CMP-9016]. The provision describes the property and interests in property of those parties as blocked pursuant to the International Emergency Economic Powers Act, and it refers the offeror to the Office of Foreign Assets Control list of Specially Designated Nationals and Blocked Persons [@CL-CMP-9191; @CL-CMP-9192]. The threshold amount is set by FAR 25.703-2(a)(2), not by this provision [@CL-CMP-9016].

Iran, paragraph (o)(3): The requirements of paragraph (o)(2) do not apply if two conditions are both met [@CL-CMP-9017; @CL-CMP-9193]. One is that the solicitation includes a trade agreements certification (for example 52.212-3(g) or a comparable agency provision) [@CL-CMP-9017]. The other is that the offeror has certified that all the offered products to be supplied are designated country end products [@CL-CMP-9193].

## How it works

The Sudan certification and the Iran answers work by submission, so offering carries them without a separate certificate [@CL-CMP-9010; @CL-CMP-9013]. Only the export answer is qualified by the offeror's knowledge and belief [@CL-CMP-9014]. The two Iran certifications reach the offeror and any person it owns or controls [@CL-CMP-9015; @CL-CMP-9016].

The Iran answers have two routes out. A waiver, if one is granted, removes them, and so does the exception in paragraph (o)(3), which needs both the trade agreements certification in the solicitation and the offeror's certification that every offered product is a designated country end product [@CL-CMP-9013; @CL-CMP-9017; @CL-CMP-9193]. The provision does not say who grants a waiver, and the linked Iran card covers the waiver reference in the related provision.

The inverted domestic corporation paragraph works on two sides. Paragraph (n)(1) limits what agencies may fund, and paragraph (n)(2) has the offeror state its own status and its subsidiary status [@CL-CMP-9011; @CL-CMP-9012]. The provision states no further result of the offeror's answer, so the funding limit and the exception or waiver it names are the only consequences the paragraph gives [@CL-CMP-9011; @CL-CMP-9012].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An offeror submits an offer to supply commercial products. It marks that it is not an inverted domestic corporation and not a subsidiary of one [@CL-CMP-9012]. Because no inverted domestic corporation or subsidiary is a party, the funding limit in paragraph (n)(1) does not reach a contract with it [@CL-CMP-9011]. The Sudan certification is made by submitting the offer, with no separate document [@CL-CMP-9010].

The solicitation includes a trade agreements certification, and the offeror has certified that all the products it offers are designated country end products. Both conditions are met, so the Iran answers in paragraph (o)(2) do not apply to this offer [@CL-CMP-9017; @CL-CMP-9193]. A second offeror in the same solicitation has not certified that all of its offered products are designated country end products and holds no waiver, so it makes the three Iran answers by submitting its offer [@CL-CMP-9013; @CL-CMP-9017].

## Never confuse

The Sudan certification is not a statement about all foreign business, because it reaches only restricted business operations in Sudan [@CL-CMP-9010]. The offeror's representation in paragraph (n)(2) states the offeror's own status, while the funding limit in paragraph (n)(1) is a restriction on agencies [@CL-CMP-9011; @CL-CMP-9012]. The Iran export representation is separate from the two certifications about sanctions and about transactions with Iran's Revolutionary Guard Corps [@CL-CMP-9014; @CL-CMP-9015; @CL-CMP-9016]. The trade agreements exception removes only the Iran answers in paragraph (o)(2), not the Sudan certification or the inverted domestic corporation representation [@CL-CMP-9017].

## Company calibration

The company must confirm who checks these certifications against its own operations before each offer is submitted [@Q-004].
