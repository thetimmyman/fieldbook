## Purpose

Shows what a contractor conveys in a patent assignment, what list it gives cooperative agreement holders and how often, and what an offeror certifies about subcontracting.

The assignment clause is inserted in assignments, as prescribed in DFARS 227.7010 [@CL-CMP-4608]. The contractor conveys to the Government, as represented by the named Secretary, the entire right, title, and interest in and to the listed patents and applications for patent, and in and to the inventions thereof [@CL-CMP-4609]. The right, title, and interest in the listed patents, applications and inventions includes all claims and demands whatsoever for infringement of them that had accrued before the assignment [@CL-CMP-4735]. The conveyance includes corresponding foreign patents and applications for patent insofar as the contractor has the right to assign them [@CL-CMP-4610]. Separately, DFARS 205.470 prescribes the cooperative agreement holder clause for use in solicitations and contracts [@CL-CMP-4739], applying it to those expected to exceed $1.5 million, including those using FAR part 12 procedures for the acquisition of commercial products and commercial services, except for those solely for the acquisition of commercially available off-the-shelf items [@CL-CMP-4740]. A cooperative agreement holder means one of these alternatives: a State or local government; a nonprofit organization; a tribal organization [@CL-CMP-4736]; or an economic enterprise, whether it is organized for profit or nonprofit purposes [@CL-CMP-8643]; which has an agreement with the Under Secretary of Defense for Acquisition and Sustainment to furnish procurement technical assistance to business entities [@CL-CMP-4737]. Upon request, the contractor must provide cooperative agreement holders with a list of those appropriate employees or offices responsible for entering into subcontracts under defense contracts [@CL-CMP-4611]. The list must include the business address, telephone number, and area of responsibility of each employee or office [@CL-CMP-4612]. The contractor need not provide the listing to a particular cooperative agreement holder more frequently than once a year [@CL-CMP-4613].

The offeror certifies that in performance of the contract it will not expend more than 50 percent of the amount paid under the contract on subcontracts unless one of three alternatives applies [@CL-CMP-4614]: the subcontract is awarded to a qualified business; the contract is for products and the subcontract is for materials not available from another qualified business; or a waiver is granted [@CL-CMP-4615]. In the certification provision, qualified business has the meaning given in the DFARS clause at 252.270-7002 of the solicitation [@CL-CMP-4738]. Under that clause, a qualified business is an S corporation as defined in section 1361(a)(1) of title 26 of the United States Code for which 100 percent of the outstanding stock is held through an employee stock ownership plan as defined in section 4975(e)(7) of title 26 of the United States Code [@CL-CMP-4719].

## How it works

Under the assignment clause the contractor conveys to the Government, as represented by the named Secretary, the entire right, title, and interest in and to the listed patents and applications for patent, and in and to the inventions thereof [@CL-CMP-4609], including claims and demands for infringement that had accrued [@CL-CMP-4735], together with corresponding foreign patents and applications for patent insofar as the contractor has the right to assign them [@CL-CMP-4610]. Upon request, the contractor shall provide cooperative agreement holders with the list, and need not provide the listing to a particular cooperative agreement holder more frequently than once a year [@CL-CMP-4611; @CL-CMP-4613]. The offeror certifies that it will not expend more than 50 percent of the amount paid under the contract on subcontracts unless one of the three alternatives applies [@CL-CMP-4614; @CL-CMP-4615].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

Assume an offeror wins a contract paying 10,000,000 for services and plans 6,000,000 of subcontracts, none to a qualified business. The plan exceeds half of the amount paid, the contract is not for products, and no waiver is granted, so the offeror cannot give the certification as planned. It would need to move work to a qualified business, rebuild the plan to stay under the cap, or obtain a waiver. Separately, assume a cooperative agreement holder asks for the subcontracting contact list in January and again in June of the same year. The contractor provides the list in January and need not provide it again in June.

## Never confuse

The assignment clause conveys the entire right, title, and interest rather than a license: the contractor conveys to the Government, as represented by the named Secretary, the entire right, title, and interest in and to the listed patents and applications for patent, and in and to the inventions thereof [@CL-CMP-4609]. The subcontract certification is also not the small business limitation on subcontracting: the offeror certifies that in performance of the contract it will not expend more than 50 percent of the amount paid under the contract on subcontracts unless, among the listed exceptions, the subcontract is awarded to a qualified business [@CL-CMP-4614]. For the small business limitation on subcontracting, see [Limitations on subcontracting and the nonmanufacturer rule](../../market/programs/mkt-limitations-on-subcontracting-and-nonmanufacturer-rule.md). For the patent rights clause, see [Patent rights with ownership by the Government](cmp-patent-rights-government-ownership.md).
