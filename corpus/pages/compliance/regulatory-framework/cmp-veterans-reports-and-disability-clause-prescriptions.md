## Purpose

Covers the notices the contracting officer furnishes for posting, the annual report on protected veterans, and the threshold, exceptions, State and local carve-out and alternate for the clause for workers with disabilities. The clause text, the veterans employment rules and the disability waivers are on the linked pages.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-6795] |
| Codified citation | FAR 22.1304 [@CL-CMP-10006; @CL-CMP-10007; @CL-CMP-10008]; FAR 22.1306, FAR 22.1402, FAR 22.1404 and FAR 22.1408 [@CL-CMP-6795; @CL-CMP-6796; @CL-CMP-8825; @CL-CMP-6799; @CL-CMP-6801]; FAR 22.1405 [@CL-CMP-10009; @CL-CMP-10010; @CL-CMP-10011] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-6795] |
| Applies to | Contractors and subcontractors that have received an award of $200,000 or more for the veterans report, and Government contracts in excess of $20,000 for the clause for workers with disabilities [@CL-CMP-7097; @CL-CMP-6799] |

The contracting officer must furnish to the contractor appropriate notices for posting when the Deputy Assistant Secretary of Labor prescribes them [@CL-CMP-6795]. The Act requires contractors and subcontractors to submit a report at least annually to the Secretary of Labor regarding employment of protected veterans, meaning active duty wartime or campaign badge veterans, Armed Forces service medal veterans, disabled veterans and recently separated veterans [@CL-CMP-6796]. The Act applies to contracts and subcontracts for personal property and nonpersonal services, including construction, of $200,000 or more, except as waived by the Secretary of Labor [@CL-CMP-7096]. The report is required where the contractor or subcontractor has itself received an award of $200,000 or more, except for awards to State and local governments and to foreign organizations whose workers are recruited outside the United States [@CL-CMP-7097]. The contractor and subcontractor must file VETS-4212, the Federal Contractor Veterans' Employment Report [@CL-CMP-6798]. The report is not required where all of the terms of the equal opportunity for veterans clause have been waived [@CL-CMP-6797]. The Director of the Office of Federal Contract Compliance Programs may waive any or all of the terms of that clause for any contract if a waiver is in the national interest [@CL-CMP-3256]. The head of the agency may waive any requirement of the veterans subpart when the contract is essential to the national security and its award without complying is necessary to the national security [@CL-CMP-3257], and must then notify the Deputy Assistant Secretary of Labor in writing within 30 days [@CL-CMP-3258].

Section 503 of the Act applies to all Government contracts in excess of $20,000 for supplies and services, including construction, except as waived by the Secretary of Labor, and the clause for workers with disabilities implements the Act [@CL-CMP-6799]. The clause at FAR 52.222-36 is inserted in solicitations and contracts that exceed or are expected to exceed $20,000 [@CL-CMP-6801]. It is not inserted when both the performance of the work and the recruitment of workers will occur outside the United States, Puerto Rico, the Northern Mariana Islands, American Samoa, Guam, the United States Virgin Islands and Wake Island [@CL-CMP-6982]. It is also not inserted when the Director of OFCCP or the agency head has waived all the terms of the clause in accordance with the waiver provisions [@CL-CMP-6802]. If the Director of OFCCP or agency head waives one or more but not all of the terms, the basic clause is used with its Alternate I [@CL-CMP-6803].

The Director of OFCCP may waive the application of any or all of the terms of the clause for any contract if a waiver is deemed to be in the national interest [@CL-CMP-3098]. The head of an agency may waive any requirement of the subpart when the contract is essential to the national security and its award without complying is necessary to the national security [@CL-CMP-3099], and must notify the Director of OFCCP in writing within 30 days [@CL-CMP-3138]. In a contract with a State or local government, or any agency, instrumentality or subdivision, the clause does not apply to an agency, instrumentality or subdivision of that government that does not participate in work on or under the contract [@CL-CMP-6800].

## How it works

The veterans items split by party. The contracting officer furnishes the notices that the Deputy Assistant Secretary of Labor prescribes, and the contractor and each subcontractor that has itself received an award of $200,000 or more file the annual report with the Secretary of Labor [@CL-CMP-6795; @CL-CMP-6796; @CL-CMP-7097]. The prime contract's value does not make a smaller subcontract reportable, and awards to State and local governments and to foreign organizations recruiting workers outside the United States are excepted [@CL-CMP-7097]. The report covers four named groups of protected veterans and is filed on VETS-4212 [@CL-CMP-6796; @CL-CMP-6798]. It is not required where all terms of the veterans clause have been waived, and waivers come from the Director of OFCCP or, on a national security determination, the agency head [@CL-CMP-6797; @CL-CMP-3256; @CL-CMP-3257]. To verify if a proposed contractor is current with its submission of the VETS-4212 Report, the contracting officer may query the Department of Labor's VETS-4212 Database via the Internet under "Filing Verification" and may contact the VETS-4212 customer support via email for confirmation, if the proposed contractor represents that it has submitted the VETS-4212 Report and is not listed on the verification file [@CL-CMP-10006; @CL-CMP-10007; @CL-CMP-10008].

On the disability side, the contracting officer must furnish to the contractor appropriate notices that state the contractor's obligations and the rights of individuals with disabilities [@CL-CMP-8825]. If performance under the clause at 52.222-36, Equal Opportunity for Workers with Disabilities, may necessitate a revision of a collective bargaining agreement, the contracting officer must advise the affected labor unions that the Department of Labor will give them appropriate opportunity to present their views [@CL-CMP-10009; @CL-CMP-10010]. Neither the contracting officer nor any representative of the contracting officer may discuss with the contractor or any labor representative any aspect of the collective bargaining agreement [@CL-CMP-10011].

The disability clause runs on a dollar threshold with two exceptions. It is inserted above the threshold unless both the work and the recruitment happen outside the listed locations, or all its terms have been waived [@CL-CMP-6801; @CL-CMP-6982; @CL-CMP-6802]. A partial waiver keeps the clause but changes it to the version with Alternate I [@CL-CMP-6803]. The statute itself applies except as waived by the Secretary of Labor, and the clause waivers are given by the Director of OFCCP or the agency head [@CL-CMP-6799; @CL-CMP-3098; @CL-CMP-3099].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor holds a 500,000 supply contract with no waiver of the veterans clause. It files VETS-4212 at least annually, because its own award is $200,000 or more [@CL-CMP-6796; @CL-CMP-6798; @CL-CMP-7097]. One subcontractor holds a 250,000 subcontract and also files, because its own award reaches $200,000 [@CL-CMP-7097]. A second subcontractor holds a 150,000 subcontract and does not file, because the report turns on the subcontractor's own award, not the prime contract's value [@CL-CMP-7097]. The contracting officer furnishes the notices for posting that the Deputy Assistant Secretary of Labor prescribes [@CL-CMP-6795].

A services solicitation expected to be 40,000 will be performed in the United States, and workers will be recruited there, and no waiver of all the clause's terms has been made. The disability clause is inserted [@CL-CMP-6801; @CL-CMP-6982; @CL-CMP-6802]. The agency head later waives only some of the clause's terms in accordance with the waiver provisions, so the basic clause is used with Alternate I [@CL-CMP-6803].

A county government is the contractor on another contract above the threshold, and its parks department takes no part in the work. The clause does not apply to that department [@CL-CMP-6800].

## Never confuse

The veterans report is not the disability clause. The report is an annual filing on protected veterans by contractors and subcontractors with awards of $200,000 or more [@CL-CMP-7097], and the disability clause is inserted above a threshold and changed or removed by waivers [@CL-CMP-6796; @CL-CMP-6797; @CL-CMP-6801; @CL-CMP-6802; @CL-CMP-6803].

A waiver of all terms removes the disability clause, while a waiver of some terms keeps it with Alternate I [@CL-CMP-6802; @CL-CMP-6803]. The State and local carve-out is different again: it excludes a non-participating subdivision from the clause's requirements rather than waiving them for the contract [@CL-CMP-6800].
