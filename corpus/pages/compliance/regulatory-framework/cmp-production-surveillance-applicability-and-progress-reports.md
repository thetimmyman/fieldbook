## Purpose

Shows what production surveillance is, which contracts the subpart covers, and when the production progress reports clause goes into a solicitation or contract.

## The rule

Production surveillance is a function of contract administration used to determine contractor progress and to identify any factors that may delay performance [@CL-CMP-2569]. It involves Government review and analysis of the contractor's performance plans, schedules, controls and industrial processes, and of the contractor's actual performance under them [@CL-CMP-2570]. The subpart on production surveillance and reporting applies to all contracts for supplies or services other than construction contracts, and Federal Supply Schedule contracts [@CL-CMP-2571].

The contracting officer shall insert the Production Progress Reports clause in solicitations and contracts when production progress reporting is required, unless a construction contract or a Federal Supply Schedule contract is contemplated [@CL-CMP-2572]. When the clause is used, the contracting officer shall specify appropriate reporting instructions in the Schedule [@CL-CMP-2573]. Under the clause, the contractor shall prepare and submit to the contracting officer the production progress reports specified in the contract Schedule [@CL-CMP-2654]. During any delay in furnishing a required report, the contracting officer may withhold from payment an amount not exceeding 25,000 dollars or 5 percent of the amount of the contract, whichever is less [@CL-CMP-2655].

## How it works

The Government reviews the contractor's plans, schedules, controls and processes, and then its actual performance under them [@CL-CMP-2570]. The surveillance subpart applies to contracts for supplies or services other than construction and Federal Supply Schedule contracts [@CL-CMP-2571]. Where progress reporting is required and a construction contract or a Federal Supply Schedule contract is not contemplated, the clause goes into the solicitation and contract, the Schedule carries the reporting instructions, and the contractor submits the reports specified in the Schedule [@CL-CMP-2572; @CL-CMP-2573; @CL-CMP-2654]. How the extent of surveillance is set is on [Production surveillance](../../operating-model/corrective-action/opm-production-surveillance.md). The criticality designators and the contents of progress reporting under the subpart are on [Criticality designators and production reporting](../../operating-model/corrective-action/opm-criticality-designators-and-production-reporting.md).

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 42 text in place of the codified text from March 16, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9759] [@CL-CMP-9761], and the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text from February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. RFO FAR 42.801 describes production surveillance as a "contract administration function" where the codified section says "a function of contract administration", and otherwise says the same about what production surveillance is used for [@CL-CMP-2569; @CL-CMP-6959]. It says the same as the codified section about what production surveillance involves [@CL-CMP-2570; @CL-CMP-6960]. RFO FAR 42.802 says the subpart applies to all contracts other than construction contracts and Federal Supply Schedule contracts, where the codified section says all contracts for supplies or services other than those [@CL-CMP-2571; @CL-CMP-6961]. RFO FAR 42.805(a) says to insert the Production Progress Reports clause in solicitations and contracts when production progress reporting is required, unless a construction contract or a Federal Supply Schedule contract is contemplated, and RFO FAR 42.805(b) says to specify appropriate reporting instructions in the Schedule when the clause is used, and both paragraphs are written as instructions that do not name the contracting officer as the codified paragraphs do [@CL-CMP-2572; @CL-CMP-2573; @CL-CMP-6962; @CL-CMP-6963]. RFO FAR 52.242-2 says the same as the codified clause on the reports the contractor must prepare and submit, and on the amount that may be withheld during any delay in furnishing a required report, which is not more than 25,000 dollars or 5 percent of the amount of the contract, whichever is less [@CL-CMP-2654; @CL-CMP-2655; @CL-CMP-6964; @CL-CMP-6965].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contracting officer awards a supply contract worth 2,000,000 and decides that monthly progress reports are needed. The officer inserts the Production Progress Reports clause and writes the reporting instructions into the Schedule. If the contractor is late with a report, the officer may withhold up to the lesser of 25,000 or 5 percent of the contract amount, which here is 25,000, while the delay lasts. Had the contract been a construction contract, the clause would not be inserted on this basis.

## Never confuse

Production surveillance is not production progress reporting. Surveillance is the Government's review and analysis of plans and performance [@CL-CMP-2569; @CL-CMP-2570], while the reporting is set up by the clause and the Schedule instructions, and the clause lets the officer withhold payment during a delay in a report [@CL-CMP-2572; @CL-CMP-2573; @CL-CMP-2655]. The codified subpart applies to contracts for supplies or services other than construction contracts and Federal Supply Schedule contracts [@CL-CMP-2571], while RFO FAR 42.802 says it applies to all contracts other than construction contracts and Federal Supply Schedule contracts [@CL-CMP-6961]. The rules on the extent of surveillance are on [Production surveillance](../../operating-model/corrective-action/opm-production-surveillance.md).
