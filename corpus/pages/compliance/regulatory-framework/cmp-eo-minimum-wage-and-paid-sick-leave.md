## Purpose

Shows how the annual Executive Order minimum wage rate is published and adjusted, what is withheld for an unpaid minimum wage violation and who decides, and the paid sick leave accrual duty and its enforcement. The minimum wage requirements and the paid sick leave requirements come from two different Executive Orders.

## The rule

|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-2390] |
| Codified citation | FAR 22.1904, FAR 22.1905, FAR 22.1906, FAR 22.2105, FAR 22.2109, FAR 22.2110 and FAR 52.222-55 [@CL-CMP-2390; @CL-CMP-2391; @CL-CMP-9531; @CL-CMP-9541; @CL-CMP-2393; @CL-CMP-2491; @CL-CMP-9542; @CL-CMP-9543; @CL-CMP-2489] |

| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-2390] |
| Applies to | Contracts covered by the minimum wage requirements, and contracts covered by the paid sick leave requirements [@CL-CMP-2391; @CL-CMP-2393] |

For the Executive Order minimum wage rate that becomes effective on January 30, 2022, and annually thereafter, the Administrator will notify the public of the new rate at least 90 days before it becomes effective by publishing a notice in the Federal Register [@CL-CMP-2390]. The Administrator will also publish and maintain the Executive Order minimum wage rate on Wage Determinations at SAM.gov or any successor site [@CL-CMP-2390; @CL-CMP-2488]. The clause on minimum wages for contractor workers requires the contractor to pay workers, while performing in the United States and performing on or in connection with the contract, a minimum hourly wage rate of $15.00 per hour beginning January 30, 2022 [@CL-CMP-2489]. It also requires the contractor to adjust the minimum wage paid, if necessary, beginning January 1, 2023, and annually thereafter, to meet the applicable annual Executive Order minimum wage [@CL-CMP-2490]. This card states no rate for a later year.

The clause at 52.222-55, Minimum Wages for Contractor Workers Under Executive Order 14026, is to be inserted in solicitations and contracts that include the clause at 52.222-6, Construction Wage Rate Requirements, or 52.222-41, Service Contract Labor Standards, where work is to be performed, in whole or in part, in the United States [@CL-CMP-9531; @CL-CMP-9541].

Contracting officers shall withhold payment at the direction of the Administrator [@CL-CMP-2391]. When the Administrator's investigation reveals that a contractor has failed to pay the applicable Executive Order minimum wage, the Administrator will notify the contractor and the contracting agency of the unpaid wage violation and request that the contractor remedy the violation [@CL-CMP-8721]. If the contractor does not remedy the violation, the Administrator may direct withholding of payments due on the contract or any other contract between the contractor and the Federal Government [@CL-CMP-2392].

The clause at 52.222-62, Paid Sick Leave Under Executive Order 13706, is likewise to be inserted in solicitations and contracts that include the clause at 52.222-6, Construction Wage Rate Requirements, or 52.222-41, Service Contract Labor Standards, where work is to be performed, in whole or in part, in the United States (the 50 States and the District of Columbia) [@CL-CMP-9542; @CL-CMP-9543].

Contractors are required to permit an employee to accrue not less than 1 hour of paid sick leave for every 30 hours worked on or in connection with a contract covered by the paid sick leave Executive Order [@CL-CMP-2393]. Contractors may instead choose to provide employees with at least 56 hours of paid sick leave at the beginning of each accrual year rather than allowing the employee to accrue such leave based on hours worked over time [@CL-CMP-2499].

Under the paid sick leave remedies, the contracting officer shall, upon his or her own action or upon written request of the Administrator of the Wage and Hour Division, take one of two alternatives [@CL-CMP-2491]. One is to withhold or cause to be withheld from the contractor under the contract covered by the Executive Order or any other Federal contract with the same contractor, so much of the accrued payments or advances as may be considered necessary to pay employees the full amount owed to compensate for any violation of E.O. 13706 or 29 CFR part 13 [@CL-CMP-2491] [@CL-CMP-2492]. The other is to take action to cause suspension of any further payment, advance, or guarantee of funds to a contractor that has failed to make available for inspection, copying, and transcription any of the records identified in 29 CFR 13.25 [@CL-CMP-8722]. Contracting agencies do not have authority to conduct compliance investigations under the paid sick leave regulations, and this does not limit the contracting officer's authority to otherwise enforce the terms and conditions of the contract [@CL-CMP-2493].

## How it works

The Administrator will notify the public of the new Executive Order minimum wage rate at least 90 days before it becomes effective [@CL-CMP-2390], and the contractor shall adjust the minimum wage paid, if necessary, beginning January 1, 2023, and annually thereafter, to meet the applicable annual Executive Order minimum wage [@CL-CMP-2490]. Contracting officers shall withhold payment at the direction of the Administrator [@CL-CMP-2391]. If the contractor does not remedy an unpaid wage violation after the Administrator's notice and request, the Administrator may direct withholding of payments due on the contract or any other contract between the contractor and the Federal Government [@CL-CMP-8721] [@CL-CMP-2392]. For paid sick leave, contractors are required to permit an employee to accrue not less than 1 hour of paid sick leave for every 30 hours worked on or in connection with a covered contract [@CL-CMP-2393], and the contracting officer takes one of the two alternatives to withhold or suspend payment [@CL-CMP-2491] [@CL-CMP-8722]. This card does not draft the remaining provisions of these sections.

Under Class Deviation 2026-O0038, DoD contracting officers shall use the revised FAR part 52 in place of the text codified at 48 CFR chapter 1, effective February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. RFO FAR 52.222-55(b)(1) provides that the contractor must pay workers, while performing in the United States and performing on, or in connection with, the contract, a minimum hourly wage rate of $15.00 per hour beginning January 30, 2022 [@CL-CMP-6886]; the codified clause says "shall pay" [@CL-CMP-2489]. RFO FAR 52.222-55(b)(2) provides that the contractor must adjust the minimum wage paid, if necessary, beginning January 1, 2023, and annually thereafter, to meet the applicable annual Executive Order minimum wage [@CL-CMP-6887]; the codified clause says "shall adjust" [@CL-CMP-2490].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An employee works 90 hours on a covered contract in a month. The answer: unless the contractor instead gives the employee at least 56 hours at the start of the accrual year, the contractor must permit the employee to accrue at least 3 hours of paid sick leave. Separately, the Administrator finds that the contractor did not pay the applicable minimum wage and the contractor does not fix it. The answer: the Administrator may direct withholding from payments due on this contract or on another contract with the Government.

## Never confuse

Under the minimum wage enforcement provisions, contracting officers shall withhold payment at the direction of the Administrator [@CL-CMP-2391]. Under the paid sick leave remedies, the contracting officer acts upon his or her own action or upon written request of the Administrator of the Wage and Hour Division [@CL-CMP-2491]. For withholding under the construction wage and overtime rules, see [withholding from and suspension of contract payments](cmp-construction-wage-withholding-and-suspension.md).
