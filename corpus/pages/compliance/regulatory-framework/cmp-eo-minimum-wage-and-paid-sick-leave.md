## Purpose

Shows how the annual Executive Order minimum wage rate is published and adjusted, what is withheld for an unpaid minimum wage violation and who decides, and the paid sick leave accrual duty and its enforcement. The minimum wage requirements and the paid sick leave requirements come from two different Executive Orders.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-2390] |
| Codified citation | FAR 22.1904, FAR 22.1905, FAR 22.1906, FAR 22.2105, FAR 22.2109, FAR 22.2110 and FAR 52.222-55 [@CL-CMP-2390; @CL-CMP-2391; @CL-CMP-9531; @CL-CMP-9541; @CL-CMP-2393; @CL-CMP-2491; @CL-CMP-9542; @CL-CMP-9543; @CL-CMP-2489] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.222-55 [@CL-CMP-6886; @CL-CMP-6887] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-2390] |
| Applies to | Contracts covered by the minimum wage requirements, and contracts covered by the paid sick leave requirements [@CL-CMP-2391; @CL-CMP-2393] |

For the Executive Order minimum wage rate that becomes effective on January 30, 2022, and annually thereafter, the Administrator will notify the public of the new rate at least 90 days before it becomes effective by publishing a notice in the Federal Register [@CL-CMP-2390]. The Administrator will also publish and maintain the Executive Order minimum wage rate on Wage Determinations at SAM.gov or any successor site [@CL-CMP-2390; @CL-CMP-2488]. The clause on minimum wages for contractor workers requires the contractor to pay workers, while performing in the United States and performing on or in connection with the contract, a minimum hourly wage rate of $15.00 per hour beginning January 30, 2022 [@CL-CMP-2489]. It also requires the contractor to adjust the minimum wage paid, if necessary, beginning January 1, 2023, and annually thereafter, to meet the applicable annual Executive Order minimum wage [@CL-CMP-2490]. This card states no rate for a later year, because the printed sections point to the annual publication for the applicable rate.

The clause at 52.222-55, Minimum Wages for Contractor Workers Under Executive Order 14026, is to be inserted in solicitations and contracts that include the clause at 52.222-6, Construction Wage Rate Requirements, or 52.222-41, Service Contract Labor Standards, where work is to be performed, in whole or in part, in the United States, and the text names no official who inserts it [@CL-CMP-9531; @CL-CMP-9541].

Contracting officers shall withhold payment at the direction of the Administrator [@CL-CMP-2391]. If the contractor does not remedy an unpaid wage violation, the Administrator may direct withholding of payments due on the contract or any other contract between the contractor and the Federal Government [@CL-CMP-2392].

The clause at 52.222-62, Paid Sick Leave Under Executive Order 13706, is likewise to be inserted in solicitations and contracts that include the clause at 52.222-6, Construction Wage Rate Requirements, or 52.222-41, Service Contract Labor Standards, where work is to be performed, in whole or in part, in the United States (the 50 States and the District of Columbia), and the text names no official who inserts it [@CL-CMP-9542; @CL-CMP-9543].

Contractors are required to permit an employee to accrue not less than 1 hour of paid sick leave for every 30 hours worked on or in connection with a contract covered by the paid sick leave Executive Order [@CL-CMP-2393]. Contractors may instead choose to provide employees with at least 56 hours of paid sick leave at the beginning of each accrual year rather than allowing the employee to accrue such leave based on hours worked over time [@CL-CMP-2499].

For a paid sick leave violation, the contracting officer shall, upon his or her own action or upon written request of the Administrator of the Wage and Hour Division, withhold or cause to be withheld from the contractor, under the covered contract or any other Federal contract with the same contractor, the amount described in the same item [@CL-CMP-2491]. That amount is so much of the accrued payments or advances as may be considered necessary to pay employees the full amount owed to compensate for any violation of the paid sick leave Executive Order or its implementing regulations [@CL-CMP-2492]. Contracting agencies do not have authority to conduct compliance investigations under the paid sick leave regulations, and this does not limit the contracting officer's authority to otherwise enforce the terms and conditions of the contract [@CL-CMP-2493].

## How it works

The Administrator announces the new minimum wage rate ahead of its effective date, and the contractor adjusts the minimum wage paid to meet the applicable annual rate [@CL-CMP-2390; @CL-CMP-2490]. Withholding for unpaid minimum wage follows the Administrator's direction, and the contracting officer carries it out [@CL-CMP-2391]. If the violation is not remedied, the Administrator may reach payments due on other contracts with the same contractor [@CL-CMP-2392]. Paid sick leave is a set of contractor duties, starting with accrual, and its enforcement section has its own withholding step [@CL-CMP-2393; @CL-CMP-2491]. Price adjustments, complaints, investigations, other remedies and the other sick leave duties are in the printed sections and are not drafted here.

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text under Class Deviation 2026-O0038, effective February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. RFO FAR 52.222-55(b)(1) has the same substance, with "must pay" where the codified clause says "shall pay": the contractor must pay workers, while performing in the United States and performing on or in connection with the contract, a minimum hourly wage rate of $15.00 per hour beginning January 30, 2022 [@CL-CMP-6886; @CL-CMP-2489]. RFO FAR 52.222-55(b)(2) has the same substance, with "must adjust" where the codified clause says "shall adjust": the contractor must adjust the minimum wage paid, if necessary, beginning January 1, 2023, and annually thereafter, to meet the applicable annual Executive Order minimum wage [@CL-CMP-6887; @CL-CMP-2490].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An employee works 90 hours on a covered contract in a month. The answer: unless the contractor instead gives the employee at least 56 hours at the start of the accrual year, the contractor must permit the employee to accrue at least 3 hours of paid sick leave. Separately, the Administrator finds that the contractor did not pay the applicable minimum wage and the contractor does not fix it. The answer: the Administrator may direct withholding from payments due on this contract or on another contract with the Government.

## Never confuse

The minimum wage enforcement section covers unpaid wages, and its withholding follows the direction of the Administrator [@CL-CMP-2391; @CL-CMP-2392]. The paid sick leave enforcement section has its own withholding step, taken by the contracting officer on the contracting officer's own action or on written request of the Administrator [@CL-CMP-2491]. Do not apply one section's withholding to the other without checking the printed source. Withholding under the construction wage and overtime rules is separate and is on [withholding from and suspension of contract payments](cmp-construction-wage-withholding-and-suspension.md).
