## Purpose

Sets out the defined terms used in the whistleblower protections for contractor employees and the route an employee follows to file a complaint of reprisal.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-7761; @CL-CMP-7746] |
| Codified citation | FAR 3.901 and FAR 3.904-1 [@CL-CMP-7761; @CL-CMP-7746] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-7761; @CL-CMP-7746] |
| Applies to | Contractor and subcontractor employees who believe they were discharged, demoted or otherwise discriminated against contrary to the policy in the policy section [@CL-CMP-7746] |

The definitions section lists the terms below as used in its subpart [@CL-CMP-7761]. Two definitions carry an express limit. One definition does not apply to a later section, and one term excludes two kinds of agreement [@CL-CMP-7741; @CL-CMP-7743].

A contractor or subcontractor employee who believes that he or she has been discharged, demoted, or otherwise discriminated against contrary to the policy in the policy section may submit a complaint with the Inspector General of the agency concerned [@CL-CMP-7746]. A complaint by the employee may not be brought under section 4712 of title 41 of the United States Code more than three years after the date on which the alleged reprisal took place [@CL-CMP-7748]. The whistleblower sections 3.900 through 3.906 do not apply to DoD, NASA, the Coast Guard, or any element of the intelligence community [@CL-CMP-2526].

## How it works

### Glossary

Each definition is stated in full in its own claim.

| Term | Full definition | Claim |
|---|---|---|
| Abuse of authority | An arbitrary and capricious exercise of authority that is inconsistent with the mission of the executive agency concerned or the successful performance of a contract of such agency | [@CL-CMP-7738] |
| Authorized official of the Department of Justice | Any person responsible for the investigation, enforcement, or prosecution of any law or regulation | [@CL-CMP-7739] |
| Inspector General | An Inspector General appointed under chapter 4 of title 5 of the United States Code and any Inspector General that receives funding from, or has oversight over contracts awarded for, or on behalf of, the executive agency concerned. The definition does not apply to section 3.907 | [@CL-CMP-7740] |
| Limit on the Inspector General definition | The definition does not apply to section 3.907 [@CL-CMP-7741] | [@CL-CMP-7741] |
| Internal confidentiality agreement or statement | A confidentiality agreement or any other written statement that the contractor requires any of its employees or subcontractors to sign regarding nondisclosure of contractor information, except that it does not include confidentiality agreements arising out of civil litigation or confidentiality agreements that contractor employees or subcontractors sign at the behest of a Federal agency | [@CL-CMP-7742; @CL-CMP-7743] |
| Exclusions from that term | It does not include confidentiality agreements arising out of civil litigation, or confidentiality agreements that contractor employees or subcontractors sign at the behest of a Federal agency | [@CL-CMP-7743] |
| Subcontract | Any contract as defined in subpart 2.1 entered into by a subcontractor to furnish supplies or services for performance of a prime contract or a subcontract, including but not limited to purchase orders, and changes and modifications to purchase orders [@CL-CMP-7744] | [@CL-CMP-7744] |
| Subcontractor | Any supplier, distributor, vendor, or firm (including a consultant) that furnishes supplies or services to or for a prime contractor or another subcontractor | [@CL-CMP-7745] |

### Filing a complaint

The filer is the contractor or subcontractor employee who believes he or she was discharged, demoted, or otherwise discriminated against contrary to the policy in the policy section. The employee may submit a complaint with the Inspector General of the agency concerned [@CL-CMP-7746]. The text says the employee may submit, which is a permission and not a duty [@CL-CMP-7746].

Procedures for submitting fraud, waste, abuse, and whistleblower complaints are generally accessible on agency Office of Inspector General hotline or whistleblower internet sites. The complainant may instead contact the cognizant Office of the Inspector General directly for submission instructions [@CL-CMP-7747].

A complaint by the employee may not be brought under section 4712 of title 41 more than three years after the date on which the alleged reprisal took place [@CL-CMP-7748]. The defined term Inspector General, as used in the subpart, covers an Inspector General appointed under chapter 4 of title 5 of the United States Code and any Inspector General that receives funding from, or has oversight over contracts awarded for, or on behalf of, the executive agency concerned [@CL-CMP-7761; @CL-CMP-7740].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An employee of a subcontractor, on a contract with an agency that is not DoD, NASA, the Coast Guard or an element of the intelligence community [@CL-CMP-2526], is demoted 2 weeks after reporting suspected invoice fraud. The employee believes the demotion is contrary to the policy in the policy section, and may submit a complaint with the Inspector General of the agency concerned [@CL-CMP-7746]. The employee files 7 months after the demotion. The three-year bar on a complaint brought under section 4712 of title 41 does not apply, because 7 months is less than 3 years [@CL-CMP-7748]. A complaint first brought 4 years after the reprisal would be barred under that section [@CL-CMP-7748]. The submission steps are generally accessible on the agency Office of Inspector General hotline or whistleblower internet sites, or the employee may directly contact the cognizant Office of the Inspector General for submission instructions [@CL-CMP-7747].

## Never confuse

The reprisal complaint is not the internal confidentiality agreement prohibition. This card only defines the term [@CL-CMP-7742]. The prohibition has its own page, [Internal confidentiality agreement prohibition](cmp-internal-confidentiality-agreement-prohibition.md). The Inspector General definition does not apply to section 3.907 [@CL-CMP-7740]. An agreement arising out of civil litigation, or one that employees or subcontractors sign at the behest of a Federal agency, falls outside the internal confidentiality agreement definition [@CL-CMP-7743]. A subcontract is any contract as defined in subpart 2.1 entered into by a subcontractor to furnish supplies or services for performance of a prime contract or a subcontract, and includes but is not limited to purchase orders and changes and modifications to purchase orders [@CL-CMP-7744]. A subcontractor is any supplier, distributor, vendor or firm (including a consultant) that furnishes supplies or services to or for a prime contractor or another subcontractor [@CL-CMP-7745]. The wider treatment of the protections is in [Whistleblower protections](cmp-whistleblower-protections.md).
