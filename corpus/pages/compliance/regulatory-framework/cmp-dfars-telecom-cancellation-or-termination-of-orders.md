## Purpose

Shows what the Government pays when it cancels or terminates ordered telecommunications services, and how that liability is limited.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-CMP-4649; @CL-CMP-4650] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 252.239-7007 [@CL-CMP-4649; @CL-CMP-4650] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-4649; @CL-CMP-4650] |
| Applies to | Contracts that include the Cancellation or Termination of Orders clause [@CL-CMP-4649] |

The clause covers the case where the Government cancels any of the services ordered before the services are made available to the Government, or terminates any of these services after they are made available to the Government [@CL-CMP-4649]. Under that paragraph the Government will reimburse the Contractor for the actual nonrecoverable costs the Contractor has reasonably incurred in providing facilities and equipment for which it has no foreseeable reuse [@CL-CMP-4650]. It will not reimburse actual nonrecoverable costs incurred after notice of award but prior to execution of the order [@CL-CMP-4651]. Actual nonrecoverable costs means the installed costs of the facilities and equipment, less cost of reusable materials, and less net salvage value [@CL-CMP-4652]. Installed costs are reduced by any costs the government may have directly reimbursed the Contractor under the Special Construction and Equipment Charges clause [@CL-CMP-4761]. Net salvage value means the salvage value less the cost of removal [@CL-CMP-4762]. The basic cancellation liability is the actual nonrecoverable cost, which the Government reimburses at the time services are cancelled, and the basic termination liability is the nonrecoverable cost amortized in equal monthly increments throughout the liability period [@CL-CMP-4763].

When feasible, the Contractor shall reuse cancelled or terminated facilities or equipment to minimize the charges to the Government [@CL-CMP-4702]. When there is another requirement or foreseeable reuse in place of them, no charge shall apply and the basic cancellation liability or basic termination liability shall be appropriately reduced [@CL-CMP-4704]. If at any time the Government requires that facilities or equipment be relocated within the Contractor's service area, it has the option of paying the relocation costs in lieu of paying any termination or cancellation charge under the clause [@CL-CMP-8649; @CL-CMP-4703]. The basic liability that applied in the former location continues to apply in the new location, and monthly recurring charges continue to be paid during the period [@CL-CMP-4764].

## How it works

The Government's liability is determined under applicable tariffs governing cancellation and termination charges, which must meet the listed conditions, one of which is that they are filed by the Contractor with a governmental regulatory body [@CL-CMP-4653]. The tariffs must also be in effect on the date of termination and provide specific cancellation or termination charges for the facilities and equipment involved or show how to determine the charges [@CL-CMP-4760]. For services not subject to a governmental regulatory body, it is determined under a mutually agreed schedule in the communication services authorization or other contractual document [@CL-CMP-4654]. If no applicable tariffs are in effect on the date of cancellation or termination or set forth in the applicable authorization or other contractual document, the Government's liability is determined under the settlement procedures that follow [@CL-CMP-4655].

Those procedures include the following. Inventory schedules are provided in such reasonable detail as the Contracting Officer may require, covering the items of property or facilities in the Contractor's possession, the cost of which is in the basic liability and for which the Contractor has no foreseeable reuse [@CL-CMP-4705]. The Contractor shall use its best efforts to sell property or facilities when it has no foreseeable reuse or when the Government has not exercised its option to take title [@CL-CMP-4706], and it shall apply any proceeds of the sale to reduce any payments by the Government under the settlement [@CL-CMP-4707]. When the basic liability established by the authorization or other contractual document is based on estimated costs, the Contractor agrees to settle on the basis of actual cost at the time of cancellation or termination [@CL-CMP-4765]. If, after settlement but within the termination liability period, the Contractor reuses equipment or facilities treated as nonreusable or nonsalvable in the settlement, it shall reimburse the Government for their value [@CL-CMP-4766]. If the total of any partial payments or payments on account exceeds the amount finally agreed or determined to be due, the Contractor shall pay the excess to the Government upon demand [@CL-CMP-4767]. Upon termination, the Government reimburses the nonrecoverable cost less amounts amortized to the date services end, over a liability period agreed between the parties that may not exceed ten years [@CL-CMP-4658]. In the case of either a cancellation or a termination, the presumed maximum liability is capped by the unpaid non-recurring charges and the monthly recurring charges set out in the contract or agreement [@CL-CMP-4656], and the cap for monthly recurring charges is the monthly recurring charges for the minimum service period and any required notice period [@CL-CMP-4657].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

The Government cancels a circuit before it is available. The installed cost was 90,000, none of it directly reimbursed under the special construction clause, reusable materials are worth 10,000 and net salvage value, after the cost of removal, is 5,000. The actual nonrecoverable cost is 75,000, which is 90,000 less 10,000 less 5,000. Had the Government directly reimbursed 20,000 of the installed cost under that clause, the installed cost would be 70,000 and the nonrecoverable cost 55,000. If the contract sets a lower cap on the presumed maximum liability, the cap limits the payment. On a second circuit, the carrier can put the same equipment to use on another order, so no charge applies for that equipment and the liability is reduced to match.
