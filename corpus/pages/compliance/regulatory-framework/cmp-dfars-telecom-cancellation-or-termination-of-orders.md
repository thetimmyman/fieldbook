## Purpose

Shows what the Government pays when it cancels or terminates ordered telecommunications services, and how that liability is limited.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-CMP-4649; @CL-CMP-4650] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 252.239-7007 [@CL-CMP-4649; @CL-CMP-4650] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-4649; @CL-CMP-4650] |
| Applies to | Contracts that include the Cancellation or Termination of Orders clause [@CL-CMP-4649] |

The clause covers the case where the Government cancels any of the services ordered before the services are made available to the Government, or terminates any of these services after they are made available to the Government [@CL-CMP-4649]. Under that paragraph the Government will reimburse the Contractor for the actual nonrecoverable costs the Contractor has reasonably incurred in providing facilities and equipment for which it has no foreseeable reuse [@CL-CMP-4650]. It will not reimburse actual nonrecoverable costs incurred after notice of award but prior to execution of the order [@CL-CMP-4651]. Actual nonrecoverable costs means the installed costs of the facilities and equipment, less cost of reusable materials, and less net salvage value [@CL-CMP-4652].

Reuse lowers the charge. When feasible, the Contractor shall reuse cancelled or terminated facilities or equipment to minimize the charges to the Government [@CL-CMP-4702]. When there is another requirement or foreseeable reuse in place of them, no charge shall apply and the basic cancellation liability or basic termination liability shall be appropriately reduced [@CL-CMP-4704]. If the Government requires that facilities or equipment be relocated within the Contractor's service area, it has the option of paying the relocation costs in lieu of paying any termination or cancellation charge under the clause [@CL-CMP-4703].

## How it works

The Government's liability is determined under applicable tariffs governing cancellation and termination charges, which must meet the listed conditions, one of which is that they are filed by the Contractor with a governmental regulatory body [@CL-CMP-4653]. For services not subject to a governmental regulatory body, it is determined under a mutually agreed schedule in the communication services authorization or other contractual document [@CL-CMP-4654]. If no applicable tariffs are in effect on the date of cancellation or termination or set forth in the applicable authorization or other contractual document, the Government's liability is determined under the settlement procedures that follow [@CL-CMP-4655].

Those procedures have these steps. Inventory schedules are provided in such reasonable detail as the Contracting Officer may require, covering the items of property or facilities in the Contractor's possession, the cost of which is in the basic liability and for which the Contractor has no foreseeable reuse [@CL-CMP-4705]. The Contractor shall use its best efforts to sell property or facilities when it has no foreseeable reuse or when the Government has not exercised its option to take title [@CL-CMP-4706], and it shall apply any proceeds of the sale to reduce any payments by the Government under the settlement [@CL-CMP-4707]. Upon termination, the Government reimburses the nonrecoverable cost less amounts amortized to the date services end, over a liability period agreed between the parties that may not exceed ten years [@CL-CMP-4658]. In the case of either a cancellation or a termination, the presumed maximum liability is capped by the unpaid non-recurring charges and the monthly recurring charges set out in the contract or agreement [@CL-CMP-4656], and the cap for monthly recurring charges is the monthly recurring charges for the minimum service period and any required notice period [@CL-CMP-4657].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

The Government cancels a circuit before it is available. The installed cost was 90,000, reusable materials are worth 10,000 and net salvage value is 5,000. The actual nonrecoverable cost is 75,000, which is 90,000 less 10,000 less 5,000. If the contract sets a lower cap on the presumed maximum liability, the cap limits the payment. On a second circuit, the carrier can put the same equipment to use on another order, so no charge applies for that equipment and the liability is reduced to match.

## Never confuse

This clause is not the general cost principle for costs of terminated contracts, which is on [Termination costs](../../close-revrec/inventory-wip/cls-termination-costs.md). Its inventory schedules are not the disposal schedules used after a contract termination, which are on [Termination inventory: disposal schedules and government property accounting](../../close-revrec/inventory-wip/cls-termination-inventory-schedules-and-property-accounting.md). The clause here applies to ordered telecommunications services and is settled under tariffs, a mutually agreed schedule or the settlement procedures [@CL-CMP-4653; @CL-CMP-4654; @CL-CMP-4655]. How orders are placed and paid for is on [Access and orders for facilities and services](cmp-dfars-telecom-access-and-orders-for-facilities.md), and direct special construction charges are on [Special construction, title and term](cmp-dfars-telecom-special-construction-title-and-term.md).
