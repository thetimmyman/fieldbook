## Purpose

Shows the deadlines for disclosing, electing and filing on a subject invention, how the Contractor may ask for more time, when the agency may ask for title, and the reporting, march-in, nonprofit and subcontract duties that follow.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-3632] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.227-11 [@CL-CMP-3632] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-3632] |
| Applies to | Each subject invention under the clause [@CL-CMP-3632] |

The Contractor shall disclose in writing each subject invention to the Contracting Officer within 2 months after the inventor discloses it in writing to Contractor personnel responsible for patent matters [@CL-CMP-3632]. The Contractor shall elect in writing whether or not to retain ownership by notifying the Contracting Officer within 2 years of disclosure to the agency [@CL-CMP-3633]. Where publication, on sale, or public use has initiated the 1-year statutory period, the agency may shorten the election period to a date no more than 60 days before the end of that period [@CL-CMP-3634]. The Contractor shall file a provisional or nonprovisional patent application, or a Plant Variety Protection Application, on an elected subject invention within 1 year after election [@CL-CMP-3635]. Where publication, on sale, or public use has initiated the 1-year statutory period, the Contractor shall file before the end of that period [@CL-CMP-3636]. For filing in additional countries or international patent offices, the clause gives a window of either 10 months of the first filed application, whether provisional or nonprovisional, or a second period [@CL-CMP-3637]. The second period is 6 months from the date permission is granted by the Commissioner of Patents to file foreign applications where a Secrecy Order prohibited such filing [@CL-CMP-3637; @CL-CMP-3663].

The Contractor may request extensions of time for disclosure, election, or filing under the three deadline paragraphs of the clause [@CL-CMP-3664]. The Contractor shall also notify the Contracting Officer of any decision not to file a nonprovisional patent application, continue the prosecution of a patent application, pay maintenance fees, or defend in a reexamination or opposition proceeding on a patent, in any country [@CL-CMP-3666]. That notice is due not less than 30 days before the expiration of the response or filing period required by the relevant patent office [@CL-CMP-3666; @CL-CMP-3667].

On written request the Contractor shall assign title to the agency if it fails to disclose or elect within the times specified or elects not to retain ownership, as one listed ground [@CL-CMP-3638]. For a failure to disclose or elect, the agency may request title only within 60 days after learning of the failure [@CL-CMP-3639]. With respect to any subject invention in which the Contractor has retained ownership, the agency has the right to require licensing, under the statute and procedures the march-in paragraph names [@CL-CMP-3665].

The Contractor shall submit, on request, periodic reports no more frequently than annually on the utilization of a subject invention, or on efforts to obtain utilization [@CL-CMP-3640]. The Contractor shall include the substance of the clause in all subcontracts for experimental, developmental, or research work to be performed by a small business concern or nonprofit organization [@CL-CMP-3641].

If the Contractor is a nonprofit organization, it shall not assign rights to a subject invention in the United States without the written approval of the agency, except where an assignment is made to an organization that has as one of its primary functions the management of inventions [@CL-CMP-3668]. The assignee under that exception shall be subject to the same provisions as the Contractor [@CL-CMP-3668; @CL-CMP-3669].

## How it works

The steps run in order: disclose to the Contracting Officer, elect whether to keep title, then file [@CL-CMP-3632; @CL-CMP-3633; @CL-CMP-3635]. A statutory period that a publication, sale or public use starts can pull the election and filing dates forward [@CL-CMP-3634; @CL-CMP-3636]. The Contractor may ask for more time on any of the three steps [@CL-CMP-3664]. Failing to disclose or elect on time lets the agency ask for title, but only within a limited window [@CL-CMP-3638; @CL-CMP-3639]. This card does not cover the remaining paragraphs of the clause, such as the contents of the disclosure, the instruments the Contractor must deliver, the preference for United States industry, and the other nonprofit rules.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An inventor reports a new alloy in writing to the patent team on Day 0. The contractor discloses it to the Contracting Officer within 2 months. No publication, sale or public use has started the statutory period, so the contractor elects within 2 years of disclosure. It elects to keep title and files within 1 year after election. Had it failed to disclose or elect on time, the agency could ask for title, but only within 60 days after learning of the failure.

## Never confuse

Election of title is the decision whether to keep ownership [@CL-CMP-3633]. Filing is the later step of applying for protection on the elected invention [@CL-CMP-3635]. The agency's right to request title for a failure to disclose or elect is limited by a time window, so it is not an open-ended right [@CL-CMP-3639]. A request for an extension is not the same as an extension; the paragraph on extensions says only that the Contractor may request one [@CL-CMP-3664]. This clause is also separate from the clause on classified subject matter, which has its own rule on filing; see [filing patent applications on classified subject matter](cmp-classified-patent-application-filing.md). Rights in data are covered separately on the [rights in data card](cmp-rights-in-data-general-definitions-and-allocation.md).
