## Purpose

Shows which contractor employees are covered by the personal conflict of interest rules and what the contractor must do.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-2342] |
| Codified citation | FAR 3.1101, FAR 3.1103 and FAR 3.1106 [@CL-CMP-2342; @CL-CMP-2346; @CL-CMP-2471] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-2342] |
| Applies to | Contractors whose employees perform acquisition functions closely associated with inherently Government functions, through the clause at FAR 52.203-16 [@CL-CMP-2346], which is prescribed for solicitations and contracts that exceed the simplified acquisition threshold and meet a second condition [@CL-CMP-2471] |

A covered employee is an individual who performs an acquisition function closely associated with inherently governmental functions and is either an employee of the contractor or a subcontractor that is a self-employed individual treated as a covered employee of the contractor [@CL-CMP-2342]. The definition states a reason for treating the self-employed subcontractor as covered, which this card does not restate. An acquisition function closely associated with inherently governmental functions means supporting or providing advice or recommendations with regard to listed activities of a Federal agency, the first of which is planning acquisitions [@CL-CMP-2343]. The listed activities include determining whether contract costs are reasonable, allocable, and allowable [@CL-CMP-2473].

By use of the contract clause at FAR 52.203-16, as prescribed at FAR 3.1106, the contracting officer shall require each contractor whose employees perform such functions to take the listed actions [@CL-CMP-2346]. That clause is prescribed for solicitations and contracts that exceed the simplified acquisition threshold [@CL-CMP-2471] and that include a requirement for services by contractor employees that involve performance of acquisition functions closely associated with inherently governmental functions for, or on behalf of, a Federal agency or department [@CL-CMP-2472].

## How it works

A personal conflict of interest is a situation in which a covered employee has a financial interest, personal activity, or relationship that could impair the employee's ability to act impartially and in the best interest of the Government when performing under the contract [@CL-CMP-2344]. A de minimis interest that would not impair that ability is not covered [@CL-CMP-2345]. The listed actions include procedures to screen covered employees by obtaining and maintaining a disclosure of interests when the employee is initially assigned to the task [@CL-CMP-2347]. They also include requiring each covered employee to update the disclosure statement whenever the employee's personal or financial circumstances change in such a way that a new personal conflict of interest might occur because of the task [@CL-CMP-2467]. Two further listed requirements are prohibiting use of non-public information for personal gain and obtaining a signed non-disclosure agreement [@CL-CMP-2348]. The contractor must report to the contracting officer any violation by a covered employee as soon as identified, with a description and the proposed actions [@CL-CMP-2349]. The section lists further actions, including oversight and discipline, which this card does not restate.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor assigns an analyst to review proposals for the agency. The analyst holds a large stock position in an offeror. The contractor obtains the analyst's disclosure at assignment. If the analyst later breaks the contractor's conflict safeguards, the contractor reports the violation to the contracting officer as soon as it is identified, with the actions it proposes to take.

## Never confuse

A personal conflict of interest concerns an individual covered employee's own interests. An organizational conflict of interest concerns the company's position; see [Organizational conflicts of interest: general rules](../../mna/diligence/mna-organizational-conflicts-of-interest-general-rules.md). Duties to disclose credible evidence of violations are covered in [Contractor code of business ethics](cmp-contractor-code-of-business-ethics.md).
