## Purpose

Explains the certification regarding responsibility matters in paragraph (h) of the commercial offeror representations provision, including the delinquent tax test and its examples, and the certification regarding knowledge of child labor for listed end products in paragraph (i), with who lists the products and what the offeror certifies.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-8991] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.212-3(h) and (i) [@CL-CMP-8991; @CL-CMP-8998] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-8991] |
| Applies to | Contracts whose value is expected to exceed the simplified acquisition threshold for paragraph (h), and solicitations where the Contracting Officer has listed end products in paragraph (i)(1) [@CL-CMP-8991; @CL-CMP-8998; @CL-CMP-9170] |

Responsibility matters, paragraph (h). The certification applies only if the contract value is expected to exceed the simplified acquisition threshold, and the offeror certifies, to the best of its knowledge and belief, as to the offeror and/or any of its principals [@CL-CMP-8991]. The simplified acquisition threshold is $350,000, with higher values for the listed contingency and defense purposes [@CL-FND-0001].

Paragraph (h)(1): the offeror certifies whether it and/or any of its principals are or are not presently debarred, suspended, proposed for debarment or declared ineligible for the award of contracts by any Federal agency [@CL-CMP-8992].

Paragraph (h)(2): the offeror certifies whether it and/or any of its principals have or have not, within a three-year period preceding the offer, been convicted of or had a civil judgment rendered against them for commission of fraud or a criminal offense in connection with obtaining, attempting to obtain or performing a Federal, state or local government contract or subcontract [@CL-CMP-8993]. The same certification covers violation of Federal or state antitrust statutes relating to the submission of offers, and embezzlement, theft, forgery, bribery, falsification or destruction of records, making false statements, tax evasion, violating Federal criminal tax laws, or receiving stolen property [@CL-CMP-9163].

Paragraph (h)(3): the offeror certifies whether it and/or any of its principals are or are not presently indicted for, or otherwise criminally or civilly charged by a Government entity with, commission of any of the offenses enumerated in paragraph (h)(2) [@CL-CMP-8994].

Paragraph (h)(4): the offeror certifies whether it and/or any of its principals have or have not, within a three-year period preceding the offer, been notified of any delinquent Federal taxes in an amount that exceeds the threshold at FAR 9.104-5(a)(2) for which the liability remains unsatisfied [@CL-CMP-8995]. The notification duty in FAR 9.104-5(a)(2) applies where an offeror indicates the existence of an indictment, charge, conviction, civil judgment, or Federal tax delinquency in an amount that exceeds $15,000 [@CL-SUP-0791].

When taxes are delinquent, paragraph (h)(4)(i): taxes are considered delinquent if both listed criteria apply, and the first criterion is that the tax liability is finally determined, which is so if the liability has been assessed and is not so if there is a pending administrative or judicial challenge [@CL-CMP-8996]. In the case of a judicial challenge to the liability, the liability is not finally determined until all judicial appeal rights have been exhausted [@CL-CMP-9164]. The second criterion is that the taxpayer is delinquent in making payment, which is so if the taxpayer has failed to pay the tax liability when full payment was due and required, and a taxpayer is not delinquent in cases where enforced collection action is precluded [@CL-CMP-8997].

Examples, paragraph (h)(4)(ii): a taxpayer that has received a statutory notice of deficiency, which entitles it to seek Tax Court review of a proposed tax deficiency, does not owe a delinquent tax because the liability is not a final tax liability [@CL-CMP-9165]. In another example the Internal Revenue Service has filed a notice of Federal tax lien for an assessed tax liability and the taxpayer has been issued a notice entitling it to request a hearing with the IRS Office of Appeals contesting the lien filing [@CL-CMP-9166]. In the hearing in that example, the taxpayer is entitled to contest the underlying tax liability because it has had no prior opportunity to do so, and the tax is not delinquent because it is not a final tax liability [@CL-CMP-9167]. A taxpayer that has entered into an installment agreement, is making timely payments and is in full compliance with the agreement terms is not delinquent because it is not currently required to make full payment [@CL-CMP-9168]. A taxpayer that has filed for bankruptcy protection is not delinquent because enforced collection action is stayed under the Bankruptcy Code [@CL-CMP-9169].

What follows from an affirmative response. When an offeror provides an affirmative response in paragraph (h), the contracting officer shall carry out the duties listed in FAR 9.104-5(a) [@CL-SUP-0789]. One duty is to promptly, upon receipt of offers, request such additional information from the offeror as the offeror deems necessary in order to demonstrate the offeror's responsibility to the contracting officer [@CL-CMP-9714]. Another duty is to notify, before proceeding with award and in accordance with agency procedures, the agency official responsible for initiating debarment or suspension action, where an offeror indicates the existence of an indictment, charge, conviction or civil judgment [@CL-SUP-0790]. Separately, an offeror that does not furnish the representation or certifications, or the information the contracting officer requests, shall be given an opportunity to remedy the deficiency [@CL-SUP-0792]. Failure to furnish the representation, certifications or information may render the offeror nonresponsible [@CL-CMP-9715].

Child labor, paragraph (i). The Contracting Officer must list in paragraph (i)(1) any end products being acquired under the solicitation that are included in the List of Products Requiring Contractor Certification as to Forced or Indentured Child Labor, unless excluded at FAR 22.1503(b) [@CL-CMP-8998]. If the Contracting Officer has identified end products and countries of origin in paragraph (i)(1), the offeror must certify to either paragraph (i)(2)(i) or paragraph (i)(2)(ii) by checking the appropriate block [@CL-CMP-9170]. Under the first block the offeror will not supply any end product listed in paragraph (i)(1) that was mined, produced or manufactured in the corresponding country as listed for that product [@CL-CMP-8999]. Under the second block the offeror may supply such a product from that country [@CL-CMP-9000]. An offeror that takes the second block certifies that it has made a good faith effort to determine whether forced or indentured child labor was used to mine, produce or manufacture any such end product furnished under the contract [@CL-CMP-9001]. On the basis of those efforts, it certifies that it is not aware of any such use of child labor [@CL-CMP-9002].

## How it works

Paragraph (h) turns on a dollar gate, a look-back and a status. The offeror answers it only for a contract expected to exceed the simplified acquisition threshold [@CL-CMP-8991]. Questions (h)(1) and (h)(3) are about the present, and questions (h)(2) and (h)(4) look back over a three-year period preceding the offer [@CL-CMP-8992; @CL-CMP-8994; @CL-CMP-8993; @CL-CMP-8995].

The tax question has a threshold and a two-part test. The threshold is the one at FAR 9.104-5(a)(2), which that section states as $15,000 [@CL-CMP-8995; @CL-SUP-0791]. Taxes are considered delinquent if the liability is finally determined and the taxpayer is delinquent in making payment [@CL-CMP-8996; @CL-CMP-8997]. The examples show cases that are not delinquent: a statutory notice of deficiency, a lien hearing where the taxpayer may still contest the liability, an installment agreement in full compliance, and a bankruptcy stay [@CL-CMP-9165; @CL-CMP-9167; @CL-CMP-9168; @CL-CMP-9169].

What follows an affirmative response is the contracting officer's duties under FAR 9.104-5, and an offeror that does not furnish the certification is given an opportunity to remedy the deficiency, although the failure may render it nonresponsible [@CL-SUP-0789; @CL-SUP-0792; @CL-CMP-9715].

Paragraph (i) depends on the Contracting Officer's list. The Contracting Officer lists the end products and countries, and the offeror then checks one of two blocks [@CL-CMP-8998; @CL-CMP-9170]. The second block carries the good faith effort certification and the not-aware certification [@CL-CMP-9001; @CL-CMP-9002].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A solicitation is expected to exceed the simplified acquisition threshold, so Delta Components answers paragraph (h) as to itself and its principals [@CL-CMP-8991]. Delta was convicted of a Federal fraud offense in connection with a contract 4 years before the offer. That is outside the three-year period preceding the offer, so Delta answers that it has not been convicted of or had a civil judgment rendered against it within that period [@CL-CMP-8993].

Delta received a statutory notice of deficiency 1 year ago, and has not been assessed a liability, so no delinquent tax exists from that notice [@CL-CMP-9165]. Separately, Delta has an assessed liability of $22,000 under an installment agreement, and it is making timely payments and is in full compliance with the agreement terms, so it is not delinquent [@CL-CMP-9168]. Delta therefore answers that it has not been notified of delinquent Federal taxes above the threshold [@CL-CMP-8995; @CL-CMP-8996; @CL-CMP-8997].

A second offeror has an assessed liability of $20,000 that it failed to pay when full payment was due, and no challenge is pending and no collection action is precluded. The liability is finally determined and the taxpayer is delinquent, so the taxes are delinquent [@CL-CMP-8996; @CL-CMP-8997]. The Internal Revenue Service notified the offeror of the delinquency within the three-year period and the liability remains unsatisfied, so the offeror answers that it has been notified of delinquent Federal taxes above the threshold [@CL-CMP-8995]. The amount exceeds $15,000, so the notification duty in FAR 9.104-5(a)(2) applies to that response, along with the other duties the contracting officer carries out [@CL-SUP-0791; @CL-SUP-0789].

For paragraph (i), the Contracting Officer lists end product P with country X as its listed country of origin [@CL-CMP-8998]. A first offeror certifies in the first block that it will not supply product P mined, produced or manufactured in country X [@CL-CMP-8999]. A second offeror checks the second block, certifies that it made a good faith effort to determine whether forced or indentured child labor was used to make product P, and certifies that it is not aware of any such use [@CL-CMP-9000; @CL-CMP-9001; @CL-CMP-9002].

## Never confuse

The delinquent tax question is not a question about any unpaid tax. It reaches delinquent Federal taxes above the FAR 9.104-5(a)(2) threshold for which the offeror was notified within three years and the liability remains unsatisfied, and a delinquent tax needs both a finally determined liability and a failure to pay [@CL-CMP-8995; @CL-CMP-8996; @CL-CMP-8997].

A pending challenge is not the same as a lost challenge. A liability under administrative or judicial challenge is not finally determined, and with a judicial challenge it stays not finally determined until all judicial appeal rights have been exhausted [@CL-CMP-8996; @CL-CMP-9164].

Paragraph (h) asks the questions, and the contracting officer's follow-up duties on an affirmative response are in FAR 9.104-5 [@CL-CMP-8991; @CL-SUP-0789].

The Contracting Officer lists the products and countries in paragraph (i)(1), and the offeror then certifies to one of the two blocks [@CL-CMP-8998; @CL-CMP-9170].
