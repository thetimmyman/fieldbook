## Purpose

Shows how a contracting office evaluates the Certificate of Independent Price Determination, which facts do not by themselves count as disclosure, and what follows when an offeror deletes or modifies part of the certificate.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-6826] |
| Codified citation | FAR 3.103-2 [@CL-CMP-6826]; FAR 3.103-3 [@CL-CMP-9627] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-6826] |
| Applies to | Offers that carry the Certificate of Independent Price Determination [@CL-CMP-6826] |

None of the following facts, in and of itself, is disclosure as the term is used in paragraph (a)(2) of the certificate [@CL-CMP-6826]: a firm's published price lists, rates or tariffs for items the Government is acquiring, and its advance notice to prospective customers of new or revised price lists [@CL-CMP-6827]; a firm's sales of the same items to commercial customers at the same prices it offers the Government, and its participation in a reverse auction [@CL-CMP-6828].

For paragraph (b)(2) of the certificate, an individual may use a blanket authorization to act as an agent for the persons who determine the offered prices if the proposed contract is clearly within the scope of the authorization [@CL-CMP-6829], and if the person giving the authorization is the person in the offeror's organization responsible for determining the offered prices when the certification is made in the particular offer [@CL-CMP-7100].

## How it works

A joint offer is certified concern by concern, because each representative's certification applies only to the activities of that concern [@CL-CMP-6830]. If the offeror deleted or modified paragraph (a)(1) or (3) or paragraph (b), the contracting officer must reject its bid or proposal [@CL-CMP-6831]. If the offeror deleted or modified paragraph (a)(2), the offeror must have furnished with its offer a signed statement of the circumstances of the disclosure of prices [@CL-CMP-6832]. The section does not say what follows when that statement is missing. The chief of the contracting office must review the altered certificate and the statement and determine in writing whether the disclosure was made for the purpose, or had the effect, of restricting competition [@CL-CMP-6833]. A positive determination means the bid or proposal is rejected, and a negative determination means it is considered for award [@CL-CMP-7101].

Whenever an offer is rejected under paragraph (b)(1) or (2), or the certificate is suspected of being false, the contracting officer must report the situation to the Attorney General in accordance with the antitrust reporting section [@CL-CMP-6834]. The chief's determination does not prevent or inhibit prosecution of any criminal or civil action involving the occurrences or transactions to which the certificate relates [@CL-CMP-7102].

A contractor that properly executed the certificate before award does not have to submit a separate certificate with each proposal to perform a work order or similar ordering instrument issued pursuant to the terms of the contract, where the Government's requirements cannot be met from another source [@CL-CMP-9627].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

Offeror A deletes paragraph (a)(2) from the certificate and attaches a signed statement describing how its price was shown to another firm before offers were due [@CL-CMP-6832]. The chief of the contracting office reviews the altered certificate and the statement and determines in writing that the disclosure was not made for the purpose, and did not have the effect, of restricting competition [@CL-CMP-6833]. That determination is negative, so A's offer is considered for award [@CL-CMP-7101], and the determination does not bar a later criminal or civil action about the same transactions [@CL-CMP-7102]. Offeror B modifies paragraph (b) of its certificate, so the contracting officer must reject B's offer [@CL-CMP-6831] and report the situation to the Attorney General [@CL-CMP-6834]. Offeror C publishes a price list for the items and tells its customers that a revised list is coming, and neither fact is disclosure in itself [@CL-CMP-6827].

## Never confuse

Deleting or modifying the certificate is a different trigger from a suspected false certificate. Deleting or modifying paragraph (a)(1) or (3) or paragraph (b) leads to rejection, and deleting or modifying paragraph (a)(2) leads to the chief's review [@CL-CMP-6831; @CL-CMP-6833]. A rejection under paragraph (b)(1) or (2), and a certificate suspected of being false, both lead to a report to the Attorney General [@CL-CMP-6834]. The provision and its companion clause sit in [Independent price determination and contingent fee clauses](../regulatory-framework/cmp-independent-price-and-contingent-fee-clauses.md), and the antitrust patterns that prompt reports are in [Antitrust red flags and reporting](../regulatory-framework/cmp-antitrust-red-flags-and-reporting.md).
