## Purpose

Shows how a contracting office evaluates the Certificate of Independent Price Determination, which facts do not by themselves count as disclosure, and what follows when an offeror deletes or modifies part of the certificate.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-6826] |
| Codified citation | FAR 3.103-2 [@CL-CMP-6826; @CL-CMP-0699]; FAR 3.103-3 [@CL-CMP-0700; @CL-CMP-9627] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-6826] |
| Applies to | Offers that carry the Certificate of Independent Price Determination [@CL-CMP-6826] |

None of the following facts, in and of itself, is considered a "disclosure" as the term is used in paragraph (a)(2) of the Certificate of Independent Price Determination [@CL-CMP-6826]: (1) the fact that a firm has published price lists, rates, or tariffs covering items being acquired by the Government, (2) the fact that a firm has informed prospective customers of proposed or pending publication of new or revised price lists for such items [@CL-CMP-6827], (3) the fact that a firm has sold the same items to commercial customers at the same prices being offered to the Government, or (4) the fact that a firm processes and submits offers through a reverse auction [@CL-CMP-6828].

For the purpose of paragraph (b)(2) of the certificate, an individual may use a blanket authorization to act as an agent for the person(s) responsible for determining the offered prices if (i) the proposed contract to which the certificate applies is clearly within the scope of the authorization, and (ii) the person giving the authorization is the person within the offeror's organization who is responsible for determining the prices being offered at the time the certification is made in the particular offer [@CL-CMP-6829; @CL-CMP-0699; @CL-CMP-7100].

## How it works

If an offer is submitted jointly by two or more concerns, the certification provided by the representative of each concern applies only to the activities of that concern [@CL-CMP-6830]. Rejection of an offer for modification of the certificate shall be made in accordance with paragraphs (b)(1) or (2): (1) if the offeror deleted or modified paragraph (a)(1) or (3) or paragraph (b) of the certificate, the contracting officer shall reject the bid or proposal [@CL-CMP-6831], and (2) if the offeror deleted or modified paragraph (a)(2) of the certificate, the offeror must have furnished with its offer a signed statement of the circumstances of the disclosure of prices [@CL-CMP-6832]. The chief of the contracting office shall review the altered certificate and the statement and shall determine, in writing, whether the disclosure was made for the purpose or had the effect of restricting competition [@CL-CMP-6833]. If the determination is positive, the bid or proposal shall be rejected, and if it is negative, the bid or proposal shall be considered for award [@CL-CMP-7101].

Whenever an offer is rejected under paragraph (b)(1) or (2) of section 3.103-2, or the certificate is suspected of being false, the contracting officer shall report the situation to the Attorney General in accordance with 3.303 [@CL-CMP-6834]. Determinations made under paragraph (b)(2) of section 3.103-2 shall not prevent or inhibit the prosecution of any criminal or civil actions contemplated by the Department of Justice for transactions or occurrences to which the certificate relates [@CL-CMP-7102].

A contractor that properly executed the certificate before award does not have to submit a separate certificate with each proposal to perform a work order or similar ordering instrument issued pursuant to the terms of the contract, where the Government's requirements cannot be met from another source [@CL-CMP-0700; @CL-CMP-9627].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

Offeror A deletes paragraph (a)(2) from the certificate and attaches a signed statement describing how its price was shown to another firm before offers were due. The chief of the contracting office reviews the altered certificate and the statement and determines in writing that the disclosure was not made for the purpose, and did not have the effect, of restricting competition. That determination is negative, so A's offer is considered for award, and the determination does not bar a later criminal or civil action about the same transactions. Offeror B modifies paragraph (b) of its certificate, so the contracting officer must reject B's offer and report the situation to the Attorney General. Offeror C publishes a price list for the items and tells its customers that a revised list is coming, and neither fact is disclosure in itself.

## Never confuse

Deleting or modifying paragraph (a)(1) or (3) or paragraph (b) of the certificate requires the contracting officer to reject the bid or proposal, while deleting or modifying paragraph (a)(2) requires a signed statement of the circumstances of the disclosure and a written determination by the chief of the contracting office [@CL-CMP-6831; @CL-CMP-6832; @CL-CMP-6833]. A rejection under paragraph (b)(1) or (2), and a certificate suspected of being false, both require the contracting officer to report the situation to the Attorney General [@CL-CMP-6834]. The provision and its companion clause sit in [Independent price determination and contingent fee clauses](../regulatory-framework/cmp-independent-price-and-contingent-fee-clauses.md), and the antitrust patterns that prompt reports are in [Antitrust red flags and reporting](../regulatory-framework/cmp-antitrust-red-flags-and-reporting.md).
