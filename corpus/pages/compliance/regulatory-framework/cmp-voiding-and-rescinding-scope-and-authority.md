## Purpose

Explains the Government's discretionary authority to declare a contract void and rescind it after a final conviction for bribery, conflict of interest or similar misconduct, or after an agency head determination about bid or proposal information. It also names who holds that authority and the separate duty on a Federal agency to consider rescission.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-7727] |
| Codified citation | FAR 3.700, FAR 3.701, FAR 3.702 and FAR 3.703 [@CL-CMP-7727; @CL-CMP-7756; @CL-CMP-9704; @CL-CMP-7732] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-7727] |
| Applies to | Contracts in relation to which there has been a final conviction for the listed misconduct, or an agency head determination about disclosure or receipt of contractor bid or proposal information or source selection information [@CL-CMP-7728; @CL-CMP-7729] |

The subpart prescribes Governmentwide policies and procedures for exercising discretionary authority to declare void and rescind contracts in relation to which one of two listed events has occurred [@CL-CMP-7727]. The first event is a final conviction for bribery, conflict of interest, disclosure or receipt of contractor bid or proposal information or source selection information in exchange for a thing of value or to give anyone a competitive advantage in the award of a Federal agency procurement contract, or similar misconduct [@CL-CMP-7728]. The second is an agency head determination that contractor bid or proposal information or source selection information has been disclosed or received in exchange for a thing of value, or for the purpose of obtaining or giving anyone a competitive advantage in the award of a Federal agency procurement contract [@CL-CMP-7729].

The subpart does not prescribe policies or procedures for, or govern the exercise of, any other remedy available to the Government with respect to such contracts, including but not limited to, the common law right of avoidance, rescission, or cancellation [@CL-CMP-7730].

## How it works

The purpose section states that the subpart provides an administrative remedy with respect to contracts in relation to which either of the two events has occurred [@CL-CMP-7756; @CL-CMP-7757; @CL-CMP-7758], and a means to deter similar misconduct in the future by those who are involved in the award, performance, and administration of Government contracts [@CL-CMP-7731].

Section 1(e) of Public Law 87-849, section 218 of title 18 of the United States Code (the Act), empowers the President or the heads of executive agencies acting under regulations prescribed by the President to declare void and rescind contracts and other transactions enumerated in the Act, in relation to which there has been a final conviction for bribery, conflict of interest, or any other violation of Chapter 11 of Title 18 of the United States Code (sections 201 through 224 of title 18) [@CL-CMP-7732; @CL-CMP-7733]. Executive Order 12448, November 4, 1983, delegates the President's authority under the Act to the heads of the executive agencies and military departments [@CL-CMP-7734].

Under section 2105(c) of title 41 of the United States Code, a Federal agency is required, upon receiving information that a contractor or a person has violated section 2102 of title 41, to consider rescission of a contract with respect to which one of two alternatives applies [@CL-CMP-7735]: the contractor or someone acting for the contractor has been convicted for an offense punishable under section 2105(a) of title 41 [@CL-CMP-7736], or the head of the agency, or designee, has determined, based upon a preponderance of the evidence, that the contractor or someone acting for the contractor has engaged in conduct constituting such an offense [@CL-CMP-7737].

The subpart defines a final conviction as a conviction, whether entered on a verdict or plea, including a plea of nolo contendere, for which sentence has been imposed [@CL-CMP-9704].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An agency learns that a person acting for a contractor has been convicted, and the conviction is final, of bribery tied to the award of a services contract. The contract is one in relation to which a final conviction for bribery has occurred, so the subpart applies [@CL-CMP-7728]. The authority to declare the contract void and rescind it is discretionary [@CL-CMP-7727], and it lies with the President or, by delegation, the head of the agency [@CL-CMP-7732; @CL-CMP-7734]. If the agency has also received information that a contractor or a person has violated section 2102 of title 41, and the conviction is for an offense punishable under section 2105(a) of title 41, the agency must also consider rescission [@CL-CMP-7735; @CL-CMP-7736]. Any other remedy available to the Government, including but not limited to the common law right of avoidance, rescission, or cancellation, is not governed by this subpart [@CL-CMP-7730]. The file records the conviction and the decision. No calculation applies.

## Never confuse

The subpart does not prescribe policies or procedures for, or govern the exercise of, any other remedy available to the Government with respect to such contracts, including but not limited to, the common law right of avoidance, rescission, or cancellation [@CL-CMP-7730].

The determination in FAR 3.700(a)(2) is an agency head determination that contractor bid or proposal information or source selection information has been disclosed or received in exchange for a thing of value, or for the purpose of obtaining or giving anyone a competitive advantage in the award of a Federal agency procurement contract [@CL-CMP-7729], whereas the determination in FAR 3.703(b)(2) is a determination by the head of the agency, or designee, based upon a preponderance of the evidence, that the contractor or someone acting for the contractor has engaged in conduct constituting an offense punishable under section 2105(a) of title 41 of the United States Code [@CL-CMP-7736; @CL-CMP-7737]. For the wider treatment of the remedy, see [Voiding and rescinding contracts](cmp-voiding-and-rescinding-contracts.md).
