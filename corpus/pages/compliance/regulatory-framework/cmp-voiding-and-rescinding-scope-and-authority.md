## Purpose

Explains the Government's discretionary authority to declare a contract void and rescind it after a final conviction for bribery, conflict of interest or similar misconduct, or after an agency head determination about bid or proposal information. It also names who holds that authority and the separate duty on a Federal agency to consider rescission.

## The rule

| Codified citation | FAR 3.700, FAR 3.701 and FAR 3.703 [@CL-CMP-7727; @CL-CMP-7756; @CL-CMP-7732] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-7727] |
| Applies to | Contracts in relation to which there has been a final conviction for the listed misconduct, or an agency head determination about disclosure or receipt of contractor bid or proposal information or source selection information [@CL-CMP-7728; @CL-CMP-7729] |

The subpart prescribes Governmentwide policies and procedures for exercising discretionary authority to declare void and rescind contracts in relation to which one of two listed events has occurred [@CL-CMP-7727]. The first event is a final conviction for bribery, conflict of interest, disclosure or receipt of contractor bid or proposal information or source selection information in exchange for a thing of value or to give anyone a competitive advantage in the award of a Federal agency procurement contract, or similar misconduct [@CL-CMP-7728]. The second is an agency head determination that contractor bid or proposal information or source selection information has been disclosed or received in exchange for a thing of value, or for the purpose of obtaining or giving anyone a competitive advantage in the award of a Federal agency procurement contract [@CL-CMP-7729].

The subpart does not prescribe policies or procedures for, or govern the exercise of, any other remedy available to the Government with respect to such contracts. Its text names the common law right of avoidance, rescission or cancellation as one example [@CL-CMP-7730].

## How it works

The purpose section repeats the same two events. The subpart provides an administrative remedy for contracts in relation to which either event has occurred [@CL-CMP-7756; @CL-CMP-7757; @CL-CMP-7758]. It also provides a means to deter similar misconduct in the future by those who are involved in the award, performance and administration of Government contracts [@CL-CMP-7731].

The authority is statutory. Section 1(e) of Public Law 87-849, at section 218 of title 18 of the United States Code (the Act), empowers the President, or the heads of executive agencies acting under regulations prescribed by the President, to declare void and rescind contracts and other transactions enumerated in the Act [@CL-CMP-7732]. The section states this power in relation to a final conviction for bribery, conflict of interest, or any other violation of chapter 11 of title 18 (sections 201 through 224) [@CL-CMP-7733]. Executive Order 12448 delegates the President's authority under the Act to the heads of the executive agencies and military departments [@CL-CMP-7734].

A separate duty falls on the Federal agency. Section 2105(c) of title 41 requires it, on receiving information that a contractor or a person has violated section 2102 of title 41, to consider rescission of a contract with respect to which a listed condition applies [@CL-CMP-7735]. One condition is that the contractor, or someone acting for the contractor, has been convicted for an offense punishable under section 2105(a) of title 41 [@CL-CMP-7736]. The other is that the head of the agency, or a designee, has determined, based upon a preponderance of the evidence, that the contractor or someone acting for the contractor has engaged in conduct constituting such an offense [@CL-CMP-7737].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An agency learns that a person acting for a contractor has been convicted, and the conviction is final, of bribery tied to the award of a services contract. The contract is one in relation to which a final conviction for bribery has occurred, so the subpart applies [@CL-CMP-7728]. The authority to declare the contract void and rescind it is discretionary [@CL-CMP-7727], and it lies with the President or, by delegation, the head of the agency [@CL-CMP-7732; @CL-CMP-7734]. If the agency has also received information that a contractor or a person has violated section 2102 of title 41, and the conviction is for an offense punishable under section 2105(a) of title 41, the agency must also consider rescission [@CL-CMP-7735; @CL-CMP-7736]. Any other remedy available to the Government, such as common law avoidance, is outside this subpart, which does not govern it [@CL-CMP-7730]. The file records the conviction and the decision. No calculation applies.

## Never confuse

Voiding or rescinding under this subpart is not the common law right of avoidance, rescission or cancellation. The subpart does not prescribe policies or procedures for it or govern its exercise [@CL-CMP-7730].

Two determinations are different. The determination in the scope section is by an agency head and concerns disclosure or receipt of contractor bid or proposal information or source selection information [@CL-CMP-7729]. The determination behind the duty to consider rescission is by the head of the agency or a designee, on a preponderance of the evidence, about conduct constituting an offense punishable under section 2105(a) of title 41 [@CL-CMP-7737]. The wider treatment of the remedy is in [Voiding and rescinding contracts](cmp-voiding-and-rescinding-contracts.md).
