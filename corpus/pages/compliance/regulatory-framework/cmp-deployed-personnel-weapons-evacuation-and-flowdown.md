## Purpose

Shows the later duties in the deployed-forces clause: military clothing, weapons, evacuation, next of kin and recovery, mortuary affairs, changes, and the flowdown to subcontracts. It does not set out every duty in those paragraphs, and it says where it stops.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-CMP-4174] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 252.225-7040 [@CL-CMP-4174] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-4174] |
| Applies to | Contracts that include the clause, and subcontracts when subcontractor personnel support deployed forces in one of the listed operations [@CL-CMP-4174; @CL-CMP-8617] |

Contractor personnel are prohibited from wearing military clothing unless specifically authorized in writing by the Combatant Commander [@CL-CMP-4164]. If the Contractor requests that its personnel performing in the designated operational area be authorized to carry weapons for individual self-defense, the request must be made through the Contracting Officer to the Combatant Commander, in accordance with the cited Department of Defense Instruction [@CL-CMP-4165]. The Combatant Commander will determine whether to authorize in-theater Contractor personnel to carry weapons and what weapons and ammunition will be allowed [@CL-CMP-4166]. When authorized in accordance with the weapons paragraph to carry arms for personal protection, Contractor personnel are only authorized to use force for individual self-defense [@CL-CMP-4212]. Whether or not weapons are Government-furnished, all liability for their use rests solely with the Contractor and the Contractor employee using the weapon [@CL-CMP-4167].

The Contractor must ensure that its personnel who are authorized to carry weapons are adequately trained to carry and use them safely, with full understanding of, and adherence to, the rules of the use of force issued by the Combatant Commander, and in compliance with applicable agency policies, agreements, rules, regulations, and other applicable law [@CL-CMP-4202; @CL-CMP-8618]. The same list of duties also includes not being barred from possession of a firearm by the federal firearms statute the clause cites, and adhering to all guidance and orders issued by the Combatant Commander regarding possession, use, safety, and accountability of weapons and ammunition [@CL-CMP-4203]. Upon redeployment or revocation by the Combatant Commander of the Contractor's authorization to issue firearms, the Contractor must ensure that all Government-issued weapons and unexpended ammunition are returned as directed by the Contracting Officer [@CL-CMP-4211].

This card does not set out every weapons duty or every condition on military clothing. The clause also covers what the Contracting Officer tells the Contractor about authorized weapons, further items in the list of duties for armed personnel, the conditions that apply to authorized military clothing, and the issue and return of protective equipment. The clause text controls.

If the Combatant Commander orders a mandatory evacuation of some or all personnel, the Government will provide assistance, to the extent available, to United States and third country national Contractor personnel [@CL-CMP-4168]. In the event of a non-mandatory evacuation order, unless authorized in writing by the Contracting Officer, the Contractor must maintain personnel on location sufficient to meet its obligations under the contract [@CL-CMP-4169].

The Contractor is responsible for notifying the employee-designated next of kin when an employee dies, requires evacuation due to an injury, or is isolated, missing, detained, captured, or abducted [@CL-CMP-4170]. In the case of isolated, missing, detained, captured, or abducted Contractor personnel, the Government will assist in personnel recovery actions in accordance with the cited Department of Defense directive on personnel recovery [@CL-CMP-4171]. Contractor personnel who die while in support of the forces are to be covered by the DoD mortuary affairs program [@CL-CMP-4172].

In addition to the changes otherwise authorized by the Changes clause, the Contracting Officer may, at any time, by written order identified as a change order, change the place of performance or Government-furnished facilities, equipment, material, services, or site [@CL-CMP-4173]. The Contractor must incorporate the substance of the clause, including its flowdown paragraph, in all subcontracts when subcontractor personnel support deployed forces in contingency operations [@CL-CMP-4174], in peace operations consistent with Joint Publication 3-07.3, or in other military operations or military exercises when designated by the Combatant Commander or as directed by the Secretary of Defense [@CL-CMP-8617].

## How it works

The Combatant Commander decides two permissions: written authorization to wear military clothing [@CL-CMP-4164] and authorization to carry weapons [@CL-CMP-4165; @CL-CMP-4166]. Authorized armed personnel may use force only for individual self-defense [@CL-CMP-4212], and liability for weapon use stays with the Contractor and the employee [@CL-CMP-4167]. Weapons duties continue after authorization, including training and return of Government-issued weapons [@CL-CMP-4202; @CL-CMP-4211].

On a mandatory evacuation order the Government will provide assistance to the extent available [@CL-CMP-4168]. On a non-mandatory order the Contractor must keep enough personnel on location unless the Contracting Officer authorizes otherwise in writing [@CL-CMP-4169].

The Contracting Officer's added power to change the place of performance or Government-furnished items sits alongside the ordinary Changes clause [@CL-CMP-4173]. Other deployed-forces duties are on [the companion card on definitions and pre-deployment](cmp-deployed-contractor-personnel-definitions-and-predeployment.md), and private security work is on [the private security card](cmp-private-security-functions-outside-the-united-states.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor asks to arm four guards for self-defense. It sends the request through the Contracting Officer to the Combatant Commander, who decides whether to approve and which weapons are allowed.

A guard later uses a weapon in an incident. Liability for that use rests with the contractor and the guard, whether or not the Government issued the weapon.

## Never confuse

In the event of a non-mandatory evacuation order, unless authorized in writing by the Contracting Officer, the Contractor must maintain personnel on location sufficient to meet its obligations under the contract [@CL-CMP-4169].

The Contracting Officer's change power under this clause is in addition to the changes otherwise authorized by the Changes clause [@CL-CMP-4173]. A contracting officer's representative is not authorized to make any commitments or changes that will affect price, quality, quantity, delivery, or any other term or condition of the contract [@CL-CMP-4198]; see [the card on the representative's authority](cmp-former-dod-official-representation-and-cor-authority.md).

When authorized to carry arms for personal protection, Contractor personnel are only authorized to use force for individual self-defense [@CL-CMP-4212], and the Contractor must ensure they are trained and follow Combatant Commander orders on weapons [@CL-CMP-4202; @CL-CMP-4203].
