## Purpose

Sets out what the aerosols clause and the foams clause require of a contractor that uses high global warming potential hydrofluorocarbons in aerosol propellants, solvents or foam blowing agents.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-5207] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.223-20 and FAR 52.223-21 [@CL-CMP-5207; @CL-CMP-5211] |
| RFO citation | Reserved in the RFO text: the RFO text lists RFO FAR 52.223-20 and RFO FAR 52.223-21 as reserved [@CL-CMP-0349; @CL-CMP-0350] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-5207] |
| Applies to | Contractors whose contracts carry the aerosols clause or the foams clause [@CL-CMP-5207; @CL-CMP-5211] |

Under the aerosols clause, unless the contract specifies otherwise, the contractor must reduce its use, release or emissions of high global warming potential hydrofluorocarbons, when feasible, from aerosol propellants or solvents under the contract [@CL-CMP-5207].

Under the foams clause, unless the contract specifies otherwise, the contractor must reduce its use, release and emissions of high global warming potential hydrofluorocarbons and refrigerant blends containing hydrofluorocarbons, when feasible, from foam blowing agents under the contract [@CL-CMP-5211].

Each clause defines high global warming potential hydrofluorocarbons as any hydrofluorocarbons in a particular end use for which EPA's Significant New Alternatives Policy program has identified other acceptable alternatives that have lower global warming potential [@CL-CMP-5302; @CL-CMP-5305]. Hydrofluorocarbons are compounds that contain only hydrogen, fluorine, and carbon [@CL-CMP-5303; @CL-CMP-5306]. Global warming potential is how much a given mass of a chemical contributes to global warming over a given time period compared to the same mass of carbon dioxide, and the potential of carbon dioxide is defined as 1.0 [@CL-CMP-5301; @CL-CMP-5304].

## How it works

Under the aerosols clause the contractor must consider environmental, technical and economic factors in judging whether using an alternative is feasible, such as in-use emission rates, energy efficiency, and safety such as flammability or toxicity [@CL-CMP-5208]. The listed factors also include the ability to meet technical performance requirements and commercial availability at a reasonable cost [@CL-CMP-5209].

Under the foams clause the contractor likewise must consider environmental, technical and economic factors, such as in-use emission rates, energy efficiency and safety [@CL-CMP-5212]. The listed factors also include the ability to meet performance requirements and commercial availability at a reasonable cost [@CL-CMP-5307].

Both clauses direct the contractor to refer to EPA's Significant New Alternatives Policy program to identify alternatives, whose list is found at 40 CFR part 82, subpart G, with supplemental tables available on the EPA website [@CL-CMP-5210; @CL-CMP-5213].

Each duty is a duty to reduce, when feasible, unless the contract specifies otherwise [@CL-CMP-5207; @CL-CMP-5211].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text under Class Deviation 2026-O0038, effective February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. The RFO text lists FAR 52.223-20 as reserved [@CL-CMP-0349]. It also lists FAR 52.223-21 as reserved [@CL-CMP-0350].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor uses an aerosol solvent whose propellant is a high global warming potential hydrofluorocarbon, and a foam sealant whose blowing agent is one too, and the contract does not specify otherwise. For the aerosol it finds a lower-potential alternative that meets the technical performance requirements, is available at a reasonable cost and is not flammable in its process, so it switches that line. For the foam it keeps the current blowing agent because no alternative it has found meets the performance requirements, and it documents that assessment in its own files. In both cases it checks EPA's alternatives program to identify alternatives.

## Never confuse

The two clauses are not the same duty: the aerosols clause covers aerosol propellants or solvents, and the foams clause adds refrigerant blends containing hydrofluorocarbons [@CL-CMP-5211; @CL-CMP-5207]. Under both, the duty is to reduce use, release or emissions when feasible, unless the contract specifies otherwise [@CL-CMP-5207; @CL-CMP-5211]; in determining feasibility the aerosols clause requires consideration of environmental, technical and economic factors, including emission rates, safety, technical performance and commercial availability at a reasonable cost, and the foams clause requires consideration of the same kinds of factor [@CL-CMP-5208; @CL-CMP-5209; @CL-CMP-5212; @CL-CMP-5307]. The codified FAR 52.223-20 and FAR 52.223-21 hold the aerosols and foams duties to reduce when feasible, while the RFO text prints both section numbers as reserved [@CL-CMP-5207; @CL-CMP-5211; @CL-CMP-0349; @CL-CMP-0350]. The clauses on ozone-depleting substances and refrigerant handling are covered in [Ozone-depleting substances and refrigerant handling](cmp-ozone-depleting-substances-and-refrigerant-handling.md).

## Company calibration

Whether product and process records show which aerosols and foams use high global warming potential hydrofluorocarbons and how feasibility was assessed. [@Q-004]
