## Purpose

Shows where contracting authority sits, who may bind the Government, and how the acquisition team reads a gap in the regulation.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-6867] |
| Codified citation | FAR 1.601, 1.602-1, 1.602-2 and 1.102-5 [@CL-CMP-6867; @CL-CMP-6868; @CL-CMP-7165; @CL-CMP-7166; @CL-CMP-6870] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-6867] |
| Applies to | Agency heads, contracting officers and the Government members of the acquisition team, and the contractors who deal with them [@CL-CMP-6867; @CL-CMP-6868; @CL-CMP-7164] |

Unless specifically prohibited by another provision of law, authority and responsibility to contract for authorized supplies and services are vested in the agency head [@CL-CMP-6867]. The agency head may establish contracting activities and delegate broad authority to manage the agency's contracting functions to the heads of those activities [@CL-CMP-7156]. Contracts may be entered into and signed on behalf of the Government only by contracting officers [@CL-CMP-6868]. In some agencies a relatively small number of high level officials are designated contracting officers solely by virtue of their positions [@CL-CMP-7157]. Contracting officers below the level of a head of a contracting activity must be selected and appointed under FAR 1.603 [@CL-CMP-7158].

Agency heads may mutually agree to assign contracting functions and responsibilities from one agency to another, and to create joint or combined offices to exercise acquisition functions and responsibilities [@CL-CMP-6869].

## How it works

The Acquisition Team consists of all participants in Government acquisition, including the technical, supply and procurement communities, the customers they serve, and the contractors who provide the products and services [@CL-CMP-7164].

Government members of the Team must be empowered to make acquisition decisions within their areas of responsibility, including selection, negotiation and administration of contracts, consistent with the Guiding Principles [@CL-CMP-7159]. In particular, the contracting officer must have the authority, to the maximum extent practicable and consistent with law, to determine the application of rules, regulations and policies on a specific contract [@CL-CMP-7160]. Authority to make decisions, and accountability for them, will be delegated to the lowest level within the System, consistent with law [@CL-CMP-7161]. The System will foster cooperative relationships between the Government and its contractors, consistent with its overriding responsibility to the taxpayers [@CL-CMP-7162].

If a policy, procedure, strategy or practice is in the best interest of the Government and is not specifically addressed in the FAR, nor prohibited by law, Executive order or other regulation, Government members of the Team should not assume it is prohibited [@CL-CMP-6870]. Absence of direction should be interpreted as permitting the Team to innovate and use sound business judgment that is otherwise consistent with law and within the limits of their authority [@CL-CMP-6871].

Contracting officers have authority to enter into, administer or terminate contracts and make related determinations and findings, but may bind the Government only to the extent of the authority delegated to them [@CL-CMP-7165]. A contracting officer's representative has no authority to make any commitments or changes that affect price, quality, quantity, delivery or other terms and conditions of the contract, nor in any way to direct the contractor or its subcontractors to operate in conflict with the contract terms and conditions [@CL-CMP-7166; @CL-CMP-7285].

Information on the limits of contracting officers' authority must be readily available to the public and agency personnel [@CL-CMP-7283], and a contracting officer's representative must be designated in writing, with copies furnished to the contractor, specifying the extent and the limitations of that representative's authority [@CL-CMP-7285]. No contract may be entered into unless the contracting officer ensures that all requirements of law, executive orders, regulations and other applicable procedures, including clearances and approvals, have been met [@CL-CMP-7284]. The linked pages cover the contracting officer's authority and the route for ratifying an unauthorized commitment: [Contracting officer authority and CORs](../../operating-model/decision-rights/opm-contracting-officer-authority-and-cors.md) and [Ratification of unauthorized commitments](../../operating-model/decision-rights/opm-ratification-of-unauthorized-commitments.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A program engineer who is not a contracting officer asks a supplier to add a test fixture and says the price will be settled later. The engineer's request is not a contract entered into and signed on behalf of the Government [@CL-CMP-6868], and a contracting officer's representative has no authority to make changes that affect price or other terms and conditions of the contract [@CL-CMP-7166; @CL-CMP-7285]. The supplier records the request but does not start work, and sends it to the contracting officer. The contracting officer, acting within the authority delegated to the officer, issues a modification worth 40,000 before the work begins [@CL-CMP-7165].

## Never confuse

Contracts may be entered into and signed on behalf of the Government only by contracting officers [@CL-CMP-6868], and contracting officers may bind the Government only to the extent of the authority delegated to them [@CL-CMP-7165]. The team rule that absence of direction should be interpreted as permitting the Team to innovate and use sound business judgment applies within the limits of their authority [@CL-CMP-6871].
