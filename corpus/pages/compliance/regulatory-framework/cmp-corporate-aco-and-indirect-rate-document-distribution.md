## Purpose

Shows when a corporate administrative contracting officer may be assigned, which agency approves a State or local government's indirect costs, and who receives the executed rate agreement and its supporting documents. The sections state their rules for Government personnel and agencies. The contractor receives the executed agreement.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-7814] |
| Codified citation | FAR 42.601, FAR 42.705-4 and FAR 42.706 [@CL-CMP-7814; @CL-CMP-7816; @CL-CMP-7818] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-7814] |
| Applies to | Contractors with more than one operational location [@CL-CMP-7814]; State and local governments with federally-funded programs and activities [@CL-CMP-7991]; executed indirect cost rate agreements [@CL-CMP-7818] |

Contractors with more than one operational location often have corporate-wide policies, procedures and activities requiring Government review and approval and affecting the work of more than one administrative contracting officer [@CL-CMP-7814]. In those circumstances, effective and consistent contract administration may require the assignment of a corporate administrative contracting officer to deal with corporate management and to perform selected contract administration functions on a corporate-wide basis [@CL-CMP-7815]. The section does not say who makes the assignment.

For State and local governments, the OMB Uniform Guidance at 2 CFR part 200, subpart E and appendix V, concerning cost principles for state and local governments, establishes the cognizant agency concept and the procedures for determining a cognizant agency [@CL-CMP-7816]. That agency is the one for approving State and local government indirect costs associated with federally-funded programs and activities [@CL-CMP-7991]. The indirect cost rates negotiated and approved by the cognizant agency for indirect costs will be used by all Federal agencies that also award contracts to these same State and local governments [@CL-CMP-7817].

## How it works

The contracting officer or auditor shall promptly distribute executed copies of the indirect cost rate agreement to the contractor and to each affected contracting agency, and shall provide copies of the agreement for the contract files [@CL-CMP-7818]. They do this in accordance with the guidance for contract modifications in FAR subpart 4.2, Contract Distribution [@CL-CMP-7992]. The section names both the contracting officer and the auditor and does not say which one acts in a given case. The contracting officer prepares the agreement under contracting officer determination and the auditor prepares it under auditor determination [@CL-RTE-0110; @CL-RTE-0216]. Both procedures are on [Final indirect cost rates: contracting officer determination procedure](../../indirect-rates/rate-lifecycle/rte-co-final-indirect-rates.md) and [Final indirect cost rates: auditor determination procedure](../../indirect-rates/rate-lifecycle/rte-final-indirect-rate-auditor-determination.md).

Copies of the negotiation memorandum prepared under contracting officer determination, or the audit report prepared under auditor determination, shall be furnished, as appropriate, to the contracting offices and Government audit offices [@CL-CMP-7819].

How the cognizant agency is set for other contractors is on [Cognizant Federal agency, completion invoices and educational institution rate settlement](../audit-retention/cmp-cognizant-agency-and-completion-invoices.md). The contract administration functions are on [Contract administration functions](../audit-retention/cmp-contract-administration-functions.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor has 4 divisions, and each division has its own administrative contracting officer. The contractor's corporate-wide policies need Government review and approval and affect the work of all 4 officers, so effective and consistent administration may require a corporate administrative contracting officer to deal with corporate management [@CL-CMP-7814; @CL-CMP-7815].

A county government has 3 federally-funded programs and holds contracts from 2 Federal agencies. The cognizant agency for indirect costs negotiates and approves its indirect cost rates, and both Federal agencies use those rates [@CL-CMP-7991; @CL-CMP-7817]. The contracting officer then distributes executed copies of the rate agreement promptly to the county and to both affected agencies, provides copies for the contract files, and follows the distribution guidance in subpart 4.2 [@CL-CMP-7818; @CL-CMP-7992]. The negotiation memorandum goes, as appropriate, to the contracting offices and the Government audit offices [@CL-CMP-7819]. The answer: one approved rate set used by every Federal agency awarding contracts to the county, with the executed agreement sent to the county and each affected agency.

## Never confuse

A corporate administrative contracting officer deals with corporate management and corporate-wide functions across locations [@CL-CMP-7815]. A corporate administrative contracting officer is not the cognizant agency, which is the agency that approves a State or local government's indirect costs [@CL-CMP-7816; @CL-CMP-7991].
