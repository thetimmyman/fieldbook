## Purpose

Shows the conduct standard that the general rule in this section sets for Government business and for the Government-contractor relationship.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-7637] |
| Codified citation | FAR 3.101-1 [@CL-CMP-7637] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-7637] |
| Applies to | Government business, transactions that expend public funds, Government-contractor relationships and Government personnel [@CL-CMP-7637; @CL-CMP-7638; @CL-CMP-7639; @CL-CMP-7640] |

Government business shall be conducted in a manner above reproach and, except as authorized by statute or regulation, with complete impartiality and with preferential treatment for none [@CL-CMP-7637]. Transactions relating to the expenditure of public funds require the highest degree of public trust and an impeccable standard of conduct [@CL-CMP-7638].

The general rule is to avoid strictly any conflict of interest, or even the appearance of one, in Government-contractor relationships [@CL-CMP-7639]. Many Federal laws and regulations place restrictions on the actions of Government personnel [@CL-CMP-7640]. Their official conduct must, in addition, be such that they would have no reluctance to make a full public disclosure of their actions [@CL-CMP-7640].

## How it works

The section is four sentences. The first sets the standard for how Government business is conducted. The words "except as authorized by statute or regulation" stand in front of the impartiality and no-preference parts, so a statute or regulation can authorize a departure from those parts [@CL-CMP-7637]. The second raises the bar for transactions that expend public funds [@CL-CMP-7638]. The third states the general rule for Government-contractor relationships [@CL-CMP-7639]. The fourth adds a disclosure test for the official conduct of Government personnel on top of the laws and regulations that already restrict them [@CL-CMP-7640].

The section does not say who decides whether conduct meets the standard. It sets no threshold, deadline, form or procedure. It names no duty for a contractor. The specific rules that reach contractors sit in other sections, for example [Gratuities, termination and exemplary damages](cmp-gratuities.md), [Procurement integrity: bid and proposal information and the disclosure and obtaining bans](cmp-procurement-integrity-prohibitions.md) and [Contracts with Government employees and mitigation of personal conflicts of interest](cmp-government-employee-contracts-and-personal-conflicts.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An agency official helps evaluate proposals for a services contract. The official owns 200 shares in one of the competing companies. The general rule is to avoid strictly any conflict of interest, or even the appearance of one, in Government-contractor relationships [@CL-CMP-7639]. This section does not prescribe the step the official or the agency must take. It states that the official's conduct must be such that the official would have no reluctance to make a full public disclosure of it [@CL-CMP-7640]. Other sections and laws set the specific steps.

## Never confuse

The disclosure test adds to the Federal laws and regulations that restrict Government personnel. It does not replace them [@CL-CMP-7640]. The general rule calls for avoiding a conflict of interest and also the appearance of one [@CL-CMP-7639]. This section states standards. It does not itself set a penalty or remedy for a contractor; see [Penalties and remedies for procurement integrity violations](cmp-procurement-integrity-penalties-and-remedies.md) and [Treatment of gratuities violations: hearing, determination and remedies](cmp-gratuities-violation-hearing-and-remedies.md) for rules that do.
