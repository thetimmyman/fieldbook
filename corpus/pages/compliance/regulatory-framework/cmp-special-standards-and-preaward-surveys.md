## Purpose

Shows when the contracting officer develops special standards of responsibility, how they are published, what a request for a preaward survey must contain, and which rules apply when the surveying activity is in another agency [@CL-CMP-7585; @CL-CMP-7588; @CL-CMP-7592].

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-7585] |
| Codified citation | FAR 9.104-2, 9.106-2 and 9.106-3 [@CL-CMP-7585; @CL-CMP-7588; @CL-CMP-7592] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-7585] |
| Applies to | Acquisitions or classes of acquisitions for which special standards are necessary, contracts for subsistence, and requests to a surveying activity, including one in a different agency [@CL-CMP-7585; @CL-CMP-7587; @CL-CMP-7588; @CL-CMP-7725] |

When it is necessary for a particular acquisition or class of acquisitions, the contracting officer must develop special standards of responsibility with the assistance of appropriate specialists [@CL-CMP-7585]. The duty to develop them falls on the contracting officer, and the section sets no threshold for when they are necessary. Special standards may be particularly desirable when experience has demonstrated that unusual expertise or specialized facilities are needed for adequate contract performance [@CL-CMP-7723]. The special standards must be set forth in the solicitation, identified as special standards, and apply to all offerors [@CL-CMP-7586].

Contracting officers must award contracts for subsistence only to prospective contractors that meet the general standards of responsibility and are approved in accordance with agency sanitation standards and procedures [@CL-CMP-7587].

The contracting officer's request to the surveying activity must identify the additional factors about which information is needed [@CL-CMP-7588]. It must include the complete solicitation package, unless that package has previously been furnished, and any information indicating prior unsatisfactory performance by the prospective contractor [@CL-CMP-7589]. It must state whether the contracting office will participate in the survey [@CL-CMP-7590]. It must specify the date by which the report is required, which should be consistent with the scope of the survey requested and normally must allow at least seven working days to conduct the survey [@CL-CMP-7591]. When appropriate, it must limit the scope of the survey [@CL-CMP-7724].

The interagency rule applies when the contracting office and the surveying activity are in different agencies [@CL-CMP-7725]. In that case the procedures of the preaward survey section and subpart 42.1 must be followed along with the regulations of the agency in which the surveying activity is located, except that reasonable special requests by the contracting office must be accommodated [@CL-CMP-7592].

## How it works

Special standards are written for the acquisition or class of acquisitions, developed by the contracting officer with specialists, identified in the solicitation and applied to every offeror [@CL-CMP-7585; @CL-CMP-7586]. The survey request is the contracting officer's instruction to the surveying activity. It says what to examine, supplies the solicitation, states whether the contracting office will take part, sets the report date and, where appropriate, limits the scope [@CL-CMP-7588; @CL-CMP-7589; @CL-CMP-7590; @CL-CMP-7591; @CL-CMP-7724]. Where the two offices sit in different agencies, the survey procedures and subpart 42.1 are followed along with the surveying activity's agency regulations, except that the contracting office's reasonable special requests must be accommodated [@CL-CMP-7725; @CL-CMP-7592]. Subsistence awards carry an extra gate through agency sanitation approval in addition to the general standards [@CL-CMP-7587]. The section does not say who decides that a survey is needed, and the linked cards cover that.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An office buying an unusual laboratory service decides that special standards are necessary, develops them with specialists, and puts them in the solicitation, marked as special standards, so they apply to all offerors [@CL-CMP-7585; @CL-CMP-7586].

The office then asks a surveying activity in another agency for a preaward survey. The request names the additional factors to be examined and attaches the solicitation package, which has not been furnished before. It includes an earlier late-delivery record for the prospective contractor and states that the contracting office will take part. It limits the scope to the contractor's laboratory facilities and sets a report date twelve working days out [@CL-CMP-7588; @CL-CMP-7589; @CL-CMP-7590; @CL-CMP-7724]. Twelve working days is more than the normal minimum of seven working days [@CL-CMP-7591]. Because the two offices are in different agencies, the survey procedures and subpart 42.1 are followed along with the surveying activity's agency regulations, except that the office's reasonable special requests are accommodated [@CL-CMP-7725; @CL-CMP-7592].

## Never confuse

Special standards of responsibility are not the general responsibility standards. The special ones are developed for the acquisition, identified in the solicitation and applied to all offerors, while a subsistence contract must meet the general standards and also be approved under agency sanitation standards [@CL-CMP-7586; @CL-CMP-7587]. The survey request is also not the survey itself, because the request tells the surveying activity what is needed and by when [@CL-CMP-7588; @CL-CMP-7591].
