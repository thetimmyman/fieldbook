## Purpose

Shows why buying-in losses may not be recovered through change orders or follow-on pricing, and the rule against unreasonably precluding a subcontractor's direct sales to the Government.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-2048] |
| Codified citation | FAR 3.501-1, FAR 3.501-2 and FAR 3.503-1 [@CL-CMP-2101; @CL-CMP-2048; @CL-CMP-2049] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-2048] |
| Applies to | Pricing of change orders and of follow-on contracts subject to cost analysis, and subcontractors' direct sales of supplies or services made or furnished under a contract [@CL-CMP-2048; @CL-CMP-2049] |

Buying-in may decrease competition or result in poor contract performance, and the contracting officer must take appropriate action to ensure buying-in losses are not recovered by the contractor through the pricing of change orders or follow-on contracts subject to cost analysis [@CL-CMP-2048]. Buying-in means submitting an offer below anticipated costs, expecting to increase the contract amount after award or to achieve the other result listed in the definition [@CL-CMP-2101]. One listed expectation is to receive follow-on contracts at artificially high prices to recover losses incurred on the buy-in contract [@CL-CMP-2101; @CL-CMP-2102].

Two statutes cited in the section require that subcontractors not be unreasonably precluded from making direct sales to the Government of any supplies or services made or furnished under a contract [@CL-CMP-2104]. This does not preclude contractors from asserting rights that are otherwise authorized by law or regulation [@CL-CMP-2049]. The section does not list which restrictions are unreasonable, so that is not established here.

## How it works

The contracting officer decides what action to take on buying-in losses [@CL-CMP-2048]. The action concerns what the contractor can recover: a change order, or a follow-on contract priced under cost analysis, is where a buying-in loss could be recovered, so those are the pricing points the officer protects [@CL-CMP-2048]. For direct sales, the rule bars unreasonable preclusion, and contractors keep the rights that law or regulation otherwise authorizes [@CL-CMP-2049]. The section does not say who decides whether a restriction is unreasonable.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor wins a first contract with a price that is 2 million below its cost. Later, the contractor submits a change order priced to recover that 2 million loss. The contracting officer must take appropriate action so the loss is not recovered through the change order price. Separately, a prime contractor's subcontract bars the subcontractor from ever selling the same part directly to the Government. Whether that bar is unreasonable is a question the section does not settle.

## Never confuse

Buying-in concerns an offer priced below anticipated costs and the recovery of the resulting loss [@CL-CMP-2101; @CL-CMP-2048], while the direct sales rule protects a subcontractor's access to the Government [@CL-CMP-2049]. A contractor asserting rights that law or regulation otherwise authorizes is not breaching the direct sales rule [@CL-CMP-2049].
