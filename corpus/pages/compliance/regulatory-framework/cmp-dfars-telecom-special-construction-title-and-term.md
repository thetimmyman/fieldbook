## Purpose

Shows how special construction and equipment are charged, who holds title to telecommunication facilities and equipment, and how service continues after the basic agreement ends.

The Government will not directly reimburse the Contractor for the cost of constructing any facilities or providing any equipment, unless the Contracting Officer authorizes direct reimbursement [@CL-CMP-4659]. A direct special construction charge shall not exceed the actual costs to the Contractor and shall not exceed an amount properly allocable to the services to be provided to the Government [@CL-CMP-4660]. It shall not include costs covered by a cancellation or termination liability or by the Contractor's recurring or other nonrecurring charges [@CL-CMP-4661].

Title to all Contractor furnished facilities and equipment remains with the Contractor even if the Government paid the costs of constructing them, and a mutually accepted communications service authorization may provide for exceptions [@CL-CMP-4663]. The Contractor shall operate and maintain all telecommunication facilities and equipment whether the Government or the Contractor has title [@CL-CMP-4664].

## How it works

If the Contractor stops using facilities or equipment which the Government has, in whole or part, directly reimbursed, the Contractor shall allow the Government credit for the value attributable to the Government's contribution [@CL-CMP-4662]. The value is determined on the basis of foreseeable reuse by the Contractor at the time use is discontinued or on the basis of the net salvage value, whichever is greater, and the Contractor shall promptly pay the Government the amount of any credit [@CL-CMP-4768]. If the Contractor must incur costs to replace facilities or equipment, the Government assumes those costs or reimburses them at mutually acceptable rates under the circumstances the paragraph lists [@CL-CMP-4769]: either the Government paid direct special construction charges, or the Government reimbursed the Contractor for those facilities or equipment as a part of the recurring charges, and in addition the need for replacement was due to circumstances beyond the control and without the fault of the Contractor [@CL-CMP-4770]. Before incurring any such costs the Government has the right to terminate the service under the Cancellation or Termination of Orders clause [@CL-CMP-4771]. The basic agreement is not a contract, and the Government incurs liability only upon issuance of a communication service authorization, which is a contract that incorporates the agreement's terms [@CL-CMP-4666]. The basic agreement continues in force from year to year unless either party ends it by 30 days' written notice, and ending it does not terminate or cancel authorizations issued before the termination [@CL-CMP-4665]. In the basic version of the clause, authorizations issued under the basic agreement may be modified to incorporate the terms and conditions of a new basic agreement negotiated with the Contractor [@CL-CMP-4709].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

The Government pays a one-time special construction charge of 40,000 for a new line, with the Contracting Officer's authorization. The carrier's actual cost was 40,000, all of it is allocable to Government service, and none of it is covered by a cancellation liability or by recurring charges. The carrier still holds title to the line and must operate and maintain it. If the carrier later stops using the line, it credits the Government for the value attributable to the Government's contribution, measured at the greater of foreseeable reuse and net salvage value, and promptly pays that amount. If the line later has to be replaced because of circumstances beyond the carrier's control and without its fault, the Government, having paid the direct charge, assumes or reimburses the replacement cost at mutually acceptable rates, and before the cost is incurred it may terminate the service under the cancellation clause. Later, the carrier ends the basic agreement on 30 days' notice, and the authorizations already issued stay in force. A new basic agreement is then negotiated, and the old authorizations may be modified to take its terms.

## Never confuse

Under the Title to Telecommunication Facilities and Equipment clause, title to Contractor furnished facilities and equipment remains with the Contractor even if the Government paid the costs of constructing them [@CL-CMP-4663]. For Government property in general, see [Title to Government-furnished and contractor-acquired property](../../close-revrec/fixed-assets/cls-title-to-gfp-and-contractor-acquired-property.md). The basic agreement is not a contract, and a communication service authorization is a contract that incorporates its terms and conditions [@CL-CMP-4666]. A direct special construction charge shall not include costs covered by a cancellation or termination liability [@CL-CMP-4661]; for that liability, see [Cancellation or termination of orders](cmp-dfars-telecom-cancellation-or-termination-of-orders.md). For access to facilities and how orders are acknowledged and paid, see [Access and orders for facilities and services](cmp-dfars-telecom-access-and-orders-for-facilities.md).
