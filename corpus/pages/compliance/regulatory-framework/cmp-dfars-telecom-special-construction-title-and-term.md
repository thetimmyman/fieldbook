## Purpose

Shows how special construction and equipment are charged, who holds title to telecommunication facilities and equipment, and how service continues after the basic agreement ends.

The Government will not directly reimburse the Contractor for the cost of constructing any facilities or providing any equipment, unless the Contracting Officer authorizes direct reimbursement [@CL-CMP-4659]. A direct special construction charge shall not exceed the actual costs to the Contractor and shall not exceed an amount properly allocable to the services to be provided to the Government [@CL-CMP-4660]. It shall not include costs covered by a cancellation or termination liability or by the Contractor's recurring or other nonrecurring charges [@CL-CMP-4661].

Title to all Contractor furnished facilities and equipment remains with the Contractor even if the Government paid the costs of constructing them, and a mutually accepted communications service authorization may provide for exceptions [@CL-CMP-4663]. The Contractor shall operate and maintain all telecommunication facilities and equipment whether the Government or the Contractor has title [@CL-CMP-4664].

## How it works

If the Contractor stops using facilities or equipment which the Government has, in whole or part, directly reimbursed, the Contractor shall allow the Government credit for the value attributable to the Government's contribution [@CL-CMP-4662]. The basic agreement is not a contract, and the Government incurs liability only upon issuance of a communication service authorization, which is a contract that incorporates the agreement's terms [@CL-CMP-4666]. The basic agreement continues in force from year to year unless either party ends it by 30 days' written notice, and ending it does not terminate or cancel authorizations issued before the termination [@CL-CMP-4665]. In the basic version of the clause, authorizations issued under the basic agreement may be modified to incorporate the terms and conditions of a new basic agreement negotiated with the Contractor [@CL-CMP-4709].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

The Government pays a one-time special construction charge of 40,000 for a new line, with the Contracting Officer's authorization. The carrier's actual cost was 40,000, all of it is allocable to Government service, and none of it is covered by a cancellation liability or by recurring charges. The carrier still holds title to the line and must operate and maintain it. If the carrier later stops using the line, it credits the Government for the value attributable to the Government's contribution. Later, the carrier ends the basic agreement on 30 days' notice, and the authorizations already issued stay in force. A new basic agreement is then negotiated, and the old authorizations may be modified to take its terms.

## Never confuse

Title staying with the Contractor even when the Government paid is a telecommunications clause rule [@CL-CMP-4663]. It is not the rule for Government property in general, which is on [Title to Government-furnished and contractor-acquired property](../../close-revrec/fixed-assets/cls-title-to-government-furnished-and-contractor-acquired-property.md). The basic agreement itself is not a contract, and the authorization is [@CL-CMP-4666]. A cancellation or termination liability, which a special construction charge must not duplicate [@CL-CMP-4661], is set out on [Cancellation or termination of orders](cmp-dfars-telecom-cancellation-or-termination-of-orders.md). Access to facilities and how orders are acknowledged and paid are on [Access and orders for facilities and services](cmp-dfars-telecom-access-and-orders-for-facilities.md).
