## Purpose

Covers the Executive Order 11246 representations in paragraph (d) and the certification about payments to influence Federal transactions in paragraph (e) of the commercial offeror representations provision, including the dollar threshold, the disclosure form and what the offeror need not report [@CL-CMP-9147; @CL-CMP-8979; @CL-CMP-8981; @CL-CMP-8982].

Paragraph (d) contains the representations required to implement the provisions of Executive Order 11246 [@CL-CMP-9147]. The offeror makes the representations [@CL-CMP-8976].

Previous contracts and compliance, paragraph (d)(1): the offeror represents whether it has or has not participated in a previous contract or subcontract subject to the Equal Opportunity clause of the solicitation [@CL-CMP-8976]. It also represents whether it has or has not filed all required compliance reports [@CL-CMP-8976].

Affirmative action compliance, paragraph (d)(2): the offeror represents either that it has developed and has on file, or has not developed and does not have on file, at each establishment, affirmative action programs required by the rules and regulations of the Secretary of Labor in 41 CFR parts 60-1 and 60-2 [@CL-CMP-8977]. Alternatively, the offeror represents that it has not previously had contracts subject to the written affirmative action programs requirement of the rules and regulations of the Secretary of Labor [@CL-CMP-8978].

Payments to influence Federal transactions, paragraph (e). The certification applies only if the contract is expected to exceed $200,000 [@CL-CMP-8979]. By submission of its offer, the offeror certifies to the best of its knowledge and belief that no Federal appropriated funds have been paid or will be paid to any person for influencing or attempting to influence an officer or employee of any agency or a Member of Congress [@CL-CMP-8980]. The certification covers payments to influence a Member of Congress, an officer or employee of Congress or an employee of a Member of Congress on his or her behalf in connection with the award of any resultant contract [@CL-CMP-9148].

If registrants under the Lobbying Disclosure Act of 1995 have made a lobbying contact on behalf of the offeror with respect to the contract, the offeror shall complete and submit, with its offer, OMB Standard Form LLL, Disclosure of Lobbying Activities, to provide the name of the registrants [@CL-CMP-8981]. The offeror need not report regularly employed officers or employees of the offeror to whom payments of reasonable compensation were made [@CL-CMP-8982].

## How it works

The paragraph (d) representations are history and status statements. The offeror states whether it took part in earlier contracts under the Equal Opportunity clause and whether it filed its compliance reports [@CL-CMP-8976]. It then states that it has, or has not, developed and kept on file the required affirmative action programs at each establishment, or that it has not previously had contracts subject to the written program requirement [@CL-CMP-8977; @CL-CMP-8978]. The program requirement that the representation refers to is set by the rules and regulations of the Secretary of Labor [@CL-CMP-8977].

The paragraph (e) certification is a separate duty with a dollar test. The offeror first asks whether the contract is expected to exceed $200,000 [@CL-CMP-8979]. If it is, the offeror certifies, to the best of its knowledge and belief, about Federal appropriated funds [@CL-CMP-8979; @CL-CMP-8980]. If a registrant under the Lobbying Disclosure Act of 1995 made a lobbying contact for the offeror about the contract, the offeror files Standard Form LLL with its offer [@CL-CMP-8981]. Regularly employed officers and employees paid reasonable compensation are left out of that report [@CL-CMP-8982].

The FAR separately directs the contracting officer to obtain certifications and disclosures under the payments-to-influence provision before awarding any contract exceeding $200,000 [@CL-CMP-2455]. The linked cards cover the prohibition, its exceptions and the clause that follows award.

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text from February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. The RFO text marks FAR 52.212-3 as reserved [@CL-CMP-1531]. The RFO text also marks FAR 52.222-22 and FAR 52.222-25 as reserved, so the paragraph (d) representations have no RFO text under those provision numbers [@CL-CMP-1589; @CL-CMP-1590]. The payment certification is in RFO FAR 52.203-11(c), where the offeror, by signing its offer, certifies to the best of its knowledge and belief that no Federal appropriated funds have been paid or will be paid to any person for influencing or attempting to influence an officer or employee of any agency or a Member of Congress [@CL-CMP-1591]. The codified paragraph has the offeror certify by submission of its offer [@CL-CMP-8980]. The RFO paragraph prints no parenthetical limiting the certification to contracts expected to exceed $200,000, which the codified paragraph (e) prints [@CL-CMP-1591; @CL-CMP-8979], and the provision says it is inserted as prescribed in 3.808(a), which this corpus does not quote [@CL-CMP-9325]. It covers payments to an officer or employee of Congress, or an employee of a Member of Congress on its behalf, in connection with the awarding of this contract, where the codified text says the award of any resultant contract [@CL-CMP-1592; @CL-CMP-9148]. Paragraph (d) of the RFO provision keeps the rule that the offeror completes and submits, with its offer, OMB Standard Form LLL to provide the name of the registrants when registrants under the Lobbying Disclosure Act of 1995 have made a lobbying contact on its behalf with respect to the contract [@CL-CMP-8981; @CL-CMP-1593], and the rule that the offeror need not report regularly employed officers or employees to whom payments of reasonable compensation were made [@CL-CMP-8982; @CL-CMP-1594].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

Cedar Logistics has participated in 2 earlier contracts subject to the Equal Opportunity clause and has filed all required compliance reports, so it represents both facts [@CL-CMP-8976]. It has developed and keeps on file at each of its 3 establishments the affirmative action programs required by the Secretary of Labor's rules, so it uses the first affirmative action representation [@CL-CMP-8977].

Cedar expects the contract to be worth $450,000, which exceeds $200,000, so it makes the paragraph (e) certification [@CL-CMP-8979]. It certifies that no Federal appropriated funds have been or will be paid to influence an officer or employee of any agency, a Member of Congress, or the other persons the paragraph names [@CL-CMP-8980; @CL-CMP-9148]. A registrant under the Lobbying Disclosure Act of 1995, paid from Cedar's own funds, contacted a Member of Congress for Cedar about this contract, so Cedar completes Standard Form LLL naming the registrant and submits it with its offer [@CL-CMP-8981]. Cedar's regularly employed sales director earns a reasonable salary, and Cedar need not report that payment [@CL-CMP-8982].

Pine Start-up Inc has not previously had contracts subject to the written affirmative action programs requirement, so it uses the alternative representation [@CL-CMP-8978]. Its contract is expected to be worth $120,000, which does not exceed $200,000, so the paragraph (e) certification does not apply under the codified text [@CL-CMP-8979], while the RFO text of FAR 52.203-11(c) prints no such limit in the paragraph [@CL-CMP-1591], and whether the RFO provision is inserted for a contract of that value depends on the prescription at 3.808(a), which this corpus does not quote [@CL-CMP-9325].

## Never confuse

The paragraph (d) representations are not a statement that the offeror has no compliance findings. They state participation and filing history and whether the written affirmative action programs are on file, not on file, or not yet required [@CL-CMP-8976; @CL-CMP-8977; @CL-CMP-8978].

The paragraph (e) certification is worded around Federal appropriated funds [@CL-CMP-8980]. The lobbying disclosure form is a separate duty within paragraph (e) that follows a registrant's lobbying contact on the offeror's behalf with respect to the contract, and paragraph (e) applies only if the contract is expected to exceed $200,000 [@CL-CMP-8981; @CL-CMP-8979].

The codified paragraph (e) applies only if the contract is expected to exceed $200,000, but the RFO text of FAR 52.203-11(c) prints no such limit in the paragraph, and the provision cites the prescription at 3.808(a) for when it is inserted [@CL-CMP-8979; @CL-CMP-1591; @CL-CMP-9325]. The codified paragraph (d) representations have no RFO text under FAR 52.222-22 or FAR 52.222-25, which the RFO text marks as reserved [@CL-CMP-9147; @CL-CMP-1589; @CL-CMP-1590].
