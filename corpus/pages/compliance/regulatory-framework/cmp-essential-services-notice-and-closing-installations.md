## Purpose

Shows what an offeror must put in the written crisis plan that goes in with its offer, and what the installation-closure clause says about who provides professional services.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-CMP-4390] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 252.237-7024 and DFARS 252.237-7022 [@CL-CMP-4390; @CL-CMP-4391] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-4390] |
| Applies to | Offerors responding to a solicitation that includes the notice provision, which is used as prescribed in DFARS 237.7603(b), and contracts that include the installation-closure clause, which is used as prescribed in DFARS 237.7402 [@CL-CMP-4390; @CL-CMP-4391] |

The offeror shall provide with its offer a written plan describing how it will continue to perform the essential contractor services listed in the attachment during periods of crisis [@CL-CMP-4323]. The plan shall identify provisions made for the acquisition of essential personnel and resources, if necessary, for continuity of operations for up to 30 days or until normal operations can be resumed [@CL-CMP-4324].

Under the installation-closure clause, professional employees shall be used by the local government to provide services under the contract to the extent that professionals are available in the area under the jurisdiction of that government [@CL-CMP-4327].

## How it works

The provision tells the offeror what the plan must address at a minimum. One listed matter is the challenges of maintaining essential services during an extended event, such as a pandemic that occurs in repeated waves [@CL-CMP-4325]. Others are the time lapse between starting to acquire essential personnel and resources and their availability on site [@CL-CMP-4406], and the components, processes, and requirements for identifying, training, and preparing personnel who can relocate to alternate facilities or work from home [@CL-CMP-4407]. The last two listed matters are any established alert and notification procedures for mobilizing essential service personnel and the approach for communicating expectations to contractor employees about their roles and responsibilities in a crisis [@CL-CMP-4326]. The installation-closure clause is a single rule about local-government use of professional employees [@CL-CMP-4327].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An offeror bids on a base-support contract with a list of essential services. It attaches a plan that names who it would bring in if staff are lost, how long that takes, who can work from home, and how it will alert and brief its people. The plan covers provisions for acquiring personnel and resources for up to 30 days or until normal operations can resume. The answer: the plan goes in with the offer, and it covers the matters the provision lists.

## Never confuse

The notice provision applies at the offer stage and asks for a plan with the offer [@CL-CMP-4323]. The contract clause covered on [Continuation of essential contractor services](cmp-continuation-of-essential-contractor-services.md) incorporates the contractor's submitted plan into the contract [@CL-CMP-4315]. For deployed-forces duties, which are a separate clause, see [Contractor personnel supporting deployed forces](cmp-deployed-contractor-personnel-definitions-and-predeployment.md).

The installation-closure clause is not about crisis continuity. It is about professional employees being used by the local government where professionals are available [@CL-CMP-4327].
