## Purpose

Shows what the clause prohibits, the exceptions, and the indemnification terms that the alternate adds.

| RFO citation | Not established. |

Under section 2692 of title 10, United States Code, as the clause cites it, the Contractor is prohibited from storing, treating, or disposing of toxic or hazardous materials not owned by DoD on a DoD installation, except to the extent authorized by a statutory exception to that section or as authorized by the Secretary of Defense [@CL-CMP-4521]. A charge may be assessed for any storage or disposal authorized under any of the exceptions to the statute the clause cites, and if a charge is to be assessed it must be identified elsewhere in the contract with payment to the Government on a reimbursable cost basis [@CL-CMP-4522].

Toxic or hazardous materials include materials referred to in section 101(14) of CERCLA and materials designated under section 102 of CERCLA [@CL-CMP-4519]. Two further listed categories are materials of an explosive, flammable, or pyrotechnic nature, and materials otherwise identified by the Secretary of Defense as specified in DoD regulations [@CL-CMP-4520]. The clause defines storage as a non-transitory, semi-permanent or permanent holding, placement, or leaving of material [@CL-CMP-4518]. The definition of storage does not include a temporary accumulation of a limited quantity of a material used in or a waste generated or resulting from authorized activities, such as servicing, maintenance, or repair of Department of Defense (DoD) items, equipment, or facilities [@CL-CMP-4554].

## How it works

The basic clause bars the Contractor from storing, treating or disposing of non-DoD toxic or hazardous materials on a DoD installation unless an exception or the Secretary of Defense authorizes it [@CL-CMP-4521]. The basic clause also requires the Contractor to include its substance, including its subcontracts paragraph, in all subcontracts at any subcontract tier that require, may require, or permit a subcontractor access to a DoD installation [@CL-CMP-4523]. Alternate I states that it adds a new paragraph (c) and revises and redesignates paragraph (c) of the basic clause as paragraph (d) [@CL-CMP-4557]. Under Alternate I, with respect to treatment or disposal authorized pursuant to DFARS 223.7104(10), and notwithstanding any other provision of the contract, the Contractor assumes all financial and environmental responsibility and liability resulting from any treatment or disposal [@CL-CMP-4524]. It must indemnify, defend, and hold the Government harmless for all costs, liability, or penalties resulting from its treatment or disposal of toxic or hazardous materials not owned by DoD on a military installation [@CL-CMP-4525].

The Alternate I flowdown requires the Contractor to include the substance of the clause, including its subcontracts paragraph, in all subcontracts that require, may require, or permit a subcontractor access to a DoD installation, at any tier [@CL-CMP-4555]. Including it does not relieve the prime Contractor of liability to the Government under the liability paragraph [@CL-CMP-4526].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contract uses Alternate I. Treatment of a non-DoD solvent on a base is authorized under the provision the alternate cites (assumed). A spill during treatment leads to a cleanup bill of 300,000 (assumed). The contractor carries that bill and holds the Government harmless for it. A subcontract with the substance of the clause (assumed) does not move the liability off the prime contractor.

## Never confuse

The indemnity in the alternate is tied to treatment or disposal authorized pursuant to the cited provision [@CL-CMP-4524], and it is not the same thing as the charge that may be assessed for authorized storage or disposal [@CL-CMP-4522]. Other cards cover hazard labels on delivered hazardous material, see [Hazard warning labels (DFARS)](cmp-dfars-hazard-warning-labels.md), indemnification for unusually hazardous risks, see [Indemnification for unusually hazardous risks (DFARS)](../../supply-chain/sourcing-evidence/sup-dfars-indemnification-unusually-hazardous-risks.md), and the allowability of insurance and indemnification costs, see [Insurance and indemnification](../cas-allowability/cmp-insurance-and-indemnification.md).
