In accordance with section 2692 of title 10 of the United States Code, the Contractor is prohibited from storing, treating, or disposing of toxic or hazardous materials not owned by DoD on a DoD installation, except to the extent authorized by a statutory exception to section 2692 of title 10 of the United States Code or as authorized by the Secretary of Defense [@CL-CMP-4521]. A charge may be assessed for any storage or disposal authorized under any of the exceptions to section 2692 of title 10 of the United States Code, and if a charge is to be assessed, then such assessment shall be identified elsewhere in the contract with payment to the Government on a reimbursable cost basis [@CL-CMP-4522].

Toxic or hazardous materials means one of three alternatives: materials referred to in section 101(14) of the Comprehensive Environmental Response, Compensation, and Liability Act (CERCLA) of 1980 and materials designated under section 102 of CERCLA (40 CFR Part 302) [@CL-CMP-4519]; materials that are of an explosive, flammable, or pyrotechnic nature; or materials otherwise identified by the Secretary of Defense as specified in DoD regulations [@CL-CMP-4520]. Storage means a non-transitory, semi-permanent or permanent holding, placement, or leaving of material [@CL-CMP-4518]. It does not include a temporary accumulation of a limited quantity of a material used in or a waste generated or resulting from authorized activities, such as servicing, maintenance, or repair of Department of Defense (DoD) items, equipment, or facilities [@CL-CMP-4554].

## How it works

The basic clause prohibits the Contractor from storing, treating, or disposing of toxic or hazardous materials not owned by DoD on a DoD installation, except to the extent authorized by a statutory exception to section 2692 of title 10 of the United States Code or as authorized by the Secretary of Defense [@CL-CMP-4521]. The basic clause also requires the Contractor to include the substance of the clause, including paragraph (c), in all subcontracts that require, may require, or permit a subcontractor access to a DoD installation, at any subcontract tier [@CL-CMP-4523]. Alternate I adds a new paragraph (c) and revises and redesignates paragraph (c) of the basic clause as paragraph (d) [@CL-CMP-4557]. Under Alternate I, with respect to treatment or disposal authorized pursuant to DFARS 223.7104(10) (section 2692(b)(10) of title 10 of the United States Code), and notwithstanding any other provision of the contract, the Contractor assumes all financial and environmental responsibility and liability resulting from any treatment or disposal of toxic or hazardous materials not owned by DoD on a military installation [@CL-CMP-4524; @CL-CMP-0440]. The Contractor shall indemnify, defend, and hold the Government harmless for all costs, liability, or penalties resulting from the Contractor's treatment or disposal of toxic or hazardous materials not owned by DoD on a military installation [@CL-CMP-4525].

The Alternate I flowdown requires the Contractor to include the substance of the clause, including paragraph (d), in all subcontracts that require, may require, or permit a subcontractor access to a DoD installation, at any tier [@CL-CMP-4555]. Inclusion of the substance of the clause in subcontracts does not relieve the prime Contractor of liability to the Government under paragraph (c) of the clause [@CL-CMP-4526].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contract uses Alternate I. Treatment of a non-DoD solvent on a military installation is authorized pursuant to DFARS 223.7104(10) (assumed). A spill during treatment leads to a cleanup bill of 300,000 (assumed). The contractor assumes that liability and holds the Government harmless for it. A subcontract with the substance of the clause (assumed) does not relieve the prime contractor of liability to the Government under paragraph (c).
