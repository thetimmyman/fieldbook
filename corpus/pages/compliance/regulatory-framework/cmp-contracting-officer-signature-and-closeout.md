## Purpose

Explains who signs for the United States, how far an agency may go in adding distribution requirements, and when a contract or a rental, use or storage agreement counts as physically completed [@CL-CMP-7550; @CL-CMP-7552; @CL-CMP-7554].

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR and codified DFARS [@CL-CMP-7550; @CL-CMP-10017] |
| Codified citation | FAR 4.101, 4.202 and 4.804-4 [@CL-CMP-7550; @CL-CMP-7552; @CL-CMP-7554]; DFARS 204.101 [@CL-CMP-10017] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-7550] |
| Applies to | Contracts signed on behalf of the United States, assignments of contract administration to an office in another agency, and the physical completion test for contracts and for rental, use and storage agreements [@CL-CMP-7550; @CL-CMP-7553; @CL-CMP-7557] |

Only contracting officers sign contracts on behalf of the United States, and the contracting officer's name and official title must be typed, stamped or printed on the contract [@CL-CMP-7550]. The contracting officer normally signs after the contractor has signed [@CL-CMP-7710]. The contracting officer must ensure that the signers have authority to bind the contractor [@CL-CMP-7551]. For DoD, DFARS 204.101, headed "Contracting officer's signature", directs that the procedures at PGI 204.101 be followed for signature of contract documents [@CL-CMP-10017].

Agencies must limit additional distribution requirements to the minimum necessary for proper performance of essential functions [@CL-CMP-7552]. When a contract is assigned for administration to a contract administration office in an agency different from the contracting office, the two agencies must agree on any necessary distribution in addition to the distribution prescribed in FAR 4.201 [@CL-CMP-7553].

Except for rental, use and storage agreements, a contract is considered physically completed when either of two things has happened [@CL-CMP-7554]. The first is that the contractor has completed the required deliveries and the Government has inspected and accepted the supplies, the contractor has performed all services and the Government has accepted them, and all option provisions, if any, have expired [@CL-CMP-7555]. The second is that the Government has given the contractor a notice of complete contract termination [@CL-CMP-7556]. A rental, use or storage agreement is considered physically completed when the Government has given the contractor a notice of complete contract termination or when the contract period has expired [@CL-CMP-7557].

## How it works

The contractor normally signs first and the contracting officer normally signs after it [@CL-CMP-7710]. The officer must ensure that the contractor's signers have authority to bind the contractor [@CL-CMP-7551].

Distribution is a limit and an agreement rule. The section caps additional requirements and requires the two agencies to agree when the administering office sits in a different agency [@CL-CMP-7552; @CL-CMP-7553].

Physical completion is a factual test. An ordinary contract needs all three parts of the delivery, service and option test, or a notice of complete termination [@CL-CMP-7555; @CL-CMP-7556]. A rental, use or storage agreement needs that notice or expiry of the contract period [@CL-CMP-7557]. The section states conditions and does not name who decides that they are met. The closeout steps and time standards that follow are on the linked cards.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor signs a supply-and-install contract, and the contracting officer signs afterwards, which is the normal order [@CL-CMP-7710]. The officer also confirms that the person who signed for the contractor can bind it [@CL-CMP-7551].

The contract calls for ten units, an installation service and an option to extend. The contractor delivers the ten units and the Government inspects and accepts them. The contractor performs the installation and the Government accepts it. The option expires unused. All three parts of the test are met, so the contract is physically completed without a termination notice [@CL-CMP-7555].

A separate storage agreement runs to its stated end date and the Government never sent a termination notice. It is physically completed because its contract period has expired [@CL-CMP-7557].

## Never confuse

Physical completion under this section turns on the listed deliveries, services, option expiry or termination notice. The section does not add final payment or a period of performance date to that test [@CL-CMP-7555; @CL-CMP-7556]. The officer's signature on the contract is also separate from the officer's duty to ensure that the contractor's signers have authority [@CL-CMP-7551]. For how each type of contractor signs, see [Contractor signature and taxpayer identification information](../audit-retention/cmp-contractor-signature-and-taxpayer-information.md).
