## Purpose

Shows what the contractor may not do about its subcontractors' direct sales to the Government, and the exceptions the clause gives.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-2645] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.203-6 [@CL-CMP-2645] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.203-6 [@CL-CMP-6214; @CL-CMP-6215; @CL-CMP-6259; @CL-CMP-6260] and Alternate I [@CL-CMP-6296; @CL-CMP-6297] |
| Edition date | Jun 2020 [@CL-CMP-6214] |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-2645] |
| Applies to | Solicitations and contracts exceeding the simplified acquisition threshold, with Alternate I for commercial products or commercial services [@CL-CMP-2685; @CL-CMP-2686] |

The clause is inserted as prescribed in FAR 3.503-2 [@CL-CMP-2645]. The contracting officer shall insert it in solicitations and contracts exceeding the simplified acquisition threshold [@CL-CMP-2685]. For the acquisition of commercial products or commercial services, the contracting officer shall use it with its Alternate I [@CL-CMP-2686]. The clause says the Contractor shall not enter into any agreement with an actual or prospective subcontractor, nor otherwise act in any manner, which has or may have the effect of restricting sales by such subcontractors directly to the Government, except as provided in paragraph (b) of the clause [@CL-CMP-2646; @CL-CMP-2648]. The restriction applies to sales of any item or process, including computer software, made or furnished by the subcontractor under this contract or under any follow-on production contract [@CL-CMP-2647].

The prohibition does not preclude the Contractor from asserting rights that are otherwise authorized by law or regulation [@CL-CMP-2648]. Under Alternate I, which is used for acquisitions of commercial products or commercial services, the prohibition applies only to the extent that any agreement restricting sales by subcontractors results in the Federal Government being treated differently from any other prospective purchaser for the sale of the commercial products and commercial services [@CL-CMP-2649; @CL-CMP-2686].

## How it works

The clause binds the prime contractor's own conduct, meaning its agreements with actual or prospective subcontractors and any other act that has or may have the effect of restricting direct sales to the Government [@CL-CMP-2646]. The scope is the subcontractor's item or process made or furnished under this contract or a follow-on production contract [@CL-CMP-2647]. The contractor keeps the rights that law or regulation otherwise authorizes [@CL-CMP-2648].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text from February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. On the points below the RFO clause reads the same as the codified clause [@CL-CMP-6215; @CL-CMP-6259; @CL-CMP-6260]. It says to insert the clause as prescribed in 3.503-2 [@CL-CMP-6214; @CL-CMP-2645]. Paragraph (a) keeps the bar: except as provided in paragraph (b), the Contractor shall not enter into any agreement with an actual or prospective subcontractor, nor otherwise act in any manner, which has or may have the effect of restricting sales by such subcontractors directly to the Government [@CL-CMP-6215; @CL-CMP-2646]. The bar still reaches sales of any item or process (including computer software) made or furnished by the subcontractor under this contract or under any follow-on production contract [@CL-CMP-6259; @CL-CMP-2647]. Paragraph (b) keeps the Contractor's ability to assert rights that are otherwise authorized by law or regulation [@CL-CMP-6260; @CL-CMP-2648]. Alternate I keeps the commercial limit: for acquisitions of commercial products or commercial services, the prohibition in paragraph (a) applies only to the extent that any agreement restricting sales by subcontractors results in the Federal Government being treated differently from any other prospective purchaser for the sale of the commercial product(s) and commercial service(s) [@CL-CMP-6296; @CL-CMP-6297; @CL-CMP-2649]. Paragraph (c) says the Contractor agrees to incorporate the substance of the clause, including paragraph (c), in all subcontracts under the contract which exceed the simplified acquisition threshold, as defined in Federal Acquisition Regulation 2.101 on the date of subcontract award [@CL-CMP-6298].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A prime contractor holds a contract above the simplified acquisition threshold. Its draft supply agreement says a subcontractor may sell a component made under the contract only to the prime. The agreement has the effect of restricting the subcontractor's direct sales to the Government, so the clause bars the prime from entering it unless an exception applies. If the acquisition is of a commercial product and Alternate I is used, the question becomes whether the agreement leaves the Government treated differently from any other prospective purchaser.

## Never confuse

This clause is not the FAR policy on unreasonably precluding a subcontractor's direct sales, or the buying-in rule. Both are covered in [Buying-in and restrictions on subcontractor direct sales](cmp-buying-in-and-subcontractor-direct-sales.md). That card covers the policy, and this card covers the clause text, which is the contractor's own obligation.
