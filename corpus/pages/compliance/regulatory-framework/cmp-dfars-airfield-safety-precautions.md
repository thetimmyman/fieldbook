## Purpose

Shows the restricted areas, the height and marking limits and the approvals a contractor needs for work on and around airfields.

## The rule

|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-CMP-4679; @CL-CMP-4681; @CL-CMP-4682] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 252.236-7005 [@CL-CMP-4679; @CL-CMP-4681; @CL-CMP-4682] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-4679; @CL-CMP-4681; @CL-CMP-4682] |
| Applies to | Contracts that include the Airfield Safety Precautions clause [@CL-CMP-4679; @CL-CMP-4681; @CL-CMP-4682] |

The clause defines the width of each primary surface as 2,000 feet, which is 1,000 feet on each side of the runway centerline [@CL-CMP-4679]. At some airfields the width of the primary surface is 1,500 feet, and in such instances the proper width is substituted in the clause [@CL-CMP-4680]. Safety precaution areas are those portions of approach-departure clearance zones and transitional zones where placement of objects incident to contract performance might result in vertical projections at or above the approach-departure clearance, or the transitional surface [@CL-CMP-4692].

The Contractor shall report to the Contracting Officer before initiating any work and notify the Contracting Officer of proposed changes to locations and operations [@CL-CMP-4681]. In the landing areas the Contractor shall place nothing without the authorization of the Contracting Officer [@CL-CMP-4682]. In the safety precaution areas it shall likewise place nothing without authorization of the Contracting Officer [@CL-CMP-4691].

## How it works

In the landing areas the Contractor shall also outline the landing areas hazardous to aircraft using, unless otherwise authorized by the Contracting Officer, red flags by day and electric, battery-operated low-intensity red flasher lights by night [@CL-CMP-4682]. At an airfield where flying is controlled, it shall obtain additional permission from the control tower operator every time before entering any landing area, unless the landing area is marked as hazardous under the clause's outlining requirement [@CL-CMP-4695]. It shall identify all vehicles it operates in landing areas by means of a flag on a staff attached to, and flying above, the vehicle [@CL-CMP-4696]. In the safety precaution areas, all equipment and materials shall be marked using red flags by day and electric, battery-operated, low-intensity red flasher lights by night, unless the Contracting Officer authorizes otherwise [@CL-CMP-4683].

Two working rules apply on the airfield. The Contractor shall not open a trench unless material is on hand and ready for placing in the trench, and as soon as practicable after material has been placed and work approved it shall backfill and compact trenches as required by the contract [@CL-CMP-4693]. At times other than while mobile equipment is actually performing work on the airfield, the Contractor shall remove all mobile equipment to locations that are approved by the Contracting Officer and at a distance of at least 750 feet from the runway centerline, plus any additional distance, among the listed conditions [@CL-CMP-4694].

The definition of safety precaution areas refers to the approach-departure clearance surface and the transitional surface [@CL-CMP-4692]. The clause describes an inclined plane that continues upward at a slope of 50:1, which is 1 foot vertically for each 50 feet horizontally, to an elevation of 500 feet above the established airfield elevation [@CL-CMP-4684]. The transitional surface is a sideways extension of all primary surfaces, clear zones, and approach-departure clearance surfaces along inclined planes; each plane begins at the edge of the surface, and the slope of the incline plane is 7:1 [@CL-CMP-4697].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

Before starting work beside a taxiway, the contractor reports to the Contracting Officer. It then wants to stack 100 pallets in a landing area. It asks for the Contracting Officer's authorization first. If the area is hazardous to aircraft, it outlines the area with red flags by day and red flasher lights by night. At an airfield with a control tower, it also gets the tower operator's permission each time before it enters the landing area, unless the area is marked as hazardous. When the day's work is done, the mobile equipment goes to a location the Contracting Officer approved, at least 750 feet from the runway centerline. A second stockpile would sit in a safety precaution area, so the contractor asks for authorization before placing it and marks the pile with red flags by day and flasher lights by night.

## Never confuse

Airfield safety precautions are about aircraft movement areas and are not the ammunition and explosives safety clause, which is on [Ammunition and explosives safety precautions](cmp-dfars-ammunition-explosives-safety-precautions.md). Landing areas and safety precaution areas are different terms in the clause [@CL-CMP-4682; @CL-CMP-4683]. In both, nothing may be placed without the Contracting Officer's authorization [@CL-CMP-4682; @CL-CMP-4691], so marking an area does not replace asking first. The drawing and waterway duties of the other construction clauses are on [Construction drawings, waterways and additive or deductive items](cmp-dfars-construction-drawings-waterways-and-additive-items.md).
