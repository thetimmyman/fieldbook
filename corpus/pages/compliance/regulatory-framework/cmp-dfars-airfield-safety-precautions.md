## Purpose

Shows the restricted areas, the height and marking limits and the approvals a contractor needs for work on and around airfields.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-CMP-4679; @CL-CMP-4681; @CL-CMP-4682] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 252.236-7005 [@CL-CMP-4679; @CL-CMP-4681; @CL-CMP-4682] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-4679; @CL-CMP-4681; @CL-CMP-4682] |
| Applies to | Contracts that include the Airfield Safety Precautions clause [@CL-CMP-4679; @CL-CMP-4681; @CL-CMP-4682] |

Landing areas include the primary surfaces, which comprise the surface of the runway, runway shoulders, and lateral safety zones, and each primary surface is as long as the runway [@CL-CMP-8620; @CL-CMP-4785]. The clause defines the width of each primary surface as 2,000 feet, which is 1,000 feet on each side of the runway centerline [@CL-CMP-4679]. At some airfields the width of the primary surface is 1,500 feet, and in such instances the proper width is substituted in the clause [@CL-CMP-4680]. The landing areas also include the clear zone beyond the ends of each runway, which extends the primary surface for 1,000 feet beyond each end of each runway [@CL-CMP-4750], all taxiways plus the lateral clearance zones along each side, whose outer edge is laterally 250 feet from the far or opposite edge of the taxiway [@CL-CMP-4751], and all aircraft parking aprons plus the area 125 feet in width extending beyond each edge all around the aprons [@CL-CMP-4752]. Safety precaution areas are those portions of approach-departure clearance zones and transitional zones where placement of objects incident to contract performance might result in vertical projections at or above the approach-departure clearance, or the transitional surface [@CL-CMP-4692].

The clause applies while the Contractor operates all ground equipment (mobile or stationary), places all materials and performs all work, upon and around all airfields [@CL-CMP-4753], and its requirements are in addition to any other safety requirements of the contract [@CL-CMP-4793]. The Contractor shall report to the Contracting Officer before initiating any work and notify the Contracting Officer of proposed changes to locations and operations [@CL-CMP-4681]. It shall not permit its equipment or personnel to use any runway for anything other than aircraft operation without the Contracting Officer's permission, unless the runway is closed by order of the Contracting Officer and marked as the outlining requirement provides [@CL-CMP-4754]. In the landing areas the Contractor shall place nothing without the authorization of the Contracting Officer [@CL-CMP-4682]. In the safety precaution areas it shall likewise place nothing without authorization of the Contracting Officer [@CL-CMP-4691].

## How it works

In the landing areas the Contractor shall also outline the landing areas hazardous to aircraft using, unless otherwise authorized by the Contracting Officer, red flags by day and electric, battery-operated low-intensity red flasher lights by night [@CL-CMP-4682]. At an airfield where flying is controlled, it shall obtain additional permission from the control tower operator every time before entering any landing area, unless the landing area is marked as hazardous under the clause's outlining requirement [@CL-CMP-4695]. It shall identify all vehicles it operates in landing areas by means of a flag on a staff attached to, and flying above, the vehicle [@CL-CMP-4696]. The flag shall be three feet square and consist of a checkered pattern of international orange and white squares of 1 foot on each side, and it may vary up to ten percent from each of these dimensions [@CL-CMP-4749]. All other equipment and materials in the landing areas are marked with the same devices as the outlining of hazardous areas [@CL-CMP-4757], and the work shall leave the part of the landing area available to aircraft free from hazards, holes, piles of material and projecting shoulders that might damage an airplane tire [@CL-CMP-4758]. In the safety precaution areas, all equipment and materials shall be marked using red flags by day and electric, battery-operated, low-intensity red flasher lights by night, unless the Contracting Officer authorizes otherwise [@CL-CMP-4683]. At night, all objects placed in safety precaution areas shall also have a red light or red lantern if they project above the approach-departure clearance surface or above the transitional surface [@CL-CMP-4759].

The Contractor shall keep all paved surfaces, such as runways, taxiways, and hardstands, clean at all times and, specifically, free from small stones which might damage aircraft propellers or jet aircraft [@CL-CMP-4784]. The Contractor shall not open a trench unless material is on hand and ready for placing in the trench, and as soon as practicable after material has been placed and work approved it shall backfill and compact trenches as required by the contract; meanwhile, all hazardous conditions shall be marked and lighted in accordance with the other provisions of the clause [@CL-CMP-4693; @CL-CMP-4756]. At times other than while mobile equipment is actually performing work on the airfield, the Contractor shall remove all mobile equipment to locations that are approved by the Contracting Officer and at a distance of at least 750 feet from the runway centerline, plus any additional distance, among the listed conditions [@CL-CMP-4694], and the locations must also be necessary to ensure compliance with the other provisions of the clause [@CL-CMP-4755].

The definition of safety precaution areas refers to the approach-departure clearance surface and the transitional surface [@CL-CMP-4692]. The approach-departure clearance surface extends the primary surface and the clear zone at each end of each runway for a distance of 50,000 feet, first along an inclined (glide angle) plane and then along a horizontal plane, both flaring symmetrically about the extended runway centerline [@CL-CMP-4786]. The inclined plane begins in the clear zone 200 feet past the end of the runway, at the same elevation as the end of the runway [@CL-CMP-4787], and continues upward at a slope of 50:1, which is 1 foot vertically for each 50 feet horizontally, to an elevation of 500 feet above the established airfield elevation [@CL-CMP-4684]. There the plane becomes horizontal and continues at that elevation to a point 50,000 feet longitudinally from the beginning of the inclined plane, where it ends [@CL-CMP-4788]. The surface is as wide as the clear zone where the inclined plane begins and flares uniformly to a maximum width of 16,000 feet at the end [@CL-CMP-4789]. The approach-departure clearance zone is the ground area under that surface [@CL-CMP-4790]. The transitional surface is a sideways extension of all primary surfaces, clear zones, and approach-departure clearance surfaces along inclined planes; each plane begins at the edge of the surface, and the slope of the incline plane is 7:1 [@CL-CMP-4697]. Each transitional plane continues to its intersection with the inner horizontal surface, 150 feet above the established airfield elevation, or the outer horizontal surface, 500 feet above it, whichever is applicable [@CL-CMP-4791; @CL-CMP-4794]. The transitional zone is the ground area under the transitional surface and adjoins the primary surface, clear zone, and approach-departure clearance zone [@CL-CMP-4792].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

Before starting work beside a taxiway, the contractor reports to the Contracting Officer. A truck it drives into a landing area flies a flag on a staff above the vehicle, three feet square with 1 foot orange and white checks; a flag 2.8 feet by 3 feet is inside the ten percent variation. It then wants to stack 100 pallets in a landing area. It asks for the Contracting Officer's authorization first. It marks the pallets with red flags by day and red flasher lights by night, the same devices that outline hazardous areas, and not with the checkered flag, which only identifies vehicles. If the area is hazardous to aircraft, it outlines the area with the same red flags and red flasher lights. At an airfield with a control tower, it also gets the tower operator's permission each time before it enters the landing area, unless the area is marked as hazardous. Each time its truck crosses a paved taxiway, it sweeps off any small stones the tires carry onto the pavement. When the day's work is done, the mobile equipment goes to a location the Contracting Officer approved, at least 750 feet from the runway centerline. A second stockpile would sit in a safety precaution area, so the contractor asks for authorization before placing it and marks the pile with red flags by day and flasher lights by night. If the pile projects above the clearance surface, it also gets a red light or lantern at night. Later the contractor plans a crane under the approach path, 10,000 feet beyond the point where the inclined plane begins. The clearance surface there is 10,000 divided by 50, or 200 feet above the runway-end elevation, so a crane reaching 220 feet would project above it; the site is in a safety precaution area, and the crane needs authorization, red flags by day, flasher lights by night and a red light or lantern at night. Farther out, 30,000 feet from the beginning of the inclined plane, the surface has already leveled off at 500 feet above the established airfield elevation and stays there until it ends 50,000 feet out, so a crane there is tested against that height. Beside the runway, a pile 140 feet out from the edge of the primary surface meets the 7:1 transitional surface at 20 feet, so a 25-foot pile there also projects above the surface.

## Never confuse

The airfield safety precautions clause is not the ammunition and explosives safety clause: the ammunition and explosives safety clause is on [Ammunition and explosives safety precautions](cmp-dfars-ammunition-explosives-safety-precautions.md). In the landing areas and in the safety precaution areas, the Contractor shall place nothing without the authorization of the Contracting Officer [@CL-CMP-4682; @CL-CMP-4691]. The drawing and waterway duties of the other construction clauses are on [Construction drawings, waterways and additive or deductive items](cmp-dfars-construction-drawings-waterways-and-additive-items.md).
