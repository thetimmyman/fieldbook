## Purpose

Gives the FAR definition of a service contract and shows how agencies treat labor disputes. Names the Department of Labor offices that agencies and contracting officers work with.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-3091] |
| Codified citation | FAR 22.001, FAR 22.101-1 and FAR 22.102-2 [@CL-CMP-3091; @CL-CMP-3092; @CL-CMP-3143] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-3091] |
| Applies to | Government contracts and subcontracts whose principal purpose is to furnish services through service employees, for the definition, agency conduct in labor disputes, for the policy, and agency use of Department of Labor offices, for the administration rule [@CL-CMP-3091; @CL-CMP-3092; @CL-CMP-3143] |

The FAR defines a service contract as any Government contract, or subcontract under it, whose principal purpose is to furnish services in the United States through the use of service employees, except as exempted by the Service Contract Labor Standards statute [@CL-CMP-3091]. Agencies shall remain impartial concerning any dispute between labor and contractor management and not undertake the conciliation, mediation, or arbitration of a labor dispute [@CL-CMP-3092].

Agencies shall cooperate with, and encourage contractors to use to the fullest extent practicable, the Department of Labor Employment and Training Administration and its affiliated local offices in meeting contractors' labor requirements [@CL-CMP-3143]. Contracting officers should contact the Wage and Hour Division's regional offices when required by the subparts relating to the wage and hour statutes unless otherwise specified [@CL-CMP-3144].

## How it works

The definition turns on the principal purpose of the contract and on the use of service employees [@CL-CMP-3091]. The impartiality rule is a duty of agencies [@CL-CMP-3092]. The administration rule has agencies work with the Department of Labor's employment and training offices, and has contracting officers contact the Wage and Hour Division's regional offices when the wage and hour subparts require it [@CL-CMP-3143; @CL-CMP-3144]. The sections also cover sound relations with industry and labor, the exchange of labor information, steps agencies should take on a dispute, a designation by the head of the contracting activity for contractor notices, and the list of wage and hour statutes the Wage and Hour Division administers, and this card does not set those out. Project labor agreements have their own card at [Project labor agreements on federal construction](cmp-project-labor-agreements.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor holds a contract to run a cafeteria in the United States with 40 hourly staff. The answer: its principal purpose is to furnish services through service employees, so it fits the definition unless an exemption applies. In a separate case, a union and a contractor dispute pay. The answer: the agency stays impartial and does not mediate or arbitrate the dispute.

## Never confuse

The definition of a service contract is not the labor standards clause itself. The clause and the wage determinations are on [Service contract labor standards clause and wage determinations](../../operating-model/talent/opm-service-contract-labor-standards.md). Labor relations policy is also not the cost treatment of strike costs, which is on [Labor relations: labor policies and strike costs in pricing and cost allowance](cmp-labor-disputes-and-labor-costs-in-pricing.md).
