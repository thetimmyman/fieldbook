## Purpose

Shows when the code of business ethics clause and the hotline poster clause go into a contract, and which disclosure and overpayment duties apply whether or not they do.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-2021] |
| Codified citation | FAR 3.1000, FAR 3.1001 and FAR 3.1002 [@CL-CMP-9720; @CL-CMP-9721; @CL-CMP-9722; @CL-CMP-9723]; FAR 3.1003 and FAR 3.1004 [@CL-CMP-2021; @CL-CMP-2022] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-2021] |
| Applies to | For the ethics clause, solicitations and contracts expected to exceed $7.5 million with a performance period of 120 days or more [@CL-CMP-2021] |

The code of business ethics clause goes into solicitations and contracts if the value of the contract is expected to exceed $7.5 million and the performance period is 120 days or more [@CL-CMP-2021]. Both conditions must hold, so meeting only one is not enough [@CL-CMP-2021]. Whether or not that clause applies, a contractor may be suspended or debarred for knowing failure by a principal to timely disclose certain matters to the Government [@CL-CMP-2022]. The matters are credible evidence in connection with the award, performance, or closeout of a Government contract performed by the contractor or a subcontract awarded under it [@CL-CMP-2022; @CL-CMP-2023]. That evidence is evidence of a violation of Federal criminal law involving fraud, conflict of interest, bribery, or gratuity violations found in Title 18 of the United States Code, or a violation of the civil False Claims Act [@CL-CMP-2023; @CL-CMP-2024]. Knowing failure to timely disclose credible evidence of any of those violations remains a cause for suspension and debarment until 3 years after final payment on a contract [@CL-CMP-2024; @CL-CMP-2025]. The printed sections do not set a deadline for what counts as timely.

The payment clauses named in the section require that, if the contractor becomes aware that the Government has overpaid on a contract financing or invoice payment, the contractor shall remit the overpayment amount to the Government [@CL-CMP-2026]. A contractor may also be suspended or debarred for knowing failure by a principal to timely disclose credible evidence of a significant overpayment, other than overpayments resulting from contract financing payments as defined in the FAR [@CL-CMP-2027]. Unless the contract is for a commercial product or commercial service or will be performed entirely outside the United States, one condition for inserting the hotline poster clause is that the contract exceeds $7.5 million or a lesser amount established by the agency [@CL-CMP-2028]. A further condition is that the agency has a fraud hotline poster or the contract is funded with disaster assistance funds [@CL-CMP-2028; @CL-CMP-2029].

## How it works

Two layers apply: the ethics clause is a contract term that depends on contract value and performance period [@CL-CMP-2021], while the disclosure exposure to suspension or debarment applies whether or not that clause is in the contract [@CL-CMP-2022]. The disclosure exposure covers the violations the section lists and lasts until 3 years after final payment on a contract [@CL-CMP-2024; @CL-CMP-2025]. The overpayment remittance duty sits in the payment clauses the section names [@CL-CMP-2026]. The hotline poster clause has its own prescription, with an amount the agency can lower [@CL-CMP-2028; @CL-CMP-2029]. For suspension and debarment itself, see [Protecting the Government's interest when subcontracting with debarred or suspended contractors](../../supply-chain/supplier-identity/sup-debarred-suspended-subcontractors.md).

The subpart implements section 3509 of title 41 of the United States Code, Notification of Violations of Federal Criminal Law or Overpayments, and prescribes policies and procedures for the establishment of contractor codes of business ethics and conduct and for display of agency Office of Inspector General (OIG) fraud hotline posters [@CL-CMP-9720]. As the subpart uses the terms, a subcontract means any contract entered into by a subcontractor to furnish supplies or services for performance of a prime contract or a subcontract [@CL-CMP-9721], and a subcontractor means any supplier, distributor, vendor, or firm that furnished supplies or services to or for a prime contractor or another subcontractor [@CL-CMP-9722]. Government contractors must conduct themselves with the highest degree of integrity and honesty, and contractors should have a written code of business ethics and conduct [@CL-CMP-9723].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contract is expected to be worth 9 million, and its performance period is 90 days. The value is above the ethics clause threshold, but the period is shorter than the clause requires, so the clause is not inserted on that basis. A principal who knowingly fails to disclose credible evidence of a listed violation can still face suspension or debarment, because that exposure does not depend on the clause. The contract is not for a commercial product or service and will not be performed entirely outside the United States, and the agency has a hotline poster, so the poster clause is inserted if the contract is above the agency's amount.

## Never confuse

The disclosure exposure does not depend on whether the ethics clause applies [@CL-CMP-2022]. The remittance of an overpayment, which the payment clauses require [@CL-CMP-2026], is different from the disclosure of a significant overpayment, which is tied to suspension and debarment [@CL-CMP-2027]. The ethics clause and the poster clause share a $7.5 million figure but have different further conditions [@CL-CMP-2021; @CL-CMP-2028; @CL-CMP-2029].
