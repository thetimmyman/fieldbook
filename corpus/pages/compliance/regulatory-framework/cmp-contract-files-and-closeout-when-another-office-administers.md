## Purpose

Shows what a contract file has to prove, and when the contracting office may close a file whose contract another office administered.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-7227] |
| Codified citation | FAR 4.801 and 4.804-2 [@CL-CMP-7227; @CL-CMP-7228]; FAR 4.804-3 [@CL-CMP-9752] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-7227] |
| Applies to | Contract files held by the contracting office [@CL-CMP-7228] |

The head of each office performing contracting, contract administration or paying functions must establish files containing the records of all contractual actions [@CL-CMP-7323]. The documentation in the files must be sufficient to constitute a complete history of the transaction, providing a complete background as a basis for informed decisions at each step in the acquisition process and supporting the actions taken [@CL-CMP-7227]. It must also serve the purposes of providing information for reviews and investigations and furnishing essential facts in the event of litigation or congressional inquiries [@CL-CMP-7324].

The files to be established include a file for cancelled solicitations, a file for each contract, and a file such as a contractor general file [@CL-CMP-7325]. A contractor general file contains documents relating to no specific contract, to more than one contract, or to the contractor in a general way, for example the contractor's management systems, past performance or capabilities [@CL-CMP-7326].

## How it works

A contract file for a contract using simplified acquisition procedures should be considered closed when the contracting officer receives evidence of receipt of property and final payment, unless agency regulation specifies otherwise [@CL-CMP-7327].

All other contract files must be closed as soon as practicable after the contracting officer receives a contract completion statement from the contract administration office [@CL-CMP-7228]. The contracting officer must ensure that all required contractual actions have been completed and prepare a statement to that effect. That statement is authority to close the contract file and must be made part of the official contract file [@CL-CMP-7229].

Separately, the paying office shall close the contract file upon issuance of the final payment voucher [@CL-CMP-9752].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A task order worth 90,000 is bought under simplified acquisition procedures. The contracting officer receives evidence of receipt of the property and of final payment, so the file should be considered closed then, unless agency regulation specifies otherwise [@CL-CMP-7327].

A separate cost-reimbursement contract is administered by another office. Its performance ends in March, and the contract administration office sends its contract completion statement in June. The contracting officer ensures that all required contractual actions have been completed and prepares a statement to that effect [@CL-CMP-7229]. The contracting office closes the file as soon as practicable after the contracting officer receives the completion statement, and the contracting officer's own statement is authority to close and goes into the official contract file [@CL-CMP-7228; @CL-CMP-7229].

## Never confuse

The contract completion statement the contracting office receives is not the closing statement it prepares: the contracting officer receives a contract completion statement from the contract administration office [@CL-CMP-7228], and the contracting officer shall ensure that all contractual actions required have been completed and shall prepare a statement to that effect, which is authority to close the contract file [@CL-CMP-7229].
