## Purpose

Shows how service reporting works when the contract is indefinite-delivery: what is reported for orders, when and where, what happens to the report afterwards, and what subcontractors must supply.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-5956] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.204-15 [@CL-CMP-5956] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-5956] |
| Applies to | Indefinite-delivery contracts that carry the clause, for orders that exceed the thresholds established in FAR 4.1703(a)(2) [@CL-CMP-5956] |

The contractor reports annually by October 31 for services performed during the preceding Government fiscal year, which runs from October 1 to September 30, under the contract for orders that exceed the thresholds established in FAR 4.1703(a)(2) [@CL-CMP-5956]. The report gives the contract number and the order number [@CL-CMP-5957]. It gives the total dollar amount invoiced for services performed during the previous Government fiscal year under the order [@CL-CMP-5958] and the number of contractor direct labor hours expended on those services [@CL-CMP-5959]. It includes the data reported by subcontractors under the subcontractor paragraph of the clause [@CL-CMP-5960].

## How it works

### Filing and review

The information is submitted via the internet at the SAM website [@CL-CMP-5961]. If the contractor fails to submit the report in a timely manner, the contracting officer will exercise appropriate contractual remedies [@CL-CMP-6534]. The contracting officer will also make the failure to comply a part of the contractor's performance information under FAR subpart 42.15 [@CL-CMP-6535].

Agencies review the reported information for reasonableness and consistency with available contract information [@CL-CMP-6536]. If the agency believes that revisions are warranted, it will notify the contractor no later than November 15 [@CL-CMP-6537]. By November 30, the contractor must revise the report or document its rationale for the agency [@CL-CMP-6538].

### Subcontractors

A first-tier subcontract is a subcontract awarded directly by the contractor for the purpose of acquiring supplies or services, including construction, for performance of a prime contract [@CL-CMP-6532]. The definition does not include the contractor's supplier agreements with vendors, such as long-term arrangements for materials or supplies that benefit multiple contracts and the costs of which are normally applied to general and administrative expenses or indirect costs [@CL-CMP-6533].

The contractor must require each first-tier subcontractor providing services under the contract, with subcontracts each valued at or above the thresholds set forth in FAR 4.1703(a)(2), to provide the detailed information in sufficient time to submit the report [@CL-CMP-5962]. The clause does not print those thresholds, and the company reads them in the FAR section the clause names [@CL-CMP-5962]. The detailed information is the subcontract number, including the subcontractor name and unique entity identifier, and the number of first-tier subcontractor direct-labor hours expended on the services performed during the previous Government fiscal year [@CL-CMP-6539]. The contractor must advise the subcontractor that the information will be made available to the public as required by section 743 of Division C of the Consolidated Appropriations Act, 2010 [@CL-CMP-5963].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An indefinite-delivery contract for services has three fixed-price orders issued after fiscal year 2015, so under FAR 4.1703(a)(2) an order is reported if its estimated total value is 500,000 or greater. Order A, estimated at 3.5 million, invoiced 3 million in the Government fiscal year that ended September 30, with 18,000 contractor direct labor hours, and a first-tier subcontractor at or above the subcontract threshold worked 5,000 direct-labor hours on it. Order B, estimated at 2.4 million, invoiced 2 million with 9,000 hours. Order C, estimated at 400,000, invoiced 300,000.

Orders A and B are above the order threshold, so the contractor reports each by October 31 through the SAM website. Report A states the contract number, order A's number, the 3 million, the 18,000 hours and the subcontractor's data, including its 5,000 hours. Report B states its own order number, the 2 million and the 9,000 hours. Order C's estimated total value is below the threshold, so it is not reported. If the agency notifies the contractor by November 15 that a figure looks inconsistent, the contractor revises the report or documents its rationale by November 30.

## Never confuse

This clause reports for orders that exceed the thresholds [@CL-CMP-5956], while the basic clause reports for services performed under the contract [@CL-CMP-5947]; the basic clause is on the [service contract reporting card](cmp-service-contract-reporting.md). The subcontractor duty still turns on subcontracts at or above the thresholds, and the contractor files the report [@CL-CMP-5962; @CL-CMP-5961].
