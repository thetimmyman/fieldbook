## Purpose

Explains the two encouraged practices in this pair of clauses, donating excess food to nonprofit organizations and adopting policies that ban text messaging while driving, and the subcontract flowdown duty each clause carries.

## The rule

Food donation. The Contractor is encouraged, to the maximum extent practicable and safe, to donate excess, apparently wholesome food to nonprofit organizations that provide assistance to food-insecure people in the United States [@CL-CMP-5441]. The Contractor, including any subcontractors, shall assume the responsibility for all the costs and the logistical support to collect, transport, maintain the safety of, or distribute the excess, apparently wholesome food to the nonprofit organization or organizations that provide assistance to food-insecure people [@CL-CMP-5521]. The Contractor will not be reimbursed for any costs incurred or associated with the donation of excess foods, and any costs incurred for excess food donations are unallowable [@CL-CMP-5442].

Definitions in the food clause. Apparently wholesome food means food that meets all quality and labeling standards imposed by Federal, State, and local laws and regulations even though the food may not be readily marketable due to appearance, age, freshness, grade, size, surplus, or other conditions [@CL-CMP-6080]. Excess food means food that is not required to meet the needs of the executive agencies and would otherwise be discarded [@CL-CMP-6081]. Food-insecure means inconsistent access to sufficient, safe, and nutritious food, and a nonprofit organization is any organization described in section 501(c) of the Internal Revenue Code of 1986 and exempt from tax under section 501(a) of that Code [@CL-CMP-6082].

Liability. The Government and the Contractor, including any subcontractors, are exempt from civil and criminal liability to the extent provided under the Bill Emerson Good Samaritan Food Donation Act, and nothing in the clause is to be construed to supersede State or local health regulations [@CL-CMP-6111].

Text messaging. The clause implements Executive Order 13513, Federal Leadership on Reducing Text Messaging while Driving, dated October 1, 2009 [@CL-CMP-6085]. The Contractor is encouraged to adopt and enforce policies that ban text messaging while driving company-owned or -rented vehicles or Government-owned vehicles, or privately-owned vehicles when on official Government business or when performing any work for or on behalf of the Government [@CL-CMP-5444]. The Contractor is also encouraged to conduct initiatives in a manner commensurate with the size of the business, such as establishing new rules and programs or re-evaluating existing programs to prohibit text messaging while driving [@CL-CMP-6112]. Such initiatives also include education, awareness, and other outreach to employees about the safety risks associated with texting while driving [@CL-CMP-6113].

Definitions in the text messaging clause. Text messaging means reading from or entering data into any handheld or other electronic device, including for the purpose of short message service texting, e-mailing, instant messaging, obtaining navigational information, or engaging in any other form of electronic data retrieval or electronic data communication [@CL-CMP-6110]. Driving means operating a motor vehicle on an active roadway with the motor running, including while temporarily stationary because of traffic, a traffic light, stop sign, or otherwise [@CL-CMP-6083]. Driving does not include operating a motor vehicle, with or without the motor running, when one has pulled over to the side of, or off, an active roadway and has halted in a location where one can safely remain stationary [@CL-CMP-6084].

## How it works

Food clause flowdown. The Contractor must insert the food clause in all contracts, task orders, delivery orders, purchase orders and other similar instruments that exceed the threshold specified in Federal Acquisition Regulation 26.404 on the date of subcontract award with its subcontractors or suppliers, at any tier [@CL-CMP-5443]. The flowdown reaches subcontractors or suppliers who will perform, under the contract, the provision, service or sale of food in the United States [@CL-CMP-5522].

Text messaging flowdown. The Contractor must insert the substance of the text messaging clause, including its subcontract paragraph, in all subcontracts that exceed the micro-purchase threshold as defined on the date of subcontract award [@CL-CMP-5445].

Navigation devices. Glancing at or listening to a navigational device that is secured in a commercially designed holder affixed to the vehicle is not text messaging, provided the destination and route are programmed into the device either before driving or while stopped in a location off the roadway where it is safe and legal to park [@CL-CMP-5446].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor with a food service operation has leftover prepared meals that would otherwise be thrown away, and it chooses to donate them to a food bank. Collecting and transporting the meals costs 1,200 and storing them safely costs 300, so the contractor bears 1,500 in total and cannot bill any of it to the Government. Separately, it awards a subcontract to a caterer that will sell food in the United States under the contract, and the subcontract exceeds the threshold in Federal Acquisition Regulation 26.404 on the award date, so the food clause is inserted. A subcontract for office cleaning does not involve food, so the food clause is not inserted there. A vehicle support subcontract that exceeds the micro-purchase threshold on its award date carries the text messaging clause, and a small order at or below that threshold does not.

## Never confuse

Encouraged is not the same as required. The donation and the driving policies are encouraged, while inserting each clause in covered subcontracts is a duty [@CL-CMP-5443; @CL-CMP-5445]. The costs of any donation fall on the Contractor and are unallowable, so encouraging a donation does not mean the Government will pay for it [@CL-CMP-5442].
