## Purpose

Shows what the organizational conflicts of interest subpart does, and who counts as a marketing consultant under it.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-8238] |
| Codified citation | FAR 9.500 and 9.501 [@CL-CMP-8238; @CL-CMP-8241] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-8238] |
| Applies to | The organizational conflicts of interest subpart and its definition of marketing consultant [@CL-CMP-8238; @CL-CMP-8241] |

The subpart prescribes responsibilities, general rules and procedures for identifying, evaluating and resolving organizational conflicts of interest [@CL-CMP-8238]. It provides examples to assist contracting officers in applying those rules and procedures to individual contracting situations [@CL-CMP-8239]. It implements section 8141 of the 1989 Department of Defense Appropriation Act [@CL-CMP-8240].

As used in the subpart, a marketing consultant is any independent contractor who furnishes advice, information, direction or assistance to an offeror or any other contractor in support of the preparation or submission of an offer for a Government contract by that offeror [@CL-CMP-8241]. An independent contractor is not a marketing consultant when rendering services excluded in subpart 37.2 [@CL-CMP-8242]. It is also not a marketing consultant when rendering routine engineering and technical services (such as installation, operation or maintenance of systems, equipment, software, components or facilities), routine legal, actuarial, auditing and accounting services, or training services [@CL-CMP-8243].

## How it works

These two sections state the purpose of the subpart and a definition; they do not themselves place a duty on a contractor [@CL-CMP-8238; @CL-CMP-8241]. The examples are for contracting officers, who apply the rules to a particular situation [@CL-CMP-8239]. The contracting officer's procedures are on the [contracting officer procedures](../../supply-chain/sourcing-evidence/sup-oci-contracting-officer-procedures.md) card, and the general rules are on the [general rules](../../mna/diligence/mna-organizational-conflicts-of-interest-general-rules.md) card. The definition has three parts: the work (advice, information, direction or assistance), the person who receives it (an offeror or any other contractor), and its purpose (support of the preparation or submission of an offer by that offeror) [@CL-CMP-8241]. The listed services are outside the definition even when they are given to an offeror [@CL-CMP-8242; @CL-CMP-8243].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A firm is paid 80,000 dollars to help draft a bidder's proposal, including the pricing narrative. Another firm is paid 80,000 dollars to maintain the same bidder's laboratory equipment. Answer: the first firm is an independent contractor furnishing assistance in support of the preparation of an offer, so it is a marketing consultant [@CL-CMP-8241]. The second firm is rendering routine maintenance of equipment, so it is not a marketing consultant [@CL-CMP-8243].

## Never confuse

A marketing consultant is not simply any consultant. The definition is a test of support for an offer, and the listed excluded services are outside it [@CL-CMP-8241; @CL-CMP-8242; @CL-CMP-8243]. The definition is also separate from the question of where the subpart applies, which is on the [applicability](./cmp-organizational-conflicts-applicability.md) card.
