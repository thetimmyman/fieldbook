## Purpose

Shows when the subpart on notifying employees of their rights under the National Labor Relations Act does not apply, and how an exemption by the Secretary works.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-8166] |
| Codified citation | FAR 22.1602, FAR 22.1603(a), (b)(1) and (b)(2) [@CL-CMP-8958; @CL-CMP-8959; @CL-CMP-8166; @CL-CMP-8167; @CL-CMP-8170] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-8166] |
| Applies to | Contracts and subcontracts that fall outside the listed exclusions in paragraph (a), unless the Secretary exempts them under paragraph (b) [@CL-CMP-8166; @CL-CMP-8167] |

The policy section says Executive Order 13496 requires contractors to post a notice informing employees of their rights under Federal labor laws [@CL-CMP-8958]. It adds that the Secretary has determined that the notice must contain employee rights under the National Labor Relations Act, section 151 and following of title 29 of the United States Code [@CL-CMP-8959]. The text names only the Secretary and does not say which Secretary.

Paragraph (a) lists three exclusions. The requirements of the subpart do not apply to contracts under the simplified acquisition threshold, to subcontracts of $10,000 or less, or to contracts or subcontracts for work performed exclusively outside the United States [@CL-CMP-8166].

Taken one at a time, the first exclusion is for contracts under the simplified acquisition threshold, and the section measures it on the contract and prints no dollar figure [@CL-CMP-8191]. The second is for subcontracts of $10,000 or less, measured on the subcontract [@CL-CMP-8192]. The third is for contracts or subcontracts for work performed exclusively outside the United States [@CL-CMP-8193].

Paragraph (b)(1): if the Secretary finds that the requirements of the Executive Order impair the ability of the Government to procure goods and services on an economical and efficient basis, or if special circumstances require an exemption in order to serve the national interest, the Secretary may exempt a contracting department or agency, or groups of departments or agencies [@CL-CMP-8167; @CL-CMP-8168].

The exemption is from the requirements of any or all of the provisions of the Executive Order with respect to a particular contract or subcontract, or any class of contracts or subcontracts [@CL-CMP-8168]. It may include the requirement to include the clause at 52.222-40, or parts of that clause, in contracts [@CL-CMP-8169].

Paragraph (b)(2): requests for exemptions may be submitted in accordance with Department of Labor regulations at 29 CFR 471.3 [@CL-CMP-8170].

## How it works

The section calls for no decision or approval by anyone for the paragraph (a) exclusions. It states that the requirements do not apply to the listed contracts and subcontracts [@CL-CMP-8166].

The exclusions are measured on different things. The first is measured on the contract, the second on the subcontract, and the third on the place where the work is performed, which must be exclusively outside the United States [@CL-CMP-8191; @CL-CMP-8192; @CL-CMP-8193].

The second route runs through the Secretary. The Secretary decides whether to exempt. The section does not name which Secretary; it refers to the Executive Order and to Department of Labor regulations for requests, and that is all it states [@CL-CMP-8167; @CL-CMP-8170].

The power is stated as may. A finding of either kind opens the way to an exemption, and the section does not say the Secretary must grant one [@CL-CMP-8167].

The exemption may reach a contracting department or agency, or groups of them, for a particular contract or subcontract or a class of them [@CL-CMP-8168]. It may reach the clause requirement at 52.222-40, or parts of that clause [@CL-CMP-8169].

A request may be submitted in accordance with the Department of Labor regulations. The section does not say who submits it and names no deadline or form [@CL-CMP-8170].

The section binds no contractor to a duty of its own. It removes requirements for the contracts it names, and the requirements themselves are on [Visa denial notice and employee rights notices under the National Labor Relations Act](cmp-visa-denial-and-nlr-employee-rights-notices.md). The thresholds are on [Acquisition thresholds](cmp-acquisition-thresholds.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A subcontract worth 8,000 dollars goes to a supplier for work performed exclusively in another country. It is at or below the 10,000 dollar line for subcontracts, and the work is performed exclusively outside the United States, so the exclusions in paragraph (a)(2) and (a)(3) each apply [@CL-CMP-8192; @CL-CMP-8193].

A second subcontract is worth 12,000 dollars, and some of its work is performed in the United States. It is above the 10,000 dollar line and its work is not performed exclusively outside the United States, so neither paragraph (a)(2) nor paragraph (a)(3) excludes it [@CL-CMP-8192; @CL-CMP-8193]. Whether the subpart's requirements reach it is then a question for the rest of the subpart. A Secretary exemption under paragraph (b) could still remove them [@CL-CMP-8167; @CL-CMP-8168].

## Never confuse

Never confuse the listed exclusions with an exemption by the Secretary. The exclusions apply by the terms of the section to the contracts and subcontracts it names. An exemption depends on a finding by the Secretary, may be shaped to a particular contract or a class of contracts, and may be requested [@CL-CMP-8166; @CL-CMP-8167; @CL-CMP-8168; @CL-CMP-8170].

Never read the subcontract line as a contract line. The $10,000 line is for subcontracts [@CL-CMP-8192]. For contracts the section uses the simplified acquisition threshold [@CL-CMP-8191; @CL-CMP-8192].
