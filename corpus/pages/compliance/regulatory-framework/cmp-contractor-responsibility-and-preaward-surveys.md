## Purpose

Shows the responsibility check that comes before award, how a preaward survey feeds it, and the limits on disclosing what the survey found.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-7180] |
| Codified citation | FAR 9.103, 9.106-1 and 9.105-3 [@CL-CMP-7180; @CL-CMP-7186; @CL-CMP-7190] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-7180] |
| Applies to | Awards to responsible prospective contractors only [@CL-CMP-7180] |

Purchases are made from, and contracts are awarded to, responsible prospective contractors only, and no purchase or award is made unless the contracting officer makes an affirmative determination of responsibility [@CL-CMP-7180]. In the absence of information clearly indicating that the prospective contractor is responsible, the contracting officer must make a determination of nonresponsibility [@CL-CMP-7181].

If the prospective contractor is a small business concern, the contracting officer must comply with subpart 19.6, Certificates of Competency and Determinations of Responsibility, and if Section 8(a) of the Small Business Act applies, see subpart 19.8 [@CL-CMP-7182].

A prospective contractor must affirmatively demonstrate its responsibility, including, when necessary, the responsibility of its proposed subcontractors [@CL-CMP-7185]. Award on lowest evaluated price alone can be false economy if there is later default, late delivery or other unsatisfactory performance that adds contractual or administrative cost [@CL-CMP-7183]. It is important that Government purchases be made at the lowest price, but that does not require an award to a supplier solely because it submitted the lowest offer [@CL-CMP-7184].

## How it works

A preaward survey is normally required only when the information on hand or readily available to the contracting officer, including information from commercial sources, is not sufficient to make a determination regarding responsibility [@CL-CMP-7186].

When the contemplated contract will have a fixed price at or below the simplified acquisition threshold, or will involve commercial products or commercial services, the contracting officer should not request a survey unless circumstances justify its cost [@CL-CMP-7187].

Before beginning a survey, the surveying activity must ascertain whether the prospective contractor is debarred, suspended or ineligible [@CL-CMP-7300]. If it is, the surveying activity must advise the contracting officer promptly and must not proceed with the survey unless the contracting officer specifically requests it to do so [@CL-CMP-7189].

When a cognizant contract administration office becomes aware of a prospective award to a contractor about which unfavorable information exists and no survey has been requested, it must promptly obtain and transmit the details to the contracting officer [@CL-CMP-7188].

Information accumulated to determine the responsibility of a prospective contractor, including the survey report, must not be released or disclosed outside the Government, except as the Freedom of Information Act subpart provides [@CL-CMP-7190]. The contracting officer may discuss survey information with the prospective contractor before determining responsibility [@CL-CMP-7301]. After award, the contracting officer or, if it is appropriate, the head of the surveying activity or a designee may discuss the survey findings with the company surveyed [@CL-CMP-7302]. Survey information may contain proprietary or source selection information and should be marked with the appropriate legend and protected accordingly [@CL-CMP-7303].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A buying office holds an offeror's financial statements but nothing about its production capacity. The offer is for a fixed-price order of commercial services priced at or below the simplified acquisition threshold. The information on hand does not clearly indicate that the offeror is responsible, so in the absence of such information the contracting officer must make a determination of nonresponsibility [@CL-CMP-7181], and no award is made unless the contracting officer makes an affirmative determination of responsibility [@CL-CMP-7180]. A survey is normally required only when the information on hand is not sufficient, and even then, because the order is a fixed price at or below the threshold and the services are commercial, the contracting officer should not request one unless circumstances justify its cost [@CL-CMP-7186; @CL-CMP-7187].

The contracting officer decides that circumstances justify the cost and requests a survey [@CL-CMP-7187]. The surveying activity finds that the offeror is suspended. It advises the contracting officer promptly and stops, because the contracting officer has not specifically asked it to continue [@CL-CMP-7300; @CL-CMP-7189]. The contracting officer may discuss the survey information with the offeror before deciding responsibility, but the survey report is not released outside the Government except as the Freedom of Information Act subpart provides [@CL-CMP-7301; @CL-CMP-7190].

## Never confuse

No purchase or award shall be made unless the contracting officer makes an affirmative determination of responsibility [@CL-CMP-7180]. A preaward survey is normally required only when the information on hand or readily available to the contracting officer is not sufficient to make a determination regarding responsibility [@CL-CMP-7186]. While it is important that Government purchases be made at the lowest price, this does not require an award to a supplier solely because that supplier submits the lowest offer [@CL-CMP-7184].

## Company calibration

Whether each bidding entity can affirmatively demonstrate responsibility, including for its proposed subcontractors, to every buying office it bids to [@Q-003].
