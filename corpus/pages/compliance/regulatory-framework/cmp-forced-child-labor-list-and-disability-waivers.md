## Purpose

Shows what the contracting officer must do when a solicitation covers end products on the forced or indentured child labor list, what the offeror certifies, and who may waive the clause for workers with disabilities.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-3093] |
| Codified citation | FAR 22.1503 and FAR 22.1403 [@CL-CMP-3093; @CL-CMP-3098] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-3093] |
| Applies to | Solicitations for supplies expected to exceed the micro-purchase threshold, for the list check, and the clause for workers with disabilities, for the waivers [@CL-CMP-3093; @CL-CMP-3098] |

When issuing a solicitation for supplies expected to exceed the micro-purchase threshold, the contracting officer must check the List of Products Requiring Contractor Certification as to Forced or Indentured Child Labor [@CL-CMP-3093]. Appearance of a product on the List is not a bar to purchase of any such product mined, produced, or manufactured in the identified country, but rather is an alert that there is a reasonable basis to believe that such product may have been mined, produced, or manufactured by forced or indentured child labor [@CL-CMP-3094]. The requirements that result from the appearance of any end product on the List do not apply to a solicitation or contract if the identified country of origin on the List is one of the countries the paragraph lists; the first listed alternative is Israel, when the anticipated value of the acquisition is $50,000 or more [@CL-CMP-3147]. Except as provided in the paragraph it names, before the contracting officer may make an award for an end product, regardless of country of origin, of a type identified by country of origin on the List, the offeror must certify one of two alternatives [@CL-CMP-3095; @CL-CMP-3096]. The first alternative is that the offeror will not supply any end product on the List that was mined, produced, or manufactured in a country identified on the List for that product, as specified in the solicitation by the contracting officer in the Certification Regarding Knowledge of Child Labor for Listed End Products [@CL-CMP-3096]. The second alternative is that the offeror has made a good faith effort to determine whether forced or indentured child labor was used to mine, produce, or manufacture any listed end product to be furnished under the contract that was mined, produced, or manufactured in a country identified on the List for that product [@CL-CMP-3145], and that, on the basis of the offeror's efforts, the offeror is unaware of any such use of child labor [@CL-CMP-3146]. Absent any actual knowledge that the certification is false, the contracting officer must rely on the offerors' certifications in making award decisions [@CL-CMP-3097]. Subject to an exception stated in the same paragraph, the contracting officer must refer the matter whenever the contracting officer has reason to believe that forced or indentured child labor was used to mine, produce, or manufacture an end product furnished under a contract awarded subject to the certification [@CL-CMP-3148], for investigation by the agency's Inspector General, the Attorney General, or the Secretary of the Treasury, whichever is determined appropriate in accordance with agency procedures [@CL-CMP-7773]. The duty to refer the matter does not apply to the extent that the end product is from the country listed in the exception paragraph, under a contract exceeding the applicable threshold [@CL-CMP-3149].

The Director of OFCCP may waive the application of any or all of the terms of the Equal Opportunity for Workers with Disabilities clause in the listed cases, one of which is any contract if a waiver is deemed to be in the national interest [@CL-CMP-3098]. The head of an agency may waive any requirement in its subpart when it is determined that the contract is essential to the national security and that its award without complying with the requirements is necessary to the national security [@CL-CMP-3099]. Upon making a national security determination to waive a requirement of the subpart, the head of the agency shall notify the Director of OFCCP in writing within 30 days [@CL-CMP-3138].

## How it works

The contracting officer checks the List when issuing the solicitation, and a listing is an alert and not a bar to purchase [@CL-CMP-3093; @CL-CMP-3094]. The offeror certifies before award, and the contracting officer relies on the certification absent actual knowledge that it is false [@CL-CMP-3095; @CL-CMP-3097]. The certification has alternatives. One is a promise not to supply a listed product from a listed country [@CL-CMP-3096], and the other rests on a good faith effort to find out whether forced or indentured child labor was used and on the offeror being unaware of any such use [@CL-CMP-3145; @CL-CMP-3146]. The section also excepts listed countries of origin above stated dollar values [@CL-CMP-3147], requires referral for investigation when the contracting officer has reason to believe forced or indentured child labor was used [@CL-CMP-3148; @CL-CMP-3149], and keeps the head of an agency's remedies.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contracting officer issues a solicitation for supplies expected to cost 80,000, which is above the micro-purchase threshold. The List shows an end product of the type being bought, with an identified country of origin. The answer: the contracting officer must check the List, the listing is an alert and not a bar, and before award the offeror must give the required certification unless the paragraph on excepted countries applies. The offeror certifies that it will not supply the listed product from the listed country. The contracting officer must rely on that certification unless it has actual knowledge that the certification is false.

## Never confuse

Appearance of a product on the List is an alert that there is a reasonable basis to believe that the product may have been made by forced or indentured child labor [@CL-CMP-3094]; absent any actual knowledge that the certification is false, the contracting officer must rely on the offerors' certifications in making award decisions [@CL-CMP-3097]. The Director of OFCCP's waiver concerns the clause at 52.222-36, Equal Opportunity for Workers with Disabilities [@CL-CMP-3098], and the child labor certification is made by the offeror [@CL-CMP-3095]. The micro-purchase figure is on [Simplified acquisition and micro-purchase thresholds](cmp-acquisition-thresholds.md).
