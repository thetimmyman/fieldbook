## Purpose

Explains what an offeror does when its offer includes foreign construction material, and how the Government evaluates such an offer.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-5412] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.225-24, basic provision [@CL-CMP-5412] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.225-24 is marked reserved [@CL-CMP-0373] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-5412] |
| Applies to | Offerors that seek a determination of inapplicability or an exception for foreign construction material [@CL-CMP-5412] |

An Offeror requesting a determination of inapplicability should submit the request to the Contracting Officer in time to allow a determination before submission of offers [@CL-CMP-6086]. The Offeror must include in the request the information and applicable supporting data required by paragraphs (c) and (d) of FAR clause 52.225-23 [@CL-CMP-6087]. The construction-material terms the paragraph lists, as used in the provision, are defined in the required-use clause, FAR clause 52.225-23 [@CL-CMP-6088].

An offeror that has not requested a determination regarding the inapplicability of section 1605 of the Recovery Act or the Buy American statute before submitting its offer, or has not received a response to a previous request, must include the information and supporting data in the offer [@CL-CMP-5412].

If the Government determines that an exception based on unreasonable cost of domestic construction material applies in accordance with FAR 25.604, the Government will evaluate an offer requesting exception by adding to the offered price of the contract [@CL-CMP-5413; @CL-CMP-5511]. Under item (i), the added amount is 25 percent of the offered price of the contract if foreign manufactured construction material is included in the offer based on an exception for the unreasonable cost of comparable manufactured domestic construction material [@CL-CMP-5511]. Under item (ii), the Government also adds 20 percent of the cost of foreign unmanufactured construction material included in the offer based on an exception for the unreasonable cost of comparable domestic unmanufactured construction material [@CL-CMP-5414].

If the solicitation specifies award on the basis of factors in addition to cost or price, the Contracting Officer applies the evaluation factors as specified in paragraph (c)(1) and uses the evaluated cost or price in determining the offer that represents the best value [@CL-CMP-5512].

## How it works

If two or more offers are equal in price and paragraph (c)(2) does not apply, the Contracting Officer gives preference to an offer that does not include foreign construction material excepted at the request of the Offeror on the basis of unreasonable cost [@CL-CMP-5415].

Under the basic provision, when an offer includes foreign construction material, other than Recovery Act designated country construction material, that the Government does not list in the solicitation in paragraph (b)(3) of FAR clause 52.225-23, the offeror also may submit an alternate offer based on use of equivalent domestic or Recovery Act designated country construction material [@CL-CMP-5416; @CL-CMP-7660]. If an alternate offer is submitted, the Offeror must submit a separate Standard Form 1442 for the alternate offer and a separate cost comparison table prepared in accordance with paragraphs (c) and (d) of FAR clause 52.225-23 [@CL-CMP-6089]. The separate cost comparison table is for the offer that is based on the use of any foreign construction material for which the Government has not yet determined an exception applies [@CL-CMP-6090].

Under the basic provision, if the Government determines that a particular exception requested in accordance with paragraph (c) of FAR clause 52.225-23 does not apply, it evaluates only those offers based on use of the equivalent domestic or Recovery Act designated country construction material [@CL-CMP-5513]. In that case the Offeror is required to furnish the equivalent domestic or Recovery Act designated country construction material [@CL-CMP-6091]. An offer based on use of the foreign construction material for which an exception was requested will be rejected as nonresponsive if the acquisition is conducted by sealed bidding, and may be accepted if revised during negotiations [@CL-CMP-5417].

Alternates. Under Alternate I, an offeror requesting a determination of inapplicability must submit the request with its offer, including the information and applicable supporting data required by paragraphs (c) and (d) of FAR clause 52.225-23 [@CL-CMP-6092]. Under Alternate II, the alternate offer provision applies when an offer includes foreign construction material, except foreign construction material from a Recovery Act designated country other than Bahrain, Mexico, or Oman that is not listed by the Government in paragraph (b)(3) of FAR clause 52.225-23 [@CL-CMP-6093]. Under Alternate II, in that case the offeror also may submit an alternate offer based on use of equivalent domestic or Recovery Act designated country construction material other than Bahraini, Mexican, or Omani construction material [@CL-CMP-6094]. Under Alternate II, if the Government determines that a requested exception does not apply, it will evaluate only those offers based on use of the equivalent domestic or Recovery Act designated country construction material other than Bahraini, Mexican, or Omani construction material [@CL-CMP-6095].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text under Class Deviation 2026-O0038, effective February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. The RFO text lists FAR 52.225-24 as reserved [@CL-CMP-0373]. It also lists FAR 52.225-23 as reserved [@CL-CMP-0374], and the codified notice provision refers to the codified clause at that number for its definitions [@CL-CMP-6088].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

Offer A is priced at 1,000,000 and includes foreign manufactured construction material under an unreasonable-cost exception. The evaluation adds 25 percent of the offered price, which is 250,000, so Offer A is evaluated at 1,250,000. Offer B is priced at 1,200,000 and uses domestic material, so it is evaluated at 1,200,000 and is lower. Offer C is priced at 800,000 and includes 40,000 of foreign unmanufactured material under an unreasonable-cost exception. The evaluation adds 20 percent of that 40,000, which is 8,000, so Offer C is evaluated at 808,000. If two offers were equal at an evaluated 900,000 and only one relied on an unreasonable-cost exception, the Contracting Officer would prefer the other, unless the best-value rule for factors beyond cost or price applied.

## Never confuse

The 25 percent is applied to the offered price of the contract, while the 20 percent is applied to the cost of the foreign unmanufactured material [@CL-CMP-5511; @CL-CMP-5414]. This provision does not itself list the excepted materials, which the Government lists in paragraph (b)(3) of FAR clause 52.225-23 [@CL-CMP-5416].

The codified FAR 52.225-24 carries the notice provision and its evaluation rules, while the RFO text prints that section number as reserved [@CL-CMP-5412; @CL-CMP-0373].

The companion clause on required use is covered in [Required use of American iron, steel and manufactured goods in construction under trade agreements](cmp-american-iron-steel-construction-trade-agreements.md), and the related clause and provision on American iron, steel and manufactured goods in construction are covered in [Required use of American iron, steel and manufactured goods, and its notice provision](../../supply-chain/supplier-identity/sup-american-iron-steel-manufactured-goods.md).
