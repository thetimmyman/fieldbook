## Purpose

Shows what a subject invention is, that the contractor may retain ownership of it, and what license each side holds depending on who has title.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-3629] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.227-11 [@CL-CMP-3629] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.227-11 [@CL-CMP-1846; @CL-CMP-1847] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-3629] |
| Applies to | Subject inventions made in the performance of work under the contract [@CL-CMP-3629] |

A subject invention is any invention of the Contractor made in the performance of work under the contract, and the Contractor may retain ownership of each subject invention throughout the world in accordance with the provisions of the clause [@CL-CMP-3629]. For an invention other than a plant variety, made means the conception or first actual reduction to practice of the invention [@CL-CMP-4232]. If the Contractor retains ownership of any subject invention, the Government shall have a nonexclusive, nontransferable, irrevocable, paid-up license to practice, or have practiced for or on its behalf, the subject invention throughout the world [@CL-CMP-3631].

The Contractor shall retain a nonexclusive royalty-free license throughout the world in each subject invention to which the Government obtains title, unless the Contractor fails to disclose the invention within the times specified in paragraph (c) of the clause [@CL-CMP-3630]. The agency may revoke or modify that license to the extent necessary to achieve expeditious practical application of the subject invention in a particular country, in accordance with the procedures the clause names [@CL-CMP-3678].

## How it works

Ownership starts with the Contractor, which may keep title to each subject invention [@CL-CMP-3629]. An invention other than a plant variety is made when it is conceived or first actually reduced to practice [@CL-CMP-4232]. If the Contractor keeps title, the Government takes a license in the invention [@CL-CMP-3631]. If the Government instead obtains title, the Contractor keeps a license, unless it failed to disclose on time, and the agency may revoke or modify that license as described [@CL-CMP-3630; @CL-CMP-3678]. The disclosure, election and filing steps, and the Government's right to take title, are on the [companion card for the later paragraphs](cmp-patent-rights-contractor-ownership-deadlines-and-title.md) of this clause.

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers must use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified FAR part 52 text, as Class Deviation 2026-O0038 directs, effective February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. The RFO text of FAR 52.227-11 keeps the definition of a subject invention as any invention of the Contractor made in the performance of work under the contract [@CL-CMP-1846; @CL-CMP-3629] and the Contractor's right to retain ownership throughout the world [@CL-CMP-1847; @CL-CMP-3629]. It keeps the meaning of made for an invention other than a plant variety [@CL-CMP-1844; @CL-CMP-4232], and for a plant variety it says made means that the Contractor has at least tentatively determined that the variety has been reproduced with recognized characteristics [@CL-CMP-1845]. It keeps the Government's license where the Contractor retains ownership [@CL-CMP-1848; @CL-CMP-3631], the Contractor's license where the Government obtains title unless the Contractor fails to disclose within the times specified [@CL-CMP-1849; @CL-CMP-3630], and the agency's power to revoke or modify that license [@CL-CMP-1865; @CL-CMP-3678].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor invents a new coating while performing the contract and elects to keep title. The coating is a subject invention. The contractor owns it, and the Government holds a paid-up license to practice it, or have it practiced for the Government, throughout the world. If the Government had obtained title instead, the contractor would keep a nonexclusive royalty-free license, unless it had failed to disclose the invention on time.

## Never confuse

A subject invention is not every invention the contractor owns. It is an invention made in the performance of work under the contract [@CL-CMP-3629]. The Government's license in an invention the contractor owns is a different grant from the contractor's license in an invention the Government owns [@CL-CMP-3631; @CL-CMP-3630]. The clause for ownership by the Government is a separate clause with its own rules, covered on the [Government ownership card](cmp-patent-rights-government-ownership.md). Rights in an invention are also separate from the Government's rights in data, which have their own card on [rights in data, definitions and allocation](cmp-rights-in-data-general-definitions-and-allocation.md).
