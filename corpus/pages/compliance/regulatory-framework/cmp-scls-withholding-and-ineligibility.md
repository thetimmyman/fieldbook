## Purpose

Explains what a violation of the Service Contract Labor Standards clause makes the contractor liable for, when the contracting officer may or must withhold payments, where the withheld money goes, and when a violator is barred from award.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-6765] |
| Codified citation | FAR 22.1022 and 22.1025 [@CL-CMP-6765; @CL-CMP-6771] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-6765] |
| Applies to | Contracts with the Service Contract Labor Standards clause [@CL-CMP-6765] |

A violation of the clause at FAR 52.222-41 renders the responsible contractor liable for the amount of any deductions, rebates, refunds or underpayments, which includes nonpayment, of compensation due employees performing the contract [@CL-CMP-6765]. The contracting officer may withhold the amount needed to pay such underpaid employees and, upon written request of the Department of Labor from a level no lower than that of Deputy Regional Administrator, Wage and Hour Division, must withhold it [@CL-CMP-6766]. The amount comes from accrued payments due the contractor on the contract, or on any other prime contract with the contractor, whether or not that other contract is subject to the Service Contract Labor Standards statute [@CL-CMP-6767].

## How it works

The agency must place the amount withheld in a deposit fund [@CL-CMP-6768]. The withheld funds must be transferred to the Department of Labor for disbursement to the underpaid employees on order of the Secretary or authorized representatives, an Administrative Law Judge, or the Administrative Review Board [@CL-CMP-6924]. The Department of Labor has given blanket approval to forward withheld funds pending completion of an investigation or other administrative proceeding when disposition of withheld funds remains the final action necessary to close out a contract [@CL-CMP-6769].

Persons or firms found to be in violation of the Service Contract Labor Standards statute will have an active exclusion record contained in the System for Award Management [@CL-CMP-6770]. No Government contract may be awarded to any violator so listed because of such a violation, or to any firm, corporation, partnership or association in which the violator has a substantial interest, without the approval of the Secretary of Labor [@CL-CMP-6771]. The prohibition against award to an ineligible contractor applies to both prime and subcontracts [@CL-CMP-6772].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An audit finds that a contractor underpaid eight employees on a service contract, and one of the eight was not paid at all, which counts as an underpayment [@CL-CMP-6765]. The contracting officer may withhold the amount needed from accrued payments due on that contract [@CL-CMP-6766; @CL-CMP-6767]. The same contractor holds a separate prime contract, which need not be subject to the statute, and the amount may also come from accrued payments on that contract [@CL-CMP-6767]. If the Department of Labor makes a written request from the level of Deputy Regional Administrator or higher, the contracting officer must withhold [@CL-CMP-6766]. The agency places the amount in a deposit fund and transfers it to the Department of Labor for disbursement on the order of the Secretary or authorized representatives, an Administrative Law Judge, or the Administrative Review Board [@CL-CMP-6768; @CL-CMP-6924].

Separately, once the contractor is found in violation, it has an active exclusion record in the System for Award Management [@CL-CMP-6770]. A firm in which the contractor has a substantial interest may receive a Government contract only with the approval of the Secretary of Labor, and the bar covers subcontracts as well as prime contracts [@CL-CMP-6771; @CL-CMP-6772].

## Never confuse

Withholding is not an exclusion. Withholding holds back money to pay underpaid employees, and it is the contracting officer's option unless the Department of Labor makes a written request from the stated level, which makes it mandatory [@CL-CMP-6766]. An exclusion blocks award to the violator and to a firm in which the violator holds a substantial interest unless the Secretary of Labor approves [@CL-CMP-6771].
