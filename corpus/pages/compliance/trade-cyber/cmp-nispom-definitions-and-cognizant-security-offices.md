## Purpose

Lists the NISPOM acronyms and definitions that finance and contracts staff meet most often, and says where cognizant security office information is found.

This card rests on the NISPOM, the rule for work involving classified information, and not on the FAR. The acronym list in the definitions section gives FCL as facility (security) clearance, FGI as foreign government information and FOCI as foreign ownership, control, or influence [@CL-CMP-3495]. It gives GCA as government contracting activity [@CL-CMP-3496]. It gives KMP as key management personnel [@CL-CMP-3497] and SMO as senior management official [@CL-CMP-3498]. A CDC is a subset of contractors cleared under the NISP who have classified contracts with the DoD [@CL-CMP-3499].

The definitions themselves add detail. A contractor is any industrial, educational, commercial, or other entity that has been granted an entity eligibility determination by a CSA [@CL-CMP-3515]. An FCL is an administrative determination that, from a security viewpoint, an entity is eligible for access to classified information of a certain level and all lower levels [@CL-CMP-3516]. A foreign interest includes any foreign government, agency of a foreign government or representative of a foreign government, and any form of business enterprise or legal entity organized, chartered or incorporated under the laws of any country other than the United States or its territories [@CL-CMP-3517]. KMP includes an entity's senior management official, facility security officer and insider threat program senior official [@CL-CMP-3518]. Need-to-know is a determination made by an authorized holder of classified information that a prospective recipient has a requirement for access to, knowledge of, or possession of the classified information to perform tasks or services essential to the fulfillment of a classified contract [@CL-CMP-3519]. The SMO is an entity employee occupying a position in the entity with ultimate authority over the facility's operations and the authority to direct actions necessary for the safeguarding of classified information in the facility [@CL-CMP-3520].

For the DoD, the cognizant security office information refers the reader to the DCSA website for a listing of office locations and areas of responsibility and for information on verification of facility clearances and safeguarding [@CL-CMP-3500]. This card does not restate the other definitions in that section.

## How it works

A finance reader who sees an acronym looks it up in the list first [@CL-CMP-3495; @CL-CMP-3496; @CL-CMP-3497; @CL-CMP-3498], then reads the separate definition where one is given [@CL-CMP-3516; @CL-CMP-3518; @CL-CMP-3520]. The CDC definition ties a CDC to the NISP and to classified contracts with the DoD [@CL-CMP-3499]. To verify a facility clearance or find the right office, the DoD entry points to the DCSA website [@CL-CMP-3500]. Entity eligibility is covered on the [NISPOM entity eligibility determination (facility clearance)](cmp-nispom-entity-eligibility-determination.md) card, and the senior management official's role is on the [NISPOM security officials, insider threat program and self-inspection](cmp-nispom-general-procedures-and-security-officials.md) card. Foreign interests and foreign government information come up on the [NISPOM international transfers and foreign government information](cmp-nispom-international-transfers-and-foreign-government-information.md) card and the [NISPOM international visits and foreign national access](cmp-nispom-international-visits-and-foreign-national-access.md) card.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A finance analyst reads a customer contract that mentions an FCL, a GCA and the KMP of a joint venture partner.

Answer: the acronym list reads these as facility (security) clearance, government contracting activity and key management personnel. The definition of KMP adds that it includes the senior management official, the facility security officer and the insider threat program senior official.

## Never confuse

An acronym list entry is not a definition. The list gives the full words only [@CL-CMP-3495; @CL-CMP-3497; @CL-CMP-3498], and KMP and SMO each have a separate definition [@CL-CMP-3518; @CL-CMP-3520]. A CDC is also a narrower group than the contractors cleared under the NISP, because it needs classified contracts with the DoD [@CL-CMP-3499].
