## Purpose

Shows which security briefings and training a contractor gives its cleared employees and when, and how classified visits and meetings are arranged.

## The rule

| Applies to | Cleared employees of the contractor [@CL-CMP-3182] |

This card rests on the NISPOM, the rule for work involving classified information, and not on the FAR. Before access to classified information is granted, contractors will provide employees with an initial security briefing whose listed contents begin with threat awareness, including insider threat awareness [@CL-CMP-3181]. The contractor will provide all cleared employees with security education and training every 12 months [@CL-CMP-3182]. The contractor will provide insider threat awareness training to all cleared employees on an annual basis, although depending upon CSA specific guidance a CSA may instead conduct such training [@CL-CMP-3184]. The contractor must provide all newly cleared employees with insider threat awareness training before granting access to classified information [@CL-CMP-3313]. The contractor will ensure all employees authorized to make derivative classification decisions are trained in the proper application of the derivative classification principles, in accordance with CSA direction, and employees are not authorized to conduct derivative classification until they receive such training [@CL-CMP-3324].

Contractors will debrief cleared employees and annotate the debriefing in the appropriate contractor records when access to classified information is no longer needed and at the time of termination of employment [@CL-CMP-3183]. The occasions for debriefing cleared employees also include when an employee's eligibility for access to classified information is terminated, suspended, or revoked, and upon termination of the entity eligibility determination [@CL-CMP-3183; @CL-CMP-3314].

The visits paragraph applies when, for a lawful and authorized USG purpose, it is anticipated that classified information will be disclosed during a visit to a cleared contractor facility or to a USG facility [@CL-CMP-3315]. The number of classified visits will be held to a minimum, and the contractor must determine that the visit is necessary and that its purpose cannot be achieved without access to, or disclosure of, classified information [@CL-CMP-3186]. If a visit requires access to classified information, the host contractor will verify the visitor's PCL level [@CL-CMP-3187]. Verification of a visitor's PCL may be accomplished by a review of a CSA-designated database that contains the information or by a visit authorization letter provided by the visitor's employer [@CL-CMP-3185]. If a CSA-designated database is not available and a VAL is required, contractors will include in all VALs, among other listed items, the contractor's name, employee's name, address, and telephone number, and the assigned CAGE code if applicable [@CL-CMP-3316]. If a meeting is conducted by a cleared contractor, the meeting is authorized by a USG agency that has agreed to assume security jurisdiction, and that agency must approve the security arrangements, announcements, attendees, and the location of the meeting [@CL-CMP-3188].

## How it works

An employee's briefings and training include an initial briefing before access [@CL-CMP-3181], refresher training every 12 months [@CL-CMP-3182], annual insider threat awareness training [@CL-CMP-3184], and a debriefing when access ends [@CL-CMP-3183]. A newly cleared employee also receives insider threat awareness training before access is granted [@CL-CMP-3313]. For a visit, the contractor first decides it is necessary and keeps visits to a minimum [@CL-CMP-3186]. The host then verifies the visitor's PCL level [@CL-CMP-3187] through a database or a visit authorization letter [@CL-CMP-3185]. A meeting run by a cleared contractor needs authorization from a USG agency, which approves the arrangements [@CL-CMP-3188]. The insider threat program that this training supports is on the [NISPOM security officials, insider threat program and self-inspection](cmp-nispom-general-procedures-and-security-officials.md) card, and the employee's eligibility is on the [NISPOM personnel eligibility for access to classified information](cmp-nispom-personnel-eligibility-determination.md) card.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A cleared employee leaves a company after 3 years. A partner also asks to send two visitors to a classified review.

Answer: the company debriefs the employee at the time of termination of employment and records it. For the visit, it must determine that the visit is necessary and, because the visit requires access to classified information, verify each visitor's PCL level.

## Never confuse

The printed text lists insider threat awareness training separately from the general refresher training [@CL-CMP-3184; @CL-CMP-3182]. A visit authorization letter is one way to verify a visitor's PCL [@CL-CMP-3185]. The contractor's own determination that a visit is necessary [@CL-CMP-3186] and the USG agency's authorization of a meeting [@CL-CMP-3188] are separate steps.
