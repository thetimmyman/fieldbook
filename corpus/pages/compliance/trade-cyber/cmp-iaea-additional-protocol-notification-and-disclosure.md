## Purpose

Shows what a contractor must notify to DoD under the Additional Protocol clause and when it may release unclassified contract information under the disclosure clause.

## The rule

| Applies to | Contractors required to report activities under Department of Commerce or Nuclear Regulatory Commission regulations in order to implement the declarations required by the Additional Protocol [@CL-CMP-2863; @CL-CMP-2864]; unclassified information pertaining to the contract or related programs [@CL-CMP-2866] |

The notification clause applies where the contractor is required to report any of its activities under Department of Commerce regulations or Nuclear Regulatory Commission regulations [@CL-CMP-2863] in order to implement the declarations required by the U.S.-International Atomic Energy Agency Additional Protocol [@CL-CMP-2864]. In that case, one listed duty of the contractor is to immediately provide written notification to the DoD Program Manager that the clause identifies [@CL-CMP-2864]. The contractor shall not delay submission of any reports required by the Department of Commerce or the Nuclear Regulatory Commission while awaiting a DoD response to a notification [@CL-CMP-2865]. The contractor shall incorporate the substance of the notification clause, including its final paragraph, in all subcontracts that are subject to the provisions of the Additional Protocol [@CL-CMP-2917].

Unless an exception applies, the contractor shall not release to anyone outside its organization any unclassified information, regardless of medium, pertaining to any part of the contract or any program related to the contract [@CL-CMP-2866]. Two of the listed exceptions are prior written approval by the contracting officer and information that is otherwise in the public domain before the date of release [@CL-CMP-2867]. The third listed exception covers information that results from or arises during the performance of a project that involves no covered defense information, as defined in the safeguarding clause, and the project must also have been scoped and negotiated by the contracting activity with the contractor and research performer and determined in writing by the contracting officer to be fundamental research [@CL-CMP-2918; @CL-CMP-2919]. That exception paragraph goes on to tie the fundamental research determination to a national policy directive and to memoranda on fundamental research, which this card does not set out.

## How it works

A request for the contracting officer's prior written approval must identify the specific information to be released, the medium to be used, and the purpose for the release, and must be submitted at least 10 business days before the proposed date for release [@CL-CMP-2868]. The disclosure clause also flows down: the contractor agrees to include a similar requirement, including its flowdown paragraph, in each subcontract under the contract [@CL-CMP-2920]. DoD's steps after it receives a notification, such as a security assessment, are also not set out on this card.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor plans to publish a technical note about contract work. It sends the contracting officer a request naming the information, the medium and the purpose 12 business days before the planned date. It waits for written approval before release.

## Never confuse

The two clauses run in different directions: the notification clause applies where the contractor is required to report any of its activities in accordance with Department of Commerce regulations or Nuclear Regulatory Commission regulations in order to implement the declarations required by the U.S.-International Atomic Energy Agency Additional Protocol [@CL-CMP-2863; @CL-CMP-2864], while under the disclosure clause, unless an exception applies, the contractor shall not release to anyone outside its organization any unclassified information pertaining to any part of the contract or any program related to the contract [@CL-CMP-2866].
