## Purpose

Shows when the defense trade cooperation treaties may be used to prepare a response to a solicitation, and what the offeror must do if it uses them.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-CMP-4021] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 252.225-7046 [@CL-CMP-4021] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | JUN 2013 [@CL-CMP-10293] |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-4021] |
| Applies to | Approved Community members responding to the solicitation [@CL-CMP-4021] |

All contract line items in the contemplated contract, except any identified in the provision, are intended to satisfy U.S. DoD Treaty-eligible requirements [@CL-CMP-4055]. Approved Community members responding to the solicitation may only export or transfer defense articles that specifically respond to the stated requirements of the solicitation [@CL-CMP-4021]. The permission to use the defense trade cooperation treaties in a response is subject to the other terms and conditions of the solicitation and the contemplated contract that affect the acceptability of foreign sources or foreign end products, components, parts, or materials [@CL-CMP-4022]. Within that limit, Approved Community members are permitted, but not required, to use the treaties for exports or transfers of qualifying defense articles in preparing a response to the solicitation [@CL-CMP-4022; @CL-CMP-4023].

If the offeror uses the procedures established under the treaties, it agrees that, for the export or transfer of a qualifying defense article associated with responding to the solicitation, it shall carry out the duties listed in the provision [@CL-CMP-4024]. One duty is to comply with the requirements and provisions of the applicable treaties, the implementing arrangements, and corresponding regulations, including the International Traffic in Arms Regulations, of the United States Government and the government of Australia or of the United Kingdom, as applicable [@CL-CMP-4056]. Another is that, before the export or transfer of a qualifying defense article, the offeror shall mark, identify, transmit, store, and handle any defense articles provided for the purpose of responding to such solicitations, as well as any defense articles provided with or developed pursuant to their responses to such solicitations, in accordance with the treaties, the implementing arrangements, and corresponding regulations of the United States Government and the government of Australia or the government of the United Kingdom, as applicable, including, but not limited to, the marking and classification requirements described in the applicable regulations [@CL-CMP-4057; @CL-CMP-7365; @CL-CMP-7366; @CL-CMP-7367]. The offeror shall check one of the boxes listed in the representation paragraph and sign the representation [@CL-CMP-4025]. The offeror shall flow down the substance of the provision, excluding the representation, to any subcontractor at any tier intending to use the treaties in responding to the solicitation [@CL-CMP-4026].

## How it works

The provision gives a permission, not an obligation: Approved Community members may use the treaties in a response but need not [@CL-CMP-4023]. The permission is limited to defense articles that specifically respond to the stated requirements, and to the terms that affect the acceptability of foreign sources [@CL-CMP-4021; @CL-CMP-4022]. An offeror that does use the treaty procedures takes on the listed duties, including compliance with the treaties and regulations and the marking and handling duty before export or transfer, and the offeror checks and signs one of the listed representations [@CL-CMP-4024; @CL-CMP-4056; @CL-CMP-4057; @CL-CMP-4025]. The offeror flows the substance of the provision down to any subcontractor at any tier intending to use the treaties in responding to the solicitation, and the flowdown leaves out the representation [@CL-CMP-4026].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An offeror preparing a proposal needs to send a technical data package to a partner facility abroad that is on the approved list and will act as a subcontractor in the response. The offeror may use the treaty procedures, but it does not have to. It sends only items that respond to the stated requirements. It follows the listed duties and ticks the representation box that matches what it did. It passes the provision to the partner, without the representation, because the partner intends to use the treaties.

## Never confuse

The provision and the clause differ in phase: this provision covers exports and transfers in preparing a response to the solicitation [@CL-CMP-4023], while the clause on exports in performance of the contract covers exports and transfers in performance of the contract [@CL-CMP-4030]; see [Exports by approved community members in performance of the contract](cmp-approved-community-exports-in-performance.md). The export-controlled items clause requires the contractor to comply with all applicable laws and regulations regarding export-controlled items [@CL-CMP-4017]; see [Export-controlled items clause](cmp-export-controlled-items-clause.md).
