## Purpose

Shows whom the CMMC Program rule binds, who chooses the status for a procurement, and how the requirements phase into DoD solicitations and contracts.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Not established. |
| Codified citation | Not established. |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Not established. |
| Applies to | DoD contract and subcontract awardees that will process, store, or transmit information meeting the standards for FCI or CUI on contractor information systems [@CL-CMP-2050] |

The CMMC rule is a DoD rule that is not part of the FAR, DFARS or CAS, so the instrument rows read Not established. It binds DoD contract and subcontract awardees that will process, store, or transmit information meeting the standards for FCI or CUI on contractor information systems in performance of the DoD contract [@CL-CMP-2050]. The same paragraph lists a second group it binds, the private-sector businesses or other entities that make up the CMMC Assessment and Certification Ecosystem [@CL-CMP-2051]. It does not apply to Federal information systems operated by contractors or subcontractors on behalf of the Government [@CL-CMP-2052].

The program requirements apply to all DoD solicitations and contracts under which a defense contractor or subcontractor will process, store, or transmit FCI or CUI on unclassified contractor information systems, with the inclusions and exceptions that the same sentence goes on to list [@CL-CMP-2125]. They include acquisitions of commercial items, other than those exclusively for COTS items, valued at greater than the micro-purchase threshold, except under the circumstances the paragraph lists [@CL-CMP-2053]. One circumstance is a procurement that occurs during Implementation Phase 1, 2 or 3, where the requirements apply in accordance with the requirements for the relevant phase-in period [@CL-CMP-2126]. Another is that DoD may waive the application of the requirements to a procurement or class of procurements in advance of the solicitation, in accordance with all applicable policies, procedures and approval requirements [@CL-CMP-2127]. The printed text does not give the amount of the micro-purchase threshold.

DoD Program Managers or requiring activities are responsible for selecting the CMMC Status that applies to a particular procurement or contract, based upon the type of information, FCI or CUI, that will be processed on, stored on, or transmitted through a contractor information system [@CL-CMP-2054]. The levels and the scope of an assessment are on the [model levels and scoping](cmp-cmmc-model-levels-and-scoping.md) card. This card does not restate the DFARS safeguarding clause; see [Safeguarding covered defense information and cyber incident reporting](cmp-safeguarding-covered-defense-information.md).

## How it works

DoD adds the requirements to solicitations and contracts in phases. Phase 1 begins on the effective date of the complementary acquisition final rule, and DoD intends to require [Level 1 (Self)](cmp-cmmc-level-1-self-assessment.md) or [Level 2 (Self)](cmp-cmmc-level-2-assessment.md) status for all applicable solicitations and contracts as a condition of contract award [@CL-CMP-2055]. Phase 2 begins one calendar year after the start of Phase 1, and DoD intends then to add a requirement for [Level 2 (C3PAO)](cmp-cmmc-level-2-assessment.md) status for applicable DoD solicitations and contracts as a condition of award [@CL-CMP-2056]. Phase 3 begins one calendar year after the start of Phase 2 [@CL-CMP-2057]. DoD then intends to require Level 2 (C3PAO) status for all applicable solicitations and contracts both as a condition of award and as a condition to exercise an option period on a contract awarded after the effective date [@CL-CMP-2136]. In Phase 3 DoD also intends to require [Level 3 (DIBCAC)](cmp-cmmc-level-3-assessment.md) status for all applicable solicitations and contracts as a condition of award [@CL-CMP-2137], though it may delay that requirement to an option period instead [@CL-CMP-2138]. Phase 4, full implementation, begins one calendar year after the start of Phase 3, and DoD will include the requirements in all applicable solicitations and contracts, including option periods on contracts awarded before Phase 4 begins [@CL-CMP-2058].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A program office issues a solicitation 14 months after Phase 1 began, for work in which the contractor will hold CUI on its own unclassified system. Phase 2 began at month 12. The program manager looks at the information involved and selects a Level 2 (C3PAO) status for the solicitation. The status in the solicitation comes from the program manager's choice, and the phase only shows what DoD intends to require at that point.

## Never confuse

The phases are not calendar dates: Phase 1 is tied to the effective date of a separate acquisition rule, and each later phase runs from the start of the one before [@CL-CMP-2055; @CL-CMP-2056]. The printed text gives no calendar date for any phase. The program manager's choice of status for a procurement is a different step from a subcontractor's status, which follows the flowdown rule described on [its own card](cmp-cmmc-affirmation-and-flowdown.md).
