## Purpose

Shows who must complete antiterrorism awareness training, when, and what contractors owe when performing or traveling outside the United States.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-CMP-4034; @CL-CMP-4036] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 252.204-7004 [@CL-CMP-4034]; DFARS 252.225-7043 [@CL-CMP-4036] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-4034; @CL-CMP-4036] |
| Applies to | Contractor personnel who require routine physical access to a Federally-controlled facility or military installation [@CL-CMP-4034]; the contractor and its subcontractors, if performing or traveling outside the United States under the contract [@CL-CMP-4036] |

Contractor personnel who require routine physical access to a Federally-controlled facility or military installation shall complete Level I antiterrorism awareness training within 30 days of requiring access and annually thereafter [@CL-CMP-4034]. Level I antiterrorism awareness training shall be completed through a DoD-sponsored and certified computer or web-based distance learning instruction, or under the instruction of a Level I antiterrorism awareness instructor [@CL-CMP-4060]. The clause opens that sentence with a reference to a Department of Defense Instruction, which this card does not set out. The contractor shall include the substance of the training clause in subcontracts, including subcontracts for commercial products and commercial services, when subcontractor performance requires routine physical access to a Federally-controlled facility or military installation [@CL-CMP-4035].

Except as provided in paragraph (c) of the clause, the contractor and its subcontractors, if performing or traveling outside the United States under the contract, shall affiliate with the Overseas Security Advisory Council if the contractor or subcontractor is a United States entity [@CL-CMP-4036; @CL-CMP-4038]. Under the same lead-in, the contractor and its subcontractors shall ensure that contractor and subcontractor personnel who are United States nationals and in-country on a non-transitory basis register with the United States Embassy [@CL-CMP-4036; @CL-CMP-4037]. They shall also provide contractor and subcontractor personnel with antiterrorism and force protection awareness information commensurate with that which the Department of Defense provides to its military and civilian personnel and their families, to the extent such information can be made available prior to travel outside the United States [@CL-CMP-4036; @CL-CMP-4061]. And they shall obtain and comply with the most current antiterrorism and force protection guidance for contractor and subcontractor personnel [@CL-CMP-4036; @CL-CMP-4062]. In this clause, the United States means the 50 States, the District of Columbia, and outlying areas [@CL-CMP-4063]. The requirements of the clause do not apply to a subcontractor that is a foreign government, a representative of a foreign government, or a foreign corporation wholly owned by a foreign government [@CL-CMP-4038].

## How it works

The two clauses work from different triggers. The training clause is triggered by routine physical access to a Federally-controlled facility or military installation, and it sets a time limit and a yearly repeat [@CL-CMP-4034]. The policy clause is triggered by performing or traveling outside the United States under the contract, and it applies to the contractor and its subcontractors, with a listed exception for certain foreign government subcontractors [@CL-CMP-4036; @CL-CMP-4038]. Its duties cover affiliation, embassy registration, awareness information and current guidance [@CL-CMP-4036; @CL-CMP-4037; @CL-CMP-4061; @CL-CMP-4062]. The contractor shall include the substance of the training clause in subcontracts when subcontractor performance requires that routine access [@CL-CMP-4035].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor assigns a technician who will badge into a military installation each week. The technician starts the access on a Monday, so Level I training must be done within the time limit that runs from that start, and again every year. A second team will work overseas under the same contract. The company affiliates with the advisory council because it is a United States entity. A subcontractor owned wholly by a foreign government is outside the policy clause.

## Never confuse

The training clause concerns routine physical access to a Federally-controlled facility or military installation. The policy clause concerns performance or travel outside the United States. The triggers, duties and flowdown rules differ.
