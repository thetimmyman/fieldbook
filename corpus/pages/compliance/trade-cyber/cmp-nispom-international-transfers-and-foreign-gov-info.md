## Purpose

Shows when a contractor may disclose classified information to foreign interests, how export authorization differs from disclosure authorization, and the conditions for handling foreign government information and international transfers.

| Applies to | Exports that involve classified information [@CL-CMP-3504] |

The transfer of articles, services and related data to a foreign person, within or outside the United States, or the movement of such material or information to any destination outside of the legal jurisdiction of the United States constitutes an export [@CL-CMP-3470]. Contractors will only disclose non-public USG information to foreign persons in accordance with specified requirements of the contract, and in the absence of any specified requirements the contractor will not disclose non-public USG information to foreign persons [@CL-CMP-3471].

Disclosure and export of classified information, authorized by an appropriate USG disclosure official, by a contractor will ensure the listed items [@CL-CMP-3479]. Under the international agreements item, contractors may not disclose classified information until agreements are signed by the participating government and disclosure guidance and security arrangements are established [@CL-CMP-3472], and the export of technical data pursuant to such agreements may be exempt by approval of the Department of State or the Department of Commerce [@CL-CMP-3473]. If a proposed disclosure is in support of a foreign government requirement, the contractor should consult with U.S. in-country officials, normally the U.S. Security Assistance/Armaments Cooperation Office or Commercial Counselor [@CL-CMP-3505]. Before a contractor makes a proposal to a foreign interest that involves the eventual disclosure of U.S. classified information, the contractor must obtain appropriate government disclosure authorization [@CL-CMP-3474]. Such disclosure authorization does not equate with authorization for export, and export authorization must be obtained from the appropriate regulatory body [@CL-CMP-3475].

For FGI, the contractor will not disclose FGI to nationals of a third country or to any other third party, or use it for any purpose other than that for which it was provided, without the prior written consent of the originating foreign government [@CL-CMP-3476]. The contractor will submit requests for other uses or further disclosure to the GCA for U.S. contracts and through the CSA for direct commercial contracts, and approval of the request by the foreign government does not eliminate the requirement for the contractor to obtain an export authorization [@CL-CMP-3507]. The requirements in paragraph (d) on international transfers of classified material do not apply to the transmission of classified material to USG activities outside the United States [@CL-CMP-3477]. All international transfers of classified material must take place through channels approved by both governments [@CL-CMP-3506]. Written transmission instructions must be prepared for all international transfers of classified material; the contractor is responsible for the preparation of instructions for direct commercial arrangements, and the GCA will prepare instructions for government arrangements [@CL-CMP-3478]. A requirement to prepare a transportation plan (TP) will be included in each arrangement that involves the international transfer of classified material as freight, and the TP will describe requirements for the secure shipment of the material from the point of origin to the ultimate destination [@CL-CMP-3508].

## How it works

The transfer of articles, services and related data to a foreign person, within or outside the United States, or the movement of such material or information to any destination outside of the legal jurisdiction of the United States constitutes an export [@CL-CMP-3470], and the section applies to those exports that involve classified information [@CL-CMP-3504]. Before a contractor makes a proposal to a foreign interest that involves the eventual disclosure of U.S. classified information, the contractor must obtain appropriate government disclosure authorization, which does not equate with authorization for export; export authorization must be obtained from the appropriate regulatory body [@CL-CMP-3474; @CL-CMP-3475]. Contractors will only disclose non-public USG information to foreign persons in accordance with specified requirements of the contract, and in the absence of any specified requirements will not disclose it [@CL-CMP-3471]. The contractor will not disclose FGI to nationals of a third country or to any other third party, or use it for any purpose other than that for which it was provided, without the prior written consent of the originating foreign government [@CL-CMP-3476]. All international transfers of classified material must take place through channels approved by both governments and have written transmission instructions prepared [@CL-CMP-3506; @CL-CMP-3478], and each arrangement that involves the international transfer of classified material as freight includes a requirement to prepare a transportation plan describing requirements for secure shipment from the point of origin to the ultimate destination [@CL-CMP-3508].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor wants to propose a classified subsystem to a foreign interest. It first obtains government disclosure authorization. It separately holds foreign government information, and a firm from a third country asks to see it.

Answer: the proposal needs disclosure authorization first, and a separate export authorization must come from the appropriate regulatory body. The contractor does not show the foreign government information to the third-country firm without the originating government's prior written consent.

## Never confuse

Disclosure authorization does not equate with authorization for export, which must be obtained from the appropriate regulatory body [@CL-CMP-3475]. The requirements in paragraph (d) on international transfers of classified material do not apply to the transmission of classified material to USG activities outside the United States [@CL-CMP-3477]. For foreign national visits and conferences, see [NISPOM international visits and foreign national access](cmp-nispom-international-visits-and-foreign-national-access.md); for marking documents that contain FGI, see [NISPOM marking requirements](cmp-nispom-marking-requirements.md); for the meaning of foreign interest, see [NISPOM acronyms, definitions and cognizant security office information](cmp-nispom-definitions-and-cognizant-security-offices.md); and for releasing classified information to a subcontractor, see [NISPOM subcontracting and information system security](cmp-nispom-subcontracting-and-information-system-security.md).
