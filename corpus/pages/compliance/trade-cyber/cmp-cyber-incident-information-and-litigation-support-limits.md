## Purpose

Shows what a contractor may and may not do with information obtained from a third party's cyber incident report and with litigation information.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-CMP-2849; @CL-CMP-2853] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 252.204-7009(b) [@CL-CMP-2849]; DFARS 252.204-7014(b)(1) [@CL-CMP-2853] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-2849; @CL-CMP-2853] |
| Applies to | Information obtained from a third party's reporting of a cyber incident under the safeguarding clause [@CL-CMP-2849]; litigation information under the litigation support clause [@CL-CMP-2853] |

The cyber incident restrictions apply to any information the contractor receives or creates in the performance of the contract that is obtained from a third party's reporting of a cyber incident under the safeguarding clause [@CL-CMP-2849]. The restrictions extend to information derived from the information obtained under the safeguarding clause [@CL-CMP-2910]. The contractor shall access and use the information only for the purpose of furnishing advice or technical assistance directly to the Government in support of the Government's activities related to the safeguarding clause, and not for any other purpose [@CL-CMP-2849; @CL-CMP-2850]. The contractor shall also protect the information against unauthorized release or disclosure and shall ensure its employees are subject to use and non-disclosure obligations consistent with the clause before they are given access [@CL-CMP-2849; @CL-CMP-2851].

For litigation support, notwithstanding any other provision of the contract, the contractor shall access and use litigation information only for the purpose of providing litigation support under the contract [@CL-CMP-2853]. The contractor shall not disclose litigation information to any entity outside its organization unless, before the disclosure, the contracting officer has provided written consent [@CL-CMP-2853; @CL-CMP-2854]. The contractor shall also not use litigation information to compete against a third party for Government or nongovernment contracts, and it shall destroy or return the information at the request of the contracting officer upon completion of the authorized litigation support activities [@CL-CMP-2853; @CL-CMP-2855].

## How it works

The cyber incident clause is flowed down: the contractor shall include it in subcontracts or similar contractual instruments for services that include support for the Government's activities related to safeguarding covered defense information and cyber incident reporting [@CL-CMP-2852]. That flowdown includes subcontracts for commercial products and commercial services and is made without alteration, except to identify the parties [@CL-CMP-2911]. The litigation support clause is flowed down too: the contractor shall include its substance, including its subcontracts paragraph, in all subcontracts, including subcontracts for commercial products or commercial services [@CL-CMP-2912]. The two clauses are separate and apply to different information. This card does not set out the litigation support clause's remedies or its indemnification terms.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A support contractor receives details from another contractor's incident report while advising the Government. It uses them only for that advice, and when a staff member asks to share them with a commercial client, the contractor refuses. In a separate litigation support job, it returns all litigation information when the contracting officer asks after the work ends.

## Never confuse

Cyber incident information is information obtained from a third party's reporting of a cyber incident under the safeguarding clause [@CL-CMP-2849]. Litigation information is any information, including sensitive information, that is furnished to the contractor by or on behalf of the Government, or that is generated or obtained by the contractor in the performance of litigation support under a contract [@CL-CMP-2913], and it does not include information that is lawfully, publicly available without restriction [@CL-CMP-2923]. Each clause allows its own purposes. The duty to report an incident, which creates the first kind of information, is on the [Safeguarding covered defense information and cyber incident reporting](cmp-safeguarding-covered-defense-information.md) card.
