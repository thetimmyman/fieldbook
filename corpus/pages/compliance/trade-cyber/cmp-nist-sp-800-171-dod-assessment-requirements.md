## Purpose

Shows which assessment an offeror must have to be considered for award, how assessment scores are posted, and what the contractor must require of subcontractors.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-CMP-2825] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 252.204-7019(b) [@CL-CMP-2825]; DFARS 252.204-7020(c) [@CL-CMP-2830] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-2825] |
| Applies to | Offerors required to implement NIST SP 800-171, for each covered contractor information system relevant to the offer [@CL-CMP-2825; @CL-CMP-2826]; the assessment clause applies to covered contractor information systems required to comply with NIST SP 800-171 under the safeguarding clause [@CL-CMP-2903] |

To be considered for award, an offeror that is required to implement NIST SP 800-171 must have a current assessment, which means one not more than 3 years old unless the solicitation specifies a lesser time [@CL-CMP-2825]. Where the provision requires a current assessment, it must be for each covered contractor information system that is relevant to the offer, contract, task order, or delivery order [@CL-CMP-2826].

The offeror must verify that summary level scores of a current assessment are posted in the Supplier Performance Risk System [@CL-CMP-2827]. An offeror without summary level scores of a current assessment posted in SPRS may conduct and submit a Basic Assessment [@CL-CMP-2828]. Summary level scores for all assessments will be posted in SPRS 30 days post-assessment to give DoD Components visibility into the summary level scores of strategic assessments [@CL-CMP-2829].

## How it works

The notice provision is the offer-stage test, and the assessment clause carries the duties into performance. The contractor must provide access to its facilities, systems, and personnel necessary for the Government to conduct a Medium or High assessment [@CL-CMP-2830]. The sentence that requires this access closes with the qualifier that the access is to be provided if necessary [@CL-CMP-2904]. Upon completion of each assessment, the contractor has 14 business days to provide additional information showing that it meets security requirements the assessment team did not observe, or to rebut the findings [@CL-CMP-2831].

The contractor shall insert the substance of the assessment clause, including its subcontracts paragraph, in all subcontracts and other contractual instruments, including subcontracts for commercial products or commercial services, excluding commercially available off-the-shelf items [@CL-CMP-2906]. The contractor shall not award a subcontract that is subject to the NIST SP 800-171 security requirements under the safeguarding clause unless the subcontractor has completed, within the last 3 years, at least a Basic assessment [@CL-CMP-2832]. That assessment is for all covered contractor information systems relevant to the subcontractor's offer that are not part of an information technology service or system operated on behalf of the Government [@CL-CMP-2905]. A subcontractor without summary level scores of a current assessment posted in SPRS may conduct and submit a Basic Assessment [@CL-CMP-2833].

The clause defines three kinds of assessment. A Basic Assessment is a contractor's self-assessment of its implementation of NIST SP 800-171 that is based on the contractor's review of its system security plans associated with covered contractor information systems [@CL-CMP-2902]. A Medium Assessment is an assessment conducted by the Government that consists of a review of a contractor's Basic Assessment, a thorough document review, and discussions with the contractor to obtain additional information or clarification, as needed [@CL-CMP-2900]. A High Assessment is an assessment conducted by Government personnel using NIST SP 800-171A that includes a review of a contractor's Basic Assessment and a thorough document review, among other listed elements [@CL-CMP-2901].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An offeror completed its Basic Assessment 2 years and 9 months before the offer date, and the summary level score is posted in SPRS. The solicitation specifies no shorter period, so the assessment is current. A proposed subcontractor whose only assessment is 4 years old completes a new Basic Assessment before the prime awards the subcontract, and the prime checks the date.

## Never confuse

The notice provision tests the offeror's own assessment at the offer stage, while the subcontract rule tests the subcontractor's assessment before the prime awards. A Basic Assessment is the contractor's own self-assessment [@CL-CMP-2902], while Medium and High assessments are conducted by the Government [@CL-CMP-2900; @CL-CMP-2901]. The security requirements themselves are on the [Safeguarding covered defense information and cyber incident reporting](cmp-safeguarding-covered-defense-information.md) card. The CMMC status duties are on the [Contractor compliance with CMMC level requirements](cmp-cmmc-level-compliance-clause.md) card, and the use of SPRS risk assessments in evaluating offers is on the [Safeguarding controls representation and SPRS risk assessments](cmp-safeguarding-controls-representation-and-sprs-notice.md) card.
