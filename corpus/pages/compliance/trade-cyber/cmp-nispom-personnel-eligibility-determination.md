## Purpose

Shows whose eligibility the contractor may ask the CSA to determine, how many requests it may make, when temporary eligibility is available, and what the contractor must do when eligibility is lost.

| Applies to | Contractor employees who may need access to classified information [@CL-CMP-3174] |

This card rests on the NISPOM, the rule for work involving classified information, and not on the FAR. The CSA is responsible for determining an employee's eligibility for access to classified information, and the contractor must determine that access is essential in the performance of tasks or services related to the fulfillment of a classified contract [@CL-CMP-3174]. A contractor may give an employee access to classified information at the same or lower level of classification as the level of the contractor's entity eligibility determination if the employee has the listed conditions, the first of which is a valid need-to-know for the classified information [@CL-CMP-3175]. The listed conditions that follow need-to-know are a favorable USG eligibility determination for access to classified information at the appropriate level and a signed non-disclosure agreement [@CL-CMP-3177].

The contractor will limit requests for determinations of eligibility for access to classified information to the minimum number of employees and consultants necessary for operational efficiency in accordance with contractual obligations and other requirements of the rule [@CL-CMP-3176]. Requests for determinations of eligibility for access to classified information will not be used to establish a cache of cleared employees [@CL-CMP-3310]. Contractors will not submit requests for determination of eligibility for access to classified information for individuals who are not their employees or consultants, nor for employees of subcontractors [@CL-CMP-3178].

The CSA may grant temporary eligibility, previously called interim eligibility, for access to classified information, as appropriate, to applicants for access to TOP SECRET, SECRET, and CONFIDENTIAL information [@CL-CMP-3179]. The paragraph on temporary eligibility says that eligibility may only be granted if there is no evidence of adverse information that calls into question an individual's eligibility for access to classified information [@CL-CMP-3311]. A temporary eligibility determination shall not exceed one year unless approved by the applicable CSA in the system of record [@CL-CMP-3180]. A temporary SECRET or CONFIDENTIAL eligibility determination is valid for access to classified information at the level of the eligibility granted, and access to RD, COMSEC information, and NATO information requires a final SECRET eligibility determination [@CL-CMP-3312].

Eligibility can also be lost. The CSA will notify the contractor when an employee's eligibility has been denied, suspended, or revoked [@CL-CMP-3909], and the contractor will immediately deny access to classified information to that employee when notified, regardless of the employee's location [@CL-CMP-3910]. The CSA may withdraw temporary eligibility when derogatory information is subsequently developed [@CL-CMP-3911]. When temporary eligibility is withdrawn for an individual who must be eligible in connection with the entity eligibility determination, the contractor must remove the individual from access to classified information and any KMP position requiring PCL eligibility, or the temporary entity eligibility determination will also be withdrawn [@CL-CMP-3912; @CL-CMP-3913].

## How it works

The contractor must determine that access is essential to a classified contract, and the CSA is responsible for determining the employee's eligibility [@CL-CMP-3174]. The contractor keeps its requests to the minimum number needed, does not use them to build a cache of cleared employees, and files them only for its own employees and consultants [@CL-CMP-3176; @CL-CMP-3310; @CL-CMP-3178]. A contractor may give access at the same or a lower level than the entity's own determination if the employee has a need-to-know, a favorable determination and a signed non-disclosure agreement [@CL-CMP-3175; @CL-CMP-3177]. Temporary eligibility is available for applicants when there is no evidence of adverse information, and it is capped at one year unless the CSA approves more [@CL-CMP-3179; @CL-CMP-3311; @CL-CMP-3180]. A temporary SECRET or CONFIDENTIAL determination is valid at the level granted [@CL-CMP-3312]. When the CSA notifies a denial, suspension or revocation, the contractor denies access at once, wherever the employee is [@CL-CMP-3909; @CL-CMP-3910], and a withdrawn temporary eligibility for an individual tied to the entity eligibility determination means removal from access and from any KMP position requiring PCL eligibility [@CL-CMP-3912; @CL-CMP-3913]. The briefings an employee receives before access are on the [NISPOM security briefings, visits and classified meetings](cmp-nispom-training-briefings-visits-and-meetings.md) card.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor with a 3,000,000 classified contract needs ten employees on the work and asks for fifty more clearance requests to have spares. It also wants to file requests for two supplier employees.

Answer: it limits requests to the minimum number needed for the contract, it does not use requests to build a cache of cleared employees, and it does not file requests for subcontractor employees.

## Never confuse

Personnel eligibility belongs to an individual employee and the CSA decides it [@CL-CMP-3174]. The company's own entity eligibility determination is a different determination, covered on the [NISPOM entity eligibility determination (facility clearance)](cmp-nispom-entity-eligibility-determination.md) card.
