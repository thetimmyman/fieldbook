## Purpose

Shows whose eligibility the contractor may ask the CSA to determine, how many requests it may make, when a request may be made before employment starts, when temporary eligibility is available and to whom it is not, and what the contractor must do when eligibility is lost.

| Applies to | Contractor employees who may need access to classified information [@CL-CMP-3174] |

This card rests on the NISPOM, the rule for work involving classified information, and not on the FAR. The CSA is responsible for determining an employee's eligibility for access to classified information, and the contractor must determine that access is essential in the performance of tasks or services related to the fulfillment of a classified contract [@CL-CMP-3174]. Access must also be clearly consistent with United States national security interests as determined by the CSA [@CL-CMP-4303]. A contractor may give an employee access to classified information at the same or lower level of classification as the level of the contractor's entity eligibility determination if the employee has the listed conditions, the first of which is a valid need-to-know for the classified information [@CL-CMP-3175]. The listed conditions that follow need-to-know are a favorable USG eligibility determination for access to classified information at the appropriate level and a signed non-disclosure agreement [@CL-CMP-3177].

The contractor will limit requests for determinations of eligibility for access to classified information to the minimum number of employees and consultants necessary for operational efficiency in accordance with contractual obligations and other requirements of the rule [@CL-CMP-3176]. Requests for determinations of eligibility for access to classified information will not be used to establish a cache of cleared employees [@CL-CMP-3310]. Contractors will not submit requests for determination of eligibility for access to classified information for individuals who are not their employees or consultants, nor for employees of subcontractors [@CL-CMP-3178]. The contractor will also not submit a request to one CSA if the employee applicant is known to be cleared or in process for eligibility for access to classified information by another CSA [@CL-CMP-4305].

The rule on employees and consultants has one printed route for a candidate who has not yet started work. If a potential employee requires access to classified information immediately upon commencement of employment, the contractor may submit a request for investigation prior to the date of employment, provided a written commitment for employment has been made by the contractor [@CL-CMP-4300] and the candidate has accepted the offer in writing [@CL-CMP-4313]. The commitment for employment must indicate employment will commence within 45 days of the employee being granted eligibility for access to classified information at a level that allows the employee to perform the tasks or services associated with the contract or USG requirement for which the employee was hired [@CL-CMP-4301]. The contractor will also comply with the requirements of paragraph (a)(5) of the section, which is the limit on the number of requests, in a pre-employment request [@CL-CMP-4302; @CL-CMP-3176].

The CSA may grant temporary eligibility, previously called interim eligibility, for access to classified information, as appropriate, to applicants for access to TOP SECRET, SECRET, and CONFIDENTIAL information [@CL-CMP-3179]. Non-United States citizens are not eligible for access to classified information on a temporary basis [@CL-CMP-4299]. The paragraph on temporary eligibility says that eligibility may only be granted if there is no evidence of adverse information that calls into question an individual's eligibility for access to classified information [@CL-CMP-3311]. A temporary eligibility determination shall not exceed one year unless approved by the applicable CSA in the system of record [@CL-CMP-3180]. A temporary SECRET or CONFIDENTIAL eligibility determination is valid for access to classified information at the level of the eligibility granted, and access to RD, COMSEC information, and NATO information requires a final SECRET eligibility determination [@CL-CMP-3312]. A temporary TOP SECRET eligibility determination is valid for access to TOP SECRET information [@CL-CMP-4307]. If an individual has a temporary TOP SECRET eligibility determination and has a final SECRET eligibility determination based on a previously completed investigation, that temporary determination is valid for access to RD, NATO, and COMSEC information at the SECRET or CONFIDENTIAL level [@CL-CMP-4306; @CL-CMP-4308].

Eligibility can also be lost. The CSA will notify the contractor when an employee's eligibility has been denied, suspended, or revoked [@CL-CMP-3909], and the contractor will immediately deny access to classified information to that employee when notified, regardless of the employee's location [@CL-CMP-3910]. If the employee's performance is at a USG facility, the contractor will also notify the appropriate GCA of the denial, revocation, or suspension [@CL-CMP-4304]. The CSA may withdraw temporary eligibility when derogatory information is subsequently developed [@CL-CMP-3911]. When temporary eligibility is withdrawn for an individual who must be eligible in connection with the entity eligibility determination, the contractor must remove the individual from access to classified information and any KMP position requiring PCL eligibility, or the temporary entity eligibility determination will also be withdrawn [@CL-CMP-3912; @CL-CMP-3913]. Withdrawal of a temporary eligibility determination is not a denial, termination, or revocation of eligibility under the rule and may not be appealed [@CL-CMP-4309].

## How it works

The contractor must determine that access is essential to a classified contract, and the CSA is responsible for determining the employee's eligibility [@CL-CMP-3174]. Access must be clearly consistent with United States national security interests as the CSA determines [@CL-CMP-4303]. The contractor keeps its requests to the minimum number needed, does not use them to build a cache of cleared employees, files them for its own employees and consultants and not for subcontractor employees, and does not file with one CSA for someone known to be cleared or in process with another [@CL-CMP-3176; @CL-CMP-3310; @CL-CMP-3178; @CL-CMP-4305]. The pre-employment request is the printed exception for a candidate who is not yet an employee, and it needs a written commitment for employment from the contractor, the candidate's written acceptance and employment that begins within the stated period after eligibility is granted [@CL-CMP-4300; @CL-CMP-4313; @CL-CMP-4301]. A contractor may give access at the same or a lower level than the entity's own determination if the employee has a need-to-know, a favorable determination and a signed non-disclosure agreement [@CL-CMP-3175; @CL-CMP-3177]. Temporary eligibility may be granted to applicants only if there is no evidence of adverse information, it may not exceed one year unless the applicable CSA approves more in the system of record, and it is not available to non-United States citizens [@CL-CMP-3179; @CL-CMP-3311; @CL-CMP-3180; @CL-CMP-4299]. A temporary SECRET or CONFIDENTIAL determination is valid at the level granted, and access to RD, COMSEC and NATO information requires a final SECRET determination [@CL-CMP-3312]. A temporary TOP SECRET determination is valid for TOP SECRET information and, for a holder who also has a final SECRET determination from an earlier investigation, for RD, NATO and COMSEC information at the SECRET or CONFIDENTIAL level [@CL-CMP-4307; @CL-CMP-4306; @CL-CMP-4308]. When the CSA notifies a denial, suspension or revocation, the contractor denies access at once, wherever the employee is, and tells the GCA if the employee works at a USG facility [@CL-CMP-3909; @CL-CMP-3910; @CL-CMP-4304], and a withdrawn temporary eligibility for an individual tied to the entity eligibility determination means removal from access and from any KMP position requiring PCL eligibility, with no appeal from the withdrawal itself [@CL-CMP-3912; @CL-CMP-3913; @CL-CMP-4309]. The briefings an employee receives before access are on the [NISPOM security briefings, visits and classified meetings](cmp-nispom-training-briefings-visits-and-meetings.md) card.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor with a 3,000,000 classified contract needs ten employees on the work and asks for fifty more clearance requests to have spares. It also wants to file requests for two supplier employees.

Answer: it limits requests to the minimum number needed for the contract, it does not use requests to build a cache of cleared employees, and it does not file requests for subcontractor employees.

Second example: one of the contractor's own employees is not a citizen and asks for temporary eligibility so work can start sooner. Answer: temporary eligibility is not available to non-United States citizens.

## Never confuse

Personnel eligibility belongs to an individual employee and the CSA decides it [@CL-CMP-3174]. The company's own entity eligibility determination is a different determination, covered on the [NISPOM entity eligibility determination (facility clearance)](cmp-nispom-entity-eligibility-determination.md) card.

A separate section covers the DHS CCIPP. In it, DHS will coordinate with other US Government agencies that have an equity with a private sector entity and the CCIPP in accordance with § 117.6(f) [@CL-CMP-10027]. The Secretary of Homeland Security has the authority to determine the eligibility for personnel security clearances and to administer the sharing of relevant classified NSI with certain private sectors or non-federal partners for the purpose of furthering cybersecurity information sharing among critical infrastructure partners pursuant to Executive Order 13691 [@CL-CMP-10028; @CL-CMP-10029]. DHS provides security oversight and assumes security responsibilities similar to those of an FSO, unless otherwise provided in the section, and participating entities will cooperate with DHS security officials to ensure the entity is in compliance with the requirements in the rule [@CL-CMP-10030].
