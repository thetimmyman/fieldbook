## Purpose

Names the security roles a cleared contractor appoints, what each must do, and the insider threat program and self-inspection duties.

## The rule

| Applies to | Contractors that appoint the security officials the rule lists [@CL-CMP-3157] |

Contractors will appoint security officials who are U.S. citizens, except in exceptional circumstances [@CL-CMP-3157]. Appointed security officials listed in paragraphs (b)(2), (b)(3), and (b)(4) of the section must oversee the implementation of the requirements of the rule [@CL-CMP-3158]. The SMO will ensure the contractor maintains a system of security controls in accordance with the requirements of the rule and will appoint a contractor employee or employees, in writing, as the FSO and appoint the same employee or a different employee as the ITPSO [@CL-CMP-3159]. The SMO may appoint a single employee for both roles, or may appoint one employee as the FSO and a different employee as the ITPSO [@CL-CMP-3301]. One listed duty of the SMO is to retain accountability for the management and operations of the facility without delegating that accountability to a subordinate manager [@CL-CMP-3160]. The FSO will supervise and direct security measures necessary for implementing the applicable requirements of the rule and the related USG security requirements to ensure the protection of classified information [@CL-CMP-3161]. The ITPSO will establish and execute an insider threat program [@CL-CMP-3162]. Contractors who are, or will be, processing classified information on an information system located at the contractor facility will appoint an employee to serve as the ISSM [@CL-CMP-3300].

Pursuant to the rule and CSA provided guidance to supplement unique CSA mission requirements, the contractor will establish and maintain an insider threat program to gather, integrate, and report relevant and available information indicative of a potential or actual insider threat, consistent with E.O. 13587 and Presidential Memorandum "National Insider Threat Policy and Minimum Standards for Executive Branch Insider Threat Programs" [@CL-CMP-3163; @CL-CMP-0428]. Contractors will review their security programs on a continuing basis and conduct a formal self-inspection at least annually and at intervals consistent with risk management principles [@CL-CMP-3164]. The contractor will prepare a formal report describing the self-inspection, its findings, and its resolution of issues discovered during the self-inspection, and will retain the formal report for CSA review until after the next CSA security review is completed [@CL-CMP-3305]. The SMO at the cleared facility will annually certify to the CSA, in writing, that a self-inspection has been conducted, that other KMP have been briefed on the results of the self-inspection, that appropriate corrective actions have been taken, and that management fully supports the security program at the cleared facility in the manner as described in the certification [@CL-CMP-3306; @CL-CMP-0429]. Depending upon the size and complexity of the contractor's security operations, a single contractor employee may serve in more than one position [@CL-CMP-3165].

## How it works

The SMO ensures the contractor maintains a system of security controls in accordance with the requirements of the rule and appoints in writing the FSO and the ITPSO [@CL-CMP-3159]. The SMO retains accountability for the management and operations of the facility without delegating that accountability to a subordinate manager [@CL-CMP-3160]. The FSO supervises and directs security measures necessary for implementing the applicable requirements of the rule and related USG security requirements to ensure the protection of classified information, and the ITPSO establishes and executes the insider threat program that the contractor establishes and maintains [@CL-CMP-3161; @CL-CMP-3162; @CL-CMP-3163; @CL-CMP-0428]. Depending upon the size and complexity of the contractor's security operations, a single contractor employee may serve in more than one position [@CL-CMP-3165]. Contractors who are, or will be, processing classified information on an information system located at the contractor facility will appoint an employee to serve as the ISSM [@CL-CMP-3300], and the [NISPOM subcontracting and information system security](cmp-nispom-subcontracting-and-information-system-security.md) card also states that contractors that are or will be processing classified information on an information system will appoint an employee ISSM [@CL-CMP-3322]. Appointed security officials listed in paragraphs (b)(2), (b)(3), and (b)(4) of the section oversee the implementation of the requirements of the rule, and the contractor conducts a formal self-inspection at least annually and at intervals consistent with risk management principles, prepares and retains a formal report for CSA review until after the next CSA security review is completed, and has the SMO annually certify the four self-inspection items to the CSA in writing [@CL-CMP-3158; @CL-CMP-3164; @CL-CMP-3305; @CL-CMP-3306; @CL-CMP-0429]. For security briefings, visits and classified meetings, see [NISPOM security briefings, visits and classified meetings](cmp-nispom-training-briefings-visits-and-meetings.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A small cleared contractor has one security employee. Its owner, acting as SMO, names that employee as both FSO and ITPSO in writing.

Answer: the SMO may appoint one employee to both roles, and the contractor still owes a formal self-inspection each year, with a formal report on it and the SMO's annual written certification to the CSA.

## Never confuse

Pursuant to the rule and CSA provided guidance to supplement unique CSA mission requirements, the insider threat program is established and maintained to gather, integrate, and report relevant and available information indicative of a potential or actual insider threat, consistent with E.O. 13587 and the Presidential Memorandum on National Insider Threat Policy [@CL-CMP-3163; @CL-CMP-0428]. For reporting ownership and control events to the CSA, see [NISPOM changed conditions and reportable ownership events](../../mna/diligence/mna-nispom-changed-condition-reporting.md). For authorizing officials at the CSA, see [NISPOM purpose, applicability and responsibilities](cmp-nispom-purpose-applicability-and-responsibilities.md).
