## Purpose

Names the security roles a cleared contractor appoints, what each must do, and the insider threat program and self-inspection duties.

This card rests on the NISPOM, the rule for work involving classified information, and not on the FAR. Contractors will appoint security officials who are US citizens, except in exceptional circumstances [@CL-CMP-3157]. The appointed security officials listed in the paragraphs that follow must oversee the implementation of the requirements of the rule [@CL-CMP-3158]. The SMO will ensure the contractor maintains a system of security controls in accordance with the rule and will appoint, in writing, a contractor employee or employees as the FSO and the same or a different employee as the ITPSO [@CL-CMP-3159]. The SMO may appoint a single employee for both the FSO and ITPSO roles, or may appoint one employee as the FSO and a different employee as the ITPSO [@CL-CMP-3301]. One listed duty of the SMO is to retain accountability for the management and operations of the facility without delegating that accountability to a subordinate manager [@CL-CMP-3160]. The FSO will supervise and direct the security measures necessary for implementing the applicable requirements of the rule and the related USG security requirements to ensure the protection of classified information [@CL-CMP-3161]. The ITPSO will establish and execute an insider threat program [@CL-CMP-3162]. Contractors who are, or will be, processing classified information on an information system located at the contractor facility will appoint an employee to serve as the ISSM [@CL-CMP-3300].

Under the rule and CSA provided guidance, the contractor will establish and maintain an insider threat program to gather, integrate, and report relevant and available information indicative of a potential or actual insider threat [@CL-CMP-3163]. Contractors will review their security programs on a continuing basis and conduct a formal self-inspection at least annually and at intervals consistent with risk management principles [@CL-CMP-3164]. The contractor will prepare a formal report describing the self-inspection, its findings, and its resolution of issues discovered during the self-inspection, and will retain the formal report for CSA review until after the next CSA security review is completed [@CL-CMP-3305]. The SMO at the cleared facility will annually certify to the CSA, in writing, among other things, that a self-inspection has been conducted, that other KMP have been briefed on the results of the self-inspection, and that appropriate corrective actions have been taken [@CL-CMP-3306]. Depending upon the size and complexity of the contractor's security operations, a single contractor employee may serve in more than one position [@CL-CMP-3165].

## How it works

The SMO, the senior management official, ensures the contractor maintains a system of security controls and appoints the FSO, the facility security officer, and the ITPSO [@CL-CMP-3159]. The SMO stays accountable for the facility and does not hand that accountability to a subordinate manager [@CL-CMP-3160]. The FSO supervises and directs the security measures, and the ITPSO executes the insider threat program that the contractor must maintain [@CL-CMP-3161; @CL-CMP-3162; @CL-CMP-3163]. A small contractor can combine roles, because one employee may hold more than one position depending on size and complexity [@CL-CMP-3165]. A contractor that is, or will be, processing classified information on an information system located at the contractor facility also appoints an ISSM [@CL-CMP-3300], and the [NISPOM subcontracting and information system security](cmp-nispom-subcontracting-and-information-system-security.md) card repeats that appointment among its information system duties [@CL-CMP-3322]. The appointed security officials listed in the rule oversee implementation of its requirements, and the contractor checks its own program through a formal self-inspection at least annually, reports on it, and has the SMO certify it to the CSA [@CL-CMP-3158; @CL-CMP-3164; @CL-CMP-3305; @CL-CMP-3306]. The training these officials and the other cleared employees receive is on the [NISPOM security briefings, visits and classified meetings](cmp-nispom-training-briefings-visits-and-meetings.md) card.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A small cleared contractor has one security employee. Its owner, acting as SMO, names that employee as both FSO and ITPSO in writing.

Answer: the SMO may appoint one employee to both roles, and the contractor still owes a formal self-inspection each year, with a formal report on it and the SMO's annual written certification to the CSA.

## Never confuse

The insider threat program is a standing program to gather and report information about insider threats [@CL-CMP-3163]; it is not the duty to report ownership and control events to the CSA, which is on the [NISPOM changed conditions and reportable ownership events](../../mna/diligence/mna-nispom-changed-condition-reporting.md) card. The role of authorizing officials at the CSA, who authorize systems, is on the [NISPOM purpose, applicability and responsibilities](cmp-nispom-purpose-applicability-and-responsibilities.md) card.
