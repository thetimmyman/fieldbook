## Purpose

Shows what the international security text says about foreign nationals visiting a contractor, attending a classified gathering or working for the contractor, and about contractor employees assigned abroad.

| Applies to | Contractors hosting visits by foreign nationals [@CL-CMP-3481] |

Disclosure and export of classified information, authorized by an appropriate USG disclosure official, by a contractor will ensure the listed items [@CL-CMP-3479]. One listed item for symposia, seminars, exhibitions and conferences is that contractors must assure that any foreign nationals who will be attending a classified gathering have the appropriate export license, disclosure authority and security assurance on file [@CL-CMP-3480]. One listed item for visits by foreign nationals to the contractor is that the contractor will limit disclosure of classified information to that specific information authorized in connection with an approved visit request and an export authorization, as required [@CL-CMP-3481].

Contractors cannot use visit authorizations to employ or otherwise acquire the services of foreign nationals that require access to export-controlled information, and an export authorization is required for such situations [@CL-CMP-3509]. Classified visits by foreign nationals must be processed by government national security authorities on behalf of the contractor through the sponsoring foreign government (normally the visitor's embassy) to the USG for approval [@CL-CMP-3510]. The contractor will establish procedures to ensure that foreign visitors are not afforded access to classified information except as authorized by an export license, approved visit request, or other exemption to the licensing requirements [@CL-CMP-3511]. The contractor will maintain a record of foreign visitors for one year when the visit involves access to classified information [@CL-CMP-3512].

The assignment of an employee who is a non-U.S. citizen outside the United States on programs that will involve access to classified information is prohibited [@CL-CMP-3513]. Because the USG is responsible for the storage, custody and control of classified information required by a U.S. contractor employee abroad, the storage of classified information by contractor employees at any location abroad that is not under USG control is prohibited [@CL-CMP-3514].

## How it works

For visits by foreign nationals to the contractor, the contractor will limit disclosure of classified information to that specific information authorized in connection with an approved visit request and an export authorization, as required [@CL-CMP-3481]. For symposia, seminars, exhibitions and conferences, contractors must assure that any foreign nationals who will be attending a classified gathering have the appropriate export license, disclosure authority and security assurance on file [@CL-CMP-3480]. Both items are ensured in the disclosure and export of classified information, authorized by an appropriate USG disclosure official, by a contractor [@CL-CMP-3479]. Classified visits by foreign nationals must be processed by government national security authorities on behalf of the contractor through the sponsoring foreign government (normally the visitor's embassy) to the USG for approval [@CL-CMP-3510], and the contractor will maintain a record of foreign visitors for one year when the visit involves access to classified information [@CL-CMP-3512]. Contractors cannot use visit authorizations to employ or otherwise acquire the services of foreign nationals that require access to export-controlled information, and an export authorization is required for such situations [@CL-CMP-3509].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor hosts a classified review that includes a visitor from a partner nation. The approved visit request covers one subsystem only.

Answer: the contractor limits disclosure to the information the approved request and the export authorization cover, keeps a record of the visitor for one year, and for any wider gathering it needs the attendee's export license, disclosure authority and security assurance on file first.

## Never confuse

Contractors cannot use visit authorizations to employ or otherwise acquire the services of foreign nationals that require access to export-controlled information; an export authorization is required for such situations [@CL-CMP-3509]. For visits by cleared employees, see [NISPOM security briefings, visits and classified meetings](cmp-nispom-training-briefings-visits-and-meetings.md); for disclosure and export authorization, see [NISPOM international transfers and foreign government information](cmp-nispom-international-transfers-and-foreign-gov-info.md); and for the meaning of foreign interest, see [NISPOM acronyms, definitions and cognizant security office information](cmp-nispom-definitions-and-cognizant-security-offices.md).
