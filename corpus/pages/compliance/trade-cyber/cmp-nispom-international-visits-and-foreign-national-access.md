## Purpose

Shows what the international security text says about foreign nationals visiting a contractor, attending a classified gathering or working for the contractor, and about contractor employees assigned abroad.

| Applies to | Contractors hosting visits by foreign nationals [@CL-CMP-3481] |

This card rests on the NISPOM, the rule for work involving classified information, and not on the FAR. The listed items apply to disclosure and export of classified information by a contractor that an appropriate USG disclosure official has authorized [@CL-CMP-3479]. One listed item for symposia, seminars, exhibitions and conferences is that contractors must assure that any foreign nationals who will be attending a classified gathering have the appropriate export license, disclosure authority and security assurance on file [@CL-CMP-3480]. One listed item for visits by foreign nationals to the contractor is that the contractor will limit disclosure of classified information to that specific information authorized in connection with an approved visit request and an export authorization, as required [@CL-CMP-3481].

A contractor cannot use visit authorizations to employ or otherwise acquire the services of foreign nationals that require access to export-controlled information, and an export authorization is required for such situations [@CL-CMP-3509]. Classified visits by foreign nationals must be processed by government national security authorities on behalf of the contractor through the sponsoring foreign government, normally the visitor's embassy, to the USG for approval [@CL-CMP-3510]. The contractor will establish procedures to ensure that foreign visitors are not afforded access to classified information except as authorized by an export license, approved visit request, or other exemption to the licensing requirements [@CL-CMP-3511]. The contractor will maintain a record of foreign visitors for one year when the visit involves access to classified information [@CL-CMP-3512].

For contractor operations abroad, the assignment of an employee who is a non-U.S. citizen outside the United States on programs that will involve access to classified information is prohibited [@CL-CMP-3513]. Because the USG is responsible for the storage, custody and control of classified information required by a U.S. contractor employee abroad, the storage of classified information by contractor employees at any location abroad that is not under USG control is prohibited [@CL-CMP-3514]. This card does not restate the other visit and foreign operations procedures in that section.

## How it works

Disclosure of classified information to a foreign visitor is limited to the specific information authorized in connection with an approved visit request and an export authorization, as required [@CL-CMP-3481]. A foreign national attending a classified gathering needs an export license, disclosure authority and a security assurance on file [@CL-CMP-3480]. Both items sit under a listing that applies when a USG disclosure official has authorized the disclosure or export [@CL-CMP-3479]. A classified visit by a foreign national is processed by government national security authorities on behalf of the contractor through the sponsoring foreign government to the USG for approval [@CL-CMP-3510], and the contractor keeps a record of such visitors for one year when the visit involves access to classified information [@CL-CMP-3512]. A visit authorization cannot be used to employ or otherwise acquire the services of a foreign national who requires access to export-controlled information, and an export authorization is required for that situation [@CL-CMP-3509].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor hosts a classified review that includes a visitor from a partner nation. The approved visit request covers one subsystem only.

Answer: the contractor limits disclosure to the information the approved request and the export authorization cover, keeps a record of the visitor for one year, and for any wider gathering it needs the attendee's export license, disclosure authority and security assurance on file first.

## Never confuse

A visit by a foreign national is not the same as employing a foreign national, because contractors cannot use visit authorizations to employ or otherwise acquire the services of foreign nationals that require access to export-controlled information [@CL-CMP-3509]. Visits by cleared employees are on the [NISPOM security briefings, visits and classified meetings](cmp-nispom-training-briefings-visits-and-meetings.md) card. The rules on disclosure and export authorization are on the [NISPOM international transfers and foreign government information](cmp-nispom-international-transfers-and-foreign-government-information.md) card, and the meaning of foreign interest is on the [NISPOM acronyms, definitions and cognizant security office information](cmp-nispom-definitions-and-cognizant-security-offices.md) card.
