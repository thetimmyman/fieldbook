## Purpose

Shows what the Government may do to manage supply chain risk, what the contractor must do about it, and what the telecommunications security equipment clause requires.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-CMP-4000; @CL-CMP-4003] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 252.239-7018 [@CL-CMP-4000]; DFARS 252.239-7016 [@CL-CMP-4003] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | DFARS 252.239-7018: DEC 2022 [@CL-CMP-10313]; DFARS 252.239-7016: DEC 1991 [@CL-CMP-10314] |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-4000; @CL-CMP-4003] |
| Applies to | Contracts with the supply chain risk clause [@CL-CMP-4000]; contracts where the solicitation or contract identifies classified or sensitive information that requires securing during telecommunications [@CL-CMP-4003] |

The contractor shall mitigate supply chain risk in the provision of supplies and services to the Government [@CL-CMP-4000]. To manage supply chain risk, the Government may use the authorities provided by section 3252 of title 10 of the United States Code, and in doing so may consider public and non-public information, including all-source intelligence, relating to the contractor's supply chain [@CL-CMP-4001]. If the Government exercises that authority to limit disclosure of information, no action it undertakes under that authority is subject to review in a bid protest before the Government Accountability Office or in any Federal court [@CL-CMP-4002].

The telecommunications security clause records that the solicitation or contract identifies classified or sensitive information that requires securing during telecommunications, and requires the contractor to secure telecommunications systems [@CL-CMP-4003]. To provide the security, the contractor shall use Government-approved telecommunications equipment, devices, techniques, or services [@CL-CMP-4004]. The equipment, devices, techniques, or services used by the contractor must be compatible or interoperable with the location and any telecommunications security equipment, device, technique, or service currently being used by the technical or requirements organization or other offices with which the contractor must communicate, as listed and identified in the clause [@CL-CMP-4048; @CL-CMP-7364]. Except as may be provided elsewhere in the contract, the contractor shall furnish all telecommunications security equipment, devices, techniques, or services necessary to perform the contract [@CL-CMP-4005]. The contractor must also meet ownership eligibility conditions for communications security equipment designated as controlled cryptographic items [@CL-CMP-4006]. The contractor agrees to include the clause, including its subcontracts paragraph, in all subcontracts which require securing telecommunications [@CL-CMP-4007].

## How it works

The supply chain risk clause puts a mitigation duty on the contractor and gives the Government a statutory route to use information about the contractor's supply chain [@CL-CMP-4000; @CL-CMP-4001]. The disclosure limit means that, once the Government uses its authority to limit disclosure, the actions taken under that authority are not subject to review in a bid protest before the Government Accountability Office or in any Federal court [@CL-CMP-4002]. The security equipment clause identifies classified or sensitive information that requires securing during telecommunications, requires Government-approved equipment, devices, techniques, or services that are compatible or interoperable with those listed and identified for the technical or requirements organization or other offices with which the contractor must communicate, and makes the contractor furnish them unless the contract provides elsewhere [@CL-CMP-4003; @CL-CMP-4004; @CL-CMP-4048; @CL-CMP-7364; @CL-CMP-4005].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor buys 200 network devices for a defense program from a distributor. The Government raises a supply chain concern about the distributor and, using its statutory authority, limits what it discloses. The contractor must still mitigate the risk, and the Government's actions under that authority are not subject to review in a bid protest. Separately, the contract names a secure voice link between two sites. The contractor buys approved equipment for that link itself, unless the contract says otherwise.

## Never confuse

For the prohibition on covered telecommunications equipment, see [Prohibition on covered defense telecommunications equipment or services](cmp-covered-defense-telecommunications-equipment-prohibition.md). The two clauses differ: the telecommunications security clause requires the contractor to secure telecommunications systems using Government-approved equipment, devices, techniques, or services [@CL-CMP-4003; @CL-CMP-4004], while the supply chain risk clause requires the contractor to mitigate supply chain risk in the provision of supplies and services to the Government [@CL-CMP-4000]. For cloud computing services, see [Cloud computing services clause](cmp-cloud-computing-services-clause.md).
