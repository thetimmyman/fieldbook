## Purpose

Shows what the Government may do to manage supply chain risk, what the contractor must do about it, and what the telecommunications security equipment clause requires.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-CMP-4000; @CL-CMP-4003] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 252.239-7018 [@CL-CMP-4000]; DFARS 252.239-7016 [@CL-CMP-4003] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-4000; @CL-CMP-4003] |
| Applies to | Contracts with the supply chain risk clause [@CL-CMP-4000]; contracts where the solicitation or contract identifies information that requires securing during telecommunications [@CL-CMP-4003] |

The contractor shall mitigate supply chain risk in the provision of supplies and services to the Government [@CL-CMP-4000]. To manage supply chain risk, the Government may use the authorities provided by section 3252 of title 10 of the United States Code, and in doing so may consider public and non-public information, including all-source intelligence, relating to the contractor's supply chain [@CL-CMP-4001]. If the Government exercises that authority to limit disclosure of information, no action it undertakes under that authority is subject to review in a bid protest before the Government Accountability Office or in any Federal court [@CL-CMP-4002].

The telecommunications security clause records that the solicitation or contract identifies classified or sensitive information that requires securing during telecommunications, and requires the contractor to secure its telecommunications systems [@CL-CMP-4003]. To provide the security, the contractor shall use Government-approved telecommunications equipment, devices, techniques, or services [@CL-CMP-4004]. The equipment, devices, techniques, or services it uses must be compatible or interoperable with the telecommunications security equipment, devices, techniques, or services that the clause lists and identifies as currently being used by the technical or requirements organization or by other offices with which the contractor must communicate [@CL-CMP-4048; @CL-CMP-7364]. Except as may be provided elsewhere in the contract, the contractor shall furnish all telecommunications security equipment, devices, techniques, or services necessary to perform the contract [@CL-CMP-4005]. The contractor must also meet ownership eligibility conditions for communications security equipment designated as controlled cryptographic items [@CL-CMP-4006]. The contractor shall include the clause in all subcontracts which require securing telecommunications [@CL-CMP-4007].

## How it works

The supply chain risk clause puts a mitigation duty on the contractor and gives the Government a statutory route to use information about the contractor's supply chain [@CL-CMP-4000; @CL-CMP-4001]. The disclosure limit means that, once the Government uses its authority to limit disclosure, the actions taken under that authority are not subject to review in a bid protest before the Government Accountability Office or in any Federal court [@CL-CMP-4002]. The security equipment clause works through the contract: it identifies the information to be secured, requires Government-approved equipment that is compatible or interoperable with the listed equipment, and makes the contractor furnish that equipment unless the contract provides otherwise [@CL-CMP-4003; @CL-CMP-4004; @CL-CMP-4048; @CL-CMP-4005].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor buys 200 network devices for a defense program from a distributor. The Government raises a supply chain concern about the distributor and, using its statutory authority, limits what it discloses. The contractor must still mitigate the risk, and the Government's actions under that authority are not subject to review in a bid protest. Separately, the contract names a secure voice link between two sites. The contractor buys approved equipment for that link itself, unless the contract says otherwise.

## Never confuse

Supply chain risk mitigation is not the same as the prohibition on covered telecommunications equipment; see [Prohibition on covered defense telecommunications equipment or services](cmp-covered-defense-telecommunications-equipment-prohibition.md). The telecommunications security clause concerns securing the contractor's own communications with approved equipment, and it is a different clause from the supply chain risk clause. Cloud services have their own security clause; see [Cloud computing services clause](cmp-cloud-computing-services-clause.md).
