## Purpose

Shows what the CMMC Program Management Office and DCMA DIBCAC each do, and what each one decides.

## The rule

| Applies to | Oversight of the CMMC Program by the Office of the DoD CIO, the CMMC PMO and DCMA DIBCAC [@CL-CMP-3210; @CL-CMP-3213] |

The Office of the Deputy CIO for Cybersecurity in the Office of the DoD CIO provides oversight of the CMMC Program and is responsible for establishing CMMC assessment, accreditation, and training requirements [@CL-CMP-3210]. The CMMC PMO retains, on behalf of that office, the prerogative to review decisions of the CMMC Accreditation Body as part of its oversight and to evaluate any alleged conflicts of interest purported to influence the Accreditation Body's objectivity [@CL-CMP-3211]. The CMMC PMO is also responsible for investigating and acting upon indications that an active CMMC Status has been called into question [@CL-CMP-3212]. Its investigative evaluations include, but are not limited to, reviewing pertinent assessment information and exercising the right to conduct a DCMA DIBCAC assessment of the OSA, as provided for under 48 CFR 252.204-7020 [@CL-CMP-3329].

If a subsequent DCMA DIBCAC assessment shows that adherence to the rule and the required CMMC Status have not been achieved or maintained, the DIBCAC results take precedence over any pre-existing CMMC Status recorded in SPRS or its successor capability [@CL-CMP-3213]. In that same paragraph the DoD will update SPRS to reflect that the OSA is out of compliance and does not meet DoD CMMC requirements [@CL-CMP-3327]. The paragraph also says that if the OSA is working on an active contract requiring CMMC compliance, then standard contractual remedies will apply [@CL-CMP-3328].

## How it works

DCMA DIBCAC assessors in support of the CMMC Program will complete CMMC Level 2 and Level 3 training, and that item opens the list of their duties [@CL-CMP-3214]. Two of the listed duties are to conduct Level 3 certification assessments and upload the results into the CMMC instantiation of eMASS [@CL-CMP-3215], and to issue Certificates of CMMC Status resulting from Level 3 certification assessments [@CL-CMP-3349]. One listed duty is to conduct an assessment of the OSA when requested by the CMMC PMO under the paragraphs on PMO investigation and DIBCAC precedence [@CL-CMP-3216; @CL-CMP-3214]. An OSC, the CMMC AB, or a C3PAO may appeal the outcome of its DCMA DIBCAC conducted assessment within 21 days by submitting a written basis for appeal [@CL-CMP-3217].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A report reaches the CMMC PMO that a supplier's recorded status may be wrong. The PMO asks DCMA DIBCAC to assess the supplier. DIBCAC finds that the required status was not maintained. The DIBCAC result takes precedence over the status in SPRS. The supplier has 21 days to appeal in writing.

## Never confuse

The CMMC PMO and DCMA DIBCAC do different jobs. The Office of the Deputy CIO for Cybersecurity oversees the program [@CL-CMP-3210], and the PMO acts on its behalf to review Accreditation Body decisions and investigates indications that a status is in doubt [@CL-CMP-3211; @CL-CMP-3212]. DCMA DIBCAC assessors conduct Level 3 certification assessments [@CL-CMP-3215], and one listed duty is to assess an OSA when the PMO requests it [@CL-CMP-3216]. The DIBCAC level of assessment is on the [DIBCAC assessment](cmp-cmmc-level-3-assessment.md) card. The body described on the [Accreditation Body](cmp-cmmc-accreditation-body.md) card is responsible for authorizing and ensuring the accreditation of C3PAOs [@CL-CMP-3218], and the PMO reviews its decisions [@CL-CMP-3211]. What a third-party assessment organization must do is on the [third-party assessment organization requirements](cmp-cmmc-c3pao-requirements.md) card. The terms used here are on the [purpose and definitions](cmp-cmmc-purpose-incorporation-and-definitions.md) card.
