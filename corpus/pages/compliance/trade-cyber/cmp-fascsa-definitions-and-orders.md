## Purpose

Shows what a FASCSA order is, who may issue each type, and the agency prohibition that an applicable order creates.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-2969] |
| Codified citation | FAR 4.2301 [@CL-CMP-2969]; FAR 4.2303 [@CL-CMP-2973] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-2969] |
| Applies to | Executive agencies and acquisitions subject to an applicable FASCSA order [@CL-CMP-2973; @CL-CMP-2974] |

A FASCSA order is any of the listed orders issued under the Federal Acquisition Supply Chain Security Act requiring the removal of covered articles from executive agency information systems or the exclusion of one or more named sources or named covered articles [@CL-CMP-2969]. The Secretary of Homeland Security may issue FASCSA orders applicable to civilian agencies, to the extent not covered by the other listed types of order [@CL-CMP-2970]. The Secretary of Defense may issue FASCSA orders applicable to the Department of Defense and national security systems other than sensitive compartmented information systems [@CL-CMP-2971]. The Director of National Intelligence may issue FASCSA orders applicable to the intelligence community and sensitive compartmented information systems, to the extent not covered by the Secretary of Defense type of order [@CL-CMP-2972].

If an applicable FASCSA order issued by the Director of National Intelligence, the Secretary of Defense, or the Secretary of Homeland Security establishes the prohibition, executive agencies are prohibited from procuring or obtaining, or extending or renewing a contract to procure or obtain, any covered article, or any products or services produced or provided by a source [@CL-CMP-2973; @CL-CMP-2974]. That prohibition covers contractor use of covered articles or sources [@CL-CMP-2974].

## How it works

The definition of covered article lists, as its first item, information technology, including cloud computing services of all types [@CL-CMP-2968]. The other listed items of that definition are not set out here. A source is a non-Federal supplier, or potential supplier, of products or services, at any tier [@CL-CMP-2938]. A reasonable inquiry is an inquiry designed to uncover any information in the entity's possession about the identity of any covered articles, or any products or services produced or provided by a source, and it applies when the covered article or the source is subject to an applicable FASCSA order [@CL-CMP-2939]. The same definitions section defines further terms, such as the Council and supply chain risk, that this card does not set out. The prohibition applies if it is established by an applicable order issued by one of the three issuing officials [@CL-CMP-2974]. How a contracting officer applies orders is on [FASCSA procedures, waivers and clauses](cmp-fascsa-procedures-waivers-and-clauses.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A civilian agency learns that a DHS order names a source and establishes the prohibition. The order is applicable to the agency. The agency may not extend or renew a contract for products from that source, and it may not buy them under a new contract either.

## Never confuse

A FASCSA order is defined by who issues it and who it covers [@CL-CMP-2970; @CL-CMP-2971; @CL-CMP-2972]. The agency prohibition is a separate rule, and it applies if an applicable order establishes it [@CL-CMP-2974].
