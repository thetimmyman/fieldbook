## Purpose

Shows what the Government-wide rule treats as covered telecommunications equipment or services, and when agencies may not buy it or contract with an entity that uses it.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-2957] |
| Codified citation | FAR 4.2100 [@CL-SUP-9440]; FAR 4.2101 [@CL-CMP-2950]; FAR 4.2102 [@CL-CMP-2957] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-2957] |
| Applies to | Agency procurements of equipment, systems or services that use covered telecommunications equipment or services as a substantial or essential component of any system or as critical technology as part of any system, and contracts with entities that use such equipment, systems or services, unless an exception or a waiver applies [@CL-CMP-2957; @CL-CMP-2958; @CL-CMP-2959] |

The subpart implements paragraphs (a)(1)(A) and (a)(1)(B) of section 889 of the National Defense Authorization Act for Fiscal Year 2019 (Public Law 115-232) [@CL-SUP-9440].

The definition of covered telecommunications equipment or services opens with telecommunications equipment produced by two named manufacturers or by any subsidiary or affiliate of such entities [@CL-CMP-2950]. The definition goes on to list further items under that lead-in [@CL-CMP-2950]. One covers video surveillance and telecommunications equipment produced by any of three named manufacturers or by any subsidiary or affiliate of such entities [@CL-CMP-2925], for the purpose of public safety, security of Government facilities, physical security surveillance of critical infrastructure, and other national security purposes [@CL-CMP-2951]. Another covers telecommunications or video surveillance services provided by such entities or using such equipment [@CL-CMP-2952]. A last listed item covers telecommunications or video surveillance equipment or services produced or provided by an entity that the Secretary of Defense, in consultation with the Director of National Intelligence or the Director of the Federal Bureau of Investigation, reasonably believes to be owned or controlled by, or otherwise connected to, the government of a covered foreign country [@CL-CMP-0139; @CL-CMP-2953]. The covered foreign country is the People's Republic of China [@CL-CMP-2954].

On or after August 13, 2019, agencies are prohibited from procuring or obtaining, or extending or renewing a contract to procure or obtain, any equipment, system, or service that uses covered telecommunications equipment or services as a substantial or essential component of any system [@CL-CMP-2957]. On or after August 13, 2020, agencies are prohibited from entering into a contract, or extending or renewing a contract, with an entity that uses any equipment, system, or service that uses covered telecommunications equipment or services as a substantial or essential component of any system [@CL-CMP-2958]. Each prohibition also reaches use as critical technology as part of any system, and each applies unless an exception at paragraph (b) of the section applies or a waiver described in the waiver section covers the equipment or services [@CL-CMP-2959]. The second prohibition applies to the use of covered telecommunications equipment or services, regardless of whether that use is in performance of work under a Federal contract [@CL-CMP-2960].

Unless an exception at paragraph (b) applies or the covered equipment or service is covered by a waiver described in 4.2104, Contracting Officers shall not [@CL-CMP-2926] procure or obtain, or extend or renew a contract (e.g., exercise an option) to procure or obtain, any equipment, system, or service that uses covered telecommunications equipment or services as a substantial or essential component of any system, or as critical technology as part of any system [@CL-CMP-0140], and shall not enter into a contract, or extend or renew a contract, with an entity that uses any equipment, system, or service that uses covered telecommunications equipment or services as a substantial or essential component of any system, or as critical technology as part of any system [@CL-CMP-0141]. Prohibitions on purchases of products or services produced or provided by entities identified in the first two items of the definition, including known subsidiaries or affiliates, will be recorded in SAM [@CL-CMP-2927]. Prohibitions on purchases of products or services produced or provided by entities identified under the last item are recorded by the Department of Defense in SAM [@CL-CMP-2928].

## How it works

A substantial or essential component is any component necessary for the proper function or performance of a piece of equipment, system, or service [@CL-CMP-2955]. Critical technology is defined by a list of categories that begins with defense articles or defense services included on the United States Munitions List set forth in the International Traffic in Arms Regulations [@CL-CMP-2956]. Under the exceptions, the subpart does not prohibit agencies from procuring or contractors from providing a service that connects to the facilities of a third-party, such as backhaul, roaming, or interconnection arrangements [@CL-CMP-2961]. The same list of exceptions also includes telecommunications equipment that cannot route or redirect user data traffic or permit visibility into any user data or packets that such equipment transmits or otherwise handles [@CL-CMP-2962; @CL-CMP-2961]. The other categories of critical technology and the other defined terms are not set out here. Who may waive the prohibition, and for how long, is on [Covered telecommunications procedures and waivers](cmp-covered-telecommunications-procedures-and-waivers.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An agency plans to renew a service contract with a provider after the 2020 date in the rule. The provider uses a router as a substantial component of its network, and the router is covered equipment. No exception or waiver applies. The renewal is an extension of a contract with an entity that uses covered equipment, so it is prohibited, even if the router only serves the provider's own offices.

## Never confuse

This card sets out the FAR definitions and the two agency prohibitions. It does not restate the defense supplement's offeror representation, contractor prohibition and reporting duties, which are on [Prohibition on covered defense telecommunications equipment or services](cmp-covered-defense-telecommunications-equipment-prohibition.md). The first prohibition covers procuring or obtaining, or extending or renewing a contract to procure or obtain, any equipment, system, or service that uses covered telecommunications equipment or services as a substantial or essential component of any system or as critical technology as part of any system, unless an exception or a waiver applies [@CL-CMP-2957; @CL-CMP-2959]. The second covers entering into a contract, or extending or renewing a contract, with an entity that uses any equipment, system, or service that uses covered telecommunications equipment or services as a substantial or essential component of any system or as critical technology as part of any system, unless an exception or a waiver applies [@CL-CMP-2958; @CL-CMP-2959].
