## Purpose

Shows what the contracting officer does with an offeror's covered telecommunications representation, and who may waive the prohibition and for how long.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-2963] |
| Codified citation | FAR 4.2103 [@CL-CMP-2963]; FAR 4.2104 [@CL-CMP-2965] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-2963] |
| Applies to | Contracting officers handling covered telecommunications representations, and requests for waiver of the prohibition [@CL-CMP-2963; @CL-CMP-2965] |

The first reliance rule concerns an offeror that selects “does not” in the listed paragraphs of the provision at 52.204-26 or of the provision at 52.212-3 [@CL-CMP-2929]. There the contracting officer may rely on the “does not” representation unless the contracting officer has reason to question it, in which case the contracting officer shall follow agency procedures [@CL-CMP-2963]. If a contractor provides a report pursuant to paragraph (d) of the clause at 52.204-25, agency procedures are to be followed [@CL-CMP-2964].

The head of an executive agency may, on a one-time basis, waive the prohibition at 4.2102(a) with respect to a Government entity that requests such a waiver [@CL-CMP-2965]. The Director of National Intelligence may provide a waiver if the Director determines the waiver is in the national security interests of the United States [@CL-CMP-2967].

## How it works

An executive agency waiver may be provided for a period not to extend beyond August 13, 2021 for the first prohibition, or beyond August 13, 2022 for the second, if the Government official seeking the waiver submits the listed items to the head of the executive agency [@CL-CMP-2966]. One listed submission is a compelling justification for the additional time to implement the requirements, as determined by the head of the executive agency [@CL-CMP-2930]. Another is a full and complete laydown or description of the presences of covered equipment or services in the relevant supply chain and a phase-out plan to eliminate them [@CL-CMP-2931].

Before the head of an executive agency can grant a waiver of the second prohibition, the agency must meet the requirements that paragraph (a)(2) lists [@CL-CMP-2932]. One is to establish participation in an information-sharing environment when and as required by the Federal Acquisition Security Council (FASC) to facilitate interagency sharing of relevant acquisition supply chain risk information [@CL-CMP-0143]. Another is to notify and consult with the Office of the Director of National Intelligence (ODNI) on the waiver request using ODNI guidance, briefings, best practices, or direct inquiry, as appropriate [@CL-CMP-0142]. A further one is to notify the ODNI and the FASC 15 days prior to granting the waiver that the agency intends to grant it [@CL-CMP-2933]. In an emergency, including a declaration of major disaster, in which prior notice and consultation with the ODNI and prior notice to the FASC is impracticable and would severely jeopardize performance of mission-critical functions, the head of an agency may grant a waiver [@CL-CMP-2934]. That waiver may be granted without meeting the notice and consultation requirements for the second prohibition, to enable effective mission critical functions or emergency response and recovery [@CL-CMP-2935].

For a waiver of the first prohibition, the head of the executive agency shall, not later than 30 days after approval, submit in accordance with agency procedures to the appropriate congressional committees the items listed in paragraph (a)(4)(i) [@CL-CMP-2936]. For a waiver of the second prohibition, the head of the executive agency shall, not later than 30 days after approval, submit in accordance with agency procedures to the appropriate congressional committees the items listed in paragraph (a)(4)(ii) [@CL-CMP-2937]. This card does not set out the further steps in the third subparagraph of paragraph (a) for an emergency waiver. For the defense supplement's own representation and reporting duties, see [Prohibition on covered defense telecommunications equipment or services](cmp-covered-defense-telecommunications-equipment-prohibition.md). The definitions behind the prohibition are on [Covered telecommunications equipment or services: definitions and prohibition](cmp-covered-telecommunications-definitions-and-prohibition.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A requirements office asks its agency head for a one-time waiver and submits the justification and the laydown and phase-out plan. The agency head may grant it, but only to a date no later than the one the rule sets. In a separate solicitation, an offeror selects “does not” in the listed paragraph, and the contracting officer has no reason to question the answer, so the contracting officer relies on it.

## Never confuse

Where an offeror selects “does not,” the contracting officer may rely on the “does not” representation unless the contracting officer has reason to question it, in which case the contracting officer shall follow agency procedures [@CL-CMP-2963]. The head of an executive agency may, on a one-time basis, waive the prohibition at 4.2102(a) with respect to a Government entity that requests such a waiver [@CL-CMP-2965], for a period not to extend beyond the dates in paragraph (a)(1) [@CL-CMP-2966]. The Director of National Intelligence may provide a waiver if the Director determines the waiver is in the national security interests of the United States [@CL-CMP-2967].
