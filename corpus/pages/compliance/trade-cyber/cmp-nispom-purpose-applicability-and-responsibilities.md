## Purpose

Shows who the National Industrial Security Program Operating Manual (NISPOM) applies to, what the heads of each cognizant security agency (CSA) oversee, and the implementation deadlines it sets for contractors.

| Applies to | Entities granted access to classified information by executive branch departments and agencies or by foreign governments, among the listed items [@CL-CMP-3152; @CL-CMP-3153] |

This card rests on the NISPOM, the rule for work involving classified information, and not on the FAR. The rule is issued for the protection of classified information that is disclosed to, or developed by, contractors of the US Government [@CL-CMP-3150]. The policy section says Executive Order 12829 established the NISP to serve as a single, integrated, cohesive industrial security program to protect classified information and preserve the Nation's economic and technological interests [@CL-CMP-10024]. When contracts, licenses, agreements, and grants to contractors require access to classified information, national security requires that this information be safeguarded in a manner equivalent to its protection within the executive branch of the US Government [@CL-CMP-10025]. National security requires that the industrial security program promote the economic and technological interests of the United States, and redundant, overlapping, or unnecessary requirements impede those interests [@CL-CMP-10026]. The applicability paragraph is a numbered list of those the rule applies to, and it begins with the Office of the Secretary of Defense, the Military Departments, the Office of the Chairman of the Joint Chiefs of Staff and the Joint Staff, and the Combatant Commands, among other listed entities [@CL-CMP-3152]. The third numbered item is all industrial, educational, commercial, or other non-USG entities granted access to classified information by executive branch departments and agencies or by foreign governments [@CL-CMP-3152; @CL-CMP-3153]. The fourth numbered item is the release of classified information by the USG to contractors, who are required to safeguard the classified information released during all phases of the contracting, agreement, licensing, and grant processes [@CL-CMP-3152; @CL-CMP-3154]. Those phases are the preparation and submission of bids and proposals, negotiation, award, performance, and termination [@CL-CMP-3302].

The rule does not limit in any manner the authority of executive branch departments and agencies to grant access to classified information under their cognizance to any individual designated by them [@CL-CMP-3155]. The CSA heads oversee the security of classified contracts and activities under their purview and provide oversight of contractors under their security cognizance [@CL-CMP-3156]. One numbered item in the paragraph on the CSA heads is to designate subordinate officials, in accordance with governing policies, to act as the authorizing official, whose duties include assessing and authorizing contractors to process classified information on information systems [@CL-CMP-3156; @CL-CMP-3303].

The purpose section prescribes that contractors implement the provisions no later than 6 months from February 24, 2021, with the exception of the foreign travel reporting requirements to the Department of Defense prescribed in SEAD 3 [@CL-CMP-3151]. Contractors under the security cognizance of the Department of Defense will begin reporting foreign travel to the Department of Defense no later than 18 months from February 24, 2021 [@CL-CMP-3304].

## How it works

The rule applies to the government bodies named in the applicability list and to the non-government entities that are granted access to classified information [@CL-CMP-3152; @CL-CMP-3153]. The CSA heads sit on the government side: they oversee classified contracts and the contractors under their security cognizance [@CL-CMP-3156]. Their authorizing officials assess and authorize contractors to process classified information on information systems, and the [NISPOM subcontracting and information system security](cmp-nispom-subcontracting-and-information-system-security.md) card covers what the contractor does before that authorization [@CL-CMP-3303]. The contractor side is a duty to safeguard the classified information released to it in every phase from the preparation of bids to termination [@CL-CMP-3154; @CL-CMP-3302]. The responsibilities section is made up of paragraphs for officials and agency heads and has no paragraph headed for contractors, so it does not state the contractor's own duties. The [NISPOM security officials, insider threat program and self-inspection](cmp-nispom-general-procedures-and-security-officials.md) card and the [NISPOM safeguarding of classified information](cmp-nispom-safeguarding-classified-information.md) card cover those duties.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A company wins a classified services contract worth 2,000,000 and receives classified drawings during its bid. The company must safeguard the drawings from the bid through termination, and its CSA oversees its security program.

Answer: the company is a non-government entity granted access, so the rule applies to it, and the CSA, not the finance team, oversees compliance.

## Never confuse

This rule protects classified information that is disclosed to, or developed by, contractors [@CL-CMP-3150]. It does not limit the authority of an agency to grant access to classified information under its cognizance to any individual the agency designates [@CL-CMP-3155]. It is also not the foreign ownership rule, which is on the [NISPOM foreign ownership, control or influence and its mitigation](../../mna/diligence/mna-nispom-foci-and-mitigation.md) card.

## Company calibration

Whether any company contract or business unit involves classified information, and which CSA oversees each one. [@Q-003]
