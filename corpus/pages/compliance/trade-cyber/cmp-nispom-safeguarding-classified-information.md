## Purpose

Shows what a contractor must do to store, check, transmit and destroy classified information in its custody, and where the printed rule points to other federal standards instead of restating them.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Not established. |
| Codified citation | Not established. |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |

| Applies to | Contractors with classified information in their custody or under their control [@CL-CMP-3189] |

Contractors will be responsible for safeguarding classified information in their custody or under their control, with approval for such storage of classified information by the applicable CSA [@CL-CMP-3189]. Individuals are responsible for safeguarding classified information entrusted to them, and contractors will provide the extent of protection to classified information sufficient to reasonably protect it from loss or compromise [@CL-CMP-4248]. Contractors authorized to store classified material will establish and maintain a system to deter and detect unauthorized introduction or removal of classified material from their facility without proper authority [@CL-CMP-4241]. If the unauthorized introduction or removal of classified material can be reasonably prevented through technical means (such as an intrusion detection system), which are encouraged, no further perimeter controls are necessary [@CL-CMP-4311]. Contractors will store classified information and material in General Services Administration (GSA)-approved security containers, vaults built to Federal Standard 832, or an open storage area constructed in accordance with 32 CFR 2001.53 [@CL-CMP-3190]. Contractors will store classified material in accordance with the specific sections of 32 CFR 2001.43: 32 CFR 2001.43(b)(3) for CONFIDENTIAL, 32 CFR 2001.43(b)(2) for SECRET, and 32 CFR 2001.43(b)(1) for TOP SECRET documents [@CL-CMP-3191]. Contractors are eligible for storage of classified material in connection with a legitimate USG or foreign government requirement if they have a favorable entity eligibility determination and a classified information safeguarding capability approved by the CSA [@CL-CMP-3323].

Contractors that store classified material will establish a system of security checks at the close of each working day to verify that all classified material and security repositories have been appropriately secured [@CL-CMP-3192]. Contractors that operate multiple work shifts will perform the security checks at the end of the last working shift in which classified material was removed from storage for use, and the checks are not required during continuous 24-hour operations [@CL-CMP-3317]. The paragraph on intrusion detection systems (IDS) specifies the minimum standards for an approved IDS when used for supplemental protection of TOP SECRET and SECRET material [@CL-CMP-3193], and CSA approval is required before installing an IDS [@CL-CMP-4247]. Combinations to locks used to secure vaults, open storage areas, and security containers that are approved for the safeguarding of classified information will be protected in the same manner as the highest level of classified information that the vault, open storage area, or security container is used to protect [@CL-CMP-4242; @CL-CMP-0431]. Contractors will establish a system to verify that classified information in their custody is used or retained only for a lawful and authorized USG purpose, and an information management system to protect and control the classified information in their possession regardless of media, to include information processed and stored on authorized information systems [@CL-CMP-4240; @CL-CMP-0430]. Contractors will establish procedures for the control of classified working papers generated in the preparation of a finished document, including dating working papers when they are created [@CL-CMP-4243].

Unless otherwise directed by the applicable CSA, the contractor will establish additional TOP SECRET controls, the first of which is to designate TOP SECRET control officials to receive, transmit, and maintain access and accountability records to TOP SECRET information [@CL-CMP-4236], to validate procedures are in place to address accountability, need to know, and retention (for example, demonstrating that TOP SECRET material stored in an electronic format on an authorized classified information system does not need to be individually numbered in series) [@CL-CMP-4310]. The additional TOP SECRET controls include conducting an annual inventory of TOP SECRET information and material and establishing a continuous receipt system for the transmittal of TOP SECRET information within and outside the contractor location [@CL-CMP-4237]. They include numbering each item of TOP SECRET material in a series and placing the copy number on TOP SECRET documents, regardless of media, and on all associated transaction documents, subject to the electronic-format example above [@CL-CMP-4238; @CL-CMP-4310]. They include establishing a record of TOP SECRET material when the material is completed as a finished document, retained for more than 180 days after creation, regardless of the stage of development, or transmitted outside the contractor location [@CL-CMP-4239]. They also include establishing procedures for destruction of TOP SECRET material by two authorized persons [@CL-CMP-4234; @CL-CMP-4236], and establishing destruction records for TOP SECRET material and maintaining the records for two years in accordance with § 117.13(d)(5) or in accordance with GCA requirements [@CL-CMP-4235; @CL-CMP-4236].

Contractors will establish procedures for transmitting and receiving classified information and material in accordance with 32 CFR 2001.46 [@CL-CMP-4141]. The contractor must have written authorization from the GCA to transmit TOP SECRET material outside the contractor location [@CL-CMP-3318]. The CSA may approve contractors to transmit SECRET or CONFIDENTIAL information within the United States and its territorial areas by means of a commercial delivery entity that is a current holder of the GSA contract for overnight delivery and which provides nation-wide, overnight service with computer tracking and reporting features [@CL-CMP-4142; @CL-CMP-4145]. Such entities do not need to be determined eligible for access to classified information [@CL-CMP-4312]. Prior to CSA approval, the contractor must establish and document procedures to ensure the proper protection of incoming and outgoing classified packages, including the street delivery address, for each cleared facility intending to use GSA-listed commercial delivery entities for overnight services [@CL-CMP-4143]. Those procedures include ensuring package integrity during transit and that incoming shipments are received by appropriately cleared personnel, and that commercial delivery entities not be used for COMSEC, NATO, or FGI [@CL-CMP-4146]. Contractors may designate cleared employees as couriers or hand carriers and will brief employees providing such services on their responsibility to safeguard classified information and keep classified material in their possession at all times [@CL-CMP-4144]. The contractor may transmit classified material to a USG activity outside the United States or a U.S. territorial area only under the provisions of a classified contract or with written authorization from the GCA [@CL-CMP-4244].

Contractors will destroy classified material in their possession based on the disposition instructions in the contract security classification specification or equivalent [@CL-CMP-3319], follow the guidance for destruction of classified material in accordance with 32 CFR 2001.47 and the destruction equipment standards in accordance with 32 CFR 2001.42(b) [@CL-CMP-4147], and, unless otherwise directed by the applicable CSA, apply the additional TOP SECRET destruction controls above [@CL-CMP-4234; @CL-CMP-4235; @CL-CMP-4236]. Contractors will follow the guidance established in 32 CFR 2001.45(b) for the reproduction of classified information [@CL-CMP-4148].

Contractors will establish procedures for review of their classified holdings on a recurring basis to ensure the classified holdings are in support of a current contract or authorization to retain beyond the end of the contract period [@CL-CMP-4245]. If the GCA does not authorize retention beyond two years, the contractor will destroy all classified material received or generated in the performance of a classified contract unless it has been declassified or the GCA has requested that the material be returned [@CL-CMP-4246].

## How it works

Contractors are responsible for safeguarding classified information in their custody or under their control, with approval for such storage by the applicable CSA, and will store classified information and material in GSA-approved security containers, vaults built to Federal Standard 832, or an open storage area constructed in accordance with 32 CFR 2001.53 [@CL-CMP-3189; @CL-CMP-3190]. Contractors are eligible for storage of classified material in connection with a legitimate USG or foreign government requirement if they have a favorable entity eligibility determination (see [NISPOM entity eligibility determination (facility clearance)](cmp-nispom-entity-eligibility-determination.md)) and a classified information safeguarding capability approved by the CSA [@CL-CMP-3323]. Contractors store classified material in accordance with 32 CFR 2001.43(b)(3) for CONFIDENTIAL, 32 CFR 2001.43(b)(2) for SECRET, and 32 CFR 2001.43(b)(1) for TOP SECRET documents [@CL-CMP-3191]. At the close of each working day, contractors that store classified material check that all classified material and security repositories have been appropriately secured, or at the end of the last working shift in which classified material was removed from storage for use when operating multiple work shifts, with checks not required during continuous 24-hour operations [@CL-CMP-3192; @CL-CMP-3317]. An approved IDS is used for supplemental protection of TOP SECRET and SECRET material, and CSA approval is required before installing an IDS [@CL-CMP-3193; @CL-CMP-4247]. Contractors authorized to store classified material establish and maintain a system to deter and detect unauthorized introduction or removal of classified material from their facility without proper authority (with no further controls necessary if reasonably prevented through technical means such as an IDS), protect lock combinations in the same manner as the highest level of classified information the vault, open storage area, or security container is used to protect, establish a system to verify that classified information in their custody is used or retained only for a lawful and authorized USG purpose and an information management system to protect and control classified information in their possession regardless of media (including information processed and stored on authorized information systems), and establish procedures for the control of classified working papers including dating them when created [@CL-CMP-4241; @CL-CMP-4311; @CL-CMP-4242; @CL-CMP-0431; @CL-CMP-4240; @CL-CMP-0430; @CL-CMP-4243]. Individuals are responsible for safeguarding classified information entrusted to them, and contractors provide protection sufficient to reasonably protect it from loss or compromise [@CL-CMP-4248]. Unless otherwise directed by the applicable CSA, the additional TOP SECRET controls require designating TOP SECRET control officials to validate procedures for accountability, need to know, and retention (for example, demonstrating that TOP SECRET material stored in an electronic format on an authorized classified information system does not need to be individually numbered in series), conducting an annual inventory, establishing a continuous receipt system, numbering each item in a series and placing the copy number on TOP SECRET documents and associated transaction documents, establishing a record when completed as a finished document, retained for more than 180 days after creation regardless of the stage of development, or transmitted outside the contractor location, establishing procedures for destruction by two authorized persons, and establishing and maintaining destruction records for two years in accordance with § 117.13(d)(5) or GCA requirements [@CL-CMP-4236; @CL-CMP-4310; @CL-CMP-4237; @CL-CMP-4238; @CL-CMP-4239; @CL-CMP-4234; @CL-CMP-4235]. Contractors establish procedures for transmitting and receiving classified information and material in accordance with 32 CFR 2001.46, and must have written authorization from the GCA to transmit TOP SECRET material outside the contractor location [@CL-CMP-4141; @CL-CMP-3318]. The CSA may approve contractors to transmit SECRET or CONFIDENTIAL information within the United States and its territorial areas by a commercial delivery entity holding the GSA contract for overnight delivery with nation-wide, overnight service and computer tracking and reporting features (which does not need to be determined eligible for access to classified information), provided that prior to CSA approval the contractor establishes and documents procedures to ensure proper protection of incoming and outgoing classified packages, including the street delivery address, for each cleared facility [@CL-CMP-4142; @CL-CMP-4145; @CL-CMP-4312; @CL-CMP-4143]. Those procedures include ensuring package integrity during transit and receipt by appropriately cleared personnel, and not using commercial delivery entities for COMSEC, NATO, or FGI (see [NISPOM CNWDI and COMSEC access](cmp-nispom-cnwdi-and-comsec.md) and [NISPOM disclosure and transfer to foreign interests](cmp-nispom-international-transfers-and-foreign-government-information.md)) [@CL-CMP-4146]. Contractors that designate cleared employees as couriers or hand carriers brief them on their responsibility to safeguard classified information and keep classified material in their possession at all times [@CL-CMP-4144]. Transmitting classified material to a USG activity outside the United States or a U.S. territorial area is permitted only under the provisions of a classified contract or with written authorization from the GCA [@CL-CMP-4244]. Destruction is based on the disposition instructions in the contract security classification specification or equivalent and follows the guidance in 32 CFR 2001.47 and the destruction equipment standards in 32 CFR 2001.42(b), along with the additional TOP SECRET destruction controls unless otherwise directed by the applicable CSA [@CL-CMP-3319; @CL-CMP-4147; @CL-CMP-4234; @CL-CMP-4235; @CL-CMP-4236]. Reproduction follows 32 CFR 2001.45(b) [@CL-CMP-4148]. Classified holdings are reviewed on a recurring basis to ensure they support a current contract or authorization to retain beyond the end of the contract period, and if the GCA does not authorize retention beyond two years, the contractor destroys all classified material received or generated in the performance of a classified contract unless it has been declassified or the GCA has requested that it be returned [@CL-CMP-4245; @CL-CMP-4246]. For a prime contractor's duties before releasing classified information to a subcontractor, see [NISPOM subcontracting and information system security](cmp-nispom-subcontracting-and-information-system-security.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor keeps SECRET documents in a GSA-approved container inside a room with an intrusion detection system. At the end of the day the container is locked. The next day it wants to send one SECRET package by an overnight carrier that it picked on price.

Answer on storage: the end-of-day check still has to verify that all classified material and repositories are secured, because the alarm only supplements the storage [@CL-CMP-3192; @CL-CMP-3193].

Answer on shipping: the lower level does not remove the transmission rules. The contractor needs transmission procedures under 32 CFR 2001.46, and commercial delivery of SECRET or CONFIDENTIAL information within the United States and its territorial areas requires CSA approval, a commercial delivery entity holding the GSA contract for overnight delivery with nation-wide overnight service and computer tracking and reporting features, and documented package-protection procedures established prior to CSA approval [@CL-CMP-4141; @CL-CMP-4142; @CL-CMP-4145; @CL-CMP-4143]. Alternatively, contractors may designate cleared employees as couriers or hand carriers and brief them on their responsibility to safeguard classified information and keep classified material in their possession at all times [@CL-CMP-4144].

Answer on destruction: when the contractor later disposes of old holdings, it destroys classified material based on the disposition instructions in the contract security classification specification or equivalent and follows 32 CFR 2001.47 and 32 CFR 2001.42(b), and for TOP SECRET material, unless otherwise directed by the applicable CSA, establishes procedures for destruction by two authorized persons and establishes and maintains destruction records for two years in accordance with § 117.13(d)(5) or in accordance with GCA requirements [@CL-CMP-3319; @CL-CMP-4147; @CL-CMP-4234; @CL-CMP-4235; @CL-CMP-4236].

## Never confuse

Destroying classified material requires following the disposition instructions in the contract security classification specification or equivalent and the guidance and equipment standards in 32 CFR 2001.47 and 32 CFR 2001.42(b), and for TOP SECRET material, unless otherwise directed by the applicable CSA, also requires establishing procedures for destruction by two authorized persons and establishing destruction records maintained for two years in accordance with § 117.13(d)(5) or in accordance with GCA requirements [@CL-CMP-3319; @CL-CMP-4147; @CL-CMP-4234; @CL-CMP-4235; @CL-CMP-4236]. Written authorization from the GCA is required to transmit TOP SECRET material outside the contractor location [@CL-CMP-3318], while contractors must establish procedures for transmitting and receiving classified information and material in accordance with 32 CFR 2001.46, and transmitting SECRET or CONFIDENTIAL information by a GSA-contract commercial delivery entity requires CSA approval and documented package-protection procedures established prior to CSA approval [@CL-CMP-4141; @CL-CMP-4142; @CL-CMP-4143]. For the contracting officer's duties at each phase of a classified acquisition, see [Safeguarding classified information within industry](../audit-retention/cmp-classified-information-safeguarding-duties.md).
