## Purpose

Shows what a contractor must do to store, check, transmit and destroy classified information in its custody, and where the printed rule points to other federal standards instead of restating them.

## The rule

| Applies to | Contractors with classified information in their custody or under their control [@CL-CMP-3189] |

This card rests on the NISPOM, the rule for work involving classified information, and not on the FAR. Contractors will be responsible for safeguarding classified information in their custody or under their control, with approval for such storage of classified information by the applicable CSA [@CL-CMP-3189]. Individuals are responsible for safeguarding classified information entrusted to them, and contractors will provide the extent of protection sufficient to reasonably protect it from loss or compromise [@CL-CMP-4248]. Contractors authorized to store classified material will establish and maintain a system to deter and detect unauthorized introduction or removal of classified material from their facility without proper authority [@CL-CMP-4241]. If the unauthorized introduction or removal of classified material can be reasonably prevented through technical means, such as an intrusion detection system, no further perimeter controls are necessary [@CL-CMP-4311]. Contractors will store classified information and material in GSA-approved security containers, vaults built to Federal Standard 832, or an open storage area constructed in accordance with a federal regulation on open storage areas [@CL-CMP-3190]. Contractors will store classified material in accordance with the specific sections of a federal storage regulation that are listed separately for CONFIDENTIAL, SECRET, and TOP SECRET documents [@CL-CMP-3191]. Contractors are eligible for storage of classified material in connection with a legitimate USG or foreign government requirement if they have a favorable entity eligibility determination and a classified information safeguarding capability approved by the CSA [@CL-CMP-3323].

Contractors that store classified material will establish a system of security checks at the close of each working day to verify that all classified material and security repositories have been appropriately secured [@CL-CMP-3192]. Contractors that operate multiple work shifts will perform the end of day security checks at the end of the last working shift in which classified material was removed from storage for use, and the checks are not required during continuous 24-hour operations [@CL-CMP-3317]. The paragraph on intrusion detection systems specifies the minimum standards for an approved IDS when used for supplemental protection of TOP SECRET and SECRET material [@CL-CMP-3193], and CSA approval is required before installing an intrusion detection system [@CL-CMP-4247]. Combinations to locks used to secure vaults, open storage areas, and security containers approved for the safeguarding of classified information will be protected in the same manner as the highest level of classified information that the vault, open storage area, or security container is used to protect [@CL-CMP-4242]. Contractors will establish a system to verify that classified information in their custody is used or retained only for a lawful and authorized USG purpose, and an information management system to protect and control the classified information in their possession regardless of media [@CL-CMP-4240]. Contractors will establish procedures for the control of classified working papers generated in the preparation of a finished document, including dating them when they are created [@CL-CMP-4243].

TOP SECRET information has its own additional controls. Unless otherwise directed by the applicable CSA, the contractor will establish additional TOP SECRET controls, the first of which is to designate TOP SECRET control officials to receive, transmit, and maintain access and accountability records [@CL-CMP-4236]. The printed text says the TOP SECRET controls are to validate that procedures are in place to address accountability, need to know, and retention, for example by demonstrating that TOP SECRET material stored in an electronic format on an authorized classified information system does not need to be individually numbered in series [@CL-CMP-4310]. The additional TOP SECRET controls include an annual inventory of TOP SECRET information and material and a continuous receipt system for the transmittal of TOP SECRET information within and outside the contractor location [@CL-CMP-4237]. They include numbering each item of TOP SECRET material in a series and placing the copy number on TOP SECRET documents, regardless of media, and on all associated transaction documents, read with the electronic-format example in the previous sentence [@CL-CMP-4238; @CL-CMP-4310]. They include establishing a record of TOP SECRET material when it is completed as a finished document, retained for more than 180 days after creation, or transmitted outside the contractor location [@CL-CMP-4239]. One of the additional TOP SECRET controls is that the contractor establish procedures for destruction of TOP SECRET material by two authorized persons [@CL-CMP-4234; @CL-CMP-4236]. Another is that the contractor establish destruction records for TOP SECRET material and maintain the records for two years in accordance with section 117.13(d)(5) of the rule or in accordance with GCA requirements [@CL-CMP-4235; @CL-CMP-4236]. As printed, the GCA alternative sits in the clause on how the destruction records are maintained, and the only exception stated for the additional controls, including destruction by two authorized persons and the destruction records, is a direction by the applicable CSA [@CL-CMP-4234; @CL-CMP-4235; @CL-CMP-4236].

Transmission is governed at every classification level: contractors will establish procedures for transmitting and receiving classified information and material in accordance with section 2001.46 of title 32 of the Code of Federal Regulations [@CL-CMP-4141]. For TOP SECRET material, the contractor must in addition have written authorization from the GCA to transmit it outside the contractor location [@CL-CMP-3318]. The CSA may approve contractors to transmit SECRET or CONFIDENTIAL information within the United States and its territorial areas by means of a commercial delivery entity that is a current holder of the GSA contract for overnight delivery [@CL-CMP-4142], and that entity is also one that provides nation-wide, overnight service with computer tracking and reporting features [@CL-CMP-4145]. Such entities do not need to be determined eligible for access to classified information [@CL-CMP-4312]. Prior to CSA approval, the contractor must establish and document procedures to ensure the proper protection of incoming and outgoing classified packages, including the street delivery address, for each cleared facility intending to use GSA-listed commercial delivery entities for overnight services [@CL-CMP-4143]. The listed procedures for commercial delivery entities include ensuring package integrity during transit and receipt of incoming shipments by appropriately cleared personnel, and an item that they not be used for COMSEC, NATO, or FGI [@CL-CMP-4146]. Contractors that designate cleared employees as couriers or hand carriers will brief them on their responsibility to safeguard classified information and keep classified material in their possession at all times [@CL-CMP-4144]. The contractor may transmit classified material to a USG activity outside the United States or a United States territorial area only under the provisions of a classified contract or with written authorization from the GCA [@CL-CMP-4244].

Contractors will destroy classified material in their possession based on the disposition instructions in the contract security classification specification or equivalent [@CL-CMP-3319], and that is not the whole destruction duty: TOP SECRET material has its own destruction controls, set out above [@CL-CMP-4234; @CL-CMP-4235; @CL-CMP-4236]. The destruction paragraph also lists following the guidance for destruction of classified material in section 2001.47 of title 32 of the Code of Federal Regulations and the destruction equipment standards in section 2001.42(b) of that title [@CL-CMP-4147]. Contractors will follow the guidance established in section 2001.45(b) of title 32 of the Code of Federal Regulations for the reproduction of classified information [@CL-CMP-4148].

Contractors will establish procedures for review of their classified holdings on a recurring basis to ensure the classified holdings are in support of a current contract or authorization to retain beyond the end of the contract period [@CL-CMP-4245]. If the GCA does not authorize retention beyond two years, the contractor will destroy all classified material received or generated in the performance of a classified contract unless it has been declassified or the GCA has requested that the material be returned [@CL-CMP-4246].

This card does not set out every safeguarding duty in the section. It does not set out the duties on oral discussions, security equipment standards and repairs, the structure of open storage areas, including false ceilings and raised floors and conflicts with local laws or building codes, the detail of intrusion detection systems, perimeter inspections and emergency procedures, information system passwords, the marking and destruction of working papers, courier identification, advance overnight storage arrangements and inventories, hand carrying on commercial aircraft, escorts, the handling of duplicate copies and bid or proposal material, the justification for retention, termination of the security agreement, disclosure, and controls for controlled unclassified information, and the reader must follow the printed safeguarding section for each of them.

## How it works

A contractor needs the CSA's approval for the storage of classified information, and it must store it in a GSA-approved security container, a vault built to the federal standard, or an open storage area built to the federal regulation [@CL-CMP-3189; @CL-CMP-3190]. Eligibility to store rests on a favorable entity eligibility determination and a safeguarding capability approved by the CSA, and the [NISPOM entity eligibility determination (facility clearance)](cmp-nispom-entity-eligibility-determination.md) card covers the first of these [@CL-CMP-3323]. The rule lists the three classification levels but points to specific sections of a separate federal regulation for each level [@CL-CMP-3191]. At the close of each working day the contractor checks that all classified material and repositories are secured, with the multiple-shift and continuous-operation rules for when the check is made [@CL-CMP-3192; @CL-CMP-3317]. An intrusion detection system adds protection for TOP SECRET and SECRET material but is described as supplemental, and it needs CSA approval before installation [@CL-CMP-3193; @CL-CMP-4247]. The contractor also keeps a system to deter and detect unauthorized introduction or removal, although no further perimeter controls are needed where technical means can reasonably prevent it, protects lock combinations at the level of what the lock secures, runs an information management system and controls working papers [@CL-CMP-4241; @CL-CMP-4311; @CL-CMP-4242; @CL-CMP-4240; @CL-CMP-4243]. Individuals share responsibility for what is entrusted to them, and the protection must be sufficient to reasonably protect against loss or compromise [@CL-CMP-4248]. TOP SECRET adds control officials, an annual inventory, a continuous receipt system, numbering, records, destruction by two authorized persons and destruction records, each unless the CSA directs otherwise, with the printed example that electronic TOP SECRET material on an authorized classified information system need not be individually numbered in series [@CL-CMP-4236; @CL-CMP-4237; @CL-CMP-4238; @CL-CMP-4239; @CL-CMP-4234; @CL-CMP-4235; @CL-CMP-4310]. Transmission procedures apply to classified information and material at every level, and TOP SECRET material outside the contractor location also needs written GCA authorization [@CL-CMP-4141; @CL-CMP-3318]. SECRET or CONFIDENTIAL information may go by a commercial delivery entity where the CSA has approved the contractor, the entity holds the GSA overnight contract, needs no eligibility determination of its own and the contractor has first documented its package-protection procedures [@CL-CMP-4142; @CL-CMP-4145; @CL-CMP-4312; @CL-CMP-4143]. Those procedures also cover package integrity and receipt by cleared personnel, and they list COMSEC, NATO and FGI as not to go this way, so the [NISPOM CNWDI and COMSEC access](cmp-nispom-cnwdi-and-comsec.md) and [NISPOM disclosure and transfer to foreign interests](cmp-nispom-international-transfers-and-foreign-government-information.md) cards matter for those items [@CL-CMP-4146]. A contractor that designates cleared employees as couriers or hand carriers briefs them on their responsibility to safeguard classified information and keep the material in their possession at all times [@CL-CMP-4144]. Shipping classified material to a USG activity outside the United States or a United States territorial area needs a classified contract or written GCA authorization [@CL-CMP-4244]. Destruction follows the disposition instructions in the contract security classification specification or equivalent and the guidance in the federal destruction regulation, and TOP SECRET destruction adds two authorized persons and destruction records unless the CSA directs otherwise, with the GCA requirements bearing on how the records are maintained and not on whether they are made or two authorized persons are used [@CL-CMP-3319; @CL-CMP-4147; @CL-CMP-4234; @CL-CMP-4235; @CL-CMP-4236]. Reproduction follows the federal reproduction guidance [@CL-CMP-4148]. Holdings are reviewed on a recurring basis, and if the GCA does not authorize retention beyond two years, classified material received or generated in the performance of a classified contract is destroyed unless it has been declassified or the GCA has requested that it be returned [@CL-CMP-4245; @CL-CMP-4246]. The listed items are not the whole safeguarding duty, and the paragraph above lists the duties this card does not set out. A prime contractor's duties before releasing classified information to a subcontractor are on the [NISPOM subcontracting and information system security](cmp-nispom-subcontracting-and-information-system-security.md) card.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor keeps SECRET documents in a GSA-approved container inside a room with an intrusion detection system. At the end of the day the container is locked. The next day it wants to send one SECRET package by an overnight carrier that it picked on price.

Answer on storage: the end-of-day check still has to verify that all classified material and repositories are secured, because the alarm only supplements the storage [@CL-CMP-3192; @CL-CMP-3193].

Answer on shipping: the lower level does not remove the transmission rules. The contractor needs transmission procedures under the federal regulation, and the section prints commercial delivery for it only where the CSA has approved the contractor, the carrier holds the GSA overnight contract with computer tracking and the contractor has documented its package-protection procedures first [@CL-CMP-4141; @CL-CMP-4142; @CL-CMP-4145; @CL-CMP-4143]. If it fits none of that, a cleared employee designated as courier or hand carrier is the other route this section prints, and that employee is briefed first [@CL-CMP-4144].

Answer on destruction: when the contractor later disposes of old holdings, following the disposition instructions and the federal destruction guidance is not enough if any of it is TOP SECRET. Unless the CSA directs otherwise, the destruction is done under procedures that use two authorized persons and is recorded in destruction records maintained for the stated period or in accordance with GCA requirements, and a GCA requirement does not remove the two authorized persons or the records [@CL-CMP-3319; @CL-CMP-4147; @CL-CMP-4234; @CL-CMP-4235; @CL-CMP-4236]. The card does not set out every other safeguarding duty, so the contractor also reads the printed section for those.

## Never confuse

Following the disposition instructions and the federal destruction and equipment guidance is not the whole destruction duty: TOP SECRET material also needs destruction by two authorized persons and destruction records, unless the CSA directs otherwise, and a GCA requirement bears only on how the destruction records are maintained [@CL-CMP-3319; @CL-CMP-4147; @CL-CMP-4234; @CL-CMP-4235; @CL-CMP-4236]. The same goes for the other safeguards named on this card: each is one duty among those in the printed section, and none is the whole safeguarding duty, which the end-of-day check, the intrusion detection system and the TOP SECRET controls illustrate [@CL-CMP-3192; @CL-CMP-3193; @CL-CMP-4236]. The written GCA authorization is the extra step for TOP SECRET transmission, not the whole transmission rule: procedures apply to every classification level, and SECRET or CONFIDENTIAL material by commercial delivery needs CSA approval and documented package procedures [@CL-CMP-4141; @CL-CMP-3318; @CL-CMP-4142; @CL-CMP-4143]. The printed section does not set out what the referenced federal transmission procedures contain, so this card does not establish them. Treat the duties this card leaves out as binding too, and read the printed safeguarding section for them. This card covers what the contractor does to safeguard classified information in its custody. The contracting officer's own duties at each phase of a classified acquisition are on the [Safeguarding classified information within industry](../audit-retention/cmp-classified-information-safeguarding-duties.md) card.
