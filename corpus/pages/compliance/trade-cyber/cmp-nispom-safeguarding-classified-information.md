## Purpose

Shows what a contractor must do to store, check, transmit and destroy classified information in its custody, and where the printed rule points to other federal standards instead of restating them.

This card rests on the NISPOM, the rule for work involving classified information, and not on the FAR. Contractors will be responsible for safeguarding classified information in their custody or under their control, with approval for such storage of classified information by the applicable CSA [@CL-CMP-3189]. Contractors will store classified information and material in GSA-approved security containers, vaults built to Federal Standard 832, or an open storage area constructed in accordance with a federal regulation on open storage areas [@CL-CMP-3190]. Contractors will store classified material in accordance with the specific sections of a federal storage regulation that are listed separately for CONFIDENTIAL, SECRET, and TOP SECRET documents [@CL-CMP-3191]. Contractors are eligible for storage of classified material in connection with a legitimate USG or foreign government requirement if they have a favorable entity eligibility determination and a classified information safeguarding capability approved by the CSA [@CL-CMP-3323].

Contractors that store classified material will establish a system of security checks at the close of each working day to verify that all classified material and security repositories have been appropriately secured [@CL-CMP-3192]. Contractors that operate multiple work shifts will perform the end of day security checks at the end of the last working shift in which classified material was removed from storage for use, and the checks are not required during continuous 24-hour operations [@CL-CMP-3317]. The paragraph on intrusion detection systems specifies the minimum standards for an approved IDS when used for supplemental protection of TOP SECRET and SECRET material [@CL-CMP-3193].

The contractor must have written authorization from the GCA to transmit TOP SECRET material outside the contractor location [@CL-CMP-3318]. Contractors will destroy classified material in their possession based on the disposition instructions in the contract security classification specification or equivalent [@CL-CMP-3319].

## How it works

A contractor needs the CSA's approval for the storage of classified information, and it must store it in a GSA-approved security container, a vault built to the federal standard, or an open storage area built to the federal regulation [@CL-CMP-3189; @CL-CMP-3190]. Eligibility to store rests on a favorable entity eligibility determination and a safeguarding capability approved by the CSA, and the [NISPOM entity eligibility determination (facility clearance)](cmp-nispom-entity-eligibility-determination.md) card covers the first of these [@CL-CMP-3323]. The rule lists the three classification levels but points to specific sections of a separate federal regulation for each level [@CL-CMP-3191]. At the close of each working day the contractor checks that all classified material and repositories are secured, with the multiple-shift and continuous-operation rules for when the check is made [@CL-CMP-3192; @CL-CMP-3317]. An intrusion detection system adds protection for TOP SECRET and SECRET material but is described as supplemental [@CL-CMP-3193]. Transmission of TOP SECRET material outside the contractor location needs written GCA authorization, and destruction follows the disposition instructions in the contract security classification specification or equivalent [@CL-CMP-3318; @CL-CMP-3319]. A prime contractor's duties before releasing classified information to a subcontractor are on the [NISPOM subcontracting and information system security](cmp-nispom-subcontracting-and-information-system-security.md) card.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor keeps SECRET documents in a GSA-approved container inside a room with an intrusion detection system. At the end of the day the container is locked.

Answer: the end-of-day check still has to verify that all classified material and repositories are secured, because the alarm only supplements the storage [@CL-CMP-3192; @CL-CMP-3193].

## Never confuse

This card covers what the contractor does to safeguard classified information in its custody. The contracting officer's own duties at each phase of a classified acquisition are on the [Safeguarding classified information within industry](../audit-retention/cmp-classified-information-safeguarding-duties.md) card.
