## Purpose

Shows what a contractor must do to store, check, transmit and destroy classified information in its custody, and where the printed rule points to other federal standards instead of restating them.

## The rule

This card rests on the NISPOM, the rule for work involving classified information, and not on the FAR. Contractors will be responsible for safeguarding classified information in their custody or under their control, with approval for such storage of classified information by the applicable CSA [@CL-CMP-3189]. Contractors will store classified information and material in GSA-approved security containers, vaults built to Federal Standard 832, or an open storage area constructed in accordance with a federal regulation on open storage areas [@CL-CMP-3190]. Contractors will store classified material in accordance with the specific sections of a federal storage regulation that are listed separately for CONFIDENTIAL, SECRET, and TOP SECRET documents [@CL-CMP-3191]. Contractors are eligible for storage of classified material in connection with a legitimate USG or foreign government requirement if they have a favorable entity eligibility determination and a classified information safeguarding capability approved by the CSA [@CL-CMP-3323].

Contractors that store classified material will establish a system of security checks at the close of each working day to verify that all classified material and security repositories have been appropriately secured [@CL-CMP-3192]. Contractors that operate multiple work shifts will perform the end of day security checks at the end of the last working shift in which classified material was removed from storage for use, and the checks are not required during continuous 24-hour operations [@CL-CMP-3317]. The paragraph on intrusion detection systems specifies the minimum standards for an approved IDS when used for supplemental protection of TOP SECRET and SECRET material [@CL-CMP-3193].

Transmission is governed at every classification level: contractors will establish procedures for transmitting and receiving classified information and material in accordance with section 2001.46 of title 32 of the Code of Federal Regulations [@CL-CMP-4141]. For TOP SECRET material, the contractor must in addition have written authorization from the GCA to transmit it outside the contractor location [@CL-CMP-3318]. The CSA may approve contractors to transmit SECRET or CONFIDENTIAL information within the United States and its territorial areas by means of a commercial delivery entity that is a current holder of the GSA contract for overnight delivery [@CL-CMP-4142], and that entity is also one that provides nation-wide, overnight service with computer tracking and reporting features [@CL-CMP-4145]. Prior to CSA approval, the contractor must establish and document procedures to ensure the proper protection of incoming and outgoing classified packages, including the street delivery address, for each cleared facility intending to use GSA-listed commercial delivery entities for overnight services [@CL-CMP-4143]. The listed procedures for commercial delivery entities include ensuring package integrity during transit and receipt of incoming shipments by appropriately cleared personnel, and an item that they not be used for COMSEC, NATO, or FGI [@CL-CMP-4146]. Contractors that designate cleared employees as couriers or hand carriers will brief them on their responsibility to safeguard classified information and keep classified material in their possession at all times [@CL-CMP-4144].

Contractors will destroy classified material in their possession based on the disposition instructions in the contract security classification specification or equivalent [@CL-CMP-3319]. The destruction paragraph also lists following the guidance for destruction of classified material in section 2001.47 of title 32 of the Code of Federal Regulations and the destruction equipment standards in section 2001.42(b) of that title [@CL-CMP-4147]. Contractors will follow the guidance established in section 2001.45(b) of title 32 of the Code of Federal Regulations for the reproduction of classified information [@CL-CMP-4148].

## How it works

A contractor needs the CSA's approval for the storage of classified information, and it must store it in a GSA-approved security container, a vault built to the federal standard, or an open storage area built to the federal regulation [@CL-CMP-3189; @CL-CMP-3190]. Eligibility to store rests on a favorable entity eligibility determination and a safeguarding capability approved by the CSA, and the [NISPOM entity eligibility determination (facility clearance)](cmp-nispom-entity-eligibility-determination.md) card covers the first of these [@CL-CMP-3323]. The rule lists the three classification levels but points to specific sections of a separate federal regulation for each level [@CL-CMP-3191]. At the close of each working day the contractor checks that all classified material and repositories are secured, with the multiple-shift and continuous-operation rules for when the check is made [@CL-CMP-3192; @CL-CMP-3317]. An intrusion detection system adds protection for TOP SECRET and SECRET material but is described as supplemental [@CL-CMP-3193]. Transmission procedures apply to classified information and material at every level, and TOP SECRET material outside the contractor location also needs written GCA authorization [@CL-CMP-4141; @CL-CMP-3318]. SECRET or CONFIDENTIAL information may go by a commercial delivery entity where the CSA has approved the contractor, the entity holds the GSA overnight contract and the contractor has first documented its package-protection procedures [@CL-CMP-4142; @CL-CMP-4145; @CL-CMP-4143]. Those procedures also cover package integrity and receipt by cleared personnel, and they list COMSEC, NATO and FGI as not to go this way, so the [NISPOM CNWDI and COMSEC access](cmp-nispom-cnwdi-and-comsec.md) and [NISPOM disclosure and transfer to foreign interests](cmp-nispom-international-transfers-and-foreign-government-information.md) cards matter for those items [@CL-CMP-4146]. A contractor that sends material by cleared courier or hand carrier briefs that employee first [@CL-CMP-4144]. Destruction follows the disposition instructions in the contract security classification specification or equivalent and the guidance in the federal destruction regulation, and reproduction follows the federal reproduction guidance [@CL-CMP-3319; @CL-CMP-4147; @CL-CMP-4148]. A prime contractor's duties before releasing classified information to a subcontractor are on the [NISPOM subcontracting and information system security](cmp-nispom-subcontracting-and-information-system-security.md) card.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor keeps SECRET documents in a GSA-approved container inside a room with an intrusion detection system. At the end of the day the container is locked. The next day it wants to send one SECRET package by an overnight carrier that it picked on price.

Answer on storage: the end-of-day check still has to verify that all classified material and repositories are secured, because the alarm only supplements the storage [@CL-CMP-3192; @CL-CMP-3193].

Answer on shipping: the lower level does not remove the transmission rules. The contractor needs transmission procedures under the federal regulation, and the section prints commercial delivery for it only where the CSA has approved the contractor, the carrier holds the GSA overnight contract with computer tracking and the contractor has documented its package-protection procedures first [@CL-CMP-4141; @CL-CMP-4142; @CL-CMP-4145; @CL-CMP-4143]. If it fits none of that, a cleared employee designated as courier or hand carrier is the other route this section prints, and that employee is briefed first [@CL-CMP-4144].

## Never confuse

The written GCA authorization is the extra step for TOP SECRET transmission, not the whole transmission rule: procedures apply to every classification level, and SECRET or CONFIDENTIAL material by commercial delivery needs CSA approval and documented package procedures [@CL-CMP-4141; @CL-CMP-3318; @CL-CMP-4142; @CL-CMP-4143]. The printed section does not set out what the referenced federal transmission procedures contain, so this card does not establish them. This card covers what the contractor does to safeguard classified information in its custody. The contracting officer's own duties at each phase of a classified acquisition are on the [Safeguarding classified information within industry](../audit-retention/cmp-classified-information-safeguarding-duties.md) card.
