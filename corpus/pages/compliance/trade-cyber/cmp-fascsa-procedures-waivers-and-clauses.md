## Purpose

Shows what the contracting officer checks before award under a FASCSA order, how waivers work, and which solicitations and contracts carry the FASCSA provision and clauses.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CMP-2975] |
| Codified citation | FAR 4.2304 [@CL-CMP-2975]; FAR 4.2305 [@CL-CMP-2977]; FAR 4.2306 [@CL-CMP-2979] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-2975] |
| Applies to | Acquisitions subject to FASCSA orders, offerors that make a disclosure, and agencies that seek a waiver [@CL-CMP-2975; @CL-CMP-2976; @CL-CMP-2978] |

Unless the program office or requiring activity instructs the contracting officer otherwise, contracts awarded by civilian agencies are subject to DHS FASCSA orders and contracts awarded by the Department of Defense are subject to DoD FASCSA orders [@CL-CMP-2975]. If an offeror provides a disclosure under paragraph (e) of the provision at 52.204-29, the contracting officer shall engage with the program office or requiring activity to determine whether to pursue a waiver, if available [@CL-CMP-2976]. That engagement is to determine whether to pursue a waiver, if available, in accordance with the waiver section and agency procedures, or not award to that offeror [@CL-CMP-2940].

The contracting officer, in accordance with agency procedures and working with the program office or requiring activity, shall decide whether to pursue a waiver or to make award to an offeror that does not require a waiver [@CL-CMP-2941], in accordance with the procedures at 4.2304(f) [@CL-CMP-0146]. If a waiver is being pursued, the contracting officer may not make an award until written approval is obtained that the waiver has been granted [@CL-CMP-2978].

The contracting officer shall insert the provision at 52.204-29, Federal Acquisition Supply Chain Security Act Orders—Representation and Disclosures, in all solicitations, except for Federal Supply Schedules, Governmentwide acquisition contracts, and multi-agency contracts [@CL-CMP-2980]. The contracting officer shall also insert it in all solicitations for Federal Supply Schedules, Governmentwide acquisition contracts, and multi-agency contracts, if FASCSA orders are applied at the contract level [@CL-CMP-2943]. The clause at 52.204-30 shall be inserted in solicitations and contracts if the conditions specified at 4.2304(a)(1) apply, except for Federal Supply Schedules, Governmentwide acquisition contracts, and multi-agency contracts [@CL-CMP-2981]. In all Federal Supply Schedules, Governmentwide acquisition contracts, and multi-agency contracts where FASCSA orders are applied at the order level, the contracting officer shall insert the clause at 52.204-28 [@CL-CMP-2979].

## How it works

An executive agency required to comply with a FASCSA order may submit a request that the order or some of its provisions not apply to the agency or to specific actions of the agency or a specific class of acquisitions, among other listed matters [@CL-CMP-2944]. A request for waiver shall be submitted by the executive agency in writing to the official that issued the order, unless other instructions for submission are provided by the applicable FASCSA order [@CL-CMP-2977]. If a waiver is being pursued, the contracting officer may not make an award until written approval is obtained that the waiver has been granted [@CL-CMP-2978]. The contracting officer shall update a solicitation or contract if the program office or requiring activity determines it is necessary to modify the contract to incorporate FASCSA orders issued after the date of contract award [@CL-CMP-0147; @CL-CMP-2942]. Any such modification should take place within a reasonable amount of time, but no later than 6 months from the determination of the program office or requiring activity [@CL-CMP-2942]. The section also has rules on when a solicitation or contract is updated, documentation when a contract is not modified in time, partial waivers, interagency acquisitions, the alternate versions of the clause and the contents of a waiver request, which this card does not set out. What a FASCSA order is, and the agency prohibition it creates, is on [FASCSA definitions and orders](cmp-fascsa-definitions-and-orders.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An offeror discloses that a product in its offer is covered by an applicable order. The contracting officer and the program office decide to seek a waiver. The agency sends a written request to the issuing official, and the contracting officer holds the award until written approval of the waiver arrives.

## Never confuse

The contracting officer shall insert the provision at 52.204-29, Federal Acquisition Supply Chain Security Act Orders—Representation and Disclosures, in all solicitations, except for Federal Supply Schedules, Governmentwide acquisition contracts, and multi-agency contracts [@CL-CMP-2980]. The clause at 52.204-30, Federal Acquisition Supply Chain Security Act Orders—Prohibition, shall be inserted in solicitations and contracts if the conditions specified at 4.2304(a)(1) apply, with the same exception [@CL-CMP-2981]. In all Federal Supply Schedules, Governmentwide acquisition contracts, and multi-agency contracts where FASCSA orders are applied at the order level, the contracting officer shall insert the clause at 52.204-28 [@CL-CMP-2979].
