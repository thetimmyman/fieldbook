## Purpose

States the DoD policy that defense contractors and subcontractors shall be required to safeguard FCI and CUI on contractor information systems. Shows who identifies the CMMC Status for a procurement, how the Program reaches new solicitations, contracts and subcontracts, and who may waive its requirements.

The section opens by stating that protection of FCI and CUI on contractor information systems is of paramount importance to the DoD and can directly impact its ability to successfully conduct essential missions and functions [@CL-CMP-8487]. It is DoD policy that defense contractors and subcontractors shall be required to safeguard FCI and CUI that is processed, stored, or transmitted on contractor information systems by applying specified security requirements [@CL-CMP-8452].

In addition, defense contractors and subcontractors may be required to implement the additional safeguards defined in NIST SP 800-172 Feb2021, with DoD specified parameters, to meet CMMC Level 3 security requirements [@CL-CMP-8453]. Those additional requirements are necessary to protect CUI being processed, stored, or transmitted in contractor information systems when designated by a requirement for CMMC Status of Level 3 (DIBCAC), as defined by a DoD program manager or requiring activity [@CL-CMP-8454]. In general, the Department will identify a requirement for that status for solicitations and resulting contracts supporting its most critical programs and technologies [@CL-CMP-8455].

Program managers and requiring activities are responsible for identifying the CMMC Status that will apply to a procurement [@CL-CMP-8456]. Selection of the status will be based on factors including but not limited to these five: criticality of the associated mission capability; type of acquisition program or technology; threat of loss of the FCI or CUI to be shared or generated in relation to the effort [@CL-CMP-8457]; impacts from exploitation of information security deficiencies; and other relevant policies and factors, including Milestone Decision Authority guidance [@CL-CMP-8458].

In accordance with the implementation plan described in § 170.3, CMMC Program requirements will apply to new DoD solicitations and contracts, and they shall flow down to subcontractors who will process, store, or transmit FCI or CUI in performance of the subcontract, as described in § 170.23 [@CL-CMP-8459].

In very limited circumstances, and in accordance with all applicable policies, procedures, and requirements, a Service Acquisition Executive or Component Acquisition Executive in the DoD, or as delegated, may elect to waive inclusion of CMMC Program requirements in a solicitation or contract [@CL-CMP-8460]. In such cases, contractors and subcontractors will remain obligated to comply with all applicable cybersecurity and information security requirements [@CL-CMP-8461].

The CMMC Program does not alter any separately applicable requirements to protect FCI or CUI. These include requirements in accordance with 48 CFR 52.204-21 on basic safeguarding of covered contractor information systems, covered defense information requirements under 48 CFR 252.204-7012, and any other applicable information protection requirements [@CL-CMP-8462; @CL-CMP-8463]. The Program provides a means of verifying implementation of the security requirements in 48 CFR 52.204-21, NIST SP 800-171 R2 and NIST SP 800-172 Feb2021, as applicable [@CL-CMP-8464].

## How it works

Who decides. A DoD program manager or requiring activity identifies the CMMC Status for a procurement [@CL-CMP-8456]. A Service Acquisition Executive or Component Acquisition Executive in the DoD, or as delegated, may elect to waive inclusion of the Program requirements [@CL-CMP-8460].

Who carries the duty. The safeguarding duty falls on defense contractors and subcontractors [@CL-CMP-8452]. The Level 3 safeguards may be required of them [@CL-CMP-8453], and those additional requirements are necessary to protect CUI being processed, stored, or transmitted in contractor information systems, when designated by a requirement for CMMC Status of Level 3 (DIBCAC) as defined by a DoD program manager or requiring activity [@CL-CMP-8454]. CMMC Program requirements shall flow down to subcontractors who will process, store, or transmit FCI or CUI in performance of the subcontract, as described in § 170.23 [@CL-CMP-8459]. See [CMMC affirmation and subcontractor flowdown](cmp-cmmc-affirmation-and-flowdown.md).

Thresholds and timing. In accordance with the implementation plan described in § 170.3, CMMC Program requirements will apply to new DoD solicitations and contracts [@CL-CMP-8459]. See [CMMC Program scope and implementation phases](cmp-cmmc-program-scope-and-phases.md). In general, the Department will identify a requirement for a CMMC Status of Level 3 (DIBCAC) for solicitations and resulting contracts supporting its most critical programs and technologies [@CL-CMP-8455].

Exceptions. A waiver of inclusion of CMMC Program requirements may be elected in very limited circumstances and in accordance with all applicable policies, procedures, and requirements [@CL-CMP-8460]. On the clause prescription, see [the subpart scope and clause prescription](../regulatory-framework/cmp-cmmc-scope-and-clause-prescription.md). On Level 3, see [CMMC Level 3 DIBCAC assessment](cmp-cmmc-level-3-assessment.md) [@CL-CMP-8453].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A prime has 3 subcontractors. Only 1 of them will process, store, or transmit FCI or CUI in performance of its subcontract. The requirements shall flow down to that 1 subcontractor [@CL-CMP-8459]. The other 2 will not process, store, or transmit FCI or CUI in performance of their subcontracts.

Later, a Service Acquisition Executive in the DoD, in very limited circumstances and in accordance with all applicable policies, procedures, and requirements, elects to waive inclusion of CMMC Program requirements in the prime's solicitation [@CL-CMP-8460]. The prime and the subcontractors remain obligated to comply with all applicable cybersecurity and information security requirements [@CL-CMP-8461].

## Never confuse

After a waiver of CMMC Program requirements, contractors and subcontractors will remain obligated to comply with all applicable cybersecurity and information security requirements [@CL-CMP-8461]. The CMMC Program does not alter any separately applicable requirements to protect FCI or CUI [@CL-CMP-8462].

Defense contractors and subcontractors shall be required to safeguard FCI and CUI on contractor information systems by applying specified security requirements [@CL-CMP-8452]. In addition, they may be required to implement additional safeguards defined in NIST SP 800-172 Feb2021 to meet CMMC Level 3 security requirements [@CL-CMP-8453], which are necessary when designated by a requirement for CMMC Status of Level 3 (DIBCAC) [@CL-CMP-8454].
