## Purpose

Shows what an offeror represents about implementing the security controls and how SPRS risk assessments are considered when the contracting officer evaluates quotations and offers.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-CMP-2842] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 252.204-7008(c)(1) [@CL-CMP-2842]; DFARS 252.204-7024(c)(1) [@CL-CMP-2846] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-2842] |
| Applies to | Offerors, for covered contractor information systems not part of an information technology service or system operated on behalf of the Government [@CL-CMP-2842] |

By submission of the offer, the offeror represents, for covered contractor information systems that are not part of an information technology service or system operated on behalf of the Government, that it will implement the security requirements that the provision specifies [@CL-CMP-2842]. Those are the requirements specified by NIST Special Publication 800-171 [@CL-CMP-2845], as in effect at the time the solicitation is issued or as authorized by the contracting officer, not later than December 31, 2017 [@CL-CMP-2843].

For those systems, an authorized representative of the DoD CIO will adjudicate offeror requests to vary from NIST SP 800-171 requirements in writing prior to contract award, and any accepted variance shall be incorporated into the resulting contract [@CL-CMP-2842; @CL-CMP-2844].

## How it works

The contracting officer will consider SPRS risk assessments during the evaluation of quotations or offers received in response to the solicitation, and item risk will be considered to determine whether the procurement represents a high performance risk to the Government [@CL-CMP-2846]. Price risk will be considered in determining if a proposed price is consistent with historical prices paid for a product or a service or otherwise creates a risk to the Government [@CL-CMP-2847]. Supplier risk, including but not limited to quality and delivery, will be considered to assess the risk of unsuccessful performance and supply chain risk [@CL-CMP-2848]. The contracting officer may also consider any other available and relevant information when evaluating a quotation or an offer [@CL-CMP-2909]. The summary level scores of the NIST assessments are on the [DoD assessment notice and requirements](cmp-nist-sp-800-171-dod-assessment-requirements.md) card. The security controls themselves, and the cyber incident duties, are on the [Safeguarding covered defense information and cyber incident reporting](cmp-safeguarding-covered-defense-information.md) card.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An offeror answers a solicitation that names the version of the standard in effect on its issue date. By submitting the offer it represents that it will implement that version. It proposes to vary from one requirement and sends a written explanation to the contracting officer. The DoD CIO representative accepts the variance in writing before award, so the variance appears in the contract. During evaluation the contracting officer reviews the offeror's SPRS risk assessments for item, price and supplier risk.

## Never confuse

The representation and the evaluation differ: by submission of the offer, the offeror represents that it will implement the specified security requirements [@CL-CMP-2842], while SPRS risk assessments are considered by the contracting officer during the evaluation of quotations or offers received in response to the solicitation [@CL-CMP-2846].

## Company calibration

Whether the offer's representation matches the controls actually in place, any proposed variances, and who owns open control findings. [@Q-014]
