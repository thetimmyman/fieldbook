## Purpose

Shows what contractor employees who classify derivatively must do, the training they need, and how a contractor challenges a classification it thinks is wrong.

## The rule

| Applies to | Contractor employees authorized to make derivative classification decisions [@CL-CMP-3453] |

Contractor personnel make derivative classification decisions when they incorporate, paraphrase, restate or generate in new form information that is already classified [@CL-CMP-3451]. Contractors will provide employees with pertinent classification guidance to fulfill their derivative classification responsibilities [@CL-CMP-3452]. The rule then lists what authorized employees will do, and the list includes marking the face of each derivatively classified document with a classification authority block that includes, among other items, the employee's name and position or personal identifier [@CL-CMP-3453]. For information derivatively classified from multiple sources, the derivative classifier will carry forward the declassification date or event that corresponds to the longest period of classification among the sources and a listing of the source materials [@CL-CMP-3454]. Another listed duty is to be trained, in accordance with § 117.12(h), in the proper application of the derivative classification principles at least once every two years [@CL-CMP-3455].

A contractor addresses challenges to classification status with the GCA and requests remedy when, among the listed conditions, information is classified improperly or unnecessarily or current security considerations justify downgrading or upgrading [@CL-CMP-3456]. Another listed condition is that security classification guidance is not provided, improper or inadequate [@CL-CMP-3501]. If the GCA does not provide a remedy and the contractor still believes that corrective action is required, the contractor will make a formal written challenge to the GCA [@CL-CMP-3457]. That challenge will include a description sufficient to identify the issue, the reasons why the contractor thinks that corrective action is required and recommendations for appropriate corrective action [@CL-CMP-3458]. Until action on the challenge is completed, the contractor will safeguard the information as required for its assigned or proposed level of classification, whichever is higher [@CL-CMP-3459]. If the contractor receives no written answer from the GCA within 60 days, it will request assistance from the CSA [@CL-CMP-3460]. If it receives no response from the GCA within 120 days, it may appeal the challenge to the Interagency Security Classification Appeals Panel through ISOO [@CL-CMP-3461]. Starting a classification challenge is not, in any way, a basis for adverse action against the contractor by the USG [@CL-CMP-3462].

## How it works

A derivative classifier working from multiple sources carries forward the declassification date or event that matches the longest period of classification and a listing of the sources [@CL-CMP-3454]. Authorized employees are trained in the proper application of the derivative classification principles at least once every two years [@CL-CMP-3455]. The contractor addresses challenges to classification status with the GCA and requests remedy when, among the listed conditions, information is classified improperly or unnecessarily [@CL-CMP-3456]. If the GCA does not provide a remedy and the contractor still believes that corrective action is required, the contractor makes a formal written challenge to the GCA [@CL-CMP-3457]. If the contractor receives no written answer from the GCA within 60 days, it requests assistance from the CSA [@CL-CMP-3460]. If it receives no response from the GCA within 120 days, it may appeal the challenge to the Interagency Security Classification Appeals Panel through ISOO [@CL-CMP-3461]. The markings that are carried forward are set out on the [NISPOM marking requirements](cmp-nispom-marking-requirements.md) card. Storage, checks and transmission of classified material are on the [NISPOM safeguarding of classified information](cmp-nispom-safeguarding-classified-information.md) card, and general briefings are on the [NISPOM security briefings, visits and classified meetings](cmp-nispom-training-briefings-visits-and-meetings.md) card.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An employee writes a new report that restates classified information from two source documents. The employee last took derivative classification training three years ago. The contractor believes a classification guide is inadequate and raises it with the GCA, receives no remedy, files a formal written challenge, and hears nothing from the GCA for 70 days.

Answer: the report is a derivative classification, so the employee carries forward the longer declassification period and lists both sources. The training is overdue. After the written answer is missing for 60 days, the contractor asks the CSA for help, and it keeps protecting the information at the higher level until action is completed.

## Never confuse

Classification guidance is the exclusive responsibility of the GCA, and the final determination of the appropriate classification rests with that activity [@CL-CMP-3502]. Meanwhile the contractor protects the information at the higher of the assigned or proposed level until action is completed [@CL-CMP-3459]. The fact that a contractor has initiated a challenge will not, in any way, serve as a basis for adverse action against the contractor by the USG [@CL-CMP-3462]. Storage of classified material belongs to the [NISPOM safeguarding of classified information](cmp-nispom-safeguarding-classified-information.md) card.
