## Purpose

Shows what a prime contractor does before releasing classified information to a subcontractor and what the contractor and the CSA each do before a system may process classified information.

| Applies to | Prime contractors releasing classified information to subcontractors, and contractors certifying information systems that process classified information [@CL-CMP-3194; @CL-CMP-3197] |

Before a prime contractor may release or disclose classified information to a subcontractor, or cause classified information to be generated by a subcontractor [@CL-CMP-3320], a determination that access to classified information will be required and such access serves a legitimate USG requirement for the performance of a classified contract in accordance with § 117.9(a) must be made [@CL-CMP-3194]. Prime contractors are responsible for communicating the appropriate security requirements to all subcontractors, and a security requirements clause and a Contract Security Classification Specification, or equivalent, will be incorporated in the solicitation and in the subcontract [@CL-CMP-3195]. The subcontractor must possess an appropriate entity eligibility determination and a classified information safeguarding capability if possession of classified information will be required [@CL-CMP-3196]. The prime contractor will verify whether the prospective subcontractors have the appropriate entity eligibility determination and also a classified information safeguarding capability, if a subcontract requirement, and this determination can be made if there is an existing contractual relationship between the parties involving classified information of the same or higher category, and must be verified by accessing the CSA-designated database or by contacting the CSA [@CL-CMP-4139; @CL-CMP-4140]. If access to classified information will not be required in the pre-award phase, prospective subcontractors are not required to possess an entity eligibility determination to receive or bid on the solicitation [@CL-CMP-3321].

The contractor will certify to the CSA that the security program for information systems to process classified information addresses management, operation, and technical controls in accordance with CSA-provided guidelines [@CL-CMP-3197]. Contractors that are or will be processing classified information on an information system will appoint an employee ISSM [@CL-CMP-3322]. The contractor will review and certify to the CSA that all systems have the appropriate protection measures in place [@CL-CMP-3198]. The CSA must authorize the system before the contractor can use the system to process classified information [@CL-CMP-3199].

## How it works

Before a prime contractor may release or disclose classified information to a subcontractor, or cause classified information to be generated by a subcontractor, a determination that access to classified information will be required and serves a legitimate USG requirement for the performance of a classified contract in accordance with § 117.9(a) must be made [@CL-CMP-3320; @CL-CMP-3194], and a security requirements clause and a Contract Security Classification Specification, or equivalent, will be incorporated in the solicitation and in the subcontract [@CL-CMP-3195]. The subcontractor must possess an appropriate entity eligibility determination and a classified information safeguarding capability if possession of classified information will be required [@CL-CMP-3196], and the prime contractor will verify whether the prospective subcontractors have the appropriate entity eligibility determination and also a classified information safeguarding capability, if a subcontract requirement; this determination can be made if there is an existing contractual relationship between the parties involving classified information of the same or higher category, and must be verified by accessing the CSA-designated database or by contacting the CSA [@CL-CMP-4139; @CL-CMP-4140]. If access to classified information will not be required in the pre-award phase, prospective subcontractors are not required to possess an entity eligibility determination to receive or bid on the solicitation [@CL-CMP-3321]. For an information system, contractors that are or will be processing classified information on an information system will appoint an employee ISSM, certify to the CSA that the security program for information systems to process classified information addresses management, operation, and technical controls in accordance with CSA-provided guidelines, and review and certify to the CSA that all systems have the appropriate protection measures in place [@CL-CMP-3322; @CL-CMP-3197; @CL-CMP-3198]. The CSA must authorize the system before the contractor can use the system to process classified information [@CL-CMP-3199]. For entity eligibility determinations, see [NISPOM entity eligibility determination (facility clearance)](cmp-nispom-entity-eligibility-determination.md); for storage, see [NISPOM safeguarding of classified information](cmp-nispom-safeguarding-classified-information.md); and for CSA authorizing officials, see [NISPOM purpose, applicability and responsibilities](cmp-nispom-purpose-applicability-and-responsibilities.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A prime contractor plans to send classified drawings to a supplier under an 800,000 subcontract. The supplier also wants to process the drawings on its own computer system.

Answer: before releasing the drawings the prime makes the access determination, verifies through the CSA-designated database or the CSA that the supplier holds the entity eligibility determination and safeguarding capability, and puts the clause and the classification specification in the solicitation and the subcontract, and the supplier's system may process the drawings only after the CSA authorizes it.

## Never confuse

Use follows authorization, not the other way round: the CSA must authorize an information system before the contractor can use the system to process classified information [@CL-CMP-3199]. For covered defense information on unclassified systems, see [Safeguarding covered defense information](cmp-safeguarding-covered-defense-information.md); for the contracting officer's duties for the classification specification, see [Safeguarding classified information within industry](../audit-retention/cmp-classified-information-safeguarding-duties.md); and for contractor security officials and the ISSM appointment in the procedures section, see [NISPOM security officials, insider threat program and self-inspection](cmp-nispom-general-procedures-and-security-officials.md).
