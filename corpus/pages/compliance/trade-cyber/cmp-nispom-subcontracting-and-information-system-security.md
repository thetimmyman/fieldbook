## Purpose

Shows what a prime contractor does before releasing classified information to a subcontractor and what the contractor and the CSA each do before a system may process classified information.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Not established. |
| Codified citation | Not established. |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Not established. |
| Applies to | Prime contractors releasing classified information to subcontractors, and contractors certifying information systems that process classified information [@CL-CMP-3194; @CL-CMP-3197] |

This card rests on the NISPOM, the rule for work involving classified information, and not on the FAR. The subcontracting paragraph's access determination comes before a prime contractor may release or disclose classified information to a subcontractor, or cause classified information to be generated by a subcontractor [@CL-CMP-3320]. A determination that access to classified information will be required and serves a legitimate USG requirement for the performance of a classified contract must be made [@CL-CMP-3194]. Prime contractors are responsible for communicating the appropriate security requirements to all subcontractors, and a security requirements clause and a Contract Security Classification Specification, or equivalent, will be incorporated in the solicitation and in the subcontract [@CL-CMP-3195]. The subcontractor must possess an appropriate entity eligibility determination and a classified information safeguarding capability if possession of classified information will be required [@CL-CMP-3196]. The prime contractor will verify whether the prospective subcontractors have the appropriate entity eligibility determination and also a classified information safeguarding capability, if a subcontract requirement, and the determination must be verified by accessing the CSA-designated database or by contacting the CSA, though it can be made where an existing contractual relationship between the parties involves classified information of the same or higher category [@CL-CMP-4139; @CL-CMP-4140]. If access to classified information will not be required in the pre-award phase, prospective subcontractors are not required to possess an entity eligibility determination to receive or bid on the solicitation [@CL-CMP-3321].

The contractor will certify to the CSA that the security program for information systems to process classified information addresses management, operation, and technical controls in accordance with CSA-provided guidelines [@CL-CMP-3197]. Contractors that are or will be processing classified information on an information system will appoint an employee ISSM [@CL-CMP-3322]. The contractor will review and certify to the CSA that all systems have the appropriate protection measures in place [@CL-CMP-3198]. The CSA must authorize the system before the contractor can use the system to process classified information [@CL-CMP-3199].

## How it works

A prime contractor makes the access determination before it releases classified information to a subcontractor [@CL-CMP-3320; @CL-CMP-3194], then passes the security requirements down through the clause and the classification specification in the solicitation and the subcontract [@CL-CMP-3195]. The subcontractor must hold the matching eligibility and safeguarding capability if it will possess classified information [@CL-CMP-3196], so the prime contractor verifies whether it holds both, by accessing the CSA-designated database or by contacting the CSA [@CL-CMP-4139; @CL-CMP-4140]. A prospective subcontractor that needs no access before award need not hold an entity eligibility determination to receive or bid on the solicitation [@CL-CMP-3196; @CL-CMP-3321]. For an information system, the contractor appoints an ISSM and certifies its security program and its systems to the CSA [@CL-CMP-3322; @CL-CMP-3197; @CL-CMP-3198]. The system may process classified information only after the CSA authorizes it [@CL-CMP-3199]. The subcontractor's eligibility is explained on the [NISPOM entity eligibility determination (facility clearance)](cmp-nispom-entity-eligibility-determination.md) card, storage on the [NISPOM safeguarding of classified information](cmp-nispom-safeguarding-classified-information.md) card, and the CSA's authorizing officials on the [NISPOM purpose, applicability and responsibilities](cmp-nispom-purpose-applicability-and-responsibilities.md) card.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A prime contractor plans to send classified drawings to a supplier under an 800,000 subcontract. The supplier also wants to process the drawings on its own computer system.

Answer: before releasing the drawings the prime makes the access determination, verifies through the CSA-designated database or the CSA that the supplier holds the entity eligibility determination and safeguarding capability, and puts the clause and the classification specification in the solicitation and the subcontract, and the supplier's system may process the drawings only after the CSA authorizes it.

## Never confuse

An information system authorization for classified work is not the cyber rule for covered defense information, which is on the [Safeguarding covered defense information](cmp-safeguarding-covered-defense-information.md) card. The contracting officer's own duties for the classification specification are on the [Safeguarding classified information within industry](../audit-retention/cmp-classified-information-safeguarding-duties.md) card. The wider set of contractor security officials, with the ISSM appointment in the procedures section, is on the [NISPOM security officials, insider threat program and self-inspection](cmp-nispom-general-procedures-and-security-officials.md) card.
