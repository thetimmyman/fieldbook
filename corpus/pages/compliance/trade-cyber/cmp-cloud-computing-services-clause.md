## Purpose

Shows what the contractor must do when it uses cloud computing in a defense contract: approval, safeguards, data location, data use limits, incident reporting and follow-on duties, third party requests and flowdown.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-CMP-3989] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 252.239-7010 [@CL-CMP-3989] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CMP-3989] |
| Applies to | Contracts with the clause, when cloud computing is used to provide information technology services in the performance of the contract [@CL-CMP-3989] |

The clause requirements apply when cloud computing is used to provide information technology services in the performance of the contract [@CL-CMP-3989]. The approval step is conditioned on the contractor having indicated in its offer that it does not anticipate the use of cloud computing services, and on events after award [@CL-CMP-3990]. In that case, if the contractor proposes after award to use cloud computing services in performance of the contract, it shall obtain approval from the Contracting Officer before using them [@CL-CMP-3990; @CL-CMP-3991]. The contractor shall implement and maintain administrative, technical, and physical safeguards and controls with the security level and services required in accordance with the Cloud Computing Security Requirements Guide [@CL-CMP-3992]. The version of that guide that applies is the one in effect at the time the solicitation is issued or as authorized by the Contracting Officer [@CL-CMP-4039]. That safeguards requirement applies unless the Contracting Officer notifies the contractor that the DoD Chief Information Officer has waived it [@CL-CMP-3993].

The contractor shall maintain within the United States or outlying areas all Government data that is not physically located on DoD premises, unless it receives written notification from the Contracting Officer to use another location [@CL-CMP-3994]. The contractor shall not access, use, or disclose Government data unless the contract or an order issued under it specifically authorizes that [@CL-CMP-3995]. If the contract or an order authorizes access to, or use or disclosure of, Government data, that access, use, or disclosure shall only be for purposes specified in the contract or order [@CL-CMP-4046]. These prohibitions and obligations survive the expiration or termination of the contract [@CL-CMP-4047]. The contractor shall use Government-related data only to manage the operational environment that supports the Government data, and for no other purpose unless the Contracting Officer gives prior written approval [@CL-CMP-3996].

The contractor shall report all cyber incidents related to the cloud computing service provided under the contract, and reports are submitted to DoD through its incident reporting website [@CL-CMP-3997]. When it discovers that a cyber incident has occurred, it shall preserve and protect images of all known affected information systems identified in the cyber incident report, and all relevant monitoring and packet capture data, for at least 90 days [@CL-CMP-3998]. That period runs from the submission of the cyber incident report, to allow DoD to request the media or decline interest [@CL-CMP-4040]. A contractor or subcontractor that discovers and isolates malicious software in connection with a reported cyber incident shall submit it in accordance with instructions provided by the Contracting Officer [@CL-CMP-4041]. Upon request by DoD, the contractor shall provide DoD with access to additional information or equipment that is necessary to conduct a forensic analysis [@CL-CMP-4042]. If DoD elects to conduct a damage assessment, the Contracting Officer will request that the contractor provide all of the damage assessment information gathered under the media preservation paragraph [@CL-CMP-4043].

The contractor shall notify the Contracting Officer promptly of any requests from a third party for access to Government data or Government-related data, including any warrants, seizures, or subpoenas it receives, including those from another Federal, State, or local agency [@CL-CMP-4044]. It shall also cooperate with the Contracting Officer to take all measures to protect Government data and Government-related data from any unauthorized disclosure [@CL-CMP-4045]. The contractor shall include the clause in all subcontracts that involve or may involve cloud services, including subcontracts for commercial services [@CL-CMP-3999].

## How it works

The clause builds its duties in layers. The approval step is written for a contractor whose offer said it did not anticipate cloud use, and it concerns a contractor that later proposes cloud use after award [@CL-CMP-3990; @CL-CMP-3991]. Data location and data use limits then control where Government data sits and who may touch it [@CL-CMP-3994; @CL-CMP-3995; @CL-CMP-3996]. Incident reporting and the preservation duty follow from a discovered cyber incident, and the clause then adds duties to hand over malicious software, give DoD forensic access, supply damage assessment information on request, and report third party access requests [@CL-CMP-3997; @CL-CMP-3998; @CL-CMP-4041; @CL-CMP-4042; @CL-CMP-4043; @CL-CMP-4044]. The same clause then passes down to every subcontract that involves or may involve cloud services [@CL-CMP-3999]. The printed text of the clause also has paragraphs on records management, facility access and spillage that this card does not restate.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor's offer says it does not expect to use cloud services. After award it wants to host a case file tool on a commercial cloud service. It asks the Contracting Officer for approval first, then uses the service only after approval. Months later it discovers a cyber incident on that service. It reports the incident and keeps images of the affected systems for 100 days from the day it submits the incident report. It also checks that the hosting subcontract carries the clause. The answer is that each step follows the clause.

## Never confuse

This clause covers cloud computing services. The general safeguarding clause for covered defense information has its own reporting duties; see [Safeguarding covered defense information and cyber incident reporting](cmp-safeguarding-covered-defense-information.md). Limits on how incident information may be used are on [Cyber incident information and litigation support limits](cmp-cyber-incident-information-and-litigation-support-limits.md). Government data and Government-related data are also different things, and the clause limits each one separately [@CL-CMP-3995; @CL-CMP-3996]. For supply chain duties see [Supply chain risk and telecommunications security equipment](cmp-supply-chain-risk-and-telecommunications-security-equipment.md), and for the CMMC level notice see [CMMC level notice and protection against compromising emanations](cmp-cmmc-notice-and-compromising-emanations.md).
