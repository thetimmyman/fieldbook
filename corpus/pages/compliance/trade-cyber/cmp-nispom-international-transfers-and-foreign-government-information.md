## Purpose

Shows when a contractor may disclose classified information to foreign interests, how export authorization differs from disclosure authorization, and the conditions for handling foreign government information and international transfers.

| Applies to | Exports that involve classified information [@CL-CMP-3504] |

This card rests on the NISPOM, the rule for work involving classified information, and not on the FAR. The transfer of articles, services and related data to a foreign person, within or outside the United States, or the movement of such material or information to any destination outside of the legal jurisdiction of the United States constitutes an export [@CL-CMP-3470]. Contractors will only disclose non-public USG information to foreign persons in accordance with specified requirements of the contract, and in the absence of any specified requirements the contractor will not disclose it [@CL-CMP-3471].

The listed assurances for disclosure and export apply to disclosure and export of classified information by a contractor that an appropriate USG disclosure official has authorized [@CL-CMP-3479]. One of them, the international agreements item, is that contractors may not disclose classified information until agreements are signed by the participating government and disclosure guidance and security arrangements are established [@CL-CMP-3472]. That item adds that the export of technical data pursuant to agreements signed by the participating government may be exempt by approval of the Department of State or the Department of Commerce [@CL-CMP-3473]. The paragraph on direct commercial arrangements includes a case where a proposed disclosure is in support of a foreign government requirement, and the contractor should then consult with U.S. in-country officials, normally the U.S. Security Assistance/Armaments Cooperation Office or Commercial Counselor [@CL-CMP-3505]. In that same paragraph, before a contractor makes a proposal to a foreign interest that involves the eventual disclosure of U.S. classified information, the contractor must obtain appropriate government disclosure authorization [@CL-CMP-3474]. Disclosure authorization does not equate with authorization for export, and export authorization must be obtained from the appropriate regulatory body [@CL-CMP-3475].

For FGI, the contractor will not disclose FGI to nationals of a third country or to any other third party, or use it for any purpose other than that for which it was provided, without the prior written consent of the originating foreign government [@CL-CMP-3476]. Under that paragraph, requests for other uses or further disclosure go to the GCA for U.S. contracts and through the CSA for direct commercial contracts, and approval of the request by the foreign government does not eliminate the requirement to obtain an export authorization [@CL-CMP-3507]. For transfers, the requirements of the paragraph on international transfers of classified material do not apply to the transmission of classified material to USG activities outside the United States [@CL-CMP-3477]. All international transfers of classified material must take place through channels approved by both governments [@CL-CMP-3506]. Written transmission instructions must be prepared for all international transfers of classified material, by the contractor for direct commercial arrangements and by the GCA for government arrangements [@CL-CMP-3478]. A requirement to prepare a transportation plan will be included in each arrangement that involves the international transfer of classified material as freight, and the plan will describe requirements for the secure shipment of the material from the point of origin to the ultimate destination [@CL-CMP-3508]. This card does not restate the other transfer conditions in that section.

## How it works

Moving articles, services or data to a foreign person or out of US jurisdiction is an export [@CL-CMP-3470], and the section applies to those exports that involve classified information [@CL-CMP-3504]. Under the paragraph on direct commercial arrangements, the contractor needs government disclosure authorization before it makes a proposal that involves the eventual disclosure of classified information to a foreign interest, and export authorization is a separate step [@CL-CMP-3474; @CL-CMP-3475]. Disclosure to a foreign person also follows the contract's specified requirements [@CL-CMP-3471]. Foreign government information cannot go to third parties or other uses without the originating government's prior written consent [@CL-CMP-3476]. An international transfer of classified material uses channels both governments approve and carries written transmission instructions [@CL-CMP-3506; @CL-CMP-3478], and a freight transfer also needs a transportation plan [@CL-CMP-3508].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor wants to propose a classified subsystem to a foreign interest. It first obtains government disclosure authorization. It separately holds foreign government information, and a firm from a third country asks to see it.

Answer: the proposal needs disclosure authorization first, and a separate export authorization must come from the appropriate regulatory body. The contractor does not show the foreign government information to the third-country firm without the originating government's prior written consent.

## Never confuse

Disclosure authorization is not export authorization [@CL-CMP-3475]. Transfer channels agreed by two governments are also not the same as shipping to a USG activity abroad, which the paragraph on international transfers leaves out [@CL-CMP-3477]. The rules for foreign national visits and conferences are on the [NISPOM international visits and foreign national access](cmp-nispom-international-visits-and-foreign-national-access.md) card, the marking of documents that contain foreign government information is on the [NISPOM marking requirements](cmp-nispom-marking-requirements.md) card, the meaning of foreign interest is on the [NISPOM acronyms, definitions and cognizant security office information](cmp-nispom-definitions-and-cognizant-security-offices.md) card, and the rules for passing classified information to a subcontractor are on the [NISPOM subcontracting and information system security](cmp-nispom-subcontracting-and-information-system-security.md) card.
