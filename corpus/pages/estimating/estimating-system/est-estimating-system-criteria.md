## Purpose

Shows what an acceptable estimating system must do, when the requirements apply, and how the Government handles disclosure and material weaknesses.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-EST-0123] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 252.215-7002 [@CL-EST-0123] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-EST-0123] |
| Applies to | The general obligation in paragraph (b) has no award threshold [@CL-EST-0123]; paragraphs (d) and (e) apply to a large business that meets a stated award threshold [@CL-EST-0259; @CL-EST-0124; @CL-EST-0125] |

The contractor must establish, maintain and comply with an acceptable estimating system, meaning one that meets the system criteria and produces verifiable, supportable, documented and timely estimates suitable for negotiating fair and reasonable prices [@CL-EST-0123; @CL-EST-0121]. That general obligation is stated without any award threshold [@CL-EST-0123]. The threshold test limits only paragraphs (d) and (e) of the clause, which apply if the contractor is a large business and meets one of two conditions [@CL-EST-0259]. The first condition is receiving $50 million or more in prior-year Department of Defense prime contracts or subcontracts for which certified cost or pricing data were required [@CL-EST-0124].

## How it works

The second condition is receiving $10 million or more but less than $50 million in such prior-year contracts, in which case paragraphs (d) and (e) attach only if the contracting officer also notifies the contractor in writing that they apply [@CL-EST-0125]. The contractor discloses the system in writing to the administrative contracting officer, and the disclosure is acceptable when it describes current practice in enough detail for an informed judgment [@CL-EST-0126; @CL-EST-0127]. The contractor then follows the disclosed system, reports significant changes on a timely basis, and keeps clear responsibility for preparation, review and approval of estimates [@CL-EST-0128; @CL-EST-0129]. The system also documents data sources and estimating rationale and provides internal review that compares projected results with actuals [@CL-EST-0130; @CL-EST-0131]. After a written initial determination of material weaknesses the contractor responds within 30 days, and within 45 days of receiving a final determination it corrects them or submits an acceptable corrective action plan [@CL-EST-0132; @CL-EST-0133]. If the contracting officer finally disapproves the system and the contract includes the Contractor Business Systems clause, payments are withheld under that clause [@CL-EST-0134].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A large business received 60,000,000 in prior-year Department of Defense prime contracts requiring certified data, so paragraphs (d) and (e) of the clause apply without further notice. The general obligation to maintain an acceptable estimating system does not depend on that threshold. Its disclosure describes estimating practice but omits who approves rates, and the contracting officer issues an initial determination. The contractor answers the initial determination within 30 days; if the contracting officer then issues a final determination of material weaknesses, the contractor corrects them or submits a corrective action plan within 45 days of receiving it, and withholding follows a final disapproval only if the contract includes the Contractor Business Systems clause.

## Never confuse

An estimating system is not the accounting system: the estimating system covers budgeting and planning controls and the generation of proposal estimates, and it must still integrate with the contractor's other management systems [@CL-EST-0122; @CL-EST-0121].
