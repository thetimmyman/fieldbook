## Purpose

Shows what an acceptable estimating system must do, when the requirements apply, and how the Government handles disclosure and material weaknesses.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-EST-0123] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 252.215-7002 [@CL-EST-0123] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-EST-0123] |
| Applies to | The general obligation in paragraph (b) has no award threshold [@CL-EST-0123]; paragraphs (d) and (e) apply to a large business that meets one of two stated conditions [@CL-EST-0259; @CL-EST-0124; @CL-EST-0125] |

The contractor must establish, maintain and comply with an acceptable estimating system, meaning one that complies with the system criteria and provides for a system that, among other requirements, is maintained, reliable and consistently applied and produces verifiable, supportable, documented and timely cost estimates that are an acceptable basis for negotiation of fair and reasonable prices [@CL-EST-0123; @CL-EST-9440; @CL-EST-0121]. That general obligation is stated without any award threshold [@CL-EST-0123]. The applicability paragraph is stated for paragraphs (d) and (e) of the clause, which apply if the contractor is a large business and meets one of two conditions [@CL-EST-0259]. The first condition is receiving $50 million or more in prior-year Department of Defense prime contracts or subcontracts for which certified cost or pricing data were required [@CL-EST-0124].

## How it works

The second condition has two parts, both in the fiscal year preceding award: receiving $10 million or more but less than $50 million in such prior-year contracts, and being notified in writing by the contracting officer that paragraphs (d) and (e) apply [@CL-EST-9437; @CL-EST-9436; @CL-EST-0125]. The contractor discloses the system in writing to the administrative contracting officer, and the disclosure is acceptable when the documentation accurately describes the policies, procedures and practices the contractor currently uses in preparing cost proposals and provides sufficient detail for the Government to reasonably make an informed judgment on the acceptability of its estimating practices [@CL-EST-0126; @CL-EST-0127; @CL-EST-9442]. If the contractor wishes the Government to protect the data and information as privileged or confidential, it must mark the documents with the appropriate legends before submission [@CL-EST-0246]. The contractor then complies with its disclosed system and discloses significant changes to the administrative contracting officer on a timely basis [@CL-EST-9443; @CL-EST-0128]. An acceptable estimating system shall accomplish listed functions [@CL-EST-9444], among them establishing clear responsibility for preparation, review and approval of cost estimates and budgets [@CL-EST-0129], identifying and documenting the sources of data and the estimating methods and rationale [@CL-EST-0130], and providing for internal review of, and accountability for, the acceptability of the estimating system, including the budgetary data supporting indirect cost estimates, comparisons of projected results to actual results, and an analysis of any differences [@CL-EST-9602; @CL-EST-0131]. After a written initial determination of material weaknesses the contractor responds within 30 days [@CL-EST-0132]. If the contractor receives the contracting officer's final determination of material weaknesses, it shall, within 45 days of receipt, either correct them or submit an acceptable corrective action plan showing milestones and actions to eliminate them [@CL-EST-9604; @CL-EST-0133]. If the contracting officer makes a final determination to disapprove the system and the contract includes the clause at 252.242-7005, Contractor Business Systems, the contracting officer will withhold payments in accordance with that clause [@CL-EST-9603; @CL-EST-0134].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A large business received 60,000,000 in prior-year Department of Defense prime contracts requiring certified data, so paragraphs (d) and (e) of the clause apply without further notice. The general obligation to maintain an acceptable estimating system does not depend on that threshold. Its disclosure describes estimating practice but omits who approves rates, and the contracting officer issues an initial determination. The contractor answers the initial determination within 30 days; if the contracting officer then issues a final determination of material weaknesses, the contractor corrects them or submits a corrective action plan within 45 days of receiving it, and withholding follows a final disapproval only if the contract includes the Contractor Business Systems clause.

## Never confuse

An estimating system is not the accounting system: the estimating system covers budgeting and planning controls and the generation of proposal estimates, and an acceptable estimating system must be consistent with and integrated with the contractor's related management systems [@CL-EST-0122; @CL-EST-9441].
