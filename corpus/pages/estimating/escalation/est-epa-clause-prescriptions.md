## Purpose

Shows when the contracting officer is told to insert each of the three economic price adjustment clauses, and how the ceiling on increases can be raised.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-EST-1164] |
| Codified citation | FAR 16.203-4 [@CL-EST-1164; @CL-EST-1167; @CL-EST-1171] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-EST-1164] |
| Applies to | Solicitations and contracts that meet the listed conditions for each clause [@CL-EST-1164; @CL-EST-1167; @CL-EST-1172] |

For the standard supplies clause, the contracting officer shall, when contracting by negotiation, insert the clause at FAR 52.216-2 or an agency-prescribed clause as authorized in paragraph (a)(2) in solicitations and contracts when all of the listed conditions apply [@CL-EST-1164; @CL-EST-1207]. The three conditions are a contemplated fixed-price contract, a requirement for standard supplies that have an established catalog or market price, and the determination specified in 16.203-3 [@CL-EST-1165]. For the semistandard supplies clause, the same duty applies to the clause at FAR 52.216-3 when all of its listed conditions apply [@CL-EST-1167; @CL-EST-1208]. The conditions are a contemplated fixed price contract [@CL-EST-1168], a requirement for semistandard supplies whose prices can be reasonably related to the prices of nearly equivalent standard supplies with an established catalog or market price, and the determination specified in 16.203-3 [@CL-EST-1169].

For the labor and material clause, the contracting officer shall, when contracting by negotiation, insert a clause substantially the same as the clause at FAR 52.216-4 or an agency-prescribed clause as authorized in paragraph (c)(2), in solicitations and contracts when all of the listed conditions apply [@CL-EST-1171; @CL-EST-1172; @CL-EST-1206]. The first two conditions are a contemplated fixed-price contract and no major element of design engineering or development work [@CL-EST-1172]. The other two are that one or more identifiable labor or material cost factors are subject to change and that the determination specified in 16.203-3 has been made [@CL-EST-1173]. Each of the three clauses carries a 10 percent limit on aggregate increases, which the contracting officer may modify by increasing it upon approval by the chief of the contracting office [@CL-EST-1166; @CL-EST-1170; @CL-EST-1174].

## How it works

The contracting officer first checks that the contract is negotiated and fixed-price, then tests the listed conditions for the clause that fits the supplies or the labor and material exposure [@CL-EST-1164; @CL-EST-1167; @CL-EST-1172]. The determination specified in 16.203-3 is a listed condition for every one of the three, so the duty to insert a clause does not arise without it [@CL-EST-1165; @CL-EST-1169; @CL-EST-1173]. The determination itself is on [Determination before economic price adjustment, and the fixed-price incentive pointer](est-epa-limitation-and-fpi-pointer.md). Raising the 10 percent limit is not the contracting officer's decision alone, because the contracting officer may increase it upon approval by the chief of the contracting office [@CL-EST-1166; @CL-EST-1170; @CL-EST-1174]. The clause terms themselves are on the linked clause cards and are not restated here, and the further DoD conditions are on [DoD rules on economic price adjustment clauses](est-dod-epa-clause-rules.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contracting officer negotiates a 24-month fixed-price order for catalog-priced parts. The requirement is for standard supplies with an established catalog price, and the determination has been made, so every listed condition holds and the standard supplies clause goes in. The base unit price is 100, so the ceiling on aggregate increases is 10 per unit. If the market looks more volatile, the contracting officer can ask the chief of the contracting office to approve a higher limit, say 12 percent.

## Never confuse

The prescription is not the clause. This card says when a clause is inserted and who approves a higher ceiling [@CL-EST-1166], while the clause cards say how the price then moves. The three types of adjustment are on [Fixed-price contracts with economic price adjustment: types and use](est-fixed-price-epa-types-and-use.md). See [Economic Price Adjustment, Standard Supplies clause](est-epa-standard-supplies.md), [Economic Price Adjustment, Semistandard Supplies clause](est-epa-semistandard-supplies.md) and [Economic Price Adjustment, Labor and Material](../../contract-finance/billing-payment/ctf-economic-price-adjustment-labor-and-material.md). The auditor's view is on [Economic price adjustments in proposals and proposal audits](est-economic-price-adjustments.md).
