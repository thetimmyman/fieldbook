## Purpose

Explains how the unit price moves with the contractor's established price under the DoD clause for basic mill products, and the conditions and deadlines on each move.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-EST-1185] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 252.216-7000 and DFARS 252.216-7007 [@CL-EST-1185; @CL-EST-1184] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-EST-1185] |
| Applies to | Fixed-price supply contracts for these mill products that include the clause [@CL-EST-1177] |

By its offer, the offeror represents that the unit price stated for the identified item is not in excess of its established price in effect on the date set for opening of bids, or on the contract date if the contract is to be negotiated, for like quantities of the same item [@CL-EST-1184].

The contractor shall promptly notify the Contracting Officer of the amount and effective date of each decrease in any established price, and each corresponding contract unit price shall be decreased by the same percentage that the established price is decreased [@CL-EST-1185]. If the established price is increased after the date set for opening of bids, or after the contract date for a negotiated contract, the unit price is to be increased by the same percentage upon the contractor's written request to the Contracting Officer, and the contract modified accordingly, provided the conditions listed in the clause are met [@CL-EST-1186; @CL-EST-1200].

## How it works

The conditions listed after the word provided include a limit of 10 percent on the aggregate of increases in any contract unit price [@CL-EST-1187; @CL-EST-1200], an effective date that depends on the written request arriving within ten days of the change [@CL-EST-1188], and no modification until the Contracting Officer has verified the increase [@CL-EST-1189]. Within 30 days after receipt of the written request, the Contracting Officer may cancel, without liability to either party, any portion of the contract affected by the requested increase and not delivered, subject to listed exceptions [@CL-EST-1190]. The DFARS prescription adds that the 10 percent figure in the clause shall not be exceeded unless approval is obtained at a level above the contracting officer [@CL-EST-1178]. The prescriptions for this clause are on [DoD rules on economic price adjustment clauses](est-dod-epa-clause-rules.md). The printed clause text sets no day count for the decrease notice beyond "promptly" [@CL-EST-1185].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contract unit price for steel bar is 200. The contractor's catalog price rises 4 percent, and its written request reaches the Contracting Officer six days after the change. Once the Contracting Officer has verified the increase, the unit price is modified to 208, with the increase effective from the date of the catalog change. If the request had arrived 20 days after the change, the new price would apply only from the date of receipt. Later the catalog price falls 2 percent, and the contractor notifies promptly so the unit price falls by the same percentage.

## Never confuse

This clause is not a general escalation clause for cost. It moves with the contractor's own established price for the item, and a decrease follows the contractor's notice while an increase follows the contractor's written request [@CL-EST-1185; @CL-EST-1186]. The steel and foreign-government clauses work differently and are on [Economic price adjustment for nonstandard steel items and foreign-government controlled wages or prices](est-epa-nonstandard-steel-and-foreign-government.md). The offeror's representation is a separate provision that goes in the solicitation, and it states the price position at bid opening or contract date [@CL-EST-1184]. The standard supplies clause under the FAR works on the same principle, as shown on [Economic Price Adjustment, Standard Supplies clause](est-epa-standard-supplies.md), and the auditor's view is on [Economic price adjustments in proposals and proposal audits](est-economic-price-adjustments.md).
