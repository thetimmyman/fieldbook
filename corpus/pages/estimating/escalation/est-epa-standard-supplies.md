## Purpose

Explains how the Economic Price Adjustment clause for standard supplies moves a contract unit price when the contractor's established price moves, and where the upward movement stops.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-EST-0750] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.216-2 [@CL-EST-0750] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.216-2 [@CL-EST-0003; @CL-EST-0006; @CL-EST-0007] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-EST-0750] |
| Applies to | Schedule line items covered by the contractor's warranty on its applicable established price [@CL-EST-0750] |

The contractor warrants that the unit price stated in the schedule is not in excess of its applicable established price in effect on the contract date for like quantities of the same item [@CL-EST-0750]. An established price is an established catalog or market price for a commercial product sold in substantial quantities to the general public, taken net of any standard trade discounts offered by the Contractor [@CL-EST-0751]. The term unit price excludes any part of the price directly resulting from requirements for preservation, packaging, or packing beyond standard commercial practice [@CL-EST-0857].

A decrease passes through on its own: the contractor promptly notifies the Contracting Officer of the amount and effective date, and each corresponding unit price falls by the same percentage as the established price [@CL-EST-0753]. If the established price is increased after the contract date, the corresponding contract unit price is increased, upon the contractor's written request, by the same percentage, subject to the limitations that follow [@CL-EST-0858; @CL-EST-1040]. That increase is capped, so the aggregate of the increases in any contract unit price cannot exceed 10 percent of the original contract unit price [@CL-EST-0752].

## How it works

An increase needs the contractor's written request [@CL-EST-0858]. It takes effect on the effective date of the established price increase when the Contracting Officer receives that request within 10 days thereafter, or, if the request arrives later, on the date the Contracting Officer receives it [@CL-EST-0754]. No modification increasing a unit price is executed until the Contracting Officer verifies the increase in the established price [@CL-EST-0756]. Within 30 days after receiving the request, the Contracting Officer may cancel, without liability to either party, any undelivered portion of the affected contract items [@CL-EST-0755]. The cap runs on the aggregate of increases in a unit price, not on each adjustment taken alone [@CL-EST-0752].

Under the RFO text, the DoD class deviation directs DoD contracting officers to use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text, effective February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. RFO FAR 52.216-2(a) keeps the warranty that the unit price stated in the Schedule is not in excess of the contractor's applicable established price in effect on the contract date for like quantities of the same item [@CL-EST-0003; @CL-EST-0750]. It keeps the exclusion from the unit price of any part of the price directly resulting from requirements for preservation, packaging, or packing beyond standard commercial practice [@CL-EST-0004; @CL-EST-0857], and it keeps the definition of the established price [@CL-EST-0005; @CL-EST-0751]. RFO FAR 52.216-2(b) keeps the rule that the contractor promptly notifies the Contracting Officer of the amount and effective date of each decrease in any applicable established price and that each corresponding contract unit price is decreased by the same percentage [@CL-EST-0006; @CL-EST-0753]. RFO FAR 52.216-2(c) keeps the rule that, if the contractor's applicable established price is increased after the contract date, the corresponding contract unit price is increased, upon the contractor's written request to the Contracting Officer, by the same percentage that the established price is increased, subject to the limitations that follow [@CL-EST-0007; @CL-EST-0008; @CL-EST-0858]. RFO FAR 52.216-2(c)(1) keeps the limit that the aggregate of the increases in any contract unit price under the clause cannot exceed 10 percent of the original contract unit price [@CL-EST-0009; @CL-EST-0752]. RFO FAR 52.216-2(c)(2) keeps the effective date: the effective date of the increase in the applicable established price if the Contracting Officer receives the written request within 10 days thereafter, or, if the request is received later, the date the Contracting Officer receives it [@CL-EST-0010; @CL-EST-0754]. RFO FAR 52.216-2(c)(4) keeps the rule that no modification increasing a contract unit price is executed until the Contracting Officer verifies the increase in the applicable established price [@CL-EST-0011; @CL-EST-0756]. RFO FAR 52.216-2(c)(5) keeps the rule that, within 30 days after receipt of the written request, the Contracting Officer may cancel, without liability to either party, any undelivered portion of the contract items affected by the requested increase [@CL-EST-0012; @CL-EST-0755].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A line item sits at a contract unit price of 100. The contractor's established price rises 8 percent and the written request reaches the Contracting Officer inside the 10-day window, so the unit price becomes 108. A second rise of 5 percent would suggest 113.40, but the aggregate of increases cannot pass 10 percent of the original price, so the unit price stops at 110.

## Never confuse

This clause is not a general reopening of the price: the unit price moves only by the same percentage as the contractor's established price, and the aggregate increase is capped [@CL-EST-0858; @CL-EST-0752]. It is also not the semistandard clause, which starts from supplies that differ from the nearest commercial equivalent because of contract specifications [@CL-EST-0758]. The labor and material clause works from scheduled labor rates and material unit prices instead [@CL-CTF-0302]; see [Economic Price Adjustment, Labor and Material](../../contract-finance/billing-payment/ctf-economic-price-adjustment-labor-and-material.md), and for the auditor's view of the same mechanism see [Economic price adjustments in proposals and proposal audits](est-economic-price-adjustments.md).

## Company calibration

That each schedule line item price sits at or below the applicable established price, and that decrease notices are issued when the price list moves. [@Q-023]
