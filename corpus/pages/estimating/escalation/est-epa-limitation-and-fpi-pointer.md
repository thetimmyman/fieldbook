## Purpose

Shows who must decide that economic price adjustment is necessary before it is used, and where the FAR sends the reader for fixed-price incentive contracts.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-EST-1162] |
| Codified citation | FAR 16.203-3 and FAR 16.204 [@CL-EST-1162; @CL-EST-1163] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-EST-1162] |
| Applies to | Fixed-price contracts with economic price adjustment [@CL-EST-1162] |

A fixed-price contract with economic price adjustment shall not be used unless the contracting officer determines that it is necessary either to protect the contractor and the Government against significant fluctuations in labor or material costs or to provide for contract price adjustment in the event of changes in the contractor's established prices [@CL-EST-1162].

For fixed-price incentive contracts, the FAR says they are covered in subpart 16.4, Incentive Contracts, points to 16.403 for more complete descriptions, application and limitations, and points to 16.406 for the prescribed clauses [@CL-EST-1163].

## How it works

The contracting officer makes the determination [@CL-EST-1162]. The determination has two possible grounds: protection of both parties against significant fluctuations in labor or material costs, or provision for price adjustment when the contractor's established prices change [@CL-EST-1162]. Without it the contract type shall not be used [@CL-EST-1162]. The same determination is also a listed condition for each of the three economic price adjustment clauses in the clause prescriptions [@CL-EST-1165; @CL-EST-1169; @CL-EST-1173], as set out on [Prescriptions for the economic price adjustment clauses](est-epa-clause-prescriptions.md). The printed text does not say how the determination is recorded or approved.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contracting officer is buying parts over a 30-month period with large swings in metal prices. The contracting officer determines that an adjustment is necessary to protect both sides against significant fluctuations in material costs, and records that finding before the clause goes into the contract. In a second case there is no sign of price instability and no change in the contractor's established prices, so no such determination can be made and the contract type is not used.

## Never confuse

An economic price adjustment is not a fixed-price incentive arrangement. An economic price adjustment revises the stated price upon specified contingencies [@CL-EST-1155], while a fixed-price incentive contract adjusts profit and sets the final price by a formula based on final negotiated total cost against total target cost [@CL-EST-1209], and has its own subpart of the FAR [@CL-EST-1163]. The types of adjustment are on [Fixed-price contracts with economic price adjustment: types and use](est-fixed-price-epa-types-and-use.md). The incentive mechanics are on [Incentive contracts: when they fit and how they are structured](../pricing/est-incentive-contracts-general.md) and on [Fixed-price incentive contracts with a firm target](../pricing/est-fpi-firm-target.md). The adjustment rules and the auditor's view are on [Economic price adjustments in proposals and proposal audits](est-economic-price-adjustments.md).
