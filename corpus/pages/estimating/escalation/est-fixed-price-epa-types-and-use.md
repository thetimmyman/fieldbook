## Purpose

Names the three types of economic price adjustment, says when the FAR allows a fixed-price contract to use one, and shows the two base-level safeguards.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-EST-1155] |
| Codified citation | FAR 16.203-1 and FAR 16.203-2 [@CL-EST-1155; @CL-EST-1159] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-EST-1155] |
| Applies to | Fixed-price contracts with economic price adjustment [@CL-EST-1155] |

A fixed-price contract with economic price adjustment provides for upward and downward revision of the stated contract price upon the occurrence of specified contingencies [@CL-EST-1155]. The FAR names three general types [@CL-EST-1156]. The first is adjustment based on established prices [@CL-EST-1156]. The second is adjustment based on actual costs of labor or material [@CL-EST-1157]. The third is adjustment based on cost indexes of labor or material [@CL-EST-1158].

Such a contract may be used when there is serious doubt about the stability of market or labor conditions during an extended period of performance, and contingencies that would otherwise be in the price can be identified and covered separately in the contract [@CL-EST-1159]. The FAR adds that adjustments based on established prices should normally be restricted to industry-wide contingencies, and adjustments based on labor and material costs should be limited to contingencies beyond the contractor's control [@CL-EST-1205].

## How it works

Each type moves the price when the basis specified for that type changes. Published or otherwise established prices drive the first type, the contractor's actual experienced labor or material costs drive the second, and cost standards or indexes named in the contract drive the third [@CL-EST-1156; @CL-EST-1157; @CL-EST-1158]. The two tests for use are joined by the word and in the text [@CL-EST-1159]. Adjustment is made from a base level, and in setting it the contracting officer shall ensure that contingency allowances are not duplicated by inclusion in both the base price and the adjustment the contractor requests under the clause [@CL-EST-1160]. Where the contract does not require certified cost or pricing data, the contracting officer shall obtain adequate data to establish the base level and may require verification of the data submitted [@CL-EST-1161].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A two-year supply contract has a base price of 200 a unit with an index-based adjustment clause. The contractor's estimate included an allowance of 10 a unit for expected material inflation. The contracting officer sees that the same inflation would also be paid through the clause, so the allowance comes out of the base price. Since the contract is not subject to certified cost or pricing data, the contracting officer also asks for data that supports the 190 base level.

## Never confuse

The three types are not interchangeable labels. They differ in what moves the price: established prices of items, the contractor's actual labor or material costs, or contract-identified cost indexes [@CL-EST-1156; @CL-EST-1157; @CL-EST-1158]. The clauses for the established-price type, for standard and for semistandard supplies, are on [Economic Price Adjustment, Standard Supplies clause](est-epa-standard-supplies.md) and [Economic Price Adjustment, Semistandard Supplies clause](est-epa-semistandard-supplies.md) [@CL-EST-1207; @CL-EST-1208]. The clause for the actual-cost type is on [Economic Price Adjustment, Labor and Material](../../contract-finance/billing-payment/ctf-economic-price-adjustment-labor-and-material.md) [@CL-EST-1206]. When each clause is inserted is on [Prescriptions for the economic price adjustment clauses](est-epa-clause-prescriptions.md), and the determination that must come first is on [Determination before economic price adjustment, and the fixed-price incentive pointer](est-epa-limitation-and-fpi-pointer.md). The fixed-price type without adjustment is on [Firm-fixed-price contracts: risk and suitability](est-firm-fixed-price-contracts.md).
