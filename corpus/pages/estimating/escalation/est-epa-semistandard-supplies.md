## Purpose

Explains the Economic Price Adjustment clause for semistandard supplies, where the contract price may sit above the contractor's established price because of contract specifications.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-EST-0757] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.216-3 [@CL-EST-0757] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-EST-0757] |
| Applies to | Semistandard supplies that have an established price apart from contract specification modifications [@CL-EST-0757] |

The contractor warrants that the schedule line items are, except for modifications required by the contract specifications, supplies for which it has an established price [@CL-EST-0757]. As of the date of the contract, any difference between the contract unit prices for those items and the contractor's established prices for like quantities of the nearest commercial equivalents is due to compliance with contract specifications [@CL-EST-0758]. The warranty also ties the difference to compliance with any contract requirements for preservation, packaging, and packing beyond standard commercial practice [@CL-EST-0859]. The contractor promptly notifies the Contracting Officer of the amount and effective date of each decrease in any applicable established price [@CL-EST-0759].

If the established price is increased after the contract date, the corresponding contract unit price is increased, upon the contractor's written request, by the same percentage [@CL-EST-0874]. The part of the unit price resulting from specifications or from packaging and similar requirements beyond standard commercial practice is excluded from that increase [@CL-EST-0860]. The aggregate of the increases in any contract unit price cannot exceed 10 percent of the original contract unit price [@CL-EST-0760].

## How it works

The warranty frame is what makes the item semistandard: it has an established price, and contract specifications account for the gap between the contract price and the nearest commercial equivalent [@CL-EST-0758]. An increase needs the contractor's written request, and it applies by percentage to the unit price without the specification and packaging part [@CL-EST-0874; @CL-EST-0860]. It takes effect on the effective date of the established price increase when the Contracting Officer receives the request within 10 days thereafter, or on the date the request is received if it arrives later [@CL-EST-0761]. Within 30 days after receiving the request, the Contracting Officer may cancel, without liability to either party, any undelivered portion of the affected items [@CL-EST-0762].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A semistandard line item is priced at 120 against an established price of 110, the 10 difference reflecting contract specifications. The established price rises 5 percent, so the 110 part of the unit price rises 5 percent, which is 5.5. The specification part of 10 is excluded from the increase, so the new contract unit price is 125.5, not 126. The total increase of 5.5 is well inside the 10 percent cap of 12 on the original price of 120.

## Never confuse

The semistandard clause is not the standard supplies clause: here the contract price may sit above the established price because of contract specifications, and the warranty explains the gap rather than forbidding it [@CL-EST-0758; @CL-EST-0859]. The standard clause instead warrants that the schedule unit price is not in excess of the applicable established price for the item itself [@CL-EST-0750]. See [Economic Price Adjustment, Standard Supplies clause](est-epa-standard-supplies.md) for that clause and [Economic price adjustments in proposals and proposal audits](est-economic-price-adjustments.md) for the auditor's view.

## Company calibration

That each semistandard line item price gap to the established price is attributable to contract specifications, and that the escalation record keeps the percentage evidence. [@Q-023]
