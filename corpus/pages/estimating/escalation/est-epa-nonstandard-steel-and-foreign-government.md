## Purpose

Explains the two special DoD clauses that adjust price by labor and steel indexes or by changes a foreign government makes to wages or material prices, with their limits and notice deadlines.

### Nonstandard steel items

The clause defines the base steel index as the contractor's established price including all applicable extras for the identified item on the date set for bid opening or the date of submission of the offer [@CL-EST-1191]. It defines the current steel index as the contractor's established price for that item, including all applicable extras, in effect a stated number of days before the first day of the month in which delivery is required [@CL-EST-1192]. The aggregate of the increases in any contract unit price shall not exceed ten percent of the original contract unit price [@CL-EST-1193].

### Foreign-government controlled wages or prices

When the government named in the clause revises wage rates or material prices, the Contracting Officer shall make an equitable adjustment in the contract price and modify the contract to the extent the contractor's actual costs of performing the contract are increased or decreased as a direct result, subject to listed rules [@CL-EST-9600; @CL-EST-1204]. One listed rule is that an increase in contract unit prices is effective on the same date as the government's increase, but only if the Contracting Officer receives the contractor's written request within 10 days of the change [@CL-EST-1196]. If the request is received later than those 10 days, the effective date is the date the Contracting Officer received it [@CL-EST-1196; @CL-EST-1197]. Another listed rule is that a decrease in contract unit prices is effective on the same date the foreign government decreased the wage rate or material price [@CL-EST-1198].

## How it works

For nonstandard steel, the contractor furnishes within 30 days after final delivery, or another period the Contracting Officer authorizes, a statement identifying the correctness of the average straight time hourly earnings and the established prices that are relevant to the index computations [@CL-EST-1194; @CL-EST-1201; @CL-EST-1202], and no modification is made until the Contracting Officer has verified the revised established price [@CL-EST-1195]. The DFARS prescription adds that the ten percent figure in the clause shall not be exceeded unless approval is obtained at a level above the contracting officer [@CL-EST-1181]. For the foreign-government clause, no modification changing unit prices is executed until the Contracting Officer has verified the change in rates or prices set by the foreign government [@CL-EST-1199]. The clause also refers to a base labor index and a current labor index [@CL-EST-1201], and its notes tell the offeror how to fill the blanks, such as the days used for the current steel index [@CL-EST-1192; @CL-EST-1203]. The printed foreign-government clause text does not state a percentage limit.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A steel item has a base steel index of 400 and a current steel index of 440, so the steel index rose by one tenth. Steel is 60 percent of a unit price of 100, so the steel part moves from 60 to 66. The labor index and the portion not subject to revision are held aside. A foreign government raises a wage rate on day 1 and the contractor's request arrives on day 8, so the increase is effective from day 1. If it had arrived on day 15, it would take effect on day 15.

## Never confuse

The nonstandard steel clause (DFARS 252.216-7001) adjusts unit prices by steel and labor indexes up to a ten percent aggregate increase cap [@CL-EST-1179; @CL-EST-1193], unlike the foreign-government clause, which tracks host-government-controlled wage or material changes effective on the foreign increase date when timely requested [@CL-EST-1196], and unlike mill-product adjustments on [Economic price adjustment for basic steel, aluminum, brass, bronze and copper mill products](est-epa-mill-products-clause.md) [@CL-EST-1185].
