## Purpose

Explains when a late bid, modification or withdrawal can still be considered, what counts as evidence of when it arrived, how an emergency can extend the closing time, and how a bid is withdrawn.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-EST-1968] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.214-7 [@CL-EST-1968] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.214-7 [@CL-EST-0326; @CL-EST-0327; @CL-EST-0328; @CL-EST-0329; @CL-EST-0330; @CL-EST-0331; @CL-EST-0332; @CL-EST-0333; @CL-EST-0334; @CL-EST-0335; @CL-EST-0336; @CL-EST-0337; @CL-EST-0338] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-EST-1968] |
| Applies to | Invitations for bids that include the late submissions provision [@CL-EST-2081] |

Bidders are responsible for submitting bids, and any modifications or withdrawals, so as to reach the Government office designated in the invitation by the time the invitation specifies [@CL-EST-2082]. If the invitation names no time, the time for receipt is 4:30 in the afternoon, local time, for the designated Government office on the date bids are due [@CL-EST-1968]. The time and the office are set in each invitation and are contract-specific [@CL-EST-2082].

A bid, modification or withdrawal received at the designated office after the exact time specified for receipt of bids is late [@CL-EST-1969]. A late bid, modification or withdrawal will not be considered unless it is received before award is made, the Contracting Officer determines that accepting it would not unduly delay the acquisition, and one of two further conditions is met [@CL-EST-1970; @CL-EST-2083].

The first condition is that, if the bid was transmitted through an electronic commerce method that the invitation authorized, it was received at the initial point of entry to the Government infrastructure not later than 5:00 in the afternoon one working day before the date specified for receipt of bids [@CL-EST-1971]. The second is acceptable evidence that the bid was received at the Government installation designated for receipt of bids and was under the Government's control before the time set for receipt of bids [@CL-EST-1972].

A late modification of an otherwise successful bid that makes its terms more favorable to the Government will be considered at any time it is received and may be accepted [@CL-EST-1973].

## How it works

Acceptable evidence of the time of receipt at the installation includes the installation's time and date stamp on the bid wrapper, other documentary evidence of receipt that the installation maintains, and oral testimony or statements of Government personnel [@CL-EST-1974].

If an emergency or unanticipated event interrupts normal Government processes so that bids cannot be received at the designated office by the exact time specified, and urgent Government requirements preclude amendment of the invitation [@CL-EST-2084], the time specified for receipt of bids is deemed extended to the same time of day on the first work day on which normal Government processes resume [@CL-EST-2085].

A bid may be withdrawn by written notice received at any time before the exact time set for receipt of bids [@CL-EST-1975]. Where the invitation authorizes facsimile bids, a bid may be withdrawn by facsimile received before that time, subject to the conditions in the facsimile bids provision, FAR 52.214-31 [@CL-EST-1976]. When facsimile bids are considered at all is covered on [Sealed bidding: amendments, bid submission and explanations](est-sealed-bidding-essentials.md).

A bid may be withdrawn in person by the bidder or its authorized representative if, before the exact time set for receipt of bids, the identity of the person requesting withdrawal is established and the person signs a receipt for the bid [@CL-EST-1977].

A late bid sample or late descriptive literature sent by mail is considered under this provision, as covered on [Bid samples under sealed bidding](est-bid-samples.md) and [Descriptive literature under sealed bidding](est-descriptive-literature.md).

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text from February 1, 2026, because the DoD part 52 class deviation directs its use [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. The RFO text of FAR 52.214-7 keeps the bidder's responsibility to reach the designated office by the time specified [@CL-EST-0326; @CL-EST-2082] and the 4:30 p.m. local-time default when the IFB specifies no time [@CL-EST-0327; @CL-EST-1968]. It keeps the rule that a bid, modification or withdrawal received after the exact time specified is late and will not be considered unless it is received before award and the Contracting Officer determines that accepting it would not unduly delay the acquisition [@CL-EST-0328; @CL-EST-9025; @CL-EST-1969; @CL-EST-1970], with the electronic commerce condition [@CL-EST-0329; @CL-EST-1971] and the acceptable-evidence condition as an alternative [@CL-EST-0330; @CL-EST-1972; @CL-EST-2083]. It keeps the rule on a late, more favorable modification of an otherwise successful bid [@CL-EST-0331; @CL-EST-1973], the kinds of acceptable evidence of the time of receipt [@CL-EST-0332; @CL-EST-1974], and the emergency extension [@CL-EST-0333; @CL-EST-2084; @CL-EST-0334; @CL-EST-2085]. It keeps withdrawal by written notice [@CL-EST-0335; @CL-EST-1975] and withdrawal in person with an established identity and a signed receipt [@CL-EST-0338; @CL-EST-1977]. One difference matters: the codified paragraph has a sentence allowing withdrawal by facsimile where the IFB authorizes facsimile bids, subject to FAR 52.214-31 [@CL-EST-1976], but the RFO paragraph goes from the written-notice sentence straight to the in-person sentence [@CL-EST-0336] and ends there [@CL-EST-0337].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An invitation sets receipt for 2:00 p.m. on the due date and does not authorize electronic bids. Bidder A's wrapper carries the installation's time and date stamp of 1:52 p.m., but the bid reaches the designated office at 2:10 p.m. It is late. Award has not been made, and the Contracting Officer determines that accepting it would not unduly delay the acquisition. The stamp is acceptable evidence that the bid was at the installation and under Government control before 2:00 p.m., so the bid may be considered.

Bidder B's bid reaches the designated office at 2:05 p.m. and nothing shows earlier receipt. It is late and is not considered.

Under a second invitation that authorizes an electronic method, Bidder C's bid was received at the initial point of entry at 4:30 p.m. on the working day before the due date and reached the designated office at 2:20 p.m. on the due date. It is late, but it meets the electronic condition, so it may be considered if award has not been made and there is no undue delay.

Bidder D is the otherwise successful bidder. A modification that lowers its price arrives at 3:00 p.m., an hour after the time set. Because it makes the terms more favorable to the Government, it is considered whenever it is received.

Bidder E appears in person at 1:30 p.m., the contracting office establishes E's identity, and E signs a receipt for the bid. The bid is withdrawn.

Separately, a storm closes the designated office on the due date, and urgent Government requirements preclude amending the invitation. The time for receipt becomes 2:00 p.m. on the first work day on which normal Government processes resume.

## Never confuse

A late bid is not the same as a late modification of an otherwise successful bid: the bid must meet the conditions above, while the modification is considered at any time it is received if it makes the terms more favorable to the Government [@CL-EST-1970; @CL-EST-1973].

The electronic condition and the evidence condition are alternatives, not two requirements [@CL-EST-2083]. Neither replaces the first two conditions, receipt before award and no undue delay [@CL-EST-1970].

A time stamp is one kind of acceptable evidence, not the only kind [@CL-EST-1974].

The codified provision has a facsimile withdrawal sentence [@CL-EST-1976]; the RFO paragraph prints no such sentence between the written-notice and in-person sentences [@CL-EST-0336] or after them [@CL-EST-0337].

The emergency extension moves the time to the same time of day on the first work day on which normal Government processes resume, and it applies where an emergency or unanticipated event interrupts normal Government processes and urgent requirements preclude amending the invitation [@CL-EST-2084; @CL-EST-2085].
