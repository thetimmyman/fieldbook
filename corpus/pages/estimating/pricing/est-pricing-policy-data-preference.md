## Purpose

Shows what a contracting officer must do to reach a fair and reasonable price, which data comes first, and what a proposal team should expect to be asked for.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-EST-1250] |
| Codified citation | FAR 15.402 [@CL-EST-1250]; FAR 15.400 [@CL-EST-9191]; FAR 2.101 [@CL-EST-9318] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 15.202(d) [@CL-EST-3367]; RFO FAR 15.402 [@CL-EST-3368; @CL-EST-3379]; RFO FAR 15.403-1 [@CL-EST-3370]; RFO FAR 15.400 [@CL-EST-2256]; RFO FAR 2.101 [@CL-EST-9363] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-EST-1250] |
| Applies to | Contracting officers purchasing supplies and services [@CL-EST-1250] |

Contracting officers shall purchase supplies and services from responsible sources at fair and reasonable prices [@CL-EST-1250]. In establishing the reasonableness of offered prices, the contracting officer shall obtain certified cost or pricing data when required by the cross-referenced section, along with data other than certified cost or pricing data as necessary to establish a fair and reasonable price [@CL-EST-1251].

When certified cost or pricing data are not required, the contracting officer shall obtain data other than certified cost or pricing data as necessary, generally using a listed order of preference [@CL-EST-1252]. One listed duty is to obtain the type and quantity of data necessary, but not more data than is necessary [@CL-EST-1256]. Under the codified FAR, the threshold for obtaining certified cost or pricing data is $2.5 million for prime contracts awarded on or after July 1, 2018 [@CL-EST-0001], and, unless an exception applies, the data are required before the listed actions expected to exceed the current threshold [@CL-EST-9458]. For DoD, certified cost or pricing data are to be obtained when a prime contract is expected to exceed $10 million and is awarded after June 30, 2026, unless an exception at FAR 15.403-2 or a further provision applies [@CL-EST-0002; @CL-EST-9456; @CL-EST-9457]. The threshold is covered on [Certified cost or pricing data threshold](est-certified-cost-or-pricing-data.md).

Subpart 15.4 prescribes the cost and price negotiation policies and procedures for pricing negotiated prime contracts (including subcontracts) and contract modifications, including modifications to contracts awarded by sealed bidding [@CL-EST-9191].

The FAR definitions section describes pricing as the process of establishing a reasonable amount or amounts to be paid for supplies or services [@CL-EST-9318]. The RFO text of FAR 2.101 defines pricing as the process of establishing a reasonable amount or amounts to be paid for supplies or services, the same in substance as the codified definition [@CL-EST-9363; @CL-EST-9318].

## How it works

One listed item in the order of preference is no additional data from the offeror when the price is based on adequate price competition, except as provided by a cross-referenced paragraph [@CL-EST-1253]. Another listed item is data other than certified cost or pricing data, such as data related to prices, including established catalog or market prices and sales to non-governmental and governmental entities [@CL-EST-1301]. For data related to prices, the contracting officer relies first on data available within the Government, second on data from sources other than the offeror, and, if necessary, on data from the offeror [@CL-EST-1254]. When obtaining data from the offeror is necessary, then unless an exception under the cross-referenced paragraphs applies, such data shall include, at a minimum, appropriate data on the prices at which the same or similar items have been sold previously [@CL-EST-1255], and prior-sale price data required from the offeror shall be adequate for evaluating the reasonableness of the price [@CL-EST-1300]. A further listed item is cost data to the extent necessary for the contracting officer to determine a fair and reasonable price [@CL-EST-1302]. If the analyses of the data obtained to date cannot establish a fair and reasonable price, the contracting officer shall require additional data sufficient to support the determination [@CL-EST-1257]. A proposal team should therefore expect requests for price data, including prior-sale prices where data must come from the offeror, and a further request if the contracting officer cannot establish a fair and reasonable price from what has been submitted [@CL-EST-1255; @CL-EST-1257].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 15 text in place of the codified text under Class Deviation 2026-O0048, whose Revision 1 took effect immediately and supersedes the March 5, 2026 version [@CL-EST-3176] [@CL-EST-3177]. RFO FAR 15.202(d) says agencies must purchase supplies and services from responsible sources at fair and reasonable prices, where the codified rule addresses contracting officers [@CL-EST-1250; @CL-EST-3367]. RFO FAR 15.402(a) keeps the duty to obtain certified cost or pricing data when required by the cross-referenced section, along with data other than certified cost or pricing data as necessary to establish a fair and reasonable price, with the cross-reference now 15.403-3 [@CL-EST-1251; @CL-EST-3368]. When certified data are not required, contracting officers must obtain data other than certified cost or pricing data as necessary [@CL-EST-1252; @CL-EST-3369], and the order of preference now sits in RFO FAR 15.403-1(a), which keeps the word generally [@CL-EST-1252; @CL-EST-3370]. In that order, no additional data from the offeror comes first when adequate price competition exists [@CL-EST-1253; @CL-EST-3371]. RFO FAR 15.403-1(e) carries the rule that if adequate price competition exists but there are unusual circumstances in which additional data are necessary to determine the reasonableness of price, contracting officers must obtain the additional data from sources other than the offeror, to the maximum extent practicable [@CL-EST-0525; @CL-EST-3372]. The codified first item of the order of preference points to that rule with the words except as provided by 15.403-3(b) [@CL-EST-1253], and the RFO first item has no such words [@CL-EST-3371]. Data related to prices come next, relying first on data available within the Government [@CL-EST-1254; @CL-EST-3373], then on data from sources other than the offeror and, if necessary, from the offeror [@CL-EST-1254; @CL-EST-3374], followed by cost data to the extent necessary [@CL-EST-1302; @CL-EST-3375]. RFO FAR 15.403-1(b)(1) states the offeror-data requirement as its own paragraph: contracting officers must require submission of data other than certified cost or pricing data to the extent necessary to determine a fair and reasonable price [@CL-EST-1255; @CL-EST-3376], and the data must include, at a minimum, appropriate data on the prices at which the same item or similar items have previously been sold, adequate for determining the reasonableness of the price [@CL-EST-1300; @CL-EST-3377]. That data is not required when price reasonableness is established through adequate price competition or prices set by law or regulation, unless paragraph (e) of that subsection applies [@CL-EST-1255; @CL-EST-3378]. RFO FAR 15.402(b) keeps the limit to the type and quantity of data necessary, but not more data than is needed [@CL-EST-1256; @CL-EST-3379], and the requirement to obtain additional data if a fair and reasonable price cannot be established from the data obtained or submitted to date [@CL-EST-1257; @CL-EST-3380]. RFO FAR 15.400 says that its subpart contains cost and price negotiation policies and procedures for pricing negotiated prime contracts (including subcontracts) and contract modifications, including modifications to contracts awarded by sealed bidding [@CL-EST-2256].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A company offers a spare part at 12,000 per unit, and the price is not based on adequate price competition. The contracting officer finds no usable price data inside the Government or from other sources, so asks the company for the prices at which the same part sold before. Those sales support the offered price, so no further data is requested. Had the sales data been too thin to establish a fair and reasonable price, the contracting officer would have required more data.

## Never confuse

Data other than certified cost or pricing data are not certified cost or pricing data. The policy asks for certified data when the cross-referenced section requires it, and otherwise for the data necessary to establish a fair and reasonable price [@CL-EST-1251; @CL-EST-1252]. The exceptions to certification are on [Exceptions to the requirement for certified cost or pricing data](est-certified-data-exceptions.md) and, for DoD, on [DoD exceptions, waivers and limits](est-dod-certified-data-exceptions-and-waivers.md). The analysis methods are on [Proposal analysis techniques](est-proposal-analysis.md), the format for submitting the data is on [Instructions for submitting cost or pricing data](est-data-submission-format-and-instructions.md), and what the negotiation file records is on [Price negotiation and the price negotiation memorandum](est-price-negotiation-documentation.md).

The codified text excuses the prior-sale data requirement where an exception under 15.403-1(b)(1) or (2) applies [@CL-EST-1255], while the RFO text excuses it when price reasonableness is established through adequate price competition or prices set by law or regulation, unless paragraph (e) of that subsection applies [@CL-EST-3378].
