## Purpose

Shows the range of contract types that the general contract-type section describes and the policies that limit the choice of type: what sealed bidding allows, what negotiation allows, the bar on cost-plus-a-percentage-of-cost contracts, and the determination-and-findings rule. The sections covered here list no factors for choosing among types.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-EST-0944] |
| Codified citation | FAR 16.101 and 16.102 [@CL-EST-0944; @CL-EST-0949] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-EST-0944] |
| Applies to | The choice of contract type under part 16: contracts resulting from sealed bidding and contracts negotiated under part 15 each have their own rule [@CL-EST-0949; @CL-EST-0950] |

A wide selection of contract types is available to the Government and contractors, to give the flexibility needed in acquiring the variety and volume of supplies and services that agencies require [@CL-EST-0974]. Contract types vary in two ways: the degree and timing of the responsibility the contractor assumes for the costs of performance, and the amount and nature of the profit incentive offered to the contractor for achieving or exceeding specified standards or goals [@CL-EST-0944].

The types are grouped into two broad categories, fixed-price contracts and cost-reimbursement contracts [@CL-EST-0945]. The range runs from firm-fixed-price, where the contractor has full responsibility for the performance costs and the resulting profit or loss, to cost-plus-fixed-fee, where the contractor has minimal responsibility for the performance costs and the negotiated fee, or profit, is fixed [@CL-EST-0946; @CL-EST-0947]. In between are the incentive contracts, in which the contractor's responsibility for performance costs and the profit or fee incentives offered are tailored to the uncertainties involved in contract performance [@CL-EST-0948].

Policies in the second section:

- Contracts resulting from sealed bidding shall be firm-fixed-price contracts or fixed-price contracts with economic price adjustment [@CL-EST-0949].
- Contracts negotiated under part 15 may be of any type or combination of types that will promote the Government's interest, except as restricted in part 16 [@CL-EST-0950].
- Contract types that the regulation does not describe shall not be used, except as a deviation under subpart 1.4 [@CL-EST-0951].
- The cost-plus-a-percentage-of-cost system of contracting shall not be used [@CL-EST-0952].
- Prime contracts, letter contracts included, other than firm-fixed-price contracts shall, by an appropriate clause, prohibit cost-plus-a-percentage-of-cost subcontracts [@CL-EST-0953].
- No contract may be awarded before the execution of any determination and findings that part 16 requires [@CL-EST-0954]. The minimum requirements for the content of such a determination and findings are specified in 1.704 [@CL-EST-0955].

## How it works

The range of types is a spectrum of contractor cost responsibility. Firm-fixed-price puts full responsibility for performance costs, and the resulting profit or loss, on the contractor [@CL-EST-0946]. Cost-plus-fixed-fee leaves the contractor minimal responsibility and fixes the negotiated fee [@CL-EST-0947]. Incentive contracts sit between, with cost responsibility and incentives tailored to the uncertainties of performance [@CL-EST-0948]. See [cost-plus-fixed-fee contracts](../../close-revrec/revenue/cls-cost-plus-fixed-fee-contracts.md), [fixed-price contracts in general](est-fixed-price-contracts-general.md) and [fixed-price incentive contracts](est-fixed-price-incentive-and-letter-contracts.md) for those types.

The route to award limits the choice. A contract resulting from sealed bidding must be firm-fixed-price or fixed-price with economic price adjustment [@CL-EST-0949]. A contract negotiated under part 15 may be of any type or combination of types that will promote the Government's interest, except as restricted in part 16 [@CL-EST-0950].

Three further rules apply. A type that the regulation does not describe shall not be used, except as a deviation under subpart 1.4 [@CL-EST-0951]. The section does not say who approves a deviation. The cost-plus-a-percentage-of-cost system shall not be used, and the section states no exception [@CL-EST-0952]. A prime contract other than firm-fixed-price, letter contracts included, shall by an appropriate clause prohibit cost-plus-a-percentage-of-cost subcontracts [@CL-EST-0953]. The section points to the clauses prescribed in subpart 44.2 for cost-reimbursement contracts and in subparts 16.2 and 16.4 for fixed-price contracts [@CL-EST-0953]. For award-fee contracts, see [award-fee contracts](est-award-fee-contracts.md).

No contract may be awarded before the execution of any determination and findings that part 16 requires [@CL-EST-0954]. The section names no official who executes it. Minimum content requirements are in 1.704 [@CL-EST-0955].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A requirement worth 3,000,000 is awarded by sealed bidding, so the contract must be firm-fixed-price or fixed-price with economic price adjustment [@CL-EST-0949]. A second requirement is negotiated under part 15. A team member proposes a cost-plus-a-percentage-of-cost arrangement to share overrun savings. That system shall not be used, so the team selects another type or combination of types that will promote the Government's interest, within the restrictions of part 16 [@CL-EST-0952; @CL-EST-0950]. The team proposes a cost-reimbursement prime contract, and that contract must by an appropriate clause prohibit cost-plus-a-percentage-of-cost subcontracts [@CL-EST-0953]. Before award, the team checks whether part 16 requires a determination and findings for the chosen type. If it does, no contract may be awarded before that determination and findings is executed [@CL-EST-0954].

## Never confuse

Sealed bidding does not leave the type open the way negotiation does. The sealed-bid route is confined to firm-fixed-price or fixed-price with economic price adjustment [@CL-EST-0949; @CL-EST-0950]. The bar on the percentage-of-cost system is not the same as the bar on undescribed types. The first shall not be used, and the section states no exception. The second shall not be used except as a deviation under subpart 1.4 [@CL-EST-0952; @CL-EST-0951]. The bar on the percentage-of-cost system and the required clause that prohibits percentage-of-cost subcontracts are two separate rules [@CL-EST-0952; @CL-EST-0953].
