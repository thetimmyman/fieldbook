## Purpose

Shows when an order under an existing contract or blanket purchase agreement may be competed by reverse auction, what a contractor agrees to by quoting, and how price and proposal information is protected.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-EST-1938] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.217-11 [@CL-EST-1938] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-EST-1938] |
| Applies to | Contracts and blanket purchase agreements under which the Contracting Officer may conduct a reverse auction to award an order [@CL-EST-1938] |

A reverse auction is the process for obtaining pricing, usually supported by an electronic tool, in which offerors see competing offerors' prices without disclosure of the competing offerors' identity and have the opportunity to submit lower priced offers until the close of the auction [@CL-EST-2024]. The Contracting Officer may conduct a reverse auction to award an order under the contract or blanket purchase agreement [@CL-EST-1938].

The contractor terms apply when a reverse auction is conducted under the contract or blanket purchase agreement [@CL-EST-2026]. The contractor's or blanket purchase agreement holder's submission of a quote or proposal in response to the solicitation for an order constitutes agreement to participate in the auction [@CL-EST-1939]. The contractor agrees that the Government may reveal to all offerors the offered prices in the auction without revealing any offeror's identity, except for the awardee's identity subsequent to an award resulting from the auction [@CL-EST-2027].

The contractor or blanket purchase agreement holder may withdraw its agreement to further participate in the reverse auction by withdrawing its offer [@CL-EST-2028]. To withdraw an offer made in response to a reverse auction solicitation issued under the contract or blanket purchase agreement, it shall notify the Contracting Officer of the request [@CL-EST-1940]. The notice must be given before the close of the auction via the contact method identified in the solicitation [@CL-EST-2049]. If the reverse auction produces only one offer, the Government reserves the right to cancel the auction [@CL-EST-1941].

The Government may use a reverse auction service provider to conduct the reverse auction [@CL-EST-2029]. A reverse auction service provider is a commercial or Government entity that provides a means for conducting reverse auctions when acquiring supplies or services to be used by the Government [@CL-EST-2025]. Any price or proposal information or source selection information that the provider receives in relation to the reverse auction shall not be released outside the Government, unless otherwise required by law [@CL-EST-1942]. This does not prevent the Government from revealing to all contractors or blanket purchase agreement holders the offered prices in the auction without revealing any identity [@CL-EST-2030]. Price or proposal information includes, but is not limited to, contractor bid or proposal information as defined at Federal Acquisition Regulation 3.104-1, and price or proposal information similarly generated for a task order, a delivery order or an order under a blanket purchase agreement [@CL-EST-2031]. It also includes information identified by the contractor or blanket purchase agreement holder as restricted from duplication, use, or disclosure, in whole or in part, for any purpose other than to evaluate its price or proposal [@CL-EST-2032]. The contact method is stated in each solicitation for an order and is contract-specific.

## How it works

The clause applies the auction rules to an order placed under an existing contract or blanket purchase agreement, at the Contracting Officer's option [@CL-EST-1938]. Participation arises from submitting a quote or proposal for the order [@CL-EST-1939]. The exit is to notify the Contracting Officer of the request to withdraw the offer, before the close of the auction and by the contact method in the solicitation [@CL-EST-1940; @CL-EST-2049]. The single-offer case leaves cancellation open to the Government [@CL-EST-1941]. The Department of Defense supplement points to its own section on a prohibition on reverse auctions for personal protective equipment and aviation critical safety items [@CL-EST-1637].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

Three blanket purchase agreement holders quote for an order in a reverse auction: holder A at 90,000, holder B at 88,000 and holder C at 86,000. Each submission is agreement to take part, and each holder sees the competing prices without names. Holder A notifies the Contracting Officer before the close, through the contact method in the solicitation, that it withdraws its offer. Two offers remain at the close, so the one-offer cancellation right does not arise. A notice sent after the close would not meet the requirement that the notice come before the close.

## Never confuse

An order auction is not the solicitation-level provision: this clause concerns an order under the contract or blanket purchase agreement [@CL-EST-1938], while the provision on [Reverse auction under a solicitation](est-reverse-auction.md) concerns a contract or blanket purchase agreement awarded under a solicitation [@CL-EST-1932]. Withdrawing agreement is done by withdrawing the offer, and the request is notified to the Contracting Officer [@CL-EST-2028; @CL-EST-1940]. Showing prices to all offerors is allowed, but price or proposal information may not be released outside the Government unless the law requires it [@CL-EST-2030; @CL-EST-1942]. The provider's own duties are on [Duties of a reverse auction service provider](est-reverse-auction-services.md), and the defense supplement's pointer is on [Ordering under multiple award contracts](est-dod-ordering-multiple-award-contracts.md).
