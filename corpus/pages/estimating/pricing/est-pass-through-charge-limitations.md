## Purpose

Explains when a charge for work done by a subcontractor becomes an excessive pass-through charge that the Government will not pay.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-EST-0604] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.215-23 [@CL-EST-0604] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.215-23 [@CL-EST-0413] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-EST-0604] |
| Applies to | Contracts that include the clause [@CL-EST-0604] |

The Government will not pay excessive pass-through charges, and the contracting officer determines whether they exist [@CL-EST-0305]. For a contractor or subcontractor that adds no or negligible value, an excessive pass-through charge is a charge to the Government for indirect costs or profit on work performed by a subcontractor, other than charges for the costs of managing subcontracts and any applicable indirect costs and associated profit based on such costs [@CL-EST-0302; @CL-EST-0303]. Under Alternate I, the contracting officer has determined that there will be no excessive pass-through charges, provided the contractor performs the disclosed value-added functions [@CL-EST-0314].

## How it works

No or negligible value means the contractor or subcontractor cannot demonstrate to the contracting officer that its effort added value to the contract or subcontract in accomplishing the work performed under the contract, including task or delivery orders [@CL-EST-0304]. Added value means the contractor performs subcontract management functions that the contracting officer determines are a benefit to the Government [@CL-EST-0301]. The Contractor must notify the contracting officer in writing if either of two changes in subcontract effort occurs [@CL-EST-0605]. The first is that, after award, the Contractor changes the amount of subcontract effort so that it exceeds 70 percent of the total cost of work to be performed under the contract, task order, or delivery order [@CL-EST-0306], and the notice identifies the revised cost of the subcontract effort and verifies that the Contractor will provide added value [@CL-EST-0307]. The second is that any subcontractor changes the amount of lower-tier subcontractor effort after award so that it exceeds 70 percent of the total cost of the work under its subcontract [@CL-EST-0308].

If the contracting officer determines that excessive pass-through charges exist, they are unallowable for other than fixed-price contracts in accordance with the provisions in FAR subpart 31.2 [@CL-EST-0309], and on applicable DoD fixed-price contracts the Government is entitled to a price reduction for the amount of excessive pass-through charges included in the contract price [@CL-EST-0310; @CL-EST-0606]. The contracting officer or an authorized representative may examine and audit the contractor's records needed to decide whether excessive pass-through charges were proposed, billed or claimed [@CL-EST-0311]. Unless the contract is with DoD, the Contractor must insert the substance of the clause in cost-reimbursement subcontracts above the simplified acquisition threshold on the date of subcontract award [@CL-EST-0312]. On a DoD contract it reaches cost-reimbursement and fixed-price subcontracts above the threshold for obtaining cost or pricing data, except those the clause identifies [@CL-EST-0313].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text from February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. RFO FAR 52.215-23 keeps the definition of added value [@CL-EST-0413; @CL-EST-0301], whose parenthetical examples of subcontract management functions are processing orders of parts or services, maintaining inventory, reducing delivery lead times, managing multiple sources for contract requirements, coordinating deliveries, and performing quality assurance functions [@CL-EST-9045], the definition of an excessive pass-through charge [@CL-EST-0414; @CL-EST-0302] with its exclusion for charges for the costs of managing subcontracts and the indirect costs and profit based on those costs [@CL-EST-0415; @CL-EST-0303], and the definition of no or negligible value [@CL-EST-0416; @CL-EST-0304]. It keeps paragraph (b), under which the Government will not pay excessive pass-through charges and the Contracting Officer determines whether they exist [@CL-EST-0417; @CL-EST-0305]. Paragraph (c) keeps the written notice duty when the Contractor changes subcontract effort after award so that it exceeds 70 percent of the total cost of work under the contract, task order, or delivery order [@CL-EST-0418; @CL-EST-0306; @CL-EST-0605], with the revised cost and the verification of added value in the notice [@CL-EST-0419; @CL-EST-0307], and keeps the second trigger for a subcontractor’s lower-tier effort [@CL-EST-0420; @CL-EST-0308]. Paragraph (d)(1) says the excessive pass-through charges are unallowable for other than fixed-price contracts in accordance with the provisions in FAR 31, where the codified paragraph cites FAR subpart 31.2 [@CL-EST-0421; @CL-EST-0309]. Paragraph (d)(2) identifies the applicable DoD fixed-price contracts by 15.110(v)(2)(i)(B), where the codified paragraph says 15.408(n)(2)(i)(B) [@CL-EST-0422; @CL-EST-0310]. Paragraph (e)(1) keeps the Contracting Officer’s right to examine and audit the Contractor’s records [@CL-EST-0423; @CL-EST-0311]. Paragraph (f) keeps the rule that, unless the contract is with DoD, the Contractor must insert the substance of the clause in cost-reimbursement subcontracts above the simplified acquisition threshold on the date of subcontract award [@CL-EST-0424; @CL-EST-0312]. For a DoD contract it says the excepted subcontracts are those identified in FAR 15.110(v)(2)(i)(B)(2) and the threshold for obtaining cost or pricing data is in FAR 15.403-3(a), where the codified paragraph says FAR 15.408(n)(2)(i)(B)(2) and FAR 15.403-4(a)(1) [@CL-EST-0425; @CL-EST-0313]. Alternate I keeps the determination that there will be no excessive pass-through charges, provided the Contractor performs the disclosed value-added functions [@CL-EST-0426; @CL-EST-0314].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

On a cost-reimbursement contract with 1,000,000 of total cost of work, the prime raises subcontract effort after award from 600,000 to 900,000, which is 90 percent of the work and above 70 percent. The prime notifies the contracting officer in writing, identifies the revised cost of the subcontract effort, and verifies that it will provide added value. Suppose the prime cannot demonstrate any added value and charges 60,000 of overhead and profit on the 900,000. If the contracting officer determines that this is an excessive pass-through charge, the 60,000 is unallowable on the cost-reimbursement contract.

## Never confuse

Whether a contractor or subcontractor adds no or negligible value is part of the definition of an excessive pass-through charge [@CL-EST-0302; @CL-EST-0304], while the 70 percent figure appears in the reporting duty [@CL-EST-0306; @CL-EST-0308]. Charges for the costs of managing subcontracts, and the indirect costs and profit based on those costs, are outside the definition of an excessive pass-through charge [@CL-EST-0303].

For a DoD contract, the RFO text of paragraph (f) names the threshold for obtaining cost or pricing data at FAR 15.403-3(a) [@CL-EST-0425], where the codified paragraph names it at FAR 15.403-4(a)(1) [@CL-EST-0313].
