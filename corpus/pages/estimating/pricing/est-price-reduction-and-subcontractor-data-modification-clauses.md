## Purpose

Shows when the Government can reduce the price of a modification because certified data were defective, how an offset and the payback charges work, and what the Contractor must require of subcontractors.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-EST-1806] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.215-11 and FAR 52.215-13 [@CL-EST-1806; @CL-EST-1812] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-EST-1806] |
| Applies to | Modifications whose pricing adjustment is expected to exceed the certified data threshold on the date of execution [@CL-EST-1806; @CL-EST-1857] |

The price reduction clause tests whether the pricing adjustment is expected to exceed the certified data threshold on the date of execution of the modification, and it does not apply to any modification if an exception under FAR 15.403-1(b) applies [@CL-EST-1806]. The reduction paragraph is triggered when any price, including profit or fee, negotiated in connection with any modification under the clause, or any cost reimbursable under the contract, was increased by any significant amount because of the causes it lists [@CL-EST-1851]. One listed ground is an increase of any significant amount caused by the Contractor or a subcontractor furnishing certified data that were not complete, accurate and current as certified [@CL-EST-1807]. Under the third listed ground, if any of the listed parties furnished data of any description that were not accurate, the price or cost shall be reduced accordingly and the contract shall be modified to reflect the reduction [@CL-EST-1808]. The right to a price reduction is limited to that resulting from defects in data relating to modifications for which the clause becomes operative [@CL-EST-1852].

The subcontractor data clause is operative only for a modification to the contract involving a pricing adjustment expected to exceed the certified data threshold on the date of execution [@CL-EST-1857]. Under it, the Contractor shall require the subcontractor to submit certified cost or pricing data, actually or by specific identification in writing, in accordance with the FAR data table instructions [@CL-EST-1858]. That requirement is qualified by the words unless an exception under FAR 15.403-1(b) applies [@CL-EST-1859]. The Contractor inserts the substance of the clause, including its paragraph (d), in each subcontract above the certified data threshold on the date of agreement on price or the date of award, whichever is later [@CL-EST-1812]. The requirement to obtain subcontractor data applies before awarding any subcontract expected to exceed the certified data threshold on the date of agreement on price or the date of award, whichever is later [@CL-EST-1879], and also before pricing any subcontract modification involving a pricing adjustment expected to exceed the certified data threshold [@CL-EST-1880]. The subcontractor's certified data include any information reasonably required to explain the subcontractor's estimating process, such as the judgmental factors applied and the mathematical or other methods used in the estimate, including those used in projecting from known data [@CL-EST-1881]. If the threshold specified in FAR 15.403-4(a)(1) is adjusted for inflation, the changed threshold applies throughout the remaining term of the contract, unless there is a subsequent threshold adjustment [@CL-EST-1882]. The subcontractor's certification is that, to the best of its knowledge and belief, the data submitted under the clause were accurate, complete and current as of the date of agreement on the negotiated price [@CL-EST-1813].

## How it works

An offset in an amount the Contracting Officer finds appropriate on the facts is allowed against a price reduction, except as the clause prohibits, if the listed conditions are met [@CL-EST-1809]. The Contractor must certify that, to the best of its knowledge and belief, it is entitled to the offset in the amount requested [@CL-EST-1853], and must prove that the certified data were available before the as-of date on its certificate and were not submitted before that date [@CL-EST-1854]. No offset is allowed if the Contractor knew the data were understated before the as-of date [@CL-EST-1855], and a further listed condition is that the Government proves the contract price would not have increased in the amount to be offset even if the available data had been submitted before that date [@CL-EST-1856]. Where defective data come from a prospective subcontractor that was not subsequently awarded the subcontract, the reduction is limited to the amount, plus applicable overhead and profit markup, that the rest of the paragraph describes [@CL-EST-1876]. That amount is the amount by which the actual subcontract, or the actual cost to the Contractor if there was no subcontract, was less than the prospective subcontract cost estimate submitted by the Contractor, provided that the actual subcontract price was not itself affected by defective certified cost or pricing data [@CL-EST-1877]. Where a reduction lowers the price of items already paid for, the Contractor owes the United States interest compounded daily when the overpayment is repaid [@CL-EST-1810]. The interest is computed on the overpayment from the date or dates of overpayment to the date the Government is repaid, at the applicable underpayment rate effective for each quarter that the Secretary of the Treasury prescribes under section 6621(a)(2) of title 26 of the United States Code [@CL-EST-1878]. A penalty equal to the overpayment applies if the Contractor or a subcontractor knowingly submitted data that were incomplete, inaccurate or noncurrent [@CL-EST-1811].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A modification above the threshold is priced at 4,000,000 from certified data. Assume the vendor was an actual subcontractor, not a prospective subcontractor that did not receive the subcontract. After award, the Government finds a vendor quote that was available before the as-of date and that lowered one material price, cutting the price by 100,000 (ignoring markups). The price is reduced by 100,000 and the contract is modified. Separately, the Contractor shows a different cost of 20,000 that was available before the as-of date but never submitted. It certifies that it is entitled to the offset and proves the data were available and unsubmitted. The Contracting Officer may allow an offset of 20,000, which would leave a net reduction of 80,000. No offset is allowed if the Contractor knew that cost was understated before the as-of date, or if the Government proves the price would not have risen by 20,000 anyway. If the reduced amount was already paid, interest compounded daily runs from the dates of overpayment to the date the Government is repaid, at the underpayment rate for each quarter. If the omission was knowing, a penalty equal to the overpayment applies as well.

## Never confuse

The subcontractor data clause makes the Contractor require and flow down subcontractor data [@CL-EST-1812], while the price reduction clause reduces the price after the fact when data prove defective [@CL-EST-1808]. An offset is a separate step from the reduction, and the Contractor supplies the certification and proof for it [@CL-EST-1853; @CL-EST-1854]. The wider treatment of defective data sits in [Defective pricing](est-defective-pricing.md), the subcontractor side sits in [Subcontractor Certified Cost or Pricing Data clause](est-subcontractor-certified-cost-or-pricing-data.md), and the exception request that can replace certified data is in [Requirements for certified cost or pricing data](est-certified-data-requirement-provision-and-modifications-clause.md).
