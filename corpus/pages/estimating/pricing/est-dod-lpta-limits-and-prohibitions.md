## Purpose

Shows when DoD may use the lowest price technically acceptable source selection process, when it should avoid it, and when it may not use it at all.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-EST-1538] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 215.101-2-70 [@CL-EST-1538] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-EST-1538] |
| Applies to | DoD source selections that consider the lowest price technically acceptable process [@CL-EST-1538] |

The process shall only be used when the listed conditions are met, and one of them is that minimum requirements can be described clearly and comprehensively and expressed in terms of performance objectives, measures, and standards that will be used to determine the acceptability of offers [@CL-EST-1538]. The paragraph lists further conditions, which this card does not set out. Contracting officers shall avoid, to the maximum extent practicable, using the process in a procurement that is predominately for listed categories, which include information technology services and cybersecurity services [@CL-EST-1539].

Contracting officers shall not use the process to procure items designated by the requiring activity as personal protective equipment or an aviation critical safety item [@CL-EST-1540], when the requiring activity advises the contracting officer that the level of quality or failure of the equipment or item could result in combat casualties [@CL-EST-1536]. Contracting officers shall not use the process to acquire engineering and manufacturing development for a major defense acquisition program for which budgetary authority is requested beginning in fiscal year 2019 [@CL-EST-1541]. For an auditing contract, award decisions are based on best value factors and criteria, as determined by the resource sponsor in accordance with agency procedures, and use of the process is prohibited [@CL-EST-1542].

## How it works

Treat the rules in three tiers. The first is a limit: the process is used only when the listed conditions are met [@CL-EST-1538]. The second is a duty to avoid it where practicable for the listed categories [@CL-EST-1539]. The third is a set of prohibitions for protective equipment and critical safety items when the combat casualties condition applies, certain development work, and auditing contracts [@CL-EST-1540; @CL-EST-1536; @CL-EST-1541; @CL-EST-1542].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A program office wants to buy cybersecurity services on a lowest price technically acceptable basis. The text says contracting officers shall avoid that process to the maximum extent practicable for such a procurement. The answer: this is a duty to avoid, not an outright ban of the kind the prohibitions set, and the listed conditions must still be met if the process is used.

## Never confuse

To avoid is not to prohibit. The duty to avoid is qualified by the words to the maximum extent practicable, so it is not worded as a prohibition [@CL-EST-1539], while the prohibitions say contracting officers shall not use the process [@CL-EST-1540; @CL-EST-1541; @CL-EST-1542]. The conditions for the process outside DoD are on [Lowest price technically acceptable source selection](est-lpta-source-selection.md).
