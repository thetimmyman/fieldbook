## Purpose

Shows how the contracting officer decides whether a nonprofit FFRDC should carry any fee at all, how the fee objective is set when a fee is appropriate, and which profit methods the contracting officer may not use [@CL-EST-2455; @CL-EST-2458; @CL-EST-2459].

For nonprofit organizations that are FFRDCs, the contracting officer should consider whether any fee is appropriate [@CL-EST-2455]. The considerations must include the FFRDC's proportion of retained earnings, as established under generally accepted accounting methods, that relates to DoD contracted effort, and its facilities capital acquisition plans [@CL-EST-2456]. They must also include the FFRDC's working capital funding as assessed on operating cycle cash needs and its provision for funding unreimbursed costs deemed ordinary and necessary to the FFRDC [@CL-EST-2457]. For these FFRDCs, when a fee is considered appropriate, the contracting officer must establish the fee objective in accordance with FFRDC fee policies in DoD Instruction 5000.77, DoD Federally Funded Research and Development Center Program [@CL-EST-2455; @CL-EST-2458]. For these FFRDCs the contracting officer must not use the weighted guidelines method or an alternate structured approach [@CL-EST-2455; @CL-EST-2459].

The FAR definitions section defines a Federally Funded Research and Development Center. It means activities that are sponsored under a broad charter by a Government agency (or agencies) for the purpose of performing, analyzing, integrating, supporting, and/or managing basic or applied research and/or development [@CL-EST-9334], and that receive 70 percent or more of their financial support from the Government [@CL-EST-9335]. Three listed conditions also apply: a long-term relationship is contemplated, most or all of the facilities are owned or funded by the Government [@CL-EST-9336], and the FFRDC has access to Government and supplier data, employees, and facilities beyond that common in a normal contractual relationship [@CL-EST-9337]. The RFO model text of FAR 2.101 defines Federally Funded Research and Development Centers as activities that are sponsored under a broad charter by a Government agency (or agencies) for the purpose of performing, analyzing, integrating, supporting, and/or managing basic or applied research and/or development [@CL-EST-9382], and that receive 70 percent or more of their financial support from the Government [@CL-EST-9383]. Three listed conditions also apply: a long-term relationship is contemplated, most or all of the facilities are owned or funded by the Government [@CL-EST-9384], and the FFRDC has access to Government and supplier data, employees, and facilities beyond that common in a normal contractual relationship [@CL-EST-9385].

## How it works

For nonprofit organizations that are FFRDCs, the contracting officer should consider whether any fee is appropriate [@CL-EST-2455]. The considerations must include the FFRDC's proportion of retained earnings (as established under generally accepted accounting methods) that relates to DoD contracted effort and its facilities capital acquisition plans [@CL-EST-2456], and also its working capital funding as assessed on operating cycle cash needs and its provision for funding unreimbursed costs deemed ordinary and necessary to the FFRDC [@CL-EST-2457]. When a fee is considered appropriate, the contracting officer must establish the fee objective in accordance with FFRDC fee policies in DoD Instruction 5000.77, DoD Federally Funded Research and Development Center Program [@CL-EST-2458]. The contracting officer must not use the weighted guidelines method or an alternate structured approach [@CL-EST-2455; @CL-EST-2459].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A nonprofit laboratory is an FFRDC for a military department. The contracting officer considers the laboratory's retained earnings tied to DoD contracted effort, its facilities capital acquisition plans, its working capital funding for the operating cycle and its provision for unreimbursed ordinary and necessary costs, and concludes that a fee is appropriate [@CL-EST-2455; @CL-EST-2456; @CL-EST-2457]. The fee objective is then established under the FFRDC fee policies in DoD Instruction 5000.77 [@CL-EST-2458]. The contracting officer does not use the weighted guidelines method or an alternate structured approach [@CL-EST-2459]. Had the same consideration led the contracting officer to conclude that no fee was appropriate, no fee objective would be needed [@CL-EST-2455; @CL-EST-2458].

## Never confuse

For nonprofit organizations that are FFRDCs, the contracting officer must not use the weighted guidelines method or an alternate structured approach [@CL-EST-2455; @CL-EST-2459], and, when a fee is considered appropriate, must establish the fee objective in accordance with FFRDC fee policies in DoD Instruction 5000.77, DoD Federally Funded Research and Development Center Program [@CL-EST-2458]. For the general DoD profit methods, see [DoD profit policy: structured approaches and when weighted guidelines are not used](est-dod-profit-structured-approaches.md).
