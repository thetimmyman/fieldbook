## Purpose

Shows how the contracting officer decides whether a nonprofit FFRDC should carry any fee at all, how the fee objective is set when a fee is appropriate, and which profit methods the contracting officer may not use [@CL-EST-2455; @CL-EST-2458; @CL-EST-2459].

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-EST-2455] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 215.404-75 [@CL-EST-2455]; FAR 2.101 [@CL-EST-9334; @CL-EST-9335; @CL-EST-9336; @CL-EST-9337] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-EST-2455] |
| Applies to | Contracting officers dealing with nonprofit organizations that are FFRDCs [@CL-EST-2455] |

For nonprofit organizations that are FFRDCs, the contracting officer should consider whether any fee is appropriate [@CL-EST-2455]. The considerations must include the FFRDC's proportion of retained earnings, as established under generally accepted accounting methods, that relates to DoD contracted effort, and its facilities capital acquisition plans [@CL-EST-2456]. They must also include the FFRDC's working capital funding as assessed on operating cycle cash needs and its provision for funding unreimbursed costs deemed ordinary and necessary to the FFRDC [@CL-EST-2457]. For these FFRDCs, when a fee is considered appropriate, the contracting officer must establish the fee objective in accordance with FFRDC fee policies in DoD Instruction 5000.77, DoD Federally Funded Research and Development Center Program [@CL-EST-2455; @CL-EST-2458]. For these FFRDCs the contracting officer must not use the weighted guidelines method or an alternate structured approach [@CL-EST-2455; @CL-EST-2459].

The FAR definitions section defines a Federally Funded Research and Development Center. It means activities that are sponsored under a broad charter by a Government agency (or agencies) for the purpose of performing, analyzing, integrating, supporting, and/or managing basic or applied research and/or development [@CL-EST-9334], and that receive 70 percent or more of their financial support from the Government [@CL-EST-9335]. Three listed conditions also apply: a long-term relationship is contemplated, most or all of the facilities are owned or funded by the Government [@CL-EST-9336], and the FFRDC has access to Government and supplier data, employees, and facilities beyond that common in a normal contractual relationship [@CL-EST-9337]. The RFO model text of FAR 2.101 defines a Federally Funded Research and Development Center in the same words as the codified definition, apart from the printed form of the apostrophe in FFRDC's [@CL-EST-9382; @CL-EST-9383; @CL-EST-9384; @CL-EST-9385].

## How it works

The first question is whether any fee is appropriate, and the contracting officer's consideration must cover four things: retained earnings tied to DoD contracted effort, facilities capital acquisition plans, working capital funding based on operating cycle cash needs, and the provision for funding unreimbursed ordinary and necessary costs [@CL-EST-2455; @CL-EST-2456; @CL-EST-2457]. If the contracting officer considers a fee appropriate, the fee objective comes from the FFRDC fee policies in the DoD Instruction rather than from the general profit methods [@CL-EST-2458]. For these nonprofit FFRDCs the weighted guidelines method and an alternate structured approach are closed to the contracting officer [@CL-EST-2455; @CL-EST-2459].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A nonprofit laboratory is an FFRDC for a military department. The contracting officer considers the laboratory's retained earnings tied to DoD contracted effort, its facilities capital acquisition plans, its working capital funding for the operating cycle and its provision for unreimbursed ordinary and necessary costs, and concludes that a fee is appropriate [@CL-EST-2455; @CL-EST-2456; @CL-EST-2457]. The fee objective is then established under the FFRDC fee policies in DoD Instruction 5000.77 [@CL-EST-2458]. The contracting officer does not use the weighted guidelines method or an alternate structured approach [@CL-EST-2459]. Had the same consideration led the contracting officer to conclude that no fee was appropriate, no fee objective would be needed [@CL-EST-2455; @CL-EST-2458].

## Never confuse

An FFRDC fee objective is not built with the weighted guidelines method or an alternate structured approach, because the contracting officer must not use them and the DoD Instruction governs the fee objective [@CL-EST-2458; @CL-EST-2459]. The structured approaches card covers the general profit methods and is not repeated here.
