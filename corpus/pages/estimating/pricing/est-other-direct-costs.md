## Purpose

Shows how other direct costs are classified and estimated in forward pricing proposals.

The primary regulatory requirements for other direct costs in forward pricing proposals revolve around cost principles in the Federal Acquisition Regulation and the Cost Accounting Standards [@CL-EST-0715]. Other direct costs are expenses identifiable with a specific contract or cost objective that are not classified as direct material or direct labor, and costs benefiting multiple contracts or general business operations are to be allocated through indirect expense pools [@CL-EST-0716]. A fundamental requirement is that the contractor's method for classifying and allocating other direct costs is consistent with its disclosed accounting practices [@CL-EST-0717], and the same sentence goes on to require compliance with all applicable Cost Accounting Standards [@CL-EST-9521; @CL-EST-9519]. The next sentence adds that CAS 402 requires consistency in allocating costs incurred for the same purpose, which is particularly relevant when evaluating factors used to estimate costs [@CL-EST-9519].

## How it works

The auditor's main objective is to determine whether the proposed other direct costs are reasonable, properly classified, and supported by current, accurate, and complete data [@CL-EST-0719]. To verify that the estimates align with the contractor's disclosed practices and comply with relevant CAS and FAR provisions, auditors need a thorough understanding of the contractor's practices, policies and the basis used to estimate these costs [@CL-EST-9520]. That verification includes ensuring that costs for government-furnished materials are excluded and that factors used for estimates do not improperly include indirect costs [@CL-EST-9520; @CL-EST-0720].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A proposal includes travel and special tooling as other direct costs. Government-furnished material is left out of the estimate, and no overhead factor is buried in the tooling line.

## Never confuse

Other direct costs are identifiable with a specific contract or cost objective and are not classified as direct material or direct labor, whereas costs benefiting multiple contracts or general business operations are to be allocated through indirect expense pools [@CL-EST-0716]. Contractors' other direct costs vary in treatment but may often include engineering, special tooling and test equipment, packaging, travel and subsistence, and field service [@CL-EST-0718].

## Company calibration

That other direct cost classifications match the disclosed accounting practices and are consistent with actuals. [@Q-023]
