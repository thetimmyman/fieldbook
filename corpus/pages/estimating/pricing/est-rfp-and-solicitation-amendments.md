## Purpose

Shows what a request for proposals must describe and when the contracting officer must amend the solicitation.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-EST-1364] |
| Codified citation | FAR 15.203, FAR 15.205 and FAR 15.206 [@CL-EST-1364; @CL-EST-2051; @CL-EST-2052; @CL-EST-1366] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 15.102(b)(3)(i) [@CL-EST-3449; @CL-EST-3450] and RFO FAR 15.106 [@CL-EST-3443; @CL-EST-3444] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-EST-1364] |
| Applies to | Requests for proposals for competitive acquisitions, and solicitations whose requirements or terms change [@CL-EST-1364; @CL-EST-1366] |

RFPs for competitive acquisitions shall at a minimum describe the Government's requirement and the anticipated terms and conditions that will apply to the contract [@CL-EST-1364]. The solicitation may authorize offerors to propose alternative terms and conditions [@CL-EST-9757]. The same list also requires the RFP to describe the information required to be in the offeror's proposal and the factors and significant subfactors that will be used to evaluate the proposal and their relative importance [@CL-EST-1365].

When the Government changes its requirements or terms and conditions, either before or after receipt of proposals, the contracting officer shall amend the solicitation [@CL-EST-1366]. The contracting officer shall issue solicitations to potential sources in accordance with the policies and procedures in FAR 5.102, FAR 19.202-4 and FAR part 6 [@CL-EST-2051]. A master solicitation, as described in FAR 14.203-3, may also be used for negotiated acquisitions [@CL-EST-2052].

## How it works

If a proposal of interest to the Government involves a departure from the stated requirements, the contracting officer shall amend the solicitation, provided this can be done without revealing to the other offerors the alternate solution proposed or any other information entitled to protection [@CL-EST-1367; @CL-EST-1481].

If, in the judgment of the contracting officer, based on market research or otherwise, an amendment proposed for issuance after offers have been received is so substantial as to exceed what prospective offerors reasonably could have anticipated [@CL-EST-1368], so that additional sources likely would have submitted offers had the substance of the amendment been known to them, the contracting officer shall cancel the original solicitation and issue a new one, regardless of the stage of the acquisition [@CL-EST-1354].

The time and manner of submission are on [Submitting, modifying, withdrawing and late proposals](est-proposal-submission-and-late-proposals.md). The provisions and clauses inserted in solicitations are on [Advisory multi-step process and source selection provisions and clauses](est-advisory-multi-step-and-source-selection-provisions.md).

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers must use the RFO FAR part 15 text in place of the codified text under Class Deviation 2026-O0048, whose Revision 1 took effect immediately and supersedes the March 5, 2026 version [@CL-EST-3176] [@CL-EST-3177]. RFO FAR 15.106(a) says that when the Government changes its requirements or terms and conditions, the contracting officer must amend the RFP [@CL-EST-3443]. The codified paragraph says that when, either before or after receipt of proposals, the Government changes its requirements or terms and conditions, the contracting officer shall amend the solicitation [@CL-EST-1366]. RFO FAR 15.102(b)(3)(i) says that, when the uniform contract format is not used, contracting officers must ensure competitive RFPs, at a minimum, describe the Government's requirement and any anticipated terms and conditions that will apply to the contract [@CL-EST-3449]. The same list continues with the information required to be in the offeror's proposal, the factors and significant subfactors that will be used to evaluate the proposal and their relative importance, and one of the statements at 15.104(d) [@CL-EST-3450]. The codified paragraph says RFPs for competitive acquisitions shall, at a minimum, describe the Government's requirement and the anticipated terms and conditions that will apply to the contract [@CL-EST-1364], and its list continues with the information required to be in the offeror's proposal and the factors and significant subfactors that will be used to evaluate the proposal and their relative importance [@CL-EST-1365]. RFO FAR 15.106(e)(1) says contracting officers must amend the RFP if the Government is interested in a proposal that involves a departure from the stated requirements [@CL-EST-3444], and also says the amendment should not reveal the alternate solution proposed or any other information that is entitled to protection [@CL-EST-3445]. RFO FAR 15.106(e)(2) says contracting officers must cancel the RFP and issue a new one, regardless of the stage of the acquisition, if an amendment is proposed after offers have been received and, in the judgment of the contracting officer, based on market research or otherwise, has a requirement change that is so substantial it exceeds what prospective offerors reasonably could have anticipated as a change to the RFP, and additional sources likely would have submitted offers had the change been in the RFP [@CL-EST-3446; @CL-EST-3447; @CL-EST-3448]. The codified paragraph states the corresponding rule with "shall" for an amendment proposed for issuance after offers have been received [@CL-EST-1368; @CL-EST-1354].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

After proposals arrive, the Government drops a required delivery site and adds two new ones. The contracting officer amends the solicitation. If the contracting officer judged the change so substantial that it exceeded what prospective offerors could reasonably have anticipated, so that other firms would likely have bid had they known, the contracting officer would cancel the solicitation and issue a new one.

## Never confuse

FAR 15.206(a) requires the contracting officer to amend the solicitation when the Government changes its requirements or terms and conditions [@CL-EST-1366]. FAR 15.206(e) requires the contracting officer to cancel the original solicitation and issue a new one when, in the judgment of the contracting officer, based on market research or otherwise, an amendment proposed for issuance after offers have been received is so substantial as to exceed what prospective offerors reasonably could have anticipated [@CL-EST-1368], so that additional sources likely would have submitted offers had the substance of the amendment been known to them [@CL-EST-1354]. Talks with industry before the solicitation issues are on [Exchanges with industry before receipt of proposals](est-exchanges-before-proposals.md).

Codified FAR 15.206(d) says the contracting officer shall amend the solicitation if a proposal of interest to the Government involves a departure from the stated requirements, provided this can be done without revealing to the other offerors the alternate solution proposed or any other information that is entitled to protection [@CL-EST-1367; @CL-EST-1481]. RFO FAR 15.106(e)(1) says contracting officers must amend the RFP if the Government is interested in a proposal that involves a departure from the stated requirements [@CL-EST-3444], and that the amendment should not reveal the alternate solution proposed or any other information that is entitled to protection [@CL-EST-3445].
