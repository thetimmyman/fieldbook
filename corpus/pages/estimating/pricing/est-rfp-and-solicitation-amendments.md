## Purpose

Shows what a request for proposals must describe and when the contracting officer must amend the solicitation.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-EST-1364] |
| Codified citation | FAR 15.203 and FAR 15.206 [@CL-EST-1364; @CL-EST-1366] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-EST-1364] |
| Applies to | Requests for proposals for competitive acquisitions, and solicitations whose requirements or terms change [@CL-EST-1364; @CL-EST-1366] |

RFPs for competitive acquisitions shall at a minimum describe the Government's requirement and the anticipated terms and conditions that will apply to the contract [@CL-EST-1364]. Two further listed items are the information required to be in the offeror's proposal and the factors and significant subfactors that will be used to evaluate the proposal with their relative importance [@CL-EST-1364; @CL-EST-1365].

When the Government changes its requirements or terms and conditions, either before or after receipt of proposals, the contracting officer shall amend the solicitation [@CL-EST-1366].

## How it works

If a proposal of interest to the Government involves a departure from the stated requirements, the contracting officer shall amend the solicitation, provided this can be done without revealing to the other offerors the alternate solution proposed or any other information entitled to protection [@CL-EST-1367; @CL-EST-1481].

Cancellation is a separate step. One condition of the cancellation rule is the contracting officer's judgment, based on market research or otherwise, that an amendment proposed after offers have been received is so substantial as to exceed what prospective offerors reasonably could have anticipated [@CL-EST-1368]. Where the other stated conditions are met and additional sources likely would have submitted offers had the substance of the amendment been known to them, the contracting officer shall cancel the original solicitation and issue a new one, regardless of the stage of the acquisition [@CL-EST-1368; @CL-EST-1354].

The time and manner of submission are on [Submitting, modifying, withdrawing and late proposals](est-proposal-submission-and-late-proposals.md). The provisions and clauses inserted in solicitations are on [Advisory multi-step process and source selection provisions and clauses](est-advisory-multi-step-and-source-selection-provisions.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

After proposals arrive, the Government drops a required delivery site and adds two new ones. The contracting officer amends the solicitation. If the contracting officer judged the change so substantial that it exceeded what prospective offerors could reasonably have anticipated, so that other firms would likely have bid had they known, the contracting officer would cancel the solicitation and issue a new one.

## Never confuse

An amendment changes the solicitation that is already out. Cancellation ends it and starts a new one, and the cancellation rule rests on the contracting officer's judgment about how far the change goes [@CL-EST-1368; @CL-EST-1354]. Talks with industry before the solicitation issues are on [Exchanges with industry before receipt of proposals](est-exchanges-before-proposals.md).
