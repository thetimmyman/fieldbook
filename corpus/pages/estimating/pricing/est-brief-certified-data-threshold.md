## Question

Which threshold for certified cost or pricing data applies to a DoD prime contract proposal, and what must be known before applying it?

## Recommendation

Check the exceptions first. Then test the expected value against the DoD deviation threshold when the prime award will follow the deviation's cutover date [@CL-EST-0002], and against the codified FAR threshold otherwise [@CL-EST-0001]. Confirm in the solicitation or contract which text governs, and treat a subcontract under an older prime as unresolved until its flowdown is read.

## Evidence

- The codified FAR requires certified cost or pricing data above $2.5 million for prime contracts awarded on or after July 1, 2018, unless an exception applies [@CL-EST-0001].
- For DoD prime contracts awarded after June 30, 2026, the deviation requires them when the contract is expected to exceed $10 million, unless an exception at FAR 15.403-2 or DFARS 215.403-71 applies [@CL-EST-0002].
- The same deviation keeps the older threshold for subcontracts under primes that still require certified data, and for primes awarded before the cutover unless the contract says otherwise [@CL-EST-0002; @S-0022-v1, pp. 23-24].
- DoD lists the deviation as its current text for the negotiation part [@S-0021-v1].

## Options

Using the codified threshold alone was rejected: DoD contracts run on the deviation text. Using the deviation threshold for every proposal was rejected: earlier primes and their subcontracts keep the older value. Testing by award date and flowdown, as recommended, is the only option that matches both texts.

## Unknowns

- Which proposals expected in the next two quarters may need certified data [@Q-047].
- Who owns proposal pricing and the estimating system description [@Q-036].
- Where the executed contracts are and who confirms clause applicability [@Q-004].
- Whether the deviation exempts nontraditional defense contractors from certified data unless waived. Not established.

## Next action

The estimating lead revisits this brief when the deviation is revised or codified, or when a proposal's expected value comes near either threshold.
