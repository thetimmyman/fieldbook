## Purpose

Shows when a contractor executes a Certificate of Current Cost or Pricing Data and what the certificate covers.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-EST-0170] |
| Codified citation | FAR 15.406-2 [@CL-EST-0170]; DFARS 215.406-2 [@CL-EST-2059] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 15.403-4 [@CL-EST-3190]; RFO FAR 15.403-3(f) [@CL-EST-3196] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-EST-0170] |
| Applies to | Actions where certified cost or pricing data are required [@CL-EST-0170] |

When certified cost or pricing data are required, the contracting officer shall require the contractor to execute a Certificate of Current Cost or Pricing Data using the format in the subsection, and must include the executed certificate in the contract file [@CL-EST-9460; @CL-EST-0170]. The certificate states that, to the best of the signer's knowledge and belief, the submitted cost or pricing data are accurate, complete, and current as of the date inserted [@CL-EST-9461; @CL-EST-0175]. The form says to insert the day, month and year when price negotiations were concluded and price agreement was reached or, if applicable, an earlier date agreed between the parties that is as close as practicable to the date of agreement on price [@CL-EST-9462]. It also covers the cost or pricing data supporting any advance agreements and forward pricing rate agreements that are part of the proposal [@CL-EST-0171].

## How it works

The certificate applies to the data upon which the contractor's judgment or estimate was based, not to the accuracy of that judgment on future costs or projections [@CL-EST-9463; @CL-EST-0172]. Possession of a certificate is not a substitute for examining and analyzing the contractor's proposal [@CL-EST-0173]. If certified data are requested by the Government and submitted but an exception is later found to apply, the data are not considered certified cost or pricing data and shall not be certified in accordance with that subsection [@CL-EST-0174].

The DFARS refers the reader to PGI 215.406-2 for additional information and guidance on Certificates of Current Cost or Pricing Data [@CL-EST-2059].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 15 text in place of the codified text under Class Deviation 2026-O0048, whose Revision 1 took effect immediately and supersedes the March 5, 2026 version [@CL-EST-3176] [@CL-EST-3177]. RFO FAR 15.403-4(a) keeps the rule that when certified cost or pricing data are required, contracting officers must require the contractor to execute a Certificate of Current Cost or Pricing Data, using the format in that paragraph, and must include the executed certificate in the contract file [@CL-EST-3190; @CL-EST-0170]. The RFO certificate form certifies, to the best of the signer's knowledge and belief, the data submitted [@CL-EST-3254], and states that the data are accurate, complete, and current as of the date inserted [@CL-EST-3191; @CL-EST-0175]. It tells the signer to insert the day, month, and year when price negotiations were concluded and price agreement was reached or, if applicable, an earlier date agreed upon between the parties that is as close as practicable to the date of agreement on price [@CL-EST-3192; @CL-EST-9462], and says the certification includes the data supporting any advance agreements and forward pricing rate agreements between the offeror and the Government that are part of the proposal [@CL-EST-3193; @CL-EST-0171]. RFO paragraph (b) says the certificate does not constitute a representation as to the accuracy of the contractor's judgment on the estimate of future costs or projections and applies to the data upon which the judgment or estimate was based [@CL-EST-3194; @CL-EST-9463; @CL-EST-0172], and paragraph (d) says possession of the certificate is not a substitute for examining and analyzing the contractor's proposal [@CL-EST-3195; @CL-EST-0173]. The rule on data later found to fall within an exception moved to RFO FAR 15.403-3(f), which says that if certified cost or pricing data are requested and submitted by an offeror, but an exception is later found to apply, the data must not be considered certified cost or pricing data and must not be certified in accordance with 15.403-4 [@CL-EST-3196]; the codified paragraph says requested by the Government and uses shall not [@CL-EST-0174].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor submits certified cost or pricing data in support of a proposal and executes a certificate stating the data are accurate, complete, and current as of the date of agreement. Later, the contracting officer finds a commercial-product exception applies. The data are then not treated as certified cost or pricing data.

## Never confuse

The certificate certifies facts and data, not the contractor's judgment on future costs; it is a statement about the data, not a substitute for the government's examination of the proposal [@CL-EST-0172; @CL-EST-0173]. A citation to FAR 15.406-2 means the certificate in the codified text [@CL-EST-0170]; in the RFO text the certificate is at 15.403-4 [@CL-EST-3190], and RFO FAR 15.406 is data to support proposal analysis [@CL-EST-3219].
