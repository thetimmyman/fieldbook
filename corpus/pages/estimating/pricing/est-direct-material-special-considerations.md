## Purpose

Shows four kinds of direct material that need a closer look in a price proposal: long-term agreements, government-furnished material, residual inventory from earlier contracts, and scrap and process loss.

When a long-term agreement exceeds the certified cost or pricing data threshold and does not meet an exception, the contractor must obtain certified cost or pricing data as of the date the agreement was executed and keep documented analyses showing continuing price reasonableness for current proposals [@CL-EST-0694]. Such agreements are valid pricing mechanisms, but their presence does not waive statutory requirements [@CL-EST-0933]. The contractor is prohibited from including cost estimates for government-furnished material or government-furnished property in its pricing proposals [@CL-EST-0695], and may not propose new shipping containers when reusable Government-owned containers are contractually available or can be modified for reuse [@CL-EST-0935]. For other direct costs, the evaluation includes ensuring that costs for government-furnished materials are excluded [@CL-EST-0720]; see [Other direct costs](est-other-direct-costs.md). Scrap, spoilage, rework and process loss may be estimated as direct costs only if consistently accounted for and estimated in accordance with the contractor's disclosed accounting practices and with applicable CAS [@CL-EST-0698].

## How it works

For residual inventory from earlier government contracts, ownership decides the price. When pricing follow-on contracts, government-owned material, for example from closed cost-type contracts or specific settlement terms, must be proposed at no cost [@CL-EST-0696]. Contractor-owned inventory, for example from firm-fixed-price contracts, completed progress-payment-clause property or settled fixed-price contracts, is priced at the lower of actual cost or current market price [@CL-EST-0697]. For open cost-type contracts, materials are proposed at actual cost, and contractors must have strong internal controls to prevent duplicate Government billing [@CL-EST-0936]. Process loss, the material lost in manufacturing such as trim, chips and excess casting, may be estimated with an overall factor or with separate factors for major subelements [@CL-EST-0699], and bill of material quantities for items made from raw material are frequently adjusted to include process loss factors [@CL-EST-0939].

In evaluating proposals, auditors need to ensure that estimated costs for scrap, spoilage and rework, however proposed, comply with applicable CAS, account for salvageable materials and reflect operational efficiency [@CL-EST-0937]. Special attention should be given to a contractor purchasing parts from surplus or salvage dealers, especially when the contractor has declared parts surplus and then repurchases similar parts at a later date [@CL-EST-9489]. This may indicate poor procurement practices and/or a condition reportable as a suspected irregularity [@CL-EST-0938]. Auditors are also to notify the contracting officer of inadequate long-term agreement analyses, start assist audits where appropriate, and consider system deficiencies if the contractor does not keep validating the pricing [@CL-EST-0934].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A follow-on proposal has leftover bar stock from a closed firm-fixed-price order that the contractor owns. Market price today is below actual cost, so it is priced at market. Another lot of material came from a government-owned pool, so it carries no cost. The contractor also adds a trim-loss factor to the sheet metal quantity and keeps its disclosed accounting method for that loss.

## Never confuse

Whether a long-term agreement needs certified data still turns on the threshold and the exceptions [@CL-EST-0694]. The contractor is prohibited from including cost estimates for government-furnished material in its pricing proposals [@CL-EST-0695]. When pricing follow-on contracts, government-owned residual material from preceding Government contracts must be proposed at no cost [@CL-EST-0696].
