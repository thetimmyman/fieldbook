## Purpose

Shows the elements of a firm target fixed-price incentive contract, how final profit moves with final cost, and when the contract type may be used.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-EST-1076] |
| Codified citation | FAR 16.403-1 [@CL-EST-1076] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |

| Applies to | Fixed-price incentive (firm target) contracts [@CL-EST-1076] |

A fixed-price incentive (firm target) contract specifies a target cost, a target profit, a price ceiling but not a profit ceiling or floor, and a profit adjustment formula, all negotiated at the outset [@CL-EST-1076]. The price ceiling is the maximum that may be paid to the contractor, except for any adjustment under other contract clauses [@CL-EST-1077].

The contract type is appropriate when the parties can negotiate at the outset a firm target cost, target profit and adjustment formula that give a fair and reasonable incentive, with a ceiling under which the contractor assumes an appropriate share of the risk [@CL-EST-1079].

## How it works

A final cost below the target cost gives a final profit above the target profit, and a final cost above the target cost gives a final profit below it, or even a net loss [@CL-EST-1078]. If the final negotiated cost exceeds the price ceiling, the contractor absorbs the difference as a loss [@CL-EST-1105]. The contract type may be used only when both listed conditions are met. The first is that the contractor's accounting system is adequate for providing data to support negotiation of final cost and incentive price revision [@CL-EST-1080]. The second is that adequate cost or pricing information for establishing reasonable firm targets is available at the time of initial contract negotiation [@CL-EST-1081].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contract has a target cost of 1,000,000, a target profit of 100,000 and a price ceiling of 1,150,000. The invented formula gives the contractor 20 cents of every dollar saved or overrun. At a final cost of 900,000 the profit is 120,000 and the price is 1,020,000. At a final cost of 1,100,000 the formula gives a profit of 80,000, but the price of 1,180,000 would pass the ceiling, so the price is held at 1,150,000 and the profit is 50,000. At a final cost of 1,200,000 the price stays at the ceiling, and the contractor absorbs a loss of 50,000.

## Never confuse

A firm target contract fixes the target cost and target profit at the outset and has a price ceiling but no profit ceiling or floor [@CL-EST-1076]. A cost-plus-incentive-fee contract instead has minimum and maximum fees [@CL-EST-1091]. In a successive targets contract, the production point at which the firm target cost and firm target profit will be negotiated is usually before delivery or shop completion of the first item [@CL-EST-1084]. For the contract-type profit factor, see [DoD weighted guidelines: contract type risk and working capital adjustment](est-weighted-guidelines-contract-type-risk.md). For the adequacy tests, see [Accounting system criteria](../../compliance/business-systems/cmp-accounting-system-criteria.md).
