## Purpose

Shows how orders are issued under a delivery or task order contract, what the Government must order under a definite quantity contract, and what the requirements clause does and does not promise the Contractor.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-EST-1835] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.216-18, FAR 52.216-20 and FAR 52.216-21 [@CL-EST-1835; @CL-EST-1837; @CL-EST-1839]; FAR 2.101 [@CL-EST-9317] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.216-18 [@CL-EST-0386]; RFO FAR 52.216-20 [@CL-EST-0391]; RFO FAR 52.216-21 [@CL-EST-0396]; RFO FAR 2.101 [@CL-EST-9367] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-EST-1835] |
| Applies to | Delivery order and task order contracts that include the ordering, definite quantity or requirements clause [@CL-EST-1835; @CL-EST-1837; @CL-EST-1839] |

Supplies and services under the contract shall be ordered by issuing delivery orders or task orders by the individuals or activities designated in the Schedule [@CL-EST-1835]. Such orders may be issued from the start date through the end date inserted in the clause [@CL-EST-1908]. If a delivery order or task order conflicts with the contract, the contract controls [@CL-EST-1836]. A delivery order or task order sent by mail is considered issued when the Government deposits the order in the mail, and one sent by fax when the Government transmits the order to the Contractor's fax number [@CL-EST-1871]. An order sent electronically is considered issued when the Government either posts a copy of the order to a Government document access system and notice is sent to the Contractor, or distributes the order by email to the Contractor's email address [@CL-EST-1909]. Orders may be issued by methods other than those enumerated in the clause only if authorized in the contract [@CL-EST-1910]. The FAR definitions section describes a task order as an order for services placed against an established contract or with Government sources [@CL-EST-9317]. RFO FAR 2.101 defines a task order as an order for services placed against an established contract or with Government sources, the same in substance as the codified definition [@CL-EST-9367; @CL-EST-9317].

Under the definite quantity clause the Government shall order the quantity of supplies or services specified in the Schedule, and the Contractor shall furnish them when ordered [@CL-EST-1837]. Except for quantity limitations in the Order Limitations clause or the Schedule, there is no limit on the number of orders that may be issued under that clause [@CL-EST-1838]. Under the requirements clause, the quantities specified in the Schedule are estimates only and are not purchased by the contract [@CL-EST-1870]. Under the definite quantity clause, an order issued during the effective period and not completed within that time shall be completed by the Contractor within the time specified in the order [@CL-EST-1912], the contract governs the rights and obligations for that order as if it were completed during the effective period [@CL-EST-1913], provided that the Contractor is not required to make deliveries after the date inserted in the clause [@CL-EST-1913; @CL-EST-9516; @CL-EST-1914]. The requirements clause says the same for an order issued during its effective period and not completed within that period [@CL-EST-1915; @CL-EST-1916; @CL-EST-9517; @CL-EST-1917].

## How it works

The ordering clause sets who may issue orders, that the contract wins in a conflict, and when an order counts as issued [@CL-EST-1835; @CL-EST-1836; @CL-EST-1871; @CL-EST-1909; @CL-EST-1910]. The definite quantity clause then fixes what the Government must order [@CL-EST-1837]. Under the requirements clause, except as the contract may otherwise provide, if requirements do not result in orders in the quantities described as estimated or maximum, that is not a basis for an equitable price adjustment [@CL-EST-1839]. In the basic clause, except as the contract otherwise provides, the Government shall order from the Contractor all the supplies or services in the Schedule that the specified activity is required to purchase [@CL-EST-1840], but is not required to purchase requirements above any limit on total orders [@CL-EST-1841]. Alternate I and Alternate III each substitute a different paragraph (c) for paragraph (c) of the basic clause, in the circumstances they describe [@CL-EST-9695; @CL-EST-9694]. Where the Government urgently requires delivery of any quantity of an item before the earliest date that delivery may be specified and the Contractor will not accept an order for accelerated delivery, the Government may acquire the urgently required goods or services from another source [@CL-EST-9518; @CL-EST-1869].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text from February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. RFO FAR 52.216-18 keeps the rules that supplies and services are ordered by delivery orders or task orders issued by the individuals or activities designated in the Schedule and that such orders may be issued from one inserted date through another [@CL-EST-0386; @CL-EST-1835; @CL-EST-1908]; that the contract controls in a conflict between an order and the contract [@CL-EST-0387; @CL-EST-1836]; that an order sent by mail is considered issued when the Government deposits it in the mail and one sent by fax when the Government transmits it to the Contractor’s fax number [@CL-EST-0388; @CL-EST-1871]; that an order sent electronically is considered issued when the Government posts a copy to a Government document access system and notice is sent to the Contractor, or distributes it by email to the Contractor’s email address [@CL-EST-0389; @CL-EST-1909]; and that orders may be issued by other methods only if authorized in the contract [@CL-EST-0390; @CL-EST-1910]. RFO FAR 52.216-20 keeps the rule that the Government shall order the Schedule quantity and the Contractor shall furnish it when ordered [@CL-EST-0391; @CL-EST-1837], and the rule that, except for limitations in the Order Limitations clause or the Schedule, there is no limit on the number of orders [@CL-EST-0392; @CL-EST-1838]. Its paragraph (d) says that an order issued during the ordering period and not completed within that time shall be completed within the time specified in the order [@CL-EST-0393], where the codified paragraph says effective period [@CL-EST-1912]. The RFO sentence on the contract governing the order keeps the words effective period [@CL-EST-0394; @CL-EST-1913], and the RFO proviso keeps the rule that no deliveries are required after the date inserted in the clause [@CL-EST-0395; @CL-EST-9516; @CL-EST-1914]. RFO FAR 52.216-21 keeps the rule that the Schedule quantities are estimates only and are not purchased by the contract [@CL-EST-0396; @CL-EST-1870], and the rule that, except as the contract may otherwise provide, requirements that do not result in orders in the estimated or maximum quantities are not a basis for an equitable price adjustment [@CL-EST-0397; @CL-EST-1839]. It keeps the Government’s duty, except as the contract otherwise provides, to order from the Contractor all the supplies or services the specified activity is required to purchase [@CL-EST-0398; @CL-EST-1840], the rule that the Government is not required to purchase requirements in excess of any limit on total orders [@CL-EST-0399; @CL-EST-1841], and the rule that the Government may acquire urgently required goods or services from another source when it urgently requires delivery of any quantity of an item before the earliest date that delivery may be specified and the Contractor will not accept an order for accelerated delivery [@CL-EST-0400; @CL-EST-0409; @CL-EST-9518; @CL-EST-1869]. Its paragraph (f) says ordering period where the codified paragraph says effective period, both in the sentence on completing orders [@CL-EST-0410; @CL-EST-1915] and in the sentence on the contract governing the order [@CL-EST-0411; @CL-EST-1916], and keeps the proviso that no deliveries are required after the date inserted in the clause [@CL-EST-0412; @CL-EST-9517; @CL-EST-1917].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A requirements contract lists an estimate of 10,000 units a year. The activity orders only 6,000 units. Because the Schedule quantities are estimates only and are not purchased by the contract, the Contractor has no basis for an equitable price adjustment, unless the contract otherwise provides. Later the activity needs 1,000 units before the earliest delivery date the contract allows. The Contractor will not accept an order for accelerated delivery, so the Government may buy those 1,000 units from another source. Under a definite quantity contract for 500 units, the Government must order those 500 units. Suppose an order for 200 units is issued in the last month of the effective period and is not finished by the expiry date. The contract still governs that order, and the Contractor completes and delivers it within the time stated in the order, but it need not make deliveries after the date inserted in the clause. An order mailed on the fifth, on a date within the period the ordering clause states, is issued when the Government puts it in the mail.

## Never confuse

Under the definite quantity clause the Government shall order the quantity specified in the Schedule [@CL-EST-1837], while under the requirements clause the Schedule quantities are estimates only and are not purchased by the contract [@CL-EST-1870] and, in the basic clause and except as the contract otherwise provides, the Government shall order from the Contractor all the supplies or services in the Schedule that the specified activity is required to purchase [@CL-EST-1840]. Minimum and maximum order rules are in a different clause, covered in [Order limitations and indefinite quantity clauses](est-order-limitations-and-indefinite-quantity-clauses.md), and fair opportunity among awardees is in [Ordering under indefinite-delivery contracts](est-far-ordering-indefinite-delivery-contracts.md). The requirements contract as a contract type is explained in [Requirements contracts](../../contract-finance/funding/ctf-requirements-contracts.md), and the provision that names the contemplated contract type is in [Type of contract and letter contract provisions](est-type-of-contract-letter-and-time-and-materials-provisions.md).

RFO FAR 52.216-20(d) says ordering period in its sentence on completing orders [@CL-EST-0393; @CL-EST-0394] but effective period in its sentence on the contract governing the order [@CL-EST-0394], while RFO FAR 52.216-21(f) says ordering period in both sentences [@CL-EST-0410; @CL-EST-0411]. The codified text says effective period in all of these sentences [@CL-EST-1912; @CL-EST-1913; @CL-EST-1915; @CL-EST-1916].
