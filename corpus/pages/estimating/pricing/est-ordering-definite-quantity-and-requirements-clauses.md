## Purpose

Shows how orders are issued under a delivery or task order contract, what the Government must order under a definite quantity contract, and what the requirements clause does and does not promise the Contractor.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-EST-1835] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.216-18, FAR 52.216-20 and FAR 52.216-21 [@CL-EST-1835; @CL-EST-1837; @CL-EST-1839] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-EST-1835] |
| Applies to | Delivery order and task order contracts that include the ordering, definite quantity or requirements clause [@CL-EST-1835; @CL-EST-1837; @CL-EST-1839] |

Supplies and services under the contract shall be ordered by issuing delivery orders or task orders by the individuals or activities designated in the Schedule [@CL-EST-1835]. Such orders may be issued from the start date through the end date that the Contracting Officer inserts in the clause [@CL-EST-1908]. If a delivery order or task order conflicts with the contract, the contract controls [@CL-EST-1836]. A delivery order or task order sent by mail is considered issued when the Government deposits the order in the mail, and one sent by fax when the Government transmits the order to the Contractor's fax number [@CL-EST-1871]. An order sent electronically is considered issued when the Government either posts a copy of the order to a Government document access system and notice is sent to the Contractor, or distributes the order by email to the Contractor's email address [@CL-EST-1909]. Orders may be issued by methods other than those enumerated in the clause only if authorized in the contract [@CL-EST-1910].

Under the definite quantity clause the Government shall order the quantity of supplies or services specified in the Schedule, and the Contractor shall furnish them when ordered [@CL-EST-1837]. Except for quantity limitations in the Order Limitations clause or the Schedule, there is no limit on the number of orders that may be issued under that clause [@CL-EST-1838]. Under the requirements clause, the quantities specified in the Schedule are estimates only and are not purchased by the contract [@CL-EST-1870]. Under the definite quantity clause, an order issued during the effective period and not completed within that time shall be completed by the Contractor within the time specified in the order [@CL-EST-1912], the contract governs the rights and obligations for that order as if it were completed during the effective period [@CL-EST-1913], and the Contractor is not required to make deliveries after the date inserted in the clause [@CL-EST-1914]. The requirements clause says the same for an order issued during its effective period and not completed within that period [@CL-EST-1915; @CL-EST-1916; @CL-EST-1917].

## How it works

The ordering clause sets who may issue orders, that the contract wins in a conflict, and when an order counts as issued [@CL-EST-1835; @CL-EST-1836; @CL-EST-1871; @CL-EST-1909; @CL-EST-1910]. The definite quantity clause then fixes what the Government must order [@CL-EST-1837]. The requirements clause works differently: except as the contract may otherwise provide, if requirements do not result in orders in the quantities described as estimated or maximum, that is not a basis for an equitable price adjustment [@CL-EST-1839]. The Government shall order from the Contractor all the supplies or services in the Schedule that the specified activity is required to purchase [@CL-EST-1840], but is not required to purchase requirements above any limit on total orders [@CL-EST-1841]. Where a quantity is wanted before the earliest date that delivery may be specified and the Contractor will not accept an order for accelerated delivery, the Government may acquire the urgently required goods or services from another source [@CL-EST-1869].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A requirements contract lists an estimate of 10,000 units a year. The activity orders only 6,000 units. Because the Schedule quantities are estimates only and are not purchased by the contract, the Contractor has no basis for an equitable price adjustment, unless the contract otherwise provides. Later the activity needs 1,000 units before the earliest delivery date the contract allows. The Contractor will not accept an order for accelerated delivery, so the Government may buy those 1,000 units from another source. Under a definite quantity contract for 500 units, the Government must order those 500 units. Suppose an order for 200 units is issued in the last month of the effective period and is not finished by the expiry date. The contract still governs that order, and the Contractor completes and delivers it within the time stated in the order, but it need not make deliveries after the date inserted in the clause. An order mailed on the fifth, on a date within the period the ordering clause states, is issued when the Government puts it in the mail.

## Never confuse

A definite quantity contract commits the Government to order the Schedule quantity [@CL-EST-1837], while a requirements contract treats the Schedule quantities as estimates and ties the Government to ordering what its activity requires [@CL-EST-1839; @CL-EST-1840; @CL-EST-1870]. Minimum and maximum order rules are in a different clause, covered in [Order limitations and indefinite quantity clauses](est-order-limitations-and-indefinite-quantity-clauses.md), and fair opportunity among awardees is in [Ordering under indefinite-delivery contracts](est-far-ordering-indefinite-delivery-contracts.md). The requirements contract as a contract type is explained in [Requirements contracts](../../contract-finance/funding/ctf-requirements-contracts.md), and the provision that names the contemplated contract type is in [Type of contract and letter contract provisions](est-type-of-contract-letter-contract-and-time-and-materials-provisions.md).
