## Purpose

Shows what an offer on a commercial products and commercial services solicitation must contain, and how submission, product samples, late receipt, withdrawal, the acceptance period, multiple offers, multiple awards, the award basis and debriefing work under the instructions to offerors provision.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-EST-2261] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.212-1 [@CL-EST-2261] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.212-1 [@CL-EST-0098; @CL-EST-0137; @CL-EST-0148] |
| Edition date | SEP 2023 [@CL-EST-9773] |
| Text status | Final [@CL-EST-2261] |
| Applies to | Solicitations that carry the instructions to offerors provision, which is inserted as prescribed in FAR 12.301(b)(1) [@CL-EST-2311] |

Signed and dated offers must be submitted to the office specified in the solicitation at or before the exact time specified in the solicitation [@CL-EST-2261]. Offers may be submitted on the SF 1449, on letterhead stationery, or as the solicitation otherwise specifies [@CL-EST-2316].

As a minimum an offer must show the solicitation number, the time set for receipt of offers, and the offeror's name, address and telephone number [@CL-EST-9471; @CL-EST-2262]. It must also show a technical description of the items offered in enough detail to evaluate compliance, which may include product literature or other documents if necessary [@CL-EST-2263]. It must also show the terms of any express warranty, its price and any discount terms, and a remit-to address if that differs from the mailing address [@CL-EST-2317]. It must also show a completed copy of the representations and certifications at FAR 52.212-3, completed electronically where FAR 52.212-3(b) requires, and an acknowledgment of solicitation amendments [@CL-EST-2318]. When past performance is included as an evaluation factor, it must also show past performance information, including recent and relevant contracts for the same or similar items and other references (including contract numbers, points of contact with telephone numbers and other relevant information) [@CL-EST-2319].

An offer that is not submitted on the SF 1449 must include a statement specifying the extent of its agreement with all terms, conditions and provisions in the solicitation [@CL-EST-2320]. An offer that fails to furnish required representations or information, or that rejects the terms and conditions of the solicitation, may be excluded from consideration [@CL-EST-2264].

The offeror agrees to hold the prices in its offer firm for 30 calendar days from the date specified for receipt of offers, unless another time period is specified in an addendum to the solicitation [@CL-EST-2265].

The NAICS code and small business size standard for the acquisition appear elsewhere in the solicitation [@CL-EST-2400]. However, the provision sets a small business size standard for a concern that proposes to furnish an end item it did not itself manufacture, process or produce, other than on a construction or service acquisition: 500 employees [@CL-EST-2312], or 150 employees for information technology value-added resellers under NAICS code 541519 [@CL-EST-2313]. That standard applies if the acquisition meets one of three conditions: it is set aside for small business and has a value above the simplified acquisition threshold, or it uses the HUBZone price evaluation preference regardless of dollar value unless the offeror waives that preference [@CL-EST-2314]; or it is an 8(a), HUBZone, service-disabled veteran-owned, economically disadvantaged women-owned or women-owned small business set-aside or sole-source award regardless of dollar value [@CL-EST-2315].

## How it works

Offerors are responsible for getting offers, modifications, revisions and withdrawals to the designated Government office by the time the solicitation specifies [@CL-EST-2323]. If the solicitation specifies no time, the time for receipt is 4:30 in the afternoon, local time, for the designated office on the date offers or revisions are due [@CL-EST-2324].

An offer, modification, revision or withdrawal received at the designated office after the exact time specified for receipt is late [@CL-EST-2325]. A late item is not considered unless it is received before award is made and the contracting officer determines that accepting it would not unduly delay the acquisition [@CL-EST-2266]. In addition, one of three further conditions must be met. The first is that, if it was transmitted through an electronic commerce method authorized by the solicitation, it was received at the initial point of entry to the Government infrastructure not later than 5:00 p.m., one working day prior to the date specified for receipt of offers [@CL-EST-2326]. The second is that acceptable evidence shows it was received at the Government installation designated for receipt and was under the Government's control before the time set [@CL-EST-2327]. The third is that, if the solicitation is a request for proposals, it was the only proposal received [@CL-EST-2328].

Acceptable evidence of the time of receipt at the Government installation includes the installation's time and date stamp on the offer wrapper, other documentary evidence of receipt that the installation maintains, or oral testimony or statements of Government personnel [@CL-EST-2329]. A late modification of an otherwise successful offer that makes its terms more favorable to the Government is considered at any time it is received and may be accepted [@CL-EST-2267].

If an emergency or unanticipated event interrupts normal Government processes so that offers cannot be received at the designated office by the exact time specified [@CL-EST-2330], and urgent Government requirements preclude amendment of the solicitation or other notice of an extension of the closing date [@CL-EST-2331], the time for receipt is deemed extended to the same time of day on the first work day on which normal Government processes resume [@CL-EST-2332].

An offer may be withdrawn by written notice received at any time before the exact time set for receipt, and an oral offer made in response to an oral solicitation may be withdrawn orally [@CL-EST-2268]. If the solicitation authorizes facsimile offers, an offer may be withdrawn by facsimile received before that time, subject to the solicitation's conditions on facsimile offers [@CL-EST-2333]. An offer may also be withdrawn in person by the offeror or its authorized representative if, before that time, the person's identity is established and the person signs a receipt for the offer [@CL-EST-2334].

When the solicitation requires product samples, they must be submitted at or before the time set for receipt of offers [@CL-EST-2321]. Unless the solicitation says otherwise, samples must be submitted at no expense to the Government and returned at the sender's request and expense, unless they are destroyed during preaward testing [@CL-EST-2322].

Offerors are encouraged to submit multiple offers presenting alternative terms and conditions, including alternative line items consistent with FAR subpart 4.10, or alternative commercial products or commercial services [@CL-EST-2271]. Each offer is evaluated separately [@CL-EST-2272].

Except on an invitation for bids, the Government intends to evaluate offers and award a contract without discussions, so the initial offer should contain the offeror's best terms from a price and technical standpoint [@CL-EST-2269]. It reserves the right to conduct discussions if the contracting officer later determines they are necessary [@CL-EST-2335]. It may reject any or all offers if that is in the public interest, accept other than the lowest offer, and waive informalities and minor irregularities [@CL-EST-2270].

The Government may accept any item or group of items of an offer unless the offeror qualifies the offer by specific limitations [@CL-EST-2336]. Unless the Schedule provides otherwise, offers may not be submitted for quantities less than those specified [@CL-EST-2337]. The Government reserves the right to award any item for a quantity less than the quantity offered, at the unit prices offered, unless the offeror specifies otherwise in the offer [@CL-EST-2338].

The General Services Administration index of Federal specifications, standards and commercial item descriptions, and copies of Federal specifications, standards and product descriptions, can be downloaded from the ASSIST website [@CL-EST-2339]. Nongovernment (voluntary) standards must be obtained from the organization responsible for their preparation, publication or maintenance [@CL-EST-2340]. Other sources of requirements documents, including those for Defense specifications and standards and for solicitations issued by certain agencies, are covered on [Availability of specifications, standards and data item descriptions](est-availability-of-specifications-standards-and-dids.md).

The unique entity identifier paragraph applies to all offers that exceed the micro-purchase threshold, and to offers at or below that threshold if the solicitation requires registration in the System for Award Management [@CL-EST-2341]. The offeror shall enter, in the block with its name and address on the cover page of its offer, the annotation Unique Entity Identifier followed by the identifier for its name and address [@CL-EST-2342]. It shall also enter its Electronic Funds Transfer indicator, if applicable, which is a four-character suffix to the identifier [@CL-EST-2343]. An offeror without an identifier should contact the entity designated at www.sam.gov to obtain one [@CL-EST-2401].

If a post-award debriefing is given to requesting offerors, the Government shall disclose, if applicable, its evaluation of the significant weak or deficient factors in the debriefed offeror's offer [@CL-EST-2344]. It shall also disclose, if applicable, the overall evaluated cost or price and technical rating of the successful and debriefed offerors, past performance information on the debriefed offeror, and the overall ranking of all offerors when the agency developed any ranking during source selection [@CL-EST-2345]. It shall also disclose, if applicable, a summary of the rationale for award and, for acquisitions of commercial products, the make and model of the product the successful offeror will deliver [@CL-EST-2346]. It shall also give reasonable responses to relevant questions about whether the source-selection procedures in the solicitation, applicable regulations and other authorities were followed [@CL-EST-2347]. See [Postaward debriefing](est-postaward-debriefing.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A solicitation sets receipt for 2:00 in the afternoon on 12 March. An offeror on its own letterhead, not the SF 1449, submits a base offer at 250,000 and an additional offer at 238,000 with alternative line items consistent with FAR subpart 4.10. Each includes a statement of the extent of its agreement with the solicitation's terms. The two offers are evaluated separately, and both prices stay firm for 30 calendar days from 12 March (60 calendar days under the RFO text [@CL-EST-0137]).

The offeror then mails a revision of the base offer. It reaches the designated office at 2:20 in the afternoon, after the exact time, so it is late. The mailroom stamp at the Government installation shows it was received there at 1:40 in the afternoon, and it was under Government control before 2:00. Award has not been made, and the contracting officer determines that accepting the revision would not unduly delay the acquisition. The revision can be considered because the installation stamp is acceptable evidence of receipt at the installation before the time set. Under the RFO text, a late revision is not considered unless it is received before award is made and the Contracting Officer determines that accepting it would not unduly delay the acquisition, and both conditions are met here [@CL-EST-0139].

## Never confuse

An offer may be withdrawn by written notice received at any time before the exact time set for receipt of offers [@CL-EST-2268], while an offer, modification, revision or withdrawal received after that time is late and will not be considered unless it is received before award is made, the contracting officer determines that accepting it would not unduly delay the acquisition, and a further condition is met [@CL-EST-2325; @CL-EST-2266]. However, a late modification of an otherwise successful offer that makes its terms more favorable to the Government will be considered at any time it is received and may be accepted [@CL-EST-2267].

Offerors are encouraged to submit multiple offers, and each offer submitted is evaluated separately [@CL-EST-2271; @CL-EST-2272]. The Government may accept any item or group of items of an offer, unless the offeror qualifies the offer by specific limitations [@CL-EST-2336].

The emergency extension needs both an interruption and urgent Government requirements that preclude amendment or other notice of an extension [@CL-EST-2330; @CL-EST-2331]. See also [Instructions to offerors for competitive acquisitions](est-instructions-to-offerors-competitive-acquisition.md) and [Late bid submissions and withdrawals](est-late-bid-submissions-and-withdrawals.md).

The acceptance period depends on the text in use: the codified provision holds prices firm for 30 calendar days [@CL-EST-2265] and the RFO provision holds them firm for 60 calendar days [@CL-EST-0137]. The late-offer conditions also differ. The codified provision also requires that one of three further conditions be met besides receipt before award and no undue delay [@CL-EST-2326] [@CL-EST-2327] [@CL-EST-2328], while the RFO provision prints only those two conditions [@CL-EST-0139]. For withdrawal, the codified provision accepts written notice received before the exact time set for receipt [@CL-EST-2268], while the RFO provision accepts written notice received at any time before award [@CL-EST-0144].
