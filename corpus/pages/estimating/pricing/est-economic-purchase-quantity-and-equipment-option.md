## Purpose

Shows the invitation to comment on purchase quantities and the mechanics of an option to purchase leased or rented equipment.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-EST-2302] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.207-4 [@CL-EST-2302]; FAR 52.207-5 [@CL-EST-2305] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.207-4 [@CL-EST-0189] and RFO FAR 52.207-5 [@CL-EST-0193] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-EST-2302] |
| Applies to | Solicitations that invite opinions on the quantity of supplies [@CL-EST-2302], and contracts under which equipment is provided on a lease or rental basis [@CL-EST-2391] |

Offerors are invited to state an opinion on whether the quantity of supplies on which bids, proposals or quotes are requested is economically advantageous to the Government [@CL-EST-2302]. An offeror that believes acquisitions in different quantities would be more advantageous is invited to recommend an economic purchase quantity, and where different quantities are recommended a total and a unit price must be quoted for applicable items [@CL-EST-2303]. An economic purchase quantity is the quantity at which a significant price break occurs [@CL-EST-2304], and if there are significant price breaks at different quantity points, this information is desired as well [@CL-EST-2388].

The information is requested to avoid acquisitions in disadvantageous quantities and to help the Government build a database for future acquisitions of these items [@CL-EST-2389]. The Government reserves the right to amend or cancel the solicitation and resolicit with respect to any individual item in the event quotations received and the Government's requirements indicate that different quantities should be acquired [@CL-EST-2390].

## How it works

The Government may purchase the equipment provided on a lease or rental basis under the contract [@CL-EST-2391]. The contracting officer may exercise the option only by a unilateral modification to the contractor, and the modification states the effective date of the purchase, which may be any time during the contract period including extensions [@CL-EST-2305]. Except for final payment and transfer of title, the lease or rental portion is complete, and lease or rental charges are discontinued, on the day immediately preceding the effective date of purchase stated in the modification [@CL-EST-2392].

The purchase conversion cost is computed as of the effective date stated in the modification [@CL-EST-2393]. It is based on the purchase price set out in the contract less the total purchase option credits accumulated during the lease or rental, calculated by the formula contained elsewhere in the contract [@CL-EST-2306]. The credits also include credits accrued during a lease or rental of the equipment under any previous Government contract if the equipment has been on continuous lease or rental [@CL-EST-2394], and moving equipment from one site to another site is continuous rental [@CL-EST-2395].

Under Class Deviation 2026-O0038, effective February 1, 2026, contracting officers shall use the revised FAR Part 52, Solicitation Provisions and Contract Clauses, in lieu of the text codified at 48 CFR chapter 1 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. The RFO economic purchase quantity provision keeps the invitation to state an opinion on whether the quantity of supplies is economically advantageous [@CL-EST-0189] [@CL-EST-2302], the invitation to recommend an economic purchase quantity, with a total and a unit price quoted for applicable items where different quantities are recommended [@CL-EST-0190] [@CL-EST-2303], the meaning of an economic purchase quantity and the request for information on price breaks at different quantity points [@CL-EST-0191] [@CL-EST-2304] [@CL-EST-2388], and the Government's right to amend or cancel the solicitation and resolicit with respect to any individual item in the event quotations received and its requirements indicate that different quantities should be acquired [@CL-EST-0192] [@CL-EST-2390]. It also keeps the statement that the information is solicited to avoid acquisitions in disadvantageous quantities and to assist the Government in developing a data base for future acquisitions of these items [@CL-EST-9071] [@CL-EST-2389]. The RFO equipment option clause keeps the Government's option to purchase equipment provided on a lease or rental basis, exercised only by a unilateral modification that states the effective date [@CL-EST-0193] [@CL-EST-0194] [@CL-EST-2391] [@CL-EST-2305], the end of lease or rental charges, except for final payment and transfer of title to the Government, on the day immediately preceding the effective date of purchase [@CL-EST-0195] [@CL-EST-2392], the computation of the purchase conversion cost as of the effective date specified in the unilateral modification [@CL-EST-9072] [@CL-EST-2393], on the basis of the purchase price less the accumulated purchase option credits, calculated by the formula contained elsewhere in the contract [@CL-EST-0196] [@CL-EST-2306], the inclusion of credits accrued under a previous Government contract on continuous lease or rental [@CL-EST-0197] [@CL-EST-2394] and the rule that moving equipment from one site to another is continuous rental [@CL-EST-0198] [@CL-EST-2395].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An offeror is asked to quote 1,000 units. It believes 1,200 units would reach a significant price break, so it recommends 1,200 as the economic purchase quantity. It quotes 1,000 units at a unit price of 50, for a total of 50,000, and 1,200 units at a unit price of 46, for a total of 55,200.

On the equipment option, the contract sets a purchase price of 80,000. The credits accumulated under the contract by the effective date, by the contract's formula, are 15,000. The same equipment was on continuous rental under a previous Government contract, including a move between sites, and that contract accrued 5,000 of credits. The total purchase option credits are 20,000, so the purchase conversion cost is 80,000 minus 20,000, or 60,000. The unilateral modification is dated 15 June and states an effective date of 1 July, so lease charges stop on 30 June. Final payment and transfer of title are handled separately.

## Never confuse

An economic purchase quantity is the quantity at which a significant price break occurs [@CL-EST-2304]. Where different quantities are recommended, the offeror must quote a total and a unit price for applicable items [@CL-EST-2303].

The Contracting Officer may exercise the equipment option only by providing a unilateral modification to the contractor [@CL-EST-2305], and the purchase conversion cost is based on the purchase price set forth in the contract, minus the total purchase option credits accumulated during the period of lease or rental [@CL-EST-2306].

The variation in quantity policy is covered in [Variation in quantity policy for supply and construction contracts](../../supply-chain/procure-to-pay/sup-variation-in-quantity-policy.md), and the option solicitation provisions and contract clauses are covered in [Option solicitation provisions and contract clauses](../../contract-finance/funding/ctf-option-clauses-and-provisions.md).
