## Purpose

Shows when a late technical proposal under step one of two-step sealed bidding can still be considered, what counts as evidence of receipt, when an emergency extends the time for receipt, and how a technical proposal is withdrawn.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-EST-2153] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.214-23 [@CL-EST-2153] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.214-23 [@CL-EST-0829; @CL-EST-0831] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-EST-2153] |
| Applies to | Technical proposals under step one of two-step sealed bidding, as set by the request for technical proposals [@CL-EST-2154] |

Bidders are responsible for submitting technical proposals, and any modifications or revisions, so as to reach the designated Government office by the time specified in the invitation for bids [@CL-EST-2153]. If the invitation for bids specifies no time, the time for receipt is 4:30 in the afternoon, local time, for the designated Government office on the date that bids or revisions are due [@CL-EST-2191].

A technical proposal under step one, or a modification, revision or withdrawal of one, received at the designated office after the exact time specified for receipt will not be considered unless the conditions that follow are met [@CL-EST-2154]. The first condition is that the Contracting Officer determines that accepting the late technical proposal would not unduly delay the acquisition, and one of the further listed conditions must also be met [@CL-EST-2155]. Three further conditions are listed and are joined by or [@CL-EST-2156; @CL-EST-2157; @CL-EST-2158].

The first further condition is that, if the proposal was transmitted through an electronic commerce method authorized by the request for technical proposals, it was received at the initial point of entry to the Government infrastructure not later than 5:00 in the afternoon one working day before the date specified for receipt of proposals [@CL-EST-2156]. The second is that there is acceptable evidence that it was received at the Government installation designated for receipt of offers and was under the Government's control before the time set for receipt [@CL-EST-2157]. The third is that it is the only proposal received and it is negotiated under part 15 of the Federal Acquisition Regulation [@CL-EST-2158].

A late modification of an otherwise successful proposal that makes its terms more favorable to the Government will be considered at any time it is received and may be accepted [@CL-EST-2192].

## How it works

Acceptable evidence of the time of receipt at the Government installation includes the installation's time and date stamp on the technical proposal wrapper, other documentary evidence of receipt maintained by the installation, or oral testimony or statements of Government personnel [@CL-EST-2159].

The time for receipt can be extended after an emergency. Two conditions apply. An emergency or unanticipated event interrupts normal Government processes so that technical proposals cannot be received at the designated office by the exact time specified in the request for technical proposals [@CL-EST-2193], and urgent Government requirements preclude amendment of the request [@CL-EST-2228]. The time specified for receipt is then deemed extended to the same time of day specified in the request for technical proposals on the first work day on which normal Government processes resume [@CL-EST-2194].

Technical proposals may be withdrawn by written notice received at any time before the exact time set for receipt of technical proposals [@CL-EST-2160]. If the request authorizes facsimile technical proposals, they may be withdrawn by facsimile received at any time before that time, subject to the conditions in the provision on facsimile bids, FAR 52.214-31 [@CL-EST-2195]. A technical proposal may be withdrawn in person by a bidder or its authorized representative if, before the exact time set for receipt, the identity of the person requesting withdrawal is established and the person signs a receipt for the technical proposal [@CL-EST-2196].

Late bids under an ordinary invitation are covered on [Late submissions, modifications and withdrawals of bids](est-late-bid-submissions-and-withdrawals.md), and when facsimile bids are considered at all is covered on [Sealed bidding: amendments, false statements, bid submission and explanations](est-sealed-bidding-essentials.md). Who may bid in step two is covered on [Step two of two-step sealed bidding: who may bid](est-two-step-sealed-bidding-step-two.md).

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text from February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. The RFO provision has the same responsibility to reach the designated office by the time specified and the same default time of 4:30 in the afternoon, local time [@CL-EST-0829; @CL-EST-0830; @CL-EST-2153; @CL-EST-2191]. It has the same late-proposal rule, with the same no-undue-delay determination and the same three alternative conditions [@CL-EST-0831; @CL-EST-0832; @CL-EST-0833; @CL-EST-0834; @CL-EST-0835; @CL-EST-2154; @CL-EST-2155; @CL-EST-2156; @CL-EST-2157; @CL-EST-2158], the same treatment of a late modification that is more favorable to the Government [@CL-EST-0836; @CL-EST-2192], and the same list of acceptable evidence of receipt [@CL-EST-0837; @CL-EST-2159]. It has the same emergency extension, with both conditions and the same deemed time [@CL-EST-0838; @CL-EST-0839; @CL-EST-0840; @CL-EST-2193; @CL-EST-2228; @CL-EST-2194]. Its paragraph (e) says technical proposals may be withdrawn by written notice received at any time before the exact time set for receipt and then goes on to withdrawal in person [@CL-EST-0841], while the codified paragraph (e) also has a sentence on withdrawal by facsimile [@CL-EST-2195]. The in-person withdrawal rule is worded the same [@CL-EST-0842; @CL-EST-2196]. RFO FAR 52.214-31 is listed as [Reserved] [@CL-EST-0843], and the codified paragraph (e) cites the provision at 52.214-31, Facsimile Bids, for withdrawal by facsimile [@CL-EST-2195].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A request for technical proposals specifies receipt at 2:00 in the afternoon on the due date and authorizes electronic transmission and facsimile technical proposals.

Bidder A's paper proposal reaches the designated office at 2:10, but the installation's time and date stamp on the wrapper reads 1:45. That stamp is acceptable evidence that the proposal was at the installation and under Government control before the time set. If the Contracting Officer also determines that accepting it would not unduly delay the acquisition, it may be considered.

Bidder B's paper proposal reaches the office at 2:05 and nothing shows earlier receipt. It was not sent electronically and other proposals were received. None of the three further conditions is met, so it is not considered, whatever the Contracting Officer finds about delay.

Bidder C sent its proposal electronically and it reached the initial point of entry at 4:00 in the afternoon on the working day before the due date. That is not later than 5:00 that day, so the electronic condition is met and the proposal may be considered if the Contracting Officer finds no undue delay. Had it reached the point of entry at 6:00 that afternoon, the condition would not be met.

In a different acquisition only one proposal arrives, at 3:00, after the time set, and it will be negotiated under part 15. That meets the third further condition, subject to the no-undue-delay determination.

The otherwise successful bidder sends a modification that lowers its price, and it arrives at 4:00. It makes the terms more favorable to the Government, so it is considered whenever it is received and may be accepted.

On the due date an emergency closes the designated office before 2:00, and urgent Government requirements preclude amending the request. The time for receipt is deemed extended to 2:00 in the afternoon on the first work day on which normal Government processes resume.

In another solicitation no time is specified, so proposals and revisions are due by 4:30 in the afternoon, local time, on the due date.

A written withdrawal notice from one bidder arrives at 1:30, before the exact time set, and the proposal is withdrawn. A facsimile withdrawal from another arrives at 1:40, which is also before that time, and is subject to the facsimile bids conditions under the codified paragraph (e) [@CL-EST-2195]; the RFO paragraph (e) prints no facsimile withdrawal sentence [@CL-EST-0841]. A bidder's authorized representative whose identity is established and who signs a receipt for the proposal at 1:50 may withdraw it in person. A written notice that arrives at 2:15 is a late withdrawal and is not considered unless the late-submission conditions are met.

## Never confuse

The three further conditions are joined by or [@CL-EST-2156; @CL-EST-2157; @CL-EST-2158], and the Contracting Officer's determination that accepting the late technical proposal would not unduly delay the acquisition is joined to them by and [@CL-EST-2155].

A late modification of an otherwise successful proposal that makes its terms more favorable to the Government will be considered at any time it is received and may be accepted [@CL-EST-2192].

The emergency extension applies where an emergency or unanticipated event interrupts normal Government processes so that technical proposals cannot be received at the Government office designated for receipt of technical proposals by the exact time specified in the request for technical proposals, and urgent Government requirements preclude amendment of the request for technical proposals [@CL-EST-2193; @CL-EST-2228]. A withdrawal of a technical proposal received at the designated office after the exact time specified for receipt will not be considered unless the further conditions of paragraph (b)(1) are met [@CL-EST-2154]; technical proposals may be withdrawn by written notice received at any time before the exact time set for receipt [@CL-EST-2160].
