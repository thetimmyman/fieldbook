## Purpose

Shows the minimum and maximum order rules in the order limitations clause, the guaranteed minimum in the indefinite quantity clause, and how the effective period works. The contract-type card covers when these contracts are used: [Indefinite-delivery and indefinite-quantity contracts](../../contract-finance/funding/ctf-indefinite-delivery-and-indefinite-quantity-contracts.md).

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-EST-1707; @CL-EST-1710] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.216-19 and FAR 52.216-22 [@CL-EST-1707; @CL-EST-1710] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.216-19 [@CL-EST-0370]; RFO FAR 52.216-22 [@CL-EST-0376] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-EST-1707; @CL-EST-1710] |
| Applies to | Contracts that include the Order Limitations clause or the Indefinite Quantity clause [@CL-EST-1707; @CL-EST-1710] |

The Order Limitations clause is inserted as a clause substantially the same as the printed text [@CL-EST-1707]. Under it, when the Government requires covered supplies or services in an amount of less than a stated figure, neither the Government nor the contractor is obligated to buy or furnish them under the contract [@CL-EST-1677]. The contractor is not obligated to honor an order for a single item, or for a combination of items, in excess of the stated dollar figure or quantity [@CL-EST-1678]. The contractor is also not obligated to honor a series of orders from the same ordering office within a stated number of days that together call for quantities exceeding either of those limitations [@CL-EST-1708].

The contractor shall nevertheless honor an order over the maximum unless it is returned to the ordering office within a stated number of days after issuance with written notice of its intent not to ship and the reasons [@CL-EST-1679]. Upon receiving written notice stating the contractor's intent not to ship the items called for and the reasons, the Government may acquire the supplies or services from another source [@CL-EST-1709].

## How it works

Under the Indefinite Quantity clause, the contract is effective for the period stated in the Schedule, and the Schedule quantities are estimates only and are not purchased by the contract [@CL-EST-1680]. Supplies or services are to be furnished when and if ordered up to the Schedule maximum, and the Government shall order at least the Schedule minimum [@CL-EST-1681]. An order issued during the effective period and not completed within that period shall be completed by the contractor within the time specified in the order [@CL-EST-1682]. The Indefinite Quantity clause adds a proviso that the contractor shall not be required to make any deliveries under the contract after a date stated in the clause [@CL-EST-1711]. The printed text of the two clauses leaves its figures, day counts and that final delivery date as blanks to be filled in, and the minimum and maximum quantities come from the Schedule [@CL-EST-1677; @CL-EST-1678; @CL-EST-1708; @CL-EST-1679; @CL-EST-1681; @CL-EST-1711].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text from February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. In RFO FAR 52.216-19, paragraph (a) keeps the rule that when the Government requires covered supplies or services in an amount of less than a stated figure, neither the Government nor the Contractor is obligated to buy or furnish them under the contract [@CL-EST-0370; @CL-EST-1677]. Paragraphs (b)(1) and (b)(2) keep the rule that the Contractor is not obligated to honor an order for a single item, or for a combination of items, in excess of the stated figure [@CL-EST-0371; @CL-EST-1678]. Paragraph (b)(3) says the series-of-orders limit by reference to paragraph (b)(1) or (2) of this section [@CL-EST-0372; @CL-EST-1708]. Paragraph (d) says that, notwithstanding paragraphs (b) and (c) of this section, the Contractor shall honor any order exceeding the maximum order limitations in paragraph (b) [@CL-EST-0373; @CL-EST-1679], and keeps the exception for an order returned to the ordering office within a stated number of days after issuance with written notice of the intent not to ship and the reasons [@CL-EST-0374; @CL-EST-1679], and the Government’s right to acquire the supplies or services from another source on receiving that notice [@CL-EST-0375; @CL-EST-1709]. In RFO FAR 52.216-22, paragraph (a) keeps the rule that the contract is effective for the period stated in the Schedule and that the Schedule quantities are estimates only and are not purchased by the contract [@CL-EST-0376; @CL-EST-1680], and paragraph (b) keeps the rules that supplies or services are furnished when and if ordered up to the Schedule maximum and that the Government shall order at least the Schedule minimum [@CL-EST-0377; @CL-EST-1681]. Paragraph (d) says that an order issued during the ordering period and not completed within that period shall be completed within the time specified in the order [@CL-EST-0378], where the codified paragraph says effective period [@CL-EST-1682]. The RFO sentence then continues to say that this time may include order options to be exercised after the ordering period but before the end of the period of performance of the order [@CL-EST-0379], where the codified sentence ends at the time specified in the order [@CL-EST-1682]. The RFO paragraph also says the contract governs the rights and obligations for that order, including options exercised, as if the order were completed during the contract’s ordering period [@CL-EST-0380], and keeps the proviso that the Contractor shall not be required to make any deliveries after a date stated in the clause [@CL-EST-0381; @CL-EST-1711].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A Schedule sets a minimum of 100,000 and a maximum of 1,000,000, and the Order Limitations clause sets a single-item limit of 500,000.

The Government must order at least 100,000 in total. The contractor need not honor one order for 600,000 of a single item, but it must honor it unless it returns the order in time with written notice of its intent not to ship and the reasons.

## Never confuse

The minimum in the Indefinite Quantity clause is what the Government shall order. The minimum order in the Order Limitations clause is a floor below which neither side is obligated under the contract [@CL-EST-1681; @CL-EST-1677].

Complaints about the award of task orders and delivery orders go to an ombudsman under a separate clause, covered on its own card: [Fixed fee for construction and the task-order ombudsman clause](est-fixed-fee-construction-and-ombudsman-clauses.md) [@CL-EST-1692].

The RFO text of FAR 52.216-22(d) says ordering period where the codified paragraph says effective period [@CL-EST-0378; @CL-EST-1682]. The RFO text of FAR 52.216-22 also prints Alternate I, under which either party may cancel the contract in whole or in part by written notice and the cancellation takes effect 30 calendar days after the other party receives the notice [@CL-EST-0382], and Alternate II, under which the Government may cancel the contract in whole or in part by written notice and the cancellation takes effect 30 calendar days after the contractor receives the notice [@CL-EST-0384]. Alternate II also lets the Contractor request to cancel by submitting a written cancellation request to the contracting officer [@CL-EST-9046]. That cancellation takes effect 30 calendar days after the Government receives the request, unless the contracting officer informs the contractor before cancellation is effective that cancellation is not approved [@CL-EST-0385], and a contractor who requests cancellation is not eligible for the minimum guarantee [@CL-EST-9047]. The codified clause says the Government shall order at least the Schedule minimum [@CL-EST-1681], and RFO Alternate I adds that if the Contractor elects to cancel, the Government will not reimburse the minimum guarantee [@CL-EST-0383].
