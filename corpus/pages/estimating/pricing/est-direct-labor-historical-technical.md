## Purpose

Shows how a direct labor estimate is built from historical cost data or from technical data, and the consistency each estimate must keep with actual cost accumulation.

Direct labor cost estimates are primarily developed from historical cost data, which suits follow-on contracts and is adjusted for anticipated changes, or from technical data, which is expected on research and development or new production work without prior history [@CL-EST-0700]. An estimate built from historical data is generally structured like the historical accounting records [@CL-EST-0701]. It must stay consistent with the contractor's established or disclosed practices and with applicable cost accounting standards, so that labor is not duplicated as direct and indirect [@CL-EST-0702].

## How it works

Technical data is non-accounting engineering, manufacturing and operational documentation that supports and quantifies direct labor hours, especially when historical cost accounting data is unavailable or insufficient [@CL-EST-0705]. The historical baseline must be normalized by removing nonrecurring costs, unique past inefficiencies and outdated data [@CL-EST-0703]. It must also come from the same manufacturing facility intended for the proposed effort, reflect current manufacturing practices, and show a consistent relationship between lot costs and equivalent units produced [@CL-EST-0704]. The primary regulatory requirements for evaluating direct labor hour estimates based on technical data are in CAS 401 and CAS 402 [@CL-EST-0706]. These standards require that a contractor's methods for estimating labor hours must be consistent with how the contractor actually accumulates and records those costs [@CL-EST-0707].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A follow-on proposal estimates 10,000 direct labor hours from last year's actuals. The estimator removes a one-time setup cost and a training inefficiency, then applies the cleaned baseline to the proposed lot.

## Never confuse

A historical-based estimate and a technical-based estimate are not the same. Historical data suits follow-on work and is adjusted for anticipated changes [@CL-EST-0700], while technical data is the support used especially when historical cost accounting data is unavailable or insufficient [@CL-EST-0705]. Rates are a separate question; see [Direct labor rates in proposals](est-direct-labor-rates.md).

## Company calibration

That the sources, methods, and rationale behind each direct labor estimate are recorded and traceable to the data used. [@Q-023]
