## Purpose

Shows what the program should-cost review clause lets the Government do and what the contractor must provide when it does.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-EST-1733] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 252.215-7015 [@CL-EST-1733; @CL-EST-1777] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-EST-1733] |
| Applies to | Contracts that use the program should-cost review clause as prescribed at DFARS 215.408(8) [@CL-EST-1777] |

The clause is the one to use as prescribed at DFARS 215.408(8) [@CL-EST-1777]. Under it, the Government has the right to perform a program should-cost review as described in FAR 15.407-4(b) [@CL-EST-1733]. The review may be conducted in support of a particular contract proposal or during contract performance to find opportunities to reduce program costs [@CL-EST-1734]. The Government will communicate the elements of the proposed should-cost review to the prime contractor [@CL-EST-1735].

If the Government performs the review, upon the Government's request the Contractor shall provide access to accurate and complete cost data and to Contractor facilities and personnel necessary to permit the review [@CL-EST-1736]. The Government has the right to use third-party experts to supplement the review team [@CL-EST-1737]. The Contractor shall provide access to its facilities and information necessary to support the review to any third-party experts who have signed non-disclosure agreements in accordance with FAR 52.203-16 [@CL-EST-1738].

## How it works

The clause gives the Government the right to review, and the review may be conducted in support of a particular contract proposal or during contract performance [@CL-EST-1733; @CL-EST-1734]. The prime contractor is told which elements the Government proposes to review [@CL-EST-1735]. If the Government performs a program should-cost review, upon the Government's request the Contractor shall provide access to accurate and complete cost data and Contractor facilities and personnel necessary to permit the Government to perform the review [@CL-EST-1736]. The Contractor shall provide access to its facilities and information necessary to support the review to any third-party experts who have signed non-disclosure agreements in accordance with FAR 52.203-16 [@CL-EST-1737; @CL-EST-1738].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

During performance of a development contract, the Government tells the prime contractor which cost elements it proposes to review. It asks for the cost data and for time with the production staff. It adds an outside expert who has signed a non-disclosure agreement. The contractor gives the data, the staff time and the expert's access to the facility.

## Never confuse

The clause gives the Government the right to review and sets the contractor's cooperation duties [@CL-EST-1733; @CL-EST-1736]. When the Department considers a review and what its team looks like are on [Program should-cost review in the DoD supplement](est-dod-program-should-cost-review.md). The general FAR concept of a should-cost review, including the overhead type, is on [Should-cost review](est-should-cost-review.md). Where the clause is placed in solicitations is on [DFARS solicitation provisions for negotiated contracts](est-dfars-solicitation-provisions-negotiated.md).
