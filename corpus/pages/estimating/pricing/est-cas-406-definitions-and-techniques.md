## Purpose

Gives the defined terms of the Standard on the cost accounting period and its techniques for application: part-period allocation, accrual and deferral practices, estimated rates for early closing, other annual periods and transitional periods. The Standard's main requirements are on the [cost accounting period card](../../compliance/cas-allowability/cmp-cas-406-cost-accounting-period.md).

### Definitions

Allocate means to assign an item of cost, or a group of items of cost, to one or more cost objectives, and the term includes both direct assignment of cost and the reassignment of a share from an indirect cost pool [@CL-EST-2875]. Cost objective means a function, organizational subdivision, contract or other work unit for which cost data are desired and for which provision is made to accumulate and measure the cost of processes, products, jobs, capitalized projects and similar work [@CL-EST-2876]. Fiscal year means the accounting period for which annual financial statements are regularly prepared, generally a period of 12 months, 52 weeks or 53 weeks [@CL-EST-2877]. An indirect cost pool means a grouping of incurred costs identified with two or more cost objectives but not identified specifically with any final cost objective [@CL-EST-2878].

Other terms defined elsewhere in part 99 have the meanings given in those definitions unless paragraph (b) of the section requires otherwise [@CL-EST-2962]. Paragraph (b) applies no modification to those terms for this Standard [@CL-EST-2963].

### Techniques for application

The cost of an indirect function that exists for only part of a cost accounting period may be allocated on the basis of data for that part of the period [@CL-EST-2879]. That is permitted if the cost is material in amount, is accumulated in a separate indirect cost pool, and is allocated on the basis of an appropriate direct measure of the activity or output of the function during that part of the period [@CL-EST-2880].

The practices required by 9904.406-40(b) shall include appropriate practices for deferrals, accruals and other adjustments to be used in identifying the cost accounting periods among which types of expense and types of adjustment to expense are distributed [@CL-EST-2881]. If an expense such as taxes, insurance or employee leave is identified with a fixed, recurring, annual period that is different from the contractor's cost accounting period, the Standard permits continued use of that different period [@CL-EST-2882]. Such expenses shall be distributed to cost accounting periods in accordance with the contractor's established practices for accruals, deferrals and other adjustments [@CL-EST-2968].

Indirect cost allocation rates, based on estimates, that are used to expedite the closing of contracts terminated or completed before the end of a cost accounting period need not be those finally determined or negotiated for that period [@CL-EST-2883]. Those rates shall still be developed to represent a full cost accounting period, except as provided for part-period allocation [@CL-EST-2884].

A contractor may, upon mutual agreement with the Government, use as its cost accounting period a fixed annual period other than its fiscal year [@CL-EST-2885]. Upon that agreement, the alternative period may be used if its use is an established practice of the contractor, it is consistently used for managing and controlling the business, and appropriate accruals, deferrals or other adjustments are made with respect to such annual periods [@CL-EST-2886].

The contracting parties may agree to use an annual period that does not coincide precisely with the cost accounting period for developing the data used in establishing an allocation base [@CL-EST-2887]. The agreed period must meet the conditions stated in the proviso [@CL-EST-2887; @CL-EST-2888; @CL-EST-2969; @CL-EST-2970]. The practice must be necessary to obtain significant administrative convenience and be consistently followed by the contractor [@CL-EST-2888]. The annual period used must be representative of the activity of the cost accounting period for which the indirect costs to be allocated are accumulated [@CL-EST-2969]. The practice must reasonably be estimated to provide a distribution to cost objectives not materially different from that which otherwise would be obtained [@CL-EST-2970].

When a transitional cost accounting period is required under 9904.406-40(a)(3), the contractor may select any one of three periods [@CL-EST-2889]. The first is the period, less than a year in length, from the end of the previous cost accounting period to the beginning of the next regular cost accounting period [@CL-EST-2971]. The second is a period longer than a year but not longer than 15 months, made by combining the first period with the previous cost accounting period [@CL-EST-2890]. The third is a period longer than a year but not longer than 15 months, made by combining the first period with the next regular cost accounting period [@CL-EST-2972]. A change in the contractor's cost accounting period is a change in accounting practices for which an adjustment in the contract price may be required in accordance with paragraph (a)(4)(ii) or (iii) of the contract clause set out at 9903.201-4(a) [@CL-EST-2891].

## How it works

The techniques permit the following practices, subject to the conditions given. The cost of an indirect function that exists for only part of a cost accounting period may be allocated on data for that part if the cost is material in amount, accumulated in a separate indirect cost pool and allocated on an appropriate direct measure of the function's activity or output during that part of the period [@CL-EST-2879; @CL-EST-2880]. Estimated closing rates are released from matching the final rates but still represent a full period, except for part-period allocation [@CL-EST-2883; @CL-EST-2884]. A fixed annual period other than the fiscal year needs mutual agreement with the Government and the three practice conditions [@CL-EST-2885; @CL-EST-2886]. A different annual period for allocation base data needs the contracting parties' agreement and all four conditions [@CL-EST-2887; @CL-EST-2888; @CL-EST-2969; @CL-EST-2970]. When a transitional cost accounting period is required under 9904.406-40(a)(3), the contractor may select any one of three periods [@CL-EST-2889], and a change in the cost accounting period is a change in accounting practices for which an adjustment in the contract price may be required [@CL-EST-2891].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A company's power function exists for only 5 months of its fiscal year. Its 900,000 cost is material, is accumulated in its own pool, and is allocated on metered usage during those 5 months, so the three conditions for part-period allocation are met [@CL-EST-2880]. Had the cost been mixed into a general pool, the separate pool condition would fail [@CL-EST-2880]. A contract terminated in August is closed on estimated indirect rates. Those rates need not be the rates finally negotiated for the year [@CL-EST-2883], but they shall represent a full cost accounting period, except as provided for part-period allocation [@CL-EST-2884].

A second company has a calendar fiscal year but manages the business on a year ending 30 September. It may use that year as its cost accounting period on mutual agreement with the Government, if the use is an established practice, is consistently used for managing and controlling the business, and appropriate accruals, deferrals or other adjustments are made with respect to such annual periods [@CL-EST-2885; @CL-EST-2886].

A third company changes its fiscal year from the calendar year to a year ending 30 June, and a transitional period is required. The gap from the end of its previous period to the start of its next regular period is 6 months, and using the gap alone is one permitted choice [@CL-EST-2971]. Combining the gap with the previous 12-month period gives 18 months, which is longer than 15 months, so the second option is not available [@CL-EST-2890]. Combining it with the next 12-month period also gives 18 months, so the third option is not available either [@CL-EST-2972]. The change is a change in accounting practices, so a contract price adjustment may be required [@CL-EST-2891].

## Never confuse

Part-period allocation covers the cost of an indirect function that exists for only part of a cost accounting period [@CL-EST-2879], while estimated indirect cost allocation rates used to expedite closing need not be those finally determined or negotiated and shall be developed to represent a full cost accounting period, except as provided for part-period allocation [@CL-EST-2883; @CL-EST-2884]. A fixed annual period other than the fiscal year may be used as the cost accounting period upon mutual agreement with the Government if the three practice conditions are met [@CL-EST-2885; @CL-EST-2886], while an annual period that does not coincide precisely with the cost accounting period for developing allocation base data may be agreed by the contracting parties if the four conditions are met [@CL-EST-2887; @CL-EST-2888; @CL-EST-2969; @CL-EST-2970]. When a transitional cost accounting period is required under 9904.406-40(a)(3), the contractor may select any one of three periods [@CL-EST-2889].
