## Purpose

States what the purpose section of the Standard on consistency in allocating costs incurred for the same purpose requires and warns against: one allocation of each type of cost, on one basis, with the same criteria for similar cost objectives, and no double counting.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified CAS [@CL-EST-2906] |
| Codified citation | 48 CFR 9904.402-20 [@CL-EST-2906] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-EST-2906] |
| Applies to | Any contract or other cost objective to which a type of cost is allocated [@CL-EST-2906] |

The purpose of the Standard is to require that each type of cost is allocated only once and on only one basis to any contract or other cost objective [@CL-EST-2906]. The criteria for determining the allocation of costs to a product, contract or other cost objective should be the same for all similar objectives [@CL-EST-2907]. Adherence to these cost accounting concepts is necessary to guard against overcharging some cost objectives and to prevent double counting [@CL-EST-2908].

## How it works

The section states the aim in two parts: each type of cost is allocated once, on one basis, and the criteria for allocating cost are the same for all similar cost objectives [@CL-EST-2906; @CL-EST-2907]. The same-criteria sentence says "should", not "shall" [@CL-EST-2907]. The section says adherence to these concepts guards against overcharging some cost objectives and prevents double counting [@CL-EST-2908]. It then names the most common source of double counting, which is allocating cost items directly to a cost objective without eliminating like cost items from the indirect cost pools that are allocated to that same objective [@CL-EST-2909].

The section states the purpose of the Standard [@CL-EST-2906]. The Standard's operative requirements are in its main card.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor charges 100,000 of engineering labor directly to a contract and leaves the same 100,000 of engineering labor in an overhead pool that is also allocated to that contract. That is the most common form of double counting the section describes, because like cost items were not eliminated from the pool before the direct allocation [@CL-EST-2909]. Removing the 100,000 from the pool leaves one allocation of that cost, on one basis [@CL-EST-2906].

The same contractor allocates the cost of one type of support effort to one contract on direct labor hours and to a second, similar contract on direct labor dollars. That departs from the section's statement that the criteria for allocating cost should be the same for all similar objectives [@CL-EST-2907].

## Never confuse

Double counting occurs "most commonly" when cost items are allocated directly to a cost objective without eliminating like cost items from indirect cost pools that are allocated to that cost objective [@CL-EST-2909]. The allocation rules are in [the Standard's main card](../../compliance/cas-allowability/cmp-cas-402-same-purpose.md) and the definitions in [the definitions and illustrations card](../../compliance/cas-allowability/cmp-cas-402-definitions-illustrations-and-interpretation.md).

## Company calibration

Whether the proposal applies one basis to each type of cost and removes like cost items from pools before any direct allocation. [@Q-036]
