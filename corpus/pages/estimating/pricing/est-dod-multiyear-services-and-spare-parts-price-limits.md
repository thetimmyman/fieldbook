## Purpose

Shows when DoD may use a multiyear contract for services, and when a sole source award for a centrally managed replenishment part is blocked by a price increase.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-EST-1593] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 217.171 and DFARS 217.7505 [@CL-EST-1593; @CL-EST-1597] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-EST-1593] |
| Applies to | Multiyear contracts for the listed services, and sole source contracts for centrally managed replenishment parts [@CL-EST-1593; @CL-EST-1597] |

The head of the agency may enter into a multiyear contract for a period of not more than 5 years for the listed types of services and related items of supply, even though funds are limited by statute to obligation only during the fiscal year for which they were appropriated [@CL-EST-1593]. Two listed covered services are operation, maintenance, and support of facilities and installations, and maintenance or modification of aircraft, ships, vehicles, and other highly complex military equipment [@CL-EST-1594]. Before entering into a multiyear contract for services, the head of the agency must make a written determination that, among other listed findings, there will be a continuing requirement for the services consistent with current plans for the proposed contract period [@CL-EST-1595]. The head of an agency may not initiate a multiyear contract for services if its value exceeds $900 million unless a law specifically provides authority for the contract [@CL-EST-1596]. The section also lists further covered services, principles for entering into the contract, and further findings for the determination, which this card does not draft.

The contracting officer shall not award, on a sole source basis, a contract for any centrally managed replenishment part when the price of the part has increased by 25 percent or more over the most recent 12-month period [@CL-EST-1597]. The contracting officer may award a contract for a part whose price exceeds the limitation if the contracting officer certifies in writing to the head of the contracting activity before award that one of two listed findings applies [@CL-EST-1598]. The two listed certifications are that the contracting officer has evaluated the price of the part and concluded that the price increase is fair and reasonable, or that the national security interests of the United States require purchase of the part despite the price increase [@CL-EST-1599]. Before computing the percentage difference between the current price and the prior price, the contracting officer adjusts for quantity, escalation, and other factors necessary to achieve comparability [@CL-EST-1648]. The source text also lets departments and agencies specify alternate percentages for contracts at or below the simplified acquisition threshold; this card does not draft that provision.

## How it works

The two rules cover different ground. The multiyear section is a limited authority for listed services that applies even though the funds are limited by statute to obligation only during the fiscal year for which they were appropriated [@CL-EST-1593]. It needs a written determination before the contract and sets a value above which a law must provide the authority [@CL-EST-1595; @CL-EST-1596]. The spare-parts section is a price-increase test on sole source awards, with a written certification route to award [@CL-EST-1597; @CL-EST-1598]. The general definition of a multiyear contract and the supply-contract conditions are on [Multiyear contracting: definition, term limit and approval conditions](../../industrialization/ramp/ind-multiyear-contracting.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An agency plans a four-year contract for operation, maintenance, and support of a facility, valued at 300 million. Four years is within the five-year limit, the value is below 900 million, and the head of the agency makes the written determination. The answer: the head of the agency may enter into the multiyear contract. Separately, a replenishment part cost 100 a year ago and now costs 130, after adjusting for quantity and escalation. The answer: that is a 30 percent increase, so a sole source award is blocked unless the contracting officer certifies in writing to the head of the contracting activity before award that the price is fair and reasonable or that national security requires the purchase.

## Never confuse

The sole source price limit is not a general price cap: it applies to a sole source contract for a centrally managed replenishment part [@CL-EST-1597]. A multiyear contract for services is not the multiyear contract for supplies, whose conditions and cancellation ceilings are on [Multiyear contracting: definition, term limit and approval conditions](../../industrialization/ramp/ind-multiyear-contracting.md).
