## Purpose

Shows how the two mobilization clauses pay for mobilization and preparatory work and for mobilization and demobilization, and what the cost limitation provision says about offers.

### Mobilization and preparatory work

The preparatory work clause is the one to use as prescribed in DFARS 236.570(b)(2) [@CL-EST-1783]. Under it, the Government will make payment to the Contractor under the procedures in the clause for mobilization and preparatory work under an item number that the clause text leaves blank [@CL-EST-1796]. Payments will be made for actual payments by the Contractor on work preparatory to commencing actual work on the construction items for which payment is provided under the contract, as the clause lists [@CL-EST-1754].

The first category listed is construction plant and equipment exceeding $25,000 in value per unit, as appraised by the Contracting Officer at the work site, acquired for the execution of the work [@CL-EST-1754; @CL-EST-1755]. The list also includes transportation of all plant and equipment to the site and material purchased for the prosecution of the contract but not to be incorporated in the work [@CL-EST-1754; @CL-EST-1787]. It further includes construction of access roads or railroads, camps, trailer courts, mess halls, dormitories or living quarters, field headquarters facilities, and construction yards, as well as personal services and hire of plant [@CL-EST-1754; @CL-EST-1788].

Requests for payment must include an account of the Contractor's actual expenditures, supporting documentation including receipted bills or copies of payrolls and freight bills, and the Contractor's documentation that the clause lists [@CL-EST-1756]. That documentation shows that the Contractor has acquired the construction plant, equipment, and material free from all encumbrances, and agrees that the construction plant, equipment, and material will not be removed from the site without the written permission of the Contracting Officer [@CL-EST-1789]. It also agrees that structures and facilities prepared or erected for the prosecution of the contract work will be maintained and not dismantled prior to the completion and acceptance of the entire work, without the written permission of the Contracting Officer [@CL-EST-1790].

Upon receiving a request for payment, the Government will make payment, less any prescribed retained percentage, if the listed conditions are met [@CL-EST-1793]. The listed conditions are two Contracting Officer findings: that the construction plant, material, equipment, and the mobilization and preparatory work performed are suitable and necessary to the efficient prosecution of the contract, and that the preparatory work has been done with proper economy and efficiency [@CL-EST-1757; @CL-EST-1758]. The other condition is that payments for construction plant, equipment, material, and structures and facilities prepared or erected for prosecution of the contract work do not exceed the Contractor's cost for the work performed less the estimated value upon completion of the contract [@CL-EST-1759], 100 percent of the cost to the contractor of any items having no appreciable salvage value [@CL-EST-1760], and 75 percent of the cost to the contractor of items which do have an appreciable salvage value [@CL-EST-1794].

Payments will continue to be made for the item, and all payments will be deducted from the contract price for the item, until the total deductions reduce the item to zero, after which no further payments will be made under the item [@CL-EST-1761]. If the total of payments made does not reduce the item to zero, the balance will be paid to the Contractor in the final payment under the contract [@CL-EST-1762]. The retained percentage will be paid in accordance with the Payments to Contractor clause of the contract [@CL-EST-1791]. The Contracting Officer shall determine the value and suitability of the construction plant, equipment, materials, structures and facilities, and those determinations are not subject to appeal [@CL-EST-1763].

### Mobilization and demobilization

The mobilization and demobilization clause is the one to use as prescribed in DFARS 236.570(b)(2) [@CL-EST-1784]. Under it, the Government will pay all costs for the mobilization and demobilization of all of the Contractor's plant and equipment at the contract lump sum price for the item [@CL-EST-1764]. The clause leaves the payment percentages blank, with one blank percentage of the lump sum price payable upon completion of mobilization at the work site and the remaining blank percentage payable upon completion of demobilization [@CL-EST-1765].

The Contracting Officer may require the Contractor to furnish cost data to justify this portion of the bid if the Contracting Officer believes that the percentages in the clause do not bear a reasonable relation to the cost of the work in the contract [@CL-EST-1766]. Failure to justify such price to the satisfaction of the Contracting Officer will result in payment, as determined by the Contracting Officer, of the listed amounts [@CL-EST-1767]. The listed amounts are actual mobilization costs at completion of mobilization, actual demobilization costs at completion of demobilization, and the remainder of the item in the final payment under the contract [@CL-EST-1767; @CL-EST-1768]. The Contracting Officer's determination of the actual costs is not subject to appeal [@CL-EST-1792].

### Cost limitation

The cost limitation provision is the one to use as prescribed in DFARS 236.570(b)(4) [@CL-EST-1785]. Under it, certain items in the solicitation are subject to statutory cost limitations, and the limitations are stated in the Schedule [@CL-EST-1795]. An offer which does not state separate prices for the items identified in the Schedule as subject to a cost limitation may be considered nonresponsive [@CL-EST-1769]. Prices stated in offers for items subject to cost limitations shall include an appropriate apportionment of all costs, direct and indirect, overhead, and profit [@CL-EST-1770]. Offers may be rejected which are materially unbalanced for the purpose of bringing items within cost limitations, or which exceed the cost limitations, unless the limitations have been waived by the Government prior to award [@CL-EST-1771].

## How it works

Under the preparatory work clause, requests for payment must include an account of the Contractor's actual expenditures and supporting documentation [@CL-EST-1756], and the Government will make payment if, among the listed conditions, the Contracting Officer finds that the construction plant, material, equipment, and the mobilization and preparatory work performed are suitable and necessary to the efficient prosecution of the contract and that preparatory work has been done with proper economy and efficiency [@CL-EST-1757; @CL-EST-1758]. Payments may not exceed the cost of the work less estimated value on completion, 100 percent of cost for items with no appreciable salvage value, and 75 percent of cost for items with appreciable salvage value [@CL-EST-1759; @CL-EST-1760; @CL-EST-1794]. All payments are deducted from the contract price for the item until the total deductions reduce the item to zero, and if the total of payments does not reduce the item to zero, the balance will be paid in the final payment under the contract [@CL-EST-1761; @CL-EST-1762]. Under the mobilization and demobilization clause, the Government will pay all costs for the mobilization and demobilization of all of the Contractor's plant and equipment at the contract lump sum price for the item, with blank percentages payable upon completion of mobilization at the work site and upon completion of demobilization [@CL-EST-1764; @CL-EST-1765]. If the Contracting Officer requires cost data to justify the bid portion and the Contractor fails to justify the price to the Contracting Officer's satisfaction, payment will be, as determined by the Contracting Officer, of actual mobilization costs at completion of mobilization, actual demobilization costs at completion of demobilization, and the remainder of the item in the final payment [@CL-EST-1766; @CL-EST-1767; @CL-EST-1768]. Under the cost limitation provision, an offer which does not state separate prices for the items identified in the Schedule as subject to a cost limitation may be considered nonresponsive [@CL-EST-1769], and offers may be rejected which are materially unbalanced for the purpose of bringing items within cost limitations, or which exceed the cost limitations, unless the limitations have been waived by the Government prior to award [@CL-EST-1771].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor buys a crane appraised at 80,000 that has real salvage value. Its cost is 80,000, and the estimated value upon completion of the contract is 30,000. It claims the crane under the preparatory work clause, with its expenditure account and receipted bills. If the Contracting Officer makes the required findings, the cost-based limit is 80,000 less 30,000, which is 50,000. The salvage limit is 75 percent of 80,000, which is 60,000. Payment for the crane may not exceed the lower figure, 50,000. Separately, suppose a lump sum mobilization item has its blanks filled in as 60 percent on mobilization and 40 percent on demobilization.

## Never confuse

Under the preparatory work clause, requests for payment must include an account of the Contractor's actual expenditures [@CL-EST-1756], and all payments will be deducted from the contract price for the item [@CL-EST-1761]. Under the mobilization and demobilization clause, the Government will pay all costs for the mobilization and demobilization of all of the Contractor's plant and equipment at the contract lump sum price for the item [@CL-EST-1764], with blank percentages payable upon completion of mobilization at the work site and upon completion of demobilization [@CL-EST-1765]. Under the cost limitation provision, an offer which does not state separate prices for the items identified in the Schedule as subject to a cost limitation may be considered nonresponsive [@CL-EST-1769].
