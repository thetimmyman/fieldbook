## Purpose

Shows what the contracting officer must decide about the data behind a price reasonableness finding, and what the contracting officer must do when a contractor voluntarily discloses defective pricing after award [@CL-EST-2443; @CL-EST-2449].

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-EST-2443] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 215.402 [@CL-EST-2443]; DFARS 215.407-1 [@CL-EST-2449] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-EST-2443] |
| Applies to | Price reasonableness determinations, and defective pricing that a contractor voluntarily discloses after contract award [@CL-EST-2443; @CL-EST-2449] |

The contracting officer is responsible for determining if the information provided by the offeror is sufficient to determine price reasonableness [@CL-EST-2443]. That responsibility includes determining whether information on the prices at which the same or similar items have previously been sold is adequate for evaluating the reasonableness of price [@CL-EST-2444]. It also includes determining the extent of uncertified cost data that should be required in cases in which price information is not adequate [@CL-EST-2445]. The contracting officer shall not limit the Government's ability to obtain any data that may be necessary to support a determination of fair and reasonable pricing by agreeing to contract terms that preclude obtaining necessary supporting information [@CL-EST-2446]. When obtaining uncertified cost data, the contracting officer must require the offeror to provide the information in the form in which it is regularly maintained in the offeror's business operations [@CL-EST-2447]. The procedures at PGI 215.402 are to be followed when conducting cost or price analysis, particularly for acquisitions of sole source commercial products or commercial services [@CL-EST-2448].

When a contractor voluntarily discloses defective pricing after contract award, the contracting officer must discuss the disclosure with the Defense Contract Audit Agency (DCAA) [@CL-EST-2449]. The discussion assists the contracting officer in determining the involvement of DCAA, which could be a limited-scope audit (for example, one limited to the affected cost elements of the disclosure), a full-scope audit, or technical assistance as appropriate for the circumstances [@CL-EST-2450]. The appropriate involvement depends on the circumstances, for example the nature or dollar amount of the disclosure [@CL-EST-2529]. At a minimum, the contracting officer must discuss with DCAA the completeness of the contractor's voluntary disclosure on the affected contract and the accuracy of the contractor's cost impact calculation for the affected contract [@CL-EST-2451]. The minimum discussion also covers the potential impact on existing contracts, task or delivery orders, or other proposals the contractor has submitted to the Government [@CL-EST-2452].

A voluntary disclosure of defective pricing is not a voluntary refund as defined in DFARS 242.7100, and it does not waive the Government entitlement to the recovery of any overpayment plus interest on the overpayments in accordance with FAR 15.407-1(b)(7) [@CL-EST-2453]. It also does not waive the Government's rights to pursue defective pricing claims on the affected contract or any other Government contract [@CL-EST-2454].

## How it works

The contracting officer's judgement is the control: the contracting officer determines whether the information provided is sufficient to determine price reasonableness and, where price information is not adequate, the extent of uncertified cost data to require, which must be provided in the form regularly maintained in the offeror's business operations [@CL-EST-2443; @CL-EST-2445; @CL-EST-2447]. Contract terms that preclude obtaining necessary supporting information may not be agreed, so the negotiation cannot trade away the data the determination rests on [@CL-EST-2446].

On a voluntary disclosure the contracting officer discusses it with DCAA, and that discussion informs the contracting officer's decision on DCAA's involvement, whether a limited-scope audit, a full-scope audit or technical assistance [@CL-EST-2449; @CL-EST-2450]. The discussion covers at least completeness, the cost impact calculation and the effect on other contracts, orders and proposals [@CL-EST-2451; @CL-EST-2452]. The Government's entitlement to the overpayment plus interest and its right to pursue defective pricing claims on the affected contract or any other Government contract both remain [@CL-EST-2453; @CL-EST-2454].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A proposal offers 800,000 for a sole source commercial service. The offeror supplies prior sales at 760,000 for the same service to two other buyers, and the contracting officer determines whether that information is adequate for evaluating the reasonableness of price [@CL-EST-2443; @CL-EST-2444]. The contracting officer follows the procedures at PGI 215.402 for the price analysis [@CL-EST-2448].

The disclosure process below is a separate case, on a different, noncommercial contract that was priced with certified cost or pricing data, not the commercial service above. After award, that contractor voluntarily discloses that a labor rate in its certified data was overstated by 40,000 [@CL-EST-2449]. The contracting officer discusses the disclosure with DCAA, deciding whether a limited-scope audit limited to the affected cost elements, a full-scope audit or technical assistance fits the circumstances [@CL-EST-2449; @CL-EST-2450]. The discussion covers the completeness of the disclosure, the accuracy of the 40,000 cost impact calculation and any effect on the contractor's other contracts, orders and proposals [@CL-EST-2451; @CL-EST-2452]. The disclosure is not treated as a voluntary refund, and the Government keeps its entitlement to recover any overpayment plus interest and its right to pursue defective pricing claims on that or any other contract [@CL-EST-2453; @CL-EST-2454].

## Never confuse

A voluntary disclosure is not a voluntary refund as defined in DFARS 242.7100, and it does not waive the Government entitlement to recover any overpayment plus interest [@CL-EST-2453]. It is also not a release of the Government's right to pursue defective pricing claims on the affected contract or any other Government contract [@CL-EST-2454]. The price reduction rules for defective certified data and the audit scope for data other than certified data are covered on their own cards and are not repeated here.

## Company calibration

That the pricing file records why the information was sufficient and who handles a post-award disclosure with the auditor. [@Q-047]
