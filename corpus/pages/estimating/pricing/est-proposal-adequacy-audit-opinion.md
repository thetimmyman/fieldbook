## Purpose

Shows when an auditor treats a price proposal as inadequate, what that does to the audit opinion, and what happens when a contractor withholds data or access.

A proposal is inadequate when the contractor fails to submit required supporting data, or when cost data is insufficient to perform the planned audit procedures [@CL-EST-0663]. When data is inadequate, the auditor cannot issue an unqualified opinion, and depending on severity issues a qualified opinion, an adverse opinion or a disclaimer of opinion as required under GAGAS [@CL-EST-0664].

## How it works

On receipt of a proposal the auditor assesses its adequacy with the standard proposal adequacy checklist in DFARS, and tells the contracting officer in writing straight away if significant inadequacies are found [@CL-EST-0665]. If the contractor refuses to provide required certified cost data, or denies access to its books and records, the auditor notifies the contracting officer immediately to obtain support and follows the agency's denial of access process if necessary [@CL-EST-0666]. The audit team also should discuss potential deficiencies and noncompliances with the contractor so they can be corrected, and take them into account when planning procedures [@CL-EST-0667].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A proposal arrives without the supporting material for a large part of its material cost. The auditor tells the contracting officer in writing and talks to the contractor. If the data still does not come, the auditor cannot give an unqualified opinion. The opinion is qualified, adverse or a disclaimer, depending on how serious the gap is.

## Never confuse

A thin proposal is not the same as a refusal. A failure to submit required supporting data, or cost data too thin for the planned audit procedures, makes the proposal inadequate [@CL-EST-0663], and when data is inadequate the auditor cannot issue an unqualified opinion [@CL-EST-0664]. A refusal to provide required certified cost data, or a denial of access to books and records, also triggers immediate notice to the contracting officer and, if necessary, the agency's access process [@CL-EST-0666]. Defective pricing is a separate subject; see [Defective pricing](est-defective-pricing.md). Opinion types on business system audits are covered in [Business system audit reporting](../../compliance/business-systems/cmp-business-system-audit-reporting.md).
