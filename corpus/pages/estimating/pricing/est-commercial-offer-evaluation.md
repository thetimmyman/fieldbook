## Purpose

Shows how offers on a commercial products and commercial services solicitation are evaluated, how options enter the evaluated price, and what a written notice of award does.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-EST-2273] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.212-2 [@CL-EST-2273] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.212-2 [@CL-EST-0089; @CL-EST-0092; @CL-EST-0094] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-EST-2273] |
| Applies to | Solicitations in which the contracting officer inserts the evaluation provision, which may be inserted substantially as it appears, as prescribed in FAR 12.301(c) [@CL-EST-2348] |

Award goes to the responsible offeror whose offer conforming to the solicitation is most advantageous to the Government, price and other factors considered [@CL-EST-2273]. The contracting officer shall insert the significant evaluation factors, such as technical capability, price and past performance, and include them in the relative order of importance of the evaluation factors [@CL-EST-2274]. The provision states, in a blank the contracting officer fills in, how important technical and past performance combined are, and it directs the contracting officer to state, in accordance with FAR 15.304, the relative importance of all other evaluation factors, combined, when compared to price [@CL-EST-2349].

## How it works

For award purposes the total price of all options is added to the total price for the basic requirement [@CL-EST-2275], and the Government may determine that an offer is unacceptable if the option prices are significantly unbalanced [@CL-EST-2276]. Evaluating options does not obligate the Government to exercise them [@CL-EST-2277].

A written notice of award or acceptance, mailed or otherwise furnished to the successful offeror within the time for acceptance specified in the offer, shall result in a binding contract without further action by either party [@CL-EST-2278]. Before the offer's specified expiration time the Government may accept the offer or part of it, whether or not there are negotiations after its receipt, unless a written notice of withdrawal is received before award [@CL-EST-2279].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers must use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text from February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. The RFO provision keeps the award to the responsible Offeror whose conforming offer is most advantageous to the Government, price and other factors considered [@CL-EST-0089] [@CL-EST-2273], the evaluation of options by adding the total price for all options to the total price for the basic requirement, with the rule that an offer may be unacceptable if option prices are significantly unbalanced [@CL-EST-0092] [@CL-EST-2275] [@CL-EST-2276], and the rule that evaluation of options does not obligate the Government to exercise them [@CL-EST-0093] [@CL-EST-2277]. It also keeps the Government's right to accept an offer or part of an offer before the specified expiration time, whether or not there are negotiations, unless a written notice of withdrawal is received before award [@CL-EST-0095] [@CL-EST-2279]. The RFO text says the provision is inserted as prescribed in FAR 12.205(a)(2), where the codified text cites FAR 12.301(c) and says the contracting officer may insert it [@CL-EST-0096] [@CL-EST-2348]. Its bracketed instruction tells the contracting officer to insert the evaluation factors in the relative order of importance, where the codified text gives technical capability, price and past performance as examples [@CL-EST-0090] [@CL-EST-2274]. The bracketed instruction also says that, for invitations for bids, only price and price-related factors are listed [@CL-EST-9068]. For a request for proposals it tells the contracting officer to state that evaluation factors other than price when combined are significantly more important than price, approximately equal to price, or significantly less important than price, where the codified blank states how important technical and past performance combined are and the relative importance of all other evaluation factors combined when compared to price [@CL-EST-0091] [@CL-EST-2349]. The RFO text says a written notice of award or acceptance furnished to the successful Offeror within the time for acceptance specified in the offer results in a binding contract without further action by either party, where the codified text says the notice is mailed or otherwise furnished [@CL-EST-0094] [@CL-EST-2278].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

The contracting officer inserts three factors in descending order of importance: technical capability, price and past performance. The blank states that technical and past performance combined are significantly more important than price. Under the RFO text the bracketed instruction for a request for proposals instead compares all evaluation factors other than price, combined, with price [@CL-EST-0091].

Offeror A prices the basic requirement at 400,000 and one option period at 100,000, so its evaluated price is 500,000. Offeror B prices the basic requirement at 420,000 and the option period at 60,000, so its evaluated price is 480,000. Neither offer's option prices are significantly unbalanced. If one were, the Government could determine that offer unacceptable. Adding the option prices does not obligate the Government to exercise the option.

Offeror B's offer states a time for acceptance of 60 calendar days. The Government mails a written notice of award to Offeror B on day 40, within that time, and a binding contract results without further action by either party.

## Never confuse

Adding the option prices into the evaluated price [@CL-EST-2275] does not obligate the Government to exercise the options [@CL-EST-2277].

A written notice of award furnished within the offer's time for acceptance results in a binding contract without further action by either party [@CL-EST-2278]. The Government's right to accept an offer or part of an offer before its expiration time, whether or not there are negotiations, does not apply if a written notice of withdrawal is received before award [@CL-EST-2279]. See also [Evaluation factors, price and past performance](est-evaluation-factors-price-and-past-performance.md) and [Instructions to offerors on commercial solicitations](est-commercial-offer-instructions.md).

The blank for the relative importance of factors works differently in the two texts. The codified provision states in a blank how important technical and past performance, combined, are, and directs the contracting officer to state the relative importance of all other evaluation factors, combined, when compared to price [@CL-EST-2349], while the RFO instruction compares all evaluation factors other than price, combined, with price [@CL-EST-0091].
