## Purpose

Shows how offers on a commercial products and commercial services solicitation are evaluated, how options enter the evaluated price, and what a written notice of award does.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-EST-2273] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.212-2 [@CL-EST-2273] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-EST-2273] |
| Applies to | Solicitations in which the contracting officer inserts the evaluation provision, which may be inserted substantially as it appears, as prescribed in FAR 12.301(c) [@CL-EST-2348] |

Award goes to the responsible offeror whose offer conforming to the solicitation is most advantageous to the Government, price and other factors considered [@CL-EST-2273]. The contracting officer inserts the significant evaluation factors, such as technical capability, price and past performance, and includes them in their relative order of importance [@CL-EST-2274]. The provision states, in a blank the contracting officer fills in, how important technical and past performance combined are, and it directs the contracting officer to state the relative importance of all other evaluation factors, combined, when compared to price [@CL-EST-2349]. The factors, their order and that blank are contract-specific.

## How it works

For award purposes the total price of all options is added to the total price for the basic requirement [@CL-EST-2275], and an offer may be found unacceptable where its option prices are significantly unbalanced [@CL-EST-2276]. Evaluating options does not obligate the Government to exercise them [@CL-EST-2277].

A written notice of award or acceptance, mailed or otherwise furnished to the successful offeror within the time for acceptance specified in the offer, results in a binding contract without further action by either party [@CL-EST-2278]. Before the offer's specified expiration time the Government may accept the offer or part of it, whether or not there are negotiations after its receipt, unless a written notice of withdrawal is received before award [@CL-EST-2279].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

The contracting officer inserts three factors in descending order of importance: technical capability, price and past performance. The blank states that technical and past performance combined are significantly more important than price.

Offeror A prices the basic requirement at 400,000 and one option period at 100,000, so its evaluated price is 500,000. Offeror B prices the basic requirement at 420,000 and the option period at 60,000, so its evaluated price is 480,000. Neither offer's option prices are significantly unbalanced. If one were, the Government could determine that offer unacceptable. Adding the option prices does not obligate the Government to exercise the option.

Offeror B's offer states a time for acceptance of 60 calendar days. The Government mails a written notice of award to Offeror B on day 40, within that time, and a binding contract results without further action by either party.

## Never confuse

Evaluating an option is not exercising it. Adding the option prices into the evaluated price [@CL-EST-2275] does not obligate the Government to exercise the options [@CL-EST-2277].

A written notice of award furnished within the offer's time for acceptance results in a binding contract without further action by either party [@CL-EST-2278]. The Government's right to accept an offer or part of an offer before its expiration time, whether or not there are negotiations, does not apply if a written notice of withdrawal is received before award [@CL-EST-2279]. See also [Evaluation factors, price and past performance](est-evaluation-factors-price-and-past-performance.md) and [Instructions to offerors on commercial solicitations](est-commercial-offer-instructions.md).
