## Purpose

Shows when an incentive contract is the right contract type, what its incentives are meant to do, and how the incentive is built around targets.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-EST-1050] |
| Codified citation | FAR 16.401 [@CL-EST-1050; @CL-EST-1055] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-EST-1050] |
| Applies to | Incentive contracts as described in the FAR subpart on incentive contracts [@CL-EST-1050] |

An incentive contract is appropriate when a firm-fixed-price contract is not appropriate and the required supplies or services can be acquired at lower costs and, in certain instances, with improved delivery or technical performance [@CL-EST-1050], by relating the amount of profit or fee payable under the contract to the contractor's performance [@CL-EST-9511]. Incentive contracts are designed to obtain specific acquisition objectives by establishing reasonable and attainable targets that are clearly communicated to the contractor [@CL-EST-1051], and by including appropriate incentive arrangements designed to motivate contractor efforts that might not otherwise be emphasized and to discourage contractor inefficiency and waste [@CL-EST-1100].

The two basic categories are fixed-price incentive contracts and cost-reimbursement incentive contracts [@CL-EST-1053]. The fixed-price kind is preferred when contract costs and performance requirements are reasonably certain [@CL-EST-1054].

## How it works

When formula-type incentives on technical performance or delivery are set in advance, profit or fee increases are paid only for achievement that surpasses the targets, and decreases apply to the extent the targets are not met [@CL-EST-1052]. The head of the contracting activity signs a determination and finding for every incentive and award-fee contract, justifying that the contract type is in the best interest of the Government [@CL-EST-1055]. The preference for the fixed-price kind rests on the Government's usual advantage when the contractor assumes substantial cost responsibility and an appropriate share of the cost risk [@CL-EST-1054]. Cost, performance and delivery incentives, the fixed-price and cost-plus-incentive-fee forms, and award fees each have their own card.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A buyer needs 100 units whose cost and performance requirements are reasonably certain. A firm-fixed-price contract is not appropriate because the buyer also wants a lower cost and an earlier delivery than the offer shows. The buyer considers an incentive contract and sets a delivery target of 90 days and a technical target of 500 units of output. The contractor earns extra profit only for results beyond those targets and loses profit to the extent it misses them. Because costs are reasonably certain, the fixed-price incentive kind is preferred over a cost-reimbursement incentive kind. The head of the contracting activity signs the determination and finding.

## Never confuse

Where predetermined formula-type incentives on technical performance or delivery are included, increases in profit or fee are provided only for achievement that surpasses the targets [@CL-EST-1052]; under a cost-plus-award-fee contract, the award amount is based upon a judgmental evaluation by the Government [@CL-EST-1064]. For profit in price analysis, see [DoD weighted guidelines: contract type risk and working capital adjustment](est-weighted-guidelines-contract-type-risk.md) and [Profit analysis in cost-based price negotiations](est-profit-analysis.md). For how incentive fee is billed after award, see [Incentive Fee clause](../../contract-finance/billing-payment/ctf-incentive-fee.md).
