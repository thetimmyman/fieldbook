## Purpose

Explains how an assumed administrative cost enters the evaluation of bids for multiple awards, how multiple technical proposals fit the first step of a two-step acquisition, and which document wins when the papers conflict.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-EST-2014] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.214-22 [@CL-EST-2014]; FAR 52.214-24 [@CL-EST-2016]; FAR 52.214-29 [@CL-EST-2017] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-EST-2014] |
| Applies to | Solicitations that include the multiple awards evaluation provision [@CL-EST-2130], the multiple technical proposals provision [@CL-EST-2132] or the order of precedence clause [@CL-EST-2134] |

In addition to other factors, bids are evaluated on the basis of advantages and disadvantages to the Government that might result from making more than one award [@CL-EST-2131]. For evaluation, the Government assumes an administrative cost of $500 for issuing and administering each contract awarded under the solicitation [@CL-EST-2014]. Individual awards are made for the items or combinations of items that result in the lowest aggregate cost to the Government, including the assumed administrative costs [@CL-EST-2015].

In the first step of a two-step acquisition, solicited sources are encouraged to submit multiple technical proposals presenting different basic approaches [@CL-EST-2016]. Each technical proposal submitted is separately evaluated, and the submitter is notified as to its acceptability [@CL-EST-2133].

Any inconsistency in the solicitation or contract is resolved by giving precedence in the following order [@CL-EST-2137]: the Schedule excluding the specifications, then representations and other instructions, then contract clauses, then other documents, exhibits and attachments, and last the specifications [@CL-EST-2017].

## How it works

The assumed administrative cost is an assumption for evaluating bids, and it applies to each contract that would be awarded [@CL-EST-2014]. A single award therefore carries one assumed cost and a split award carries one for each contract it would produce [@CL-EST-2014]. The comparison adds the assumed costs to the item prices of each arrangement and selects the one with the lowest aggregate cost [@CL-EST-2015].

A source that submits several technical proposals gets a separate evaluation and a separate notice of acceptability for each [@CL-EST-2133].

To resolve a conflict, find the higher-ranked class of document and follow it. A conflict between a contract clause and the specifications, for example, is resolved in favor of the clause, because contract clauses rank above the specifications [@CL-EST-2017]. The negotiated-acquisition clause is covered on [Request for information, facsimile proposals and order of precedence](est-planning-solicitations-facsimile-and-precedence.md), and awards under order contracts are covered on [Single or multiple awards for order contracts and advisory services](est-single-or-multiple-awards.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

Two line items can be bought by one award or by two awards. The item prices total 40,000 under one award and 39,400 under two awards. The assumed administrative cost is 500 for each contract, so the one award totals 40,500 and the two awards total 40,400. Two awards give the lowest aggregate cost, so individual awards are made for the items under the two-award arrangement.

In the first step of a two-step acquisition, a source submits two technical proposals with different basic approaches. Each is evaluated separately, and the source is told whether each is acceptable.

The Schedule, excluding the specifications, states delivery in 30 days, while an attached specification states 45 days. The Schedule ranks first and the specifications rank last, so the 30 days govern.

## Never confuse

The assumed administrative cost is not added once for the whole solicitation: it is assumed for each contract awarded [@CL-EST-2014].

The provision does not prefer multiple awards: it directs evaluation of the advantages and disadvantages of making more than one award [@CL-EST-2131].

Order of precedence ranks documents, not bidders: it resolves an inconsistency in the solicitation or contract [@CL-EST-2137].

Each technical proposal is evaluated on its own, not as part of one combined proposal [@CL-EST-2133].
