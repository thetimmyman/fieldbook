## Purpose

Shows when a contracting officer reaches outside the buying activity for field pricing assistance, and what that request may and may not cover.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-EST-0201] |
| Codified citation | FAR 15.404-2 [@CL-EST-0201]; DFARS 215.404-2 [@CL-EST-2058] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 15.406 [@CL-EST-3219] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-EST-0201] |
| Applies to | Acquisitions where information at the buying activity is inadequate to determine a fair and reasonable price [@CL-EST-0201] |

Field pricing assistance should be requested when the information available at the buying activity is inadequate to determine a fair and reasonable price [@CL-EST-0201]. The request must be tailored to reflect the minimum essential supplementary information needed to conduct a technical or cost or pricing analysis [@CL-EST-0202], and the type of information and level of detail requested must be tailored in accordance with the specialized resources available at the buying activity and the magnitude and complexity of the required analysis [@CL-EST-0203].

## How it works

Field pricing assistance is generally available to provide technical, audit, and special reports associated with the cost elements of a proposal, including subcontracts [@CL-EST-0204]. It is also generally available to provide information on related pricing practices and history [@CL-EST-0439]. Among the information that helps contracting officers determine commerciality and a fair and reasonable price is the identification of general market conditions affecting those determinations [@CL-EST-9472; @CL-EST-0212]. When assistance is requested, contracting officers are encouraged to team with appropriate field experts throughout the acquisition process, including negotiations [@CL-EST-0211]. Audit and field pricing information, whether written or reported telephonically or electronically, must be made a part of the official contract file [@CL-EST-0206]. Field pricing information and other reports may include proprietary or source selection information, which must be appropriately identified and protected [@CL-EST-0205]. The auditor may not reveal audit conclusions or recommendations to the offeror or contractor without the contracting officer's concurrence, but may discuss statements of facts with the contractor [@CL-EST-0207].

The DFARS refers the reader to PGI 215.404-2 for guidance on obtaining field pricing or audit assistance [@CL-EST-2058].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 15 text in place of the codified text under Class Deviation 2026-O0048, whose Revision 1 took effect immediately and supersedes the March 5, 2026 version [@CL-EST-3176] [@CL-EST-3177]. RFO FAR 15.406(a)(1) keeps the rule that contracting officers should request field pricing assistance when the information available at the buying activity is inadequate to determine a fair and reasonable price [@CL-EST-3219; @CL-EST-0201], and that the request must reflect the minimum essential supplementary information needed to conduct a technical or cost or pricing analysis [@CL-EST-3220; @CL-EST-0202]. Paragraph (a)(2) keeps the requirement that contracting officers tailor the type of information and level of detail requested in accordance with the specialized resources available at the buying activity and the magnitude and complexity of the required analysis [@CL-EST-3221; @CL-EST-0203]. It keeps the list of what field pricing assistance is generally available to provide, including technical, audit, and special reports associated with the cost elements of a proposal, including subcontracts, and information on related pricing practices and history [@CL-EST-3222; @CL-EST-0204; @CL-EST-0439]. Among the information to help contracting officers determine commerciality and a fair and reasonable price, it lists identifying general market conditions affecting those determinations [@CL-EST-9473; @CL-EST-3223; @CL-EST-0212]. In RFO FAR 15.406 the (a)(2) list ends with item (iv), information relative to the business, technical, production, or other capabilities and practices of an offeror, and is followed directly by paragraph (b), Reporting field pricing information [@CL-EST-3224]. The codified paragraph between them encourages contracting officers to team with appropriate field experts when field pricing assistance is requested [@CL-EST-0211]. Paragraph (b)(2) keeps the rule that audit and field pricing information, whether written or reported telephonically or electronically, must be made a part of the official contract file [@CL-EST-3225; @CL-EST-0206]. Paragraph (c)(1)(i) keeps the rule that the auditor must not reveal the audit conclusions or recommendations to the offeror or contractor without the contracting officer's concurrence, but may discuss statements of facts with the contractor [@CL-EST-3226; @CL-EST-0207]. Paragraph (c)(2) keeps the rule that the contracting officer must not request a separate preaward audit of indirect costs unless the information already available from an existing audit, completed within the preceding 12 months, is considered inadequate for determining the reasonableness of the proposed indirect costs [@CL-EST-3227; @CL-EST-0208]. Paragraph (d) keeps the rule that the administrative contracting officer or the auditor, as appropriate, must notify the contracting officer immediately if the data provided for review is so deficient as to preclude review or audit, or if the contractor or offeror has denied access to any records considered essential to conduct a satisfactory review or audit [@CL-EST-3228; @CL-EST-9475; @CL-EST-0209; @CL-EST-9474], and that if the offeror or contractor again refuses to provide adequate data or access to necessary data, the contracting officer must withhold the award or price adjustment and refer the contract action to a higher authority [@CL-EST-3229; @CL-EST-0210].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A buyer receives a proposal whose labor build-up cannot be judged from the file. The buyer asks for field pricing assistance covering only the disputed cost elements rather than the whole proposal. The request is properly tailored, because it reflects the minimum essential supplementary information needed and a level of detail that matches the resources available and the complexity of the analysis. The answer is that the request meets the rule, and the reports that come back belong in the official contract file.

## Never confuse

Field pricing assistance is broader than an audit: it is generally available to provide technical, audit, and special reports, among other information, so an audit report is one form of it [@CL-EST-0204]. The contracting officer must not request a separate preaward audit of indirect costs unless information already available from an existing audit, completed within the preceding 12 months, is considered inadequate for determining the reasonableness of the proposed indirect costs [@CL-EST-0208]. Deficient or withheld data follows a different path: the administrative contracting officer or the auditor notifies the contracting officer immediately when the data provided is so deficient as to preclude review or audit, or when the contractor or offeror has denied access to any records considered essential to conduct a satisfactory review or audit [@CL-EST-0209; @CL-EST-9474], and a further refusal leads to a withheld award or price adjustment and a referral to a higher authority [@CL-EST-0210]. A citation to FAR 15.404-2 means data to support proposal analysis in the codified text [@CL-EST-0201], but in the RFO text 15.404-2 is cost analysis [@CL-EST-3230] and the field pricing rules are at 15.406 [@CL-EST-3219].
