## Purpose

Shows when a contracting officer reaches outside the buying activity for field pricing assistance, and what that request may and may not cover.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-EST-0201] |
| Codified citation | FAR 15.404-2 [@CL-EST-0201] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-EST-0201] |
| Applies to | Acquisitions where information at the buying activity is inadequate to determine a fair and reasonable price [@CL-EST-0201] |

Field pricing assistance should be requested when the information available at the buying activity is inadequate to determine a fair and reasonable price [@CL-EST-0201]. The request must be tailored to reflect the minimum essential supplementary information needed to conduct a technical or cost or pricing analysis [@CL-EST-0202], and the type of information and level of detail requested must be tailored in accordance with the specialized resources available at the buying activity and the magnitude and complexity of the required analysis [@CL-EST-0203].

## How it works

Field pricing assistance is generally available to provide technical, audit, and special reports associated with the cost elements of a proposal, including subcontracts [@CL-EST-0204]. It is also generally available to provide information on related pricing practices and history [@CL-EST-0439]. Among the information that helps contracting officers determine commerciality and a fair and reasonable price is the identification of general market conditions affecting those determinations [@CL-EST-0212]. When assistance is requested, contracting officers are encouraged to team with appropriate field experts throughout the acquisition process, including negotiations [@CL-EST-0211]. Audit and field pricing information, whether written or reported telephonically or electronically, must be made a part of the official contract file [@CL-EST-0206]. The auditor may not reveal audit conclusions or recommendations to the offeror or contractor without the contracting officer's concurrence, but may discuss statements of facts with the contractor [@CL-EST-0207].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A buyer receives a proposal whose labor build-up cannot be judged from the file. The buyer asks for field pricing assistance covering only the disputed cost elements rather than the whole proposal. The request is properly tailored, because it reflects the minimum essential supplementary information needed and a level of detail that matches the resources available and the complexity of the analysis. The answer is that the request meets the rule, and the reports that come back belong in the official contract file.

## Never confuse

Field pricing assistance is broader than an audit: it is generally available to provide technical, audit, and special reports, among other information, so an audit report is one form of it [@CL-EST-0204]. The contracting officer must not request a separate preaward audit of indirect costs unless information already available from an existing audit, completed within the preceding 12 months, is considered inadequate for determining the reasonableness of the proposed indirect costs [@CL-EST-0208]. Deficient or withheld data follows a different path: the administrative contracting officer or the auditor notifies the contracting officer immediately when the data provided is so deficient as to preclude review or audit [@CL-EST-0209], and a further refusal leads to a withheld award or price adjustment and a referral to a higher authority [@CL-EST-0210].
