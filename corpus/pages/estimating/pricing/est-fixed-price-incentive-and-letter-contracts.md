## Purpose

Shows how a fixed-price incentive contract works and when it fits, and what the FAR says about using a letter contract and the clauses it carries. The printed text for the letter-contract sections does not cover time-and-materials contracts.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-EST-1568] |
| Codified citation | FAR 16.403, FAR 16.603-2 and FAR 16.603-4 [@CL-EST-1568; @CL-EST-1572; @CL-EST-1576] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-EST-1568] |
| Applies to | Fixed-price incentive contracts, and letter contracts [@CL-EST-1568; @CL-EST-1572] |

A fixed-price incentive contract is a fixed-price contract that provides for adjusting profit and establishing the final contract price by application of a formula based on the relationship of total final negotiated cost to total target cost [@CL-EST-1568]. It is appropriate when listed conditions are met, the first being that a firm-fixed-price contract is not suitable [@CL-EST-1569], and another that the nature of the supplies or services and other circumstances are such that the contractor's assumption of a degree of cost responsibility will provide a positive profit incentive for effective cost control and performance [@CL-EST-1570]. A further listed condition is that, if the contract also includes incentives on technical performance or delivery, the performance requirements provide a reasonable opportunity for the incentives to have a meaningful impact on the contractor's management of the work [@CL-EST-1639].

A letter contract may be used when the Government's interests demand a binding commitment so that work can start immediately and negotiating a definitive contract is not possible in sufficient time to meet the requirement [@CL-EST-1572]. Its schedule will provide for definitization within 180 days after the date of the letter contract or before completion of 40 percent of the work to be performed, whichever occurs first [@CL-EST-1573], and the contracting officer may, in extreme cases and according to agency procedures, authorize an additional period [@CL-EST-1574]. The maximum liability of the Government inserted in the Limitation of Government Liability clause shall be the estimated amount necessary to cover the contractor's requirements for funds before definitization [@CL-EST-1649]. That maximum liability shall not exceed 50 percent of the estimated cost of the definitive contract unless approved in advance by the official that authorized the letter contract [@CL-EST-1575].

## How it works

A fixed-price incentive contract adjusts profit and sets the final price by a formula that compares total final negotiated cost with total target cost, and its billing prices may be adjusted, within the ceiling limits, upon request of either party when it becomes apparent that final negotiated cost will be substantially different from the target cost [@CL-EST-1568; @CL-EST-1571]. A letter contract starts work before the definitive contract is agreed, so its schedule sets the path to definitization and its Government liability is limited [@CL-EST-1572; @CL-EST-1573; @CL-EST-1575]. When a letter contract is contemplated, the contracting officer shall insert in solicitations and contracts, among listed clauses, the clause on Execution and Commencement of Work, except that it may be omitted from letter contracts awarded on SF 26 [@CL-EST-1576]. The printed text of the two letter-contract sections covers letter contracts, not time-and-materials contracts, so this card does not establish FAR application or clause rules for time-and-materials contracts; see [Time-and-materials contracts: use, ceiling and surveillance](../../contract-finance/billing-payment/ctf-time-and-materials-contracts.md).

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text from February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. RFO FAR 52.216-24 says to insert the Limitation of Government Liability clause as prescribed in 16.603-4(b)(2) [@CL-EST-9238], without the words in the codified prescription line that say the clause goes in solicitations and contracts when a letter contract is contemplated [@CL-FPA-0093]. Under paragraph (a) of the RFO clause, in performing the contract the Contractor is not authorized to make expenditures or incur obligations exceeding the dollar amount stated in the blank [@CL-EST-9239]. Under paragraph (b), the maximum amount for which the Government shall be liable if the contract is terminated is the dollar amount stated in the blank [@CL-EST-9240].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A supplier holds a fixed-price incentive contract with a target cost of 1,000,000. Midway it becomes apparent that final negotiated cost will be about 1,300,000. The answer: either party may ask to adjust the billing prices, within the ceiling limits, and the final price is still set later by the formula that compares final negotiated cost with target cost.

## Never confuse

A fixed-price incentive contract is not a letter contract: the first adjusts profit by formula, while the second is a binding commitment so that work can start before the definitive contract is agreed [@CL-EST-1568; @CL-EST-1572]. The DoD rules on definitization schedules for undefinitized contract actions are on [DoD undefinitized contract actions: definition, obligation limit and schedule](est-dod-undefinitized-contract-actions.md). The general description of incentive contracts is on [Incentive contracts: when they fit and how they are structured](est-incentive-contracts-general.md), and the definitization clause is on [Contract definitization for letter contracts](../../contract-finance/funding/ctf-contract-definitization.md).
