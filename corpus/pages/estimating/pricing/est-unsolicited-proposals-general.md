## Purpose

Shows what makes a proposal a valid unsolicited proposal, what is not one, and what an agency must tell potential offerors.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-EST-1426] |
| Codified citation | FAR 15.603 [@CL-EST-1426] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 15.503-1 [@CL-EST-3512]; RFO FAR 15.502 [@CL-EST-3518] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-EST-1426] |
| Applies to | Proposals that meet the listed tests for a valid unsolicited proposal [@CL-EST-1426; @CL-EST-1427; @CL-EST-1428] |

A valid unsolicited proposal must, among its listed requirements, be innovative and unique, be independently originated and developed by the offeror, and be prepared without Government supervision, endorsement, direction, or direct Government involvement [@CL-EST-1426]. It must also include sufficient detail to permit a determination that Government support could be worthwhile and the proposed work could benefit the agency's research and development or other mission responsibilities [@CL-EST-1426; @CL-EST-1427].

It must not be an advance proposal for a known agency requirement that can be acquired by competitive methods, and it must not address a previously published agency requirement [@CL-EST-1426; @CL-EST-1428].

## How it works

Advertising material, commercial product or commercial service offers, contributions, and routine correspondence on technical issues are not unsolicited proposals [@CL-EST-1429]. Agencies must make available to potential offerors at least a listed set of information, one item being the definition and content of an unsolicited proposal acceptable for formal evaluation [@CL-EST-1430]. Another listed item is instructions for identifying and marking proprietary information so that it is protected and restrictive legends conform to the section on limited use of data [@CL-EST-1430; @CL-EST-1473]. Only the cognizant contracting officer has the authority to bind the Government regarding unsolicited proposals [@CL-EST-1431].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 15 text in place of the codified text under Class Deviation 2026-O0048, whose Revision 1 took effect immediately and supersedes the March 5, 2026 version [@CL-EST-3176] [@CL-EST-3177]. RFO FAR 15.503-1(b) lists the same requirements for a valid unsolicited proposal: it must be innovative and unique, be independently originated and developed by the offeror, and be prepared without Government supervision, endorsement, direction, or direct Government involvement [@CL-EST-3512; @CL-EST-1426]; it must include sufficient detail to permit a determination that Government support could be worthwhile and the proposed work could benefit the agency's research and development or other mission responsibilities [@CL-EST-3513; @CL-EST-1427]; and it must not be an advance proposal for a known agency requirement that can be acquired by competitive methods or address a previously published agency requirement [@CL-EST-3514; @CL-EST-1428]. RFO FAR 15.503-1(c) says advertising material, commercial product or commercial service offers, contributions, or routine correspondence on technical issues are not unsolicited proposals, where the codified paragraph reads "contributions, as defined in 15.601" [@CL-EST-3515; @CL-EST-1429]. RFO FAR 15.502(d) says agencies must make the following information available to potential offerors of unsolicited proposals, where the codified section says at least the following information, and its seventh item is instructions for identifying and marking proprietary information so that restrictive legends conform to "this subpart" instead of to 15.609 [@CL-EST-3516; @CL-EST-3517; @CL-EST-1430; @CL-EST-1473]. RFO FAR 15.502(b) says only the cognizant contracting officer has the authority to bind the Government regarding unsolicited proposals [@CL-EST-3518; @CL-EST-1431].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A company sends an agency a research idea it developed alone, with enough detail to judge its worth. The agency has no published requirement for it and no competitive buy planned. The idea meets the tests. A brochure for a catalogue product sent the same day does not, because it is advertising. The answer: the first is a valid unsolicited proposal and the second is not.

## Never confuse

A valid unsolicited proposal must not address a previously published agency requirement [@CL-EST-1426; @CL-EST-1428]. A commercial product offer or a brochure is not an unsolicited proposal either [@CL-EST-1429]. Only the cognizant contracting officer has the authority to bind the Government regarding unsolicited proposals [@CL-EST-1431]. The tests for a valid unsolicited proposal are in codified FAR 15.603(c) [@CL-EST-1426] and in RFO FAR 15.503-1(b) [@CL-EST-3512], and the authority sentence that the codified text prints in FAR 15.604(b) [@CL-EST-1431] is in RFO FAR 15.502(b) [@CL-EST-3518]. What such a proposal should contain is on [Content of unsolicited proposals](est-unsolicited-proposal-content.md), and how data in it is restricted is on [Limited use of data in unsolicited proposals](est-unsolicited-proposal-data-legend.md).
