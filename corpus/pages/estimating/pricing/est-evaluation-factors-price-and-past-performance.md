## Purpose

Shows how price or cost must figure in a source selection, what the solicitation must tell offerors, and what that means for a pricing team.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR and DFARS [@CL-EST-1294; @CL-EST-1299] |
| Codified citation | FAR 15.304 [@CL-EST-1294]; DFARS 215.304 [@CL-EST-1299]; FAR 2.101 [@CL-EST-9311] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 15.104 [@CL-EST-3244; @CL-EST-3245]; RFO FAR 2.101 [@CL-EST-9362] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-EST-1294] |
| Applies to | Source selections, subject to a listed exception for DoD, NASA, and the Coast Guard [@CL-EST-1294; @CL-EST-1295] |

The evaluation factors and significant subfactors that apply to an acquisition and their relative importance are within the broad discretion of agency acquisition officials, subject to the listed requirements [@CL-EST-1312]. Price or cost to the Government shall be evaluated in every source selection, subject to the exception listed in a cross-referenced paragraph [@CL-EST-1294]. For DoD, NASA, and the Coast Guard, the contracting officer may choose not to include price or cost as an evaluation factor for award when a solicitation meets listed conditions [@CL-EST-1295]. Those conditions are that the solicitation has an estimated value above the simplified acquisition threshold, will result in multiple-award contracts for the same or similar services, and states that the Government intends to make an award to each and all qualifying offerors [@CL-EST-1296]. That exception shall not apply to solicitations for multiple-award contracts that provide for sole source orders under section 8(a) of the Small Business Act [@CL-EST-1310].

Past performance, except as set forth in a separate listed paragraph, shall be evaluated in all source selections for negotiated competitive acquisitions expected to exceed the simplified acquisition threshold [@CL-EST-1297]. Past performance need not be evaluated if the contracting officer documents the reason past performance is not an appropriate evaluation factor for the acquisition [@CL-EST-1311]. In the definitions section of the FAR, past performance means an offeror's or contractor's performance on active and physically completed contracts, with a cross-reference to 4.804-4 [@CL-EST-9311]. The RFO text of FAR 2.101 defines past performance as an offeror's or contractor's performance on active and physically completed contracts, with a cross-reference to 4.804-4, the same in substance as the codified definition [@CL-EST-9362; @CL-EST-9311].

All factors and significant subfactors that will affect contract award and their relative importance shall be stated clearly in the solicitation [@CL-EST-1313]. Unless the exception that lets the contracting officer leave price or cost out of the award factors applies, the solicitation shall also state, at a minimum, whether all evaluation factors other than cost or price, when combined, are significantly more important than cost or price, approximately equal to cost or price, or in a further listed alternative [@CL-EST-1298]. The three listed alternatives are significantly more important than, approximately equal to, and significantly less important than cost or price [@CL-EST-1309].

## How it works

The printed rule requires that price or cost be evaluated in every source selection, subject to the listed exception, and leaves the choice of other factors to the broad discretion of agency acquisition officials [@CL-EST-1294; @CL-EST-1312]. For DoD, when procuring supplies or services, the contracting officer shall ensure Supplier Performance Risk System assessments of price risk and supplier risk are considered as a part of the award decision [@CL-EST-1299]. For a pricing team, the price will therefore be evaluated in every source selection, subject to the listed exception [@CL-EST-1294; @CL-EST-1295], and, unless that exception applies, the solicitation will say how all other factors combined compare in importance with cost or price [@CL-EST-1298; @CL-EST-1309]. The pricing team should read that statement in each solicitation before choosing how much estimate support to prepare.

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 15 text in place of the codified text under Class Deviation 2026-O0048, whose Revision 1 took effect immediately and supersedes the March 5, 2026 version [@CL-EST-3176] [@CL-EST-3177]. The evaluation factors rule is in RFO FAR 15.104(b), which keeps the rule that the evaluation factors and significant subfactors that apply to an acquisition and their relative importance are within the broad discretion of agency acquisition officials, subject to the listed requirements [@CL-EST-3244; @CL-EST-1312]. It says price or cost to the Government must be evaluated in every competitive source selection, where the codified text says every source selection, subject to the exception listed in a cross-referenced paragraph [@CL-EST-3245; @CL-EST-1294]. For DoD, NASA, and the Coast Guard, it keeps the rule that contracting officers may choose not to include price or cost as an evaluation factor for award when an RFP meets listed conditions [@CL-EST-3246; @CL-EST-1295]. Those conditions are that the RFP has an estimated value above the simplified acquisition threshold, will result in multiple-award contracts for the same or similar services, and states that the Government intends to make an award to each and all qualifying offerors [@CL-EST-3247; @CL-EST-1296]. It keeps the rule that the exception must not apply to RFPs for multiple-award contracts that provide for sole source orders pursuant to section 8(a) of the Small Business Act [@CL-EST-3248; @CL-EST-1310]. For past performance, RFO FAR 15.104(b)(3)(i) says it must be evaluated in every competitive source selection, unless the contracting officer documents the reason past performance is not an appropriate evaluation factor for the acquisition [@CL-EST-3249; @CL-EST-1311], where the codified text says all source selections for negotiated competitive acquisitions expected to exceed the simplified acquisition threshold, except as set forth in a separate listed paragraph [@CL-EST-1297]. RFO FAR 15.104(a) keeps the rule that all factors and significant subfactors that will affect contract award and their relative importance must be stated clearly in the RFP [@CL-EST-3250; @CL-EST-1313]. Under RFO paragraph (d), unless the exception at paragraph (b)(1)(ii) applies, the RFP must also state, at a minimum, whether all evaluation factors other than cost or price, when combined, are listed alternatives [@CL-EST-3251; @CL-EST-1298]: significantly more important than, approximately equal to, or significantly less important than cost or price [@CL-EST-3252; @CL-EST-1309].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A DoD solicitation states that all non-price factors combined are approximately equal to price. The pricing team prepares a fully supported price, because price will be evaluated and weighed against the other factors. It also reviews its own assessments in the Supplier Performance Risk System, because price risk and supplier risk are considered in the award decision.

## Never confuse

Evaluating price or cost in a source selection is not the same as the price analysis a contracting officer uses to decide that a price is fair and reasonable. Those techniques are on [Proposal analysis techniques](est-proposal-analysis.md) and [Should-cost review](est-should-cost-review.md). The data the contracting officer asks for to establish a fair and reasonable price is on [Pricing policy and the order of preference for data](est-pricing-policy-data-preference.md). A citation to FAR 15.304 means evaluation factors in the codified text [@CL-EST-1294] but defective certified cost or pricing data after award in the RFO text [@CL-EST-3231]; the RFO text states the price or cost evaluation rule at 15.104(b)(1)(i) [@CL-EST-3245].
