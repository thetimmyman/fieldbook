## Purpose

Shows how far a contracting officer looks into subcontract prices when pricing a prime contract, and what the prime contractor must obtain from its own subcontractors.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-EST-0213] |
| Codified citation | FAR 15.404-3 [@CL-EST-0213] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 15.404-8 [@CL-EST-3476; @CL-EST-3478; @CL-EST-3480] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-EST-0213] |
| Applies to | Prime contracts whose price includes subcontracting costs [@CL-EST-0213] |

The contracting officer is responsible for determining a fair and reasonable price for the prime contract, including subcontracting costs [@CL-EST-0213]. In doing so the contracting officer should consider whether the contractor or a subcontractor has an approved purchasing system, has performed cost or price analysis of proposed subcontractor prices, or has negotiated subcontract prices before the prime negotiation [@CL-EST-0214], in determining the reasonableness of the prime contract price [@CL-EST-0440]; none of that relieves the contracting officer from analyzing the contractor's submission, including subcontractor's certified cost or pricing data [@CL-EST-0215].

The contractor side is a chain of duties. A prime or subcontractor conducts appropriate cost or price analyses to establish the reasonableness of proposed subcontract prices and includes the results in the price proposal [@CL-EST-0216; @CL-EST-0217]. A contractor or subcontractor that is required to submit certified cost or pricing data must also obtain and analyze certified data before awarding any subcontract, purchase order, or modification expected to exceed the certified cost or pricing data threshold [@CL-EST-0218], unless an exception applies to that action [@CL-EST-0441]. The contractor must submit, or cause the subcontractor to submit, certified data to the Government for subcontracts that are the lower of $20 million or more, or both more than the pertinent threshold and more than 10 percent of the prime contractor's proposed price, unless the contracting officer believes submission of that data is unnecessary [@CL-EST-0442; @CL-EST-0219]. Below those levels the contracting officer should require subcontractor certified data [@CL-EST-0220], and should also require data other than certified cost or pricing data that the contracting officer considers necessary for adequately pricing the prime contract [@CL-EST-0443].

## How it works

The contracting officer starts from the prime submission and the subcontract costs inside it [@CL-EST-0213]. An approved purchasing system, a completed subcontract price analysis, or subcontract prices already negotiated before the prime negotiation are factors to weigh, not substitutes for analyzing the submission, including subcontractor certified data [@CL-EST-0214; @CL-EST-0215]. The prime then carries the duty down: analyze proposed subcontract prices, put the results in the price proposal, and, where the prime must submit certified cost or pricing data, obtain and analyze subcontractor certified data before awarding any subcontract, purchase order, or modification expected to exceed the certified cost or pricing data threshold, unless an exception applies [@CL-EST-0216; @CL-EST-0217; @CL-EST-0218; @CL-EST-0441]. Government receipt of subcontractor certified data is triggered by the lower of the $20 million test or the combined test of more than the pertinent threshold and more than 10 percent of the prime contractor's proposed price, unless the contracting officer believes submission unnecessary [@CL-EST-0442; @CL-EST-0219]. The contracting officer should require more where pricing the prime contract needs it [@CL-EST-0220; @CL-EST-0443]. Subcontractor certified cost or pricing data must be current, accurate, and complete as of the date of price agreement or, if applicable, an earlier date agreed upon by the parties and specified on the contractor's certificate [@CL-EST-0221].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 15 text in place of the codified text under Class Deviation 2026-O0048, whose Revision 1 took effect immediately and supersedes the March 5, 2026 version [@CL-EST-3176] [@CL-EST-3177]. RFO FAR 15.404-8(a) says contracting officers are responsible for the determination of a fair and reasonable price for the prime contract, including subcontracting costs [@CL-EST-3476; @CL-EST-0213], and that to support this effort the prime contractor or subcontractor must conduct appropriate cost or price analyses to establish the reasonableness of proposed subcontract prices and include the results of these analyses in the price proposal [@CL-EST-3477; @CL-EST-0216; @CL-EST-0217]. RFO FAR 15.404-8(b) says any contractor or subcontractor that is required to submit certified cost or pricing data also must obtain and analyze certified cost or pricing data before awarding any subcontract, purchase order, or modification expected to exceed the certified cost or pricing data threshold, unless an exception in 15.403-2 applies to that action [@CL-EST-3478; @CL-EST-3479; @CL-EST-0218; @CL-EST-0441]. RFO FAR 15.404-8(b)(1) says the contractor must submit, or cause to be submitted by the subcontractor(s), certified cost or pricing data to the Government for subcontracts that are the lower of either $20 million or more, or both more than the pertinent certified cost or pricing data threshold and more than 10 percent of the prime contractor's proposed price, unless the contracting officer believes such submission is unnecessary [@CL-EST-3480; @CL-EST-3481; @CL-EST-0442; @CL-EST-0219]. RFO FAR 15.404-8(b)(2) says contracting officers should require the contractor or subcontractor to submit to the Government (or cause submission of) subcontractor certified cost or pricing data below the thresholds in paragraph (b)(1) of that section, and also data other than certified cost or pricing data that the contracting officer considers necessary for adequately pricing the prime contract [@CL-EST-3482; @CL-EST-3483; @CL-EST-0220; @CL-EST-0443]. RFO FAR 15.404-8(b)(4) says subcontractor certified cost or pricing data must be current, accurate, and complete as of the date of price agreement, or, if applicable, an earlier date agreed upon by the parties and specified on the contractor's Certificate of Current Cost or Pricing Data [@CL-EST-3484; @CL-EST-0221]. RFO FAR 15.404-8(c) says contracting officers should consider whether a contractor or subcontractor has an approved purchasing system, has performed cost or price analysis of proposed subcontractor prices, or has negotiated the subcontract prices before negotiation of the prime contract, in determining the reasonableness of the prime contract price, and that this does not relieve contracting officers from the responsibility to analyze the contractor's submission, including subcontractor's certified cost or pricing data [@CL-EST-3485; @CL-EST-3486; @CL-EST-0214; @CL-EST-0440; @CL-EST-0215].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A prime proposal is priced at 100 million, and the prime must submit certified cost or pricing data. One subcontract is priced at 15 million, above the pertinent threshold and above 10 percent of the prime price, so certified subcontractor data must be submitted for it unless the contracting officer believes submission unnecessary. A second subcontract is priced at 8 million and clears neither test, so the threshold provision does not reach it, although the contracting officer may still ask for data needed to price the prime contract. Where three prospective subcontractors compete for one work package, the prime contractor need only submit certified cost or pricing data to the Government for the one most likely to receive the award [@CL-EST-0222].

## Never confuse

An approved purchasing system and a subcontract price analysis are separate considerations the contracting officer weighs [@CL-EST-0214], and consent to a subcontract is a different action again [@CL-SUP-0153]. Consent to subcontracts and review of contractors' purchasing systems are separate actions covered in part 44 [@CL-SUP-0153], while the analyses described here exist to establish the reasonableness of the subcontract prices inside the prime proposal [@CL-EST-0216]. Nor is the analysis a substitute for the Government's own review of what the contractor submitted [@CL-EST-0215].

The approved purchasing system consideration is in codified FAR 15.404-3(a) [@CL-EST-0214; @CL-EST-0440] but in RFO FAR 15.404-8(c) [@CL-EST-3485], and the codified threshold rule in FAR 15.404-3(c)(1) [@CL-EST-0442] is in RFO FAR 15.404-8(b)(1) [@CL-EST-3480]. The exception to the duty to obtain and analyze certified data points to 15.403-2 in RFO FAR 15.404-8(b) [@CL-EST-3479], where codified FAR 15.404-3(c) points to 15.403-1(b) [@CL-EST-0441].
