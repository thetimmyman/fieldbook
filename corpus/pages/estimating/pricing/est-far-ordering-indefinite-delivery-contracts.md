## Purpose

Shows how orders under multiple-award indefinite-delivery contracts reach the awardees, which exceptions to fair opportunity the FAR lists, and where the dollar thresholds in the ordering section fall.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-EST-1553] |
| Codified citation | FAR 16.505 [@CL-EST-1553] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-EST-1553] |
| Applies to | Orders exceeding the micro-purchase threshold under multiple delivery-order or multiple task-order contracts, subject to listed exceptions [@CL-EST-1553; @CL-EST-1625] |

The contracting officer must provide each awardee a fair opportunity to be considered for each order exceeding the micro-purchase threshold issued under multiple delivery-order or multiple task-order contracts, except as provided for in the paragraph on exceptions to the fair opportunity process, the first of the listed exceptions [@CL-EST-1553]. The other listed exception is orders issued under 19.504(c)(1)(ii) [@CL-EST-1625]. If an order does not exceed the simplified acquisition threshold, the contracting officer need not contact each of the multiple awardees before selecting an order awardee if the contracting officer has information available to ensure that each awardee is provided a fair opportunity [@CL-EST-1554]. Each order exceeding the simplified acquisition threshold shall be placed on a competitive basis in accordance with the fair-notice paragraph, unless supported by a written determination that one of the circumstances described in the exceptions paragraph applies to the order and the requirement is waived on the basis of a justification prepared in accordance with the justification paragraph [@CL-EST-1555; @CL-EST-1556].

The contracting officer shall give every awardee a fair opportunity to be considered for a delivery order or task order exceeding the micro-purchase threshold unless one of the listed statutory exceptions applies [@CL-EST-1560]. One listed exception is that the agency need for the supplies or services is so urgent that providing a fair opportunity would result in unacceptable delays [@CL-EST-1561]. Another is that only one awardee is capable of providing the supplies or services required at the level of quality required because they are unique or highly specialized [@CL-EST-1562]. A third is a sole-source order that is a logical follow-on to an order already issued under the contract, provided that all awardees were given a fair opportunity to be considered for the original order [@CL-EST-1563]. A fourth is that it is necessary to place an order to satisfy a minimum guarantee [@CL-EST-1626]. A fifth, for orders exceeding the simplified acquisition threshold, is that a statute expressly authorizes or requires that the purchase be made from a specified source [@CL-EST-1627]. A sixth is that contracting officers may, at their discretion, set aside orders for any of the small business concerns identified in the small business part [@CL-EST-1628]. For DoD, NASA, and the Coast Guard, a listed exception is that the order satisfies one of the exceptions to full and open competition listed in the FAR, and the public interest exception shall not be used unless Congress is notified [@CL-EST-1564].

The justification for an exception to fair opportunity shall be in writing as specified in the two paragraphs on the documentation of the exception [@CL-EST-1630]. No justification is needed for the exception described in paragraph (b)(2)(i)(F) of this section, which is the exception for setting aside orders for small business concerns [@CL-EST-1629; @CL-EST-1628]. For orders exceeding the simplified acquisition threshold, each justification shall include, as a minimum, the listed information and be approved in accordance with the approval paragraph [@CL-EST-1631]. Listed items include identification of the exception to fair opportunity and the supporting rationale, including a demonstration that the proposed contractor's unique qualifications or the nature of the acquisition requires use of the exception cited [@CL-EST-1631; @CL-EST-1632], and a determination by the contracting officer that the anticipated cost to the Government will be fair and reasonable [@CL-EST-1631; @CL-EST-1633]. Except as provided in the paragraph on posting exceptions, within 14 days after placing an order exceeding the simplified acquisition threshold that does not provide for fair opportunity, the contracting officer shall publish a notice [@CL-EST-1635]. The section lists further justification items, approval levels and rules on making the justification public, which this card does not draft.

## How it works

The ordering section ties the fair opportunity steps to three thresholds: the micro-purchase threshold, the simplified acquisition threshold, and $7.5 million [@CL-EST-1553; @CL-EST-1554; @CL-EST-1557]. For task or delivery orders in excess of $7.5 million, the fair opportunity shall include, at a minimum, a notice with a clear statement of the agency's requirements, a reasonable response period, and disclosure of the significant factors and subfactors, including cost or price [@CL-EST-1557; @CL-EST-1558]. It shall also include a written statement documenting the basis for award, where award is made on a best value basis, and an opportunity for a postaward debriefing [@CL-EST-1559]. The contracting officer shall notify unsuccessful awardees when the total price of a task or delivery order exceeds $7.5 million [@CL-EST-1636]. The DFARS adds a postaward debriefing requirement for DoD orders, described on [DoD ordering under multiple-award contracts: DFARS additions](est-dod-ordering-multiple-award-contracts.md). No protest of an order under the FAR protest subpart is authorized, subject to listed exceptions, including a protest that the order increases the scope, period, or maximum value of the contract [@CL-EST-1550], a protest of an order valued in excess of $10 million for agencies other than DoD, NASA, and the Coast Guard [@CL-EST-1551], and a protest of an order valued in excess of $25 million for DoD, NASA, or the Coast Guard [@CL-EST-1552].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A multiple-award task order contract has three awardees. The agency wants a services order valued at 9 million and no exception applies. The order is above the simplified acquisition threshold, so the agency places it on a competitive basis, and because it is above 7.5 million the notice states the agency's requirements, gives a reasonable response period and discloses the significant factors, including price. The answer: all three awardees receive the notice, the fair opportunity includes a postaward debriefing opportunity, and the agency must notify the unsuccessful awardees because the order exceeds 7.5 million.

## Never confuse

The fair opportunity rule starts above the micro-purchase threshold, while the competitive placement rule starts above the simplified acquisition threshold [@CL-EST-1553; @CL-EST-1555]. The contract types, minimums and multiple-award preference are not repeated here; they are on [Indefinite-delivery and indefinite-quantity contracts](../../contract-finance/funding/ctf-indefinite-delivery-and-indefinite-quantity-contracts.md).
