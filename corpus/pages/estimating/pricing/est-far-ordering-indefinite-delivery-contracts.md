## Purpose

Shows how orders under multiple-award indefinite-delivery contracts reach the awardees, which exceptions to fair opportunity the FAR lists, and where the dollar thresholds in the ordering section fall.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-EST-1553] |
| Codified citation | FAR 16.505 [@CL-EST-1553]; FAR 2.101 [@CL-EST-9374] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 2.101 [@CL-EST-9375] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-EST-1553] |
| Applies to | Orders exceeding the micro-purchase threshold under multiple delivery-order or multiple task-order contracts, subject to listed exceptions [@CL-EST-1553; @CL-EST-1625] |

The contracting officer must provide each awardee a fair opportunity to be considered for each order exceeding the micro-purchase threshold issued under multiple delivery-order or multiple task-order contracts, except as provided for in paragraph (b)(2) of the section [@CL-EST-1553], or for orders issued under 19.504(c)(1)(ii) [@CL-EST-1625]. The FAR definitions section says a delivery order is an order for supplies placed against an established contract or with Government sources [@CL-EST-9374]. RFO FAR 2.101 defines a delivery order in the same words [@CL-EST-9375; @CL-EST-9374]. If an order does not exceed the simplified acquisition threshold, the contracting officer need not contact each of the multiple awardees before selecting an order awardee if the contracting officer has information available to ensure that each awardee is provided a fair opportunity [@CL-EST-1554]. Each order exceeding the simplified acquisition threshold shall be placed on a competitive basis in accordance with paragraph (b)(1)(iii)(B) of the section, unless supported by a written determination that one of the circumstances described at 16.505(b)(2)(i) applies to the order and the requirement is waived on the basis of a justification that is prepared in accordance with 16.505(b)(2)(ii)(B) [@CL-EST-1555; @CL-EST-1556].

The contracting officer shall give every awardee a fair opportunity to be considered for a delivery order or task order exceeding the micro-purchase threshold unless one of the listed statutory exceptions applies [@CL-EST-1560]. One listed exception is that the agency need for the supplies or services is so urgent that providing a fair opportunity would result in unacceptable delays [@CL-EST-1561]. Another is that only one awardee is capable of providing the supplies or services required at the level of quality required because they are unique or highly specialized [@CL-EST-1562]. A third is an order issued on a sole-source basis in the interest of economy and efficiency because it is a logical follow-on to an order already issued under the contract, provided that all awardees were given a fair opportunity to be considered for the original order [@CL-EST-1563]. A fourth is that it is necessary to place an order to satisfy a minimum guarantee [@CL-EST-1626]. A fifth, for orders exceeding the simplified acquisition threshold, is that a statute expressly authorizes or requires that the purchase be made from a specified source [@CL-EST-1627]. A sixth is that contracting officers may, at their discretion, set aside orders for any of the small business concerns identified in 19.000(a)(3), in accordance with section 1331 of Public Law 111-240 (section 644(r) of title 15 of the United States Code) [@CL-EST-1628]; when they set aside orders for small business concerns, the specific small business program eligibility requirements identified in part 19 apply [@CL-EST-9435]. For DoD, NASA, and the Coast Guard, a listed exception is that the order satisfies one of the exceptions permitting the use of other than full and open competition listed in 6.302, and the public interest exception shall not be used unless Congress is notified in accordance with section 3204(a)(7) of title 10 of the United States Code [@CL-EST-1564].

The justification for an exception to fair opportunity shall be in writing as specified in paragraph (b)(2)(ii)(A) or (B) of the section [@CL-EST-1630]. No justification is needed for the exception described in paragraph (b)(2)(i)(F) of this section, which is the exception for setting aside orders for small business concerns [@CL-EST-1629; @CL-EST-1628]. For orders exceeding the simplified acquisition threshold, each justification shall include, as a minimum, the listed information and be approved in accordance with paragraph (b)(2)(ii)(C) of the section [@CL-EST-1631]. Listed items include identification of the exception to fair opportunity and the supporting rationale, including a demonstration that the proposed contractor's unique qualifications or the nature of the acquisition requires use of the exception cited [@CL-EST-1631; @CL-EST-1632], and a determination by the contracting officer that the anticipated cost to the Government will be fair and reasonable [@CL-EST-1631; @CL-EST-1633]. Except as provided in paragraph (b)(2)(ii)(D)(5) of the section, within 14 days after placing an order exceeding the simplified acquisition threshold that does not provide for fair opportunity, the contracting officer shall publish a notice in accordance with 5.301 [@CL-EST-1635] and make publicly available the justification required at paragraph (b)(2)(ii)(B) of the section [@CL-EST-9692].

## How it works

Each awardee must be given a fair opportunity for each order exceeding the micro-purchase threshold [@CL-EST-1553]. If the order does not exceed the simplified acquisition threshold, the contracting officer need not contact each awardee when information is available to ensure that each awardee is provided a fair opportunity [@CL-EST-1554]. For task or delivery orders in excess of $7.5 million, the fair opportunity requirement includes steps at a minimum [@CL-EST-1557]. For task or delivery orders in excess of $7.5 million, the fair opportunity shall include, at a minimum, a notice with a clear statement of the agency's requirements, a reasonable response period, and disclosure of the significant factors and subfactors, including cost or price, that the agency expects to consider in evaluating proposals, and their relative importance [@CL-EST-1557; @CL-EST-1558; @CL-EST-9691]. It shall also include, where award is made on a best value basis, a written statement documenting the basis for award and the relative importance of quality and price or cost factors, and an opportunity for a postaward debriefing in accordance with paragraph (b)(6) of the section [@CL-EST-1559]. The contracting officer shall notify unsuccessful awardees when the total price of a task or delivery order exceeds $7.5 million [@CL-EST-1636]. The DFARS additions for DoD orders are described on [DoD ordering under multiple-award contracts: DFARS additions](est-dod-ordering-multiple-award-contracts.md). No protest of an order under the FAR protest subpart is authorized, subject to listed exceptions, including a protest that the order increases the scope, period, or maximum value of the contract [@CL-EST-1550], a protest of an order valued in excess of $10 million for agencies other than DoD, NASA, and the Coast Guard [@CL-EST-1551], and a protest of an order valued in excess of $25 million for DoD, NASA, or the Coast Guard [@CL-EST-1552].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A multiple-award task order contract has three awardees. The agency wants a services order valued at 9 million and no exception applies. The order is above the simplified acquisition threshold, so the agency places it on a competitive basis, and because it is above 7.5 million the notice states the agency's requirements, gives a reasonable response period and discloses the significant factors, including price. The answer: all three awardees receive the notice, the fair opportunity includes a postaward debriefing opportunity, and the agency must notify the unsuccessful awardees because the order exceeds 7.5 million.

## Never confuse

The fair opportunity rule starts above the micro-purchase threshold, while the competitive placement rule starts above the simplified acquisition threshold [@CL-EST-1553; @CL-EST-1555]. Contract types, minimums and the multiple-award preference are on [Indefinite-delivery and indefinite-quantity contracts](../../contract-finance/funding/ctf-indefinite-delivery-and-indefinite-quantity-contracts.md).
