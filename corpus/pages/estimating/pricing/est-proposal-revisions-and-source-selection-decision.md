## Purpose

Shows what happens to proposal revisions after discussions, who may request or allow them, when final proposal revisions are requested, who fixes the common cut-off date, and what the source selection authority must base, decide and document.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-EST-2737] |
| Codified citation | FAR 15.307 and FAR 15.308 [@CL-EST-2737; @CL-EST-2742] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-EST-2737] |
| Applies to | Offerors in the competitive range, the contracting officer, and the source selection authority [@CL-EST-2737; @CL-EST-2739; @CL-EST-2742] |

If an offeror's proposal is eliminated or otherwise removed from the competitive range, no further revisions to that offeror's proposal shall be accepted or considered [@CL-EST-2737]. The contracting officer may request or allow proposal revisions to clarify and document understandings reached during negotiations [@CL-EST-2738]. At the conclusion of discussions, each offeror still in the competitive range shall be given an opportunity to submit a final proposal revision [@CL-EST-2739]. The contracting officer is required to establish a common cut-off date only for receipt of final proposal revisions [@CL-EST-2740]. Requests for final proposal revisions shall advise offerors that the final proposal revisions shall be in writing and that the Government intends to make award without obtaining further revisions [@CL-EST-2741].

The source selection authority's decision shall be based on a comparative assessment of proposals against all source selection criteria in the solicitation [@CL-EST-2742]. While the source selection authority may use reports and analyses prepared by others, the source selection decision shall represent the authority's independent judgment [@CL-EST-2743]. The decision shall be documented, and the documentation shall include the rationale for any business judgments and tradeoffs made or relied on by the authority, including benefits associated with additional costs [@CL-EST-2744]. Although the rationale must be documented, the documentation need not quantify the tradeoffs that led to the decision [@CL-EST-2745].

## How it works

The contracting officer decides whether to request or allow proposal revisions to clarify and document understandings reached during negotiations, because the text says may [@CL-EST-2738]. The final proposal revision is different: each offeror still in the competitive range shall be given the opportunity at the conclusion of discussions [@CL-EST-2739]. The text states that opportunity in the passive voice and does not name who gives it, and the contracting officer is the one required to set the common cut-off date for receiving final revisions [@CL-EST-2739; @CL-EST-2740]. The text requires a common date only for final revisions [@CL-EST-2740]. The request shall advise offerors that their final revisions shall be in writing and that the Government intends to make award without obtaining further revisions [@CL-EST-2741].

The source selection authority makes the decision. Under the source selection responsibilities card, the contracting officer is the source selection authority unless the agency head appoints another individual for a particular acquisition or group of acquisitions [@CL-EST-1376]. See [Source selection responsibilities and evaluation](est-source-selection-responsibilities-and-evaluation.md). The authority may rely on reports and analyses prepared by others, but the decision shall be the authority's own independent judgment, documented with its rationale [@CL-EST-2743; @CL-EST-2744].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

Three offerors are in the competitive range. When discussions conclude, each of the three is given the opportunity to submit a final proposal revision [@CL-EST-2739]. The contracting officer sets one common cut-off date, ten days after the request, for receipt of final proposal revisions [@CL-EST-2740]. The request states that the revisions shall be in writing and that the Government intends to make award without obtaining further revisions [@CL-EST-2741].

Before the cut-off date, one offeror is removed from the competitive range. Any further revision it sends is not accepted or considered [@CL-EST-2737]. The other two submit written final revisions by the cut-off date.

The source selection authority compares the two proposals against all source selection criteria in the solicitation [@CL-EST-2742]. The authority reads the evaluation team's reports and may use them, but the decision it signs must represent its own independent judgment, not simply the team's recommendation [@CL-EST-2743]. It selects the proposal priced 200,000 higher and documents the rationale, including the benefits associated with the additional cost [@CL-EST-2744]. The documentation does not need to put a dollar value on each tradeoff [@CL-EST-2745].

## Never confuse

The common cut-off date is not a deadline for every exchange. The contracting officer is required to establish it only for receipt of final proposal revisions [@CL-EST-2740].

A request or allowance for revisions to clarify and document understandings is permissive, while the opportunity to submit a final proposal revision is mandatory for each offeror still in the competitive range [@CL-EST-2738; @CL-EST-2739].

Independent judgment does not mean working alone. The authority may use reports and analyses prepared by others, but the decision shall represent its own independent judgment [@CL-EST-2743].
