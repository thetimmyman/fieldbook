## Purpose

Shows what DoD requires before a time-and-materials or labor-hour contract or order is used: who approves the determination and findings, and which contract type DoD prefers in a listed case.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-EST-1581] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 216.601 [@CL-EST-1581; @CL-EST-1586] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-EST-1581] |
| Applies to | Time-and-materials and labor-hour contracts and orders [@CL-EST-1581] |

DFARS sets approval rules for the determination and findings for time-and-materials or labor-hour contracts where the base period plus any option periods is three years or less [@CL-EST-1581]. Within that three-year case, one listed rule is that, where the time-and-materials or labor-hour portion exceeds $1 million, the senior contracting official within the contracting activity is the approval authority [@CL-EST-1582]. That authority may not be delegated [@CL-EST-1642; @CL-EST-1582]. Another listed rule is that, where that portion is less than or equal to $1 million, the determination and findings shall be approved one level above the contracting officer [@CL-EST-1583]. Where the base period plus any option periods exceeds three years, the authority of the head of the contracting activity to approve the determination and findings may not be delegated [@CL-EST-1584]. These approval requirements do not apply to contracts that, as determined by the head of the contracting activity, support contingency or humanitarian or peacekeeping operations, among other listed categories [@CL-EST-1585].

For contracts and orders for other than commercial products or commercial services awarded to contractors with adequate accounting systems, a cost-plus-fixed-fee term contract type shall be preferred over a time-and-materials or labor-hour contract type [@CL-EST-1586]. The determination and findings shall contain sufficient facts and rationale to justify that no other contract type is suitable and, at a minimum, shall include the listed elements [@CL-EST-1643]. One listed element is to establish that it is not possible at the time of placing the contract or order to accurately estimate the extent or duration of the work or to anticipate costs with any reasonable degree of certainty [@CL-EST-1643; @CL-EST-1644]. The section also lists further elements, how indefinite-delivery contracts are to be structured, and a solicitation provision; this card does not draft those points.

## How it works

DoD sets approval rules for the determination and findings of a time-and-materials or labor-hour contract or order [@CL-EST-1581]. Who approves depends on the length of the base period plus options and on the value of the time-and-materials or labor-hour portion [@CL-EST-1581; @CL-EST-1582; @CL-EST-1583; @CL-EST-1584]. The preference for a cost-plus-fixed-fee term type applies to the listed contract class [@CL-EST-1586]. The general conditions for using the type, the ceiling price and surveillance are on [Time-and-materials contracts: use, ceiling and surveillance](../../contract-finance/billing-payment/ctf-time-and-materials-contracts.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contracting activity plans a time-and-materials order with a base period of two years and no options. The time-and-materials portion is valued at 1,500,000. The answer: the base period is within three years and the portion exceeds 1,000,000, so the senior contracting official within the contracting activity approves the determination and findings, unless the head of the contracting activity has determined that an exception category applies.

## Never confuse

This card covers only the DFARS section on limitations, not the general conditions for using a time-and-materials contract. The value that matters for the approval level is the time-and-materials or labor-hour portion, not the whole contract [@CL-EST-1582; @CL-EST-1583].
