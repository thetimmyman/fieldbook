## Purpose

Explains what makes an offer unbalanced, which circumstances carry the greatest risk, and what the contracting officer must do when cost or price analysis techniques indicate that an offer is unbalanced.

Unbalanced pricing may increase performance risk and could result in payment of unreasonably high prices [@CL-EST-2543]. It exists when, despite an acceptable total evaluated price, the price of one or more line items is significantly over or understated as indicated by the application of cost or price analysis techniques [@CL-EST-2544]. The greatest risks occur when startup work, mobilization, first articles, or first article testing are separate line items, or when base quantities and option quantities are separate line items [@CL-EST-2545], or when the evaluated price is the aggregate of estimated quantities to be ordered under separate line items of an indefinite-delivery contract [@CL-EST-2546]. All offers with separately priced line items or subline items must be analyzed to determine if the prices are unbalanced [@CL-EST-2547]. If cost or price analysis techniques indicate that an offer is unbalanced, contracting officers must consider the risks to the Government associated with the unbalanced pricing in determining the competitive range and in making the source selection decision [@CL-EST-2548], and must consider whether award of the contract will result in paying unreasonably high prices for contract performance [@CL-EST-2549]. An offer may be rejected if the contracting officer determines that the lack of balance poses an unacceptable risk to the Government [@CL-EST-2550].

## How it works

The analysis duty is stated without a party. It covers every offer with separately priced line items or subline items [@CL-EST-2547]. The section sets no percentage or dollar test for a price that is significantly over or understated; the definition relies on what cost or price analysis techniques indicate [@CL-EST-2544].

The consequences fall on contracting officers. They must consider the risk in the competitive range and the source selection decision [@CL-EST-2548], and must consider whether award means paying unreasonably high prices [@CL-EST-2549]. The determination that the lack of balance poses an unacceptable risk is the contracting officer's; the section does not say who rejects the offer. It says an offer may be rejected, not that it must be [@CL-EST-2550].

DoD contracting officers must use the revised FAR part 15 in place of the codified text [@CL-EST-3176], under Class Deviation 2026-O0048 Revision 1, which took effect immediately and supersedes the version issued on March 5, 2026 [@CL-EST-3177].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An offer for an indefinite-delivery contract has three separately priced line items, and its evaluated price is the aggregate of estimated quantities, 5,000,000 in total [@CL-EST-2546]. The startup line is priced at 900,000 against an estimated cost of 150,000. The other two lines are priced at 2,100,000 and 2,000,000 against estimated costs of 2,300,000 and 2,100,000. The offer is analyzed because it has separately priced line items [@CL-EST-2547]. The total evaluated price is acceptable, but cost analysis indicates that the startup line is significantly overstated, so the pricing is unbalanced [@CL-EST-2544]. The contracting officer must consider the risks to the Government in determining the competitive range and in making the source selection decision, and must consider whether award will mean paying unreasonably high prices [@CL-EST-2548; @CL-EST-2549]. The offer may be rejected if the contracting officer determines that the lack of balance poses an unacceptable risk to the Government [@CL-EST-2550]. This example does not make that determination.

## Never confuse

Unbalanced pricing is not the same as an unreasonable total price. The definition applies even when the total evaluated price is acceptable [@CL-EST-2544]. It turns on whether the price of one or more line items is significantly over or understated as cost or price analysis techniques indicate [@CL-EST-2544].

## Company calibration

Confirm which bids carry separately priced startup, mobilization, first article, base and option, or indefinite-delivery lines that would be analyzed for imbalance [@Q-012].
