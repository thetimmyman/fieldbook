## Purpose

Sets out what a reverse auction service provider must do, and must not do, when it runs an auction for the Government, including registration, data protection and its own fees.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-EST-2037] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.217-12 [@CL-EST-2037] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.217-12 [@CL-EST-0546] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-EST-2037] |
| Applies to | A contractor that provides reverse auction services to the Government [@CL-EST-2037] |

The clause defines three terms. Government data means any information, document, media, or machine-readable material, regardless of physical form or characteristics, that is created or obtained by the Government in the course of official Government business [@CL-EST-2033]. Government-related data means any information, document, media, or machine-readable material, regardless of physical form or characteristics, that is created or obtained by a contractor through the storage, processing, or communication of Government data [@CL-EST-2034]. It does not include a contractor's business records, such as financial and legal records, or data such as operating procedures, software coding, or algorithms that are not uniquely applied to the Government data [@CL-EST-2035]. A reverse auction is the process for obtaining pricing, usually supported by an electronic tool, in which offerors see competing offerors' prices without disclosure of the competing offeror's identity and have the opportunity to submit lower priced offers until the close of the auction [@CL-EST-2036].

The duties apply to the contractor, called here the provider, when it provides reverse auction services to the Government [@CL-EST-2037]. The provider shall not assert or imply that it can or will obtain a Government contract for the participants of a reverse auction [@CL-EST-1943]. It shall allow entities to register, at no cost, as potential offerors for any reverse auction conducted on behalf of the Government on its platform [@CL-EST-1944]. As part of registration, it shall allow each entity the opportunity to execute a proprietary data protection agreement with the provider [@CL-EST-2038]. It shall not negotiate terms in that agreement that affect the terms and conditions of a Government solicitation or contract [@CL-EST-1945].

The provider shall limit access to, use of, and disclosure of Government data and Government-related data [@CL-EST-2039]. It shall not access, use, or disclose Government data unless specifically authorized by the terms of the contract or a task order or delivery order issued under it [@CL-EST-1946]. If the contract or such an order authorizes access to, or use or disclosure of, Government data, that access, use or disclosure shall only be for purposes specified in the contract or order [@CL-EST-2040]. The provider shall ensure that its employees are subject to all such access, use, and disclosure prohibitions and obligations [@CL-EST-2041]. These prohibitions and obligations survive the expiration or termination of the contract [@CL-EST-2042]. It shall notify the Contracting Officer promptly of any requests from a third party for access to Government data or Government-related data, including any warrants, seizures, or subpoenas it receives, including those from another Federal, State, or local agency [@CL-EST-1947]. It shall cooperate with the Contracting Officer to take all measures to protect Government data and Government-related data from any unauthorized disclosure [@CL-EST-2043].

The provider shall assert no right or license in the data gathered or generated during a reverse auction [@CL-EST-1948]. It shall use Government-related data only to manage the operational environment that supports the Government data and for no other purpose, unless otherwise permitted with the prior written approval of the Contracting Officer [@CL-EST-1949]. It shall protect from unauthorized use or disclosure, and not release outside of the Government, any price or proposal information or any source selection information received by it in relation to a reverse auction [@CL-EST-2044]. Price or proposal information includes, but is not limited to, contractor bid or proposal information as defined at FAR 3.104-1, and price or proposal information similarly generated for a task order, a delivery order or an order under a blanket purchase agreement [@CL-EST-2045]. It also includes information identified by the reverse auction participant as restricted from duplication, use, or disclosure, in whole or in part, for any purpose other than to evaluate the participant's price or proposal [@CL-EST-2046].

The provider shall allow offerors to see the successive lowest prices offered in the auction without revealing an offeror's identity [@CL-EST-1950]. It shall not participate as an offeror in any reverse auction that it is hosting on behalf of the Government [@CL-EST-1951]. That prohibition includes participation by any entity with which the provider has a relationship that raises an actual or potential conflict of interest [@CL-EST-1952].

At the close of each auction, the provider shall give the Contracting Officer the successful offer, with information that separately identifies the offeror's price and the price for each provider fee or charge included in the total price [@CL-EST-1953]. It shall also provide the Contracting Officer with all information and documentation received from reverse auction participants in response to the reverse auction [@CL-EST-1954].

## How it works

The provider acts as a channel for the Government's auction: it may not bid, may not promise a contract, and may not assert a right or license in the auction data [@CL-EST-1951; @CL-EST-1943; @CL-EST-1948]. Registration costs an entity nothing [@CL-EST-1944], but provider fees or charges may be included in the total price, and the provider reports them to the Contracting Officer separately from the offeror's price [@CL-EST-1953]. Government data moves only where the contract or an order specifically authorizes it, the duties outlast the contract, and a third-party demand is reported to the Contracting Officer [@CL-EST-1946; @CL-EST-2042; @CL-EST-1947]. What the contract or an order authorizes is contract-specific.

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text under Class Deviation 2026-O0038, effective February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. RFO FAR 52.217-12 keeps the definition of Government data [@CL-EST-0546; @CL-EST-2033], the definition of Government-related data [@CL-EST-9169; @CL-EST-2034] and its exclusion of a contractor’s business records and of data such as operating procedures, software coding, or algorithms that are not uniquely applied to the Government data [@CL-EST-9170; @CL-EST-2035], and the definition of reverse auction [@CL-EST-9171; @CL-EST-2036]. It keeps the duty not to assert or imply that the Contractor can or will obtain a Government contract for the participants of a reverse auction [@CL-EST-0547; @CL-EST-2037; @CL-EST-1943], and the duty to allow entities to register, at no cost, as potential offerors on the provider’s platform [@CL-EST-0548; @CL-EST-1944]. As part of registration it keeps the Contractor’s duty to allow each entity the opportunity to execute a proprietary data protection agreement with the Contractor [@CL-EST-9172; @CL-EST-2038], and the bar on negotiating terms in that agreement that affect the terms and conditions of a Government solicitation or contract [@CL-EST-9173; @CL-EST-1945]. It keeps the duty to limit access to, use of, and disclosure of Government data and Government-related data, and the bar on accessing, using, or disclosing Government data unless specifically authorized by the terms of the contract or a task order or delivery order issued under it [@CL-EST-0549; @CL-EST-2039; @CL-EST-1946], the limit that any authorized access, use, or disclosure be only for purposes specified in the contract or order [@CL-EST-9174; @CL-EST-2040], the Contractor’s duty to ensure that its employees are subject to the prohibitions and obligations and the survival of those prohibitions and obligations after expiration or termination of the contract [@CL-EST-9175; @CL-EST-2041; @CL-EST-2042], the duty to notify the Contracting Officer promptly of third party requests for access to Government data or Government-related data, including warrants, seizures, or subpoenas from another Federal, State, or local agency [@CL-EST-0550; @CL-EST-1947], and the duty to cooperate with the Contracting Officer to take all measures to protect that data from any unauthorized disclosure [@CL-EST-9176; @CL-EST-2043]. It keeps the rule that the Contractor asserts no right or license in the data gathered or generated during a reverse auction [@CL-EST-0551; @CL-EST-1948], and the use of Government-related data only to manage the operational environment that supports the Government data and for no other purpose unless otherwise permitted with the prior written approval of the Contracting Officer [@CL-EST-9177; @CL-EST-1949]. It keeps the duty to protect from unauthorized use or disclosure, and not release outside of the Government, any price or proposal information or source selection information received in relation to a reverse auction [@CL-EST-9178; @CL-EST-2044], with price or proposal information including contractor bid or proposal information as defined at FAR 3.104-1 and price or proposal information similarly generated for a task order, delivery order or order under a blanket purchase agreement [@CL-EST-9179; @CL-EST-2045], and information identified by the reverse auction participant as restricted from duplication, use, or disclosure for any purpose other than to evaluate the participant’s price or proposal [@CL-EST-9180; @CL-EST-2046]. It keeps the duty to allow offerors to see the successive lowest prices offered in the auction without revealing an offeror’s identity [@CL-EST-9181; @CL-EST-1950], and the bar on participating as an offeror in an auction the Contractor is hosting, including participation by an entity with a relationship that raises an actual or potential conflict of interest [@CL-EST-0552; @CL-EST-1951; @CL-EST-1952]. At the close of each auction it keeps the duty to give the Contracting Officer the successful offer with the offeror’s price and the price of each provider fee or charge separately identified [@CL-EST-0553; @CL-EST-1953], and all information and documentation received from participants [@CL-EST-0554; @CL-EST-1954].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A provider runs an auction for the Government. The winning offeror's price is 290,000 and the provider's platform fee is 10,000, so the total price is 300,000. At the close the provider gives the Contracting Officer the successful offer, with information that identifies the offeror's price of 290,000 and the platform fee of 10,000 separately, as parts of the total price of 300,000. It also gives the Contracting Officer all information and documentation received from the participants. Later, a state agency serves the provider with a subpoena for Government data. The provider notifies the Contracting Officer promptly and cooperates in protecting the data. An affiliate of the provider with a conflict-of-interest relationship asks to bid in the auction the provider hosts. The prohibition on participating as an offeror reaches that affiliate.

## Never confuse

A reverse auction service provider is not the Contracting Officer: it runs the means for the auction and reports to the Contracting Officer, and it may not appear as an offeror [@CL-EST-1951; @CL-EST-1953]. Free registration for offerors is not the same as free services: provider fees or charges may be included in the total price and are identified separately at the close [@CL-EST-1944; @CL-EST-1953]. Government data and Government-related data are different defined terms, and the second excludes the contractor's business records [@CL-EST-2033; @CL-EST-2034; @CL-EST-2035]. How offerors take part is on [Reverse auction under a solicitation](est-reverse-auction.md).
