## Purpose

Shows how an offeror takes part in a reverse auction under a solicitation, what the Government may show other offerors, how the offeror can leave the auction, and how price and proposal information is protected.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-EST-1931] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.217-10 [@CL-EST-1931] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-EST-1931] |
| Applies to | Solicitations under which the Government intends to conduct a reverse auction to award a contract or blanket purchase agreement [@CL-EST-1932] |

A reverse auction is the process for obtaining pricing, usually supported by an electronic tool, in which offerors see competing offerors' prices without disclosure of the competing offerors' identity and have the opportunity to submit lower priced offers until the close of the auction [@CL-EST-1931]. The Government states that it intends to conduct a reverse auction under the solicitation to award a contract or blanket purchase agreement [@CL-EST-1932].

By submitting a quote or proposal in response to the solicitation, the offeror agrees to participate in the reverse auction and agrees that the Government may reveal the offered prices in the auction to all offerors without revealing any offeror's identity [@CL-EST-1933]. The Government reveals no offeror's identity this way, except for the awardee's identity subsequent to an award resulting from the auction [@CL-EST-1934]. The offeror may withdraw its agreement to further participate by withdrawing its offer before the close of the auction, notifying the Contracting Officer via the contact method identified in the solicitation [@CL-EST-1935]. If the reverse auction produces only one offer, the Government reserves the right to cancel the auction [@CL-EST-1936].

The Government may use a reverse auction service provider to conduct the reverse auction [@CL-EST-2020]. A reverse auction service provider is a commercial or Government entity that provides a means for conducting reverse auctions when acquiring supplies or services to be used by the Government [@CL-EST-2019]. Any price or proposal information or source selection information that the provider receives in relation to the reverse auction shall not be released outside the Government, unless otherwise required by law [@CL-EST-1937]. This does not prevent the Government from revealing to all offerors the offered prices in the auction without revealing any offeror's identity [@CL-EST-2021]. Price or proposal information includes, but is not limited to, contractor bid or proposal information as defined at Federal Acquisition Regulation 3.104-1 [@CL-EST-2022], and information identified by the offeror as restricted from duplication, use, or disclosure, in whole or in part, for any purpose other than to evaluate the offeror's price or proposal [@CL-EST-2023]. The contact method for withdrawal is stated in each solicitation and is contract-specific.

## How it works

The auction is a price competition with one-sided visibility: offerors see prices, not identities [@CL-EST-1931]. Agreement comes from submitting a quote or proposal rather than from a separate signature, and it includes agreement that the Government may show the offered prices to the other offerors [@CL-EST-1933]. The exit is tied to the offer itself, so it must be taken before the auction closes [@CL-EST-1935]. A single-offer auction gives the Government a right to cancel, not a duty to award [@CL-EST-1936]. Information that the offeror identifies as restricted is price or proposal information, so the provider may not release it outside the Government unless the law requires it [@CL-EST-1937; @CL-EST-2023].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

Three offerors quote in an auction for a blanket purchase agreement: A at 250,000, B at 240,000 and C at 238,000. Each sees the competing offerors' prices without names. Offeror A decides to leave and notifies the Contracting Officer, through the contact method named in the solicitation, that it withdraws its offer before the close. Two offers remain at the close, so the one-offer cancellation right does not arise. Had B also withdrawn before the close, only C's offer would remain and the Government could cancel the auction. If the Government awards to C, its identity as awardee may be revealed after the award, and the exception covers only the awardee.

## Never confuse

A reverse auction is not sealed bidding: offerors see competing prices and may submit lower priced offers until the auction closes [@CL-EST-1931]. The sealed bidding rules are on [Sealed bidding: amendments, bid submission and explanations](est-sealed-bidding-essentials.md). Withdrawing agreement works by withdrawing the offer before the close, so it is not a lower priced offer [@CL-EST-1935]. Showing prices to all offerors is allowed, but price or proposal information may not be released outside the Government unless the law requires it [@CL-EST-2021; @CL-EST-1937]. Orders placed under an existing contract or agreement are covered on [Reverse auction for orders under a contract or agreement](est-reverse-auction-orders.md), and the provider's own duties are on [Duties of a reverse auction service provider](est-reverse-auction-services.md).
