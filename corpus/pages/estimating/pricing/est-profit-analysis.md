## Purpose

Shows how the profit or fee portion of a prenegotiation objective is set when a price negotiation is based on cost analysis.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-EST-0162] |
| Codified citation | FAR 15.404-4 [@CL-EST-0162] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-EST-0162] |
| Applies to | Price negotiations based on cost analysis [@CL-EST-0162] |

This subsection prescribes policies for establishing the profit or fee portion of the prenegotiation objective in price negotiations based on cost analysis [@CL-EST-0162]. Profit or fee objectives do not necessarily represent net income to contractors; they represent that element of the potential total remuneration that contractors may receive over and above allowable costs [@CL-EST-0163].

The contracting officer shall not negotiate a fee that exceeds 15 percent of estimated cost, excluding fee, for experimental, developmental, or research work under a cost-plus-fixed-fee contract [@CL-EST-0164], or 10 percent of estimated cost, excluding fee, for other cost-plus-fixed-fee contracts [@CL-EST-0166], and for architect-engineer services for public works or utilities the contract price or the estimated cost and fee for production and delivery of designs, plans, drawings, and specifications shall not exceed 6 percent of the estimated cost of construction, excluding fees [@CL-EST-0165].

## How it works

When the negotiation is not based on cost analysis, the contracting officer is not required to analyze profit [@CL-EST-0167]. When it is based on cost analysis, the contracting officer considers each of the common factors outlined in the regulation, including contractor effort, contract cost risk, federal socioeconomic programs, capital investments, cost-control and other past accomplishments, and independent development, unless clearly inappropriate or not applicable [@CL-EST-0168]. Subject to the authorities in FAR 1.301(c), agencies making noncompetitive contract awards over $100,000 totaling $50 million or more a year shall use a structured approach for determining the profit or fee objective in acquisitions that require cost analysis, and may prescribe specific exemptions where mandatory use would be clearly inappropriate [@CL-EST-0169; @CL-EST-0251].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A cost-plus-fixed-fee contract for research work has an estimated cost of 4,000,000 excluding fee. The fee is capped at 15 percent, so the fee may not exceed 600,000. A fee negotiated at 500,000 is within the limit, while 700,000 would exceed it.

## Never confuse

Profit analysis sets the profit or fee objective over and above allowable costs, which does not necessarily represent the contractor's net income [@CL-EST-0163], and contracting officers are not required to analyze profit when the price negotiation is not based on cost analysis [@CL-EST-0167].
