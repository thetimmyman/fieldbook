## Purpose

Shows how the profit or fee portion of a prenegotiation objective is set when a price negotiation is based on cost analysis.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-EST-0162] |
| Codified citation | FAR 15.404-4 [@CL-EST-0162] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 15.404-9 [@CL-EST-3381] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-EST-0162] |
| Applies to | Price negotiations based on cost analysis [@CL-EST-0162] |

This subsection prescribes policies for establishing the profit or fee portion of the Government prenegotiation objective in price negotiations based on cost analysis [@CL-EST-0162]. Profit or fee prenegotiation objectives do not necessarily represent net income to contractors; they represent that element of the potential total remuneration that contractors may receive for contract performance over and above allowable costs [@CL-EST-0163].

The contracting officer shall not negotiate a price or fee that exceeds the statutory limitations [@CL-EST-9523]: for experimental, developmental, or research work performed under a cost-plus-fixed-fee contract, the fee shall not exceed 15 percent of the contract's estimated cost, excluding fee [@CL-EST-0164]; for other cost-plus-fixed-fee contracts, the fee shall not exceed 10 percent of the contract's estimated cost, excluding fee [@CL-EST-0166]; and for architect-engineer services for public works or utilities, the contract price or the estimated cost and fee for production and delivery of designs, plans, drawings, and specifications shall not exceed 6 percent of the estimated cost of construction of the public work or utility, excluding fees [@CL-EST-0165].

## How it works

When the negotiation is not based on cost analysis, the contracting officer is not required to analyze profit [@CL-EST-0167]. Unless clearly inappropriate or not applicable, each common factor outlined in paragraphs (d)(1)(i) through (vi) of the subsection shall be considered by agencies in developing their structured approaches and by contracting officers in analyzing profit, whether or not using a structured approach [@CL-EST-0168; @CL-EST-9705]. The six common factors are contractor effort, contract cost risk, Federal socioeconomic programs, capital investments, cost-control and other past accomplishments, and independent development [@CL-EST-9706; @CL-EST-9526; @CL-EST-9527; @CL-EST-9528; @CL-EST-9529; @CL-EST-9530; @CL-EST-9531]. Subject to the authorities in FAR 1.301(c), agencies making noncompetitive contract awards over $100,000 totaling $50 million or more a year shall use a structured approach for determining the profit or fee objective in those acquisitions that require cost analysis, and may prescribe specific exemptions for situations in which mandatory use of a structured approach would be clearly inappropriate [@CL-EST-0169; @CL-EST-9524; @CL-EST-0251].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 15 text in place of the codified text under Class Deviation 2026-O0048, whose Revision 1 took effect immediately and supersedes the March 5, 2026 version [@CL-EST-3176] [@CL-EST-3177]. RFO FAR 15.404-9(a) keeps the statement that the subsection prescribes policies for establishing the profit or fee portion of the Government prenegotiation objective in price negotiations based on cost analysis [@CL-EST-0162; @CL-EST-3381]. Paragraph (a) is followed directly by paragraph (b) Policy, where codified paragraph (a)(1) goes on to say that profit or fee objectives do not necessarily represent net income [@CL-EST-0163; @CL-EST-3382]. RFO FAR 15.404-9(c) lists contracting officer responsibilities, and its item (4) says contracting officers must not negotiate a price or fee that exceeds the statutory limitations [@CL-EST-9525; @CL-EST-3383], and the limits are kept: the fee must not exceed 15 percent of the contract's estimated cost, excluding fee, for experimental, developmental, or research work performed under a cost-plus-fixed-fee contract [@CL-EST-0164; @CL-EST-3384], the contract price or the estimated cost and fee for production and delivery of designs, plans, drawings, and specifications must not exceed 6 percent of the estimated cost of construction of the public work or utility, excluding fees, for architect-engineer services for public works or utilities [@CL-EST-0165; @CL-EST-3385], and the fee must not exceed 10 percent of the contract's estimated cost, excluding fee, for other cost-plus-fixed-fee contracts [@CL-EST-0166; @CL-EST-3386]. The rule that contracting officers are not required to analyze profit when the price negotiation is not based on cost analysis is now RFO FAR 15.404-9(b)(3) [@CL-EST-0167; @CL-EST-3387], and each factor in paragraphs (d)(1)(i) through (vi) must be considered, unless clearly inappropriate or not applicable, by agencies in developing their structured approaches and by contracting officers in analyzing profit, whether or not using a structured approach [@CL-EST-3388]. Agencies making noncompetitive contract awards over $100,000 totaling $50 million or more a year must use a structured approach in acquisitions that require cost analysis, subject to the authorities in part 1 where the codified text says 1.301(c) [@CL-EST-0169; @CL-EST-9524; @CL-EST-3389], and may prescribe specific exemptions for situations in which mandatory use of a structured approach would be clearly inappropriate [@CL-EST-0251; @CL-EST-3390].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A cost-plus-fixed-fee contract for research work has an estimated cost of 4,000,000 excluding fee. The fee is capped at 15 percent, so the fee may not exceed 600,000. A fee negotiated at 500,000 is within the limit, while 700,000 would exceed it.

## Never confuse

Profit or fee prenegotiation objectives do not necessarily represent net income to contractors; they represent that element of the potential total remuneration that contractors may receive for contract performance over and above allowable costs [@CL-EST-0163], and contracting officers are not required to analyze profit when the price negotiation is not based on cost analysis [@CL-EST-0167].

The sentence that profit or fee prenegotiation objectives do not necessarily represent net income is in codified FAR 15.404-4(a)(1) [@CL-EST-0163], and RFO FAR 15.404-9(a) is a one-sentence general paragraph followed directly by paragraph (b) [@CL-EST-3382].
