## Purpose

Explains when a sealed bid must be supported by descriptive literature, what that literature is used for, what happens when it is late or does not show the product meets the solicitation, and how Alternate I allows a waiver for a product supplied under a prior contract.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-EST-2010] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.214-21 [@CL-EST-2011]; FAR 2.101 [@CL-EST-9333] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.214-21 [@CL-EST-0160; @CL-EST-0182] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-EST-2010] |
| Applies to | Solicitations that include the descriptive literature provision [@CL-EST-2120], and those that add its Alternate I [@CL-EST-2124] |

Descriptive literature is information furnished by a bidder, such as cuts, illustrations, drawings and brochures, that shows a product's characteristics or construction or explains its operation [@CL-EST-2010]. The term includes only that information required to evaluate the acceptability of the product and excludes other information for operating or maintaining the product [@CL-EST-2121]. The FAR definitions section defines the term for the whole FAR as information provided by an offeror, such as cuts, illustrations, drawings, and brochures, that shows a product's characteristics or construction of a product or explains its operation [@CL-EST-9333]. The provision on this card says information furnished by a bidder [@CL-EST-2010].

It is required to establish, for the purpose of evaluation and award, details of the product offered that are specified elsewhere in the solicitation and pertain to significant elements [@CL-EST-2011]. The significant elements include design, materials, components, performance characteristics, and methods of manufacture, assembly, construction or operation [@CL-EST-2122].

## How it works

Descriptive literature required elsewhere in the solicitation must be identified to show the items of the offer to which it applies and received by the time specified in the solicitation [@CL-EST-2123]. If the bidder fails to submit it on time, the Government will reject the bid, except that late descriptive literature sent by mail may be considered under the late submissions provision [@CL-EST-2012]. If the literature fails to show that the product offered conforms to the requirements of the solicitation, the Government will reject the bid [@CL-EST-2013]. Late items sent by mail are handled under [Late submissions, modifications and withdrawals of bids](est-late-bid-submissions-and-withdrawals.md).

Alternate I adds two paragraphs to the basic provision [@CL-EST-2124]. Under the first, the Contracting Officer may waive the requirement for furnishing descriptive literature if the offeror has supplied a product that is the same as that required by the solicitation under a prior contract [@CL-EST-2125]. A bidder that requests the waiver must provide the prior contract number, the date of the prior contract, the line item number of the product supplied, the name and address of the Government activity to which delivery was made, and the date of final delivery [@CL-EST-2126].

Under the second, bidders must submit bids on the basis of required descriptive literature or on the basis of a previously supplied product under the waiver paragraph [@CL-EST-2127]. A bidder submitting a bid on one of these two bases may not elect to have its bid considered on the alternative basis after the time specified for receipt of bids [@CL-EST-2128]. The Government will disregard a bidder's request for a waiver if that bidder has submitted the descriptive literature requested under the solicitation [@CL-EST-2129].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text from February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. The RFO provision keeps the definition of descriptive literature [@CL-EST-0160] [@CL-EST-2010], including that the term includes only the information required to evaluate the acceptability of the product and excludes other information for operating or maintaining it [@CL-EST-0161] [@CL-EST-2121]. It keeps the rule that descriptive literature required elsewhere in the solicitation must be identified to show the items of the offer to which it applies and received by the time specified [@CL-EST-0182] [@CL-EST-2123], the rejection of a bid for late literature, except that late literature sent by mail may be considered under the late submissions provision [@CL-EST-0183] [@CL-EST-2012], and the rejection of a bid whose literature fails to show that the product conforms [@CL-EST-0184] [@CL-EST-2013]. Alternate I keeps the Contracting Officer's authority to waive the requirement when the offeror has supplied the same product under a prior contract [@CL-EST-0185] [@CL-EST-2125], the requirement to bid on the basis of the literature or of a previously supplied product [@CL-EST-0186] [@CL-EST-2127], the bar on switching bases after the time for receipt of bids [@CL-EST-0187] [@CL-EST-2128] and the rule that a waiver request is disregarded if the bidder submitted the literature [@CL-EST-0188] [@CL-EST-2129].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A solicitation requires descriptive literature on flow rate for a pump, and bids are due at 3:00 p.m. on a Friday. Bidder A sends brochures that show the housing and say nothing about the flow rate the solicitation specifies. The literature fails to show that the product offered conforms, so the Government rejects the bid.

Bidder B's literature is complete but is received after the time specified. B mailed it, so it may be considered under the late submissions provision. Without that, the bid is rejected for late literature.

Under a solicitation with Alternate I, Bidder C bids on the basis of a previously supplied product. It gives the prior contract number, the date of that contract, the line item number, the name and address of the Government activity that took delivery, and the date of final delivery, and the Contracting Officer may waive the literature. Bidder D requests the waiver but also submits the literature the solicitation requested, so the Government disregards the waiver request. After the time for receipt of bids passes, neither C nor D may ask to be considered on the other basis.

## Never confuse

Descriptive literature is not a bid sample: literature is information such as cuts, illustrations, drawings and brochures, while a sample is a product sample [@CL-EST-2010; @CL-EST-2005]. See [Bid samples under sealed bidding](est-bid-samples.md).

Lateness and conformance are separate tests: late literature sent by mail may be considered [@CL-EST-2012], while literature that arrives on time but fails to show conformance leads to rejection [@CL-EST-2013].

The definition covers only information required to evaluate acceptability, and excludes other information for operating or maintaining the product [@CL-EST-2121].

A waiver under Alternate I is something the Contracting Officer may grant, not a right, and a request made alongside submitted literature is disregarded [@CL-EST-2125; @CL-EST-2129].
