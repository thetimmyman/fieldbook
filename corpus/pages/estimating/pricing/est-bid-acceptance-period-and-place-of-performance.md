## Purpose

Explains how long a sealed bid stays open for acceptance, how a minimum acceptance period set by the Government works, and what the bidder states about plants or facilities at another address.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-EST-1999] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.214-14 [@CL-EST-1999]; FAR 52.214-15 [@CL-EST-2000]; FAR 52.214-16 [@CL-EST-2001] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.214-14 [@CL-EST-0026; @CL-EST-0027]; RFO FAR 52.214-15 [@CL-EST-0028; @CL-EST-0029]; RFO FAR 52.214-16 [@CL-EST-0030; @CL-EST-0032; @CL-EST-0036] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-EST-1999] |
| Applies to | Invitations for bids that include the place of performance provision [@CL-EST-2103] or the period for acceptance provision [@CL-EST-2105]; the minimum period provision is inserted in invitations for bids, except for construction, if the Contracting Officer determines that a minimum acceptance period must be specified [@CL-EST-2004] |

In the period for acceptance provision, the bidder agrees, if its bid is accepted within a stated number of calendar days (60 calendar days unless a different period is inserted by the bidder) from the date specified in the solicitation for receipt of bids, to furnish any or all items upon which prices are bid at the price set opposite each item, delivered at the designated points, within the time specified in the Schedule [@CL-EST-2000; @CL-EST-2106].

In the minimum bid acceptance period provision, the acceptance period is the number of calendar days available to the Government for awarding a contract, counted from the date specified in the solicitation for receipt of bids [@CL-EST-2001]. The provision supersedes any language on the acceptance period that appears elsewhere in the solicitation [@CL-EST-2107]. The Government states its minimum in a blank that the Contracting Officer fills in with a number of calendar days [@CL-EST-2108]. In the space provided, bidders may specify a longer acceptance period than the minimum [@CL-EST-2002]. A bid allowing less than the minimum will be rejected [@CL-EST-2003].

The bidder agrees to execute all that it has undertaken to do, in compliance with its bid, if the bid is accepted in writing within the minimum period or within any longer period the bidder stated [@CL-EST-2109].

## How it works

In the place of performance provision, the bidder checks one box to state whether it intends or does not intend to use one or more plants or facilities located at a different address from the bidder's address in the bid [@CL-EST-1999]. A bidder that checks intends must insert the required information in the spaces provided: the place of performance (street address, city, county, state, zip code) and the name and address of the owner and operator of the plant or facility if other than the bidder [@CL-EST-2104].

The provisions contain blanks and boxes to complete: the Government's minimum number of calendar days [@CL-EST-2108], any longer period the bidder writes [@CL-EST-2002], the period the bidder inserts in place of the default [@CL-EST-2000], and the plant or facility details [@CL-EST-2104]. The negotiated-acquisition version of the place of performance statement is covered on [Place of performance in a solicitation response](est-place-of-performance.md).

Under the RFO text, the DoD class deviation directs DoD contracting officers to use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text, effective February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. RFO FAR 52.214-14(a) keeps the checked statement that the bidder intends or does not intend to use one or more plants or facilities located at a different address from the address of the bidder as indicated in the bid [@CL-EST-0026; @CL-EST-1999]. RFO FAR 52.214-14(b) keeps the rule that a bidder that checks intends inserts in the spaces provided the place of performance (street address, city, county, state, zip code) and the name and address of the owner and operator of the plant or facility if other than the bidder [@CL-EST-0027; @CL-EST-2104]. RFO FAR 52.214-15 keeps the bid acceptance period of a stated number of calendar days, 60 calendar days unless the bidder inserts a different period, from the date specified in the solicitation for receipt of bids [@CL-EST-0028; @CL-EST-2000], and keeps the bidder's agreement to furnish any or all items upon which prices are bid at the price set opposite each item, delivered at the designated points, within the time specified in the Schedule [@CL-EST-0029; @CL-EST-2106]. RFO FAR 52.214-16(a) keeps the definition of the acceptance period as the number of calendar days available to the Government for awarding a contract from the date specified in the solicitation for receipt of bids [@CL-EST-0030; @CL-EST-2001]. RFO FAR 52.214-16(b) keeps the rule that the provision supersedes any language pertaining to the acceptance period that may appear elsewhere in the solicitation [@CL-EST-0031; @CL-EST-2107]. RFO FAR 52.214-16(c) keeps the Government's minimum acceptance period, a number of calendar days the Contracting Officer inserts [@CL-EST-0032; @CL-EST-2108]. RFO FAR 52.214-16(d) keeps the rule that, in the space provided, bidders may specify a longer acceptance period than the Government's minimum requirement [@CL-EST-0033; @CL-EST-2002]. RFO FAR 52.214-16(e) keeps the rule that a bid allowing less than the Government's minimum acceptance period will be rejected [@CL-EST-0034; @CL-EST-2003]. RFO FAR 52.214-16(f) keeps the bidder's agreement to execute all that it has undertaken to do, in compliance with its bid, if the bid is accepted in writing within the acceptance period stated in paragraph (c) or any longer acceptance period stated in paragraph (d) [@CL-EST-0035; @CL-EST-2109]. The RFO introductory line for FAR 52.214-16 says to insert the following provision as prescribed in 14.207(g) and states no limit to invitations for bids other than construction or to cases where the contracting officer determines that a minimum acceptance period must be specified, which are the limits the codified introductory line states [@CL-EST-0036; @CL-EST-2004].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An invitation sets a minimum acceptance period of 45 calendar days and a receipt date of 1 June. Bidder A writes 30 calendar days in the space for a longer period, so its bid allows less than the minimum and will be rejected.

Bidder B writes 60 calendar days, which is longer than the minimum. The Government accepts B's bid in writing on 20 July, 49 days after 1 June, which is inside B's period, so B agrees to execute all that it has undertaken to do in compliance with its bid.

Bidder B also checks intends in the place of performance provision. It inserts the address of a plant that another company owns and operates, and it names that company and its address as owner and operator.

## Never confuse

The acceptance period is not a delivery period: it counts the calendar days available to the Government for awarding a contract [@CL-EST-2001], while the delivery time is the one stated in the Schedule [@CL-EST-2106].

A longer period than the minimum is allowed, and a shorter one is not: a bid allowing less than the minimum will be rejected [@CL-EST-2002; @CL-EST-2003].

The period in the period for acceptance provision is 60 calendar days unless the bidder inserts a different period [@CL-EST-2000]; the minimum provision supersedes any language pertaining to the acceptance period that may appear elsewhere in the solicitation [@CL-EST-2107].

The place of performance box concerns plants or facilities at an address different from the bidder's address in the bid [@CL-EST-1999].

The codified line introducing the minimum provision limits it to invitations for bids other than construction where the contracting officer determines that a minimum period must be specified [@CL-EST-2004], and the RFO introductory line for that provision does not repeat those limits [@CL-EST-0036].
