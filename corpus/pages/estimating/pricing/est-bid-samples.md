## Purpose

Explains when a sealed bid must include product samples, how the Government tests them, what happens when samples arrive late or do not conform, and how Alternates I and II let the Contracting Officer waive the requirement.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-EST-2005] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.214-20 [@CL-EST-2006] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-EST-2005] |
| Applies to | Solicitations that include the bid samples provision [@CL-EST-2110], and those that add its Alternate I [@CL-EST-2112] or Alternate II [@CL-EST-2116] |

A bid sample is a product sample required to be submitted by a bidder to show those characteristics of the offered products that cannot adequately be described by specifications, purchase descriptions or the invitation for bid [@CL-EST-2005]. Bidders must furnish bid samples as part of the bid, and the Government must receive them by the time specified in the invitation for bids [@CL-EST-2006].

If the bidder fails to submit samples on time, the Government will reject the bid, except that the Contracting Officer will consider a late sample sent by mail under the late submissions provision of the solicitation [@CL-EST-2007].

## How it works

The Government will test or evaluate bid samples to determine compliance with all the characteristics listed for examination in the solicitation, and will reject the bid when the sample fails to conform to the required characteristics [@CL-EST-2008]. Products delivered under any resulting contract must conform to the approved sample for the characteristics listed for test or evaluation and to the specifications for all other characteristics [@CL-EST-2111].

Unless the solicitation says otherwise, bid samples are submitted at no expense to the Government and are returned at the bidder's request and expense, unless they are destroyed during preaward testing [@CL-EST-2009]. Late samples sent by mail are handled under [Late submissions, modifications and withdrawals of bids](est-late-bid-submissions-and-withdrawals.md).

Under Alternate I, the Contracting Officer may, at the Contracting Officer's discretion, waive the bid sample requirement for a bidder if, among other conditions, the bid states that the offered product is the same as a product offered by the bidder to the office or activity named in the blank [@CL-EST-2113]. The waiver also requires the Contracting Officer to determine that the previously offered product was accepted or tested and found to comply with specification and other requirements for technical acceptability conforming in every material respect with those in the solicitation [@CL-EST-2114].

Alternate II has the same discretion, but its first condition also requires that the product was offered on a previous acquisition [@CL-EST-2117; @CL-EST-2118]. Its second condition is that the Contracting Officer must determine that the previously offered product was accepted or tested and found to comply with specification and other requirements for technical acceptability conforming in every material respect with those of the solicitation [@CL-EST-2118]. It also requires that the product offered under the solicitation will be produced under a resulting contract at the same plant in which the previously acquired or tested product was produced [@CL-EST-2119].

In both alternates the office or activity in the blank is the one the Contracting Officer designates, and it is contract-specific [@CL-EST-2115].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A solicitation for 500 units requires 2 samples to be received by 10 March. Bidder A's samples arrive on 9 March. The Government tests them against the characteristics listed for examination, and both conform. The bid stays in the competition. One sample is destroyed during the preaward testing and is not returned. The other is returned when Bidder A asks, at A's expense. If A wins, the 500 units must conform to the approved sample for the tested characteristics and to the specifications for every other characteristic.

Bidder B mails its samples and they arrive on 12 March. They are late, but because they were sent by mail the Contracting Officer will consider them under the late submissions provision.

Under a solicitation with Alternate I, Bidder C states that its product is the same as a product it offered to the office named in the blank, and the Contracting Officer determines that the product was previously tested and found to comply. The Contracting Officer may waive the sample requirement for C.

## Never confuse

A bid sample is not the product delivered under the contract: the delivered products must conform to the approved sample for the tested characteristics and to the specifications for all others [@CL-EST-2111].

Lateness and conformance are separate tests: a late sample sent by mail will be considered under the late submissions provision [@CL-EST-2007], while a sample that arrives on time and does not conform still leads to rejection [@CL-EST-2008].

A waiver under either alternate is at the Contracting Officer's discretion, not a right of the bidder [@CL-EST-2113; @CL-EST-2117].

A bid sample is not descriptive literature, which is information such as cuts, illustrations, drawings and brochures [@CL-EST-2010]; see [Descriptive literature under sealed bidding](est-descriptive-literature.md).
