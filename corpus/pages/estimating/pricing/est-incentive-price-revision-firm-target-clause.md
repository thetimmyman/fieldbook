## Purpose

Shows the data the contractor submits, how the total final price is computed, the ceiling, and how billing prices move under the firm target clause. The contract-type card covers when a fixed-price incentive contract fits, so this card does not repeat it: [Fixed-price incentive contracts and letter contracts](est-fixed-price-incentive-and-letter-contracts.md).

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-EST-1659] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.216-16 [@CL-EST-1659] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-EST-1659] |
| Applies to | Schedule items the clause identifies as subject to price revision [@CL-EST-1660] |

The clause makes the Schedule items it identifies subject to price revision, provided that in no event may their total final price exceed the stated ceiling price [@CL-EST-1660].

The contractor submits its data within a number of days set by the contracting officer after the end of the month in which it delivered the last unit of supplies and completed the services for the items [@CL-EST-1661]. Two listed data items are a detailed statement of all costs incurred up to the end of that month and an estimate of costs of further performance, if any, that may be necessary to complete all work under the items [@CL-EST-1662]. The listed data items also include a list of all residual inventory and an estimate of its value, and any other relevant data that the contracting officer may reasonably require [@CL-EST-1703].

## How it works

The total final price is found by applying an adjustment for profit or loss to the total final negotiated cost, and the adjustment equals the total target profit when total final negotiated cost equals total target cost [@CL-EST-1663]. Above target cost, the adjustment is the total target profit less a stated percent of the overrun [@CL-EST-1664]; below target cost, it is the total target profit plus a stated percent of the underrun [@CL-EST-1665]. Pending the final price, billing prices are the target prices shown in the contract [@CL-EST-1666], and the parties shall negotiate a reduction if it appears from the contractor's information that billing prices will be substantially greater than estimated final prices [@CL-EST-1667]. The parties may negotiate an increase in billing prices by any or all of the difference between target prices and the ceiling price, on factual data showing that final cost will be substantially greater than target cost [@CL-EST-1668].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

Target cost is 1,000,000, target profit is 100,000 and the ceiling price is 1,200,000. The clause fills in a 20 percent share of any overrun.

Final negotiated cost is 1,100,000. The overrun is 100,000, so the adjustment is 100,000 less 20,000, which is 80,000. The total final price is 1,180,000, which is under the ceiling.

## Never confuse

Billing prices and the total final price are different things. Billing prices apply pending the modification that sets the total final price, and they start at the target prices [@CL-EST-1666].

This clause is also not the successive targets clause, which has its own card: [Incentive price revision clause with successive targets](est-incentive-price-revision-successive-targets-clause.md). Nor is it price redetermination, which has its own card: [Prospective price redetermination clause](est-prospective-price-redetermination-clause.md).
