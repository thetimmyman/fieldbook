## Purpose

Lists what an unsolicited proposal should contain, including the price or cost information.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-EST-1432] |
| Codified citation | FAR 15.605 [@CL-EST-1432] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-EST-1432] |
| Applies to | Unsolicited proposals, which should contain the listed information [@CL-EST-1432] |

Unsolicited proposals should contain listed information to permit consideration in an objective and timely manner, beginning with basic information that includes the offeror's name, address and type of organization [@CL-EST-1432]. The cost or price element is the proposed price or total estimated cost for the effort in sufficient detail for meaningful evaluation [@CL-EST-1438].

The text of this section says nothing about certified cost or pricing data and names no format for the price.

## How it works

The rest of the basic information is the names and telephone numbers of technical and business contacts, identification of proprietary data to be used only for evaluation, the names of other agencies or parties receiving or funding the proposal, the date of submission, and the signature of a person authorized to represent and contractually obligate the offeror [@CL-EST-1432; @CL-EST-1433; @CL-EST-1434]. The technical information includes a concise title and abstract of approximately 200 words, a reasonably complete discussion of objectives, approach, results and mission support, names and biographies of key personnel, and the support needed from the agency [@CL-EST-1435; @CL-EST-1436; @CL-EST-1437]. The supporting information includes the price element, the period the proposal is valid with a 6-month minimum suggested, the contract type preferred, and the proposed duration [@CL-EST-1438; @CL-EST-1439]. It also lists a brief description of the organization, previous experience, relevant past performance and facilities to be used, and other statements, if applicable, about organizational conflicts of interest, security clearances and environmental impacts [@CL-EST-1438; @CL-EST-1455], and the names and telephone numbers of agency points of contact already contacted regarding the proposal [@CL-EST-1438; @CL-EST-1456].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An offeror drafts an unsolicited proposal for a study. It gives a one-page abstract, names two key staff, asks for no Government property, and shows a total estimated cost of 90,000 with labor, material and travel broken out. It states the proposal is valid for six months. The answer: the cost element is given in enough detail to be evaluated.

## Never confuse

The section asks for a price or total estimated cost in sufficient detail for meaningful evaluation [@CL-EST-1438] and does not mention certified cost or pricing data, so its price element should not be read as either requiring or excluding that data. The section also says proposals should contain these items, not that they must [@CL-EST-1432]. What makes a proposal valid is on [Valid unsolicited proposals and agency points of contact](est-unsolicited-proposals-general.md), and how data in it is restricted is on [Limited use of data in unsolicited proposals](est-unsolicited-proposal-data-legend.md).
