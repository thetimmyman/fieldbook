## Purpose

Lists what an unsolicited proposal should contain, including the price or cost information.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-EST-1432] |
| Codified citation | FAR 15.605 [@CL-EST-1432] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 15.503-2 [@CL-EST-3502] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-EST-1432] |
| Applies to | Unsolicited proposals, which should contain the listed information [@CL-EST-1432] |

Unsolicited proposals should contain listed information to permit consideration in an objective and timely manner, beginning with basic information that includes the offeror's name, address and type of organization [@CL-EST-1432]. The cost or price element is the proposed price or total estimated cost for the effort in sufficient detail for meaningful evaluation [@CL-EST-1438].


## How it works

The rest of the basic information is the names and telephone numbers of technical and business personnel to be contacted for evaluation or negotiation purposes, identification of proprietary data to be used only for evaluation purposes, the names of other Federal, State, or local agencies or parties receiving the proposal or funding the proposed effort, the date of submission, and the signature of a person authorized to represent and contractually obligate the offeror [@CL-EST-1432; @CL-EST-1433; @CL-EST-1434]. The technical information includes a concise title and abstract of approximately 200 words, a reasonably complete discussion of objectives, approach, results and mission support, names and biographical information on the offeror's key personnel who would be involved, including alternates, and the type of support needed from the agency, such as Government property or personnel resources [@CL-EST-1435; @CL-EST-1436; @CL-EST-1437]. The supporting information includes the price element, the period the proposal is valid with a 6-month minimum suggested, the contract type preferred, and the proposed duration [@CL-EST-1438; @CL-EST-1439]. It also lists a brief description of the organization, previous experience, relevant past performance and facilities to be used, and other statements, if applicable, about organizational conflicts of interest, security clearances and environmental impacts [@CL-EST-1438; @CL-EST-1455], and the names and telephone numbers of agency technical or other agency points of contact already contacted regarding the proposal [@CL-EST-1438; @CL-EST-1456].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 15 text in place of the codified text under Class Deviation 2026-O0048, whose Revision 1 took effect immediately and supersedes the March 5, 2026 version [@CL-EST-3176] [@CL-EST-3177]. RFO FAR 15.503-2 says unsolicited proposals must contain the listed information to permit consideration in an objective and timely manner, where the codified section says they should contain it [@CL-EST-3502; @CL-EST-1432]. Its basic information is the offeror's name and address and type of organization, the names and telephone numbers of technical and business personnel to be contacted for evaluation or negotiation purposes, identification of proprietary data to be used only for evaluation purposes, the names of other Federal, State, or local agencies or parties receiving the proposal or funding the proposed effort, the date of submission, and the signature of a person authorized to represent and contractually obligate the offeror, the same as the codified list [@CL-EST-3502; @CL-EST-3503; @CL-EST-3504; @CL-EST-1432; @CL-EST-1433; @CL-EST-1434]. Its technical information asks for a concise title and short abstract of the proposed effort, where the codified section says abstract (approximately 200 words) [@CL-EST-3505; @CL-EST-1435], and otherwise lists the same discussion of objectives, approach, results and mission support, names and biographical information on key personnel including alternates, and type of support needed from the agency [@CL-EST-3506; @CL-EST-3507; @CL-EST-1436; @CL-EST-1437]. Its supporting information lists the same proposed price or total estimated cost in sufficient detail for meaningful evaluation, the validity period with a 6-month minimum suggested, the type of contract preferred, the proposed duration, a brief description of the organization, previous experience, relevant past performance and facilities to be used, other statements, if applicable, about organizational conflicts of interest, security clearances and environmental impacts, and the names and telephone numbers of agency points of contact already contacted [@CL-EST-3508; @CL-EST-3509; @CL-EST-3510; @CL-EST-3511; @CL-EST-1438; @CL-EST-1439; @CL-EST-1455; @CL-EST-1456].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An offeror drafts an unsolicited proposal for a study. It gives a one-page abstract, names two key staff, asks for no Government property, and shows a total estimated cost of 90,000 with labor, material and travel broken out. It states the proposal is valid for six months. The answer: the cost element is given in enough detail to be evaluated.

## Never confuse

Codified FAR 15.605 says unsolicited proposals should contain the listed information [@CL-EST-1432], while RFO FAR 15.503-2 says they must contain it [@CL-EST-3502]. What makes a proposal valid is on [Valid unsolicited proposals and agency points of contact](est-unsolicited-proposals-general.md), and how data in it is restricted is on [Limited use of data in unsolicited proposals](est-unsolicited-proposal-data-legend.md).
