## Purpose

Gives the definitions of delivery-order contract and task-order contract that the indefinite-delivery subpart states for its own use, and the description and permitted use of a definite-quantity contract.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-EST-2977; @CL-EST-2979] |
| Codified citation | FAR 16.501-1 and 16.502 [@CL-EST-2977; @CL-EST-2979] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-EST-2977; @CL-EST-2979] |
| Applies to | The terms delivery-order contract and task-order contract, as used in this subpart [@CL-EST-2993; @CL-EST-2977; @CL-EST-2978], and definite-quantity contracts [@CL-EST-2979; @CL-EST-2980] |

### Terms

| Term | Full definition | Elements and exclusion | Claim |
|---|---|---|---|
| Delivery-order contract | A contract for supplies that does not procure or specify a firm quantity of supplies (other than a minimum or maximum quantity) and that provides for the issuance of orders for the delivery of supplies during the period of the contract | For supplies; no firm quantity procured or specified, with a minimum or maximum quantity excluded from that bar; provides for orders for delivery during the contract period | [@CL-EST-2977] |
| Task-order contract | A contract for services that does not procure or specify a firm quantity of services (other than a minimum or maximum quantity) and that provides for the issuance of orders for the performance of tasks during the period of the contract | For services; no firm quantity procured or specified, with a minimum or maximum quantity excluded from that bar; provides for orders for the performance of tasks during the contract period | [@CL-EST-2978] |
| Definite-quantity contract | A contract that provides for delivery of a definite quantity of specific supplies or services for a fixed period, with deliveries or performance to be scheduled at designated locations upon order | Definite quantity; specific supplies or services; fixed period; scheduling at designated locations upon order | [@CL-EST-2979] |

The definitions of the first two terms are stated for use in this subpart [@CL-EST-2993]. The description of a definite-quantity contract is the first paragraph of its own section [@CL-EST-2979].

A definite-quantity contract may be used when it can be determined in advance that two things hold [@CL-EST-2980]. The first is that a definite quantity of supplies or services will be required during the contract period [@CL-EST-2980]. The second is that the supplies or services are regularly available or will be available after a short lead time [@CL-EST-2980]. The section does not say who makes that determination.

## How it works

A delivery-order contract and a task-order contract carry no firm quantity apart from a minimum or maximum quantity. They differ in subject matter, with supplies and deliveries in the first and services and tasks in the second [@CL-EST-2977; @CL-EST-2978]. Each provides for orders issued during the contract period [@CL-EST-2977; @CL-EST-2978].

A definite-quantity contract fixes the quantity for a fixed period. Deliveries or performance are scheduled at designated locations upon order [@CL-EST-2979]. The general section of the subpart lists definite-quantity contracts as one of three types of indefinite-delivery contract [@CL-CTF-1207].

The permission to use a definite-quantity contract depends on both conditions in the application paragraph [@CL-EST-2980]. See [what the indefinite-delivery subpart covers](est-indefinite-delivery-scope.md) for the scope of the subpart, and [indefinite-delivery and indefinite-quantity contracts](../../contract-finance/funding/ctf-indefinite-delivery-and-indefinite-quantity-contracts.md) for the types. For ordering, see [ordering under indefinite-delivery contracts](est-far-ordering-indefinite-delivery-contracts.md) and [ordering, definite quantity and requirements clauses](est-ordering-definite-quantity-and-requirements-clauses.md). For the terms that the contract-types part defines, such as the award-fee roles and the established price, see [contract-type definitions](est-contract-type-definitions.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An agency expects to buy between 100 and 400 test sets over three years and cannot fix the quantity. It uses a contract for supplies that specifies no firm quantity apart from a minimum of 100 and a maximum of 400 and that provides for orders for delivery during the contract period. That contract meets the definition of a delivery-order contract [@CL-EST-2977].

For a separate requirement, the agency can determine in advance that exactly 250 pumps will be required during the contract period, and the pumps are regularly available. Both conditions in the application paragraph hold, so a definite-quantity contract may be used [@CL-EST-2980]. The contract provides for delivery of the 250 pumps for a fixed period, with deliveries scheduled at the depot upon order [@CL-EST-2979].

## Never confuse

A delivery-order or task-order contract may state a minimum or maximum quantity and still fit its definition, because the definition's bar on a firm quantity sets those quantities aside [@CL-EST-2977; @CL-EST-2978]. A definite-quantity contract is described by its definite quantity, not by the absence of one [@CL-EST-2979]. The definitions in the first section are stated for this subpart and name only delivery-order and task-order contracts [@CL-EST-2993; @CL-EST-2977; @CL-EST-2978]. A definite-quantity contract provides for a definite quantity, and deliveries or performance are scheduled upon order [@CL-EST-2979].
