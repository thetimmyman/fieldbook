## Purpose

Shows when a contracting officer may not require certified cost or pricing data, and what data may be required instead.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-EST-0152] |
| Codified citation | FAR 15.403-1(b) [@CL-EST-0152] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 15.403-2(b) [@CL-EST-3198] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-EST-0152] |
| Applies to | Acquisitions at or below the simplified acquisition threshold, and acquisitions that meet a listed exception [@CL-EST-0151; @CL-EST-0152] |

Certified cost or pricing data shall not be obtained for acquisitions at or below the simplified acquisition threshold [@CL-EST-0151]. The contracting officer shall not require certified cost or pricing data to support any action, including contracts, subcontracts, or modifications, when one of the listed circumstances applies [@CL-EST-0152].

The exceptions include adequate price competition, prices set by law or regulation, a commercial product or commercial service, and a waiver [@CL-EST-0153; @CL-EST-0154; @CL-EST-0155; @CL-EST-0156]. In an acquisition that does not require certified cost or pricing data, the contracting officer carries out the steps in paragraph (a)(1) of FAR 15.403-3 [@CL-EST-0520]. These include requiring data other than certified cost or pricing data from the offeror to the extent necessary to determine a fair and reasonable price [@CL-EST-0521], if the contracting officer determines that adequate data from sources other than the offeror are not available [@CL-EST-0522].

FAR 2.101 defines data other than certified cost or pricing data as pricing data, cost data, and judgmental information necessary for the contracting officer to determine a fair and reasonable price or to determine cost realism [@CL-EST-9291]. The first sentence of the RFO model text definition of data other than certified cost or pricing data in FAR 2.101 reads the same as the codified first sentence, and the RFO text goes on to say that such data may include the identical types of data as certified cost or pricing data but without the certification, that the data may also include, for example, sales data and any information reasonably required to explain the offeror's estimating process, and that it may include, but is not limited to, (1) the judgmental factors applied and the mathematical or other methods used in the estimate, including those used in projecting from known data, and (2) the nature and amount of any contingencies included in the proposed price [@CL-EST-9353; @CL-EST-9395; @CL-EST-9396; @CL-EST-9291]. Such data may include the identical types of data as certified cost or pricing data, consistent with Table 15-2 of FAR 15.408, but without the certification, and the definition goes on to say that the data may also include, for example, sales data and any information reasonably required to explain the offeror's estimating process, and lists (1) the judgmental factors applied and the mathematical or other methods used in the estimate, including those used in projecting from known data, and (2) the nature and amount of any contingencies included in the proposed price [@CL-EST-9292; @CL-EST-9400; @CL-EST-9401].

## How it works

Adequate price competition generally requires that two or more responsible offerors, competing independently, submit priced offers that satisfy the Government's expressed requirement, that award be made to the best-value offeror with price a substantial factor in source selection, and that there be no finding that the otherwise successful offeror's price is unreasonable; however, for agencies other than DoD, NASA, and the Coast Guard, a price may also be based on adequate price competition under specific conditions involving a reasonable expectation of competition or price analysis, even if only one offer is received [@CL-EST-0157; @CL-EST-0247]. The head of the contracting activity may, without power of delegation, waive the requirement in exceptional cases [@CL-EST-0158]. Where data other than certified cost or pricing data are required, the data the offeror submits must at a minimum include appropriate data on the prices at which the same item or similar items have previously been sold, unless the exception for adequate price competition or for prices set by law or regulation applies [@CL-EST-0523; @CL-EST-0153; @CL-EST-0154]. When adequate price competition exists, generally no additional data are necessary to determine the reasonableness of price [@CL-EST-0524]. If there are unusual circumstances where additional data are necessary, the contracting officer shall, to the maximum extent practicable, obtain the additional data from sources other than the offeror [@CL-EST-0525]. An offeror who does not comply with a requirement to submit data in accordance with paragraph (a)(1) of FAR 15.403-3 is ineligible for award unless the head of the contracting activity determines that award to that offeror is in the best interest of the Government [@CL-EST-0526]. That determination considers the effort made to obtain the data, the need for the item or service, and increased cost or significant harm to the Government if award is not made [@CL-EST-0527].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 15 text in place of the codified text under Class Deviation 2026-O0048, whose Revision 1 took effect immediately and supersedes the March 5, 2026 version [@CL-EST-3176] [@CL-EST-3177]. RFO FAR 15.403-2(a), headed At or below the simplified acquisition threshold, says certified cost or pricing data must not be obtained for acquisitions at or below the simplified acquisition threshold, which is the bar the codified paragraph states with shall not [@CL-EST-3197; @CL-EST-0151]. RFO FAR 15.403-2(b) says contracting officers must not require certified cost or pricing data to support any contracts, subcontracts, or modifications in the circumstances it lists [@CL-EST-3198; @CL-EST-0152]. Its lead-in runs straight from those supported actions to the circumstances, where the codified lead-in adds a parenthetical that the contracting officer may require data other than certified cost or pricing data to support a determination of a fair and reasonable price or cost realism [@CL-EST-3198; @CL-EST-0152]. The RFO text puts that subject in 15.403-1(b)(2), which says contracting officers may require data other than certified cost or pricing data to determine the cost realism of competing offers or to evaluate competing approaches [@CL-EST-3255]. The list includes adequate price competition and prices set by law or regulation [@CL-EST-3199; @CL-EST-0153; @CL-EST-0154], and a commercial product or commercial service and a waiver [@CL-EST-3200; @CL-EST-0155; @CL-EST-0156]. A price is based on adequate price competition when two or more responsible offerors, competing independently, submit priced offers that satisfy the Government's expressed requirement [@CL-EST-3201; @CL-EST-0157], award will be made to the offeror whose proposal represents the best value where price is a substantial factor in source selection, and there is no finding that the price of the otherwise successful offeror is unreasonable [@CL-EST-3202; @CL-EST-0247]. RFO FAR 15.403-2(c)(4)(i) says that in exceptional cases the head of the contracting activity may, without power of delegation, waive the requirement for submission of certified cost or pricing data if the price can be determined to be fair and reasonable without submission of certified cost or pricing data [@CL-EST-3203]; the codified sentence states the power in exceptional cases without that condition [@CL-EST-0158]. The rule on data other than certified cost or pricing data moved to RFO FAR 15.403-1, whose paragraph (a) says that for acquisitions that do not require certified cost or pricing data, contracting officers must generally use an order of preference to determine the type of data to require [@CL-EST-3204], where the codified paragraph lists steps the contracting officer shall carry out [@CL-EST-0520]. The first item of that order of preference is no additional data from the offeror, when adequate price competition exists [@CL-EST-3205], where the codified text says generally no additional data are necessary [@CL-EST-0524]. The second item is data related to prices, for example established catalog or market prices and sales to non-governmental and governmental entities [@CL-EST-3256], relying first on data available within the Government, then on data obtained from sources other than the offeror, and then, if necessary, on data obtained from the offeror [@CL-EST-3209]. RFO FAR 15.403-1(b)(1) says contracting officers must require submission of data other than certified cost or pricing data to the extent necessary to determine a fair and reasonable price [@CL-EST-3206; @CL-EST-0521]. That sentence states no condition that the contracting officer first determine that adequate data from sources other than the offeror are not available, which the codified paragraph states [@CL-EST-3206; @CL-EST-0522]. The RFO order of preference reaches data from the offeror last and only if necessary [@CL-EST-3209], and for commercial products and commercial services, RFO FAR 15.403-1(f) lists requiring the offeror to submit data other than certified cost or pricing data when the contracting officer is unable to determine a price fair and reasonable after obtaining data from sources other than the offeror [@CL-EST-3261; @CL-EST-3562]. The same RFO sentence says the data must include, at a minimum, appropriate data on the prices at which the same item or similar items have previously been sold, adequate for determining the reasonableness of the price [@CL-EST-3207; @CL-EST-0523]. The next RFO sentence says this data is not required when price reasonableness is established through adequate price competition or prices set by law or regulation, unless paragraph (e) of that subsection applies [@CL-EST-3208], where the codified paragraph says unless an exception under 15.403-1(b)(1) or (2) applies [@CL-EST-0523]. Paragraph (e) says that if adequate price competition exists, but there are unusual circumstances in which additional data are necessary to determine the reasonableness of price, contracting officers must obtain the additional data from sources other than the offeror, to the maximum extent practicable [@CL-EST-3210; @CL-EST-0525]. RFO FAR 15.403-1(d) says an offeror who does not comply with a requirement to submit data for a contract or subcontract in accordance with paragraph (a)(1) of that subsection is ineligible for award unless the head of the contracting activity determines that it is in the best interest of the Government [@CL-EST-3211; @CL-EST-0526]; paragraph (a)(1) of RFO FAR 15.403-1 is the item on no additional data from the offeror when adequate price competition exists [@CL-EST-3205]. That determination considers the effort made to obtain the data, the need for the item or service, and increased cost or significant harm to the Government if award is not made [@CL-EST-3212; @CL-EST-0527].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A company submits a quote priced at 80,000, below the simplified acquisition threshold, so no certified cost or pricing data is obtained. On a separate 2 million bid, two responsible offerors compete independently and the contracting officer finds adequate price competition, so certified data is not required and, absent unusual circumstances, no additional data is required from the offerors to support the price.

## Never confuse

Data other than certified cost or pricing data are what the contracting officer must require, to the extent necessary to determine a fair and reasonable price, when certified cost or pricing data are not required and adequate data from sources other than the offeror are not available [@CL-EST-0520; @CL-EST-0521; @CL-EST-0522]. A citation to FAR 15.403-3 means the rule on requiring data other than certified cost or pricing data in the codified text [@CL-EST-0520], but in the RFO text 15.403-3 is the section on certified cost or pricing data and its threshold [@CL-EST-3213], and the rule on data other than certified cost or pricing data is at 15.403-1 [@CL-EST-3204].
