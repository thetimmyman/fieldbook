## Purpose

Gives the meanings that the Standard on allocation of home office expenses to segments assigns to allocate, home office, segment and tangible capital asset. The Standard's requirements are on the [home office allocation card](../../compliance/cas-allowability/cmp-cas-403-home-office-allocation.md).

## The rule

Allocate means to assign an item of cost, or a group of items of cost, to one or more cost objectives, and the term includes both direct assignments of cost and the reassignment of a share from an indirect cost pool [@CL-EST-2863]. A home office means an office responsible for directing or managing two or more, but not necessarily all, segments of an organization [@CL-EST-2864]. A segment means one of two or more divisions, product departments, plants or other subdivisions of an organization reporting directly to a home office, usually identified with responsibility for profit and/or producing a product or service [@CL-EST-2867].

The term segment includes Government-owned contractor-operated (GOCO) facilities, and joint ventures and subsidiaries, domestic and foreign, in which the organization has a majority ownership [@CL-EST-2868]. It also includes joint ventures and subsidiaries, domestic and foreign, in which the organization has less than a majority of ownership but over which it exercises control [@CL-EST-2869]. A tangible capital asset means an asset that has physical substance, more than minimal value, and is expected to be held by an enterprise for continued use or possession beyond the current accounting period for the services it yields [@CL-EST-2870].

Other terms defined elsewhere in part 99 have the meanings given in those definitions unless paragraph (b) of the section requires otherwise [@CL-EST-2958]. Paragraph (b) applies no modification to those terms for this Standard [@CL-EST-2959].

## How it works

A home office typically establishes policy for the segments and provides guidance to them in their operations, usually performs management, supervisory or administrative functions, and may also perform service functions in support of the segments [@CL-EST-2865]. An organization with intermediate levels, such as groups, may have several home offices reporting to a common home office, and an intermediate organization may be both a segment and a home office [@CL-EST-2866]. The segment definition covers divisions, product departments, plants and other subdivisions reporting directly to a home office [@CL-EST-2867], and includes the listed facilities, joint ventures and subsidiaries [@CL-EST-2868; @CL-EST-2869].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A group runs two divisions that report directly to a policy office, and the policy office sets accounting practice for both. The policy office is responsible for directing or managing two segments, so it is a home office [@CL-EST-2864]. Each division is a subdivision reporting directly to it, usually identified with responsibility for producing a product, so each is a segment [@CL-EST-2867]. The policy office spends 900,000 on management and guidance. Moving a 450,000 share of that pooled cost to each division is an allocation, because the term includes reassigning a share from an indirect cost pool [@CL-EST-2863]. A joint venture in which the group owns less than a majority but exercises control is also a segment [@CL-EST-2869]. Whether this split is acceptable is a question for the Standard's requirements on the main card.

## Never confuse

Under CAS 403, a home office directs or manages two or more segments of an organization [@CL-EST-2864], unlike a segment, which is a division, plant, or other subdivision reporting directly to a home office [@CL-EST-2867]—although one intermediate organization may be both a segment and a home office [@CL-EST-2866]—and allocating covers both direct assignment and indirect pool reassignment [@CL-EST-2863].
