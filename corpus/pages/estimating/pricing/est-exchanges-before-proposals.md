## Purpose

Shows which techniques the Government may use to talk with industry before proposals arrive, and what limits apply to the information shared.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-EST-1358] |
| Codified citation | FAR 15.201 [@CL-EST-1358] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-EST-1358] |
| Applies to | Exchanges among interested parties from the earliest identification of a requirement through receipt of proposals [@CL-EST-1358] |

Exchanges of information among all interested parties, from the earliest identification of a requirement through receipt of proposals, are encouraged, and any exchange must be consistent with procurement integrity requirements [@CL-EST-1358].

Some listed techniques to promote early exchanges include industry or small business conferences, public hearings and market research [@CL-EST-1359]. Another listed technique is one-on-one meetings with potential offerors, and any such meeting substantially involved with potential contract terms and conditions should include the contracting officer [@CL-EST-1359; @CL-EST-1360]. Five further listed techniques are presolicitation notices, draft RFPs, RFIs, presolicitation or preproposal conferences, and site visits [@CL-EST-1359; @CL-EST-1361]. The advisory multi-step presolicitation notice is on [Advisory multi-step process and source selection provisions and clauses](est-advisory-multi-step-and-source-selection-provisions.md).

## How it works

General information about agency mission needs and future requirements may be disclosed at any time, and after release of the solicitation the contracting officer must be the focal point of any exchange with potential offerors [@CL-EST-1362]. When specific information about a proposed acquisition that would be necessary for the preparation of proposals is disclosed to one or more potential offerors, it must be made available to the public as soon as practicable, but no later than the next general release of information [@CL-EST-1363]. Information provided to a potential offeror in response to its request must not be disclosed if doing so would reveal the potential offeror's confidential business strategy and the information is protected under the cross-referenced provisions [@CL-EST-1480].

This section states no separate rule that exchanges must stop at a given point. It speaks of exchanges through receipt of proposals [@CL-EST-1358].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

Before a solicitation is released, the contracting officer holds a one-on-one meeting with a potential offeror and mentions the planned delivery schedule, so that specific information must be made public as soon as practicable, and no later than the next general release of information. After release, another potential offeror asks a question whose answer is specific information needed to prepare proposals. The contracting officer answers and also makes that information available to the public no later than the next general release. All later questions go through the contracting officer.

## Never confuse

Exchanges before receipt of proposals are not the clarifications, communications and discussions that follow receipt. Those are on [Exchanges with offerors after receipt of proposals](est-exchanges-after-proposals-and-competitive-range.md) and have their own limits. This section's exchanges run through receipt of proposals [@CL-EST-1358]. What a request for proposals must describe is on [Requests for proposals and amending the solicitation](est-rfp-and-solicitation-amendments.md).
