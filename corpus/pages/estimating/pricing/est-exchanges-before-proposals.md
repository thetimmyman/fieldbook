## Purpose

Shows which techniques the Government may use to talk with industry before proposals arrive, and what limits apply to the information shared.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-EST-1358] |
| Codified citation | FAR 15.201 [@CL-EST-1358]; FAR 15.200 [@CL-EST-9189] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 15.101 [@CL-EST-3271; @CL-EST-3272; @CL-EST-3273; @CL-EST-3274; @CL-EST-3275; @CL-EST-3276]; RFO FAR 15.201(c) [@CL-EST-3265] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-EST-1358] |
| Applies to | Exchanges among interested parties from the earliest identification of a requirement through receipt of proposals [@CL-EST-1358] |

Exchanges of information among all interested parties, from the earliest identification of a requirement through receipt of proposals, are encouraged, and any exchange must be consistent with procurement integrity requirements [@CL-EST-1358].

Some listed techniques to promote early exchanges include industry or small business conferences, public hearings and market research [@CL-EST-1359]. Another listed technique is one-on-one meetings with potential offerors, and any such meeting substantially involved with potential contract terms and conditions should include the contracting officer [@CL-EST-1359; @CL-EST-1360]. Five further listed techniques are presolicitation notices, draft RFPs, RFIs, presolicitation or preproposal conferences, and site visits [@CL-EST-1359; @CL-EST-1361]. The advisory multi-step presolicitation notice is on [Advisory multi-step process and source selection provisions and clauses](est-advisory-multi-step-and-source-selection-provisions.md).

Subpart 15.2 prescribes policies and procedures for exchanging information with industry prior to receipt of proposals, for preparing and issuing requests for proposals (RFPs) and requests for information (RFIs), and for receiving proposals and information [@CL-EST-9189].

## How it works

General information about agency mission needs and future requirements may be disclosed at any time, and after release of the solicitation the contracting officer must be the focal point of any exchange with potential offerors [@CL-EST-1362]. When specific information about a proposed acquisition that would be necessary for the preparation of proposals is disclosed to one or more potential offerors, it must be made available to the public as soon as practicable, but no later than the next general release of information [@CL-EST-1363]. Information provided to a potential offeror in response to its request must not be disclosed if doing so would reveal the potential offeror's confidential business strategy, and is protected under 3.104 or subpart 24.2 [@CL-EST-1480].

Exchanges of information among all interested parties are encouraged from the earliest identification of a requirement through receipt of proposals [@CL-EST-1358].

Under Class Deviation 2026-O0048 Revision 1, contracting officers must use the revised FAR part 15, Contracting by Negotiation, in lieu of the text codified at 48 CFR chapter 1 [@CL-EST-3176], and the attached revised DFARS part 215 in lieu of the text codified at 48 CFR chapter 2 [@CL-EST-9667]; effective immediately, it revises and supersedes Class Deviation 2026O0048, issued on March 5, 2026 [@CL-EST-3177]. RFO FAR 15.101(a) says exchanges of information among all interested parties are encouraged and can occur at any time between the identification of a requirement through the receipt of proposals [@CL-EST-3271], where the codified text encourages exchanges from the earliest identification of a requirement through receipt of proposals [@CL-EST-1358]. RFO FAR 15.101(a) also says any exchange of information must be consistent with procurement integrity requirements in part 3 and protected in accordance with part 24 [@CL-EST-3272], where the codified sentence refers to procurement integrity requirements and to 3.104 [@CL-EST-1358]. RFO FAR 15.101(b) encourages agencies to release draft RFPs and conduct conferences with industry before issuing competitive RFPs [@CL-EST-3273], and RFO FAR 15.101(c) encourages agencies to release RFIs when the Government does not currently intend to award a contract but wants price, delivery, market, or capabilities information for planning purposes [@CL-EST-3274], while the codified text sets out a list of techniques that includes conferences, one-on-one meetings, draft RFPs and RFIs [@CL-EST-1359; @CL-EST-1360; @CL-EST-1361]. The one-on-one meeting item and its sentence on including the contracting officer are in the codified list [@CL-EST-1360], while RFO FAR 15.101(b) and (c) name conferences with industry, draft RFPs and RFIs [@CL-EST-3273; @CL-EST-3274]. RFO FAR 15.101(d) says general information on an agency's current or anticipated needs and requirements may be disclosed at any time [@CL-EST-3275], as the codified text says of general information about agency mission needs and future requirements [@CL-EST-1362]. RFO FAR 15.101(d) also says information necessary for the preparation of proposals that is disclosed to one or more potential offerors should be made available to the public as soon as practicable, but no later than the next general release of information, to avoid creating an unfair competitive advantage [@CL-EST-3276] [@CL-EST-3588], where the codified text says it must be made available [@CL-EST-1363]. RFO FAR 15.201(c) says that after release of a RFP the contracting officer must serve as the focal point for inquiries from actual or prospective offerors [@CL-EST-3265], where the codified text says that after release of the solicitation the contracting officer must be the focal point of any exchange with potential offerors [@CL-EST-1362]. RFO FAR 15.101(d) addresses disclosure of general information [@CL-EST-3275] and of information necessary for the preparation of proposals [@CL-EST-3276], while the codified paragraph also bars disclosure of information provided to a potential offeror in response to its request if doing so would reveal the potential offeror's confidential business strategy, and is protected under 3.104 or subpart 24.2 [@CL-EST-1480]. RFO FAR 15.101(c)(4) says information received in response to an RFI must be safeguarded adequately from unauthorized disclosure [@CL-EST-3586].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

Before a solicitation is released, the contracting officer holds a one-on-one meeting with a potential offeror and mentions the planned delivery schedule, which is specific information about the proposed acquisition that is necessary for preparing proposals, so that information must be made public as soon as practicable, and no later than the next general release of information (the RFO text says should rather than must [@CL-EST-3276]). After release, another potential offeror asks a question whose answer is specific information needed to prepare proposals. The contracting officer answers and also makes that information available to the public no later than the next general release. All later questions go through the contracting officer.

## Never confuse

Exchanges under FAR 15.201 run from requirement identification only up to receipt of proposals [@CL-EST-1358; @CL-EST-3271; @CL-EST-9189], unlike clarifications, communications and competitive-range discussions, which take place after proposals arrive on [Exchanges with offerors after receipt of proposals](est-exchanges-after-proposals-and-competitive-range.md) [@CL-EST-1383].
