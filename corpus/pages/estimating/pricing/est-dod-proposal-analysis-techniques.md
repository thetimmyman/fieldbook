## Purpose

Shows what the DoD supplement tells the contracting officer to do when analysing a proposal, and which data and offices support the analysis.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-EST-1530] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 215.404-1 [@CL-EST-1530] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-EST-1530] |
| Applies to | DoD proposal analysis, with the market-price preference for cases without adequate price competition [@CL-EST-1530] |

In the absence of adequate price competition in response to the solicitation, pricing based on market prices is the preferred method to establish a fair and reasonable price [@CL-EST-1530]. For spare parts or support equipment, the section calls for an analysis of listed line items [@CL-EST-1529]. The first group is the line items where the proposed price exceeds by 25 percent or more the lowest price the Government has paid within the most recent 12-month period based on reasonably available data [@CL-EST-1529]. This card does not set out the other groups in that list. If the contracting officer determines that the pricing information submitted is not sufficient to determine the reasonableness of price, the contracting officer shall request other relevant information, to include cost data [@CL-EST-1531], but no cost data may be required where there are sufficient non-Government sales of the same item to establish reasonableness of price [@CL-EST-1532].

## How it works

Two sources of support are the DoD cadre of experts and the Supplier Performance Risk System. The contracting officer may consult the DoD cadre of experts, who give advice on commercial product or commercial service determinations and on price reasonableness [@CL-EST-1533]. For a service or end product identified by a material identifier available under the cited guidance, the contracting officer shall consider the Supplier Performance Risk System price risk assessments [@CL-EST-1534]. The section also refers to procedures in the DoD guidance and information documents, which this card does not cover.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A spare part is proposed at 130. The lowest price the Government paid in the last twelve months, from the data reasonably available, was 100. The proposed price is 30 percent above it, so the line item is analysed. The offeror then shows non-Government sales of the same item that are enough to establish that the price is reasonable. The answer: the contracting officer cannot also require cost data for that item.

## Never confuse

The preference for market prices applies when adequate price competition is absent; it is not a ban on asking for cost data [@CL-EST-1530; @CL-EST-1531]. Cost data are requested when other pricing information is not enough, and they may not be required where sufficient non-Government sales exist [@CL-EST-1531; @CL-EST-1532]. The FAR choice among price analysis, cost analysis and cost realism is on [Proposal analysis techniques](est-proposal-analysis.md); this card is the DoD layer. The DoD provisions that ask the offeror for data are on [DFARS solicitation provisions and contract clauses for negotiated contracts](est-dfars-solicitation-provisions-negotiated.md).
