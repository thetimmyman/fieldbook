## Purpose

Shows what the DoD supplement tells the contracting officer to do when analysing a proposal, and which data and offices support the analysis.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-EST-1530] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 215.404-1 [@CL-EST-1530] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-EST-1530] |
| Applies to | DoD proposal analysis, with the market-price preference for cases without adequate price competition [@CL-EST-1530] |

In the absence of adequate price competition in response to the solicitation, pricing based on market prices is the preferred method to establish a fair and reasonable price [@CL-EST-1530]. For spare parts or support equipment, an analysis is to be performed of the line items in each of the groups listed [@CL-EST-1529]. The first group is the line items where the proposed price exceeds by 25 percent or more the lowest price the Government has paid within the most recent 12-month period based on reasonably available data [@CL-EST-1529]. The next groups are line items where a comparison of the item description and the proposal price indicates a potential for overpricing, and significant high-dollar-value items, with an analysis of a random sample of items if there are no obvious high-dollar-value items [@CL-EST-9661]. The last group is a random sample of the remaining low-dollar value items [@CL-EST-9662]. If the contracting officer determines that the pricing information submitted is not sufficient to determine the reasonableness of price, the contracting officer shall request other relevant information, to include cost data [@CL-EST-1531], but no cost data may be required where there are sufficient non-Government sales of the same item to establish reasonableness of price [@CL-EST-1532].

## How it works

The contracting officer may consult the DoD cadre of experts, who are available to provide expert advice to the acquisition workforce in assisting with commercial product or commercial service determinations and price reasonableness determinations [@CL-EST-1533]. When procuring a service or an end product identified by a material identifier that is available as described at PGI 204.7603, the contracting officer shall consider the Supplier Performance Risk System price risk assessments [@CL-EST-1534] in determining if a proposed price is consistent with historical prices paid for an item or otherwise creates a risk to the Government [@CL-EST-9663].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A spare part is proposed at 130. The lowest price the Government paid in the last twelve months, from the data reasonably available, was 100. The proposed price is 30 percent above it, so the line item is analysed. The offeror then shows non-Government sales of the same item that are enough to establish that the price is reasonable. The answer: the contracting officer cannot also require cost data for that item.

## Never confuse

The preference for market prices applies in the absence of adequate price competition in response to the solicitation [@CL-EST-1530]. If the contracting officer determines that the pricing information submitted is not sufficient to determine the reasonableness of price, the contracting officer shall request other relevant information, to include cost data [@CL-EST-1531], but no cost data may be required in any case in which there are sufficient non-Government sales of the same item to establish reasonableness of price [@CL-EST-1532]. The FAR choice among price analysis, cost analysis and cost realism is on [Proposal analysis techniques](est-proposal-analysis.md); this card is the DoD layer. The DoD provisions that ask the offeror for data are on [DFARS solicitation provisions and contract clauses for negotiated contracts](est-dfars-solicitation-provisions-negotiated.md).
