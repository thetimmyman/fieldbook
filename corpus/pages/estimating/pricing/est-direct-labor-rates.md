## Purpose

Shows how direct labor rates are proposed as individual, average, or pre-established rates, and how uncompensated overtime must be handled.

Direct labor cost estimates may be proposed using expected individual rates or average labor rates, developed either per proposal or pre-established across multiple proposals [@CL-EST-0708]. Use of individual rates is appropriate when specific, known employees with unique skills are involved [@CL-EST-0711].

Uncompensated overtime is hours worked without extra pay beyond the standard workweek by FLSA-exempt direct charge employees, and it must be explicitly identified on labor-hour service proposals above the simplified acquisition threshold [@CL-EST-0709]. A contractor proposing it is required to calculate adjusted hourly rates, keep estimating consistent with accounting accumulation, provide its uncompensated overtime policy, and support the Contracting Officer's risk assessment [@CL-EST-0710].

## How it works

The individual-rate method is described as precise but often impractical [@CL-EST-0712]. Most proposals use average rates, and the contractor must stay consistent in the practice it uses to propose labor rates [@CL-EST-0875]. An average rate may be a single plant-wide average or a separate average for a function, grade, class of labor, cost center, department, or production process [@CL-EST-0852]. If premium pay for overtime or extra shifts is charged to an overhead, it stays out of the direct labor rate calculation to avoid distortion [@CL-EST-0853]. A contractor may also set rates in advance for use in all proposals over a certain period, similar to a Forward Pricing Rate Agreement [@CL-EST-0713]. The auditor does not automatically accept those rates and examines each proposal to confirm they are still valid and appropriate for the specific work contemplated [@CL-EST-0714]. If a contractor proposes uncompensated overtime without disclosing the specific hours and adjusted rates, or does not submit its policy with the proposal, the auditor should immediately notify the Contracting Officer of the noncompliance [@CL-EST-0854].

## Worked example

A proposal uses a plant-wide average rate of 50 per hour. A second proposal needs only senior specialists, so the standard average rate is not used for that proposal.

## Never confuse

An average rate covers a group such as a function or department, while an individual rate applies to specific, known employees [@CL-EST-0852; @CL-EST-0711]. Pre-established rates are set in advance and are not automatically accepted by the auditor [@CL-EST-0713; @CL-EST-0714]. Uncompensated overtime is hours worked without extra pay, which is a different matter from premium pay charged to an overhead [@CL-EST-0709; @CL-EST-0853].

## Company calibration

Who owns proposal pricing and the estimating system description, and that proposed rates follow it. [@Q-036]
