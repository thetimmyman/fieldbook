## Purpose

States what the indefinite-delivery subpart covers, the preference it creates, what it does not limit or displace, the architect-engineer carve-out, and where the small business set-aside procedures are.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-EST-0961] |
| Codified citation | FAR 16.500 [@CL-EST-0961] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-EST-0961] |
| Applies to | Awards of indefinite-delivery contracts, subject to the limits stated in the section [@CL-EST-0961; @CL-EST-0962; @CL-EST-0963; @CL-EST-0965] |

The subpart prescribes policies and procedures for making awards of indefinite-delivery contracts. It establishes a preference for making multiple awards of indefinite-quantity contracts [@CL-EST-0961].

What the subpart does not do:

- It does not limit the use of other than competitive procedures authorized by part 6 [@CL-EST-0962].
- Nothing in it restricts the authority of the General Services Administration to enter into schedule, multiple award, or task or delivery order contracts under any other provision of law [@CL-EST-0963]. General Services Administration regulations and the coverage for the Federal Supply Schedule program in subpart 8.4 and part 38 take precedence over the subpart [@CL-EST-0964].
- The statutory multiple award preference that the subpart implements does not apply to architect-engineer contracts subject to the procedures in subpart 36.6 [@CL-EST-0965].

Architect-engineer exception to the carve-out: agencies are not precluded from making multiple awards for architect-engineer services using the subpart's procedures, provided the selection of contractors and the placement of orders are consistent with subpart 36.6 [@CL-EST-0966].

The section refers to subpart 19.5 for procedures to set aside part or parts of multiple-award contracts for small businesses, to reserve one or more awards for small business on multiple-award contracts, and to set aside orders for small businesses under multiple-award contracts [@CL-EST-0967].


## How it works

The subpart supplies the procedures for awarding indefinite-delivery contracts and states a preference for multiple awards of indefinite-quantity contracts [@CL-EST-0961]. Three limits bound its reach.

First, use of other than competitive procedures authorized by part 6 is not limited by the subpart [@CL-EST-0962]. Second, the General Services Administration keeps its authority under any other provision of law, and its regulations and the Federal Supply Schedule coverage take precedence over the subpart [@CL-EST-0963; @CL-EST-0964]. Third, the statutory multiple award preference does not apply to architect-engineer contracts subject to subpart 36.6 [@CL-EST-0965].

Architect-engineer work is not closed to multiple awards. Agencies may make them using the subpart's procedures if the selection of contractors and the placement of orders are consistent with subpart 36.6 [@CL-EST-0966]. For the types of indefinite-delivery contract and the multiple-award preference, see [indefinite-delivery and indefinite-quantity contracts](../../contract-finance/funding/ctf-indefinite-delivery-and-indefinite-quantity-contracts.md). For ordering, see [ordering under indefinite-delivery contracts](est-far-ordering-indefinite-delivery-contracts.md). For the defense supplement additions, see [DoD indefinite-delivery scope and orders](../../market/programs/mkt-dfars-indefinite-delivery-scope-and-orders.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An agency plans three multiple-award indefinite-quantity contracts for a service and expects 20 orders in the first year. The subpart establishes a preference for making multiple awards of indefinite-quantity contracts, which supports the plan [@CL-EST-0961]. The agency also reviews whether part of the multiple-award contracts could be set aside for small businesses, and it finds the procedures in subpart 19.5 [@CL-EST-0967]. For architect-engineer services, the statutory multiple award preference does not apply if the contracts are subject to the procedures in subpart 36.6 [@CL-EST-0965]. The agency may still make multiple awards for those services using the subpart's procedures if contractor selection and order placement are consistent with subpart 36.6 [@CL-EST-0966]. Where the General Services Administration enters into a schedule contract under its own authority, nothing in the subpart restricts that authority, and the General Services Administration regulations and Federal Supply Schedule coverage take precedence [@CL-EST-0963; @CL-EST-0964].

## Never confuse

The subpart does not limit the use of other than competitive procedures authorized by part 6 [@CL-EST-0962], and nothing in it restricts the authority of the General Services Administration to enter into schedule, multiple award, or task or delivery order contracts under any other provision of law [@CL-EST-0963]. The statutory multiple award preference does not apply to architect-engineer contracts subject to subpart 36.6 [@CL-EST-0965], but agencies are not precluded from making multiple awards for architect-engineer services using the subpart's procedures, provided the selection of contractors and the placement of orders are consistent with subpart 36.6 [@CL-EST-0966].
