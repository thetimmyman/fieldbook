## Purpose

Explains the four profit factors of the DoD weighted guidelines method, how a normal value and a designated range work, and how the performance risk factor is weighted and applied.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-EST-0773] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 215.404-71-1 [@CL-EST-0773] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-EST-0773] |
| Applies to | Profit objectives built from a value multiplied by a base [@CL-EST-0774] |

The method focuses on four profit factors: performance risk, contract type risk, facilities capital employed and cost efficiency [@CL-EST-0773]. The contracting officer assigns a value to each factor, and the value multiplied by the base gives that factor's profit objective [@CL-EST-0774]. Except for the cost efficiency special factor, each factor has a normal value and a designated range [@CL-EST-0774].

In the price negotiation documentation the contracting officer need not explain assignment of the normal value, but should address the conditions that justify a value other than normal [@CL-EST-0775].

## How it works

Performance risk has a technical part, covering technical uncertainties of performance, and a management/cost control part, covering the management effort needed to meet contract requirements and to reduce and control costs [@CL-EST-0776]. The two elements carry weights that total 100 percent [@CL-EST-0777]. Values come from a table in which the standard normal value is 5 percent with a designated range of 3 to 7 percent and the technology incentive normal value is 9 percent with a range of 7 to 11 percent [@CL-EST-0778]. For the technical factor only, contracting officers may use the technology incentive range for acquisitions that include development, production, or application of innovative new technologies [@CL-EST-0866], but the range does not apply to efforts restricted to studies, analyses, or demonstrations that have a technical report as their primary deliverable [@CL-EST-9432]. The base is the Block 20 amount, which is total contract costs excluding facilities capital cost of money [@CL-EST-0779].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A technical weight of 60 percent at a value of 5.0 and a management/cost control weight of 40 percent at a value of 4.0 give a composite of 4.6 percent. On a Block 20 base of 2,000,000 the performance risk profit objective is 92,000.

## Never confuse

Performance risk is only one of four factors: it covers technical uncertainties and management effort, while the cost risk accepted under the contract type is a separate factor [@CL-EST-0773; @CL-EST-0776]. The cost efficiency special factor has no normal value at all, while each other factor has a normal value and a designated range [@CL-EST-0774]. The contracting officer need not explain assignment of the normal value, but should address the conditions that justify any other value [@CL-EST-0775]. These factor results feed the wider profit analysis; see [Profit analysis](est-profit-analysis.md).
