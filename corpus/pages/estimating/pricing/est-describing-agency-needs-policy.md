## Purpose

Shows the policy rules on how an agency describes what it needs: competition, commercial products, performance terms, metric use, sustainability, accessibility, Internet Protocol and telework.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-EST-0990] |
| Codified citation | FAR 11.000 [@CL-EST-2138] and FAR 11.002 [@CL-EST-0990] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-EST-0990] |
| Applies to | Agencies, the acquisition officials who state requirements, requiring activities, and contracting officers, as each paragraph below says [@CL-EST-0990; @CL-EST-0992; @CL-EST-1002; @CL-EST-1004] |

In fulfilling the requirements of the statutes that paragraph (a) lists, agencies shall specify needs using market research in a manner designed to promote full and open competition, or maximum practicable competition when using simplified acquisition procedures, with due regard to the nature of the supplies or services to be acquired [@CL-EST-0990; @CL-EST-1123]. Agencies shall only include restrictive provisions or conditions to the extent necessary to satisfy the needs of the agency or as authorized by law [@CL-EST-0991].

To the maximum extent practicable, agencies shall ensure that acquisition officials state requirements in terms of functions to be performed, performance required, or essential physical characteristics [@CL-EST-0992]. To the same extent, agencies shall ensure that acquisition officials do four more things. They define requirements in terms that enable and encourage offerors to supply commercial products or commercial services or, to the extent that suitable commercial products are not available, nondevelopmental items [@CL-EST-0993]. They give offerors of commercial products, commercial services and nondevelopmental items an opportunity to compete in any acquisition to fill such requirements [@CL-EST-0994]. They require prime contractors and subcontractors at all tiers under the agency contracts to incorporate commercial products, commercial services or nondevelopmental items as components of items supplied to the agency [@CL-EST-0995]. They modify requirements in appropriate cases so that commercial products or commercial services or, to the extent that suitable commercial products are not available, nondevelopmental items can meet them [@CL-EST-0996].

The Metric Conversion Act, as the paragraph states, designates the metric system of measurement as the preferred system of weights and measures for United States trade and commerce [@CL-EST-1124], and it requires each agency to use the metric system of measurement in its acquisitions, except to the extent that such use is impracticable or is likely to cause significant inefficiencies or loss of markets to United States firms [@CL-EST-1001]. Requiring activities are responsible for establishing guidance implementing this policy in formulating their requirements [@CL-EST-1002].

To the extent practicable and consistent with subpart 9.5, potential offerors should be given an opportunity to comment on agency requirements or to recommend application and tailoring of requirements documents and alternative approaches [@CL-EST-0997]. Requiring agencies should apply specifications, standards and related documents initially for guidance only, and make final decisions on their application and tailoring as a product of the design and development process [@CL-EST-0998]. Requiring agencies should not dictate detailed design solutions prematurely [@CL-EST-0999]. Some or all of the performance levels or performance specifications in a solicitation may be identified as targets rather than as fixed or minimum requirements [@CL-EST-1000].

Agencies shall procure sustainable products and services, as defined in 2.101, in accordance with subpart 23.1 [@CL-EST-1038]. Unless it is not practicable or an exception or exemption applies, agencies shall incorporate the use of sustainable products and services when developing, reviewing, or revising Federal and military specifications, product descriptions (including commercial item descriptions) and standards, when describing Government requirements for products and services, and when developing source-selection factors [@CL-EST-1039; @CL-EST-1108]. The Green Procurement Compilation (GPC) provides a comprehensive list of sustainable products and services and other related sustainable acquisition guidance, and the paragraph gives the web address where it is available [@CL-EST-1122; @CL-EST-1125]. Agencies should consult the GPC when determining which purchasing programs apply to a specific product or service [@CL-EST-1109]. They should also incorporate into agency requirements any required standards, specifications or ecolabels that the GPC identifies for that product or service [@CL-EST-1109].

In accordance with section 508 of the Rehabilitation Act of 1973, the contracting officer shall obtain the requirement documents from the requiring activity [@CL-EST-1004]. Those documents must identify the needs of current and future users with disabilities, to determine how those users will perform the functions that the information and communication technology (ICT) supports and how the ICT will be developed, installed, configured and maintained to support them [@CL-EST-1037]. They must also identify the applicable ICT accessibility standards and any such standards that cannot be met because of an exception or an exemption for a component or portion of the product [@CL-EST-1005].

Unless the agency Chief Information Officer waives the requirement, when an agency acquires information technology using Internet Protocol, the requirements documents must include reference to the appropriate technical capabilities defined in the USGv6 Profile [@CL-EST-1110] and to the corresponding declarations of conformance defined in the USGv6 Test Program [@CL-EST-1121]. How IPv6 applies to agency networks, infrastructure and applications specific to individual acquisitions will follow the agency's Enterprise Architecture [@CL-EST-1111].

Agencies shall not include in a solicitation a requirement that prohibits an offeror from permitting its employees to telecommute, unless the contracting officer executes a written determination in accordance with FAR 7.108(a) [@CL-EST-1003].

FAR 2.101 defines a nondevelopmental item through numbered paragraphs, and paragraph (1) covers any previously developed item of supply used exclusively for governmental purposes by a Federal agency, a State or local government, or a foreign government with which the United States has a mutual defense cooperation agreement [@CL-EST-9295]. This card does not summarize the other paragraphs of the definition.

## How it works

Part 11 prescribes policies and procedures for describing agency needs [@CL-EST-2138].

The agency, through its acquisition officials, shapes the requirement. The contracting officer makes the written determination that allows a solicitation to prohibit telework, and obtains the accessibility requirement documents from the requiring activity [@CL-EST-1003; @CL-EST-1004]. The agency Chief Information Officer may waive the Internet Protocol reference [@CL-EST-1110]. Requiring activities are responsible for establishing the metric guidance in formulating their requirements [@CL-EST-1002].

The duties on stating requirements, commercial items, competition for them, subcontract flow-down and modifying requirements all carry the lead-in "to the maximum extent practicable" [@CL-EST-0992]. The flow-down duty reaches contractors: acquisition officials are to require the prime contractor and its subcontractors at all tiers to incorporate commercial products, commercial services or nondevelopmental items as components of what they supply [@CL-EST-0995].

Paragraph (c) of the section uses "should", not "shall", for comment by potential offerors, guidance-only use of specifications and the ban on dictating design solutions too early [@CL-EST-0997; @CL-EST-0998; @CL-EST-0999]. Targets are optional: a solicitation may identify some or all performance levels or performance specifications as targets rather than as fixed or minimum requirements, and the paragraph does not name who decides [@CL-EST-1000].

For related rules on requirements documents and commercial market acceptance, see [Requirements documents, standardization and commercial market acceptance](../../supply-chain/sourcing-evidence/sup-requirements-documents-and-market-acceptance.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An agency needs 200 rugged laptops for field inspectors. The solicitation states the functions the inspectors perform and the drop rating that is essential [@CL-EST-0992]. It states a battery life of 10 hours and identifies it as a target, not a minimum [@CL-EST-1000]. It is written so that a commercial product can meet it, and an offeror of a commercial product can compete [@CL-EST-0993; @CL-EST-0994]. The solicitation also requires the prime contractor and its subcontractors at all tiers to incorporate commercial components, to the maximum extent practicable [@CL-EST-0995]. It contains one restrictive condition, an encryption standard that the agency ties to its mission need [@CL-EST-0991].

## Never confuse

A commercial product and a nondevelopmental item are not interchangeable. The nondevelopmental item is the fallback, and it applies to the extent that commercial products suitable to meet the agency's needs are not available [@CL-EST-0993]. A performance level identified as a target is not a fixed or minimum requirement [@CL-EST-1000]. A ban on telework in a solicitation needs the contracting officer's written determination [@CL-EST-1003]. A waiver of the Internet Protocol reference comes from the agency Chief Information Officer, not the contracting officer [@CL-EST-1110].

## Company calibration

The company must confirm, for each requirement set it prices, which version of the requirement documents, performance targets and tailoring decisions it relied on. [@Q-023]
