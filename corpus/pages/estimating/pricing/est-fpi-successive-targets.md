## Purpose

Shows how a successive targets contract starts with initial targets, sets firm targets at a production point, and then either fixes the price or sets a final price formula.

## The rule

|---|---|

| Codified citation | FAR 16.403-2 [@CL-EST-1082]; DFARS 216.403-2 [@CL-EST-9220] |

A fixed-price incentive (successive targets) contract specifies an initial target cost and an initial target profit, among elements that are all negotiated at the outset [@CL-EST-1082]. It also specifies an initial profit adjustment formula for establishing the firm target profit, with a ceiling and floor for that profit [@CL-EST-1082; @CL-EST-1083]. It further specifies the production point where firm targets will be negotiated, which is usually before delivery or shop completion of the first item [@CL-EST-1082; @CL-EST-1084].

The contract type is appropriate when, among its conditions, available information is not enough for a realistic firm target cost and profit before award but is enough for initial targets [@CL-EST-1088]. It may be used only when the contractor's accounting system is adequate for providing data for negotiating firm targets and a realistic profit adjustment formula, as well as later negotiation of final costs [@CL-EST-1103], and when cost or pricing information adequate for establishing a reasonable firm target cost is reasonably expected to be available at an early point in contract performance [@CL-EST-1089].

## How it works

When the production point is reached, the parties negotiate the firm target cost, giving consideration to cost experience under the contract and other pertinent factors, and the formula sets the firm target profit [@CL-EST-1085]. The parties may then negotiate a firm fixed price, using the firm target cost plus the firm target profit as a guide [@CL-EST-1086]. If a firm fixed price is inappropriate, they may negotiate a formula for the final price using the firm target cost and firm target profit [@CL-EST-1087]. The final cost is then negotiated at completion, and the final profit is established by formula, as under the firm target type [@CL-EST-1104]. The DFARS section with the same number says to see PGI 216.403-2 for guidance on the use of fixed-price incentive (successive targets) contracts [@CL-EST-9220].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contract sets an initial target cost of 1,000,000 and an initial target profit of 100,000. The initial formula limits the firm target profit to a range from 80,000 to 120,000. At the production point, after 20 units, cost experience leads the parties to a firm target cost of 900,000, and the formula sets a firm target profit of 90,000. They then use 990,000 as a guide and agree a firm fixed price of 985,000, so the incentive phase ends.

## Never confuse

Unlike a firm-target incentive contract that fixes final targets at award, a fixed-price incentive (successive targets) contract sets initial target cost, initial target profit, and an initial formula with a ceiling and floor for the firm target profit to be negotiated later in performance [@CL-EST-1082; @CL-EST-1083].

## Company calibration

How the initial targets, and the cost experience later used for the firm target cost, are frozen and compared with actuals. [@Q-023]
