## Purpose

Shows who is responsible for a source selection, what the source selection authority must do, and how cost or price and past performance are evaluated.

## The rule

Agency heads are responsible for source selection, and the contracting officer is designated as the source selection authority, unless the agency head appoints another individual for a particular acquisition or group of acquisitions [@CL-EST-1376]. The objective of source selection is to select the proposal that represents the best value [@CL-EST-2053]. The source selection authority shall (1) establish an evaluation team, tailored for the particular acquisition, that includes appropriate contracting, legal, logistics, technical, and other expertise to ensure a comprehensive evaluation of offers [@CL-EST-1377], (2) approve the source selection strategy or acquisition plan, if applicable, before solicitation release, (3) ensure consistency among the solicitation requirements, notices to offerors, proposal preparation instructions, evaluation factors and subfactors, solicitation provisions or contract clauses, and data requirements, (4) ensure that proposals are evaluated based solely on the factors and subfactors contained in the solicitation, (5) consider the recommendations of advisory boards or panels (if any), and (6) select the source or sources whose proposal is the best value to the Government [@CL-EST-0053; @CL-EST-0054; @CL-EST-1378].

An agency shall evaluate competitive proposals and then assess their relative qualities solely on the factors and subfactors specified in the solicitation [@CL-EST-1379]. The source selection authority may reject all proposals received in response to a solicitation, if doing so is in the best interest of the Government [@CL-EST-1489].

Subpart 15.3 prescribes policies and procedures for selection of a source or sources in competitive negotiated acquisitions [@CL-EST-9190].

## How it works

Normally, competition establishes price reasonableness, so when contracting on a firm-fixed-price or fixed-price with economic price adjustment basis, comparison of the proposed prices will usually satisfy the requirement to perform a price analysis, and a cost analysis need not be performed [@CL-EST-1380]. In limited situations, a cost analysis may be appropriate to establish reasonableness of the otherwise successful offeror's price [@CL-EST-1486]. When contracting on a cost-reimbursement basis, evaluations shall include a cost realism analysis to determine what the Government should realistically expect to pay for the proposed effort, the offeror's understanding of the work, and the offeror's ability to perform the contract [@CL-EST-1381]. Cost realism analyses may also be used on fixed-price incentive contracts or, in exceptional cases, on other competitive fixed-price-type contracts, and the contracting officer shall document the cost or price evaluation [@CL-EST-0058].

The solicitation shall describe the approach for evaluating past performance, including evaluating offerors with no relevant performance history, and shall provide offerors an opportunity to identify past or current contracts (including Federal, State, and local government and private) for efforts similar to the Government requirement [@CL-EST-1488; @CL-EST-0052]. In the case of an offeror without a record of relevant past performance or for whom information on past performance is not available, the offeror may not be evaluated favorably or unfavorably on past performance [@CL-EST-1487]. Which factors apply, including price or cost and past performance, is on [Evaluation factors: price or cost, past performance and the DoD additions](est-evaluation-factors-price-and-past-performance.md). The analysis techniques themselves are on [Proposal analysis techniques](est-proposal-analysis.md). The process in which award goes to the lowest evaluated price among acceptable proposals is on [Lowest price technically acceptable source selection](est-lpta-source-selection.md), and the exchanges that follow receipt of proposals are on [Exchanges with offerors after receipt of proposals](est-exchanges-after-proposals-and-competitive-range.md).

DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 15 text in place of the codified text under Class Deviation 2026-O0048, whose Revision 1 took effect immediately and supersedes the March 5, 2026 version [@CL-EST-3176; @CL-EST-3177]. RFO FAR 15.201(a) states that agency heads are responsible for source selection and that a contracting officer is designated as the source selection authority (SSA), unless the agency head appoints another individual for a particular acquisition or group of acquisitions [@CL-EST-3463]. RFO FAR 15.201(b) states that the SSA must (1) establish an evaluation team tailored for the acquisition that includes appropriate contracting, legal, logistics, technical, and other expertise to ensure a comprehensive evaluation of proposals [@CL-EST-3464], (2) approve the source selection approach or acquisition plan, if applicable, before issuance of the request for proposal (RFP), (3) ensure consistency among the RFP requirements, notices to offerors, proposal preparation instructions, evaluation factors and subfactors, solicitation provisions or contract clauses, and data requirements, (4) ensure that proposals are evaluated based solely on the factors and subfactors contained in the RFP, (5) consider the recommendations of advisory boards or panels, when applicable, and (6) select the offeror or offerors whose proposal is the best value to the Government [@CL-EST-0055; @CL-EST-0056; @CL-EST-3465]. RFO FAR 15.201(c)(3) states that the SSA may reject all proposals received in response to an RFP, if doing so is in the best interest of the Government [@CL-EST-3466]. RFO FAR 15.202(a)(1) states that agencies must evaluate each competitive proposal based solely on the factors and subfactors in the RFP [@CL-EST-3467]. RFO FAR 15.202(c)(1) states that an offeror without a record of relevant past performance or for whom information on past performance is not available may not be evaluated favorably or unfavorably on past performance [@CL-EST-3475], and RFO FAR 15.104(b)(3)(ii)(A) and (B) state that the RFP must describe the general approach for evaluating past performance information, including evaluating offerors with no relevant performance history, and provide offerors an opportunity to identify past or current contracts (including Federal, State, and local government, private sector, and other contracts) for efforts similar to the Government requirement, and provide information on problems encountered on the identified contracts and the offeror corrective actions [@CL-EST-3474; @CL-EST-0057; @CL-EST-0059]. RFO FAR 15.202(d) states that agencies must purchase supplies and services from responsible sources at fair and reasonable prices and that contracting officers are responsible for evaluating and documenting the reasonableness of the awarded price in accordance with subpart 15.4 [@CL-EST-3468]. RFO FAR 15.404(a)(2) states that price analysis must be used when certified cost or pricing data are not required, and that when a fair and reasonable price cannot be determined through price analysis alone, cost analysis may also be used to evaluate data other than certified cost or pricing data to determine cost reasonableness or cost realism [@CL-EST-3469; @CL-EST-3470]. RFO FAR 15.404-1(b) states that the Government may use various price analysis techniques and procedures to ensure a fair and reasonable price, including, but not limited to, comparison of proposed prices received in response to the RFP [@CL-EST-3471]. RFO FAR 15.404-3(b) states that cost realism analyses must be performed on cost-reimbursement contracts to determine the probable cost of performance for each offeror [@CL-EST-3472], and RFO FAR 15.404-3(a) describes cost realism analysis as the process of independently reviewing and evaluating specific elements of each offeror's proposed cost estimate to determine whether the estimated proposed cost elements are realistic for the work to be performed, reflect a clear understanding of the requirements, and are consistent with the unique methods of performance and materials described in the offeror's technical proposal [@CL-EST-3473; @CL-EST-3497]. RFO FAR 15.200 states that its subpart addresses policies and procedures for evaluating and negotiating competitive proposals and awarding contracts [@CL-EST-2252].

Supplies may be evaluated under comparable in-use conditions without a further test plan, provided offerors are so advised in the solicitation [@CL-EST-9182]. The results of such tests or demonstrations may be used to rate the proposal, to determine technical acceptability, or otherwise to evaluate the proposal [@CL-EST-9183].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An agency runs a competition for a firm-fixed-price supply item. The contracting officer is the source selection authority, builds a team with technical, legal and contracting members, and compares the offered prices. A cost analysis is not ordinarily needed because the prices compete, though one may be used in a limited situation to test the winner's price. For a cost-reimbursement study contract, the team instead tests each offer's labor hours and rates for realism before choosing the best value.

## Never confuse

Competition normally establishes price reasonableness, so when contracting on a firm-fixed-price or fixed-price with economic price adjustment basis, the comparison of proposed prices will usually satisfy the price analysis requirement [@CL-EST-1380]. See [Proposal analysis techniques](est-proposal-analysis.md). The source selection authority role belongs to the contracting officer by default, unless the agency head appoints another individual for a particular acquisition or group of acquisitions [@CL-EST-1376].

Under the codified rule for a firm-fixed-price or fixed-price with economic price adjustment acquisition, comparison of the proposed prices will usually satisfy the requirement to perform a price analysis and a cost analysis need not be performed [@CL-EST-1380]. Under RFO FAR 15.404(a)(2), price analysis must be used when certified cost or pricing data are not required [@CL-EST-3469], and cost analysis may also be used to evaluate data other than certified cost or pricing data when a fair and reasonable price cannot be determined through price analysis alone [@CL-EST-3470].
