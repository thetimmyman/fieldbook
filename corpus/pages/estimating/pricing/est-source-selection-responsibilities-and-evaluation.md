## Purpose

Shows who is responsible for a source selection, what the source selection authority must do, and how cost or price and past performance are evaluated.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-EST-1376] |
| Codified citation | FAR 15.303 and FAR 15.305 [@CL-EST-1376; @CL-EST-1379] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-EST-1376] |
| Applies to | Source selections in which competitive proposals are evaluated [@CL-EST-1379] |

Agency heads are responsible for source selection, and the contracting officer is the source selection authority unless the agency head appoints another individual for a particular acquisition or group of acquisitions [@CL-EST-1376]. The source selection authority shall establish an evaluation team, tailored for the particular acquisition, that includes appropriate contracting, legal, logistics, technical, and other expertise [@CL-EST-1377]. One listed duty of the source selection authority is to select the source or sources whose proposal is the best value to the Government [@CL-EST-1377; @CL-EST-1378].

An agency shall evaluate competitive proposals and then assess their relative qualities solely on the factors and subfactors specified in the solicitation [@CL-EST-1379]. The source selection authority may reject all proposals received in response to a solicitation, if doing so is in the best interest of the Government [@CL-EST-1489].

## How it works

Normally competition establishes price reasonableness, so when contracting on a firm-fixed-price or fixed-price with economic price adjustment basis, comparison of the proposed prices will usually satisfy the requirement to perform a price analysis, and a cost analysis need not be performed [@CL-EST-1380]. In limited situations, a cost analysis may be appropriate to establish reasonableness of the otherwise successful offeror's price [@CL-EST-1486]. When contracting on a cost-reimbursement basis, evaluations shall include a cost realism analysis to determine what the Government should realistically expect to pay for the proposed effort, the offeror's understanding of the work, and the offeror's ability to perform the contract [@CL-EST-1381].

The solicitation shall describe the approach for evaluating past performance, including evaluating offerors with no relevant performance history [@CL-EST-1488]. In the case of an offeror without a record of relevant past performance or for whom information on past performance is not available, the offeror may not be evaluated favorably or unfavorably on past performance [@CL-EST-1487]. Which factors apply, including price or cost and past performance, is on [Evaluation factors: price or cost, past performance and the DoD additions](est-evaluation-factors-price-and-past-performance.md). The analysis techniques themselves are on [Proposal analysis techniques](est-proposal-analysis.md). The process in which award goes to the lowest evaluated price among acceptable proposals is on [Lowest price technically acceptable source selection](est-lpta-source-selection.md), and the exchanges that follow receipt of proposals are on [Exchanges with offerors after receipt of proposals](est-exchanges-after-proposals-and-competitive-range.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An agency runs a competition for a firm-fixed-price supply item. The contracting officer is the source selection authority, builds a team with technical, legal and contracting members, and compares the offered prices. A cost analysis is not ordinarily needed because the prices compete, though one may be used in a limited situation to test the winner's price. For a cost-reimbursement study contract, the team instead tests each offer's labor hours and rates for realism before choosing the best value.

## Never confuse

Competition normally establishes price reasonableness, so in a fixed-price source selection the comparison of proposed prices usually satisfies the price analysis requirement [@CL-EST-1380]. See [Proposal analysis techniques](est-proposal-analysis.md). The source selection authority role belongs to the contracting officer by default, but the agency head may appoint another individual [@CL-EST-1376].
