## Purpose

Shows when the lowest price technically acceptable process fits, what it does not allow, and the conditions on its use.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-EST-1350] |
| Codified citation | FAR 15.101-2 [@CL-EST-1350] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 15.103-2 [@CL-EST-3301; @CL-EST-3302; @CL-EST-3303; @CL-EST-3589; @CL-EST-3304; @CL-EST-3305; @CL-EST-3306; @CL-EST-3307; @CL-EST-3308; @CL-EST-3309]; RFO FAR 15.202 [@CL-EST-3310; @CL-EST-3311]; RFO FAR 15.104(b)(3)(i) [@CL-EST-3249] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-EST-1350] |
| Applies to | Source selections that use the lowest price technically acceptable process [@CL-EST-1351] |

The process is appropriate when best value is expected to result from selection of the technically acceptable proposal with the lowest evaluated price [@CL-EST-1350]. When it is used, the requirements listed in paragraph (b) apply [@CL-EST-1351]. Three listed requirements are that tradeoffs are not permitted, that proposals are evaluated for acceptability but not ranked using the non-cost/price factors, and that exchanges may occur [@CL-EST-1351; @CL-EST-1352]. Another listed requirement is that solicitations specify that award will be made on the basis of the lowest evaluated price of proposals meeting or exceeding the acceptability standards for non-cost factors [@CL-EST-1351; @CL-EST-1353].

Except for DoD, the process shall only be used when the listed conditions are met [@CL-EST-1355]. One listed condition is that the agency can comprehensively and clearly describe the minimum requirements in terms of performance objectives, measures, and standards that will be used to determine the acceptability of offers [@CL-EST-1355; @CL-EST-1356]. Another is that the agency would realize no, or minimal, value from a proposal that exceeds the minimum technical or performance requirements [@CL-EST-1355; @CL-EST-1475]. A third is that the agency believes the technical proposals will require no, or minimal, subjective judgment by the source selection authority as to the desirability of one offeror's proposal versus a competing proposal [@CL-EST-1355; @CL-EST-1476].

Further listed conditions are that the agency has a high degree of confidence that reviewing the technical proposals of all offerors would not result in the identification of characteristics that could provide value or benefit to the agency [@CL-EST-1355; @CL-EST-1499], and that the agency determined that the lowest price reflects the total cost, including operation and support, of the products or services being acquired [@CL-EST-1355; @CL-EST-1477]. The last listed condition is that the contracting officer documents the contract file describing the circumstances that justify use of the process [@CL-EST-1355; @CL-EST-1357].

## How it works

The solicitation specifies that award will be made on the basis of the lowest evaluated price of proposals meeting or exceeding the acceptability standards for non-cost factors [@CL-EST-1351; @CL-EST-1353]. Proposals are evaluated for acceptability and are not ranked using the non-cost/price factors [@CL-EST-1351; @CL-EST-1352]. Because tradeoffs are not permitted, a higher price cannot be traded for a stronger technical rating [@CL-EST-1351; @CL-EST-1352]. Exchanges may occur [@CL-EST-1351; @CL-EST-1352], and the rules for them are on [Exchanges with offerors after receipt of proposals](est-exchanges-after-proposals-and-competitive-range.md).

If the contracting officer documents the file as the cross-referenced paragraph provides, past performance need not be an evaluation factor in lowest price technically acceptable source selections [@CL-EST-1351; @CL-EST-1478]. If the contracting officer elects to consider past performance as an evaluation factor, it is evaluated under the proposal evaluation section, but the comparative assessment in a cross-referenced paragraph does not apply [@CL-EST-1351; @CL-EST-1479]. The general proposal evaluation rules are on [Source selection responsibilities and proposal evaluation](est-source-selection-responsibilities-and-evaluation.md). Read the section itself before relying on the process.

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 15 text in place of the codified text under Class Deviation 2026-O0048, whose Revision 1 took effect immediately and supersedes the March 5, 2026 version [@CL-EST-3176] [@CL-EST-3177]. RFO FAR 15.103-2(a) says the lowest price technically acceptable approach is appropriate when it is in the Government's best interest to award to the offeror that submits a technically acceptable proposal with the lowest evaluated price, and that the approach does not permit tradeoffs [@CL-EST-3301], where the codified paragraph says the process is appropriate when best value is expected to result from selecting that proposal [@CL-EST-1350] and lists, among its requirements, that tradeoffs are not permitted [@CL-EST-1351; @CL-EST-1352]. RFO FAR 15.103-2(b) says the RFP must identify the evaluation factors that establish the minimum requirements for acceptability and specify that award will be made on the basis of the lowest evaluated price of proposals meeting or exceeding the requirements for acceptability [@CL-EST-3302], where the codified paragraph speaks of the acceptability standards for non-cost factors [@CL-EST-1353]. RFO FAR 15.202(b)(1) says that when using the approach proposals are evaluated for technical acceptability only [@CL-EST-3310], where the codified list says proposals are evaluated for acceptability but not ranked using the non-cost/price factors and that exchanges may occur [@CL-EST-1352]. RFO FAR 15.103-2(c)(1) says that, except for DoD, the approach must only be used when the listed conditions are met [@CL-EST-3303] [@CL-EST-3589], and it lists the same six conditions as the codified paragraph: a comprehensive and clear description of the minimum requirements [@CL-EST-3304; @CL-EST-1356], no or minimal value from a proposal that exceeds the minimum requirements [@CL-EST-3305; @CL-EST-1475], no or minimal subjective judgment by the source selection authority [@CL-EST-3306; @CL-EST-1476], a high degree of confidence that reviewing all technical proposals would identify no valuable characteristics [@CL-EST-3307; @CL-EST-1499], a determination that the lowest price reflects the total cost, including operation and support [@CL-EST-3308; @CL-EST-1477], and a contract file documenting the circumstances that justify use [@CL-EST-3309; @CL-EST-1357]. RFO FAR 15.202(c)(2) says that if past performance is evaluated when using the approach, contracting officers must only determine if the performance is technically acceptable and not perform a comparative assessment of the information [@CL-EST-3311], where the codified text says past performance need not be an evaluation factor if the contracting officer documents the file [@CL-EST-1478] and that if it is considered the comparative assessment does not apply [@CL-EST-1479], while RFO FAR 15.104(b)(3)(i) says past performance must be evaluated in every competitive source selection, unless the contracting officer documents the reason past performance is not an appropriate evaluation factor for the acquisition [@CL-EST-3249].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A solicitation for a simple supply item says award goes to the lowest evaluated price among proposals rated acceptable on every non-cost factor. Offeror A prices 900 and is acceptable. Offeror B prices 850 and is unacceptable on one factor. Offeror C prices 880 and is acceptable. Award goes to C. B's lower price does not count, and A's technical rating is not compared with C's.

## Never confuse

This process is not a tradeoff selection, because under it tradeoffs are not permitted [@CL-EST-1351; @CL-EST-1352]. It is also not a plain lowest-price award, because under it a proposal must first meet or exceed the acceptability standards for non-cost factors [@CL-EST-1351; @CL-EST-1353]. The codified text says the process is appropriate when best value is expected to result from the technically acceptable proposal with the lowest evaluated price [@CL-EST-1350], while RFO FAR 15.103-2(a) says it is appropriate when it is in the Government's best interest to award to that offeror [@CL-EST-3301]. The codified list says proposals are evaluated for acceptability but not ranked using the non-cost/price factors [@CL-EST-1352], while RFO FAR 15.202(b)(1) says proposals are evaluated for technical acceptability only [@CL-EST-3310]. How evaluation factors are set and weighed is on [Evaluation factors: price or cost, past performance and the DoD additions](est-evaluation-factors-price-and-past-performance.md).
