## Purpose

Shows when the lowest price technically acceptable process fits, what it does not allow, and the conditions on its use.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-EST-1350] |
| Codified citation | FAR 15.101-2 [@CL-EST-1350] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-EST-1350] |
| Applies to | Source selections that use the lowest price technically acceptable process [@CL-EST-1351] |

The process is appropriate when best value is expected to result from selection of the technically acceptable proposal with the lowest evaluated price [@CL-EST-1350]. When it is used, the requirements listed in paragraph (b) apply [@CL-EST-1351]. Three listed requirements are that tradeoffs are not permitted, that proposals are evaluated for acceptability but not ranked using the non-cost/price factors, and that exchanges may occur [@CL-EST-1351; @CL-EST-1352]. Another listed requirement is that solicitations specify that award will be made on the basis of the lowest evaluated price of proposals meeting or exceeding the acceptability standards for non-cost factors [@CL-EST-1351; @CL-EST-1353].

Except for DoD, the process shall only be used when the listed conditions are met [@CL-EST-1355]. One listed condition is that the agency can comprehensively and clearly describe the minimum requirements in terms of performance objectives, measures, and standards that will be used to determine the acceptability of offers [@CL-EST-1355; @CL-EST-1356]. Another is that the agency would realize no, or minimal, value from a proposal that exceeds the minimum technical or performance requirements [@CL-EST-1355; @CL-EST-1475]. A third is that the agency believes the technical proposals will require no, or minimal, subjective judgment by the source selection authority as to the desirability of one offeror's proposal versus a competing proposal [@CL-EST-1355; @CL-EST-1476].

Further listed conditions are that the agency has a high degree of confidence that reviewing the technical proposals of all offerors would not result in the identification of characteristics that could provide value or benefit to the agency [@CL-EST-1355; @CL-EST-1499], and that the agency determined that the lowest price reflects the total cost, including operation and support, of the products or services being acquired [@CL-EST-1355; @CL-EST-1477]. The last listed condition is that the contracting officer documents the contract file describing the circumstances that justify use of the process [@CL-EST-1355; @CL-EST-1357].

## How it works

The solicitation specifies that award will be made on the basis of the lowest evaluated price of proposals meeting or exceeding the acceptability standards for non-cost factors [@CL-EST-1351; @CL-EST-1353]. Proposals are evaluated for acceptability and are not ranked using the non-cost/price factors [@CL-EST-1351; @CL-EST-1352]. Because tradeoffs are not permitted, a higher price cannot be traded for a stronger technical rating [@CL-EST-1351; @CL-EST-1352]. Exchanges may occur [@CL-EST-1351; @CL-EST-1352], and the rules for them are on [Exchanges with offerors after receipt of proposals](est-exchanges-after-proposals-and-competitive-range.md).

If the contracting officer documents the file as the cross-referenced paragraph provides, past performance need not be an evaluation factor in lowest price technically acceptable source selections [@CL-EST-1351; @CL-EST-1478]. If the contracting officer elects to consider past performance as an evaluation factor, it is evaluated under the proposal evaluation section, but the comparative assessment in a cross-referenced paragraph does not apply [@CL-EST-1351; @CL-EST-1479]. The general proposal evaluation rules are on [Source selection responsibilities and proposal evaluation](est-source-selection-responsibilities-and-evaluation.md). Read the section itself before relying on the process.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A solicitation for a simple supply item says award goes to the lowest evaluated price among proposals rated acceptable on every non-cost factor. Offeror A prices 900 and is acceptable. Offeror B prices 850 and is unacceptable on one factor. Offeror C prices 880 and is acceptable. Award goes to C. B's lower price does not count, and A's technical rating is not compared with C's.

## Never confuse

This process is not a tradeoff selection, because under it tradeoffs are not permitted [@CL-EST-1351; @CL-EST-1352]. It is also not a plain lowest-price award, because under it a proposal must first meet or exceed the acceptability standards for non-cost factors [@CL-EST-1351; @CL-EST-1353]. How evaluation factors are set and weighed is on [Evaluation factors: price or cost, past performance and the DoD additions](est-evaluation-factors-price-and-past-performance.md).

## Company calibration

Which of the company's expected bids may be evaluated this way, and who reviews the price before it is submitted. [@Q-012]
