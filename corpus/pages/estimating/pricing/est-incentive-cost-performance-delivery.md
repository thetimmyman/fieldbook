## Purpose

Shows what each kind of incentive does and why every incentive contract with other incentives must also carry a cost incentive or constraint.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-EST-1068] |
| Codified citation | FAR 16.402-1 to 16.402-4 [@CL-EST-1068; @CL-EST-1072; @CL-EST-1073; @CL-EST-1074] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-EST-1068] |
| Applies to | Incentive contracts that use cost, performance or delivery incentives [@CL-EST-1068; @CL-EST-1072; @CL-EST-1073] |

Most incentive contracts include only cost incentives, which take the form of a profit or fee adjustment formula meant to motivate the contractor to manage costs effectively [@CL-EST-1068]. No incentive contract may provide for other incentives without also providing a cost incentive or constraint [@CL-EST-1069].

Performance incentives may be considered in connection with specific product characteristics or other specific elements of the contractor's performance [@CL-EST-1106]. Positive and negative performance incentives shall be considered, to the maximum extent practicable, for service contracts with objectively measurable tasks when quality of performance is critical and incentives are likely to motivate the contractor [@CL-EST-1072]. Delivery incentives should be considered when improvement from a required delivery schedule is a significant Government objective [@CL-EST-1073].

## How it works

Except for award-fee contracts, an incentive contract has a target cost, a target profit or fee, and an adjustment formula that works within a price ceiling or minimum and maximum fee [@CL-EST-1070]. Under the formula, actual cost that meets the target gives the target profit or fee, actual cost above the target adjusts it down, and actual cost below the target adjusts it up [@CL-EST-1070; @CL-EST-1071]. With more than one incentive, a properly structured arrangement should motivate the contractor to strive for outstanding results in all incentive areas and compel trade-off decisions among the incentive areas, consistent with the Government's overall objectives for the acquisition [@CL-EST-1074]. All multiple-incentive contracts must include a cost incentive or constraint that operates to preclude rewarding a contractor for superior technical performance or delivery results when the cost of those results outweighs their value to the Government [@CL-EST-1075].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contract sets a target cost of 1,000,000 and a target profit of 80,000, and it adds a reward for each week of early delivery. The contractor delivers 4 weeks early but its actual cost is 1,150,000, above target. Because the contract carries a cost incentive, the cost overrun adjusts profit downward and offsets part of the delivery reward. Without a cost incentive or constraint, the contract could not have carried the delivery incentive at all.

## Never confuse

Cost incentives take the form of a profit or fee adjustment formula [@CL-EST-1068], performance incentives may be considered in connection with specific product characteristics or other specific elements of the contractor's performance [@CL-EST-1106], and delivery incentives should be considered when improvement from a required delivery schedule is a significant Government objective [@CL-EST-1073]. No incentive contract may provide for other incentives without also providing a cost incentive or constraint [@CL-EST-1069]. For how incentive fee is billed after award, see [Incentive Fee clause](../../contract-finance/billing-payment/ctf-incentive-fee.md).
