## Purpose

Defines advertising material, a commercial product or commercial service offer and a contribution as the unsolicited proposal subpart uses them, and states the Government policy that encourages new and innovative ideas and lets ideas outside publicized topic areas be submitted as unsolicited proposals.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-EST-2750] |
| Codified citation | FAR 15.601 and FAR 15.602 [@CL-EST-2750; @CL-EST-2754]; DFARS 215.602 [@CL-EST-2060]; FAR 15.600 [@CL-EST-9192] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 15.501 [@CL-EST-3519]; RFO FAR 15.503-1(a) [@CL-EST-3523] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-EST-2750] |
| Applies to | Terms used in the unsolicited proposal subpart, and new and innovative ideas that do not fall under publicized topic areas [@CL-EST-2778; @CL-EST-2755] |

The terms below are defined as used in the unsolicited proposal subpart [@CL-EST-2778]. Advertising material means material designed to acquaint the Government with a prospective contractor's present products, services or potential capabilities, or designed to stimulate the Government's interest in buying such products or services [@CL-EST-2750]. A commercial product or commercial service offer means an offer of a commercial product or commercial service that the vendor wishes to see introduced in the Government's supply system as an alternate or a replacement for an existing supply item [@CL-EST-2751]. That term does not include innovative or unique configurations or uses of commercial products or commercial services that are being offered for further development and that may be submitted as an unsolicited proposal [@CL-EST-2752]. A contribution means a concept, suggestion or idea presented to the Government for its use with no indication that the source intends to devote any further effort to it on the Government's behalf [@CL-EST-2753].

It is the policy of the Government to encourage the submission of new and innovative ideas in response to Broad Agency Announcements, Small Business Innovation Research topics, Small Business Technology Transfer Research topics and Program Research and Development Announcements [@CL-EST-2754], or any other Government-initiated solicitation or program [@CL-EST-2779]. When the new and innovative ideas do not fall under topic areas publicized under those programs or techniques, the ideas may be submitted as unsolicited proposals [@CL-EST-2755].

The FAR's general definitions say a broad agency announcement is a general announcement of an agency's research interest that includes criteria for selecting proposals and solicits the participation of all offerors capable of satisfying the Government's needs, and they point to 6.102(d)(2) [@CL-EST-9274].

Subpart 15.6 sets forth policies and procedures concerning the submission, receipt, evaluation, and acceptance or rejection of unsolicited proposals [@CL-EST-9192].

## How it works

The policy has two parts. The Government encourages ideas in response to its own publicized solicitations and programs, and an idea that does not fall under a publicized topic area may be submitted as an unsolicited proposal [@CL-EST-2754; @CL-EST-2779; @CL-EST-2755]. The text says may, so it describes a permitted route and does not say it is the only one [@CL-EST-2755].

The three defined terms describe different things. Advertising material promotes what a contractor already offers or can do. A commercial offer asks that a commercial product or service be introduced into the supply system as an alternate or a replacement for an existing supply item. A contribution hands the Government an idea with no indication of further effort by the source [@CL-EST-2750; @CL-EST-2751; @CL-EST-2753]. Under the general requirements for unsolicited proposals, advertising material, commercial product or commercial service offers and contributions are not unsolicited proposals [@CL-EST-1429]. See [Unsolicited proposals: general requirements](est-unsolicited-proposals-general.md).

The DFARS says the policy at FAR 15.602 applies to commercial solutions openings [@CL-EST-2060].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 15 text in place of the codified text under Class Deviation 2026-O0048, whose Revision 1 took effect immediately and supersedes the March 5, 2026 version [@CL-EST-3176] [@CL-EST-3177]. RFO FAR 15.501 defines advertising material, a commercial product or commercial service offer and a contribution, as used in the RFO unsolicited proposal subpart, in the same words as the codified definitions [@CL-EST-3519; @CL-EST-3520; @CL-EST-3522; @CL-EST-2750; @CL-EST-2751; @CL-EST-2753; @CL-EST-2778], and it also says a commercial product or commercial service offer does not include innovative or unique configurations or uses of commercial products or commercial services that are being offered for further development and that may be submitted as an unsolicited proposal [@CL-EST-3521; @CL-EST-2752]. On policy, RFO FAR 15.503-1(a) says unsolicited proposals may be submitted for new and innovative ideas that do not already fall under topic areas publicized under BAA, SBIR topics, STTR topics, program research and development announcements, or any other Government-initiated RFP or program, where the codified section opens with a sentence stating a Government policy to encourage the submission of new and innovative ideas [@CL-EST-3523; @CL-EST-2754; @CL-EST-2755; @CL-EST-2779].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A vendor sends a brochure about an item it already sells. The brochure is designed to acquaint the Government with the vendor's present products, so it is advertising material [@CL-EST-2750]. The vendor separately offers that item as a replacement for an item already in the supply system, so that is a commercial product offer [@CL-EST-2751].

A third submission describes an innovative configuration of the same item that is offered for further development. It is outside the commercial offer definition and may be submitted as an unsolicited proposal [@CL-EST-2752].

A researcher emails a one-paragraph idea and says nothing about doing further work on it. That is a contribution [@CL-EST-2753]. Another researcher has a new and innovative idea that falls under no publicized topic area. The idea may be submitted as an unsolicited proposal [@CL-EST-2755].

## Never confuse

Codified FAR 15.602 states a Government policy to encourage new and innovative ideas [@CL-EST-2754], while RFO FAR 15.503-1(a) is one sentence saying that unsolicited proposals may be submitted for new and innovative ideas that do not already fall under publicized topic areas [@CL-EST-3523].

A commercial product or commercial service offer is not the same as an innovative or unique configuration or use offered for further development. The second is outside the definition of the first and may be submitted as an unsolicited proposal [@CL-EST-2751; @CL-EST-2752].

A contribution is not an unsolicited proposal. It carries no indication of further effort by the source, and the general requirements list contributions among the submissions that are not unsolicited proposals [@CL-EST-2753; @CL-EST-1429].
