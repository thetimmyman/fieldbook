## Purpose

Shows what clarifications, communications and discussions each allow, how the competitive range is set, and what the Government may not reveal.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-EST-1383] |
| Codified citation | FAR 15.306 [@CL-EST-1383] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-EST-1383] |
| Applies to | Exchanges between the Government and offerors after receipt of proposals [@CL-EST-1384] |

Clarifications are limited exchanges between the Government and offerors that may occur when award without discussions is contemplated [@CL-EST-1383]. Communications are exchanges between the Government and offerors, after receipt of proposals, leading to establishment of the competitive range [@CL-EST-1384]. Paragraph (b) sets listed limits on the communications held if a competitive range is to be established [@CL-EST-1494]. One listed limit is that such communications shall not be used to cure proposal deficiencies or material omissions, materially alter the technical or cost elements of the proposal, or otherwise revise the proposal [@CL-EST-1494; @CL-EST-1385].

## How it works

Agencies shall evaluate all proposals and, if discussions are to be conducted, establish the competitive range [@CL-EST-1490]. When discussions are to be conducted, the competitive range comprises all of the most highly rated proposals based on their ratings against all evaluation criteria, unless the range is further reduced for purposes of efficiency [@CL-EST-1490; @CL-EST-1386]. Provided the solicitation notifies offerors that the competitive range can be limited for purposes of efficiency, the contracting officer may limit the number of proposals in the competitive range to the greatest number that will permit an efficient competition [@CL-EST-1386; @CL-EST-1491].

Negotiations are exchanges between the Government and offerors that are undertaken with the intent of allowing the offeror to revise its proposal [@CL-EST-1387]. Discussions are tailored to each offeror's proposal and must be conducted by the contracting officer with each offeror within the competitive range [@CL-EST-1388]. At a minimum, the contracting officer must, subject to the cross-referenced paragraphs, indicate to, or discuss with, each offeror still being considered for award deficiencies, significant weaknesses, and adverse past performance information to which the offeror has not yet had an opportunity to respond [@CL-EST-1492; @CL-EST-1493].

Government personnel involved in the acquisition shall not engage in conduct that favors one offeror over another or that reveals an offeror's technical solution, including unique technology [@CL-EST-1389]. They shall not reveal an offeror's price without that offeror's permission, but the contracting officer may inform an offeror that its price is too high or too low and reveal the results of the analysis supporting that conclusion [@CL-EST-1389; @CL-EST-1390]. One listed limit is that they shall not reveal the names of individuals providing reference information about an offeror's past performance [@CL-EST-1389; @CL-EST-1391].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

After proposals arrive, the contracting officer finds a proposal whose standing is uncertain and holds a communication to clear up an ambiguity. The offeror may not use that exchange to fix a missing section. Later, the proposal is in the competitive range. In discussions, the contracting officer tells the offeror its price looks too high and shares the analysis behind that view, but does not say what any rival offered.

## Never confuse

Communications and discussions differ in purpose. Communications held if a competitive range is to be established may not be used to revise a proposal [@CL-EST-1494; @CL-EST-1385], while negotiations are undertaken so that the offeror may revise it [@CL-EST-1387]. Telling an offeror its price is too high or too low is allowed, but revealing an offeror's price without that offeror's permission is not [@CL-EST-1389; @CL-EST-1390]. The earlier exchanges with industry are on [Exchanges with industry before receipt of proposals](est-exchanges-before-proposals.md). The lowest price technically acceptable process, in which exchanges may occur, is on [Lowest price technically acceptable source selection](est-lpta-source-selection.md).
