## Purpose

Shows what clarifications, communications and discussions each allow, how the competitive range is set, and what the Government may not reveal.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-EST-1383] |
| Codified citation | FAR 15.306 [@CL-EST-1383] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 15.201 [@CL-EST-3265; @CL-EST-3266; @CL-EST-3267]; RFO FAR 15.202 [@CL-EST-3262; @CL-EST-3263; @CL-EST-3264]; RFO FAR 15.204-1 [@CL-EST-3268; @CL-EST-3269]; RFO FAR 15.204-2 [@CL-EST-3270]; RFO FAR 15.203 [@CL-EST-2250; @CL-EST-2251] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-EST-1383] |
| Applies to | Exchanges between the Government and offerors after receipt of proposals [@CL-EST-1384] |

Clarifications are limited exchanges between the Government and offerors that may occur when award without discussions is contemplated [@CL-EST-1383]. Communications are exchanges between the Government and offerors, after receipt of proposals, leading to establishment of the competitive range [@CL-EST-1384]. Paragraph (b) sets listed limits on the communications held if a competitive range is to be established [@CL-EST-1494]. One listed limit is that such communications shall not be used to cure proposal deficiencies or material omissions, materially alter the technical or cost elements of the proposal, or otherwise revise the proposal [@CL-EST-1494; @CL-EST-1385].

## How it works

Agencies shall evaluate all proposals and, if discussions are to be conducted, establish the competitive range [@CL-EST-1490]. When discussions are to be conducted, the competitive range comprises all of the most highly rated proposals based on their ratings against all evaluation criteria, unless the range is further reduced for purposes of efficiency [@CL-EST-1490; @CL-EST-1386]. After evaluating all proposals, the contracting officer may determine that the number of most highly rated proposals that might otherwise be included in the competitive range exceeds the number at which an efficient competition can be conducted [@CL-EST-9505]. Provided the solicitation notifies offerors that the competitive range can be limited for purposes of efficiency, the contracting officer may then limit the number of proposals in the competitive range to the greatest number that will permit an efficient competition [@CL-EST-1386; @CL-EST-1491].

Negotiations are exchanges between the Government and offerors that are undertaken with the intent of allowing the offeror to revise its proposal [@CL-EST-1387]. Discussions are tailored to each offeror's proposal and must be conducted by the contracting officer with each offeror within the competitive range [@CL-EST-1388]. At a minimum, the contracting officer must, subject to the cross-referenced paragraphs, indicate to, or discuss with, each offeror still being considered for award deficiencies, significant weaknesses, and adverse past performance information to which the offeror has not yet had an opportunity to respond [@CL-EST-1492; @CL-EST-1493].

Government personnel involved in the acquisition shall not engage in conduct that favors one offeror over another or that reveals an offeror's technical solution, including unique technology [@CL-EST-1389]. They shall not reveal an offeror's price without that offeror's permission, but the contracting officer may inform an offeror that its price is too high or too low and reveal the results of the analysis supporting that conclusion [@CL-EST-1389; @CL-EST-1390]. One listed limit is that they shall not reveal the names of individuals providing reference information about an offeror's past performance [@CL-EST-1389; @CL-EST-1391].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 15 text in place of the codified text under Class Deviation 2026-O0048, whose Revision 1 took effect immediately and supersedes the March 5, 2026 version [@CL-EST-3176] [@CL-EST-3177]. RFO FAR 15.202(a)(2) defines clarifications as exchanges in which offerors are given the opportunity to resolve minor or clerical errors or clarify certain aspects of their proposal [@CL-EST-3262], which may occur, at the contracting officer's discretion, at any time after receipt of proposals through contract award [@CL-EST-3263], where the codified text ties clarifications to when award without discussions is contemplated [@CL-EST-1383]. RFO FAR 15.202(a)(2)(iii) also says clarifications do not permit offerors to revise their proposal and cannot be used to cure proposal deficiencies or material omissions or materially alter the technical or cost elements of the proposal [@CL-EST-3264], and adds that contracting officers may request additional information or documentation provided the cost/price or other material elements of the proposal are unchanged [@CL-EST-3344], where the codified text states a matching limit for communications held if a competitive range is to be established [@CL-EST-1494; @CL-EST-1385]. RFO FAR 15.202(a)(2)(ii) says clarifications include, but are not limited to, addressing ambiguities of the proposal and other concerns such as perceived deficiencies, weaknesses, errors, omissions, or mistakes [@CL-EST-3587], and also the relevance of an offeror's past performance information and adverse past performance information to which the offeror has not previously had an opportunity to respond [@CL-EST-3345]. RFO FAR 15.201(c) says that, after receipt of proposals, contracting officers must control communications with offerors [@CL-EST-3265]. RFO FAR 15.204-1(a) defines the competitive range as the group of evaluated proposals that the contracting officer determines are best suited for further negotiation and requires one if negotiations will occur after evaluating competitive proposals [@CL-EST-3268], where the codified text says the range comprises all of the most highly rated proposals unless it is further reduced for efficiency [@CL-EST-1386]. RFO FAR 15.204-1(b) says the contracting officer may limit the number of proposals in the competitive range to the greatest number that will permit an efficient competition among the most highly evaluated proposals, in a sentence that states no condition about notice in the solicitation [@CL-EST-3269], where the codified text requires the solicitation to notify offerors that the range can be limited for efficiency [@CL-EST-1491]. RFO FAR 15.204-2(b)(1) says contracting officers must negotiate with each responsible offeror within the competitive range and, at a minimum, indicate to, or negotiate with, each offeror any deficiencies or significant weaknesses in the proposal [@CL-EST-3270], where the codified minimum lists deficiencies, significant weaknesses, and adverse past performance information to which the offeror has not yet had an opportunity to respond [@CL-EST-1493]. RFO FAR 15.201(d)(1)-(2) keeps the bar on conduct that favors one offeror over another or that reveals an offeror's technical solution, including unique technology [@CL-EST-3266; @CL-EST-1389]. RFO FAR 15.201(d)(3) states the bar on revealing an offeror's price without that offeror's permission and is followed directly by paragraph (d)(4), where the codified paragraph continues with a sentence letting the contracting officer inform an offeror that its price is too high or too low [@CL-EST-3267; @CL-EST-1390]. RFO FAR 15.201(d)(4) bars revealing the names of individuals providing information about an offeror's past performance, where the codified text says reference information [@CL-EST-3267; @CL-EST-1391]. RFO FAR 15.203 says that contracting officers may make competitive awards without negotiations if the RFP states that the Government intends to evaluate proposals and make award without negotiation [@CL-EST-2250], and that if the RFP includes such a notice and the Government determines it is necessary to negotiate, the rationale for doing so must be documented in the contract file [@CL-EST-2251].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

After proposals arrive, the contracting officer finds a proposal whose standing is uncertain and holds a communication to clear up an ambiguity. The offeror may not use that exchange to fix a missing section. Later, the proposal is in the competitive range. In discussions, the contracting officer tells the offeror its price looks too high and shares the analysis behind that view, but does not say what any rival offered. That follows the codified rule [@CL-EST-1390], and the RFO price paragraph reads differently [@CL-EST-3267].

## Never confuse

Communications and discussions differ in purpose. Communications held if a competitive range is to be established may not be used to revise a proposal [@CL-EST-1494; @CL-EST-1385], while negotiations are undertaken so that the offeror may revise it [@CL-EST-1387]. Government personnel may not reveal an offeror's price without that offeror's permission, but the contracting officer may inform an offeror that its price is considered by the Government to be too high or too low and reveal the results of the analysis supporting that conclusion [@CL-EST-1389; @CL-EST-1390]. The codified text lets the contracting officer tell an offeror its price is too high or too low [@CL-EST-1390], but RFO FAR 15.201(d)(3) states the bar on revealing an offeror's price without that offeror's permission and is followed directly by paragraph (d)(4) [@CL-EST-3267]. The codified minimum for discussions includes adverse past performance information [@CL-EST-1493], while the RFO minimum for negotiations names deficiencies and significant weaknesses [@CL-EST-3270] and RFO FAR 15.202(a)(2)(ii) lists adverse past performance information among the matters clarifications include [@CL-EST-3345].
