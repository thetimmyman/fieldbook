## Purpose

Shows what a service purchase description must contain, and when a solicitation must carry the Brand Name or Equal provision or the provision on alternatives to Government-unique standards.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-EST-1006] |
| Codified citation | FAR 11.104 [@CL-EST-2139; @CL-EST-2140; @CL-EST-2141], FAR 11.106 and 11.107 [@CL-EST-1006; @CL-EST-1010] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-EST-1006] |
| Applies to | Agency requiring activities that draft service contract purchase descriptions; solicitations with brand name or equal purchase descriptions; solicitations that use Government-unique standards [@CL-EST-1006; @CL-EST-1010; @CL-EST-1011] |

In drafting purchase descriptions for service contracts, agency requiring activities shall ensure that inherently governmental functions are not assigned to a contractor [@CL-EST-1006]. These purchase descriptions shall reserve final determination for Government officials [@CL-EST-1007]. They shall require proper identification of contractor personnel who attend meetings, answer Government telephones, or work in situations where their actions could be construed as acts of Government officials, unless, in the judgment of the agency, no harm can come from failing to identify themselves [@CL-EST-1008]. They shall also require suitable marking of all documents or reports produced by contractors [@CL-EST-1009].

The provision at 52.211-6, Brand Name or Equal, must be inserted when brand name or equal purchase descriptions are included in a solicitation [@CL-EST-1010]. The provision at 52.211-7, Alternatives to Government-Unique Standards, must be inserted in solicitations that use Government-unique standards when the agency uses the transaction-based reporting method to report its use of voluntary consensus standards to the National Institute of Standards and Technology, with a cross-reference to OMB Circular A-119, “Federal Participation in the Development and Use of Voluntary Consensus Standards and in Conformity Assessment Activities” [@CL-EST-1011; @CL-EST-1126]. Use of that provision is optional for agencies that report their use of voluntary consensus standards to that Institute using the categorical reporting method [@CL-EST-1012].

Agencies that manage their specifications on a contract-by-contract basis use the transaction-based method of reporting [@CL-EST-1013]. Agencies that manage their specifications centrally use the categorical method [@CL-EST-1014]. Agency regulations regarding specification management describe which method is used [@CL-EST-1015].

## How it works

In drafting purchase descriptions for service contracts, agency requiring activities shall ensure that inherently governmental functions are not assigned to a contractor [@CL-EST-1006]. These purchase descriptions shall reserve final determination for Government officials [@CL-EST-1007]. Whether no harm can come from failing to identify contractor personnel is a matter of the judgment of the agency [@CL-EST-1008].

These purchase descriptions shall require proper identification of contractor personnel who attend meetings, answer Government telephones, or work in situations where their actions could be construed as acts of Government officials, unless, in the judgment of the agency, no harm can come from failing to identify themselves [@CL-EST-1008]. They shall also require suitable marking of all documents or reports produced by contractors [@CL-EST-1009].

The provision at 52.211-7, Alternatives to Government-Unique Standards, is inserted in solicitations that use Government-unique standards when the agency uses the transaction-based reporting method to report its use of voluntary consensus standards to the National Institute of Standards and Technology [@CL-EST-1011]. Use of the provision is optional for agencies that report their use of voluntary consensus standards to that Institute using the categorical reporting method [@CL-EST-1012]. Agency regulations regarding specification management describe which method is used [@CL-EST-1015]. The provision at 52.211-6, Brand Name or Equal, is inserted when brand name or equal purchase descriptions are included in a solicitation [@CL-EST-1010].

The policy on brand name or equal descriptions is in section 11.104 [@CL-EST-2139]. While performance specifications are preferred to encourage offerors to propose innovative solutions, brand name or equal purchase descriptions may be advantageous under certain circumstances [@CL-EST-2139]. Such a description must include, in addition to the brand name, a general description of those salient physical, functional, or performance characteristics of the brand name item that an “equal” item must meet to be acceptable for award [@CL-EST-2140]. Brand name or equal descriptions are to be used when the salient characteristics are firm requirements [@CL-EST-2141].

For what the two provisions say once they are in a solicitation, see [Material requirements and brand name or equal](../../supply-chain/sourcing-evidence/sup-material-requirements-and-brand-name-or-equal.md) and [Examining specifications and proposing alternatives to Government-unique standards](est-specification-examination-and-alternative-standards.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An agency buys grounds maintenance for 12 sites. The description reserves acceptance decisions to Government officials [@CL-EST-1007]. It requires crew badges when crew members attend meetings with building tenants, because the agency judges that failing to identify them could do harm [@CL-EST-1008]. It requires the contract number on every page of the monthly report as suitable marking [@CL-EST-1009]. The description calls for one brand of mower "or equal", so it is a brand name or equal purchase description and the solicitation includes the Brand Name or Equal provision [@CL-EST-1010]. If the solicitation also used a Government-unique standard and the agency reported by the categorical method, inserting the provision on alternatives to Government-unique standards would be optional [@CL-EST-1011; @CL-EST-1012].

## Never confuse

The provision at 52.211-6 is inserted when brand name or equal purchase descriptions are included in a solicitation [@CL-EST-1010]. The provision at 52.211-7 is inserted in solicitations that use Government-unique standards when the agency uses the transaction-based reporting method to report its use of voluntary consensus standards to the National Institute of Standards and Technology [@CL-EST-1011].

Agencies that manage their specifications on a contract-by-contract basis use the transaction-based method of reporting [@CL-EST-1013], and agencies that manage their specifications centrally use the categorical method of reporting [@CL-EST-1014].

The purchase descriptions shall require proper identification of contractor personnel unless, in the judgment of the agency, no harm can come from failing to identify themselves [@CL-EST-1008].

## Company calibration

The company must confirm, for every service solicitation it bids, which of the listed purchase-description requirements the agency wrote in and whether the Brand Name or Equal provision is present. [@Q-004]
