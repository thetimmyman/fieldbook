## Purpose

Shows what a service purchase description must contain, and when a solicitation must carry the Brand Name or Equal provision or the provision on alternatives to Government-unique standards.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-EST-1006] |
| Codified citation | FAR 11.104 [@CL-EST-2139; @CL-EST-2140; @CL-EST-2141], FAR 11.106 and 11.107 [@CL-EST-1006; @CL-EST-1010] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-EST-1006] |
| Applies to | Agency requiring activities that draft service contract purchase descriptions; solicitations with brand name or equal purchase descriptions; solicitations that use Government-unique standards [@CL-EST-1006; @CL-EST-1010; @CL-EST-1011] |

In drafting purchase descriptions for service contracts, agency requiring activities shall ensure that inherently governmental functions are not assigned to a contractor [@CL-EST-1006]. These purchase descriptions shall reserve final determination for Government officials [@CL-EST-1007]. They shall require proper identification of contractor personnel who attend meetings, answer Government telephones, or work in situations where their actions could be construed as acts of Government officials, unless, in the judgment of the agency, no harm can come from failing to identify themselves [@CL-EST-1008]. They shall also require suitable marking of all documents or reports produced by contractors [@CL-EST-1009].

The provision at 52.211-6, Brand Name or Equal, must be inserted when brand name or equal purchase descriptions are included in a solicitation [@CL-EST-1010]. The provision at 52.211-7, Alternatives to Government-Unique Standards, must be inserted in solicitations that use Government-unique standards when the agency uses the transaction-based reporting method to report its use of voluntary consensus standards to the National Institute of Standards and Technology [@CL-EST-1011]. That sentence ends with a cross-reference to OMB Circular A-119 [@CL-EST-1126]. Use of that provision is optional for agencies that report their use of voluntary consensus standards to that Institute using the categorical reporting method [@CL-EST-1012].

Agencies that manage their specifications on a contract-by-contract basis use the transaction-based method of reporting [@CL-EST-1013]. Agencies that manage their specifications centrally use the categorical method [@CL-EST-1014]. Agency regulations regarding specification management describe which method is used [@CL-EST-1015].

## How it works

The requiring activity carries the first duty. It must keep inherently governmental functions out of the contractor's scope [@CL-EST-1006]. The description it drafts must then reserve final determination for Government officials [@CL-EST-1007]. The agency, not the contractor, judges whether failing to identify contractor personnel can do no harm [@CL-EST-1008].

Contractors feel the other two features as terms of the description. The description must require proper identification of their personnel in the situations listed, unless the agency judges that no harm can come from failing to identify themselves [@CL-EST-1008]. It must also require suitable marking of their documents and reports [@CL-EST-1009].

Two tests decide the provision on alternatives to Government-unique standards: the solicitation must use Government-unique standards, and the agency must use the transaction-based reporting method [@CL-EST-1011]. For an agency that uses the categorical method, the provision is optional [@CL-EST-1012]. The agency's own regulations say which method it uses [@CL-EST-1015]. The Brand Name or Equal provision depends on one test: the solicitation includes brand name or equal purchase descriptions [@CL-EST-1010].

The policy on brand name or equal descriptions is in section 11.104. While performance specifications are preferred to encourage offerors to propose innovative solutions, brand name or equal purchase descriptions may be advantageous under certain circumstances [@CL-EST-2139]. Such a description must include, in addition to the brand name, a general description of those salient physical, functional, or performance characteristics of the brand name item that an “equal” item must meet to be acceptable for award [@CL-EST-2140]. The descriptions are to be used when the salient characteristics are firm requirements, and the text names no official who uses them [@CL-EST-2141].

For what the two provisions say once they are in a solicitation, see [Material requirements and brand name or equal](../../supply-chain/sourcing-evidence/sup-material-requirements-and-brand-name-or-equal.md) and [Examining specifications and proposing alternatives to Government-unique standards](est-specification-examination-and-alternative-standards.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An agency buys grounds maintenance for 12 sites. The description reserves acceptance decisions to Government officials [@CL-EST-1007]. It requires crew badges when crew members attend meetings with building tenants, because the agency judges that failing to identify them could do harm [@CL-EST-1008]. It requires the contract number on every page of the monthly report as suitable marking [@CL-EST-1009]. The description calls for one brand of mower "or equal", so it is a brand name or equal purchase description and the solicitation includes the Brand Name or Equal provision [@CL-EST-1010]. If the solicitation also used a Government-unique standard and the agency reported by the categorical method, inserting the provision on alternatives to Government-unique standards would be optional [@CL-EST-1011; @CL-EST-1012].

## Never confuse

The two provisions have different triggers. The Brand Name or Equal provision follows brand name or equal descriptions [@CL-EST-1010]. The provision on alternatives to Government-unique standards follows both the use of Government-unique standards and the transaction-based reporting method [@CL-EST-1011].

The transaction-based method goes with contract-by-contract specification management, and the categorical method with central management [@CL-EST-1013; @CL-EST-1014].

The exception to the identification duty is the agency's judgment that no harm can come from failing to identify, not the contractor's [@CL-EST-1008].

## Company calibration

The company must confirm, for every service solicitation it bids, which of the listed purchase-description requirements the agency wrote in and whether the Brand Name or Equal provision is present. [@Q-004]
