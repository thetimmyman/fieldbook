## Purpose

Sets out the firm price and the adjustable price, what an adjustable price may contain, when the ceiling or target price may be adjusted, the duty of the contracting officer when acquiring commercial products and commercial services, and which contracts are not fixed-price.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-EST-0956] |
| Codified citation | FAR 16.201 [@CL-EST-0956] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-EST-0956] |
| Applies to | Fixed-price contracts, including those with an adjustable price [@CL-EST-0956; @CL-EST-0957] |

Fixed-price types of contracts provide for a firm price or, in appropriate cases, an adjustable price [@CL-EST-0956]. Fixed-price contracts providing for an adjustable price may include a ceiling price, a target price (including target cost), or both [@CL-EST-0957].

Unless otherwise specified in the contract, the ceiling price or target price is subject to adjustment only by operation of contract clauses providing for equitable adjustment or other revision of the contract price under stated circumstances [@CL-EST-0958].

The contracting officer shall use firm-fixed-price or fixed-price with economic price adjustment contracts when acquiring commercial products and commercial services, except as provided in 12.207(b) [@CL-EST-0959]. The duty falls on the contracting officer [@CL-EST-0959].

Time-and-materials contracts and labor-hour contracts are not fixed-price contracts [@CL-EST-0960].

## How it works

A fixed-price contract provides for a firm price or, in appropriate cases, an adjustable price [@CL-EST-0956]. An adjustable price may include a ceiling price, a target price (including target cost), or both [@CL-EST-0957]. The ceiling or target price moves only by operation of contract clauses that provide for equitable adjustment or other revision of the contract price under stated circumstances, unless the contract specifies otherwise [@CL-EST-0958]. The contract can therefore specify a different rule [@CL-EST-0958].

For commercial products and commercial services, the contracting officer shall use firm-fixed-price or fixed-price with economic price adjustment contracts, except as provided in 12.207(b) [@CL-EST-0959]. Time-and-materials and labor-hour contracts are outside the fixed-price category [@CL-EST-0960]. For how the choice of type is framed, see [selecting contract types](est-selecting-contract-types.md). For one adjustable-price form, see [fixed-price incentive contracts](est-fixed-price-incentive-and-letter-contracts.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A fixed-price contract for non-commercial supplies provides for an adjustable price and includes a target price of 800,000 and a ceiling price of 900,000, which an adjustable-price contract may do [@CL-EST-0957]. Costs then rise by 60,000. Unless the contract specifies otherwise, the rise alone does not move the target or ceiling price. Each is subject to adjustment only by operation of contract clauses providing for equitable adjustment or other revision of the contract price under stated circumstances [@CL-EST-0958]. If the contract does specify otherwise, that specification governs [@CL-EST-0958]. For a different purchase of commercial services, the contracting officer shall use firm-fixed-price or fixed-price with economic price adjustment unless 12.207(b) provides otherwise [@CL-EST-0959]. A labor-hour contract at 75 per hour would not be a fixed-price contract [@CL-EST-0960].

## Never confuse

Under a firm-fixed-price contract, the price is not subject to adjustment on the basis of the contractor's cost experience in performing the contract, placing maximum risk and full responsibility for all costs and resulting profit or loss on the contractor [@CL-EST-0956; @CL-EST-1150], and under an adjustable fixed-price contract, any ceiling price or target price moves, unless the contract specifies otherwise, only by operation of contract clauses providing for equitable adjustment or other revision of the contract price under stated circumstances [@CL-EST-0957; @CL-EST-0958]—unlike cost-reimbursement contracts, which provide for payment of allowable incurred costs to the extent prescribed in the contract within an estimated total-cost ceiling that the contractor may not exceed, except at its own risk, without the contracting officer's approval [@CL-CLS-0567; @CL-CLS-0680].
