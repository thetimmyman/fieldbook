## Purpose

Shows what an undefinitized contract action is under DFARS, how its definitization schedule is limited by the date of a qualifying proposal and by funds obligated, and what happens if the qualifying proposal is late.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-EST-1587] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 217.7401, DFARS 217.7404 and DFARS 217.7404-3 [@CL-EST-1587; @CL-EST-1588; @CL-EST-1589] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-EST-1587] |
| Applies to | Undefinitized contract actions [@CL-EST-1587] |

An undefinitized contract action is any contract action for which the contract terms, specifications, or price are not agreed upon before performance is begun under the action [@CL-EST-1587]. Undefinitized contract actions shall contain definitization schedules that provide for definitization by the earlier of two listed dates, one being 180 days after the contractor submits a qualifying proposal [@CL-EST-1589]. The other listed date is the date on which the amount of funds obligated under the contract action is equal to more than 50 percent of the not-to-exceed price [@CL-EST-1590]. The 180-day date may not be extended beyond an additional 90 days without a written determination by the head of the contracting activity without power of redelegation, the commander of the combatant command concerned, or the Under Secretary of Defense for Acquisition and Sustainment, that it is in the best interests of the military department or the defense agency, the combatant command, or the Department of Defense, respectively, to continue the action [@CL-EST-1645; @CL-EST-1322].

An undefinitized contract action with a value greater than $50 million may not be unilaterally definitized until the listed requirements are all met, and the first is the earlier of two listed dates, one being the end of the 180-day period beginning on the date the contractor submits a qualifying proposal to definitize the contractual terms, specifications, and price [@CL-EST-1588; @CL-EST-9506; @CL-EST-9501]. The second of those two dates is the date on which the amount of funds expended under the contractual action is equal to more than 50 percent of the negotiated overall not-to-exceed price for the contractual action [@CL-EST-1646; @CL-EST-1588]. Another listed requirement is that the head of the contracting activity, without power of redelegation, approves the definitization in writing [@CL-EST-1647; @CL-EST-1588]. Another is that the contracting officer provides a copy of the written approval to the contractor [@CL-EST-9501], and the last is that a period of 30 calendar days has elapsed after the written approval is provided to the contractor [@CL-EST-1634; @CL-EST-9501]. If the contractor does not submit a qualifying proposal in accordance with the contract definitization schedule, the contracting officer may, notwithstanding FAR 52.216-26, Payments of Allowable Costs Before Definitization, withhold an amount necessary to protect the interests of the Government [@CL-EST-1591], and the amount is not to exceed 5 percent of all subsequent financing requests [@CL-EST-1592]. The text also lets the contracting officer take other appropriate actions, which this card does not draft.

## How it works

Two measures run the schedule: the time after a qualifying proposal and the share of the not-to-exceed price that has been obligated [@CL-EST-1589; @CL-EST-1590]. The limit on unilateral definitization for large actions and the withholding cap for a missed qualifying proposal sit on top of the schedule [@CL-EST-1588; @CL-EST-1591; @CL-EST-1592]. The text also limits how far the first date can be extended [@CL-EST-1645]. The printed text also defines definitization and a qualifying proposal, and lists further conditions for foreign military sales and for unilateral definitization; this card does not draft those points.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An undefinitized contract action has a not-to-exceed price of 40,000,000 and a value of 40,000,000. The contractor submits a qualifying proposal on day zero. By day 100 the obligated funds reach 22,000,000. The answer: obligated funds are more than half of the not-to-exceed price by then, so the earlier of the two dates is the date the obligations passed half, which came no later than day 100 and before the 180-day date. The value is below 50,000,000, so the limit on unilateral definitization for larger actions does not apply.

## Never confuse

The unilateral-definitization limit counts funds expended, while the schedule counts funds obligated [@CL-EST-1646; @CL-EST-1590]. The FAR letter-contract schedule runs from the date of the letter contract and from the share of work completed [@CL-EST-1573], while the DFARS schedule here runs from a qualifying proposal and from funds obligated [@CL-EST-1589; @CL-EST-1590]. The definitization clause for letter contracts is on [Contract definitization for letter contracts](../../contract-finance/funding/ctf-contract-definitization.md), and the FAR rules on letter contracts are on [Fixed-price incentive contracts and letter contracts: description, use and clauses](est-fixed-price-incentive-and-letter-contracts.md).
