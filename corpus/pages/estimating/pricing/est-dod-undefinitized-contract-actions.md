## Purpose

Shows what an undefinitized contract action is under DFARS, how its definitization schedule is limited by the date of a qualifying proposal and by funds obligated, and what happens if the qualifying proposal is late.

An undefinitized contract action is any contract action for which the contract terms, specifications, or price are not agreed upon before performance is begun under the action [@CL-EST-1587]. Undefinitized contract actions shall contain definitization schedules that provide for definitization by the earlier of two listed dates, one being 180 days after the contractor submits a qualifying proposal [@CL-EST-1589]. The other listed date is the date on which the amount of funds obligated under the contract action is equal to more than 50 percent of the not-to-exceed price [@CL-EST-1590]. The 180-day date may not be extended beyond an additional 90 days without a written determination by the head of the contracting activity without power of redelegation, the commander of the combatant command concerned, or the Under Secretary of Defense for Acquisition and Sustainment, that it is in the best interests of the military department or the defense agency, the combatant command, or the Department of Defense, respectively, to continue the action [@CL-EST-1645; @CL-EST-1322].

An undefinitized contract action with a value greater than $50 million may not be unilaterally definitized until the listed requirements are all met, and the first is the earlier of two listed dates, one being the end of the 180-day period beginning on the date the contractor submits a qualifying proposal to definitize the contractual terms, specifications, and price [@CL-EST-1588; @CL-EST-9506; @CL-EST-9501]. The second of those two dates is the date on which the amount of funds expended under the contractual action is equal to more than 50 percent of the negotiated overall not-to-exceed price for the contractual action [@CL-EST-1646; @CL-EST-1588]. Another listed requirement is that the head of the contracting activity, without power of redelegation, approves the definitization in writing [@CL-EST-1647; @CL-EST-1588]. Another is that the contracting officer provides a copy of the written approval to the contractor [@CL-EST-9501], and the last is that a period of 30 calendar days has elapsed after the written approval is provided to the contractor [@CL-EST-1634; @CL-EST-9501]. If the contractor does not submit a qualifying proposal in accordance with the contract definitization schedule, the contracting officer may, notwithstanding FAR 52.216-26, Payments of Allowable Costs Before Definitization, withhold an amount necessary to protect the interests of the Government [@CL-EST-1591], not to exceed 5 percent of all subsequent financing requests [@CL-EST-1592], or take other appropriate actions, such as documenting the noncompliance in the contractor's past performance evaluation or terminating the contract for default [@CL-EST-9665].

## How it works

Two measures run the schedule: the time after a qualifying proposal and the share of the not-to-exceed price that has been obligated [@CL-EST-1589; @CL-EST-1590]. Separately, an undefinitized contract action with a value greater than $50 million may not be unilaterally definitized until the listed requirements are met [@CL-EST-1588], and a missed qualifying proposal may lead to a withholding not to exceed 5 percent of all subsequent financing requests [@CL-EST-1591; @CL-EST-1592]. The 180-day date may not be extended beyond an additional 90 days without a written determination [@CL-EST-1645]. Definitization means the agreement on, or determination of, contract terms, specifications, and price, which converts the undefinitized contract action to a definitive contract [@CL-EST-9666]. A qualifying proposal is a proposal that contains sufficient information to enable DoD to conduct meaningful analyses and audits of the information contained in the proposal [@CL-MKT-1698].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An undefinitized contract action has a not-to-exceed price of 40,000,000 and a value of 40,000,000. The contractor submits a qualifying proposal on day zero. By day 100 the obligated funds reach 22,000,000. The answer: obligated funds are more than half of the not-to-exceed price by then, so the earlier of the two dates is the date the obligations passed half, which came no later than day 100 and before the 180-day date. The value is below 50,000,000, so the limit on unilateral definitization for larger actions does not apply.

## Never confuse

The unilateral-definitization limit counts funds expended, while the schedule counts funds obligated [@CL-EST-1646; @CL-EST-1590]. The FAR letter-contract schedule provides for definitization within 180 days after the date of the letter contract or before completion of 40 percent of the work to be performed, whichever occurs first [@CL-EST-1573], while the DFARS schedule provides for definitization by the earlier of 180 days after the contractor submits a qualifying proposal and the date on which the amount of funds obligated is equal to more than 50 percent of the not-to-exceed price [@CL-EST-1589; @CL-EST-1590]. The definitization clause for letter contracts is on [Contract definitization for letter contracts](../../contract-finance/funding/ctf-contract-definitization.md), and the FAR rules on letter contracts are on [Fixed-price incentive contracts and letter contracts: description, use and clauses](est-fixed-price-incentive-and-letter-contracts.md).
