## Purpose

Shows what data other than certified cost or pricing data is, when certified data is not required, and what an auditor may examine as opposed to what can only be reviewed by agreed-upon procedures.

Data other than certified cost or pricing data is pricing data, cost estimates, sales records and judgmental explanations needed to determine price reasonableness or cost realism when certified data is not required [@CL-EST-0668]. It may look identical to certified data, such as labor rates, bills of material and overhead cost models, and the primary difference is that it is not certified [@CL-EST-0669]. The manual restates that certified data is not required below the simplified acquisition threshold or where a statutory exception applies [@CL-EST-0670]; the exceptions themselves are set out in [Certified data exceptions](est-certified-data-exceptions.md).

Auditors cannot perform examinations or render formal opinions on proposals supported solely by sales or pricing data, because there are no objective, measurable attestation criteria under current FAR standards [@CL-EST-0671; @CL-EST-0672]. They can instead provide agreed-upon procedures to assist the contracting officer [@CL-EST-0672], and because that effort varies from procurement to procurement they need to communicate with the requestor before starting the engagement to ensure an understanding of the procedures [@CL-EST-0909].

## How it works

Auditors may perform examinations on proposals supported by cost data, and the volume and quality of the data decide the type of opinion [@CL-EST-0673]. Before starting fieldwork the auditor must coordinate with the contracting officer to assess risk, evaluate the data level required, and help decide whether an examination, agreed-upon procedures or advisory services best serve the procurement [@CL-EST-0674]. The deciding line is the data behind the proposal: cost data can be examined, while for sales or pricing data alone the manual points to agreed-upon procedures rather than an examination [@CL-EST-0672; @CL-EST-0673]. The auditor also evaluates whether the submitted data is adequate for an examination, recognizing that deficiencies may come from the contractor missing solicitation requirements or from the contracting officer asking for too little data [@CL-EST-0910]. The auditor applies professional judgment, advises the contracting officer if required information was not provided, or recommends that the contracting officer solicit more data to protect the Government's interest [@CL-EST-0911].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An offeror supports a commercial item price only with its catalog sales history. The auditor cannot examine that proposal or give a formal opinion, so the contracting officer asks for agreed-upon procedures that test the sales records. Another offeror supplies a cost build-up. The auditor can examine it, and the opinion depends on how complete the data is.

## Never confuse

The same kinds of data can appear in both kinds of proposal, the difference being the certification [@CL-EST-0669]. What changes the audit service here is the type of support, cost data or sales and pricing data [@CL-EST-0672; @CL-EST-0673]. The certified data rules are in [Certified cost or pricing data](est-certified-cost-or-pricing-data.md).
