## Purpose

Defines the proposal terms used in the negotiated acquisition part of the FAR, says how the request for proposals is treated in a sole source and in a competitive negotiated acquisition, and explains the best value continuum.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-EST-2726] |
| Codified citation | FAR 15.001, FAR 15.002 and FAR 15.101 [@CL-EST-2726; @CL-EST-2731; @CL-EST-2734] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 15.001 [@CL-EST-3312; @CL-EST-3313; @CL-EST-3314; @CL-EST-3315]; RFO FAR 15.002 [@CL-EST-3316; @CL-EST-3317; @CL-EST-3318]; RFO FAR 15.103 [@CL-EST-3319; @CL-EST-3320] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-EST-2726] |
| Applies to | Negotiated acquisitions, in a sole source or a competitive environment [@CL-EST-2731; @CL-EST-2732; @CL-EST-2734] |

The terms below are defined as used in FAR part 15 [@CL-EST-2776]. A proposal modification is a change made to a proposal before the solicitation closing date and time, or made in response to an amendment, or made to correct a mistake at any time before award [@CL-EST-2727]. A proposal revision is a change made after the solicitation closing date, at the request of or as allowed by a contracting officer, as the result of negotiations [@CL-EST-2728]. A deficiency is a material failure of a proposal to meet a Government requirement, or a combination of significant weaknesses in a proposal that increases the risk of unsuccessful contract performance to an unacceptable level [@CL-EST-2726]. A weakness is a flaw in the proposal that increases the risk of unsuccessful contract performance, and a significant weakness is a flaw that appreciably increases that risk [@CL-EST-2729; @CL-EST-2730].

In a sole source environment, the request for proposals should be tailored to remove unnecessary information and requirements, such as evaluation criteria and voluminous proposal preparation instructions [@CL-EST-2731]. The text says should, not shall [@CL-EST-2731]. In a competitive environment, the procedures of this part are intended to minimize the complexity of the solicitation, the evaluation and the source selection decision, while maintaining a process designed to foster an impartial and comprehensive evaluation of offerors' proposals, leading to selection of the proposal representing the best value to the Government [@CL-EST-2732; @CL-EST-2733].

## How it works

An agency can obtain best value in negotiated acquisitions by using any one or a combination of source selection approaches [@CL-EST-2734]. The relative importance of cost or price may vary between acquisitions [@CL-EST-2735]. Where the requirement is clearly definable and the risk of unsuccessful contract performance is minimal, cost or price may play a dominant role in source selection [@CL-EST-2735]. The less definitive the requirement, the more development work required, or the greater the performance risk, the more technical or past performance considerations may play a dominant role [@CL-EST-2736]. The text on the continuum says can and may, not shall [@CL-EST-2734; @CL-EST-2735; @CL-EST-2736].

The label for a change to a proposal depends on its timing and cause: a revision also depends on the contracting officer's request or allowance and on negotiations [@CL-EST-2727; @CL-EST-2728]. Who the source selection authority is, and how proposals are evaluated, are on [Source selection responsibilities and evaluation](est-source-selection-responsibilities-and-evaluation.md). The tradeoff approach and the lowest price technically acceptable approach each have their own cards: [Tradeoff and prenegotiation objectives](../../market/programs/mkt-tradeoff-and-prenegotiation-objectives.md) and [Lowest price technically acceptable source selection](est-lpta-source-selection.md).

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 15 text in place of the codified text under Class Deviation 2026-O0048, whose Revision 1 took effect immediately and supersedes the March 5, 2026 version [@CL-EST-3176] [@CL-EST-3177]. RFO FAR 15.001 defines a proposal modification as a change made to a proposal before the RFP closing date and time, or made in response to an amendment, or made to correct a mistake at any time before award [@CL-EST-3313], which matches the codified definition apart from RFP closing date and time in place of solicitation closing date and time [@CL-EST-2727]. RFO FAR 15.001 defines a proposal revision as a change to material elements of a proposal made after the RFP closing date, at the request of or as allowed by a contracting officer, as the result of negotiations [@CL-EST-3314], where the codified definition says a change to a proposal made after the solicitation closing date [@CL-EST-2728]. RFO FAR 15.001 defines a deficiency as any part of an offer that does not conform to a material requirement of a RFP, and a material requirement as one that affects price, quantity, quality, or delivery, or that the RFP requires to be met at the time of proposal submission [@CL-EST-3312], where the codified definition is a material failure of a proposal to meet a Government requirement or a combination of significant weaknesses that increases the risk of unsuccessful contract performance to an unacceptable level [@CL-EST-2726]. RFO FAR 15.001 defines a weakness as a flaw in the proposal that increases the risk of unsuccessful contract performance, and a significant weakness as a flaw that appreciably increases that risk [@CL-EST-3315], as the codified definitions do [@CL-EST-2729; @CL-EST-2730]. RFO FAR 15.002(a) says that when an award is made to a single vendor without soliciting offers from multiple sources, agencies should remove unnecessary information and requirements from the request for proposal, including voluminous proposal preparation instructions and evaluation factors [@CL-EST-3316], where the codified text says the request for proposals should be tailored to remove unnecessary information and requirements in a sole source environment, such as evaluation criteria and voluminous proposal preparation instructions [@CL-EST-2731]. RFO FAR 15.002(b) says that in a competitive acquisition agencies should tailor the complexity of the RFP, evaluation, and source selection decision to the circumstances of the acquisition [@CL-EST-3317], while maintaining a process that promotes an impartial and comprehensive evaluation of proposals, leading to selection of the proposal representing the best value to the Government [@CL-EST-3318], where the codified text says the procedures of the part are intended to minimize that complexity [@CL-EST-2732; @CL-EST-2733]. RFO FAR 15.103 says agencies can obtain best value in competitive negotiated acquisitions by using any one or a combination of source selection approaches, including but not limited to the approaches mentioned in that section [@CL-EST-3319], where the codified sentence speaks of negotiated acquisitions [@CL-EST-2734]. RFO FAR 15.103 also says that agencies use a range of approaches for evaluating competitive offers, where the importance of cost or price varies relative to other factors such as technical merit and past performance [@CL-EST-3320], where the codified text gives examples in which cost or price may play a dominant role [@CL-EST-2735] and in which technical or past performance considerations may [@CL-EST-2736].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An offeror corrects a price sheet five days before the closing date and time. That change was made before the solicitation closing date and time, so it is a proposal modification [@CL-EST-2727]. Ten days after closing, during negotiations, the contracting officer asks the offeror to change its staffing plan. That change was made after the closing date, at the contracting officer's request, as the result of negotiations, so it is a proposal revision [@CL-EST-2728].

An evaluator finds that one proposal omits a required test. If that is a material failure to meet a Government requirement, the proposal has a deficiency [@CL-EST-2726]. Another proposal has three significant weaknesses that together raise the risk of unsuccessful performance to an unacceptable level, so the combination is also a deficiency [@CL-EST-2726]. Both deficiency examples follow the codified definition [@CL-EST-2726]; the RFO definition of a deficiency reads differently [@CL-EST-3312].

For a clearly defined purchase of routine supplies with minimal performance risk, cost or price may play a dominant role [@CL-EST-2735]. For a new development effort with greater performance risk, technical or past performance considerations may play a dominant role [@CL-EST-2736].

## Never confuse

A proposal modification is not a proposal revision. A modification can be made before the closing date and time, in response to an amendment, or to correct a mistake before award, while a revision is made after the closing date, at the request of or as allowed by a contracting officer, as the result of negotiations [@CL-EST-2727; @CL-EST-2728].

Under the codified text, a weakness is not a deficiency. The codified definition of a deficiency lists a material failure to meet a Government requirement or a combination of significant weaknesses that raises the risk to an unacceptable level, and it does not list a single weakness [@CL-EST-2726; @CL-EST-2729; @CL-EST-2730]. In the RFO text, a deficiency is any part of an offer that does not conform to a material requirement of a RFP [@CL-EST-3312]. The best value continuum is codified FAR 15.101 [@CL-EST-2734] but RFO FAR 15.103 [@CL-EST-3319; @CL-EST-3320], and RFO FAR 15.101 holds early exchanges with industry [@CL-EST-3271].
