## Purpose

Shows how an offeror files a Disclosure Statement, where the copies and amendments go, and what filing does and does not settle.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified CAS [@CL-EST-2484] |
| Codified citation | FAR 30.202-5 [@CL-EST-2148], 48 CFR 9903.202-5, 9903.202-9, 9903.202-10 and 9903.303 [@CL-EST-2484; @CL-EST-9218; @CL-EST-9219; @CL-EST-2488] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-EST-2484] |
| Applies to | Offerors that file a Disclosure Statement [@CL-EST-2486] |

The FAR section on filing disclosure statements, 30.202-5, refers the reader to 48 CFR 9903.202-5 [@CL-EST-2148].

Disclosure must be on Form Number CASB DS-1 or CASB DS-2, as applicable [@CL-EST-2484]. The data that are required to be disclosed are set forth in detail in Form CASB-DS-1 [@CL-EST-9218], and the data required to be disclosed by educational institutions are set forth in detail in Form CASB DS-2 [@CL-EST-9219]; each section illustrates its form after that sentence. The forms may be obtained from the cognizant Federal agency, meaning the cognizant administrative contracting officer or the cognizant Federal agency official authorized to act in that capacity, or from the looseleaf version of the Federal Acquisition Regulation [@CL-EST-2629].

When a contractor requests it in advance, the cognizant Federal agency may authorize disclosure based on computer generated reproductions of the applicable form [@CL-EST-2485]. The request must come in advance, and the agency decides whether to authorize.

Offerors are required to file the original and one copy with the cognizant administrative contracting officer or the cognizant Federal agency official acting in that capacity, and one copy with the cognizant Federal auditor [@CL-EST-2486].

Amendments and revisions are submitted to the administrative contracting officer or agency official acting in that capacity, as applicable, and the Federal auditor of the currently cognizant Federal agency [@CL-EST-2487].

## How it works

A disclosure of a cost accounting practice does not determine the allowability of particular items of cost [@CL-EST-2488]. Whatever practices the contractor has disclosed, whether and to what extent a specific element of cost is allowed under a contract remains for consideration in each specific instance [@CL-EST-2630].

The determination of allowability remains a responsibility of the contracting officers under the applicable procurement regulations [@CL-EST-2489].

The individual Disclosure Statement may be used in audits of contracts or in negotiation of prices leading to contracts [@CL-EST-2490]. The authority of the audit agencies and the contracting officers is in no way abrogated by the material the contractor presents in the statement [@CL-EST-2631].

Disclosures must be complete and accurate, because the practices disclosed may have a significant impact on the ways the contractor will be required to comply with the Cost Accounting Standards [@CL-EST-2491].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An offeror prepares its Disclosure Statement on the applicable form, which in this example is Form CASB DS-1. It files the original and one copy with the cognizant administrative contracting officer and one copy with the cognizant Federal auditor [@CL-EST-2484; @CL-EST-2486]. Before filing, it asked the cognizant Federal agency to authorize a computer generated reproduction of the form, and it uses the reproduction only because the agency authorized it [@CL-EST-2485].

Later the offeror changes a pool base. It submits the amendment to the administrative contracting officer or agency official and to the Federal auditor of the currently cognizant Federal agency [@CL-EST-2487].

The statement lists the pool, but that does not settle whether any cost in the pool is allowable. The contracting officer keeps that determination [@CL-EST-2488; @CL-EST-2489]. The statement may be used in audits of contracts or in negotiation of prices, and neither the audit agencies nor the contracting officers lose authority because of it [@CL-EST-2490; @CL-EST-2631].

## Never confuse

Filing is not a determination that the disclosed costs are allowable, and it does not move the allowability question away from the contracting officer [@CL-EST-2488; @CL-EST-2489].

Nor is a statement a waiver of audit or negotiation: auditors and contracting officers keep their authority [@CL-EST-2631].
