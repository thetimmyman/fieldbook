## Purpose

Explains the contract type risk profit factor and the working capital adjustment: when the adjustment applies, how the formula is built, and the ceiling it cannot pass.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-EST-0780] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 215.404-71-3 [@CL-EST-0780] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-EST-0780] |
| Applies to | Fixed-price contracts that provide for progress payments, for the working capital adjustment [@CL-EST-0780] |

The contract type risk factor focuses on the degree of cost risk accepted by the contractor under varying contract types [@CL-EST-0867]. The working capital adjustment is added to the profit objective for contract type risk, and it only applies to fixed-price contracts that provide for progress payments [@CL-EST-0780]. It uses a formula approach yet is not an exact calculation of the cost of working capital [@CL-EST-0781].

The computed amount is the working capital adjustment, and it may not exceed 4 percent of the contract costs in Block 20 [@CL-EST-0782].

Cost-plus contracts do not receive the working capital adjustment [@CL-EST-0784]. Where a substantial portion of the costs is incurred before definitization, the contracting officer may assign contract type risk as low as zero percent regardless of contract type [@CL-EST-0785]. If a contractor submits a qualifying proposal to definitize an undefinitized contract action and the contracting officer definitizes after the end of the 180-day period beginning on the submission date, the profit allowed must accurately reflect the cost risk as it existed on the submission date [@CL-EST-0870; @CL-EST-0873]. The reason for the specific contract type risk value, including reduced cost risk in the undefinitized period, goes into the price negotiation memorandum [@CL-EST-0786].

## How it works

Costs financed equal total costs multiplied by the portion of costs financed by the contractor [@CL-EST-0869]. That portion is generally the part not covered by progress payments, that is, 100 percent minus the customary progress payment rate [@CL-EST-0783]. On the form, the working capital block lists costs financed, length factor, interest rate and profit objective [@CL-EST-0868]. The adjustment is found by multiplying the costs financed, length factor and interest rate entries [@CL-EST-0782; @CL-EST-0868].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A fixed-price contract with progress payments has total contract costs of 1,000,000 and an 80 percent progress payment rate, so the contractor finances 20 percent, or 200,000. A contract length factor of 0.90 and an interest rate of 5 percent give an adjustment of 9,000, well under the 4 percent ceiling of 40,000.

## Never confuse

The working capital adjustment is not the contract type risk value itself: it is an amount added to the profit objective for contract type risk [@CL-EST-0780]. It is not an exact calculation of the cost of working capital, and it is capped [@CL-EST-0781; @CL-EST-0782]. It is also unavailable on cost-plus contracts [@CL-EST-0784]. Contract type risk is one of the four weighted guidelines factors; see [DoD weighted guidelines: general policy and performance risk](est-weighted-guidelines-general-and-performance-risk.md).

## Company calibration

That the working capital adjustment is computed only on eligible contract types, and that the price negotiation memorandum carries the contract type risk value and any undefinitized period. [@Q-012]
