## Purpose

Explains the contract type risk profit factor and the working capital adjustment: when the adjustment applies, how the formula is built, and the ceiling it cannot pass.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-EST-0780] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 215.404-71-3 [@CL-EST-0780] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-EST-0780] |
| Applies to | Fixed-price contracts that provide for progress payments, for the working capital adjustment [@CL-EST-0780] |

The contract type risk factor focuses on the degree of cost risk accepted by the contractor under varying contract types [@CL-EST-0867]. The working capital adjustment is added to the profit objective for contract type risk, and it only applies to fixed-price contracts that provide for progress payments [@CL-EST-0780]. Though it uses a formula approach, it is not intended to be an exact calculation of the cost of working capital [@CL-EST-0781].

The computed amount is the working capital adjustment, and it may not exceed 4 percent of the contract costs in Block 20 [@CL-EST-0782].

Cost-plus contracts shall not receive the working capital adjustment [@CL-EST-0784]. Where a substantial portion of the costs is incurred before definitization, the contracting officer may assign contract type risk as low as zero percent regardless of contract type [@CL-EST-0785]. However, if a contractor submits a qualifying proposal to definitize an undefinitized contract action and the contracting officer for such action definitizes the contract after the end of the 180-day period beginning on the date on which the contractor submitted the qualifying proposal, the profit allowed on the contract shall accurately reflect the cost risk of the contractor as such risk existed on the date the contractor submitted the qualifying proposal [@CL-EST-0870; @CL-EST-0873]. Contracting officers shall document in the price negotiation memorandum the reason for assigning a specific contract type risk value, to include the extent to which any reduced cost risk during the undefinitized period of performance was considered in determining the negotiation objective [@CL-EST-0786].

## How it works

Costs financed equal total costs multiplied by the portion of costs financed by the contractor [@CL-EST-0869]. That portion is generally the part not covered by progress payments, that is, 100 percent minus the customary progress payment rate [@CL-EST-0783]. On the form, the working capital block lists costs financed, length factor, interest rate and profit objective [@CL-EST-0868]. The adjustment is found by multiplying entry (5) by entry (6) by entry (7) [@CL-EST-0782]. Entry (5) is the amount computed per paragraph (e) and entry (6) is the appropriate figure from paragraph (f) [@CL-EST-9717], and entry (7) uses the interest rate established by the Secretary of the Treasury, with no other interest rate to be used [@CL-EST-9718].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A fixed-price contract with progress payments has total contract costs of 1,000,000 and an 80 percent progress payment rate, so the contractor finances 20 percent, or 200,000. A contract length factor of 0.90 and an interest rate of 5 percent give an adjustment of 9,000, well under the 4 percent ceiling of 40,000.

## Never confuse

The working capital adjustment is an adjustment added to the profit objective for contract type risk [@CL-EST-0780]. It is not intended to be an exact calculation of the cost of working capital [@CL-EST-0781], and it shall not exceed 4 percent of the contract costs in Block 20 [@CL-EST-0782]. Cost-plus contracts shall not receive it [@CL-EST-0784]. The weighted guidelines method focuses on four profit factors, one of which is contract type risk [@CL-EST-0773]; see [DoD weighted guidelines: general policy and performance risk](est-weighted-guidelines-general-and-performance-risk.md).

## Company calibration

That the working capital adjustment is computed only on eligible contract types, and that the price negotiation memorandum carries the contract type risk value and any undefinitized period. [@Q-012]
