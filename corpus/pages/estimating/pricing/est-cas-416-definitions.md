## Purpose

Gives the meanings that the Standard on accounting for insurance costs assigns to actual cash value, insurance administration expenses, projected average loss, self-insurance and self-insurance charge. The Standard's requirements are on the [insurance card](../../compliance/cas-allowability/cmp-cas-416-insurance.md).

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|

Self-insurance means the assumption or retention of the risk or loss by the contractor, whether voluntarily or involuntarily, and it includes the deductible portion of purchased insurance [@CL-EST-2895]. A self-insurance charge means a cost which represents the projected average loss under a self-insurance plan [@CL-EST-2896]. Projected average loss means the estimated long-term average loss per period for periods of comparable exposure to risk of loss [@CL-EST-2894].

Insurance administration expenses means the contractor's costs of administering an insurance program, such as the costs of operating an insurance or risk-management department, processing claims, actuarial fees, and service fee paid to insurance companies, trustees or technical consultants [@CL-EST-2893]. Actual cash value means the cost of replacing damaged property with other property of like kind and quality in the physical condition of the property immediately before the damage [@CL-EST-2892].

Other terms defined elsewhere in part 99 have the meanings given in those definitions unless paragraph (b) of the section requires otherwise [@CL-EST-2964]. Paragraph (b) applies no modification to those terms for this Standard [@CL-EST-2965].

## How it works

A self-insurance charge is defined by the projected average loss, and the projected average loss is a long-term average per period for periods of comparable exposure to risk of loss [@CL-EST-2896; @CL-EST-2894]. Self-insurance covers retention of risk whether voluntary or involuntary, and it reaches the deductible portion of purchased insurance [@CL-EST-2895]. Insurance administration expenses are the contractor's costs of administering an insurance program [@CL-EST-2893]. Actual cash value is measured by replacing damaged property with property of like kind and quality in the condition the property was in immediately before the damage [@CL-EST-2892]. The measurement and allocation requirements are on the main card.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor buys a policy with a 200,000 deductible. The deductible portion is self-insurance, because the contractor retains that risk of loss [@CL-EST-2895]. The contractor estimates the long-term average loss per period inside the deductible layer for periods of comparable exposure at 60,000, which is a projected average loss [@CL-EST-2894]. A cost that represents that 60,000 under its self-insurance plan is a self-insurance charge [@CL-EST-2896]. Running the risk-management department and processing claims costs a further 300,000, which is insurance administration expenses [@CL-EST-2893]. A press destroyed in a fire was eight years old. Its actual cash value is what replacing it with a press of like kind and quality in that eight-year-old condition would cost, for example 30,000, not the 100,000 price of a new press [@CL-EST-2892].

## Never confuse

In CAS 416, self-insurance is the contractor's retention of risk or loss—including purchased insurance deductibles—and a self-insurance charge is the projected average loss under a self-insurance plan [@CL-EST-2895; @CL-EST-2896], rather than an unprojected realized catastrophe loss or the replacement measure of actual cash value [@CL-EST-2892].

## Company calibration

Confirm which risks the company retains, including deductibles, and how the self-insurance charge is projected. [@Q-012]
