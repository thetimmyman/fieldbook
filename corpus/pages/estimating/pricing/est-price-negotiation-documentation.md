## Purpose

Shows what the price negotiation memorandum must record and which estimate records it relies on.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-EST-1258; @CL-EST-1259] |
| Codified citation | FAR 15.405 [@CL-EST-1258]; FAR 15.406-3 [@CL-EST-1259] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 15.407 [@CL-EST-3355]; RFO FAR 15.408-2 [@CL-EST-3356] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-EST-1259] |
| Applies to | Price negotiations documented in the contract file [@CL-EST-1259] |

The purpose of performing cost or price analysis is to develop a negotiation position that permits the contracting officer and the offeror an opportunity to reach agreement on a fair and reasonable price [@CL-EST-1258]. The contracting officer shall document in the contract file the principal elements of the negotiated agreement, and the documentation, for example a price negotiation memorandum, shall include listed items [@CL-EST-1259].

The listed items include a summary of the contractor's proposal, any field pricing assistance recommendations, including the reasons for any pertinent variances from them, the Government's negotiation objective, and the negotiated position [@CL-EST-1262]. Where the determination of a fair and reasonable price is based on cost analysis, the summary shall address each major cost element, and where it is based on price analysis, the summary shall include the source and type of data used to support the determination [@CL-EST-1263]. The listed items also include the current status of any contractor systems, such as purchasing, estimating, accounting and compensation, to the extent they affected and were considered in the negotiation [@CL-EST-1260].

## How it works

One listed item is the current status of any contractor systems, such as purchasing, estimating, accounting and compensation, to the extent they affected and were considered in the negotiation [@CL-EST-1260]. If certified cost or pricing data were not required in any price negotiation exceeding the certified cost or pricing data threshold, one listed item is the exception used and the basis for it [@CL-EST-1261]. Another listed item is a summary of the contractor's proposal, any field pricing assistance recommendations with the reasons for pertinent variances, the Government's negotiation objective, and the negotiated position [@CL-EST-1262]. Where the price determination rests on cost analysis, that summary addresses each major cost element, and where it rests on price analysis, the summary includes the source and type of data used to support the determination [@CL-EST-1263]. A further listed item is the basis for the profit or fee prenegotiation objective and the profit or fee negotiated [@CL-EST-1264].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 15 text in place of the codified text under Class Deviation 2026-O0048, whose Revision 1 took effect immediately and supersedes the March 5, 2026 version [@CL-EST-3176] [@CL-EST-3177]. RFO FAR 15.407(a) opens with the statement that the purpose of performing cost or price analysis is to develop a negotiation position that permits the contracting officer and the offeror an opportunity to reach agreement on a fair and reasonable price [@CL-EST-1258; @CL-EST-3355]. RFO FAR 15.408-2(a) keeps the duty to document in the contract file the principal elements of the negotiated agreement, with documentation such as a price negotiation memorandum including the listed items [@CL-EST-1259; @CL-EST-3356]. The list keeps the current status of contractor systems to the extent they affected and were considered in the negotiation [@CL-EST-1260; @CL-EST-3357], the exception used and the basis for it where certified cost or pricing data were not required above the threshold [@CL-EST-1261; @CL-EST-3358], the summary of the proposal, field pricing recommendations and reasons for variances, the negotiation objective and the negotiated position [@CL-EST-1262; @CL-EST-3359], and the basis for the profit or fee prenegotiation objective and the profit or fee negotiated [@CL-EST-1264; @CL-EST-3362]. The sentences on the cost analysis summary and the price analysis summary are now separate subparagraphs, (7)(i) for each major cost element [@CL-EST-1263; @CL-EST-3360] and (7)(ii) for the source and type of data used [@CL-EST-1263; @CL-EST-3361].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A company proposes a price built from labor, material and subcontract elements. The negotiation file summarizes the proposal and the field pricing recommendations, shows each major cost element, records the status of the company's estimating system to the extent it affected the negotiation, and notes the basis of the profit objective. The company should be able to reproduce the version of the estimate that the summary describes.

## Never confuse

The listed item records the basis for the profit or fee prenegotiation objective and the profit or fee negotiated [@CL-EST-1264]. The method for that objective is on [Profit analysis in cost-based price negotiations](est-profit-analysis.md) and, for DoD, on [DoD profit policy](est-dod-profit-structured-approaches.md). The exception used where certified data were not required is recorded here [@CL-EST-1261], while the exceptions themselves are on [DoD exceptions, waivers and limits](est-dod-certified-data-exceptions-and-waivers.md). Field pricing support, should-cost reviews and proposal analysis techniques are covered on [Field pricing support](est-field-pricing-support.md), [Should-cost review](est-should-cost-review.md) and [Proposal analysis techniques](est-proposal-analysis.md). The data the contracting officer asks for is on [Pricing policy and the order of preference for data](est-pricing-policy-data-preference.md).

RFO FAR 15.408-2(a)(7)(i) and (7)(ii) put the cost-element summary and the price-analysis data summary in their own subparagraphs [@CL-EST-3360; @CL-EST-3361], where codified FAR 15.406-3(a)(7) holds them in one paragraph [@CL-EST-1263].
