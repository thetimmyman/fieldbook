## Purpose

Shows the two DoD duties that sit alongside the general best value and tradeoff rules: the contracting officer must award contracts for tents and other temporary structures for the Armed Forces that provide the best value, and must consider using a tradeoff process when conducting a source selection for fuel for an overseas contingency operation [@CL-EST-2426; @CL-EST-2430].

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-EST-2426] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 215.101-70 [@CL-EST-2426]; DFARS 215.101-71 [@CL-EST-2430] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-EST-2426] |
| Applies to | Tents and other temporary structures acquired for use by the Armed Forces, and source selections for fuel for an overseas contingency operation that is expected to exceed the simplified acquisition threshold [@CL-EST-2426; @CL-EST-2430] |

When acquiring tents or other temporary structures for use by the Armed Forces, the contracting officer must award contracts that provide the best value [@CL-EST-2426]. Covered structures are nonpermanent buildings, including tactical shelters, nonpermanent modular or pre-fabricated buildings, and portable or relocatable buildings such as trailers or equipment configured for occupancy [@CL-EST-2427]. Determining best value includes consideration of the total life-cycle costs of the tents or structures, including the costs of any equipment, fuel or electricity needed to heat, cool or light them [@CL-EST-2428]. The requirements apply to any agency or department that acquires tents or other temporary structures on behalf of DoD [@CL-EST-2429].

When conducting a source selection for fuel that is for an overseas contingency operation and is expected to exceed the simplified acquisition threshold, the contracting officer must consider using a tradeoff process in accordance with FAR 15.101-1 [@CL-EST-2430]. In any such tradeoff process the contracting officer should consider using past performance, cost, anticorruption training and anticorruption compliance as evaluation factors [@CL-EST-2431]. If a tradeoff process was not considered, the contracting officer must, before the solicitation is issued, justify in writing why a tradeoff process was not considered and obtain approval by an official one level above the contracting officer, and that authority is not delegable [@CL-EST-2432]. The contracting officer must include the justification in the contract file [@CL-EST-2433].

## How it works

For structures, best value is judged with life-cycle cost in the determination, so the cost of the equipment, fuel and electricity needed to run the structure counts alongside the purchase price [@CL-EST-2428]. A buying office outside DoD that acquires structures on behalf of DoD follows the same rule [@CL-EST-2429].

For fuel, the contracting officer must consider using a tradeoff process, which is not the same as being required to use one, and the four listed factors are suggestions to consider rather than required factors [@CL-EST-2430; @CL-EST-2431]. Where a tradeoff process was not considered, the written justification states why, goes to an official one level above the contracting officer for approval before the solicitation issues, and is kept in the contract file [@CL-EST-2432; @CL-EST-2433].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A buying office compares two offers for relocatable shelters. Offer A costs 200,000 and needs 30,000 a year for fuel and electricity over five years of use. Offer B costs 260,000 and needs 10,000 a year. Life-cycle costs come to 350,000 for offer A and 310,000 for offer B, so the contracting officer considers those life-cycle costs, including the 40,000 difference, in the best value determination and does not judge best value on purchase price alone [@CL-EST-2428].

A contracting officer plans a source selection for fuel for an overseas contingency operation, expected to exceed the simplified acquisition threshold, and considers using a tradeoff process, with past performance, cost, anticorruption training and anticorruption compliance as the evaluation factors considered [@CL-EST-2430; @CL-EST-2431]. Had a tradeoff process not been considered, a written justification would have had to be approved one level above the contracting officer, with no delegation, before the solicitation issued, and placed in the contract file [@CL-EST-2432; @CL-EST-2433].

## Never confuse

The tent rule is not a preference for the cheaper structure, because life-cycle cost including energy is part of the best value determination [@CL-EST-2428]. The fuel rule is not a command to use a tradeoff process, because it requires the contracting officer to consider one and, where one was not considered, to justify that in writing [@CL-EST-2430; @CL-EST-2432]. The general source selection duties and the limits on lowest price technically acceptable awards are covered on their own cards and are not repeated here.

## Company calibration

That the program records a life-cycle comparison for shelter buys and a tradeoff justification for contingency fuel before the solicitation issues. [@Q-012]
