## Purpose

Shows how a proposal must distribute costs across supply line items, and what an offeror must disclose about uncompensated overtime in its proposal.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-EST-1720; @CL-EST-1722] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.215-14 and FAR 52.237-10 [@CL-EST-1720; @CL-EST-1722] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.215-14 [@CL-EST-0876; @CL-EST-0879; @CL-EST-9098] and RFO FAR 52.237-10 [@CL-EST-0881; @CL-EST-0882; @CL-EST-9100] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-EST-1720; @CL-EST-1722] |
| Applies to | Proposals for the negotiation of prices for items of supplies, and proposals with labor hours subject to an adjusted hourly rate [@CL-EST-1693; @CL-EST-1698] |

In the basic clause, any proposal for negotiating prices for items of supplies shall distribute costs within contracts on a basis that ensures that unit prices are in proportion to the items' base cost [@CL-EST-1693]. Any method of distributing costs to line items that distorts unit prices shall not be used; for example, distributing costs equally among line items is not acceptable except when there is little or no variation in base cost [@CL-EST-1694]. The clause's cost-distribution paragraph does not require submission of certified cost or pricing data that is not otherwise required by law or regulation [@CL-EST-1695].

In the basic clause, when requested by the contracting officer, the offeror or contractor shall also identify the supplies that it will not manufacture or to which it will not contribute significant value [@CL-EST-1696]. Alternate I, as prescribed in 15.408(f)(2), substitutes a paragraph (b) stating that the offeror or contractor shall also identify those supplies [@CL-EST-1721], whereas the basic paragraph (b) applies when requested by the Contracting Officer [@CL-EST-1696].

## How it works

Whenever there is uncompensated overtime, the adjusted hourly rate including uncompensated overtime, rather than the hourly rate, shall be applied to all proposed hours, whether regular or overtime [@CL-EST-1697]. The adjusted hourly rate including uncompensated overtime is the hourly rate for a 40-hour work week multiplied by 40 and then divided by the proposed hours per week, which include uncompensated overtime hours over and above the standard 40-hour work week [@CL-EST-1723]. All proposed labor hours subject to the adjusted hourly rate shall be identified as either regular or overtime hours, by labor categories, and described at the same level of detail [@CL-EST-1698]. The offeror shall include a copy of its policy addressing uncompensated overtime with its proposal [@CL-EST-1699]. Proposals that include unrealistically low labor rates, or that do not otherwise demonstrate cost realism, will be considered in a risk assessment and will be evaluated for award in accordance with that assessment [@CL-EST-1724].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text from February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. The RFO integrity of unit prices clause has the same cost-distribution rule, the same bar on methods that distort unit prices and the same rule that equal distribution is not acceptable except when there is little or no variation in base cost, and the same statement that the paragraph requires no certified cost or pricing data not otherwise required [@CL-EST-0876; @CL-EST-0877; @CL-EST-0878; @CL-EST-1693; @CL-EST-1694; @CL-EST-1695]. Its paragraph (b) has the same identification of supplies when requested by the Contracting Officer [@CL-EST-0879; @CL-EST-1696]. Its Alternate I says it is prescribed in 15.110(n)(2) where the codified Alternate I says 15.408(f)(2) [@CL-EST-0880; @CL-EST-1721], and the substituted paragraph is worded the same [@CL-EST-9099; @CL-EST-1721]. Under the RFO, FAR 52.215-14 is inserted as prescribed in 15.110(n)(1), where the codified clause says 15.408(f)(1) [@CL-EST-9098; @CL-EST-1720], and FAR 52.237-10 is inserted as prescribed in 37.802-5(f), where the codified provision says 37.115-3 [@CL-EST-9100; @CL-EST-1722]. The RFO uncompensated overtime provision has the same adjusted hourly rate definition and the same rule that the adjusted rate is applied to all proposed hours [@CL-EST-0882; @CL-EST-0881; @CL-EST-1723; @CL-EST-1697]. It has the same identification of hours by labor category, the same copy of the overtime policy, and the same risk assessment of unrealistically low labor rates [@CL-EST-0883; @CL-EST-0884; @CL-EST-0885; @CL-EST-1698; @CL-EST-1699; @CL-EST-1724].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A proposal prices two supply items. Item A costs 900 to make and item B costs 100 to make, with a combined price of 1,200.

Pricing each item at 600 would spread cost equally and distort the unit prices. Prices in proportion to base cost would be 1,080 for item A and 120 for item B. Separately, staff proposed at 48 hours a week on a 40-hour basis at 30 per hour get an adjusted rate of 25 per hour, applied to all 48 hours.

## Never confuse

The unit-price rule requires costs to be distributed within contracts on a basis that ensures that unit prices are in proportion to the items' base cost [@CL-EST-1693], and nothing in that paragraph requires submission of certified cost or pricing data not otherwise required by law or regulation [@CL-EST-1695]. Under FAR 52.237-10, whenever there is uncompensated overtime, the adjusted hourly rate (including uncompensated overtime), rather than the hourly rate, shall be applied to all proposed hours, whether regular or overtime hours [@CL-EST-1697].

Fixed hourly rates by labor category and source in time-and-materials proposals have their own card: [Time-and-materials and labor-hour proposal provisions](est-time-and-materials-proposal-rate-provisions.md).
