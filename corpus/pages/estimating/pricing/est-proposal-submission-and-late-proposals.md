## Purpose

Shows who must get a proposal in on time, when a late proposal can still be considered, and how a proposal is withdrawn.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-EST-1369] |
| Codified citation | FAR 15.208 [@CL-EST-1369]; FAR 2.101 [@CL-EST-9338; @CL-EST-9339; @CL-EST-9340] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 15.107 [@CL-EST-3430; @CL-EST-3432; @CL-EST-3441]; RFO FAR 2.101 [@CL-EST-9359; @CL-EST-9360; @CL-EST-9361] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-EST-1369] |
| Applies to | Proposals, revisions and modifications submitted in response to a solicitation [@CL-EST-1369] |

Offerors are responsible for submitting proposals, revisions and modifications so as to reach the designated Government office by the time specified in the solicitation [@CL-EST-1369]. If the solicitation specifies no time, the time for receipt is 4:30 p.m., local time, for the designated Government office on the date that proposals are due [@CL-EST-1370].

The FAR definitions section defines an offer as a response to a solicitation that, if accepted, would bind the offeror to perform the resultant contract [@CL-EST-9338]. Responses to invitations for bids (sealed bidding) are offers called bids or sealed bids, and responses to requests for proposals (negotiation) are offers called proposals, but responses to requests for quotations (simplified acquisition) are quotations, not offers [@CL-EST-9339]. The term offeror means offeror or bidder [@CL-EST-9340]. The RFO text of FAR 2.101 defines an offer as a response to a solicitation that, if accepted, would bind the offeror to perform the resulting contract, where the codified definition says the resultant contract [@CL-EST-9359; @CL-EST-9338]. The RFO definition lists the inclusions and the exclusion as numbered paragraphs, so that responses to invitations for bids and to requests for proposals are included and responses to requests for quotations are not [@CL-EST-9360]; the codified definition instead says that responses to requests for quotations (simplified acquisition) are quotations, not offers [@CL-EST-9339]. The RFO text says an offeror is an entity that makes an offer or bid, where the codified text says offeror or bidder [@CL-EST-9361; @CL-EST-9340].

## How it works

A proposal, modification or revision received at the designated Government office after the exact time specified for receipt of proposals is late [@CL-EST-1482]. A late proposal will not be considered unless it is received before award, the contracting officer determines that accepting it would not unduly delay the acquisition, and a listed condition is met [@CL-EST-1482; @CL-EST-1371].

One listed condition is that the proposal was transmitted through an electronic commerce method authorized by the solicitation and was received at the initial point of entry to the Government infrastructure not later than 5:00 p.m., one working day before the date specified for receipt of proposals [@CL-EST-1371; @CL-EST-1372]. Two further listed conditions are acceptable evidence that the proposal was received at the Government installation designated for receipt and was under the Government's control before the time set for receipt, or that it was the only proposal received [@CL-EST-1371; @CL-EST-1373]. Acceptable evidence of the time of receipt at the Government installation includes the time and date stamp of the installation on the proposal wrapper, other documentary evidence of receipt maintained by the installation, or oral testimony or statements of Government personnel [@CL-EST-1483].

A late modification of an otherwise successful proposal that makes its terms more favorable to the Government will be considered at any time it is received and may be accepted [@CL-EST-1374]. Proposals may be withdrawn by written notice at any time before award, and oral proposals in response to oral solicitations may be withdrawn orally [@CL-EST-1375].

If an emergency or unanticipated event interrupts normal Government processes so that proposals cannot be received at the designated office by the exact time specified in the solicitation [@CL-EST-1484], and urgent Government requirements preclude amendment of the solicitation closing date [@CL-EST-1485], the time specified for receipt is deemed extended to the same time of day on the first work day on which normal Government processes resume [@CL-EST-1484; @CL-EST-1485].

What the solicitation must say is on [Requests for proposals and amending the solicitation](est-rfp-and-solicitation-amendments.md). The facsimile proposals provision is on [Advisory multi-step process and source selection provisions and clauses](est-advisory-multi-step-and-source-selection-provisions.md).

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 15 text in place of the codified text under Class Deviation 2026-O0048, whose Revision 1 took effect immediately and supersedes the March 5, 2026 version [@CL-EST-3176] [@CL-EST-3177]. RFO FAR 15.107(a) keeps the rule that offerors are responsible for submitting proposals, and any revisions or modifications, so as to reach the Government office designated in the RFP by the time specified in the RFP, and that if no time is specified in the RFP the time for receipt is 4:30 p.m., local time, for the designated Government office on the date that proposals are due [@CL-EST-3430; @CL-EST-3431; @CL-EST-1369; @CL-EST-1370]. RFO FAR 15.107(b)(1) keeps the late-proposal test: a proposal, modification or revision received at the designated Government office after the exact time specified for receipt of proposals is late and will not be considered, unless it is received before award is made, the contracting officer determines that accepting the late proposal would not unduly delay the acquisition, and either the proposal was transmitted through an electronic commerce method authorized by the RFP and was received at the initial point of entry to the Government infrastructure not later than 5:00 p.m., one working day prior to the date specified for receipt of proposals, or there is acceptable evidence that it was received at the Government installation designated for receipt of proposals and was under the Government's control prior to the time set for receipt of proposals, or it was the only proposal received [@CL-EST-3432; @CL-EST-3433; @CL-EST-3434; @CL-EST-3435; @CL-EST-1482; @CL-EST-1371; @CL-EST-1372; @CL-EST-1373]. RFO FAR 15.107(b)(2) says a late modification of an otherwise successful proposal, that makes its terms more favorable to the Government, will be considered at any time it is received and may be accepted by the contracting officer [@CL-EST-3436; @CL-EST-1374]. RFO FAR 15.107(b)(3) says acceptable evidence to establish the time of receipt at the Government installation includes electronic timestamps, the time/date stamp of that installation on the proposal wrapper, other documentary evidence of receipt maintained by the installation, or oral testimony or statements of Government personnel [@CL-EST-3437; @CL-EST-3438; @CL-EST-1483]. RFO FAR 15.107(c) keeps the extension rule: if proposals cannot be received at the Government office designated for receipt of proposals by the exact time specified in the RFP because an emergency or unanticipated event interrupts normal Government processes, and urgent Government requirements preclude amendment of the RFP closing date, the time specified is deemed to be extended to the same time of day specified in the RFP on the first work day on which normal Government processes resume [@CL-EST-3439; @CL-EST-3440; @CL-EST-1484; @CL-EST-1485]. RFO FAR 15.107(d) keeps the withdrawal rules: proposals may be withdrawn by written notice at any time before award, and oral proposals in response to oral RFPs may be withdrawn orally [@CL-EST-3441; @CL-EST-3442; @CL-EST-1375].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

Proposals are due Friday at 2:00 p.m. An offeror sends its proposal by the electronic method the solicitation allows. It reaches the Government's initial point of entry on Thursday at 4:00 p.m., but it is not delivered to the contracting office until Friday at 2:15 p.m. It is late. It can still be considered if it is received before award, the contracting officer finds no undue delay, and the entry-point time meets the listed cutoff, which here it does.

## Never confuse

A late proposal and a late modification of an otherwise successful proposal that is more favorable to the Government are treated differently. A late proposal needs the listed conditions [@CL-EST-1371], while that kind of late modification will be considered at any time it is received [@CL-EST-1374]. Withdrawal is also different from revision: a withdrawal is made by written notice at any time before award, except that oral proposals in response to oral solicitations may be withdrawn orally [@CL-EST-1375], while a revision is to reach the designated office by the time specified in the solicitation [@CL-EST-1369].

The RFO text adds electronic timestamps to the examples of acceptable evidence of the time of receipt at the Government installation, listing them first in RFO FAR 15.107(b)(3) [@CL-EST-3437], while the codified list starts with the time/date stamp of the installation on the proposal wrapper [@CL-EST-1483].
