## Purpose

Shows when the Government is entitled to a price reduction after award because certified cost or pricing data were defective, and what the contractor can set against that reduction.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-EST-0101] |
| Codified citation | FAR 15.407-1(b) [@CL-EST-0102] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 15.304(a) [@CL-EST-3231; @CL-EST-3233] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-EST-0102] |
| Applies to | Contracts that include one of the prescribed price reduction clauses [@CL-EST-0102] |

Before agreement on price, an officer who learns that the certified data are defective must raise the matter with the prospective contractor whether the data raise or lower the price [@CL-EST-0101]. After award, the Government is entitled to a price adjustment, including profit or fee, of any significant amount by which the price was increased because of the defective data [@CL-EST-0102].

## How it works

The entitlement is secured by placing one of the prescribed price reduction clauses in the contract, and the clause reaches defective data from the contractor, a prospective subcontractor or an actual subcontractor [@CL-EST-0102]. A sole source position or a superior bargaining position does not defeat the entitlement [@CL-EST-0104]. Subject to conditions, an offset for understated data may be allowed, capped at the Government's claim for overstated data arising from the same pricing action; the contractor must certify its entitlement and prove the data were available but not submitted before the as-of date, and no offset is allowed if the contractor knew the data were understated before that date or the Government proves the price would not have increased by the offset amount [@CL-EST-0103]. Overpayment arises only from payment for accepted supplies or services, not from amounts paid as contract financing [@CL-EST-0105]. Where the submission was knowing, the officer obtains a penalty equal to the overpayment and seeks the advice of counsel before acting on penalties [@CL-EST-0106].

The clause itself adds four points. The price or cost is reduced and the contract is modified to reflect the reduction [@CL-EST-0177]. A reduction for defective data from a prospective subcontractor that did not receive the subcontract is limited to the amount, plus applicable overhead and profit markup, by which the actual subcontract, or the contractor's actual cost where there was no subcontract, was less than the prospective estimate, provided the actual subcontract price was not itself affected by defective data [@CL-EST-0178; @CL-EST-0253; @CL-EST-0254]. Besides the sole source defense, the contractor may not argue that the officer should have known the data were defective, that only total cost was agreed and not the cost of each item, or that no certificate was submitted [@CL-EST-0179; @CL-EST-0255; @CL-EST-0256; @CL-EST-0257]. Where items already paid for are repriced, the contractor owes interest compounded daily on the overpayment [@CL-EST-0181].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 15 text in place of the codified text under Class Deviation 2026-O0048, whose Revision 1 took effect immediately and supersedes the March 5, 2026 version [@CL-EST-3176] [@CL-EST-3177]. The after-award rule is in RFO FAR 15.304(a), which says that if, after award, certified cost or pricing data are found to be inaccurate, incomplete, or noncurrent [@CL-EST-3231] as of the date of final agreement on price or an earlier date agreed upon by the parties given on the contractor's or subcontractor's Certificate of Current Cost or Pricing Data [@CL-EST-3232; @CL-EST-0102], the Government is entitled to a price adjustment, including profit or fee, of any significant amount by which the price was increased because of the defective data [@CL-EST-3233; @CL-EST-0102]. The entitlement is ensured by including in the contract one of the clauses prescribed in 15.110(j) and (k), where the codified text points to 15.408(b) and (c) [@CL-EST-3234; @CL-EST-0102], and the clauses give the Government the right to a price adjustment for defects in data submitted by the contractor, a prospective subcontractor, or an actual subcontractor [@CL-EST-3235; @CL-EST-0102]. The Government's right is still not affected by the contractor or subcontractor having been a sole source supplier or otherwise in a superior bargaining position [@CL-EST-3236; @CL-EST-0104]. Subject to paragraphs (a)(5) and (6), contracting officers must allow an offset for any understated certified cost or pricing data submitted in support of price negotiations, up to the amount of the Government's claim for overstated pricing data [@CL-EST-3237; @CL-EST-0103] arising out of the same pricing action [@CL-EST-3238; @CL-EST-0103]. An offset must be allowed only in an amount supported by the facts and if the contractor certifies to the contracting officer that, to the best of the contractor's knowledge and belief, the contractor is entitled to the offset in the amount requested [@CL-EST-3257], and proves that the certified cost or pricing data were available before the "as of" date specified on the Certificate of Current Cost or Pricing Data but were not submitted [@CL-EST-3258]. An offset must not be allowed if the understated data were known by the contractor to be understated before that "as of" date [@CL-EST-3259], or if the Government proves that the facts demonstrate that the price would not have increased in the amount to be offset even if the available data had been submitted before that date [@CL-EST-3260]. Overpayment still occurs only when payment is made for supplies or services accepted by the Government, and amounts paid as contract financing do not create an overpayment [@CL-EST-3239; @CL-EST-0105]. Where the submission of defective data was a knowing submission, contracting officers must obtain an amount equal to the amount of overpayment made and must obtain the advice of counsel before taking contractual actions concerning penalties [@CL-EST-3240; @CL-EST-0106]. RFO FAR 15.304(b) says contracting officers must not reprice the contract solely because the profit was greater than forecast or because a contingency specified in the submission failed to materialize [@CL-EST-3241; @CL-EST-0107]. The duty before agreement on price is in RFO FAR 15.405-1(a): if the contracting officer learns that any certified cost or pricing data submitted are inaccurate, incomplete, or noncurrent [@CL-EST-3242; @CL-EST-0101], the contracting officer must immediately bring the matter to the attention of the prospective contractor, whether the defective data increase or decrease the contract price [@CL-EST-3243; @CL-EST-0101].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A prime contractor certified cost or pricing data for a price of 1,200,000 that included a subcontract quote of 400,000. Before the date of final agreement on price, the subcontractor had already offered 340,000, and the prime did not disclose the new offer. The data were not current, and they raised the price by 60,000 plus the indirect cost and profit applied to it, so the Government is entitled to a price reduction of that amount.

## Never confuse

Defective pricing is not a general claim that a price was too high; it is an adjustment measured by the amount the price was increased because certified data were inaccurate, incomplete or noncurrent [@CL-EST-0102]. A higher actual profit is not enough, because the officer may not reprice solely because profit exceeded the forecast or a contingency did not materialize [@CL-EST-0107]. A citation to FAR 15.304 means the evaluation factors rule in the codified text [@CL-EST-1294] but the after-award defective data entitlement in the RFO text [@CL-EST-3231].

## Company calibration

That estimate versions, source quotations and assumptions are frozen and compared with actuals before certified data are signed. [@Q-023]
