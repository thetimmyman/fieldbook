## Purpose

Shows how DoD orders the evaluation of offers by small business tier, when the contracting officer may issue a tiered solicitation, and what a past performance evaluation must take in when the small business clauses are in the solicitation [@CL-EST-2434; @CL-EST-2436; @CL-EST-2439].

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-EST-2434] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 215.203-70 [@CL-EST-2434]; DFARS 215.305 [@CL-EST-2439]; FAR 15.101-3 [@CL-EST-2050] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-EST-2434] |
| Applies to | Solicitations with a tiered evaluation of offers, and solicitations in which FAR 15.304 requires a past performance evaluation and the small business clauses are included [@CL-EST-2436; @CL-EST-2439; @CL-EST-2440] |

The tiered or cascading order of precedence used for tiered evaluation of offers must be consistent with FAR part 19 [@CL-EST-2434]. Consideration must be given to the tiers of small businesses, for example 8(a), HUBZone small business, service-disabled veteran-owned small business and small business, before offers from other than small business concerns are evaluated [@CL-EST-2435]. The FAR separately says an agency shall not create a tiered (or "cascading") evaluation of offers, as described in 13 CFR 125.2, for multiple-award contracts unless an agency has statutory authority [@CL-EST-2050].

The contracting officer is prohibited from issuing a solicitation with a tiered evaluation of offers unless the contracting officer conducts market research, in accordance with FAR Part 10 and Part 210, to determine whether the criteria in FAR part 19 are met for setting aside the acquisition for small business [@CL-EST-2436]. For a task or delivery order, the market research may instead determine whether there are a sufficient number of qualified small business concerns available to justify limiting competition under the terms of the contract [@CL-EST-2437]. If the contracting officer cannot determine whether the criteria in paragraph (c)(1) are met, the contracting officer must also include a written explanation in the contract file as to why the determination could not be made [@CL-EST-2438].

When a past performance evaluation is required by FAR 15.304 and the solicitation includes the clause at FAR 52.219-8, Utilization of Small Business Concerns, the evaluation factors must include the past performance of offerors in complying with requirements of that clause [@CL-EST-2439]. The same applies where the solicitation includes the clause at FAR 52.219-9, Small Business Subcontracting Plan [@CL-EST-2440]. Contracting officers must consider an offeror's failure to make a good faith effort to comply with its comprehensive subcontracting plan under the Test Program described at 219.702-70 as part of the evaluation of past performance [@CL-EST-2441]. When evaluating the past performance of an offeror that is a small business concern in response to a competitive solicitation, contracting officers must consider relevant past performance information provided for affiliates of the offeror [@CL-EST-2442].

## How it works

The tiers fix the order in which offers are looked at, and that order must be consistent with FAR part 19 [@CL-EST-2434; @CL-EST-2435]. Market research is a condition for issuing a tiered solicitation: the contracting officer researches whether the FAR part 19 set-aside criteria are met or, for a task or delivery order, whether enough qualified small business concerns are available to justify limiting competition [@CL-EST-2436; @CL-EST-2437]. If the contracting officer cannot determine that, the file also carries a written explanation of why not [@CL-EST-2438].

The small business clauses then bring compliance history into the past performance evaluation, and the Test Program adds consideration of any failure by the offeror to make a good faith effort to comply with its comprehensive subcontracting plan [@CL-EST-2439; @CL-EST-2440; @CL-EST-2441]. For a small business offeror responding to a competitive solicitation, the contracting officer must consider relevant past performance information provided for its affiliates [@CL-EST-2442].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contracting officer plans a solicitation with a tiered evaluation of offers. Before issuing it, the contracting officer conducts market research under FAR Part 10 and Part 210 to determine whether the FAR part 19 criteria for a small business set-aside are met [@CL-EST-2436]. The research cannot settle the question, so the contracting officer places a written explanation of why in the contract file before issuing the solicitation [@CL-EST-2438]. When the solicitation is issued, the small business tiers are considered before offers from other than small business concerns are evaluated [@CL-EST-2435].

The solicitation requires a past performance evaluation and includes the clause at FAR 52.219-8, so the evaluation factors include each offeror's past performance in complying with that clause [@CL-EST-2439]. A small business offeror with a short record of its own responds to this competitive solicitation with relevant past performance information for an affiliate, and the contracting officer considers it [@CL-EST-2442].

## Never confuse

A tiered evaluation is not itself a set-aside, because the market research asks whether the FAR part 19 criteria for setting aside the acquisition are met (or, for a task or delivery order, whether enough qualified small business concerns are available to justify limiting competition), and the tiered order must itself be consistent with FAR part 19 [@CL-EST-2434; @CL-EST-2436; @CL-EST-2437]. The subcontracting plan content rules and the Test Program rules are covered on their own cards and are not repeated here.

## Company calibration

That the company can produce the tier order and its own and affiliate past performance records before a DoD competitive solicitation. [@Q-012]
