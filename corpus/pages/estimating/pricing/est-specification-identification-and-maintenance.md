## Purpose

Shows how a solicitation must identify the requirements documents it cites, when the contracting office furnishes them, where documents and nongovernment standards are found, and how standardization documents are changed.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-EST-1016] |
| Codified citation | FAR 11.201, 11.202 and 11.203 [@CL-EST-1016; @CL-EST-1026; @CL-EST-2142; @CL-EST-2143] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-EST-1016] |
| Applies to | Solicitations that cite requirements documents; contracting offices; agencies that cite, buy or change standardization documents [@CL-EST-1016; @CL-EST-1020; @CL-EST-1028] |

Solicitations citing requirements documents listed in the GSA Index of Federal Specifications, Standards and Commercial Item Descriptions, available on the ASSIST website [@CL-EST-1112], or listed in another agency index shall identify each document's approval date and the dates of any applicable amendments and revisions [@CL-EST-1016]. Solicitations must not use general identification references, such as the issue in effect on the date of the solicitation [@CL-EST-1017].

Contracting offices will not normally furnish these cited documents with the solicitation [@CL-EST-1018]. There are three exceptions. The first is when the requirements document must be furnished with the solicitation to enable prospective contractors to make a competent evaluation of the solicitation [@CL-EST-1018]. The second is when, in the judgment of the contracting officer, it would be impracticable for prospective contractors to obtain the documents in reasonable time to respond to the solicitation [@CL-EST-1019]. The third is when a prospective contractor requests a copy of a Government promulgated requirements document [@CL-EST-1019].

Contracting offices shall clearly identify in the solicitation any pertinent documents not listed in the GSA Index or ASSIST [@CL-EST-1020]. Such documents shall be furnished with the solicitation, or specific instructions shall be furnished for obtaining or examining them [@CL-EST-1021].

When documents refer to other documents, the references shall be restricted to documents, or appropriate portions of documents, that apply in the acquisition [@CL-EST-1022]. They shall also cite the extent of their applicability, not conflict with other documents and provisions of the solicitation, and identify all applicable first tier references [@CL-EST-1023].

The section also says where documents can be found. The GSA Index may be viewed at the ASSIST website [@CL-EST-1113], and most unclassified Defense specifications and standards may be downloaded from it [@CL-EST-1114]. Defense documents that the ASSIST website does not offer may be ordered from the Defense Standardization Program Office, by using the ASSIST feedback module [@CL-EST-1115] or by contacting that office by telephone or email [@CL-EST-1116]. The paragraph gives the web addresses, telephone number and email address. The FIPS PUBS may be obtained from a web address that the paragraph gives [@CL-EST-1117]. They may also be purchased from the Superintendent of Documents or from the National Technical Information Service [@CL-EST-1118]. The paragraph gives an address and telephone number for each office, and an email address for that Service [@CL-EST-1118; @CL-EST-1127].

Agencies may purchase some nongovernment standards, including voluntary consensus standards, from the National Technical Information Service's Fedworld Information Network [@CL-EST-1024]. Agencies may also obtain nongovernment standards from the standards developing organization responsible for preparing, publishing or maintaining the standard, or from an authorized document reseller [@CL-EST-1025]. The National Institute of Standards and Technology can assist agencies in identifying sources for, and content of, nongovernment standards [@CL-EST-1119]. Defense activities may obtain from the Defense Standardization Program Office those nongovernment standards, including voluntary consensus standards, adopted for use by defense activities [@CL-EST-1120].

Recommendations for changes to standardization documents listed in the GSA Index should be submitted to the General Services Administration, Federal Supply Service, Office of Acquisition [@CL-EST-1026]. Agencies shall submit recommendations for changes to standardization documents available at the ASSIST website to the cognizant preparing activity [@CL-EST-1027]. When an agency cites an existing standardization document but modifies it to meet its needs, the agency shall follow the guidance in the Federal Standardization Manual and, for Defense components, DoD Manual 4120.24, Defense Standardization Program Procedures [@CL-EST-1028].

## How it works

The text states the identification duty for the solicitation and does not name an official for it. The identification shows which approval date and which amendments and revisions the solicitation cites [@CL-EST-1016].

For indexed documents the starting point is that contracting offices will not normally furnish the cited documents with the solicitation [@CL-EST-1018]. Three exceptions turn on competent evaluation, the contracting officer's judgment about timing, or a contractor's request [@CL-EST-1018; @CL-EST-1019]. For documents outside the GSA Index and ASSIST, the solicitation must identify the document and either furnish it or give specific instructions for obtaining or examining it [@CL-EST-1020; @CL-EST-1021]. References inside a cited document are bounded, and the first tier references must be listed [@CL-EST-1022; @CL-EST-1023].

The duties on changing standardization documents that the text states in the recommendation and modification paragraphs name agencies as the actor [@CL-EST-1027; @CL-EST-1028]. The text uses "should" for changes to documents in the GSA Index and "shall" for documents at the ASSIST website [@CL-EST-1026; @CL-EST-1027].

Section 11.203 adds a customer check [@CL-EST-2142]. Acquisition organizations shall communicate with customers to determine how well the requirements document reflects the customer's needs and to obtain suggestions for corrective actions [@CL-EST-2142]. Whenever practicable, the agency may provide affected industry an opportunity to comment on the requirements documents [@CL-EST-2143].

For where the availability provisions point offerors, see [Where to get specifications, standards and data item descriptions](est-availability-of-specifications-standards-and-dids.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A solicitation cites a coating standard that is listed in the GSA Index, and an agency handbook that is not. For the coating standard, the solicitation states the approval date, 2019, and the date of one amendment [@CL-EST-1016]. It does not furnish the standard, which is the normal course [@CL-EST-1018]. For the handbook, the solicitation identifies the document and says it may be examined at the agency's technical library by appointment [@CL-EST-1020; @CL-EST-1021]. An offeror that prices the prior revision of the coating standard is pricing a document the solicitation does not cite. If the offeror requests a copy of the coating standard and it is a Government promulgated requirements document, that request is one of the exceptions to the normal course [@CL-EST-1019].

## Never confuse

A general reference to the issue in effect on the solicitation date is not an identification, because the solicitation must name the dates [@CL-EST-1016; @CL-EST-1017].

Not furnishing an indexed document is normal [@CL-EST-1018]. For a pertinent document outside the GSA Index and ASSIST, the office must either furnish it or give specific instructions for obtaining or examining it [@CL-EST-1021].

## Company calibration

The company must confirm which approval date, amendment and revision of each cited document it priced against, and how it obtained the document. [@Q-004]
