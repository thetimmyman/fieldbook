## Purpose

Explains what the contracting officer must consider, determine and document when an offeror informs the contracting officer that it intends to award subcontracts for more than 70 percent of the total cost of work, and which actions are exempt [@CL-EST-2551; @CL-EST-2555].

When an offeror informs the contracting officer, pursuant to clause 52.215-22, Limitations on Pass-Through Charges-Identification of Subcontractor Effort [@CL-EST-2559], that it intends to award subcontracts for more than 70 percent of the total cost of work to be performed under the contract, task or delivery order, the contracting officer must act as follows [@CL-EST-2551]. The contracting officer must consider the availability of alternative contract vehicles and the feasibility of contracting directly with a subcontractor or subcontractors that will perform the bulk of the work [@CL-EST-2552]. If such alternative approaches are selected, any resulting RFP must be issued in accordance with the competition requirements under FAR part 6 [@CL-EST-2553]. The contracting officer must make a written determination that the contracting approach selected is in the best interest of the Government [@CL-EST-2554] and must document the basis for that determination [@CL-EST-2558]. Contract actions set aside for small business, 8(a), HUBZone, SDVOSB, and WOSB concerns are exempt from these requirements [@CL-EST-2555].

## How it works

The contracting officer's duties arise when an offeror informs the contracting officer, pursuant to clause 52.215-22, that it intends to award subcontracts for more than 70 percent of the total cost of work to be performed under the contract, task or delivery order [@CL-EST-2559; @CL-EST-2551].

The duties fall on the contracting officer. The contracting officer considers alternatives [@CL-EST-2552] and, if an alternative approach is selected, any resulting RFP must meet the competition requirements of FAR part 6 [@CL-EST-2553]. The written determination and its documented basis concern the contracting approach selected [@CL-EST-2554; @CL-EST-2558]. The linked cards cover that clause and the related proposal provision.

Contract actions set aside for small business, 8(a), HUBZone, SDVOSB and WOSB concerns are exempt [@CL-EST-2555]. The codified FAR words the exemption differently, for contract actions awarded pursuant to subparts 19.5, 19.8, 19.13, 19.14, or 19.15 [@CL-EST-2557].

DoD contracting officers must use the revised FAR part 15 in place of the codified text [@CL-EST-3176], under Class Deviation 2026-O0048 Revision 1, which took effect immediately and supersedes the version issued on March 5, 2026 [@CL-EST-3177].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A task order solicitation is not set aside. An offeror informs the contracting officer, pursuant to clause 52.215-22, that it intends to award subcontracts for 80 percent of the total cost of work to be performed under the task order [@CL-EST-2559]. That is more than 70 percent [@CL-EST-2551]. The contracting officer considers alternative contract vehicles and contracting directly with the two subcontractors that would perform the bulk of the work [@CL-EST-2552]. The contracting officer selects the existing approach, makes a written determination that it is in the best interest of the Government [@CL-EST-2554] and documents the basis [@CL-EST-2558]. If an alternative approach had been selected, any resulting RFP would have to meet the FAR part 6 competition requirements [@CL-EST-2553]. If the same action had been set aside for small business concerns, these requirements would not apply [@CL-EST-2555].

## Never confuse

The limit on pass-through charges and the offeror's proposal identification duty are on the linked cards. Once an offeror informs the contracting officer of an intent to award subcontracts for more than 70 percent of the total cost of work, the contracting officer must consider alternative contract vehicles, make a written determination and document its basis [@CL-EST-2551; @CL-EST-2552; @CL-EST-2554; @CL-EST-2558].
