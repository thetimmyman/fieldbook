## Purpose

Shows the terms that the simplified acquisitions clause for other than commercial products and commercial services carries, how it incorporates other clauses by reference, and the conditions and thresholds that attach to the conditional clauses named here.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-EST-2280] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.213-4 [@CL-EST-2280] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-EST-2280] |
| Applies to | Contracts that contain the clause titled for simplified acquisitions other than commercial products and commercial services, which is inserted as prescribed in FAR 13.302-5(d) [@CL-EST-2350] |

The contractor must comply with the FAR clauses that the first paragraph lists as incorporated by reference [@CL-EST-2280]. The first group in that paragraph implements provisions of law or executive order [@CL-EST-2351]. The second group lists additional clauses that apply, including Payments, Discounts for Prompt Payment, Extras and Prompt Payment [@CL-EST-2283], Unenforceability of Unauthorized Obligations [@CL-EST-2352], and the Disputes, Subcontracts for Commercial Products and Commercial Services and Computer Generated Forms clauses [@CL-EST-2284].

A second paragraph lists clauses incorporated by reference with which the contractor must comply unless the circumstances stated for a clause do not apply [@CL-EST-2281]. Clauses incorporated by reference have the same force and effect as if given in full text, and the contracting officer makes the full text available on request [@CL-EST-2282].

## How it works

The conditions below belong to the conditional clauses named here; each of those clauses carries its own condition or exception where it states one. The whistleblower rights clause does not apply to contracts of the Department of Defense, the National Aeronautics and Space Administration, the Coast Guard or applicable elements of the intelligence community [@CL-EST-2353].

The executive compensation and first-tier subcontract reporting clause applies to contracts valued at or above the threshold in FAR 4.1403(a) on the date of award [@CL-EST-2354]. The child labor clause applies to contracts for supplies exceeding the micro-purchase threshold on the date of award [@CL-EST-2355]. The materials, supplies, articles and equipment clause applies to supply contracts over the threshold in FAR 22.602 on the date of award, in the United States, Puerto Rico or the United States Virgin Islands [@CL-EST-2356].

The Equal Opportunity for Veterans clause applies to contracts valued at or above the threshold in FAR 22.1303(a) on the date of award [@CL-EST-2357], and the Employment Reports on Veterans clause applies on the same threshold [@CL-EST-2360]. The Equal Employment for Workers with Disabilities clause applies to contracts over the threshold in FAR 22.1408(a) on the date of award, unless the work is to be performed outside the United States by employees recruited outside the United States [@CL-EST-2358]. For that clause the United States includes the 50 States, the District of Columbia, Puerto Rico, the Northern Mariana Islands, American Samoa, Guam, the United States Virgin Islands and Wake Island [@CL-EST-2359].

The Service Contract Labor Standards clause applies to service contracts over $2,500 that are subject to the Service Contract Labor Standards statute and will be performed in the United States or in another place that the clause lists [@CL-EST-2291]. The other places are the District of Columbia, Puerto Rico, the Northern Mariana Islands, American Samoa, Guam, the United States Virgin Islands, Johnston Island, Wake Island and the outer Continental Shelf [@CL-EST-2361]. The Combating Trafficking in Persons clause applies to all solicitations and contracts [@CL-EST-2362].

The minimum wage clause under Executive Order 14026 applies when 52.222-6 or 52.222-41 is in the contract and performance in whole or in part is in the United States [@CL-EST-2363]. For that clause the United States means the 50 States, the District of Columbia, Puerto Rico, the Northern Mariana Islands, American Samoa, Guam, the United States Virgin Islands, Johnston Island, Wake Island and the outer Continental Shelf as the Outer Continental Shelf Lands Act defines it [@CL-EST-2364]. The paid sick leave clause under Executive Order 13706 applies when 52.222-6 or 52.222-41 is in the contract and performance in whole or in part is in the 50 States and the District of Columbia [@CL-EST-2365].

The Buy American—Supplies clause applies to contracts for supplies, and to contracts for services involving the furnishing of supplies, for use in the United States or its outlying areas [@CL-EST-2366]. It applies only if the value of the supply contract or supply portion of a service contract exceeds the micro-purchase threshold on the date of award and the acquisition meets one of two conditions [@CL-EST-2367]. One condition is that the acquisition is set aside for small business concerns; the other is that it cannot be set aside for small business concerns and does not exceed $50,000 [@CL-EST-2292].

The food donation clause applies to contracts greater than the threshold in FAR 26.404 on the date of award that provide for the provision, the service or the sale of food in the United States [@CL-EST-2368]. The electronic funds transfer clause that uses the System for Award Management applies when payment will be made by electronic funds transfer and the payment office uses that system as its source of the information [@CL-EST-2369]. The other electronic funds transfer clause applies when payment will be made that way and the payment office does not use the System for Award Management database as its source [@CL-EST-2370].

Paragraph (b) lists more conditional clauses than this card describes. They include the pollution prevention clause, which applies to services performed on Federal facilities [@CL-EST-2402]; the sustainable products and services clause, which applies to contracts when the agency identifies the products and services in the statement of work or elsewhere in the contract [@CL-EST-2403]; the clause on preference for privately owned United States-flag commercial vessels, which applies to supplies transported by ocean vessels except for the types of subcontracts listed at FAR 47.504(d) [@CL-EST-2404]; and the clause on reporting by United States-flag air carriers on human trafficking training, which applies to contracts with a United States-flag carrier for the transportation by air of passengers but not to contracts awarded by the Department of Defense or contracts for commercial products [@CL-EST-2405]. Alternate I of the trafficking clause applies only if the Contracting Officer has filled in the stated information [@CL-EST-2406], and Alternate I of the Buy American—Supplies clause applies only if the Contracting Officer has filled in the domestic content threshold [@CL-EST-2407].

The second group in paragraph (b) lists additional clauses that may apply [@CL-EST-2371]. The safeguarding clause applies when the contractor or a subcontractor at any tier may have Federal contract information residing in or transiting through its information system [@CL-EST-2372]. The clause on subcontracting with debarred, suspended or proposed-for-debarment contractors applies to contracts over the threshold in FAR 9.405-2(b) on the date of award [@CL-EST-2373]. The Delivery of Excess Quantities clause applies to fixed-price supplies, the origin delivery clause applies to supplies if delivery is free on board at origin, and the destination delivery clause applies to supplies if delivery is free on board at destination [@CL-EST-2374].

The contractor tenders for acceptance only items that conform to the contract, and the Government may inspect or test anything tendered [@CL-EST-2285]. The Government may require repair or replacement of nonconforming supplies or reperformance of nonconforming services at no increase in contract price [@CL-EST-2375]. The Government must exercise its postacceptance rights within a reasonable period after the defect was discovered or should have been discovered, and before a substantial change in the item's condition unless the change is due to the defect [@CL-EST-2286].

The contractor is liable for default unless nonperformance is caused by an occurrence beyond its reasonable control and without its fault or negligence [@CL-EST-2287]. The clause gives acts of God or the public enemy, acts of the Government in its sovereign or contractual capacity, fires, floods, epidemics, quarantine restrictions, strikes, unusually severe weather and delays of common carriers as examples [@CL-EST-2376]. The contractor notifies the contracting officer in writing as soon as reasonably possible after an excusable delay begins, setting out the full particulars [@CL-EST-2377]. It also remedies the occurrence with all reasonable dispatch and promptly gives written notice when it ceases [@CL-EST-2378].

The Government may terminate the contract or any part of it for its sole convenience, and the contractor must then immediately stop all work and cause its suppliers and subcontractors to cease work [@CL-EST-2379]. Subject to the terms of the contract, the contractor is paid a percentage of the contract price reflecting the percentage of the work performed before the notice of termination [@CL-EST-2288], plus reasonable charges it can demonstrate to the Government's satisfaction, using its standard record keeping system, have resulted from the termination [@CL-EST-2380]. The contractor need not comply with the cost accounting standards or contract cost principles for this purpose, and the paragraph gives the Government no right to audit the contractor's records [@CL-EST-2289]. The contractor is not paid for any work performed or costs incurred that reasonably could have been avoided [@CL-EST-2381].

The Government may terminate the contract or any part of it for cause on any default, on a failure to comply with any contract terms and conditions, or on a failure to give adequate assurances of future performance when it asks for them [@CL-EST-2382]. On termination for cause the Government is not liable for any amount for supplies or services not accepted, and the contractor is liable for the rights and remedies provided by law [@CL-EST-2290]. If it is determined that the Government improperly terminated the contract for default, the termination is deemed a termination for convenience [@CL-EST-2383]. The contractor warrants and implies that the items delivered are merchantable and fit for use for the particular purpose described in the contract [@CL-EST-2384].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A simplified order for non-commercial services is priced at 40,000 and performed in the United States by service employees covered by the Service Contract Labor Standards statute. The order is over 2,500, so the Service Contract Labor Standards clause applies.

After the contractor has performed 25 percent of the work, a strike that is beyond its reasonable control and without its fault or negligence delays performance. A strike is one of the listed examples, and because it is beyond the contractor's reasonable control and without its fault or negligence, the delay is excusable rather than a default. The contractor also notifies the contracting officer in writing as soon as it is reasonably possible. The Government then terminates for convenience. The percentage-of-price payment is 25 percent of 40,000, or 10,000. The contractor demonstrates 1,500 of reasonable charges that resulted from the termination, so the payment is 11,500. Any cost that reasonably could have been avoided is not paid, and the Government has no audit right for this payment.

## Never confuse

Paragraph (a) lists clauses the contractor shall comply with [@CL-EST-2280], while paragraph (b) lists clauses the contractor shall comply with unless the circumstances do not apply [@CL-EST-2281]. Incorporation by reference gives a clause the same force and effect as full text [@CL-EST-2282].

Termination for convenience and termination for cause reach different money outcomes. A convenience termination pays a percentage of the contract price plus demonstrated charges [@CL-EST-2288; @CL-EST-2380]. A cause termination owes nothing for supplies or services not accepted, and the contractor is liable for the rights and remedies provided by law [@CL-EST-2290]. A termination for default that was improper is deemed a termination for convenience [@CL-EST-2383].

This clause is for simplified acquisitions other than commercial products and commercial services [@CL-EST-2350]. The commercial terms are in a different clause: see [Commercial contract terms: inspection, warranty and liability](../../contract-finance/billing-payment/ctf-commercial-terms-inspection-warranty-and-liability.md) and [Commercial contract terms: termination for convenience and cause](../../contract-finance/billing-payment/ctf-commercial-terms-termination-for-convenience-and-cause.md).
