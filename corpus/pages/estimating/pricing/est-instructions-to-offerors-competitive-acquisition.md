## Purpose

Shows how an offeror submits, changes, withdraws and holds open a proposal under the competitive acquisition instructions, when a late submission can still be considered, and what the default is on discussions, award and debriefing.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-EST-1797] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.215-1 [@CL-EST-1797] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-EST-1797] |
| Applies to | Competitive solicitations where the Government intends to award a contract without discussions [@CL-EST-1850] |

A proposal, modification or revision received after the exact time specified for receipt of offers is late and will not be considered unless it is received before award, the Contracting Officer determines that accepting it would not unduly delay the acquisition, and one of the further listed conditions is met [@CL-EST-1797]. The further conditions are alternatives: receipt through an electronic commerce method authorized by the solicitation at the Government's initial point of entry not later than 5:00 p.m., one working day before the date specified for receipt [@CL-EST-1798], acceptable evidence of receipt at the designated Government installation and Government control before the time set for receipt, or being the only proposal received [@CL-EST-1799]. Acceptable evidence of the time of receipt includes the installation's time and date stamp on the proposal wrapper, other documentary evidence of receipt maintained by the installation, or oral testimony or statements of Government personnel [@CL-EST-1872]. A late modification of an otherwise successful proposal that makes its terms more favorable to the Government will be considered at any time it is received and may be accepted [@CL-EST-1800]. If an emergency or unanticipated event interrupts normal Government processes so that proposals cannot be received at the designated office by the exact time specified in the solicitation, and urgent Government requirements preclude amendment of the solicitation [@CL-EST-1873], the time specified for receipt of proposals will be deemed to be extended to the same time of day specified in the solicitation on the first work day on which normal Government processes resume [@CL-EST-1874].

If the solicitation specifies no time, the time for receipt is 4:30 p.m., local time, for the designated Government office on the date the proposal or revision is due [@CL-EST-1847]. A written award or acceptance of proposal mailed or otherwise furnished to the successful offeror within the time specified in the proposal shall result in a binding contract without further action by either party [@CL-EST-1848]. If a post-award debriefing is given to requesting offerors, the Government shall disclose, if applicable, the agency's evaluation of the significant weak or deficient factors in the debriefed offeror's offer, among the other listed information [@CL-EST-1849].

## How it works

Offerors may modify a proposal before the closing date and time, in response to an amendment, or to correct a mistake before award [@CL-EST-1802]. A revised proposal is allowed only if the Contracting Officer requests or allows it [@CL-EST-1803]. A proposal may be withdrawn at any time before award, and the withdrawal is effective when the Contracting Officer receives notice [@CL-EST-1801]. The Government intends to award without discussions, except clarifications, so the first proposal should carry the offeror's best cost or price and technical terms [@CL-EST-1804]. The Government reserves the right to conduct discussions if the Contracting Officer later determines them to be necessary [@CL-EST-1875]. A proposal stays valid for the period on the solicitation cover sheet unless the offeror proposes a different period [@CL-EST-1805].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A solicitation closes at noon on the fifth of the month. The offeror's courier delivers a changed price volume at two in the afternoon that day. Assume the solicitation did not authorize electronic submission, no emergency interrupted Government processes, and the change is not a more favorable modification of an otherwise successful proposal. The submission is late. It can be considered only if it reached the Government before award, the Contracting Officer finds no undue delay, and one alternative applies, for example that it is the only proposal received. If the solicitation had named no time at all, the default would have been 4:30 in the afternoon, local time, so the same delivery would have been on time. The offeror can still withdraw the whole proposal before award. If the Government then mails a written acceptance within the period the proposal states, a binding contract results without further action.

## Never confuse

A modification is the offeror's own change under the closing-date rule [@CL-EST-1802], while a revised proposal needs a request or permission from the Contracting Officer [@CL-EST-1803]. The default of award without discussions still lets the Government hold clarifications [@CL-EST-1804], and the Government reserves the right to conduct discussions if the Contracting Officer later determines them to be necessary [@CL-EST-1875]. The binding-contract effect attaches to a written award or acceptance furnished within the time the proposal specifies [@CL-EST-1848]. A debriefing comes only if one is given to a requesting offeror [@CL-EST-1849]. For the wider handling of late proposals, see [Submitting, modifying, withdrawing and late proposals](est-proposal-submission-and-late-proposals.md), for exchanges once a competitive range is set, see [Exchanges after proposals and the competitive range](est-exchanges-after-proposals-and-competitive-range.md), and for what a debriefing must contain, see [Postaward debriefing of offerors](est-postaward-debriefing.md). The pricing data an offeror must send with a proposal are covered in [Requirements for certified cost or pricing data](est-certified-data-requirement-provision-and-modifications-clause.md), the contract type the Government names is covered in [Type of contract and letter contract provisions](est-type-of-contract-letter-contract-and-time-and-materials-provisions.md), and a cost the offeror must propose in its offer is covered in [Facilities capital cost of money](est-facilities-capital-cost-of-money-provision-waiver-and-prb-reversion.md).
