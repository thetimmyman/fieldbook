## Purpose

Shows how an offeror submits, changes, withdraws and holds open a proposal under the competitive acquisition instructions, when a late submission can still be considered, and what the default is on discussions, award and debriefing.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-EST-1797] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.215-1 [@CL-EST-1797] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.215-1 [@CL-EST-0293; @CL-EST-0294; @CL-EST-0295; @CL-EST-0296; @CL-EST-0297; @CL-EST-0298; @CL-EST-0299; @CL-EST-0300; @CL-EST-0315; @CL-EST-0316; @CL-EST-0317; @CL-EST-0318; @CL-EST-0319; @CL-EST-0320; @CL-EST-0321; @CL-EST-0322; @CL-EST-0323; @CL-EST-0324; @CL-EST-0325]; RFO FAR 15.105-2 [@CL-EST-2244; @CL-EST-2245] |
| Edition date | NOV 2021 [@CL-EST-9794] |
| Text status | Final [@CL-EST-1797] |
| Applies to | Competitive solicitations where the Government intends to award a contract without discussions [@CL-EST-1850] |

A proposal, modification or revision received at the Government office designated in the solicitation [@CL-EST-9752] after the exact time specified for receipt of offers is late and will not be considered unless it is received before award is made, the Contracting Officer determines that accepting the late offer would not unduly delay the acquisition, and one of the further listed conditions is met [@CL-EST-1797]. The further conditions are alternatives: receipt through an electronic commerce method authorized by the solicitation at the Government's initial point of entry not later than 5:00 p.m., one working day before the date specified for receipt [@CL-EST-1798], acceptable evidence of receipt at the designated Government installation and Government control before the time set for receipt, or being the only proposal received [@CL-EST-1799]. Acceptable evidence of the time of receipt includes the installation's time and date stamp on the proposal wrapper, other documentary evidence of receipt maintained by the installation, or oral testimony or statements of Government personnel [@CL-EST-1872]. A late modification of an otherwise successful proposal that makes its terms more favorable to the Government will be considered at any time it is received and may be accepted [@CL-EST-1800]. If an emergency or unanticipated event interrupts normal Government processes so that proposals cannot be received at the designated office by the exact time specified in the solicitation, and urgent Government requirements preclude amendment of the solicitation [@CL-EST-1873], the time specified for receipt of proposals will be deemed to be extended to the same time of day specified in the solicitation on the first work day on which normal Government processes resume [@CL-EST-1874].

If the solicitation specifies no time, the time for receipt is 4:30 p.m., local time, for the designated Government office on the date the proposal or revision is due [@CL-EST-1847]. A written award or acceptance of proposal mailed or otherwise furnished to the successful offeror within the time specified in the proposal shall result in a binding contract without further action by either party [@CL-EST-1848]. If a post-award debriefing is given to requesting offerors, the Government shall disclose, if applicable, the agency's evaluation of the significant weak or deficient factors in the debriefed offeror's offer, among the other listed information [@CL-EST-1849].

## How it works

Offerors may submit modifications to their proposals at any time before the solicitation closing date and time, and may submit modifications in response to an amendment, or to correct a mistake at any time before award [@CL-EST-1802]. A revised proposal is allowed only if the Contracting Officer requests or allows it [@CL-EST-1803]. A proposal may be withdrawn at any time before award, and the withdrawal is effective when the Contracting Officer receives notice [@CL-EST-1801]. The Government intends to evaluate proposals and award a contract without discussions with offerors (except clarifications as described in FAR 15.306(a)), so the offeror's initial proposal should carry the offeror's best cost or price and technical terms [@CL-EST-1804]. The Government reserves the right to conduct discussions if the Contracting Officer later determines them to be necessary [@CL-EST-1875]. A proposal stays valid for the period on the solicitation cover sheet unless the offeror proposes a different period [@CL-EST-1805].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers must use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text from February 1, 2026, because the DoD part 52 class deviation directs its use [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. RFO FAR 52.215-1 keeps the default receipt time of 4:30 in the afternoon, local time, when the RFP specifies no time [@CL-EST-0293; @CL-EST-1847]. It keeps the rule that a late proposal, modification or revision is not considered unless it is received before award and the Contracting Officer determines that accepting it would not unduly delay the acquisition [@CL-EST-0294; @CL-EST-9022; @CL-EST-1797], with the electronic commerce alternative of receipt at the initial point of entry not later than 5:00 in the afternoon one working day before the date specified for receipt [@CL-EST-0295; @CL-EST-1798] and the alternatives of acceptable evidence of receipt at the designated Government installation and Government control before the time set for receipt of offers, or being the only proposal received [@CL-EST-0296; @CL-EST-1799]. It keeps the kinds of acceptable evidence of the time of receipt [@CL-EST-0297; @CL-EST-1872], the rule on a late, more favorable modification of an otherwise successful proposal [@CL-EST-0298; @CL-EST-1800], and the emergency extension of the receipt time [@CL-EST-0299; @CL-EST-1873; @CL-EST-0300; @CL-EST-1874]. It keeps the rules that offerors may submit modifications [@CL-EST-0315; @CL-EST-1802], that revised proposals are allowed only if requested or allowed by the Contracting Officer [@CL-EST-0316; @CL-EST-1803], that proposals may be withdrawn before award effective on notice to the Contracting Officer [@CL-EST-0317; @CL-EST-1801], and that proposals are valid for the number of days on the cover sheet unless the offeror proposes a different period [@CL-EST-0318; @CL-EST-1805]. The binding-contract rule is numbered paragraph (f)(9) in the RFO text [@CL-EST-0321] and paragraph (f)(10) in the codified text [@CL-EST-1848]. RFO FAR 52.215-1(a) defines a proposal revision as a change to material elements of a proposal made after the request for proposal closing date, at the request of or as allowed by a Contracting Officer, as the result of negotiations [@CL-EST-9023], where the codified definition says a change to a proposal made after the solicitation closing date, at the request of or as allowed by a Contracting Officer as the result of negotiations [@CL-EST-9024]. Where the codified text says "solicitation", the RFO text says "RFP", for example in the default receipt time rule [@CL-EST-0293; @CL-EST-1847]. In paragraph (f)(4), the RFO text says the Government intends to award "without negotiations" with offerors, except clarifications as described in FAR 15.202(b) [@CL-EST-0319], where the codified text says "without discussions" and cites FAR 15.306(a) [@CL-EST-1804]. The RFO text also says the Government reserves the right to conduct negotiations if the Contracting Officer later determines them to be necessary [@CL-EST-0320], where the codified text says discussions [@CL-EST-1875]. In the debriefing list, the RFO text puts the item at paragraph (f)(10)(i) and says the agency's evaluation of the significant weaknesses or deficiencies in the offer [@CL-EST-0322], where the codified text says significant weak or deficient factors at paragraph (f)(11)(i) [@CL-EST-1849]. The RFO list also adds, for certain DoD contracts, an option to request disclosure of the agency's written source selection decision document, redacted to protect the confidential and proprietary information of other offerors, for DoD contracts in excess of $10 million but not in excess of $100 million with a small business or nontraditional defense contractor [@CL-EST-0323; @CL-EST-0324], and disclosure of that document, with the same redaction, for award of a DoD contract in excess of $100 million [@CL-EST-0325]. RFO FAR 15.105-2 says that competitive RFPs must advise offerors whether the Government intends to evaluate proposals and award a contract with or without conducting negotiations with offerors whose proposals have been determined to be within the competitive range [@CL-EST-2244], and that FAR provision 52.215-1, Instructions to Offerors-Competitive Acquisition, and its Alternate I, contain this disclosure [@CL-EST-2245].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A solicitation closes at noon on the fifth of the month. The offeror's courier delivers a changed price volume at two in the afternoon that day. Assume the solicitation did not authorize electronic submission, no emergency interrupted Government processes, and the change is not a more favorable modification of an otherwise successful proposal. The submission is late. It can be considered only if it reached the Government before award, the Contracting Officer finds no undue delay, and one alternative applies, for example that it is the only proposal received. If the solicitation had named no time at all, the default would have been 4:30 in the afternoon, local time, so the same delivery would have been on time. The offeror can still withdraw the whole proposal before award. If the Government then mails a written acceptance within the period the proposal states, a binding contract results without further action.

## Never confuse

Under FAR 52.215-1(c)(3)(i) and (ii)(A), a proposal, modification, or revision received at the designated Government office after the exact deadline (or 4:30 p.m., local time, when no time is specified) is late and is not considered unless it is received before award, the Contracting Officer determines that accepting it would not unduly delay the acquisition, and one of paragraph (A)'s further conditions is met [@CL-EST-1847; @CL-EST-0293; @CL-EST-9752; @CL-EST-1797; @CL-EST-1798; @CL-EST-1799; @CL-EST-0294; @CL-EST-9022; @CL-EST-0295; @CL-EST-0296]—apart from the separate rule in FAR 52.215-1(c)(3)(ii)(B) that a late modification of an otherwise successful proposal making its terms more favorable to the Government will be considered at any time it is received and may be accepted [@CL-EST-1800; @CL-EST-0298]—unlike post-receipt clarifications on [Exchanges after proposals and the competitive range](est-exchanges-after-proposals-and-competitive-range.md) that may occur when award without discussions is contemplated [@CL-EST-1383].
