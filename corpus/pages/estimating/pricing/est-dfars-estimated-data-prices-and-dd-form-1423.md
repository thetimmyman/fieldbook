## Purpose

Shows why DoD requires estimates of data prices, how the Contract Data Requirements List reaches the solicitation, and what the contracting officer must check before the contract requires data, including how duplicate data are priced [@CL-EST-2460; @CL-EST-2461; @CL-EST-2462; @CL-EST-2463].

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-EST-2460] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 215.470 [@CL-EST-2460] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-EST-2460] |
| Applies to | Solicitations and contracts under which data are required to be delivered [@CL-EST-2461] |

DoD requires estimates of the prices of data in order to evaluate the cost to the Government of data items in terms of their management, product, or engineering value [@CL-EST-2460]. When data are required to be delivered under a contract, DD Form 1423, Contract Data Requirements List, must be included in the solicitation [@CL-EST-2461]. The section points to PGI 215.470(b) for guidance on the use of DD Form 1423 [@CL-EST-2526].

The contracting officer must ensure that the contract does not include a requirement for data that the contractor has delivered or is obligated to deliver to the government under another contract or subcontract, and that the successful offeror identifies any such data required by the solicitation [@CL-EST-2462]. However, where duplicate data are desired, the contract price must include the costs of duplication, but not of preparation, of such data [@CL-EST-2463].

## How it works

The estimate gives a value to the data item itself, measured by its management, product or engineering value, so the Government can evaluate what the item costs it [@CL-EST-2460]. The Contract Data Requirements List is the vehicle, because DD Form 1423 goes into the solicitation whenever data are required to be delivered under the contract [@CL-EST-2461].

The contracting officer's check is on the contract itself: it must not require data the contractor has delivered, or is obligated to deliver, under another contract or subcontract, and the contracting officer must ensure that the successful offeror identifies any such data that the solicitation requires [@CL-EST-2462]. Where duplicate data are desired, the contract price includes the costs of duplication and not the costs of preparation [@CL-EST-2463].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A solicitation requires data under a contract and includes DD Form 1423 [@CL-EST-2461]. One listed item is a technical manual that the contractor delivered under an earlier contract. The successful offeror identifies the manual, and the contracting officer ensures the contract does not include a requirement for it [@CL-EST-2462]. A second item is a further copy of a different manual, which the program wants delivered again as duplicate data. The contract price for that copy includes the 2,000 cost of duplication and not the 60,000 it cost to prepare the manual [@CL-EST-2463].

## Never confuse

An estimate of a data price is not a charge for the same data twice, because the contracting officer must ensure the contract does not require data already delivered or owed under another contract or subcontract, except where duplicate data are desired and the price covers duplication only [@CL-EST-2462; @CL-EST-2463]. Nor is a duplicate priced as new work where duplicate data are desired, since the price includes duplication and not preparation [@CL-EST-2463]. The deferred delivery and previously delivered data card covers the ordering mechanics and is not repeated here.

## Company calibration

That each proposal with data deliverables prices them and checks them against data already delivered. [@Q-047]
