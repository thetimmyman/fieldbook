## Purpose

Covers bidder duties in sealed bidding: acknowledging amendments, giving complete and accurate information, packing and marking bids, and getting a written explanation of the solicitation in time.

## The rule

|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-EST-1955; @CL-EST-1959; @CL-EST-1962] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.214-3 [@CL-EST-1955]; FAR 52.214-4 [@CL-EST-2151]; FAR 52.214-5 [@CL-EST-1959]; FAR 52.214-6 [@CL-EST-1962]; FAR 2.101 [@CL-EST-9341] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.214-3 [@CL-EST-0578]; RFO FAR 52.214-4 [@CL-EST-0581]; RFO FAR 52.214-5 [@CL-EST-0582]; RFO FAR 52.214-6 [@CL-EST-0585] |

If the solicitation is amended, all terms and conditions which are not modified remain unchanged [@CL-EST-1955]. Bidders shall acknowledge receipt of any amendment to the solicitation by signing and returning the amendment, or by identifying the amendment number and date in the space provided on the form for submitting a bid [@CL-EST-1956]. Acknowledgement may also be made by letter, by facsimile if facsimile bids are authorized in the solicitation, or by email if email bids are authorized in the solicitation [@CL-EST-1957]. The Government must receive the acknowledgement by the time and at the place specified for receipt of bids [@CL-EST-1958].

Bidders must provide full, accurate and complete information as required by the solicitation and its attachments [@CL-EST-2151]. The penalty for making false statements in bids is prescribed in section 1001 of title 18 of the United States Code [@CL-EST-2152].

Bids and bid modifications shall be submitted in sealed envelopes or packages, unless submitted by electronic means, addressed to the office specified in the solicitation and showing the time and date specified for receipt, the solicitation number, and the name and address of the bidder [@CL-EST-1959]. Bidders using commercial carrier services shall ensure that the bid is addressed and marked on the outermost envelope or wrapper in that way when delivered to the office specified in the solicitation [@CL-EST-2047]. Facsimile bids, modifications, or withdrawals will not be considered unless authorized by the solicitation [@CL-EST-1960]. Bids submitted by electronic commerce shall be considered only if the electronic commerce method was specifically stipulated or permitted by the solicitation [@CL-EST-1961].

A prospective bidder desiring an explanation or interpretation of the solicitation, drawings, or specifications must request it in writing soon enough to allow a reply to reach all prospective bidders before the submission of their bids [@CL-EST-1962]. Oral explanations or instructions given before the award of a contract will not be binding [@CL-EST-1963]. Any information given a prospective bidder concerning a solicitation will be furnished promptly to all other prospective bidders as an amendment to the solicitation, if that information is necessary in submitting bids or if the lack of it would be prejudicial to other prospective bidders [@CL-EST-1964].

## How it works

Two timing rules govern the exchange, and neither sets a number of days. The acknowledgement must reach the Government by the time and at the place specified for receipt of bids [@CL-EST-1958]. A request for an explanation must be in writing and early enough for a reply to reach all prospective bidders before bids are submitted [@CL-EST-1962]. Information given to one bidder becomes an amendment for the others when it is necessary for bids or its absence would prejudice them [@CL-EST-1964]. Oral guidance does not bind before award [@CL-EST-1963]. The time and place for receipt, and which methods the solicitation authorizes, are contract-specific.

Class Deviation 2026-O0038 directs DoD contracting officers to use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text, effective February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. The RFO text of FAR 52.214-3 keeps the rule that, if the solicitation is amended, all terms and conditions which are not modified remain unchanged [@CL-EST-0578] [@CL-EST-1955], and keeps the rule that the Government shall receive the acknowledgement by the time and at the place specified for receipt of bids [@CL-EST-0580] [@CL-EST-1958]. It says bidders shall acknowledge receipt of any amendment [@CL-EST-9116], and lists four items: signing and returning the amendment, identifying the amendment number and date in the space provided on the form for submitting a bid, a letter, or an email if email bids are authorized in the solicitation, a list that has no facsimile item [@CL-EST-0579]; the codified list also allows facsimile if facsimile bids are authorized in the solicitation [@CL-EST-1957]. The RFO text of FAR 52.214-4 keeps the duty to provide full, accurate, and complete information as required by the solicitation and its attachments, and the penalty for false statements in bids prescribed in section 1001 of title 18 of the United States Code [@CL-EST-0581] [@CL-EST-2151] [@CL-EST-2152]. The RFO text of FAR 52.214-5 keeps the rule that bids and bid modifications shall be submitted in sealed envelopes or packages, unless submitted by electronic means, addressed to the office specified in the solicitation and showing the time and date specified for receipt, the solicitation number, and the name and address of the bidder [@CL-EST-0582] [@CL-EST-1959], and the rule for bidders using commercial carrier services [@CL-EST-0583] [@CL-EST-2047]. In the RFO provision the electronic commerce rule is paragraph (c), following paragraph (b) directly, where the codified provision has it as paragraph (d) after a paragraph on facsimile bids, modifications or withdrawals, which the RFO provision does not print [@CL-EST-0584] [@CL-EST-1961] [@CL-EST-1960]. The RFO text of FAR 52.214-6 keeps the rule that a prospective bidder desiring an explanation or interpretation of the solicitation, drawings, specifications, etc., shall request it in writing soon enough to allow a reply to reach all prospective bidders before the submission of their bids [@CL-EST-0585] [@CL-EST-1962], keeps the rule that oral explanations or instructions given before the award of a contract will not be binding [@CL-EST-0586] [@CL-EST-1963], and keeps the rule that information given a prospective bidder is furnished promptly to all other prospective bidders as an amendment if it is necessary in submitting bids or if the lack of it would be prejudicial to other prospective bidders [@CL-EST-0587] [@CL-EST-1964].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An invitation for bids is amended twice, and bids are due at 2 pm. The bidder signs and returns both amendments, and the Government receives them by 2 pm, which meets the requirement. A second bidder acknowledges the second amendment only by a letter that reaches the Government at 3 pm, which is after the time specified for receipt, so that acknowledgement does not meet the requirement. The first bidder asks in writing, 10 days before bids are due, how to read a drawing, early enough for the reply to reach all prospective bidders. If the information in the reply is necessary for bids, the Government furnishes it to all prospective bidders as an amendment. A verbal answer from a buyer the day before bids are due is not binding. The bid goes in a sealed envelope addressed to the office in the solicitation, marked with the time and date for receipt, the solicitation number, and the bidder's name and address. A courier's outer wrapper carries the same markings.

## Never confuse

What bidders acknowledge by signing and returning the amendment or by the other listed methods is receipt of the amendment, and all terms and conditions which are not modified remain unchanged [@CL-EST-1956; @CL-EST-1955]. Oral explanations or instructions given before the award of a contract will not be binding, while any information given a prospective bidder concerning a solicitation will be furnished promptly to all other prospective bidders as an amendment if that information is necessary in submitting bids or if the lack of it would be prejudicial to other prospective bidders [@CL-EST-1963; @CL-EST-1964]. The sealed envelope rule excepts bids submitted by electronic means [@CL-EST-1959], and bids submitted by electronic commerce shall be considered only if the electronic commerce method was specifically stipulated or permitted by the solicitation [@CL-EST-1961]. The FAR definitions section defines electronic commerce as electronic techniques for accomplishing business transactions including electronic mail or messaging, World Wide Web technology, electronic bulletin boards, purchase cards, electronic funds transfer, and electronic data interchange [@CL-EST-9341]. The RFO model text of FAR 2.101 defines electronic commerce in the same words as the codified definition [@CL-EST-9349; @CL-EST-9341]. Reverse auctions, in which offerors see competing prices, are on [Reverse auction under a solicitation](est-reverse-auction.md), and negotiated proposals are on [Submitting, modifying, withdrawing and late proposals](est-proposal-submission-and-late-proposals.md).

Facsimile is where the two texts part. The codified text allows acknowledgement of an amendment by facsimile if facsimile bids are authorized in the solicitation, and says facsimile bids, modifications or withdrawals will not be considered unless authorized by the solicitation [@CL-EST-1957] [@CL-EST-1960]. The RFO text lists no facsimile way to acknowledge an amendment and prints no paragraph on facsimile bids [@CL-EST-0579] [@CL-EST-0584]. The electronic commerce rule is paragraph (d) of the codified provision and paragraph (c) of the RFO provision [@CL-EST-1961] [@CL-EST-0584].
