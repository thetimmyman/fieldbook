## Purpose

Shows how firm targets are set at the negotiation point under the successive targets clause, the profit formula, and the option to agree a firm fixed price. The contract-type card covers fixed-price incentive contracts in general: [Fixed-price incentive contracts and letter contracts](est-fixed-price-incentive-and-letter-contracts.md).

Within the number of days the contracting officer inserts after the end of the month in which the contractor has completed the item stated in the clause blank [@CL-EST-9513], the contractor shall submit data that include a proposed firm fixed price or total firm target price for supplies delivered and to be delivered and services performed and to be performed [@CL-EST-1704]. Upon the contracting officer's receipt of the data [@CL-EST-9514], the contracting officer and the contractor shall promptly establish either a firm fixed price or a profit adjustment formula for determining final profit, and the parties shall negotiate a total firm target cost based upon the data submitted under the data paragraph [@CL-EST-1670].

Total initial target profit is decreased if total firm target cost is more than total initial target cost, and increased if total firm target cost is less [@CL-EST-1671]. The initial target profit is increased or decreased by a stated percent of the difference between the total initial target cost and the total firm target cost, and the resulting amount is the total firm target profit [@CL-EST-1705]. The total firm target profit may in no event be less than one stated percent or more than another stated percent of the total initial target cost [@CL-EST-1672].

## How it works

If total firm target cost plus total firm target profit represent a reasonable price for the part of the contract subject to price revision, the parties may agree on a firm fixed price [@CL-EST-1673]. A failure to agree to a firm fixed price is not a dispute under the Disputes clause, and if agreement is not reached or a firm fixed price is inappropriate the parties shall establish a profit adjustment formula [@CL-EST-1674]. Under that formula, the total final price is established by applying to the total final negotiated cost an adjustment for profit or loss [@CL-EST-9677]. When total final negotiated cost equals total firm target cost, the adjustment is the total firm target profit [@CL-EST-1706]. When total final negotiated cost is greater than total firm target cost, the adjustment is the total firm target profit less a stated percent of the overrun [@CL-EST-1675]. When it is less, the adjustment is the total firm target profit plus a stated percent of the underrun [@CL-EST-1676].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text from February 1, 2026, because the DoD part 52 class deviation directs its use [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. The RFO text of FAR 52.216-17 keeps the contractor's data submission that includes a proposed firm fixed price or total firm target price [@CL-EST-0283; @CL-EST-1704]. It says the parties shall promptly establish either a firm fixed price or a profit adjustment formula and negotiate a total firm target cost, with the data reference reading "paragraph (c) of this section" where the codified text reads "paragraph (c) above" [@CL-EST-0284; @CL-EST-1670]. It keeps the decrease or increase of total initial target profit as total firm target cost is more or less than total initial target cost [@CL-EST-0285; @CL-EST-1671], the stated percent of the difference that gives the total firm target profit [@CL-EST-0286; @CL-EST-1705], and the stated lower and upper percents of total initial target cost [@CL-EST-0287; @CL-EST-1672]. It keeps the firm fixed price option where total firm target cost plus total firm target profit represent a reasonable price [@CL-EST-0288; @CL-EST-9026; @CL-EST-1673], the rule that failure to agree is not a dispute and a profit adjustment formula follows [@CL-EST-0289; @CL-EST-9027; @CL-EST-1674], and the formula cases for equal cost [@CL-EST-0290; @CL-EST-1706], overrun [@CL-EST-0291; @CL-EST-1675] and underrun [@CL-EST-0292; @CL-EST-1676].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

Initial target cost is 1,000,000 and initial target profit is 100,000. The clause fills in a 50 percent adjustment of the cost difference, and limits for the firm target profit of 4 percent and 15 percent of the initial target cost. The parties agree a firm target cost of 1,100,000.

The cost difference is 100,000, so the profit falls by 50 percent of that, which is 50,000, to 50,000. The limits work out to 40,000 and 150,000, so 50,000 stands as the firm target profit. If the parties then use a profit adjustment formula with a 30 percent share, and final negotiated cost comes to 1,200,000, the overrun against the firm target cost is 100,000. The adjustment is 50,000 less 30,000, which is 20,000.

## Never confuse

If total firm target cost plus total firm target profit represent a reasonable price for the part of the contract subject to price revision, the parties may agree on a firm fixed price [@CL-EST-1673].

For the firm target clause, see [Incentive price revision clause with a firm target](est-incentive-price-revision-firm-target-clause.md).
