## Purpose

Shows how firm targets are set at the negotiation point under the successive targets clause, the profit formula, and the option to agree a firm fixed price. The contract-type card covers fixed-price incentive contracts in general: [Fixed-price incentive contracts and letter contracts](est-fixed-price-incentive-and-letter-contracts.md).

The contractor shall submit data that include a proposed firm fixed price or total firm target price for supplies delivered and to be delivered and services performed and to be performed [@CL-EST-1704]. The contracting officer and the contractor shall promptly establish either a firm fixed price or a profit adjustment formula for determining final profit, and the parties shall negotiate a total firm target cost based upon the data submitted under the data paragraph [@CL-EST-1670].

Total initial target profit is decreased if total firm target cost is more than total initial target cost, and increased if total firm target cost is less [@CL-EST-1671]. The initial target profit is increased or decreased by a stated percent of the difference between the total initial target cost and the total firm target cost, and the resulting amount is the total firm target profit [@CL-EST-1705]. The total firm target profit may in no event be less than one stated percent or more than another stated percent of the total initial target cost [@CL-EST-1672].

## How it works

If total firm target cost plus total firm target profit represent a reasonable price for the part of the contract subject to price revision, the parties may agree on a firm fixed price [@CL-EST-1673]. A failure to agree to a firm fixed price is not a dispute under the Disputes clause, and if agreement is not reached or a firm fixed price is inappropriate the parties shall establish a profit adjustment formula [@CL-EST-1674]. Under that formula, when total final negotiated cost equals total firm target cost, the adjustment is the total firm target profit [@CL-EST-1706]. When total final negotiated cost is greater than total firm target cost, the adjustment is the total firm target profit less a stated percent of the overrun [@CL-EST-1675]. When it is less, the adjustment is the total firm target profit plus a stated percent of the underrun [@CL-EST-1676].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

Initial target cost is 1,000,000 and initial target profit is 100,000. The clause fills in a 50 percent adjustment of the cost difference, and limits for the firm target profit of 4 percent and 15 percent of the initial target cost. The parties agree a firm target cost of 1,100,000.

The cost difference is 100,000, so the profit falls by 50 percent of that, which is 50,000, to 50,000. The limits work out to 40,000 and 150,000, so 50,000 stands as the firm target profit. If the parties then use a profit adjustment formula with a 30 percent share, and final negotiated cost comes to 1,200,000, the overrun against the firm target cost is 100,000. The adjustment is 50,000 less 30,000, which is 20,000.

## Never confuse

Firm targets are not a firm fixed price. Firm targets feed the profit adjustment formula, and the parties may instead agree a firm fixed price if the targets represent a reasonable price [@CL-EST-1673].

The incentive price revision clause with a firm target is a separate clause with its own card: [Incentive price revision clause with a firm target](est-incentive-price-revision-firm-target-clause.md).
