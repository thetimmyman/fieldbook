## Purpose

Gives the meanings that the Standard on accounting for unallowable costs assigns to directly associated cost, expressly unallowable cost, indirect cost and unallowable cost. The Standard's requirements are on the [unallowable costs card](../../compliance/cas-allowability/cmp-cas-405-unallowable-costs.md).

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified CAS [@CL-EST-2874] |
| Codified citation | 48 CFR 9904.405-30 [@CL-EST-2874] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-EST-2874] |

An unallowable cost means any cost which, under the provisions of any pertinent law, regulation or contract, cannot be included in prices, cost reimbursements or settlements under a Government contract to which it is allocable [@CL-EST-2874]. An expressly unallowable cost means a particular item or type of cost which, under the express provisions of an applicable law, regulation or contract, is specifically named and stated to be unallowable [@CL-EST-2872].

A directly associated cost means any cost which is generated solely as a result of the incurrence of another cost, and which would not have been incurred had the other cost not been incurred [@CL-EST-2871]. An indirect cost means any cost not directly identified with a single final cost objective, but identified with two or more final cost objectives or with at least one intermediate cost objective [@CL-EST-2873].

Other terms defined elsewhere in part 99 have the meanings given in those definitions unless paragraph (b) of the section requires otherwise [@CL-EST-2960]. Paragraph (b) applies no modification to those terms for this Standard [@CL-EST-2961].

## How it works

An unallowable cost is barred by any pertinent law, regulation or contract [@CL-EST-2874]. An expressly unallowable cost needs an express provision of an applicable law, regulation or contract that specifically names the item or type of cost and states that it is unallowable [@CL-EST-2872]. A contract can be the source of the bar in both definitions [@CL-EST-2874; @CL-EST-2872]. The unallowable cost definition is tied to a Government contract to which the cost is allocable [@CL-EST-2874]. A directly associated cost is defined by cause: it is generated solely by another cost and would not have been incurred without it [@CL-EST-2871]. What the Standard requires for each kind of cost is on the main card.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A bid includes 40,000 of airfare and 6,000 of booking fees that exist only because the airfare was bought. The booking fees are directly associated costs of the airfare, because they were generated solely as a result of incurring the airfare and would not have been incurred without it [@CL-EST-2871]. If an applicable regulation specifically names that type of airfare and states that it is unallowable, the airfare is an expressly unallowable cost [@CL-EST-2872]. If a pertinent contract provision only bars the airfare from prices without naming it as unallowable, the airfare is an unallowable cost under the broader definition but is not expressly unallowable [@CL-EST-2874; @CL-EST-2872]. How the Standard treats the airfare and the fees is stated on the main card.

## Never confuse

An expressly unallowable cost must be specifically named and stated to be unallowable, while an unallowable cost is any cost that a pertinent law, regulation or contract bars from prices, cost reimbursements or settlements [@CL-EST-2872; @CL-EST-2874]. A directly associated cost is a cost generated solely as a result of the incurrence of another cost [@CL-EST-2871]. An indirect cost is a cost not directly identified with a single final cost objective, but identified with two or more final cost objectives or with at least one intermediate cost objective [@CL-EST-2873].
