## Purpose

Explains the cost efficiency special factor, the ceiling on the profit it can add, and the record form that carries the weighted guidelines result.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-EST-0793] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 215.404-71-5 [@CL-EST-0793] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-EST-0793] |
| Applies to | A special factor used where demonstrated cost reduction efforts benefit the pending contract [@CL-EST-0794] |

The factor gives contractors an incentive to reduce costs [@CL-EST-0793]. To the extent the contractor demonstrates cost reduction efforts that benefit the pending contract, the contracting officer may raise the prenegotiation profit objective by an amount not exceeding 4 percent of total objective cost [@CL-EST-0794]. The increase is measured against total objective cost shown at Block 20 of DD Form 1547 and recognized at Block 29 [@CL-EST-0872].

The factor has no normal value, and the contracting officer exercises sound business judgment in selecting a value when it is used [@CL-EST-0795].

## How it works

The contracting officer considers criteria such as participation in Single Process Initiative improvements and actual cost reductions achieved on prior contracts [@CL-EST-0796]. Selecting the percentage is a matter of maximum flexibility [@CL-EST-0798], but the contracting officer must consider how quantity differences, learning, changes in scope and inflation or deflation affect the reduction [@CL-EST-0797]. DD Form 1547 is the Record of Weighted Guidelines Application [@CL-EST-0789]. The Pricing and Contracting guidance procedures for that form are followed whenever a structured approach to profit analysis is required [@CL-EST-0799].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

Total objective cost is 3,000,000 and the contracting officer accepts a cost efficiency value of 2 percent, so the profit objective rises by 60,000, still below the 4 percent ceiling of 120,000.

## Never confuse

The cost efficiency factor is not valued like the other factors: it is a special factor with no normal value, and it lifts the prenegotiation profit objective by an amount capped at 4 percent of total objective cost [@CL-EST-0795; @CL-EST-0794]. It is a separate factor from performance risk, contract type risk and facilities capital employed [@CL-EST-0773].

## Company calibration

Whether the company can evidence cost reduction efforts that benefit a specific pending proposal, and where the record of the weighted guidelines result and its approval route sit. [@Q-012]
