## Purpose

Shows what price breakdown a contractor must furnish with a modification proposal and how the prices in a bidding schedule are treated.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-EST-1747; @CL-EST-1752] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 252.236-7000 [@CL-EST-1747; @CL-EST-1781]; DFARS 252.236-7008 [@CL-EST-1752; @CL-EST-1782] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-EST-1747; @CL-EST-1752] |
| Applies to | Contracts that use the price breakdown clause as prescribed in DFARS 236.570(a) [@CL-EST-1781], and solicitations that use the bidding schedule provision as prescribed in DFARS 236.570(b)(6) [@CL-EST-1782] |

The price breakdown clause is the one to use as prescribed in DFARS 236.570(a) [@CL-EST-1781]. Under it, the Contractor shall furnish a price breakdown, itemized as required and within the time specified by the Contracting Officer, with any proposal for a contract modification [@CL-EST-1747]. The price breakdown must include sufficient detail to permit an analysis of profit, and of all costs for material, labor, equipment, subcontracts and overhead [@CL-EST-1748]. The price breakdown must also cover all work involved in the modification, whether the work was deleted, added, or changed [@CL-EST-1748; @CL-EST-1749]. The Contractor shall provide similar price breakdowns to support any amounts claimed for subcontracts [@CL-EST-1750]. The proposal shall include a justification for any time extension proposed [@CL-EST-1751].

The bidding schedule provision is the one to use as prescribed in DFARS 236.570(b)(6) [@CL-EST-1782]. Under it, the Government's payment for the items listed in the Bidding Schedule constitutes full compensation to the Contractor for, among other listed things, furnishing all plant, labor, equipment, appliances, and materials [@CL-EST-1752]. The second listed thing is performing all operations required to complete the work in conformity with the drawings and specifications [@CL-EST-1752; @CL-EST-1773]. The Contractor shall include in the prices for the items listed in the Bidding Schedule all costs for work in the specifications, whether or not specifically listed in the Bidding Schedule [@CL-EST-1753].

## How it works

A modification proposal travels with a breakdown that lets the Government test both profit and each cost category [@CL-EST-1747; @CL-EST-1748]. Because the breakdown must reach deleted, added and changed work, deleted work appears in the breakdown beside the added and changed work [@CL-EST-1749]. Subcontract amounts need their own breakdowns, and any time extension needs a justification [@CL-EST-1750; @CL-EST-1751]. For the bidding schedule, the scheduled prices must carry every cost for the work in the specifications, whether or not a line lists that work [@CL-EST-1752; @CL-EST-1753]. The printed text does not set a format for the breakdown beyond itemization as the Contracting Officer requires, so the source does not establish one [@CL-EST-1747]. It also does not name any contract type or work type beyond a contract modification and a Bidding Schedule, so the source does not establish a wider scope.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A modification deletes a 10,000 item, adds a 30,000 item and changes a third for 5,000. The breakdown lists material, labor, equipment, subcontracts, overhead and profit for all three pieces of work. A subcontractor's 12,000 share has its own breakdown. The proposal also justifies a requested extension of time.

## Never confuse

A price breakdown goes with a proposal for a contract modification, while the bidding schedule provision concerns the prices stated for the items in the Bidding Schedule [@CL-EST-1747; @CL-EST-1753].
