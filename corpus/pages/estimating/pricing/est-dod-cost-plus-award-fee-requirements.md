## Purpose

Shows what DoD requires for cost-plus-award-fee contracts: how much award fee must stay available for the final evaluation, the cap on base fee, and the award fee reduction or denial for jeopardizing the health or safety of Government personnel.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-EST-1577] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 216.405-2, DFARS 216.470 and DFARS 252.216-7004 [@CL-EST-1577; @CL-EST-9222; @CL-EST-1580] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-EST-1577] |
| Applies to | Cost-plus-award-fee contracts, and contracts that include the award fee reduction or denial clause [@CL-EST-1577; @CL-EST-1580] |

The contracting officer shall perform an analysis of appropriate fee distribution to ensure at least 40 percent of the award fee is available for the final evaluation [@CL-EST-1577]. The percentage of award fee available for the final evaluation may be set below 40 percent if the contracting officer determines that a lower percentage is appropriate and the head of the contracting activity approves that determination, and the head of the contracting activity may not delegate this approval authority [@CL-EST-1578]. The base fee of a cost-plus-award-fee contract shall not exceed three percent of the estimated cost of the contract exclusive of the fee [@CL-EST-1579].

Under the clause on award fee reduction or denial for jeopardizing the health or safety of Government personnel, if in performance of the contract the Contractor's or its subcontractor's actions cause serious bodily injury or death of civilian or military Government personnel, the Government may reduce or deny the award fee for the period in which the covered incident occurred [@CL-EST-1580]. The Government's right under the clause to reduce or deny the award fee for the period in which the covered incident occurred includes the recovery of all or part of any award fees paid for any previous period during which the covered incident occurred [@CL-EST-1640]. The clause defines a covered incident as one in which the Contractor, through a criminal, civil, or administrative proceeding that results in a disposition listed in the definition, has been determined at fault as described next [@CL-EST-1641]. A covered incident is one of two alternatives. One is an incident in which the Contractor has been determined, in the performance of the contract, to have caused serious bodily injury or death of any civilian or military personnel of the Government through gross negligence or with reckless disregard for the safety of such personnel [@CL-EST-9498]. The other is an incident in which the Contractor has been determined to be liable for actions of a subcontractor of the Contractor that caused serious bodily injury or death of any civilian or military personnel of the Government through gross negligence or with reckless disregard for the safety of such personnel [@CL-EST-9650].

## How it works

The final-period rule is meant so that the award fee is appropriately distributed over all evaluation periods [@CL-EST-1577]. The final share may be set lower on the contracting officer's determination of a lower percentage, approved by the head of the contracting activity, who may not delegate that approval [@CL-EST-1578]. The base fee shall not exceed three percent of the estimated cost of the contract exclusive of the fee [@CL-EST-1579]. The health or safety clause lets the Government reduce or deny award fee for the period of a covered incident, and that right includes recovery of award fees paid for an earlier period in which the covered incident occurred [@CL-EST-1580; @CL-EST-1640]. The section points to PGI 216.405-2 for guidance on the use of cost-plus-award-fee contracts [@CL-EST-9499]. Section 216.470 says to see PGI 216.470 for guidance on other applications of award fees [@CL-EST-9222].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A cost-plus-award-fee contract has an award-fee pool of 500,000 and an estimated cost of 10,000,000 exclusive of fee. The plan makes 100,000 available for the final evaluation, which is 20 percent. The answer: 20 percent is below 40 percent, so unless the contracting officer determines that a lower percentage is appropriate and the head of the contracting activity approves it, at least 200,000 must be available for the final evaluation. A base fee of 300,000 is the most the base fee can be, which is three percent of the estimated cost.

## Never confuse

The three percent cap is on the base fee, measured against the estimated cost of the contract exclusive of the fee [@CL-EST-1579]. The general rules for award-fee plans, earning and the determination and findings are on [Award-fee contracts: cost-plus-award-fee and fixed-price with award fee](est-award-fee-contracts.md).
