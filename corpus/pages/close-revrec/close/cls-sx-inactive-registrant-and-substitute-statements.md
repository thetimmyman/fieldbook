## Purpose

Covers the unaudited statements that an inactive registrant may file for reports under the Securities Exchange Act of 1934, the conditions that make a registrant inactive, and the Commission's powers to permit omitted or substituted statements and to require other statements [@CL-CLS-0383; @CL-CLS-0390; @CL-CLS-0391].

| Applies to | Inactive entities, for reports under the Securities Exchange Act of 1934, and filings in which the Commission permits omission or substitution or requires other statements [@CL-CLS-0383; @CL-CLS-0390; @CL-CLS-0391] |

If a registrant is an inactive entity as defined in the section, the financial statements required by the regulation for purposes of reports pursuant to the Securities Exchange Act of 1934 may be unaudited [@CL-CLS-0383]. An inactive entity is one meeting all of the following conditions [@CL-CLS-0384]: gross receipts from all sources for the fiscal year are not in excess of $100,000 [@CL-CLS-0385]; the registrant has not purchased or sold any of its own stock, granted options therefor, or levied assessments upon outstanding stock [@CL-CLS-0386]; expenditures for all purposes for the fiscal year are not in excess of $100,000 [@CL-CLS-0387]; no material change in the business has occurred during the fiscal year, including any bankruptcy, reorganization, readjustment or succession or any material acquisition or disposition of plants, mines, mining equipment, mine rights or leases [@CL-CLS-0388]; and no exchange upon which the shares are listed, or governmental authority having jurisdiction, requires the furnishing to it or the publication of audited financial statements [@CL-CLS-0389].

The Commission may, upon the informal written request of the registrant, and where consistent with the protection of investors, permit the omission of one or more of the financial statements required by the regulation or the filing in substitution therefor of appropriate statements of comparable character [@CL-CLS-0390]. The Commission may also by informal written notice require the filing of other financial statements in addition to, or in substitution for, the statements required by the regulation in any case where such statements are necessary or appropriate for an adequate presentation of the financial condition of any person whose financial statements are required, or whose statements are otherwise necessary for the protection of investors [@CL-CLS-0391; @CL-CLS-0392].

## How it works

If a registrant is an inactive entity meeting all of the listed conditions [@CL-CLS-0384; @CL-CLS-0385; @CL-CLS-0386; @CL-CLS-0387; @CL-CLS-0388; @CL-CLS-0389], the financial statements required by the regulation for purposes of reports pursuant to the Securities Exchange Act of 1934 may be unaudited [@CL-CLS-0383]. Separately, the Commission may, upon the informal written request of the registrant, and where consistent with the protection of investors, permit the omission of one or more of the required financial statements or the filing in substitution therefor of appropriate statements of comparable character [@CL-CLS-0390], and may by informal written notice require the filing of other financial statements in addition to, or in substitution for, the required statements in any case where such statements are necessary or appropriate for an adequate presentation of the financial condition of any person whose financial statements are required, or whose statements are otherwise necessary for the protection of investors [@CL-CLS-0391; @CL-CLS-0392]. One of the conditions in the nine-to-twelve-month rule is that the Commission so permits pursuant to § 210.3-13 or § 210.8-01(e) [@CL-CLS-0379], as covered in [Financial statements covering nine to twelve months](cls-sx-nine-to-twelve-month-statements.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

Case A. A registrant has gross receipts of 60,000 and expenditures of 40,000 for the fiscal year. It did not buy or sell its own stock, grant options or levy assessments. No material change occurred in its business, and no exchange or governmental authority requires audited statements. It meets every listed condition, so the statements required for its reports under the Securities Exchange Act of 1934 may be unaudited.

Case B. A registrant that sold some of its own stock during the year does not meet the stock condition. It is not an inactive entity under this section.

## Never confuse

An inactive entity is one meeting all of the following conditions: gross receipts from all sources for the fiscal year are not in excess of $100,000 [@CL-CLS-0385]; the registrant has not purchased or sold any of its own stock, granted options therefor, or levied assessments upon outstanding stock [@CL-CLS-0386]; expenditures for all purposes for the fiscal year are not in excess of $100,000 [@CL-CLS-0387]; no material change in the business has occurred during the fiscal year, including any bankruptcy, reorganization, readjustment or succession or any material acquisition or disposition of plants, mines, mining equipment, mine rights or leases [@CL-CLS-0388]; and no exchange upon which the shares are listed, or governmental authority having jurisdiction, requires the furnishing to it or the publication of audited financial statements [@CL-CLS-0384; @CL-CLS-0389]. Under § 210.3-13, the Commission may, upon the informal written request of the registrant, and where consistent with the protection of investors, permit the omission of one or more of the required financial statements or the filing in substitution therefor of appropriate statements of comparable character [@CL-CLS-0390], and may by informal written notice require the filing of other financial statements in addition to, or in substitution for, the required statements in any case where such statements are necessary or appropriate for an adequate presentation of the financial condition of any person whose financial statements are required, or whose statements are otherwise necessary for the protection of investors [@CL-CLS-0391; @CL-CLS-0392].
