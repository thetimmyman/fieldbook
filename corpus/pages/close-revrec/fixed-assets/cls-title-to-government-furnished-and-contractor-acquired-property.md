## Purpose

Shows when title to Government-furnished property stays with the Government and when title to contractor-acquired property vests in the Government, with the differences by contract type.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CLS-0712] |
| Codified citation | FAR 45.401 and 45.402 [@CL-CLS-0712; @CL-CLS-0713] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CLS-0712] |
| Applies to | Government-furnished property and property acquired or fabricated by the contractor [@CL-CLS-0712; @CL-CLS-0713] |

The Government retains title to all Government-furnished property until properly disposed of, as authorized by law or regulation [@CL-CLS-0712]. Title vests in the Government for all property acquired or fabricated by the contractor in accordance with the financing provisions or other specific requirements for passage of title in the contract [@CL-CLS-0713].

Under fixed-price type contracts, in the absence of financing provisions or other specific requirements for passage of title in the contract, the contractor retains title to all property acquired by the contractor for use on the contract, except for property identified as a deliverable end item [@CL-CLS-0714]. Under cost type and time-and-material contracts, the Government acquires title to all property to which the contractor is entitled to reimbursement, in accordance with paragraph (e)(3) of clause 52.245-1 [@CL-CLS-0716].

## How it works

The contract's financing provisions or other specific passage-of-title terms are the first thing to check, because where they exist, title vests in the Government for all property acquired or fabricated by the contractor in accordance with them [@CL-CLS-0713]. If a deliverable item is to be retained by the contractor for use after inspection and acceptance by the Government, it shall be made accountable to the contract through a contract modification listing the item as Government-furnished property [@CL-CLS-0715]. For the clause rules on title, see [Government Property clause](cls-government-property-clause.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A fixed-price contract has no financing provisions and no special title terms. The contractor buys tooling for 40,000 for use on the contract and also builds a deliverable end item. The answer: the contractor keeps title to the tooling, while the end item is the exception to that rule. If the contract paid progress payments, the financing terms would decide title instead.

## Never confuse

The Government retains title to all Government-furnished property until properly disposed of, as authorized by law or regulation, whereas under fixed-price type contracts without financing provisions or other specific title-passage requirements, the contractor retains title to all property acquired by the contractor for use on the contract except for property identified as a deliverable end item [@CL-CLS-0712; @CL-CLS-0714]. For title under progress payments, see [Government title to progress payment inventory](../inventory-wip/cls-government-title-progress-payment-inventory.md). For title and risk of loss under performance-based payments, see [Performance-based payments title and risk of loss](../../contract-finance/financing/ctf-pbp-title-and-risk-of-loss.md).
