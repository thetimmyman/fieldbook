## Purpose

Shows when screening of excess contractor inventory begins, how long standard screening lasts, what each party must do and by when, and the special rules for particular kinds of property.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CLS-0362] |
| Codified citation | FAR 45.602-3 [@CL-CLS-0362]; FAR 2.101 [@CL-CLS-1525] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CLS-0362] |
| Applies to | Inventory disposal schedules that the plant clearance officer has accepted [@CL-CLS-0362] |

The screening period begins when the plant clearance officer accepts an inventory disposal schedule [@CL-CLS-0362]. That officer decides whether standard or special screening is appropriate and starts the screening actions [@CL-CLS-0362]. The standard screening period is 46 days [@CL-CLS-0363].

The FAR definitions section describes a plant clearance officer as an authorized representative of the contracting officer, appointed in accordance with agency procedures, responsible for screening, redistributing, and disposing of contractor inventory from a contractor's plant or work site [@CL-CLS-1525]. The RFO text of FAR 2.101 defines a plant clearance officer as an authorized representative of the contracting officer, appointed in accordance with agency procedures, responsible for screening, redistributing, and disposing of contractor inventory from a contractor's plant or work site, the same in substance as the codified definition [@CL-CLS-1537; @CL-CLS-1525].

## How it works

Standard screening runs in two stages.

| Days | What happens | Who | Claim |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 through 20 | Screening of the property on the schedule for other use within the agency and for transfer of educationally useful equipment | Contracting agency | [@CL-CLS-0364; @CL-CLS-0365] |
| No later than day 21 | Four copies of the revised schedules and Standard Form (SF) 120, or an electronic equivalent, go to GSA | Plant clearance officer | [@CL-CLS-0367] |
| 21 through 41 | Transfer requests normally honored first come, first served; donation screening completed | GSA; other Federal agencies | [@CL-CLS-0471; @CL-CLS-0368; @CL-CLS-0369] |
| 42 through 46 | Reserved for allocation to donees | GSA | [@CL-CLS-0471; @CL-CLS-0369] |

The heading of the second stage describes days 21 through 46 as 21 days of concurrent screening plus 5 days of donation processing [@CL-CLS-0471]. With the 20 days of the first stage [@CL-CLS-0364], the stages add up to the 46-day standard period [@CL-CLS-0363].

In the first 20 days the contracting agency screens the property reported on the schedule for other use within the agency [@CL-CLS-0364]. It also screens for transfer of educationally useful equipment to other Federal agencies that have expressed a need, and to schools and nonprofit organizations if a Federal agency has not expressed a need [@CL-CLS-0364; @CL-CLS-0365]. In that stage, excess personal property that meets the conditions of 45.603 may be abandoned, destroyed, or donated to public bodies [@CL-CLS-0366].

The plant clearance officer must submit the four copies of the revised schedules and SF 120, or an electronic equivalent, to GSA no later than the 21st day [@CL-CLS-0367].

From the 21st day [@CL-CLS-0471], GSA will normally honor requests for transfers on a first-come-first-served basis through the 41st day. When a request is honored, the GSA regional office must promptly send the plant clearance officer an approved transfer order that includes shipping instructions [@CL-CLS-0368]. Screening for donation is also completed during days 21 through 41 [@CL-CLS-0369]. Property is not available for allocation to donees until screening is complete, and days 42 through 46 are reserved for GSA to make that allocation [@CL-CLS-0369].

If an agency receives an intra-agency transfer request during the screening periods in paragraph (a)(2), the plant clearance officer must request GSA approval to withdraw the item from the inventory disposal schedule [@CL-CLS-0370].

The section does not say what happens to property that no agency, school, nonprofit organization or donee claims by the end of the period. It states no step after the days reserved for allocation [@CL-CLS-0369]. The conditions for abandoning, destroying or donating excess personal property are in 45.603 [@CL-CLS-0366]. See [Abandonment, destruction and contractor scrap](../inventory-wip/cls-abandonment-destruction-and-contractor-scrap.md) and [Inventory disposal schedules and reutilization](../inventory-wip/cls-inventory-disposal-schedules-and-reutilization.md).

Special screening applies to particular property. Agencies follow the standard procedures for special tooling and special test equipment without commercial components, and this property, when owned by the Department of Defense or the National Aeronautics and Space Administration, may be screened for reutilization only within these agencies [@CL-CLS-0371; @CL-CLS-0372].

For special test equipment with commercial components, agencies must complete the standard screening [@CL-CLS-0457]. If an agency has no further need for the property and the contractor has not expressed an interest in using or acquiring it by annotating the inventory disposal schedule, the plant clearance officer must forward the schedule to the GSA regional office that serves the region in which the property is located [@CL-CLS-0458; @CL-CLS-0459].

If the contractor has expressed an interest in using the property on another Government contract, the plant clearance officer must contact the contracting officer for that contract [@CL-CLS-0460]. If that contracting officer concurs, the contracting officer for the contract under which the property is accountable must transfer accountability to the contract on which the contractor proposes to use it [@CL-CLS-0461]. If that contracting officer does not concur, the plant clearance officer must deny the contractor's request and continue the screening process [@CL-CLS-0462].

If the property is contractor-acquired or -produced, the contractor or a subcontractor has expressed an interest in acquiring it, and no other party expresses an interest during agency or GSA screening, the property may be sold to that contractor or subcontractor at acquisition cost [@CL-CLS-0463].

Agencies must report all excess printing equipment to the Public Printer, Government Publishing Office, after screening within the agency; the paragraph gives the street address [@CL-CLS-0464]. If the Public Printer does not express a need within 21 days, the agency must submit the report to GSA for further use and donation screening as in the standard procedure [@CL-CLS-0465].

Non-nuclear hazardous materials, hazardous wastes and classified items are screened in accordance with agency procedures. The paragraph directs that non-nuclear hazardous materials be reported to GSA if the agency has no requirement for them; it does not name who reports [@CL-CLS-0466].

Certain nuclear materials are subject to the regulatory controls of the Nuclear Regulatory Commission, and contracting activities must screen excess nuclear materials in three categories [@CL-CLS-0467]. The categories are by-product material, source material and special nuclear material [@CL-CLS-0468; @CL-CLS-0469; @CL-CLS-0470]. By-product material is any radioactive material (except special nuclear material) yielded in or made radioactive by exposure to the radiation incident to producing or using special nuclear material [@CL-CLS-0468]. Source material is uranium or thorium, or any combination, in any physical or chemical form [@CL-CLS-0469]. It also covers ores that contain by weight one-twentieth of 1 percent (0.05 percent) or more of uranium, thorium, or any combination [@CL-CLS-0469]. It does not include special nuclear material [@CL-CLS-0469]. Special nuclear material is plutonium, Uranium 233, uranium enriched in the isotope 233 or in the isotope 235, any other material that the Nuclear Regulatory Commission determines to be special nuclear material (but not including source material), or any material artificially enriched by any nuclear material [@CL-CLS-0470].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

The plant clearance officer accepts a disposal schedule listing three items, A, B and C, and treats the acceptance day as day 1. Assume none of the items falls in a special category in paragraph (b). The officer decides standard screening is appropriate, so the period is 46 days [@CL-CLS-0362; @CL-CLS-0363]. By day 20 the contracting agency finds no use for any item within the agency [@CL-CLS-0364]. On day 20 the officer sends four copies of the revised schedules and SF 120 to GSA, which is no later than day 21 [@CL-CLS-0367].

On day 30 GSA honors a transfer request from another Federal agency for item A. Day 30 falls inside the period through day 41 [@CL-CLS-0368]. The GSA regional office promptly sends the officer an approved transfer order with shipping instructions [@CL-CLS-0368]. On day 33 an agency receives an intra-agency transfer request for item B, so the officer requests GSA approval to withdraw item B from the schedule [@CL-CLS-0370].

Item C draws no request. Donation screening for item C is completed by day 41, and item C is not available for allocation to donees until screening is complete, and days 42 through 46 are reserved for GSA to make that allocation [@CL-CLS-0369]. The section does not say what happens if no donee takes it [@CL-CLS-0369].

The answer is that item A moves by GSA transfer order, item B comes off the schedule once GSA approves, and item C waits for the allocation days.

## Never confuse

Special screening does not always replace the standard procedure. For special tooling and special test equipment without commercial components, and for special test equipment with commercial components, agencies follow or complete the standard screening [@CL-CLS-0371; @CL-CLS-0457]. Non-nuclear hazardous materials, hazardous wastes and classified items are instead screened in accordance with agency procedures [@CL-CLS-0466].

The 46-day period is the standard screening period [@CL-CLS-0363]. The 21 days that the Public Printer has to express a need are a separate window that applies to printing equipment [@CL-CLS-0465].

Allocation to donees is not part of the screening itself: property is not available for allocation to donees until after screening is complete [@CL-CLS-0369]. The first-stage paragraph separately allows excess personal property that meets the conditions of 45.603 to be abandoned, destroyed or donated to public bodies [@CL-CLS-0366].

A GSA transfer request is not an intra-agency transfer request. GSA normally honors transfer requests through the 41st day, while an intra-agency transfer request during the screening periods requires the plant clearance officer to ask GSA to approve withdrawal of the item from the schedule [@CL-CLS-0368; @CL-CLS-0370].
