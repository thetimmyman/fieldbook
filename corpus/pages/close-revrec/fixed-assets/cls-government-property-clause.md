## Purpose

Specifies the contractor's duties under the Government Property clause for internal controls and authorized use, and the rules on title to property across contract types.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CLS-0581] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.245-1 [@CL-CLS-0581] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.245-1 [@CL-CLS-0001] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CLS-0581] |
| Applies to | Contracts that include the Government Property clause [@CL-CLS-0581] |

Under the clause, the contractor shall have a system of internal controls to manage (control, use, preserve, protect, repair, and maintain) Government property in its possession, and the system shall be adequate to satisfy the requirements of the clause [@CL-CLS-0581].

The contractor shall use Government property, either furnished or acquired under the contract, only for performing the contract, unless otherwise provided for in the contract or approved by the contracting officer [@CL-CLS-0582].

## How it works

The Government retains title to all Government-furnished property, and title to Government property is not affected by its incorporation into or attachment to any property not owned by the Government [@CL-CLS-0583]. Under fixed price type contracts, in the absence of financing provisions or other specific requirements for passage of title in the contract, the contractor retains title to all property acquired for use on the contract, except for property identified as a deliverable end item [@CL-CLS-0584].

The paragraph on reimbursable property is headed title under cost-reimbursement or time-and-material contracts or cost-reimbursable line items under fixed-price contracts [@CL-CLS-0692]. Under it, title to all property purchased by the contractor for which the contractor is entitled to be reimbursed as a direct item of cost passes to and vests in the Government upon the vendor's delivery of such property [@CL-CLS-0585]. Title to all other property, the cost of which is reimbursable to the contractor, passes to the Government upon the events listed in the same paragraph [@CL-CLS-0693]. Those events are issuance of the property for use in contract performance, commencement of processing of the property for use in contract performance, or reimbursement of the cost of the property by the Government, whichever occurs first [@CL-CLS-0586].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text from February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. RFO FAR 52.245-1 has the same internal-controls requirement [@CL-CLS-0001; @CL-CLS-0581] and the same limit that Government property is used only for performing the contract unless the contract provides otherwise or the contracting officer approves [@CL-CLS-0002; @CL-CLS-0582]. It has the same rule that the Government retains title to all Government-furnished property [@CL-CLS-0003; @CL-CLS-0583], and its title-not-affected sentence goes on to say that Government property does not become a fixture or lose its identity as personal property by being attached to real property [@CL-CLS-0004]. Its fixed price type rule is the same [@CL-CLS-0005; @CL-CLS-0584]. For reimbursable property, the RFO text has the same paragraph heading [@CL-CLS-0006; @CL-CLS-0692], the same vendor-delivery rule for directly reimbursed purchases [@CL-CLS-0007; @CL-CLS-0585], and the same rule and the same three events for all other reimbursable property [@CL-CLS-0008; @CL-CLS-0693; @CL-CLS-0009; @CL-CLS-0586].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor on a cost-reimbursement contract buys electronic parts for 50,000 as a direct item of cost, and the vendor delivers them to the contractor on Day 5. The answer: title to the parts passes to and vests in the Government when the vendor delivers them on Day 5, before the contractor bills for reimbursement.

## Never confuse

Title passage differs by contract type. On a fixed price type contract with no financing or other title terms, the contractor keeps title to acquired property other than a deliverable end item, whereas on the reimbursable paragraph, title to directly reimbursed property vests in the Government on the vendor's delivery [@CL-CLS-0584; @CL-CLS-0585].
