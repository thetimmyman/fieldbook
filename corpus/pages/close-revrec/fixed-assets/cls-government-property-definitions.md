## Purpose

Fixes the meaning of the property terms as they are used in the Government property part [@CL-CLS-0454].

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CLS-0340] |
| Codified citation | FAR 45.101 [@CL-CLS-0340]; FAR 2.101 [@CL-CLS-1523; @CL-CLS-1524] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CLS-0340] |
| Applies to | The defined terms as they are used in the part [@CL-CLS-0454] |

The section defines each term below except real property, for which it gives only a cross-reference [@CL-CLS-0358]. Several definitions include an express exclusion (equipment, material, Government property and loss of Government property), and the exclusion is part of the meaning [@CL-CLS-0339; @CL-CLS-0350; @CL-CLS-0344; @CL-CLS-0346].

### Glossary

| Term | Definition in the section | Claim |
|---|---|---|
| Cannibalize | To remove parts from Government property for use or for installation on other Government property | [@CL-CLS-0328] |
| Contractor-acquired property | Property acquired, fabricated, or otherwise provided by the contractor for performing a contract and to which the Government has title | [@CL-CLS-0329] |
| Contractor inventory | Covers three items. First, property acquired by and in the possession of a contractor or subcontractor under a contract, title to which is vested in the Government, that exceeds the amounts needed to complete full performance under the entire contract. Second, property that the Government is obligated or has the option to take over under any type of contract, for example as a result of changes in the specifications or plans, or of termination of the contract (or subcontract) before completion of the work, for the convenience or at the option of the Government. Third, Government-furnished property that exceeds the amounts needed to complete full performance under the entire contract | [@CL-CLS-0330; @CL-CLS-0331; @CL-CLS-0332; @CL-CLS-0333] |
| Contractor's managerial personnel | The contractor's directors, officers, managers, superintendents, or equivalent representatives who have supervision or direction of all or substantially all of the contractor's business, of all or substantially all of its operation at any one plant or separate location, or of a separate and complete major industrial operation | [@CL-CLS-0334; @CL-CLS-0335] |

| Equipment | A tangible item that is functionally complete for its intended purpose, durable, nonexpendable, and needed for the performance of a contract. It is not intended for sale and does not ordinarily lose its identity or become a component part of another article when put into use. It does not include material, real property, special test equipment or special tooling | [@CL-CLS-0338; @CL-CLS-0339] |
| Government-furnished property | Property in the possession of, or directly acquired by, the Government and subsequently furnished to the contractor for performance of a contract. It includes, but is not limited to, spares and property furnished for repair, maintenance, overhaul, or modification. It also includes contractor-acquired property if that property is a deliverable under a cost contract when accepted by the Government for continued use under the contract | [@CL-CLS-0340; @CL-CLS-0341; @CL-CLS-0342] |
| Government property | All property owned or leased by the Government. It includes Government-furnished property, contractor-acquired property, material, equipment, special tooling, special test equipment, and real property. It does not include intellectual property and software | [@CL-CLS-0343; @CL-CLS-0344] |
| Loss of Government property | Unintended, unforeseen or accidental loss, damage, or destruction of Government property that reduces the Government's expected economic benefits of the property. It does not include occurrences such as purposeful destructive testing, obsolescence, normal wear and tear, or manufacturing defects. It includes, but is not limited to, items that cannot be found after a reasonable search, theft, damage resulting in unexpected harm to property requiring repair to restore the item to usable condition, and destruction resulting from incidents that render the item useless for its intended purpose or beyond economical repair | [@CL-CLS-0345; @CL-CLS-0346; @CL-CLS-0347; @CL-CLS-0348] |
| Material | Property that may be consumed or expended during the performance of a contract, component parts of a higher assembly, or items that lose their individual identity through incorporation into an end-item. It does not include equipment, special tooling, special test equipment or real property | [@CL-CLS-0349; @CL-CLS-0350] |
| Nonseverable | Property that cannot be removed after construction or installation without substantial loss of value or damage to the installed property or to the premises where installed | [@CL-CLS-0351] |
| Precious metals | Silver, gold, platinum, palladium, iridium, osmium, rhodium, and ruthenium | [@CL-CLS-0352] |
| Production scrap | Unusable material resulting from production, engineering, operations and maintenance, repair, and research and development contract activities. It may have value when re-melted or reprocessed, for example textile and metal clippings, borings, and faulty castings and forgings | [@CL-CLS-0353; @CL-CLS-0455] |
| Property | All tangible property, both real and personal | [@CL-CLS-0354] |
| Property Administrator | An authorized representative of the contracting officer appointed in accordance with agency procedures, responsible for administering the contract requirements and obligations relating to Government property in the possession of a contractor | [@CL-CLS-0355] |
| Property records | The records created and maintained by the contractor in support of its stewardship responsibilities for the management of Government property | [@CL-CLS-0356] |
| Provide | To furnish, as in Government-furnished property, or to acquire, as in contractor-acquired property | [@CL-CLS-0357] |
| Real property | Not defined in the section; the entry refers to Federal Management Regulation 102-71.20 (41 CFR 102-71.20) | [@CL-CLS-0358] |
| Sensitive property | Property potentially dangerous to the public safety or security if stolen, lost, or misplaced, or that shall be subject to exceptional physical security, protection, control, and accountability. Examples include weapons, ammunition, explosives, controlled substances, radioactive materials, hazardous materials or wastes, and precious metals | [@CL-CLS-0359; @CL-CLS-0456] |
| Unit acquisition cost | For Government-furnished property, the dollar value assigned by the Government and identified in the contract. For contractor-acquired property, the cost derived from the contractor's records that reflect consistently applied generally accepted accounting principles | [@CL-CLS-0360; @CL-CLS-0361] |

The section as printed gives no separate definition of acquisition cost (apart from unit acquisition cost), plant equipment, special tooling or special test equipment. It names special tooling and special test equipment only inside the definitions of equipment, material and Government property [@CL-CLS-0339; @CL-CLS-0344; @CL-CLS-0350].

The definitions section of FAR part 2 does define special tooling as jigs, dies, fixtures, molds, patterns, taps, gauges, and all components of these items including foundations and similar improvements necessary for installing special tooling [@CL-CLS-1523], of such a specialized nature that without substantial modification or alteration their use is limited to the development or production of particular supplies or parts thereof or to the performance of particular services [@CL-CLS-1524]. It does not include material, special test equipment, real property, equipment, machine tools, or similar capital items, so an item in any of those classes is not special tooling even if it is specialized [@CL-CLS-1527]. The RFO text of FAR 2.101 says special tooling does not include material, special test equipment, real property, equipment, machine tools, or similar capital items, the same in substance as the codified definition [@CL-CLS-1543; @CL-CLS-1527].

## How it works

Government property is defined by ownership or lease. It includes both Government-furnished property and contractor-acquired property, and also material, equipment, special tooling, special test equipment and real property [@CL-CLS-0343; @CL-CLS-0344]. Government-furnished property is also defined to take in contractor-acquired property that is a deliverable under a cost contract once the Government accepts it for continued use under the contract [@CL-CLS-0342].

Equipment and material each exclude the other, and both exclude special tooling, special test equipment and real property. An item that fits the description of one term therefore fails the definition if it falls in an excluded class [@CL-CLS-0339; @CL-CLS-0350].

Two definitions set who holds a role or a record. The Property Administrator is the contracting officer's authorized representative, appointed in accordance with agency procedures, who administers the contract requirements and obligations relating to Government property in the contractor's possession [@CL-CLS-0355]. Property records are the contractor's own records, created and maintained in support of its stewardship responsibilities [@CL-CLS-0356].

Unit acquisition cost depends on the property. For Government-furnished property, the Government assigns the dollar value and identifies it in the contract. For contractor-acquired property, the cost comes from the contractor's records that reflect consistently applied generally accepted accounting principles [@CL-CLS-0360; @CL-CLS-0361].

Loss of Government property turns on unintended, unforeseen or accidental loss, damage, or destruction that reduces the Government's expected economic benefits [@CL-CLS-0345]. Purposeful destructive testing, obsolescence, normal wear and tear, and manufacturing defects are named as occurrences it does not include [@CL-CLS-0346]. The lists of included and excluded occurrences are open: the definition uses "includes, but is not limited to" for the first and "such as" for the second [@CL-CLS-0347; @CL-CLS-0346].

The definitions state no dollar threshold or deadline.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor holds 40 laptops that the Government owns. Assume each laptop is functionally complete for its intended purpose, durable, nonexpendable, needed for the performance of the contract, not intended for sale, and does not ordinarily lose its identity when put into use. The laptops are then equipment [@CL-CLS-0338], and because the Government owns them they are Government property [@CL-CLS-0343; @CL-CLS-0344].

The contractor also holds 250 fasteners that lose their individual identity when incorporated into an end-item. The fasteners are material, and material does not include equipment [@CL-CLS-0349; @CL-CLS-0350]. Material is Government property when the Government owns or leases it [@CL-CLS-0343; @CL-CLS-0344].

A software package that the contractor holds is software, and Government property does not include software [@CL-CLS-0344]. If one laptop is stolen, the theft is a loss of Government property [@CL-CLS-0347]. If another wears out through normal wear and tear, that is not a loss [@CL-CLS-0346].

The answer is that the laptops are equipment, the fasteners are material, the software is outside Government property, and the stolen laptop is a loss.

## Never confuse

Equipment and material are not interchangeable. Equipment is durable and does not ordinarily lose its identity, while material is property that may be consumed or expended, component parts of a higher assembly, or items that lose their individual identity through incorporation into an end-item. Each definition excludes the other [@CL-CLS-0338; @CL-CLS-0349; @CL-CLS-0339; @CL-CLS-0350].

Government-furnished property and contractor-acquired property are different terms, but Government-furnished property also includes contractor-acquired property that is a deliverable under a cost contract when the Government accepts it for continued use under the contract [@CL-CLS-0342].

Real property is not defined in the section, which refers to the Federal Management Regulation instead. Property is all tangible property, both real and personal [@CL-CLS-0358; @CL-CLS-0354].

The Property Administrator is not the contracting officer. The Property Administrator is the contracting officer's authorized representative [@CL-CLS-0355].
