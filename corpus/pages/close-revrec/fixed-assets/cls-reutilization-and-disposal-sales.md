## Purpose

Explains the scope of reutilization of Government property that is not required for continued performance of a contract, how surplus personal property that has completed screening is sold, where the sale proceeds go, and when agencies should consider the exchange/sale authority [@CL-CLS-1398; @CL-CLS-1400; @CL-CLS-1404; @CL-CLS-1405]. The subpart that holds these sections covers contractor inventory excess to contracts and property that forms the basis of a claim against the Government, and does not apply to the disposal of real property or to the liquidated-payment property its scope section describes [@CL-CLS-1406; @CL-CLS-1407].

Reutilization section. The section on reutilization applies to the reutilization, including transfer and donation, of Government property that is not required for continued performance of a Government contract [@CL-CLS-1398]. Except for 45.602-1, it does not apply to scrap other than scrap aircraft parts [@CL-CLS-1399].

Subpart scope. The subpart establishes policies and procedures for the reporting, reutilization and disposal of contractor inventory excess to contracts and of property that forms the basis of a claim against the Government, for example termination inventory under fixed-price contracts [@CL-CLS-1406]. It does not apply to the disposal of real property, or to property in which the Government has a lien or title solely because of advance, progress or performance-based payments that have been liquidated [@CL-CLS-1407].

Sales procedures. Surplus personal property that has completed screening under 45.602-3(a) shall be sold under the Federal Management Regulation policy on the sale of surplus personal property, at 41 CFR part 102-38 [@CL-CLS-1400]. The sentence does not name who sells the property [@CL-CLS-1400]. Agencies may specify implementing procedures [@CL-CLS-1401].

GSA sponsored sales centers. Under the section titled Use of GSA sponsored sales centers, agencies may use sales center services [@CL-CLS-1402]. Use of such centers for sale of surplus property is authorized when it is in the best interest of the Government and consistent with contract terms and conditions [@CL-CLS-1403]. The section does not say who decides either condition [@CL-CLS-1403].

Proceeds. Under the section titled Proceeds from sales of surplus property, proceeds of any sale are to be credited to the Treasury of the United States as miscellaneous receipts, unless otherwise authorized by statute, or unless the contract or any subcontract under it authorizes the proceeds to be credited to the price or cost of the work [@CL-CLS-1404]. The paragraph ends by citing sections 571 and 574 of title 40 of the United States Code, and does not say what they provide [@CL-CLS-1408]. The section does not say who credits the proceeds [@CL-CLS-1404].

Exchange/sale authority. Agencies should consider the sale of property under the exchange/sale authority in FMR 102-39 (41 CFR part 102-39) when they are acquiring or plan to acquire similar products and the other requirements of the authority are satisfied [@CL-CLS-1405]. The text says "should", so this is a consideration, not a duty [@CL-CLS-1405].

## How it works

The reutilization section covers Government property that the contract no longer requires, including transfer and donation of that property [@CL-CLS-1398]. For the schedules and screening steps that precede a sale, see [Inventory disposal schedules and reutilization priorities](../inventory-wip/cls-inventory-disposal-schedules-and-reutilization.md) and [Excess inventory standard screening](cls-excess-inventory-standard-screening.md).

Once surplus personal property has completed screening, it shall be sold under the Federal Management Regulation policy at 41 CFR part 102-38 [@CL-CLS-1400]. Agencies may add implementing procedures [@CL-CLS-1401]. Agencies may use sales center services (GSA sponsored sales centers), and that use is authorized when it is in the best interest of the Government and consistent with contract terms and conditions [@CL-CLS-1402; @CL-CLS-1403].

The default for proceeds of a sale of surplus property is the Treasury, as miscellaneous receipts [@CL-CLS-1404]. Two exceptions are stated: a statute may otherwise authorize a different treatment, and the contract or any subcontract under it may authorize crediting the proceeds to the price or cost of the work [@CL-CLS-1404]. The contract or subcontract route is the one place where these sections reach a contractor's price or cost of the work [@CL-CLS-1404]. The sentences quoted from 45.604-1 to 45.604-4 state no duty for a contractor [@CL-CLS-1400; @CL-CLS-1401; @CL-CLS-1402; @CL-CLS-1403; @CL-CLS-1404; @CL-CLS-1405].

Exchange/sale is a route that agencies should consider while they are acquiring or planning to acquire similar products, if the other requirements of the authority are satisfied [@CL-CLS-1405]. It is not a disposal the agency must use [@CL-CLS-1405].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contract holds property worth 20,000 that it no longer needs, and the property is surplus personal property that has completed screening. It shall be sold under the Federal Management Regulation policy [@CL-CLS-1400]. Assume that using a GSA sponsored sales center is in the best interest of the Government and consistent with the contract terms and conditions, so that use is authorized (the section does not say who decides) [@CL-CLS-1402; @CL-CLS-1403]. The sale brings 5,000. No statute otherwise authorizes a different treatment, and neither the contract nor any subcontract authorizes crediting the proceeds to the price or cost of the work, so the 5,000 is credited to the Treasury as miscellaneous receipts [@CL-CLS-1404]. Had the contract authorized crediting to the price or cost of the work, the 5,000 would be credited there instead [@CL-CLS-1404]. If the agency is also acquiring similar products, it should consider the exchange/sale authority as another route, provided that authority's other requirements are satisfied [@CL-CLS-1405].

## Never confuse

Reutilization is not the same as a sale: reutilization includes transfer and donation of property the contract no longer requires, while the sales rules speak of surplus personal property that has completed screening [@CL-CLS-1398; @CL-CLS-1400]. Authorization to use a sales center is not a duty to use one, because the text says agencies may use sales center services [@CL-CLS-1402]. Exchange/sale is not a disposal the agency must use, because the text says agencies should consider it when similar products are being acquired and the other requirements of the authority are satisfied [@CL-CLS-1405]. The exclusion of scrap other than scrap aircraft parts, apart from 45.602-1, is a scope limit on the reutilization section, not a statement of what happens to that scrap [@CL-CLS-1399]. For scrap, see [Abandonment, destruction and contractor scrap](../inventory-wip/cls-abandonment-destruction-and-contractor-scrap.md).
