## Purpose

Explains the scope of reutilization of Government property that is not required for continued performance of a contract, how surplus personal property that has completed screening is sold, where the sale proceeds go, and when agencies should consider the exchange/sale authority [@CL-CLS-1398; @CL-CLS-1400; @CL-CLS-1404; @CL-CLS-1405]. The subpart that holds these sections covers contractor inventory excess to contracts and property that forms the basis of a claim against the Government, and does not apply to the disposal of real property or to the liquidated-payment property its scope section describes [@CL-CLS-1406; @CL-CLS-1407].

Reutilization section. The section on reutilization applies to the reutilization, including transfer and donation, of Government property that is not required for continued performance of a Government contract [@CL-CLS-1398]. Except for 45.602-1, it does not apply to scrap other than scrap aircraft parts [@CL-CLS-1399].

Subpart scope. The subpart establishes policies and procedures for the reporting, reutilization and disposal of contractor inventory excess to contracts and of property that forms the basis of a claim against the Government, for example termination inventory under fixed-price contracts [@CL-CLS-1406]. It does not apply to the disposal of real property, or to property in which the Government has a lien or title solely because of advance, progress or performance-based payments that have been liquidated [@CL-CLS-1407].

Sales procedures. Surplus personal property that has completed screening in accordance with 45.602-3(a) shall be sold in accordance with the policy for the sale of surplus personal property contained in the Federal Management Regulation, at 41 CFR part 102-38 [@CL-CLS-1400]. Agencies may specify implementing procedures [@CL-CLS-1401].

GSA sponsored sales centers. Agencies may use sales center services [@CL-CLS-1402]. Use of such centers for sale of surplus property is authorized when in the best interest of the Government, consistent with contract terms and conditions [@CL-CLS-1403].

Proceeds. Proceeds of any sale are to be credited to the Treasury of the United States as miscellaneous receipts, unless otherwise authorized by statute or the contract or any subcontract thereunder authorizes the proceeds to be credited to the price or cost of the work (40 USC 571 and 574) [@CL-CLS-1404; @CL-CLS-1408].

Exchange/sale authority. Agencies should consider the sale of property pursuant to the exchange/sale authority in FMR 102-39 (41 CFR part 102-39) when agencies are acquiring or plan to acquire similar products and other requirements of the authority are satisfied [@CL-CLS-1405].

## How it works

The reutilization section covers Government property that is not required for continued performance of a Government contract, including transfer and donation of that property [@CL-CLS-1398]. For the schedules and screening steps that precede a sale, see [Inventory disposal schedules and reutilization priorities](../inventory-wip/cls-inventory-disposal-schedules-and-reutilization.md) and [Excess inventory standard screening](cls-excess-inventory-standard-screening.md).

Once surplus personal property has completed screening in accordance with 45.602-3(a), it shall be sold in accordance with the Federal Management Regulation policy at 41 CFR part 102-38 [@CL-CLS-1400]. Agencies may specify implementing procedures [@CL-CLS-1401]. Agencies may use sales center services (GSA sponsored sales centers), and that use is authorized when in the best interest of the Government, consistent with contract terms and conditions [@CL-CLS-1402; @CL-CLS-1403].

Proceeds of any sale of surplus property are to be credited to the Treasury of the United States as miscellaneous receipts, unless otherwise authorized by statute or the contract or any subcontract thereunder authorizes the proceeds to be credited to the price or cost of the work [@CL-CLS-1404].

Agencies should consider the sale of property pursuant to the exchange/sale authority in FMR 102-39 (41 CFR part 102-39) when agencies are acquiring or plan to acquire similar products and other requirements of the authority are satisfied [@CL-CLS-1405].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contract holds property worth 20,000 that it no longer needs, and the property is surplus personal property that has completed screening. It is sold under the Federal Management Regulation policy. Assume that using a GSA sponsored sales center is in the best interest of the Government and consistent with the contract terms and conditions, so that use is authorized. The sale brings 5,000. No statute otherwise authorizes a different treatment, and neither the contract nor any subcontract authorizes crediting the proceeds to the price or cost of the work, so the 5,000 is credited to the Treasury as miscellaneous receipts. Had the contract authorized crediting to the price or cost of the work, the 5,000 would be credited there instead. If the agency is also acquiring similar products, it considers the exchange/sale authority as another route, provided that authority's other requirements are satisfied.

## Never confuse

Reutilization is not the same as a sale: reutilization includes transfer and donation of Government property that is not required for continued performance of a Government contract, while the sales rules cover surplus personal property that has completed screening in accordance with 45.602-3(a) [@CL-CLS-1398; @CL-CLS-1400]. Agencies may use sales center services, and use of such centers for sale of surplus property is authorized when in the best interest of the Government, consistent with contract terms and conditions [@CL-CLS-1402; @CL-CLS-1403]. Agencies should consider the sale of property pursuant to the exchange/sale authority in FMR 102-39 (41 CFR part 102-39) when agencies are acquiring or plan to acquire similar products and other requirements of the authority are satisfied [@CL-CLS-1405]. Except for 45.602-1, the reutilization section does not apply to scrap other than scrap aircraft parts [@CL-CLS-1399]. For scrap, see [Abandonment, destruction and contractor scrap](../inventory-wip/cls-abandonment-destruction-and-contractor-scrap.md).
