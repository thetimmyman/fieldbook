## Purpose

Shows how the agency checks the contractor's property management system, how a written notice and corrective action plan lead to remedies, and when property may move from one contract to another.

## The rule

|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CLS-0704] |
| Codified citation | FAR 45.105 and 45.106 [@CL-CLS-0704; @CL-CLS-0705; @CL-CLS-0709] |

The agency responsible for contract administration shall analyze the contractor's property management policies, procedures, practices, and systems as frequently as conditions warrant, in accordance with agency procedures [@CL-CLS-0704].

Government property shall be transferred from one contract to another only when firm requirements exist under the gaining contract, and such transfers shall be documented by modifications to both the gaining and losing contracts [@CL-CLS-0709]. Once transferred, all property shall be considered Government-furnished property to the gaining contract [@CL-CLS-0710]. The warranties of suitability of use and timely delivery of Government-furnished property do not apply to contractor-acquired property that is subsequently transferred to another contract with the same contractor [@CL-CLS-0711].

## How it works

When the contractor's property management system does not comply with contractual requirements, the property administrator notifies the contractor in writing and requests prompt correction of deficiencies [@CL-CLS-0705]. The corrective action plan requested from the contractor is to include a schedule for correction of the deficiencies [@CL-CLS-0750]. If the contractor does not correct the deficiencies in accordance with the schedule, the contracting officer must notify the contractor in writing that failure to take the required corrective action may result in consequences that the paragraph lists [@CL-CLS-0706]. Those consequences are revocation of the Government's assumption of risk for loss of Government property, the exercise of other rights or remedies available to the contracting officer, or both [@CL-CLS-0707]. If the contractor still fails to take the required corrective action in response to that notification, the contracting officer must notify the contractor in writing of any Government decision to apply the remedies [@CL-CLS-0708], which are the remedies the earlier paragraph describes [@CL-CLS-0751]. The clause that sets the system criteria and approval process is on [Contractor Property Management System Administration clause](cls-property-management-system.md). The rules on when property may be furnished at all are on [Contractor-furnished property as the default](../../industrialization/investment-case/ind-contractor-furnished-property-default.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A property administrator finds that a contractor's system does not comply and requests a corrective action plan with a correction schedule. The contractor misses the schedule, so the contracting officer sends a written warning that revocation of the Government's assumption of risk may follow. Separately, the contractor asks to move 500 units of Government property to a second contract that has no firm requirement for them. The answer: the transfer is not permitted until a firm requirement exists, and an approved transfer needs modifications to both contracts.

## Never confuse

A transfer between contracts is not a disposal. Transferred property becomes Government-furnished property to the gaining contract [@CL-CLS-0710]. The agency analysis in this section is also not the same as the clause determination process, which is on the linked card.
