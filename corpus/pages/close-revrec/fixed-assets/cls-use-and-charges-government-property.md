## Purpose

Explains when a contractor may use Government property for commercial or other nongovernmental work, and how rental charges are calculated and paid.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CLS-0222] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.245-9 [@CL-CLS-0222] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.245-9 [@CL-CLS-0010] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CLS-0222] |
| Applies to | Contractor use of Government property for work other than rent-free work, with the contracting officer's written permission or a Schedule provision [@CL-CLS-0222] |

If granted written permission by the contracting officer, or if it is specifically provided for in the Schedule, the contractor may use the Government property (except material) for a rental fee for work other than the rent-free work listed in the clause [@CL-CLS-0222].

Rent is due 60 days following completion of the rental period or as otherwise specified in the contract [@CL-CLS-0225].

## How it works

Rental requests shall be submitted to the Administrative Contracting Officer, identify the property for which rental is requested, propose a rental period and compute an estimated rental charge by using the contractor's best estimate of rental time in the formulae described in paragraph (e) of the clause [@CL-CLS-0318; @CL-CLS-0076]. The contractor shall not use Government property for nongovernmental purposes, including Independent Research and Development, until a rental charge for real property, or estimated rental charge for other property, is agreed upon [@CL-CLS-0223].

For other Government property, the contractor may, at its option, determine the property rental charge on the appraisal basis used for real property, subject to its constraints, or by the stated formula [@CL-CLS-0319]. Under the formula, the hourly rental charge is calculated by multiplying 2 percent of the acquisition cost by the hours of rental time, and dividing by 720 [@CL-CLS-0224], with rental time expressed in increments of not less than one hour and portions of hours rounded to the next higher hour [@CL-CLS-0320].

Payment of the rent shall be made by check payable to the Treasurer of the United States and sent to the contract administration office identified in the contract, unless the contracting officer specifies otherwise [@CL-CLS-0321]. Interest will be charged if payment is not made by the date specified in paragraph (f)(1) of the clause [@CL-CLS-0322].

Effective February 1, 2026, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in lieu of the codified text at 48 CFR part 52 [@CL-CMP-9760; @CL-CMP-9762]. RFO FAR 52.245-9 states the same rental permission rule [@CL-CLS-0010], the same 60-day due date [@CL-CLS-0011], and the same submission and content requirements for a rental request [@CL-CLS-0013; @CL-CLS-0014]. It states the same bar on nongovernmental use until a rental charge or estimated rental charge is agreed upon [@CL-CLS-0015], and the same election between the appraisal method and the formula, with rental time in increments of not less than one hour rounded to the next higher hour and the hourly charge of 2 percent of the acquisition cost multiplied by the hours of rental time and divided by 720 [@CL-CLS-0016; @CL-CLS-0017]. Its payment rule [@CL-CLS-0012] and its interest rule [@CL-CLS-0018] are the same, as are its rent-free uses: contracts with the Government that specifically authorize such use, subcontracts of any tier under Government prime contracts if the contracting officer having cognizance of the prime contract approves a subcontract specifically authorizing such use or otherwise authorizes such use in writing, and other work if the Contracting Officer specifically authorizes it in writing [@CL-CLS-0019; @CL-CLS-1584; @CL-CLS-0020].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor receives written permission from the contracting officer to use a Government-owned test chamber with an acquisition cost of 360,000 for commercial testing, and elects the formula. Two percent of the acquisition cost is 7,200, which divided by 720 yields an hourly rental charge of 10. The contractor uses the equipment for 50 hours of rental time, which includes set-up and restoration time, so the total rental charge is 500. The answer: the rent is 500, and it is due 60 days after the rental period ends unless the contract says otherwise.

## Never confuse

Rent-free use covers contracts with the Government that specifically authorize such use, subcontracts of any tier under Government prime contracts if the contracting officer having cognizance of the prime contract approves a subcontract specifically authorizing such use or otherwise authorizes such use in writing, and other work if the contracting officer specifically authorizes it in writing [@CL-CLS-0316; @CL-CLS-1583; @CL-CLS-0317]. Rental use requires written permission by the contracting officer or specific provision in the Schedule, and the contractor shall not use Government property for nongovernmental purposes until a rental charge for real property, or estimated rental charge for other property, is agreed upon [@CL-CLS-0222; @CL-CLS-0223].
