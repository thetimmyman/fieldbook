## Purpose

Explains when a contractor may use Government property for commercial or other nongovernmental work, and how rental charges are calculated and paid.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CLS-0222] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.245-9 [@CL-CLS-0222] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.245-9 [@CL-CLS-0010] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CLS-0222] |
| Applies to | Contractor use of Government property for work other than rent-free work, with the contracting officer's written permission or a Schedule provision [@CL-CLS-0222] |

If granted written permission by the contracting officer, or if it is specifically provided for in the Schedule, the contractor may use the Government property (except material) for a rental fee for work other than the rent-free work listed in the clause [@CL-CLS-0222].

Rent is due 60 days following completion of the rental period or as otherwise specified in the contract [@CL-CLS-0225].

## How it works

Rental requests go to the Administrative Contracting Officer, identify the property, propose a rental period and compute an estimated rental charge from the contractor's best estimate of rental time [@CL-CLS-0318]. The contractor shall not use Government property for nongovernmental purposes, including independent research and development, until a rental charge for real property, or an estimated rental charge for other property, is agreed upon [@CL-CLS-0223].

For other Government property, the contractor may elect the appraisal method used for real property, subject to its constraints, or the stated formula [@CL-CLS-0319]. Under the formula, the hourly rental charge is 2 percent of the acquisition cost multiplied by the hours of rental time and divided by 720 [@CL-CLS-0224], with rental time expressed in increments of not less than one hour and portions of hours rounded to the next higher hour [@CL-CLS-0320].

Payment of the rent is made by check payable to the Treasurer of the United States and sent to the contract administration office identified in the contract, unless the contracting officer specifies otherwise [@CL-CLS-0321]. Interest is charged if payment is not made by the due date [@CL-CLS-0322].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text from February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. RFO FAR 52.245-9 has the same rental permission rule [@CL-CLS-0010; @CL-CLS-0222], the same 60-day due date [@CL-CLS-0011; @CL-CLS-0225], and the same content for a rental request [@CL-CLS-0013; @CL-CLS-0014; @CL-CLS-0318]. It has the same bar on nongovernmental use until a rental charge or estimated rental charge is agreed upon [@CL-CLS-0015; @CL-CLS-0223], and the same election between the appraisal method and the formula, with rental time in increments of not less than one hour rounded up and the hourly charge of 2 percent of the acquisition cost multiplied by the hours of rental time and divided by 720 [@CL-CLS-0016; @CL-CLS-0017; @CL-CLS-0319; @CL-CLS-0320; @CL-CLS-0224]. Its payment rule [@CL-CLS-0012; @CL-CLS-0321] and its interest rule [@CL-CLS-0018; @CL-CLS-0322] are the same, and so are its rent-free uses for contracts that specifically authorize such use and for other work that the Contracting Officer specifically authorizes in writing [@CL-CLS-0019; @CL-CLS-0020; @CL-CLS-0316; @CL-CLS-0317].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor receives written permission from the contracting officer to use a Government-owned test chamber with an acquisition cost of 360,000 for commercial testing, and elects the formula. Two percent of the acquisition cost is 7,200, which divided by 720 yields an hourly rental charge of 10. The contractor uses the equipment for 50 hours of rental time, which includes set-up and restoration time, so the total rental charge is 500. The answer: the rent is 500, and it is due 60 days after the rental period ends unless the contract says otherwise.

## Never confuse

Rent-free use is not rental use. Rent-free use covers contracts that specifically authorize it, other listed cases, and other work the contracting officer specifically authorizes in writing [@CL-CLS-0316; @CL-CLS-0317]. Rental use needs the contracting officer's written permission or a Schedule provision, and no nongovernmental use may start until the rental charge or estimated charge is agreed upon [@CL-CLS-0222; @CL-CLS-0223].
