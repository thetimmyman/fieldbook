## Purpose

Shows the contract types under which a contractor is generally not liable for loss of Government property, the contracting officer's power to revoke the Government's assumption of risk, and what a prime contractor keeps when it provides property to a subcontractor.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CLS-0700] |
| Codified citation | FAR 45.104 [@CL-CLS-0700; @CL-CLS-0701; @CL-CLS-0702; @CL-CLS-0703] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CLS-0700] |
| Applies to | Loss of Government property under the listed contract types [@CL-CLS-0700; @CL-CLS-0701] |

Generally, contractors are not held liable for loss of Government property under cost-reimbursement, time-and-material and labor-hour contracts [@CL-CLS-0700], and under fixed-price contracts awarded on the basis of submission of certified cost or pricing data [@CL-CLS-0701].

The contracting officer may revoke the Government's assumption of risk when the property administrator determines that the contractor's property management practices are noncompliant with contract requirements [@CL-CLS-0702]. A prime contractor that provides Government property to a subcontractor shall not be relieved of any responsibility to the Government that the prime contractor may have under the terms of the prime contract [@CL-CLS-0703].

## How it works

The no-liability rule is stated by contract type, and the listed types are cost-reimbursement, time-and-material, labor-hour and fixed-price contracts awarded on the basis of submission of certified cost or pricing data [@CL-CLS-0700; @CL-CLS-0701]. Revocation turns on a determination by the property administrator that the contractor's property management practices are noncompliant with contract requirements [@CL-CLS-0702]. Providing property to a subcontractor does not shift the prime contractor's responsibility to the Government [@CL-CLS-0703]. The contractor's own duties under the Government Property clause are on [Government Property clause](cls-government-property-clause.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor on a cost-reimbursement contract loses a furnished machine worth 80,000 in a fire. Under the general rule the contractor is not held liable for the loss. Later the property administrator finds the contractor's property practices noncompliant, so the contracting officer may revoke the Government's assumption of risk. The answer: the first loss falls under the general rule, and the revocation power is what the Government can use against noncompliant practices.

## Never confuse

A general rule of no liability is not a promise that the Government always bears a loss. The rule is stated as general, the Government's assumption of risk can be revoked, and a prime contractor stays responsible to the Government when it passes property to a subcontractor [@CL-CLS-0700; @CL-CLS-0702; @CL-CLS-0703].
