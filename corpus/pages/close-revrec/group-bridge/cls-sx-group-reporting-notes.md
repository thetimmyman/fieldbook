## Purpose

Shows the notes a registrant gives about restrictions on dividends and on transfers from subsidiaries, summarized information on unconsolidated subsidiaries and persons owned 50 percent or less [@CL-CLS-0846], related-party transactions and intercompany profits, and when separate statements of a majority-owned subsidiary that is not consolidated are filed. Regulation S-X is not FAR, DFARS or CAS. It binds financial statements filed with the Commission, which is the Securities and Exchange Commission, and a contractor that files nothing with the Commission is not bound [@CL-CLS-0850]. Whether it binds a company depends on the company's filing obligations with the Commission.

The general notes on dividend restrictions, summarized information and related party transactions are set forth, if applicable to the person for which the financial statements are filed, on the face of the appropriate statement or in appropriately captioned notes [@CL-CLS-0852]. The registrant describes the most significant restrictions on the payment of dividends by the registrant, with their sources, pertinent provisions and the amount of retained earnings or net income restricted or free of restrictions [@CL-CLS-0843]. When material, it also describes the nature of restrictions on the ability of consolidated subsidiaries and unconsolidated subsidiaries to transfer funds to the registrant in the form of cash dividends, loans or advances [@CL-CLS-0844]. Summarized information as to assets, liabilities and results of operations is presented in notes on an individual or group basis for subsidiaries not consolidated, which is one of two listed groups [@CL-CLS-0845]. The other listed group is persons owned 50 percent or less and accounted for by the equity method by the registrant or by a subsidiary, if the criteria for a significant subsidiary are met [@CL-CLS-0846]. Summarized information of subsidiaries not consolidated shall not be combined for disclosure purposes with the summarized information of persons owned 50 percent or less [@CL-CLS-0853]. Amounts of related party transactions should be stated on the face of the balance sheet, statement of comprehensive income, or statement of cash flows [@CL-CLS-0847]. Where separate financial statements are presented for the registrant, certain investees, or subsidiaries, any intercompany profits or losses from related party transactions and their effects shall be disclosed [@CL-CLS-0848].

If a note covering substantially the same subject matter is required for two or more financial statements relating to the same or affiliated persons, for which separate sets of notes are presented, the required information may be shown in a note to only one of those statements [@CL-CLS-1453]. That holds provided that a clear and specific reference to it is made in each of the other statements for which the note is required [@CL-CLS-1454].

Separate financial statements must be filed for a majority-owned subsidiary not consolidated by the registrant or by a subsidiary, when the conditions are met with 20 percent substituted for 10 percent in the tests used to determine a significant subsidiary [@CL-CLS-0849]. The rule looks to any one of the conditions set forth in the significant subsidiary definition being met [@CL-CLS-0855]. What is not established: the significant subsidiary tests sit in a separate definition, section 210.1-02(w), cited in the notes rule [@CL-CLS-0846], and the sections used for this card do not include that definition. This page therefore does not state the tests, their measures or their percentage cutoffs, and without the base tests it cannot say what the 20 percent is measured against [@CL-CLS-0849]. The detail of the summarized information, which section 210.1-02(bb) sets out [@CL-CLS-0845], is likewise not provided by the sections used for this card. This card covers only the subsidiary sentence of the separate statements rule.

## How it works

The registrant gives the dividend and transfer restrictions in its notes [@CL-CLS-0843; @CL-CLS-0844]. For subsidiaries it does not consolidate, and for equity-method persons owned 50 percent or less that meet the significance criteria, it presents summarized information [@CL-CLS-0845; @CL-CLS-0846]. It states related-party amounts on the face of the statements and discloses intercompany profits or losses where separate statements are presented [@CL-CLS-0847; @CL-CLS-0848]. A majority-owned subsidiary that is not consolidated and meets the conditions needs separate financial statements, and the tests are not established here [@CL-CLS-0849; @CL-CLS-0855].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A registrant holds a majority-owned subsidiary that it does not consolidate. The subsidiary meets the significance tests at the raised percentage, so the registrant files the subsidiary's separate financial statements. It also notes a restriction that stops the subsidiary from paying cash dividends to the registrant, and it discloses 25 of intercompany profit from sales between the two.

## Never confuse

The summarized information note is not the separate statements rule: the note gives summarized information, while the separate statements rule requires financial statements of the subsidiary once its conditions are met [@CL-CLS-0845; @CL-CLS-0849]. Summarized information of subsidiaries not consolidated is not combined with that of persons owned 50 percent or less, which are the other listed group [@CL-CLS-0853; @CL-CLS-0846]. This is not a segment disclosure; for the business description by segment, see [Business description by segment](../../fpa/kpi/fpa-business-description-by-segment.md). For the liquidity discussion, see [MD&A objective and liquidity](../../fpa/planning-chain/fpa-mdna-objective-and-liquidity.md).
