## Purpose

Shows how many years of audited statements of comprehensive income and cash flows a registrant files, what interim periods add, and how changes in equity are analysed. The rules are part of Regulation S-X, which is not FAR, DFARS or CAS. They bind financial statements filed with the Commission, which is the Securities and Exchange Commission, and a contractor that files nothing with the Commission is not bound [@CL-CLS-0850]. Whether they bind a company depends on the company's filing obligations with the Commission.

Outside the Article 8 election for smaller reporting companies [@CL-CLS-0860], the registrant files audited statements of comprehensive income and cash flows, consolidated for the registrant and its subsidiaries and covering its predecessors, for each of the three fiscal years preceding the date of the most recent audited balance sheet being filed [@CL-CLS-0807]. The period is the three fiscal years or such shorter period as the registrant, including predecessors, has been in existence [@CL-CLS-0808]. In a Securities Act registration statement for the initial public offering of its equity securities, an emerging growth company may provide those audited statements for each of the two fiscal years preceding the date of the most recent audited balance sheet [@CL-CLS-0809].

Statements of comprehensive income and cash flows are also provided for any interim period between the latest audited balance sheet and the most recent interim balance sheet being filed, and for the corresponding period of the preceding fiscal year [@CL-CLS-0810]. Those interim statements may be unaudited [@CL-CLS-0811]. A separate rule on changes in equity requires an analysis of the changes in each caption of stockholders' equity and noncontrolling interests presented in the balance sheets, given in a note or separate statement [@CL-CLS-0812]. The analysis is a reconciliation of the beginning balance to the ending balance for each period for which a statement of comprehensive income is required to be filed, with all significant reconciling items described by appropriate captions and with contributions from and distributions to owners shown separately [@CL-CLS-0813; @CL-CLS-0483].

## How it works

The registrant counts back from the date of the most recent audited balance sheet and files audited income and cash flow statements for each of the three fiscal years [@CL-CLS-0807]. A registrant that has existed for a shorter time files for that shorter period [@CL-CLS-0808]. An emerging growth company may file two years in an initial public offering registration statement [@CL-CLS-0809]. Separately, Article 8 applies to financial statements filed for smaller reporting companies [@CL-CLS-0859]. A smaller reporting company that elects the Article 8 form and content need not apply the other form and content requirements, except for listed ones [@CL-CLS-0860], and it files audited statements of comprehensive income, cash flows and changes in stockholders' equity for each of the two fiscal years preceding the most recent audited balance sheet, or a shorter period in business [@CL-CLS-0861]. Interim statements cover the gap since the last audited balance sheet and the matching period a year earlier [@CL-CLS-0810]. The equity analysis follows the same periods as the income statements [@CL-CLS-0813].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A registrant with a long history files its annual report. It files audited statements of comprehensive income and cash flows for the 3 latest fiscal years. It also files a 6-month interim period and the matching 6 months of the prior year, and these may be unaudited. The equity note reconciles each equity caption from its opening to its closing balance for each of the 3 years.

## Never confuse

The three-year rule is not the only period: an emerging growth company may present two years in its initial public offering registration statement [@CL-CLS-0809], and a smaller reporting company that elects Article 8 files two years of income, cash flow and equity statements whether or not it is an emerging growth company [@CL-CLS-0860; @CL-CLS-0861]. A filer should confirm which article it follows before preparing a third audited year. The balance sheet rule asks for balance sheets as of dates, while this rule asks for statements covering periods; see [Consolidated balance sheets required by Regulation S-X](cls-sx-consolidated-balance-sheets.md). The balance sheet shows each equity caption at a date, while the equity analysis, given in a note or separate statement, reconciles the beginning balance to the ending balance of each caption for each period [@CL-CLS-0812; @CL-CLS-0813]. For the management discussion of results, see [MD&A results of operations](../../fpa/kpi/fpa-mdna-results-of-operations.md).
