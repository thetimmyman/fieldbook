## Purpose

Shows which audited consolidated balance sheets a registrant files, how a new registrant and an early filing change the dates, and when an interim balance sheet is added. The rule is part of Regulation S-X, which is not FAR, DFARS or CAS. It binds financial statements filed with the Commission, which is the Securities and Exchange Commission, and a contractor that files nothing with the Commission is not bound [@CL-CLS-0850]. Whether it binds a company depends on the company's filing obligations with the Commission.

The registrant files audited balance sheets, for the registrant and its subsidiaries consolidated and for its predecessors, as of the end of each of the two most recent fiscal years [@CL-CLS-0800]. A registrant that has been in existence for less than one fiscal year files an audited balance sheet as of a date within 135 days of the date of filing the registration statement [@CL-CLS-0801].

Subject to listed conditions, a filing made soon after year end may use earlier balance sheets. For a filing other than on Form 10-K or Form 10, made within 45 days after the end of the fiscal year, where audited statements for the most recent fiscal year are not available, the balance sheets may be as of the end of the two preceding fiscal years [@CL-CLS-0802]. That relief comes with a requirement that the filing include an additional balance sheet as of an interim date at least as current as the end of the third fiscal quarter of the most recently completed fiscal year [@CL-CLS-0803]. Paragraph (c) of the rule extends the same instruction to filings, other than on Form 10-K or Form 10, made after 45 days but within the number of days that paragraph (i) sets, provided the listed conditions are met [@CL-CLS-0804]. One condition is that the registrant files annual, quarterly and other reports under the Securities Exchange Act of 1934 and all reports due have been filed [@CL-CLS-0856]. A second is that the registrant reasonably and in good faith expects to report income attributable to the registrant, after taxes, for the most recent fiscal year, for which audited statements are not yet available [@CL-CLS-0857]. A third is that the registrant reported income attributable to the registrant, after taxes, for at least one of the two fiscal years immediately before that year [@CL-CLS-0858]. For those later filings, where the conditions are not met, the filing must include the audited balance sheets required by paragraph (a) [@CL-CLS-0805]. Paragraph (i) sets the number of days at 60 days (75 days for fiscal years ending before December 15, 2006) for large accelerated filers, 75 days for accelerated filers and 90 days for all other registrants [@CL-CLS-0851]. An interim balance sheet provided under the rule may be unaudited [@CL-CLS-0806], and the most recent interim balance sheet included in a filing must be at least as current as the most recent balance sheet filed with the Commission on Form 10-Q [@CL-CLS-1566].

A separate rule covers discount on shares: discount on shares, or any unamortized balance of it, shall be shown separately as a deduction from the applicable account or accounts as circumstances require [@CL-CLS-1455].

## How it works

The registrant starts with two audited year-end balance sheets, consolidated with its subsidiaries [@CL-CLS-0800]. Two cases change the dates: a registrant in existence for less than one fiscal year [@CL-CLS-0801], and an early filing made before audited statements for the latest year are available [@CL-CLS-0802; @CL-CLS-0803]. The later window is measured in a number of days that paragraph (i) of the rule sets by type of filer [@CL-CLS-0804; @CL-CLS-0851]. An interim balance sheet may be unaudited [@CL-CLS-0806], but the most recent one in a filing must be at least as current as the most recent balance sheet filed on Form 10-Q [@CL-CLS-1566].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A new registrant has existed for 8 months and files a registration statement. Its audited balance sheet is dated 100 days before the filing date. That is within 135 days, so it meets the new-registrant rule. A balance sheet dated 150 days before filing would not.

## Never confuse

The 135 days measure the age of a new registrant's audited balance sheet at the filing date, and the 45 days measure how soon after year end a filing may use the earlier year-end balance sheets [@CL-CLS-0801; @CL-CLS-0802]. The statements of comprehensive income and cash flows follow their own period rule, covered in [Statements of comprehensive income, cash flows and equity under Regulation S-X](cls-sx-income-cash-flow-equity-statements.md). Regulation S-X is a rule for financial statements filed with the Commission, so FAR and CAS cost rules do not create this duty [@CL-CLS-0850]. For the management discussion that accompanies the financial statements, see [MD&A objective and liquidity](../../fpa/planning-chain/fpa-mdna-objective-and-liquidity.md).

## Company calibration

Whether the company is a registrant, and if not, which lender or investor calendar sets its audited balance sheet dates. [@Q-006]
