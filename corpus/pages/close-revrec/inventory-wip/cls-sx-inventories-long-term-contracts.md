## Purpose

Shows what a registrant discloses about inventories: the classes, the basis and cost elements, general and administrative costs and LIFO, and for long-term contracts the excess production costs, claims and progress payments netted against inventory. This card covers only the inventories item of the balance sheet rule. Regulation S-X is not FAR, DFARS or CAS. It binds financial statements filed with the Commission, which is the Securities and Exchange Commission, and a contractor that files nothing with the Commission is not bound [@CL-CLS-0850]. Whether it binds a company depends on the company's filing obligations with the Commission.

The amounts of major classes of inventory such as finished goods, inventoried costs relating to long-term contracts or programs, work in process and raw materials are stated separately in the balance sheet or in a note, if practicable [@CL-CLS-0826]. If the LIFO method is used, the excess of replacement or current cost over stated LIFO value is stated parenthetically or in a note, if material [@CL-CLS-0827]. The basis of determining the amounts is stated, and if cost is used for any portion, the description includes the nature of the cost elements in inventory [@CL-CLS-0828]. Elements of cost include, among other items, retained costs representing the excess of manufacturing or production costs over amounts charged to cost of sales or delivered or in-process units, initial tooling or other deferred startup costs, or general and administrative costs [@CL-CLS-0829]. If any general and administrative costs are charged to inventory, a note states the aggregate amount incurred in each period and the actual or estimated amount remaining in inventory at each balance sheet date [@CL-CLS-0830].

One listed kind of long-term contract or program is one for which gross profits are recognized on a percentage-of-completion method or any variant of it [@CL-CLS-0831]. In either listed case, the contracts or programs have associated with them material amounts of inventories or unbilled receivables and have been or are expected to be performed over a period of more than twelve months [@CL-CLS-0832]. Contracts or programs of shorter duration may also be included, if deemed appropriate [@CL-CLS-0854]. For all long-term contracts or programs, the listed information, if applicable, is stated in a note [@CL-CLS-0833]. One listed item is the aggregate manufacturing or production cost and related deferred cost that exceeds the aggregate estimated cost of in-process and delivered units on the estimated average cost basis [@CL-CLS-0834]. Another is the aggregate amount of claims or other similar items subject to uncertainty, with a description of the nature and status of the principal items [@CL-CLS-0835]. A third is the amount of progress payments netted against inventory at the balance sheet date [@CL-CLS-0836].

## How it works

The registrant splits inventory into classes and states the basis of its amounts [@CL-CLS-0826; @CL-CLS-0828]. Where it uses cost, it names the cost elements, and general and administrative costs charged to inventory get their own note [@CL-CLS-0829; @CL-CLS-0830]. For long-term contracts it adds a note on excess production cost, claims and uncertain items, and progress payments netted against inventory [@CL-CLS-0833; @CL-CLS-0834; @CL-CLS-0835; @CL-CLS-0836]. The source also lists a LIFO excess that is stated when material [@CL-CLS-0827]. The receivables item of the same rule is covered in [Receivables and long-term contract amounts on the balance sheet under Regulation S-X](../revenue/cls-sx-receivables-balance-sheet.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A registrant carries inventory of 500, made up of 200 finished goods, 250 contract inventory and 50 raw materials. It charges 30 of general and administrative cost to inventory in the year, and 12 of that remains in inventory at the balance sheet date. The note states both amounts. Progress payments of 80 are netted against the contract inventory, and the note states the 80.

## Never confuse

The progress payment note states how much was netted against inventory at the balance sheet date, and it does not say who holds title [@CL-CLS-0836]. For title, risk of loss and transfers under the Progress Payments clause, see [Government title to progress payment inventory](cls-government-title-progress-payment-inventory.md). The general and administrative note is a financial statement disclosure about costs charged to inventory, and it is not a rule for allocating those costs under cost accounting standards [@CL-CLS-0830].
