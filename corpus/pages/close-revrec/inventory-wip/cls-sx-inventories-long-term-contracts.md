## Purpose

Shows what a registrant discloses about inventories: the major classes, the basis and cost elements, general and administrative costs and LIFO, and for long-term contracts or programs the excess manufacturing or production costs, claims and progress payments netted against inventory [@CL-CLS-0826; @CL-CLS-0827; @CL-CLS-0828; @CL-CLS-0830; @CL-CLS-0833; @CL-CLS-0834; @CL-CLS-0835; @CL-CLS-0836].

The amounts of major classes of inventory such as finished goods, inventoried costs relating to long-term contracts or programs, work in process and raw materials shall be stated separately in the balance sheet or in a note thereto, if practicable [@CL-CLS-0826]. If the LIFO inventory method is used, the excess of replacement or current cost over stated LIFO value shall, if material, be stated parenthetically or in a note to the financial statements [@CL-CLS-0827]. The basis of determining the amounts shall be stated, and if cost is used to determine any portion of the inventory amounts, the description of this method shall include the nature of the cost elements included in inventory [@CL-CLS-0828]. Elements of cost include, among other items, retained costs representing the excess of manufacturing or production costs over the amounts charged to cost of sales or delivered or in-process units, initial tooling or other deferred startup costs, or general and administrative costs [@CL-CLS-0829]. If any general and administrative costs are charged to inventory, a note to the financial statements shall state the aggregate amount of the general and administrative costs incurred in each period and the actual or estimated amount remaining in inventory at the date of each balance sheet [@CL-CLS-0830].

For purposes of the receivables and inventories items of the balance sheet rule (§§ 210.5-02.3 and 210.5-02.6), long-term contracts or programs include two kinds: one is all contracts or programs for which gross profits are recognized on a percentage-of-completion method of accounting or any variant thereof, such as delivered unit, cost to cost, or physical completion [@CL-CLS-0831]. The other is any contracts or programs accounted for on a completed contract basis of accounting [@CL-CLS-1589]. In either case, the contracts or programs have associated with them material amounts of inventories or unbilled receivables and have been or are expected to be performed over a period of more than twelve months [@CL-CLS-0832]. Contracts or programs of shorter duration may also be included, if deemed appropriate [@CL-CLS-0854]. For all long-term contracts or programs, the listed information, if applicable, shall be stated in a note to the financial statements [@CL-CLS-0833]. One listed item is the aggregate amount of manufacturing or production costs and any related deferred costs (such as initial tooling costs) which exceeds the aggregate estimated cost of all in-process and delivered units on the basis of the estimated average cost of all units expected to be produced under long-term contracts and programs not yet complete, as well as that portion of such amount which would not be absorbed in cost of sales based on existing firm orders at the latest balance sheet date [@CL-CLS-0834; @CL-CLS-0484]. Another is the aggregate amount representing claims or other similar items subject to uncertainty concerning their determination or ultimate realization, with a description of the nature and status of the principal items comprising such aggregate amount [@CL-CLS-0835]. A third is the amount of progress payments netted against inventory at the date of the balance sheet [@CL-CLS-0836].

## How it works

The registrant states separately the amounts of major classes of inventory, if practicable, and states the basis of determining the amounts [@CL-CLS-0826; @CL-CLS-0828]. Where cost is used, the description includes the nature of the cost elements, and general and administrative costs charged to inventory are disclosed in a note [@CL-CLS-0828; @CL-CLS-0829; @CL-CLS-0830]. For all long-term contracts or programs, a note states, if applicable, the excess manufacturing or production and related deferred costs, claims or similar items subject to uncertainty, and progress payments netted against inventory [@CL-CLS-0833; @CL-CLS-0834; @CL-CLS-0835; @CL-CLS-0836]. If the LIFO inventory method is used, the excess of replacement or current cost over stated LIFO value shall, if material, be stated parenthetically or in a note to the financial statements [@CL-CLS-0827]. The receivables item of the same rule is covered in [Receivables and long-term contract amounts on the balance sheet under Regulation S-X](../revenue/cls-sx-receivables-balance-sheet.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A registrant carries inventory of 500, made up of 200 finished goods, 250 contract inventory and 50 raw materials. It charges 30 of general and administrative cost to inventory in the year, and 12 of that remains in inventory at the balance sheet date. The note states both amounts. Progress payments of 80 are netted against the contract inventory, and the note states the 80.

## Never confuse

The progress payment note item requires stating the amount of progress payments netted against inventory at the date of the balance sheet [@CL-CLS-0836]. For title, risk of loss and transfers under the Progress Payments clause, see [Government title to progress payment inventory](cls-government-title-progress-payment-inventory.md). If any general and administrative costs are charged to inventory, a note to the financial statements states the aggregate amount of those costs incurred in each period and the actual or estimated amount remaining in inventory at the date of each balance sheet [@CL-CLS-0830].
