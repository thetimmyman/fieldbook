## Purpose

Governs the choice between the preferred inventory basis and the total-cost basis for a termination settlement proposal, and what each basis must include and deduct.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CLS-0508] |
| Codified citation | FAR 49.206-2 and 49.100 [@CL-CLS-0508; @CL-CLS-0662] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CLS-0508] |
| Applies to | Settlement of contracts terminated for convenience: additional principles applicable to the termination for convenience and settlement of fixed-price and cost-reimbursement contracts are included in subparts 49.2 and 49.3 [@CL-CLS-0662] |

Use of the inventory basis for settlement proposals is preferred, and under this basis the contractor may propose only costs allocable to the terminated portion of the contract [@CL-CLS-0508].

When use of the inventory basis is not practicable or will unduly delay settlement, the total-cost basis (SF-1436) may be used if approved in advance by the termination contracting officer as in the examples the section gives, such as if production has not commenced and the accumulated costs represent planning and preproduction or get ready expenses [@CL-CLS-0510; @CL-CLS-1593].

## How it works

Under the inventory basis, an allowance for profit (49.202) or adjustment for loss (49.203(b)) must be made to complete the gross settlement proposal, and all unliquidated advance and progress payments and all disposal and other credits known when the proposal is submitted must then be deducted [@CL-CLS-0509].

When the total-cost basis is used under a complete termination, the contractor must itemize all costs incurred under the contract up to the effective date of termination, and the costs of settlements with subcontractors and applicable settlement expenses must also be added [@CL-CLS-0651]. An allowance for profit (49.202) or adjustment for loss (49.203(c)) must be made [@CL-CLS-0652]. The contract price for all end items delivered or to be delivered and accepted must be deducted, and all unliquidated advance and progress payments and disposal and other credits known when the proposal is submitted must also be deducted [@CL-CLS-0653].

When the total-cost basis is used under a partial termination, the settlement proposal shall not be submitted until completion of the continued portion of the contract [@CL-CLS-0511]. Settlement proposals may not be submitted on any basis other than paragraph (a) or (b) of the section (the inventory basis or the total-cost basis) without the prior approval of the chief of the contracting or contract administration office [@CL-CLS-0512].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor suffers a complete termination on a production line where unit costs for work in process cannot readily be established. With advance approval from the termination contracting officer, it files on the total-cost basis. It itemizes incurred costs of 1,200,000 and adds settlement expenses of 40,000 and a profit allowance of 100,000. It then deducts 500,000 for end items already delivered and accepted and 200,000 of unliquidated progress payments. The answer: the net proposal is 640,000.

## Never confuse

The inventory basis and the total-cost basis are not interchangeable: the inventory basis confines the proposal to costs allocable to the terminated portion, whereas the total-cost basis under a complete termination starts from all costs incurred under the contract up to the effective date of termination and deducts the contract price of delivered and accepted end items [@CL-CLS-0508; @CL-CLS-0651; @CL-CLS-0653]. For how profit and loss are set, see [Termination settlements: profit allowance and adjustment for loss](../revenue/cls-termination-settlements-profit-and-loss.md). For the deduction and ceiling rules that apply to the settlement amount, see [Termination settlements: deductions, completed end items, and settlement limitation](../revenue/cls-termination-settlement-deductions-and-limitations.md). For allowability of the costs themselves, see [Termination costs](cls-termination-costs.md). For progress payment title and recovery, see [Government title to progress payment inventory](cls-government-title-progress-payment-inventory.md). For the clause that sets deadlines and settlement mechanics, see [Termination for convenience settlement](../../contract-finance/billing-payment/ctf-termination-for-convenience-settlement.md).
