## Purpose

Explains the nature of Government title to inventory and work in process under progress payments, the rules for inventory transfers, and the contractor's risk of loss.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CLS-0217] |
| Codified citation | FAR 32.503-15 [@CL-CLS-0217] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CLS-0217] |
| Applies to | Contracts with the Progress Payments clause [@CL-CLS-0217] |

Property to which the Government obtains title solely by operation of the Progress Payments clause is not, as a consequence, Government-furnished property [@CL-CLS-0217].

Under the Progress Payments clause, and except for normal spoilage, the contractor bears the risk of loss for Government property under the clause, even though title is vested in the Government, unless the Government has expressly assumed this risk [@CL-CLS-0219].

## How it works

When title to materials or other inventories is vested in the Government under the Progress Payments clause, the contractor may transfer inventory items from the contract for its own use or other disposition only if, and on terms, approved by the contracting officer [@CL-CLS-0218]. When it transfers items with that approval, it shall eliminate the allocable costs from the costs of contract performance and repay or credit the Government the unliquidated progress payments allocable to the transferred property [@CL-CLS-0311]. The contractor may sell or otherwise dispose of current production scrap in the ordinary course of business on its own volition, even if title has vested, and the contracting officer requires it to credit the proceeds to the costs of contract performance [@CL-CLS-0312; @CL-CLS-0313]. Under defense regulations, an administrative contracting officer determination that the contractor's material management and accounting system meets the criteria at DFARS 252.242-7004(d)(7) constitutes the required approval under FAR 32.503-15(d) [@CL-CLS-0221]. If a loss occurs in connection with property for which the contractor bears the risk, the contractor must repay the unliquidated progress payments based on costs allocable to the property [@CL-CLS-0220]. Excess property that remains after performance is complete, all contractor obligations are satisfied and progress payments are fully liquidated is outside the scope of the Progress Payments clause [@CL-CLS-0314].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor holds 200,000 of work-in-process cost financed by progress payments. A flood destroys property carrying 50,000 of that cost, and the contract has no express assumption of the risk by the Government. The progress payments based on the 50,000 of cost are 40,000 and none has been liquidated. The answer: the contractor bears the risk of loss even though title is vested in the Government, and it repays the 40,000 of unliquidated progress payments allocable to the lost property.

## Never confuse

Title obtained solely under the Progress Payments clause does not make the property Government-furnished property [@CL-CLS-0217]. Vested title under the Progress Payments clause does not shift risk of loss to the Government, and the clauses related to progress payments, default and terminations are not a Government assumption of that risk [@CL-CLS-0219; @CL-CLS-0315].
