## Purpose

Explains the nature of Government title to inventory and work in process under progress payments, the rules for inventory transfers, and the contractor's risk of loss.

## The rule

Property to which the Government obtains title by operation of the Progress Payments clause solely is not, as a consequence, Government-furnished property [@CL-CLS-0217].

Under the Progress Payments clause, and except for normal spoilage, the contractor bears the risk of loss for Government property under the clause, even though title is vested in the Government, unless the Government has expressly assumed this risk [@CL-CLS-0219].

## How it works

When the title to materials or other inventories is vested in the Government under the Progress Payments clause, the contractor may transfer the inventory items from the contract for its own use or other disposition only if, and on terms, approved by the contracting officer [@CL-CLS-0218]. When it transfers items with that approval, the contractor shall eliminate the costs allocable to the transferred property from the costs of contract performance and repay or credit to the Government an amount equal to the unliquidated progress payments allocable to the transferred property [@CL-CLS-0311]. The contractor may sell or otherwise dispose of current production scrap in the ordinary course of business on its own volition, even if title has vested in the Government under the Progress Payments clause, and the contracting officer shall require the contractor to credit the costs of the contract performance with the proceeds of the scrap disposition [@CL-CLS-0312; @CL-CLS-0313]. Under defense regulations, an administrative contracting officer determination that the contractor's material management and accounting system conforms to the system criteria at DFARS 252.242-7004(d)(7) constitutes the contracting officer approval requirement of FAR 32.503-15(d) [@CL-CLS-0221]. If a loss occurs in connection with property for which the contractor bears the risk, the contractor is obligated to repay to the Government the amount of unliquidated progress payments based on costs allocable to the property [@CL-CLS-0220]. If excess property remains after the contract performance is complete and all contractor obligations under the contract are satisfied, including full liquidation of progress payments, the excess property is outside the scope of the Progress Payments clause [@CL-CLS-0314].

Effective February 1, 2026, DoD contracting officers shall use the revised FAR Part 32 published on the Revolutionary FAR Overhaul web page in lieu of the text codified at 48 CFR chapter 1 [@CL-CTF-4046]. RFO FAR 32.503-15 states the rules described above: property to which the Government obtains title by operation of the Progress Payments clause solely is not, as a consequence, Government-furnished property [@CL-CLS-1239]. The contractor may sell or otherwise dispose of current production scrap in the ordinary course of business on its own volition, even if title has vested in the Government under the Progress Payments clause [@CL-CLS-1240], and the contracting officer must require the contractor to credit the costs of the contract performance with the proceeds of the scrap disposition [@CL-CLS-1241]. When the title to materials or other inventories is vested in the Government under the Progress Payments clause, the contractor may transfer the inventory items from the contract for its own use or other disposition only if, and on terms, approved by the contracting officer [@CL-CLS-1242]. The contractor is required to eliminate the costs allocable to the transferred property from the costs of contract performance and to repay or credit to the Government an amount equal to the unliquidated progress payments allocable to the transferred property [@CL-CLS-1243]. If excess property remains after the contract performance is complete and all contractor obligations under the contract are satisfied, including full liquidation of progress payments, the excess property is outside the scope of the Progress Payments clause [@CL-CLS-1244]. RFO FAR 32.503-16(a) states that under the Progress Payments clause, and except for normal spoilage, the contractor bears the risk of loss for Government property under the clause, even though title is vested in the Government, unless the Government has expressly assumed this risk [@CL-CLS-1245], and that the clauses prescribed in the regulation related to progress payments, default, and terminations do not constitute a Government assumption of this risk [@CL-CLS-1246]. RFO FAR 32.503-16(b) states that if a loss occurs in connection with property for which the contractor bears the risk, the contractor is obligated to repay to the Government the amount of unliquidated progress payments based on costs allocable to the property [@CL-CLS-1247].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor holds 200,000 of work-in-process cost financed by progress payments. A flood destroys property carrying 50,000 of that cost, and the contract has no express assumption of the risk by the Government. The progress payments based on the 50,000 of cost are 40,000 and none has been liquidated. The answer: the contractor bears the risk of loss even though title is vested in the Government, and it repays the 40,000 of unliquidated progress payments allocable to the lost property.

## Never confuse

Property to which the Government obtains title by operation of the Progress Payments clause solely is not, as a consequence, Government-furnished property [@CL-CLS-0217]. Vested title under the Progress Payments clause does not shift risk of loss to the Government, and the clauses prescribed in the regulation related to progress payments, default, and terminations do not constitute a Government assumption of this risk [@CL-CLS-0219; @CL-CLS-0315].
