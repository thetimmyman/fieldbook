## Purpose

Explains the clause that tests the contractor's system for planning, controlling and accounting for material, and what follows when material weaknesses are found.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-CLS-0109] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 252.242-7004 [@CL-CLS-0109] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CLS-0109] |

The system must ensure that costs of purchased and fabricated material charged or allocated to a contract are based on valid time-phased requirements [@CL-CLS-0111].

Acceptable means that the system generally complies with the system criteria in the clause [@CL-CLS-0110].

## How it works

The system must cost material from valid time-phased requirements, and its costing methodology may be standard or actual cost or any of the inventory costing methods in 48 CFR 9904.411-50(b), provided the contractor maintains consistency across all contract and customer types and from accounting period to accounting period [@CL-CLS-0111; @CL-CLS-0117].

A 98 percent bill of material accuracy and a 95 percent master production schedule accuracy are desirable goals, and a 95 percent record accuracy level is desirable [@CL-CLS-0112; @CL-CLS-0113]. A system below those levels must provide adequate evidence of no material harm to the Government and that the cost to meet the goal is excessive in relation to the impact on the Government [@CL-CLS-0166; @CL-CLS-0167]. Record accuracy also requires periodic reconciliation of recorded inventory quantities to physical inventory by part number [@CL-CLS-0113].

Parts and their costs should move within the same billing period, except that in the few instances where this is not appropriate the contractor may use the loan/pay-back technique [@CL-CLS-0116], whose procedures must be approved by the administrative contracting officer [@CL-CLS-0115].

Significant system changes must be disclosed to that officer at least 30 days before implementation, and internal review results go to that officer on request [@CL-CLS-0114; @CL-CLS-0121].

The contractor must respond within 30 days to a written initial determination [@CL-CLS-0118], stating its rationale in writing if it disagrees [@CL-CLS-0165]. The final determination concerns remaining material weaknesses and the adequacy of corrective action [@CL-CLS-0163], and system disapproval if one or more material weaknesses remain [@CL-CLS-0164]. After the final determination of material weaknesses the contractor has 45 days from receipt to correct them or submit an acceptable corrective action plan [@CL-CLS-0119]. If the final determination disapproves the system and the contract includes the Contractor Business Systems clause, payments are withheld in accordance with that clause [@CL-CLS-0120].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A bill of materials is 90 percent accurate, below the stated goal. The contractor shows that the gap causes no material harm to the Government and that the cost of closing it is excessive in relation to the impact on the Government. Inventory records reconcile at 95 percent, which meets the desired level.

## Never confuse

This is not the purchasing system review, and it is not a cost allowability finding on a material purchase.

It tests the material planning, charging and accounting logic against the system criteria [@CL-CLS-0110].

The disclosure duty runs on significant system changes rather than on every transaction [@CL-CLS-0114].
