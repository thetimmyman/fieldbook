## Purpose

Explains the clause that tests the contractor's system for planning, controlling and accounting for material, and what follows when material weaknesses are found.

The system must ensure that costs of purchased and fabricated material charged or allocated to a contract are based on valid time-phased requirements [@CL-CLS-0111].

Acceptable material management and accounting system means a material management and accounting system that generally complies with the system criteria in paragraph (d) of the clause [@CL-CLS-0110].

## How it works

The system must ensure that costs of purchased and fabricated material charged or allocated to a contract are based on valid time-phased requirements [@CL-CLS-0111]. Its costing methodology may be standard or actual cost or any of the inventory costing methods in 48 CFR 9904.411-50(b), and the contractor shall maintain consistency across all contract and customer types, and from accounting period to accounting period, for initial charging and transfer charging [@CL-CLS-0117]. The clause still points to 48 CFR 9904.411-50(b), but CAS 411 was wholly rescinded with an effective date of August 7, 2026, and section 9904.411 is printed as reserved, as covered in [CAS-GAAP conformance](../../compliance/cas-allowability/cmp-cas-gaap-conformance-2026.md) [@CL-CMP-1500; @CL-CMP-1501; @CL-CMP-1524].

A 98 percent bill of material accuracy and a 95 percent master production schedule accuracy are desirable as a goal in order to ensure that requirements are both valid and appropriately time-phased, and a 95 percent record accuracy level is desirable [@CL-CLS-0112; @CL-CLS-0113]. If systems have accuracy levels below those, the contractor shall provide adequate evidence both that there is no material harm to the Government due to lower accuracy levels and that the cost to meet the accuracy goal is excessive in relation to the impact on the Government [@CL-CLS-0166; @CL-CLS-0167]. Record accuracy also includes reconciliation of recorded inventory quantities to physical inventory by part number on a periodic basis [@CL-CLS-0113].

The system should transfer parts and associated costs within the same billing period, except that in the few instances where this may not be appropriate the contractor may accomplish the material transaction using a loan/pay-back technique [@CL-CLS-0116], whose procedures must be approved by the Administrative Contracting Officer [@CL-CLS-0115].

The contractor shall disclose significant changes in its MMAS to the Administrative Contracting Officer at least 30 days prior to implementation, and shall provide to the Administrative Contracting Officer, upon request, the results of internal reviews that it has conducted to ensure compliance with established MMAS policies, procedures, and operating instructions [@CL-CLS-0114; @CL-CLS-0121].

The contractor shall respond within 30 days to a written initial determination from the contracting officer that identifies material weaknesses in its MMAS [@CL-CLS-0118], stating its rationale in writing if it disagrees [@CL-CLS-0165]. The contracting officer will evaluate the response and notify the contractor in writing of the contracting officer's final determination concerning remaining material weaknesses, the adequacy of any proposed or completed corrective action, and system disapproval if the contracting officer determines that one or more material weaknesses remain [@CL-CLS-0163; @CL-CLS-0164]. If the contractor receives the contracting officer's final determination of material weaknesses, it shall within 45 days of receipt of the final determination either correct the material weaknesses or submit an acceptable corrective action plan showing milestones and actions to eliminate the material weaknesses [@CL-CLS-0119; @CL-CLS-1588; @CL-CLS-1606]. If the contracting officer makes a final determination to disapprove the contractor's MMAS and the contract includes the clause at 252.242-7005, Contractor Business Systems, the contracting officer will withhold payments in accordance with that clause [@CL-CLS-0120].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A bill of materials is 90 percent accurate, below the stated goal. The contractor shows that the gap causes no material harm to the Government and that the cost of closing it is excessive in relation to the impact on the Government. Inventory records reconcile at 95 percent, which meets the desired level.

## Never confuse

A material management and accounting system is not the property system of the Government Property clause: it is the contractor's system or systems for planning, controlling, and accounting for the acquisition, use, issuing, and disposition of material, and an acceptable system is one that generally complies with the system criteria in paragraph (d) of the clause [@CL-CLS-0109; @CL-CLS-0110].

The disclosure duty runs on significant changes in the MMAS, which must be disclosed to the Administrative Contracting Officer at least 30 days prior to implementation [@CL-CLS-0114].
