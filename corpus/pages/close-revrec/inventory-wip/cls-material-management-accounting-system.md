## Purpose

Explains the clause that tests the contractor's system for planning, controlling and accounting for material, and what follows when material weaknesses are found.

The system must ensure that costs of purchased and fabricated material charged or allocated to a contract are based on valid time-phased requirements [@CL-CLS-0111].

Acceptable means that the system generally complies with the system criteria in the clause [@CL-CLS-0110].

## How it works

The system must cost material from valid time-phased requirements, and its costing methodology may be standard or actual cost or any of the inventory costing methods in 48 CFR 9904.411-50(b), and the contractor shall maintain consistency across all contract and customer types, and from accounting period to accounting period, for initial charging and transfer charging [@CL-CLS-0111; @CL-CLS-0117]. The clause still points to 48 CFR 9904.411-50(b), but CAS 411 was wholly rescinded with effect from August 7, 2026, and section 9904.411 is now printed as reserved [@CL-CMP-1500; @CL-CMP-1501; @CL-CMP-1524]. The CAS conformance brief explains what moved and what was removed.

A 98 percent bill of material accuracy and a 95 percent master production schedule accuracy are desirable goals, and a 95 percent record accuracy level is desirable [@CL-CLS-0112; @CL-CLS-0113]. If a system has accuracy levels below those, the contractor shall provide adequate evidence both that there is no material harm to the Government due to the lower accuracy levels and that the cost to meet the accuracy goal is excessive in relation to the impact on the Government [@CL-CLS-0166; @CL-CLS-0167]. Record accuracy also requires periodic reconciliation of recorded inventory quantities to physical inventory by part number [@CL-CLS-0113].

Parts and their costs should move within the same billing period, except that in the few instances where this is not appropriate the contractor may use the loan/pay-back technique [@CL-CLS-0116], whose procedures must be approved by the administrative contracting officer [@CL-CLS-0115].

Significant system changes must be disclosed to that officer at least 30 days before implementation, and internal review results go to that officer on request [@CL-CLS-0114; @CL-CLS-0121].

The contractor shall respond within 30 days to a written initial determination from the contracting officer that identifies material weaknesses in its system [@CL-CLS-0118], stating its rationale in writing if it disagrees [@CL-CLS-0165]. The contracting officer will evaluate the response and notify the contractor in writing of a final determination concerning remaining material weaknesses and the adequacy of any proposed or completed corrective action [@CL-CLS-0163], and system disapproval if the contracting officer determines that one or more material weaknesses remain [@CL-CLS-0164]. If the contractor receives the final determination of material weaknesses, it shall within 45 days of receipt either correct the material weaknesses or submit an acceptable corrective action plan showing milestones and actions to eliminate them [@CL-CLS-0119; @CL-CLS-1588; @CL-CLS-1606]. If the final determination disapproves the system and the contract includes the Contractor Business Systems clause, the contracting officer will withhold payments in accordance with that clause [@CL-CLS-0120].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A bill of materials is 90 percent accurate, below the stated goal. The contractor shows that the gap causes no material harm to the Government and that the cost of closing it is excessive in relation to the impact on the Government. Inventory records reconcile at 95 percent, which meets the desired level.

## Never confuse

This is not the purchasing system review, and it is not a cost allowability finding on a material purchase.

It tests the material planning, charging and accounting logic against the system criteria [@CL-CLS-0110].

The disclosure duty runs on significant system changes rather than on every transaction [@CL-CLS-0114].
