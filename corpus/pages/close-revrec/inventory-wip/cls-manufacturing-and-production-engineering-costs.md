## Purpose

Explains the allowability of manufacturing and production engineering effort and distinguishes production engineering from research and capitalized assets.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CLS-0210] |
| Codified citation | FAR 31.205-25 [@CL-CLS-0210] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CLS-0210] |
| Applies to | Contracts with the cost principles clause [@CL-CLS-0210] |

The costs of manufacturing and production engineering effort described in paragraphs (a)(1) through (a)(4) of the cost principle are all allowable [@CL-CLS-0210].

The principle does not cover basic and applied research effort related to new technology, materials, systems, processes, methods, equipment, tools and techniques [@CL-CLS-0308]. Where manufacturing or production development costs are capitalized or required to be capitalized under the contractor's capitalization policies, allowable cost is determined in accordance with the depreciation requirements in FAR 31.205-11 [@CL-CLS-0212].

## How it works

The allowable effort includes developing and deploying new or improved materials, systems, processes, methods, equipment, tools and techniques used or expected to be used in production, and developing and deploying pilot production lines [@CL-CLS-0305]. It also includes improving current production functions, such as plant layout, production scheduling and control, and inspection techniques [@CL-CLS-0306], and producibility analysis for production suitability and to optimize manufacturing processes [@CL-CLS-0307]. Development effort for manufacturing or production materials, systems, processes, methods, equipment, tools and techniques that are intended for sale is also governed by FAR 31.205-18 [@CL-CLS-0211]. Where costs are capitalized or required to be capitalized under the contractor's policies, the depreciation requirements decide the allowable cost [@CL-CLS-0212].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor incurs 80,000 to analyze manufacturing producibility and to improve the plant layout of an active production line. The contractor's policies do not require the cost to be capitalized, and the effort does not produce anything intended for sale. The answer: the effort is producibility analysis and an improvement to a current production function, so the 80,000 falls within the allowable effort.

## Never confuse

Production engineering is not research. The principle does not cover basic and applied research effort [@CL-CLS-0308], and development effort for manufacturing or production methods and tools that are intended for sale is also governed by FAR 31.205-18 [@CL-CLS-0211]. The independent research and development rules are described in [IR&D and B&P costs](../../compliance/cas-allowability/cmp-ird-and-bp-costs.md), and depreciation of capitalized development cost in [Depreciation](../../compliance/cas-allowability/cmp-depreciation.md).
