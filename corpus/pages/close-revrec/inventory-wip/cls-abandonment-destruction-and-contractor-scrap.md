## Purpose

Shows when contractor inventory may be abandoned or destroyed, what the inventory disposal report must cover and how contractor scrap is handled.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CLS-0742] |
| Codified citation | FAR 45.603, 45.605 and 45.606 [@CL-CLS-0742; @CL-CLS-0746; @CL-CLS-0748]; FAR 2.101 [@CL-CLS-1526] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CLS-0742] |
| Applies to | Contractor inventory and scrap handled through plant clearance [@CL-CLS-0742; @CL-CLS-0748] |

The conditions that precede the actions permitted without further approval are that contractor inventory has been processed through the reutilization screening process without success under the process prescribed in 45.602-2 and that the property has no commercial value, does not require demilitarization, and does not constitute a danger to public health or welfare [@CL-CLS-0742]. Provided the property meets those property conditions, plant clearance officers or other authorized officials may without further approval take the listed actions [@CL-CLS-0743]. The listed actions are alternatives: directing the contractor to destroy the property, abandoning non-sensitive property at the contractor's or subcontractor's premises, or abandoning sensitive property at those premises with contractor consent [@CL-CLS-0744]. Unless the property qualifies for one of the exceptions under FMR 102-36.330, the plant clearance officer or requesting official will ensure prior public notice of such actions of abandonment or destruction consistent with FMR 102-36.325 [@CL-CLS-1587].

Approval by a Government reviewing official at least one level higher than the plant clearance officer or other agency authorized official is a condition of the authority of those officials to authorize the abandonment, or order the destruction, of other contractor inventory [@CL-CLS-0745]. That authority covers inventory at the contractor's or subcontractor's premises, may be exercised in accordance with the cited Federal Management Regulation provisions and consistent with the criteria the paragraph lists [@CL-CLS-0752]. The first listed criterion is that the property is not considered sensitive, does not require demilitarization, has no commercial value or reutilization, transfer or donation potential, and does not constitute a danger to public health or welfare [@CL-CLS-0753]. The second is that the estimated cost of continued care and handling of the property, including advertising, storage and other costs associated with making the sale, exceed the estimated proceeds from its sale [@CL-CLS-0754].

The FAR definitions section describes scrap as personal property that has no value except its basic metallic, mineral, or organic content [@CL-CLS-1526]. The RFO text of FAR 2.101 defines scrap as personal property that has no value except its basic metallic, mineral, or organic content, the same in substance as the codified definition [@CL-CLS-1538; @CL-CLS-1526].

## How it works

The plant clearance officer shall promptly prepare an SF 1424, Inventory Disposal Report, following disposition of the property identified on an inventory disposal schedule and the crediting of any related proceeds [@CL-CLS-0746]. The report shall identify any lost or otherwise unaccounted for property and any changes in quantity or value of the property made by the contractor after submission of the initial inventory disposal schedule [@CL-CLS-0747]. For scrap, the property administrator should, in coordination with the plant clearance officer, ensure that contractor scrap disposal processes, methods, and practices allow for effective, efficient, and proper disposition and are properly documented in the contractor's property management procedures [@CL-CLS-0748]. Absent contract terms and conditions to the contrary, the Government may abandon parts removed and replaced from property as a result of normal maintenance actions or removed from property as a result of the repair, maintenance, overhaul, or modification process [@CL-CLS-0749].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

Reutilization screening of a lot of obsolete fasteners fails, and the lot has no commercial value, needs no demilitarization and poses no danger to public health or welfare. The plant clearance officer may direct destruction without further approval. After disposition, the officer prepares the disposal report and lists 3 units that the contractor could not account for. The answer: the report must identify those lost units.

## Never confuse

Abandoning contractor inventory is not the same as disposing of scrap. Scrap disposal has its own documentation expectation and a separate permission to abandon parts removed in normal maintenance, absent contrary contract terms [@CL-CLS-0748; @CL-CLS-0749]. For termination inventory, see [Termination inventory: disposal schedules and government property accounting](cls-termination-inventory-schedules-and-property-accounting.md).
