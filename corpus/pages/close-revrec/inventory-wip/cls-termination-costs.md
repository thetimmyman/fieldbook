## Purpose

Explains the principles governing the allowability of costs resulting from contract termination, including common inventory items, continuing costs, and settlement expenses.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CLS-0213] |
| Codified citation | FAR 31.205-42 [@CL-CLS-0213] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 31.205-42 [@CL-CLS-1226; @CL-CLS-1227; @CL-CLS-1228; @CL-CLS-1229; @CL-CLS-1230; @CL-CLS-1231] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CLS-0213] |
| Applies to | Terminated contracts, with the termination cost principles used in conjunction with the other cost principles in subpart 31.2 [@CL-CLS-0309] |

The costs of common items reasonably usable on the contractor's other work shall not be allowable unless the contractor submits evidence that the items could not be retained at cost without sustaining a loss [@CL-CLS-0213].

Costs which cannot be discontinued immediately after the effective date of termination despite all reasonable efforts by the contractor are generally allowable [@CL-CLS-0214].

## How it works

The cost principles peculiar to termination situations are to be used in conjunction with the other cost principles in subpart 31.2 [@CL-CLS-0309]. When determining if items can reasonably be used on other work of the contractor, the contracting officer should consider the contractor's plans and orders for current and planned production [@CL-CLS-1590], and contemporaneous purchases of common items by the contractor shall be regarded as evidence that such items are reasonably usable on the contractor's other work [@CL-CLS-1591]. Despite all reasonable efforts by the contractor, costs which cannot be discontinued immediately after the effective date of termination are generally allowable, whereas any costs continuing after the effective date of the termination due to the negligent or willful failure of the contractor to discontinue the costs shall be unallowable [@CL-CLS-0214; @CL-CLS-0215]. Settlement expenses are generally allowable, including accounting, legal, clerical, and similar costs reasonably necessary for the preparation and presentation, including supporting data, of settlement claims to the contracting officer and for the termination and settlement of subcontracts [@CL-CLS-0310; @CL-CLS-1592]. If settlement expenses are significant, a cost account or work order shall be established to separately identify and accumulate them [@CL-CLS-0216]. How the settlement itself is agreed or determined is covered in [Termination for convenience settlement](../../contract-finance/billing-payment/ctf-termination-for-convenience-settlement.md) and [Termination settlement under cost-reimbursement contracts](../../contract-finance/billing-payment/ctf-termination-cost-reimbursement-settlement.md).

DoD contracting officers must use the FAR part 31 text published on the Revolutionary FAR Overhaul web page in lieu of the text codified at 48 CFR chapter 1 [@CL-CMP-9350], under Revision 1 effective immediately and superseding Class Deviation 2026-O0032 issued on December 19, 2025 [@CL-CMP-9351]. RFO FAR 31.205-42 states that the cost principles apply to contract terminations and are used in conjunction with the other cost principles in the subpart [@CL-CLS-1226]. Under RFO FAR 31.205-42(a)(1), the costs of items reasonably usable on the contractor's other work must not be allowable unless the contractor submits evidence that the items could not be retained at cost without sustaining a loss [@CL-CLS-1227]. Despite all reasonable efforts by the contractor, costs that cannot be discontinued immediately after the effective date of termination are generally allowable under RFO FAR 31.205-42(b)(1) [@CL-CLS-1228], and costs continuing after the effective date of the termination due to the negligent or willful failure of the contractor to discontinue the costs must be unallowable under RFO FAR 31.205-42(b)(2) [@CL-CLS-1229]. Settlement expenses, including accounting, legal, clerical, and similar costs reasonably necessary for the preparation and presentation of settlement claims, including supporting data, to the contracting officer, are generally allowable under RFO FAR 31.205-42(g)(1) [@CL-CLS-1230], and if settlement expenses are significant, a cost account or work order must be established to separately identify and accumulate them under RFO FAR 31.205-42(g)(2) [@CL-CLS-1231].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

Following a partial termination, a contractor holds standard raw material that is reasonably usable on another active production line, and it submits no evidence that the material could not be retained at cost without a loss. The material cost is not allowable. Separately, facilities costs continue for two weeks after the effective date of termination while the contractor winds down, despite all reasonable efforts to discontinue them. The answer: those continuing costs are generally allowable, and they would be unallowable only if the contractor had negligently or willfully failed to discontinue them.

## Never confuse

Allowability after a termination turns on whether the costs can be discontinued rather than on the contractor's preference: costs which cannot be discontinued immediately after the effective date of termination despite all reasonable efforts by the contractor are generally allowable, whereas any costs continuing after the effective date of the termination due to the negligent or willful failure of the contractor to discontinue the costs shall be unallowable [@CL-CLS-0214; @CL-CLS-0215]. The costs of items reasonably usable on the contractor's other work shall not be allowable unless the contractor submits evidence that the items could not be retained at cost without sustaining a loss [@CL-CLS-0213].
