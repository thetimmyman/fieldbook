## Purpose

Covers the cost-plus-fixed-fee contract: a fee fixed at inception that does not vary with actual cost, when the type is suitable, and the completion and term forms.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CLS-0576] |
| Codified citation | FAR 16.306 [@CL-CLS-0576] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CLS-0576] |
| Applies to | Cost-plus-fixed-fee contracts [@CL-CLS-0576] |

A cost-plus-fixed-fee contract is a cost-reimbursement contract that provides for payment to the contractor of a negotiated fee that is fixed at the inception of the contract [@CL-CLS-0576].

The fixed fee does not vary with actual cost, but may be adjusted as a result of changes in the work [@CL-CLS-0576]. No cost-plus-fixed-fee contract shall be awarded unless the contracting officer complies with all limitations in the two cross-referenced sections [@CL-CLS-0691].

## How it works

A cost-plus-fixed-fee contract is suitable for use when the conditions of FAR 16.301-2 are present and, for example, the contract is for the performance of research or preliminary exploration or study and the level of effort required is unknown [@CL-CLS-0577]. The two examples are alternatives: the other is a contract for development and test where using a cost-plus-incentive-fee contract is not practical [@CL-CLS-0686; @CL-CLS-1596].

The contract may take a completion form or a term form. The completion form describes the scope of work by stating a definite goal or target and specifying an end product [@CL-CLS-0578]. It normally requires the contractor to complete and deliver the specified end product within the estimated cost, if possible, as a condition for payment of the entire fixed fee [@CL-CLS-0687]. If the work cannot be completed within the estimated cost, the Government may require more effort without increase in fee, provided the Government increases the estimated cost [@CL-CLS-0688].

The term form describes the scope of work in general terms and obligates the contractor to devote a specified level of effort for a stated time period [@CL-CLS-0579]. Under it, if the Government considers performance satisfactory, the fixed fee is payable at the expiration of the agreed-upon period, upon contractor statement that the level of effort specified in the contract has been expended [@CL-CLS-0689]. The term form shall not be used unless the contractor is obligated by the contract to provide a specific level of effort within a definite time period [@CL-CLS-0690]. The completion form is preferred over the term form whenever the work, or specific milestones for the work, can be defined well enough to permit development of estimates within which the contractor can be expected to complete the work [@CL-CLS-0580].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor performs an exploratory research contract on the completion form with an estimated cost of 800,000 and a fixed fee of 60,000. It delivers the final research report with allowable costs of 750,000, within the estimated cost. The answer: the contractor is reimbursed the 750,000 of allowable cost and is paid the full 60,000 fee, because the fee does not vary with actual cost and the report was delivered within the estimated cost.

## Never confuse

A cost-plus-fixed-fee contract is not an incentive-fee contract: the fixed fee does not vary with actual cost [@CL-CLS-0576]. A cost-plus-fixed-fee term form also differs from a firm-fixed-price, level-of-effort contract, because the cost-plus type is a cost-reimbursement contract [@CL-CLS-0576]. For fee that does vary, see [Incentive fee](../../contract-finance/billing-payment/ctf-incentive-fee.md). For how fixed fee is paid and withheld, see [Fixed fee](../../contract-finance/billing-payment/ctf-fixed-fee.md). For the fixed-price effort type, see [Firm-fixed-price, level-of-effort term contracts](cls-firm-fixed-price-level-of-effort.md). For the general cost-reimbursement framework, see [Cost-reimbursement contracts](cls-cost-reimbursement-contracts.md).
