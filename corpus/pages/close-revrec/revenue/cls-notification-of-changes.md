## Purpose

Shows what the Contractor must notify under the notification of changes clause, to whom, what the notice must contain, how the Government responds, and how the notice affects an equitable adjustment.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CLS-1094] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.243-7 [@CL-CLS-1094] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.243-7 [@CL-CLS-0124] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CLS-1094] |
| Applies to | Contracts containing the Notification of Changes clause [@CL-CLS-1094] |

Except for changes identified as such in writing and signed by the Contracting Officer, the Contractor shall notify the Administrative Contracting Officer in writing promptly [@CL-CLS-1094]. The notice period is a number of calendar days left blank in the printed clause to be negotiated, running from the date that the Contractor identifies any Government conduct, including actions, inactions, and written or oral communications, that the Contractor regards as a change to the contract terms and conditions [@CL-CLS-1138]. The source does not establish the number of days, because the printed clause leaves it to be negotiated.

On the basis of the most accurate information available to the Contractor, the notice shall state the matters that the clause lists [@CL-CLS-1095]. The list includes the date, nature, and circumstances of the conduct regarded as a change, and the name, function, and activity of each Government individual and Contractor official or employee involved in or knowledgeable about the conduct [@CL-CLS-1096]. Following submission of the notice, the Contractor shall diligently continue performance of the contract to the maximum extent possible [@CL-CLS-1139].

The Contracting Officer shall promptly, within a number of calendar days left blank in the printed clause to be negotiated, after receipt of the notice, respond to the notice in writing [@CL-CLS-1097]. In responding, the Contracting Officer shall either take one of the listed responses, which include confirming that the conduct constitutes a change and, when necessary, directing the mode of further performance, and countermanding any communication regarded as a change [@CL-CLS-1098]. The listed responses also include denying that the conduct constitutes a change and, when necessary, directing the mode of further performance, and a separate listed response for the event that the Contractor's notice information is inadequate to make a decision [@CL-CLS-1140].

## How it works

The Contractor notifies the Administrative Contracting Officer of conduct it regards as a change [@CL-CLS-1094; @CL-CLS-1138], and the notice carries the listed content [@CL-CLS-1095; @CL-CLS-1096]. The Contractor keeps performing after the notice [@CL-CLS-1139]. The Contracting Officer responds in writing by taking one of the listed responses [@CL-CLS-1097; @CL-CLS-1098; @CL-CLS-1140]. An equitable adjustment is available where the Contracting Officer confirms that Government conduct effected a change as alleged and the conduct causes an increase or decrease in cost or time of performance [@CL-CLS-1099]. The adjustment is made in the contract price or delivery schedule or both, and in such other provisions as may be affected [@CL-CLS-1100]. It does not include increased costs or time extensions for delay resulting from the Contractor's failure to provide notice or to continue performance as the clause provides [@CL-CLS-1101].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers must use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified FAR part 52 text, as Class Deviation 2026-O0038 directs, effective February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. RFO FAR 52.243-7(b) says that, except for changes identified in writing and signed by the Contracting Officer, the Contractor shall notify the Administrative Contracting Officer in writing promptly [@CL-CLS-0124], where the codified clause says changes identified as such in writing [@CL-CLS-1094]. The RFO notice period is a number of calendar days left blank to be negotiated, running from the date the Contractor identifies any Government conduct that it regards as a change to the contract terms and conditions [@CL-CLS-0125]. The RFO text gives actions, inactions, and written or oral communications as examples of conduct that may be regarded as a change, in a separate sentence [@CL-CLS-0126], where the codified clause puts them in a parenthetical inside the notice-period sentence [@CL-CLS-1138]. On the basis of the most accurate information available to the Contractor, the RFO notice shall state the date, nature, and circumstances of the conduct regarded as a change [@CL-CLS-0127; @CL-CLS-1095] and the name, function, and activity of each Government individual and Contractor official or employee involved in or knowledgeable about the conduct [@CL-CLS-0128; @CL-CLS-1096]. In RFO paragraph (c), following the notice the Contractor shall diligently continue performance to the maximum extent possible in accordance with the contract terms and conditions as construed by the Contractor [@CL-CLS-0129; @CL-CLS-1139], unless the notice reports a direction of the Contracting Officer or a communication from a SAR of the Contracting Officer, in either of which events the Contractor shall continue performance [@CL-CLS-0130]. The RFO text keeps the Contracting Officer's duty to respond in writing promptly, within a number of calendar days left blank to be negotiated, after receipt of notice [@CL-CLS-0131; @CL-CLS-1097]. It keeps the listed responses of confirming that the conduct constitutes a change and, when necessary, directing the mode of further performance, and of countermanding any communication regarded as a change [@CL-CLS-0132; @CL-CLS-1098], and of denying that the conduct constitutes a change and, when necessary, directing the mode of further performance [@CL-CLS-0133; @CL-CLS-1140]. It also keeps a separate listed response for the event that the Contractor's notice information is inadequate to make a decision [@CL-CLS-0134; @CL-CLS-0135; @CL-CLS-1140]. Where the Contracting Officer confirms that Government conduct effected a change as alleged by the Contractor [@CL-CLS-0136; @CL-CLS-1099], and the conduct causes an increase or decrease in the Contractor's cost of, or the time required for, performance of any part of the work [@CL-CLS-0137; @CL-CLS-1099], the RFO text keeps an equitable adjustment in the contract price or delivery schedule or both, and in such other provisions of the contract as may be affected [@CL-CLS-0138; @CL-CLS-1100]. It keeps the exclusion of increased costs or time extensions for delay resulting from the Contractor's failure to provide notice or to continue performance as provided [@CL-CLS-0139; @CL-CLS-1101].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A site engineer gives a verbal direction that the Contractor regards as a change. The Contractor notifies the Administrative Contracting Officer in writing within the negotiated number of days, with the date, the people involved and an estimated price effect of 80,000. The Contracting Officer confirms the change in writing. An equitable adjustment is then made to price and schedule; because the notice was timely, the exclusion for delay resulting from a failure to give notice does not apply.

## Never confuse

A notice is not an equitable adjustment. The printed adjustment provision applies where the Contracting Officer confirms the change as alleged [@CL-CLS-1099], and delay caused by a failure to give the required notice is excluded [@CL-CLS-1101]. Accounting for the resulting change is on [Change order accounting](cls-change-order-accounting.md). The construction changes clause has its own notice for other written or oral orders, and it is on [Changes clause for construction contracts](cls-changes-construction.md).
