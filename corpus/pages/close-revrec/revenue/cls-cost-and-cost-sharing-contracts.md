## Purpose

Explains cost contracts and cost-sharing contracts: both pay the contractor no fee, and they differ in how much of allowable cost is reimbursed.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CLS-0572] |
| Codified citation | FAR 16.302 and FAR 16.303 [@CL-CLS-0572; @CL-CLS-0574] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CLS-0572] |
| Applies to | Cost-reimbursement contracts in which the contractor receives no fee [@CL-CLS-0572; @CL-CLS-0574] |

A cost contract is a cost-reimbursement contract in which the contractor receives no fee [@CL-CLS-0572].

A cost-sharing contract is a cost-reimbursement contract in which the contractor receives no fee and is reimbursed only for an agreed-upon portion of its allowable costs [@CL-CLS-0574].

## How it works

A cost contract may be appropriate for research and development work, particularly with nonprofit educational institutions or other nonprofit organizations [@CL-CLS-0573]. A cost-sharing contract may be used when the contractor agrees to absorb a portion of the costs, in the expectation of substantial compensating benefits [@CL-CLS-0575].

For the limitations on each type, the regulation points to the limitations for cost-reimbursement contracts [@CL-CLS-0684; @CL-CLS-0685]. Those limitations say a cost-reimbursement contract may be used only when all four listed conditions are met: the factors in FAR 16.104 have been considered, a written acquisition plan has been approved and signed at least one level above the contracting officer, the contractor's accounting system is adequate for determining costs applicable to the contract or order, and, prior to award, adequate Government resources are available to award and manage a contract other than firm-fixed-priced [@CL-CLS-0569; @CL-CLS-0570]. The use of cost-reimbursement contracts is also prohibited for the acquisition of commercial products and commercial services [@CL-CLS-0571].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A company enters a cost-sharing contract for propulsion research. The agreed shares are 80 percent for the Government and 20 percent for the contractor, with no fee. The contractor incurs 500,000 of allowable costs. The answer: the contractor is reimbursed 400,000, absorbs the remaining 100,000 in the expectation of compensating benefits, and receives no fee.

## Never confuse

A cost contract is not a cost-sharing contract. Under both, the contractor receives no fee, but a cost-sharing contract reimburses only an agreed-upon portion of allowable costs [@CL-CLS-0572; @CL-CLS-0574]. For cost-reimbursement contracts generally, see [Cost-reimbursement contracts](cls-cost-reimbursement-contracts.md).
