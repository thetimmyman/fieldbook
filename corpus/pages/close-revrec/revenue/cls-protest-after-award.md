## Purpose

Shows the stop-work order the Contracting Officer may issue after a protest, what the Contractor must do, and the equitable adjustment when the order is cancelled.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CLS-1111] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.233-3 [@CL-CLS-1111] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.233-3 [@CL-CLS-0147] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CLS-1111] |
| Applies to | Contracts containing the Protest After Award clause [@CL-CLS-1111] |

Upon receipt of a notice of protest or a determination that a protest is likely, the Contracting Officer may, by written order to the Contractor, direct the Contractor to stop performance of the work called for by the contract [@CL-CLS-1111]. Upon receipt of the stop-work order, the Contractor shall immediately comply with its terms and take all reasonable steps to minimize the incurrence of costs allocable to the work covered by the order during the period of work stoppage [@CL-CLS-1112].

Upon receipt of the final decision in the protest, the Contracting Officer shall either cancel the stop-work order or terminate the work covered by the order as provided in the Default, or the Termination for Convenience of the Government, clause of the contract [@CL-CLS-1113].

If a stop-work order issued under the protest clause is canceled either before or after a final decision in the protest, the Contractor shall resume work [@CL-CLS-1114]. On cancellation, the Contracting Officer shall make an equitable adjustment in the delivery schedule or contract price, or both, and modify the contract in writing, if both of the listed conditions are met [@CL-CLS-1115]. The first listed condition is that the stop-work order results in an increase in the time required for, or in the Contractor's cost properly allocable to, the performance of any part of the contract [@CL-CLS-1116]. The second listed condition is that the Contractor asserts its right to an adjustment within 30 days after the end of the period of work stoppage, although the Contracting Officer may receive and act upon a proposal submitted at any time before final payment if the Contracting Officer decides the facts justify the action [@CL-CLS-1117].

If a stop-work order is not canceled and the work covered by the order is terminated for the convenience of the Government, the Contracting Officer shall allow reasonable costs resulting from the stop-work order in arriving at the termination settlement [@CL-CLS-1144]. If the order is not canceled and the work is terminated for default, the Contracting Officer shall allow, by equitable adjustment or otherwise, reasonable costs resulting from the stop-work order [@CL-CLS-1145].

Alternate I of the protest clause replaces, in paragraph (b), the words about an equitable adjustment in the delivery schedule or contract price, or both, with words about an equitable adjustment in the delivery schedule, the estimated cost, the fee, or a combination thereof, and in any other terms of the contract that may be affected [@CL-CLS-1146].

## How it works

After a notice of protest or a determination that a protest is likely, the Contracting Officer may, by written order to the Contractor, direct the Contractor to stop performance of the work called for by the contract [@CL-CLS-1111]. The Contractor shall immediately comply with the order's terms and take all reasonable steps to minimize the incurrence of costs allocable to the work covered by the order during the period of work stoppage [@CL-CLS-1112]. Upon receipt of the final decision in the protest, the Contracting Officer shall either cancel the stop-work order or terminate the work covered by the order [@CL-CLS-1113]. If the order is canceled, the Contractor shall resume work [@CL-CLS-1114], and the Contracting Officer shall make an equitable adjustment if both of the listed conditions are met [@CL-CLS-1115; @CL-CLS-1116; @CL-CLS-1117]. If the order is not canceled and the work covered by the order is terminated for the convenience of the Government, the Contracting Officer shall allow reasonable costs resulting from the stop-work order in arriving at the termination settlement [@CL-CLS-1144]; if it is terminated for default, the Contracting Officer shall allow, by equitable adjustment or otherwise, reasonable costs resulting from the stop-work order [@CL-CLS-1145].

Class Deviation 2026-O0038 provides that contracting officers shall use the revised FAR Part 52 published on the Revolutionary FAR Overhaul web page in lieu of the text codified at 48 CFR chapter 1 [@CL-CMP-9760], effective February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9762]. In RFO FAR 52.233-3, paragraph (a) opens with the Contractor's duty, upon receipt of a stop-work order, to comply immediately with its terms and take all reasonable steps to minimize incurring costs allocable to the work covered by the order during the period of work stoppage [@CL-CLS-0147], where the codified paragraph (a) first says that the Contracting Officer may, by written order after a notice of protest or a determination that a protest is likely, direct the Contractor to stop performance [@CL-CLS-1111] and then states the Contractor's duty [@CL-CLS-1112]. RFO paragraph (a) provides that after receiving the final decision in the protest the Contracting Officer must either cancel the stop-work order or terminate the work covered by the order as provided in the Default, or the Termination for Convenience of the Government, clause of the contract [@CL-CLS-0148]. RFO paragraph (b) provides that if a stop-work order issued under the clause is canceled either before or after a final decision in the protest, the Contractor must resume work [@CL-CLS-0149]. The Contracting Officer must make an equitable adjustment in the delivery schedule or contract price, or both, and the contract must be modified, in writing, accordingly, if (1) the stop-work order results in an increase in the time required for, or in the Contractor's cost properly allocable to, the performance of any part of the contract [@CL-CLS-0150; @CL-CLS-0151], and (2) the Contractor asserts its right to an adjustment within 30 days after the end of the period of work stoppage [@CL-CLS-0152]; provided, that if the Contracting Officer decides the facts justify the action, the Contracting Officer may receive and act upon a proposal submitted at any time before final payment under the contract [@CL-CLS-0153]. RFO paragraph (c) provides that, if a stop-work order is not canceled and the work covered by the order is terminated for the convenience of the Government, the Contracting Officer must allow reasonable costs resulting from the stop-work order in arriving at the termination settlement [@CL-CLS-0154], and paragraph (d) provides that, if the work is terminated for default, the Contracting Officer must allow, by equitable adjustment or otherwise, reasonable costs resulting from the stop-work order [@CL-CLS-0155]. RFO Alternate I provides that in paragraph (b) the words “an equitable adjustment in the delivery schedule, the estimated cost, the fee, or a combination thereof, and in any other terms of the contract that may be affected” are substituted for the words “an equitable adjustment in the delivery schedule or contract price, or both” [@CL-CLS-0156].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A protest is filed after award, and the Contracting Officer orders work stopped. The Contractor stops at once and limits costs. The protest is later decided, and the order is cancelled. The stop added six weeks to the schedule and 90,000 of cost properly allocable to the work. The Contractor asserts its right within the assertion period, and the Contracting Officer adjusts the schedule and price and modifies the contract.
