## Purpose

Shows the stop-work order the Contracting Officer may issue after a protest, how long it runs, what the Contractor must do, and the equitable adjustment when the order is cancelled.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CLS-1111] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.233-3 [@CL-CLS-1111] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.233-3 [@CL-CLS-0147] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CLS-1111] |
| Applies to | Contracts containing the Protest After Award clause [@CL-CLS-1111] |

Upon receipt of a notice of protest or a determination that a protest is likely, the Contracting Officer may, by written order to the Contractor, direct the Contractor to stop performance of the work called for by the contract [@CL-CLS-1111]. Upon receipt of the stop-work order, the Contractor shall immediately comply with its terms and take all reasonable steps to minimize the incurrence of costs allocable to the work covered by the order during the period of work stoppage [@CL-CLS-1112].

The printed clause does not set a fixed number of days for the stop-work order. Upon receipt of the final decision in the protest, the Contracting Officer shall either cancel the stop-work order or terminate the work covered by the order as provided in the Default, or the Termination for Convenience of the Government, clause of the contract [@CL-CLS-1113].

If a stop-work order issued under the protest clause is canceled either before or after a final decision in the protest, the Contractor shall resume work [@CL-CLS-1114]. On cancellation, the Contracting Officer shall make an equitable adjustment in the delivery schedule or contract price, or both, and modify the contract in writing, if both of the listed conditions are met [@CL-CLS-1115]. The first listed condition is that the stop-work order results in an increase in the time required for, or in the Contractor's cost properly allocable to, the performance of any part of the contract [@CL-CLS-1116]. The second listed condition is that the Contractor asserts its right to an adjustment within 30 days after the end of the period of work stoppage, although the Contracting Officer may act upon a proposal submitted at any time before final payment if the Contracting Officer decides the facts justify the action [@CL-CLS-1117].

If a stop-work order is not canceled and the work covered by the order is terminated for the convenience of the Government, the Contracting Officer shall allow reasonable costs resulting from the stop-work order in arriving at the termination settlement [@CL-CLS-1144]. If the order is not canceled and the work is terminated for default, the Contracting Officer shall allow, by equitable adjustment or otherwise, reasonable costs resulting from the stop-work order [@CL-CLS-1145]. The printed clause does not state a separate rule for an order that simply expires, so the source does not establish one.

Alternate I of the protest clause replaces, in paragraph (b), the words about an equitable adjustment in the delivery schedule or contract price, or both, with words about an equitable adjustment in the delivery schedule, the estimated cost, the fee, or a combination thereof, and in any other terms of the contract that may be affected [@CL-CLS-1146].

## How it works

The Contracting Officer issues the order in writing after a notice of protest or a determination that a protest is likely [@CL-CLS-1111]. The Contractor complies at once with the order's terms and limits costs [@CL-CLS-1112]. On receipt of the final decision in the protest, the Contracting Officer either cancels the order or terminates the work [@CL-CLS-1113]. If the order is canceled, the Contractor resumes work and an equitable adjustment follows where both of the listed conditions are met [@CL-CLS-1114; @CL-CLS-1115; @CL-CLS-1116; @CL-CLS-1117]. If the order is not canceled and the work is terminated, reasonable costs resulting from the order are allowed in the termination settlement or, for default, by equitable adjustment or otherwise [@CL-CLS-1144; @CL-CLS-1145].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers must use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified FAR part 52 text, as Class Deviation 2026-O0038 directs, effective February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. In RFO FAR 52.233-3, paragraph (a) opens with the Contractor's duty, upon receipt of a stop-work order, to comply immediately with its terms and take all reasonable steps to minimize incurring costs allocable to the work covered by the order during the period of work stoppage [@CL-CLS-0147], where the codified paragraph (a) first says that the Contracting Officer may, by written order after a notice of protest or a determination that a protest is likely, direct the Contractor to stop performance [@CL-CLS-1111] and then states the Contractor's duty [@CL-CLS-1112]. RFO paragraph (a) keeps the rule that after receiving the final decision in the protest the Contracting Officer must either cancel the stop-work order or terminate the work covered by the order as provided in the Default, or the Termination for Convenience of the Government, clause of the contract [@CL-CLS-0148; @CL-CLS-1113]. RFO paragraph (b) keeps the rule that if a stop-work order is canceled either before or after a final decision in the protest, the Contractor must resume work [@CL-CLS-0149; @CL-CLS-1114]. It keeps the equitable adjustment in the delivery schedule or contract price, or both, with the contract modified in writing, if the conditions that follow are met [@CL-CLS-0150; @CL-CLS-1115]: the stop-work order results in an increase in the time required for, or in the Contractor's cost properly allocable to, the performance of any part of the contract [@CL-CLS-0151; @CL-CLS-1116], and the Contractor asserts its right to an adjustment within 30 days after the end of the period of work stoppage [@CL-CLS-0152; @CL-CLS-1117], although if the Contracting Officer decides the facts justify the action, the Contracting Officer may receive and act upon a proposal submitted at any time before final payment under the contract [@CL-CLS-0153; @CL-CLS-1117]. RFO paragraph (c) keeps the rule that, if the order is not canceled and the work is terminated for the convenience of the Government, the Contracting Officer must allow reasonable costs resulting from the stop-work order in arriving at the termination settlement [@CL-CLS-0154; @CL-CLS-1144], and paragraph (d) keeps the rule that, if the work is terminated for default, the Contracting Officer must allow, by equitable adjustment or otherwise, reasonable costs resulting from the stop-work order [@CL-CLS-0155; @CL-CLS-1145]. RFO Alternate I keeps the paragraph (b) substitution of the words about an equitable adjustment in the delivery schedule, the estimated cost, the fee, or a combination thereof, and in any other terms of the contract that may be affected [@CL-CLS-0156; @CL-CLS-1146].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A protest is filed after award, and the Contracting Officer orders work stopped. The Contractor stops at once and limits costs. The protest is later decided, and the order is cancelled. The stop added six weeks to the schedule and 90,000 of cost properly allocable to the work. The Contractor asserts its right within the assertion period, and the Contracting Officer adjusts the schedule and price and modifies the contract.

## Never confuse

This clause is not the general stop-work order clause. The general clause, its approvals and its cost rules are on [Stop-work orders](../../compliance/regulatory-framework/cmp-stop-work-orders.md). A stop-work order is also not a termination: on final decision, the Contracting Officer either cancels the order or terminates the work [@CL-CLS-1113]. The termination clauses are on [Termination for convenience clauses and when each goes in](cls-termination-for-convenience-clauses.md) and [Termination for default: effect, notices and repurchase against the contractor](cls-termination-for-default-effect-and-repurchase.md).

In the codified clause, paragraph (a) lets the Contracting Officer direct the Contractor to stop performance by written order after a notice of protest or a determination that a protest is likely [@CL-CLS-1111], while the RFO paragraph (a) opens instead with the Contractor's duty upon receipt of a stop-work order [@CL-CLS-0147].
