## Purpose

Explains when the termination contracting officer sends a settlement proposal or a subcontract settlement to audit, what the audit agency does, and why the audit report does not decide the settlement.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CLS-0914] |
| Codified citation | FAR 49.107 [@CL-CLS-0914; @CL-CLS-0917] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CLS-0914] |
| Applies to | Prime contractor settlement proposals and subcontract settlements received for approval or ratification [@CL-CLS-0914; @CL-CLS-0917] |

The TCO shall refer each prime contractor settlement proposal valued at or above the threshold for obtaining certified cost or pricing data, set forth in the FAR section on that threshold, to the appropriate audit agency for review and recommendations [@CL-CLS-0914]. The section refers to that threshold only by cross-reference and does not give its amount, so the amount is not established here [@CL-CLS-0914].

For a proposal valued under that threshold, the TCO may submit it to the audit agency [@CL-CLS-0950]. When a formal examination of such a proposal is not warranted, the TCO performs or has performed a desk review and includes a written summary of it in the termination case file [@CL-CLS-0915]. The audit agency develops requested information, may make further accounting reviews it considers appropriate, and submits written comments and recommendations to the TCO [@CL-CLS-0916].

## How it works

Two alternatives send a subcontract settlement received for approval or ratification to audit. Under the first, the amount exceeds the threshold for obtaining certified cost or pricing data [@CL-CLS-0917]. Under the second, the TCO determines that a complete or partial accounting review is advisable [@CL-CLS-0918]. The audit report is advisory only, and it is for the TCO to use in negotiating a settlement or issuing a unilateral determination [@CL-CLS-0919]. The review by the audit agency does not relieve the prime contractor or higher tier subcontractor of the responsibility for performing an accounting review [@CL-CLS-0951].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

Assume, for this example only, a threshold of 1,000,000. A prime contractor files a settlement proposal of 1,400,000, and the TCO refers it to the audit agency, which returns written comments and recommendations. A second proposal of 300,000 gets a desk review, with a written summary placed in the case file.

A subcontract settlement of 250,000 arrives for approval. The TCO decides a partial accounting review is advisable and refers it. Answer: the first proposal and the third item are referred, the second gets a desk review, and the audit report is advice that the TCO uses in negotiating.

## Never confuse

The audit report is advisory and is for the TCO to use, so it is not the settlement itself [@CL-CLS-0919]. The audit review also does not replace the accounting review that the prime contractor or higher tier subcontractor must perform [@CL-CLS-0951]. The threshold used here is the one for obtaining certified cost or pricing data, and this page does not state its amount because the section refers to it only by cross-reference [@CL-CLS-0914].
