## Purpose

Explains when the termination contracting officer settles by determination, the notice the contractor receives first, and the contractor's appeal rights.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CLS-0925] |
| Codified citation | FAR 49.109-7 [@CL-CLS-0925; @CL-CLS-0926] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CLS-0925] |
| Applies to | Terminations where the contractor and the TCO cannot agree, or where no settlement proposal is submitted in the required period [@CL-CLS-0925] |

If the contractor and TCO cannot agree on a termination settlement, or if a settlement proposal is not submitted within the period required by the termination clause, the TCO shall issue a determination of the amount due consistent with the termination clause [@CL-CLS-0925]. Before issuing it, the TCO shall give the contractor at least 15 days notice by certified mail (return receipt requested) to submit written evidence, so as to reach the TCO on or before a stated date, substantiating the amount previously proposed [@CL-CLS-0926].

The transmittal letter shall advise the contractor that the determination is a final decision from which the contractor may appeal under the Disputes clause, except as shown in the appeals paragraph of the section [@CL-CLS-0927]. The contractor may appeal any settlement by determination, except when it failed to submit the settlement proposal within the time provided in the contract and failed to request an extension of time [@CL-CLS-0928]. The only fixed number of days in the section is the minimum notice before the determination [@CL-CLS-0926].

## How it works

The TCO issues the determination of the amount due consistent with the termination clause [@CL-CLS-0925]. Before it does, the contractor gets at least 15 days notice, by certified mail, to submit written evidence [@CL-CLS-0926]. The determination is a final decision that the contractor may appeal under the Disputes clause [@CL-CLS-0927]. The appeal is not available in one listed case: the contractor failed to submit the settlement proposal in the contract's time and failed to request an extension [@CL-CLS-0928]. The section does not state a deadline to appeal or the period for submitting a proposal, so neither is established here [@CL-CLS-0928].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor and the TCO cannot agree on a 600,000 settlement proposal. The TCO sends notice by certified mail giving the contractor 20 days to submit written evidence, then issues a determination of 540,000.

Answer: the 20 days is longer than the 15-day minimum, so the notice step is met. The determination is a final decision, and the contractor may appeal it under the Disputes clause because it did submit its proposal in time.

## Never confuse

A settlement by determination is not a negotiated settlement, because it follows a failure to agree or a failure to submit a proposal [@CL-CLS-0925]. The notice of at least 15 days is the TCO's notice to the contractor to submit evidence, and it is not the period to appeal [@CL-CLS-0926].
