## Purpose

Sets out what the termination clause of a cost-reimbursement contract settles, what the contractor files with the terminating contracting officer (TCO) and by when, and when the TCO sends the proposal to audit. The sections sit in the FAR subpart on cost-reimbursement contracts terminated for convenience [@CL-CLS-1332]; the three submission and audit sections are in FAR 49.303, headed procedure after discontinuing vouchers [@CL-CLS-1333].

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CLS-1304] |
| Codified citation | FAR 49.301, 49.303-1, 49.303-2 and 49.303-3 [@CL-CLS-1304; @CL-CLS-1306; @CL-CLS-1330; @CL-CLS-1311] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CLS-1304] |
| Applies to | Terminated cost-reimbursement contracts: the settlement of costs and fee, the final settlement proposal, inventory disposal schedules and audit [@CL-CLS-1304; @CL-CLS-1306; @CL-CLS-1330; @CL-CLS-1311] |

Termination clauses for cost-reimbursement contracts provide for the settlement of costs and fee, if any, and the section refers to 49.503(a) for those clauses [@CL-CLS-1304]. The contract clauses governing costs shall determine what costs are allowable [@CL-CLS-1305].

The contractor shall submit a final settlement proposal covering unvouchered costs and any proposed fee to the TCO within 1 year from the effective date of termination, unless the TCO extends the period [@CL-CLS-1306]. The contractor shall use the form prescribed in 49.602-1, unless the TCO authorizes otherwise [@CL-CLS-1307]. The proposal shall not include costs that have been finally disallowed by the contracting officer, or costs previously vouchered and formally questioned by the Government but not yet decided as to allowability [@CL-CLS-1308].

Subject to the terms of the termination clause, and whenever termination inventory is involved, the contractor shall submit complete inventory disposal schedules to the TCO reflecting inventory that is allocable to the terminated portion of the contract [@CL-CLS-1330]. The schedules shall be submitted within 120 days from the effective date of termination unless the TCO extends that period based on a written justification to support the extension [@CL-CLS-1309]. The schedules shall be prepared on Standard Form 1428, Inventory Disposal Schedule [@CL-CLS-1310].

The TCO shall submit the settlement proposal to the appropriate audit agency for review, but if the settlement proposal is limited to an adjustment of fee, no referral to the audit agency is required [@CL-CLS-1311]. The section refers to 49.107 for the audit [@CL-CLS-1311]; see [Termination settlement audit referral](cls-termination-settlement-audit-referral.md).

## How it works

| Duty or decision | Falls on | Period or condition |
|---|---|---|
| File the final settlement proposal for unvouchered costs and any proposed fee | The contractor | Within 1 year from the effective date of termination, unless the TCO extends the period [@CL-CLS-1306] |
| Use the prescribed form | The contractor | Unless the TCO authorizes otherwise [@CL-CLS-1307] |
| Keep out of the proposal costs finally disallowed, and costs previously vouchered, formally questioned and not yet decided | The contractor | Both kinds of cost are excluded [@CL-CLS-1308] |
| File complete inventory disposal schedules for inventory allocable to the terminated portion | The contractor | Whenever termination inventory is involved and subject to the termination clause [@CL-CLS-1330] |
| Meet the schedule period | The contractor | Within 120 days from the effective date of termination unless the TCO extends it on a written justification [@CL-CLS-1309] |
| Extend either period or authorize another form | The TCO | Only the schedule extension is tied to a written justification in the text [@CL-CLS-1306; @CL-CLS-1309; @CL-CLS-1307] |
| Send the proposal to the audit agency | The TCO | Not required if the proposal is limited to an adjustment of fee [@CL-CLS-1311] |

Both periods run from the effective date of termination, and they are separate: the proposal period and the schedule period [@CL-CLS-1306; @CL-CLS-1309]. The contracting officer is the one who finally disallows costs, and the sections print no deadline for the audit referral [@CL-CLS-1308; @CL-CLS-1311]. The exclusion of questioned costs covers only costs that are previously vouchered, formally questioned by the Government and not yet decided as to allowability [@CL-CLS-1308].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A cost-reimbursement contract is terminated and the contractor discontinues vouchers [@CL-CLS-1333]. The contractor has 250,000 of unvouchered cost and proposes 30,000 of fee. Earlier it vouchered 20,000 that the Government formally questioned and has not yet decided as to allowability. The contracting officer also finally disallowed 10,000. Neither of those two amounts goes into the proposal [@CL-CLS-1308].

The contractor files the final settlement proposal 300 days after the effective date of termination, which is inside the 1 year allowed [@CL-CLS-1306]. It holds 80,000 of inventory allocable to the terminated portion, so it files complete inventory disposal schedules on Standard Form 1428 [@CL-CLS-1330; @CL-CLS-1310]. It files them 110 days after the effective date, inside the 120 days [@CL-CLS-1309]. Had it needed more time, the schedules would still be due at 120 days unless the TCO extended the period on a written justification [@CL-CLS-1309].

The proposal covers unvouchered cost and fee, so it is not limited to an adjustment of fee, and the TCO submits it to the appropriate audit agency [@CL-CLS-1311]. A proposal limited to an adjustment of fee would need no referral [@CL-CLS-1311].

## Never confuse

The two exclusions in the proposal are different. A cost the contracting officer has finally disallowed is out, and so is a cost that was previously vouchered, formally questioned and not yet decided; a cost that was vouchered but not questioned is not within that second exclusion [@CL-CLS-1308].

The proposal period and the inventory schedule period are different periods with different lengths, 1 year and 120 days [@CL-CLS-1306; @CL-CLS-1309]. The screening and reutilization of the listed inventory is covered in [Inventory disposal schedules and reutilization](../inventory-wip/cls-inventory-disposal-schedules-and-reutilization.md).

## Company calibration

Whether the proposal and inventory schedule periods are tracked from the termination effective date, and whether any extension is supported in writing. [@Q-027]
