## Purpose

Shows the two routes to a change under the construction changes clause, the written notice the Contractor must give on the second route, and the limits on recovery.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CLS-1088] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.243-4 [@CL-CLS-1088] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.243-4 [@CL-CLS-0047] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CLS-1088] |
| Applies to | Contracts containing the Changes clause for construction [@CL-CLS-1088] |

The first route is a written change order. The Contracting Officer may at any time, without notice to the sureties, if any, by written order designated or indicated to be a change order, make changes in the work within the general scope of the contract, including the changes the clause lists [@CL-CLS-1088]. The changes the clause lists include changes in the specifications (including drawings and designs), in the method or manner of performance of the work, in the Government-furnished property or services, or directing acceleration in the performance of the work [@CL-CLS-1137].

The second route is another written or oral order. Any other written or oral order from the Contracting Officer that causes a change shall be treated as a change order under the clause, followed by a proviso [@CL-CLS-1089]. The proviso is that the Contractor gives the Contracting Officer written notice stating the date, circumstances, and source of the order and that the Contractor regards the order as a change order [@CL-CLS-1090]. Except as the clause provides, no order, statement, or conduct of the Contracting Officer shall be treated as a change under the clause or entitle the Contractor to an equitable adjustment [@CL-CLS-1135].

Where a change causes an increase or decrease in the Contractor's cost of, or the time required for, performance of any part of the work, whether or not changed by any such order, the Contracting Officer shall make an equitable adjustment and modify the contract in writing [@CL-CLS-1091; @CL-CLS-0494]. Except for an adjustment based on defective specifications, no adjustment for any change under the paragraph on other written or oral orders shall be made for any costs incurred more than 20 days before the Contractor gives written notice as required [@CL-CLS-1092]. For defective specifications for which the Government is responsible, the equitable adjustment shall include any increased cost reasonably incurred by the Contractor in attempting to comply with them [@CL-CLS-1136].

The Contractor must assert its right to an adjustment within 30 days after receipt of a written change order under the first route or the furnishing of a written notice under the second route [@CL-CLS-1132]. The Contractor asserts the right by submitting to the Contracting Officer a written statement describing the general nature and amount of proposal, unless the period for doing so is extended by the Government [@CL-CLS-1133]. The prescription note states that the 30-day period may be varied according to agency procedures [@CL-CLS-1134].

No proposal by the Contractor for an equitable adjustment shall be allowed if asserted after final payment under the contract [@CL-CLS-1093].

## How it works

A change reaches the Contractor either through a written change order or through another written or oral order that the Contractor identifies by notice [@CL-CLS-1088; @CL-CLS-1089; @CL-CLS-1090]. The equitable adjustment, made by modifying the contract in writing, follows a change that moves cost or time [@CL-CLS-1091; @CL-CLS-0494]. On the second route, no adjustment is made for costs incurred more than 20 days before the written notice, except for an adjustment based on defective specifications [@CL-CLS-1092]. The Contractor asserts its right within the assertion period [@CL-CLS-1132; @CL-CLS-1133], and a proposal asserted after final payment is not allowed [@CL-CLS-1093].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text from February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. RFO FAR 52.243-4 has the same rule that another written or oral order that causes a change is treated as a change order if the Contractor gives written notice [@CL-CLS-0050; @CL-CLS-0051; @CL-CLS-1089; @CL-CLS-1090], and the same rule that no other order, statement, or conduct is treated as a change or entitles the Contractor to an equitable adjustment [@CL-CLS-0052; @CL-CLS-1135]. It has the same equitable adjustment rule [@CL-CLS-0056; @CL-CLS-0055; @CL-CLS-1091], the same 20-day limit on costs before written notice, with the same exception for defective specifications [@CL-CLS-0053; @CL-CLS-1092], the same rule for defective specifications for which the Government is responsible [@CL-CLS-0054; @CL-CLS-1136], and the same bar on a proposal asserted after final payment [@CL-CLS-0059; @CL-CLS-1093]. Paragraph (a) differs in wording: the RFO text says the Contracting Officer may issue a written order, identified as a change order, to make changes in the work within the scope of the contract [@CL-CLS-0047], where the codified paragraph says a written order designated or indicated to be a change order and the general scope of the contract [@CL-CLS-1088]. The four listed changes are the same [@CL-CLS-0048; @CL-CLS-1137], and the RFO text states in a separate subparagraph that the Contracting Officer does not need to notify any sureties of a written order [@CL-CLS-0049]. The RFO paragraph (e)(1) has the Contractor assert its right by submitting a written statement describing the general nature and amount of proposal within 30 days, unless the Government extends the period, after receipt of a written change order or the furnishing of a written notice [@CL-CLS-0057; @CL-CLS-0058], which is the same assertion period and extension as the codified paragraph (e) [@CL-CLS-1132; @CL-CLS-1133]. Its prescription says that agency procedures may vary the 30-day period, in different words from the codified prescription note [@CL-CLS-0060; @CL-CLS-1134].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A construction contractor receives an oral instruction to move a wall. It gives written notice 10 days after the instruction, stating the date, the source and that it regards the instruction as a change order. Costs of 30,000 were incurred in those first 10 days, and 50,000 afterwards. No cost was incurred more than 20 days before the notice, so the pre-notice limit removes nothing, and both amounts can be priced in the proposal. The Contractor submits a written statement of the general nature and amount of its proposal within the assertion period after the notice, and well before final payment.

## Never confuse

A written change order is not the same as another order treated as a change order. The written-notice proviso [@CL-CLS-1089; @CL-CLS-1090] and the pre-notice cost limit [@CL-CLS-1092] are stated for the second route. The accounting for change orders is on [Change order accounting](cls-change-order-accounting.md), and the fixed-price clause is on [Changes, Fixed-Price clause and equitable adjustment](../../contract-finance/funding/ctf-changes-fixed-price-equitable-adjustment.md). The cost-reimbursement version is on [Changes clause for cost-reimbursement contracts](cls-changes-cost-reimbursement.md), the time-and-materials version is on [Changes clause for time-and-materials and labor-hour contracts](cls-changes-time-and-materials-labor-hours.md), and a separate notice route is on [Notification of changes clause](cls-notification-of-changes.md). The claims process is on [Disputes clause, claims and certification](cls-disputes-clause.md).

The RFO text sets out the written change order and written notice events as (e)(1)(i) and (ii) [@CL-CLS-0058], where the codified clause numbers them (1) and (2) within paragraph (e) [@CL-CLS-1132], and it puts the sureties rule at (a)(2) [@CL-CLS-0049], where the codified clause has it in the lead-in of paragraph (a) [@CL-CLS-1088].
