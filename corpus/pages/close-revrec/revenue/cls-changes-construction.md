## Purpose

Shows the two routes to a change under the construction changes clause, the written notice the Contractor must give on the second route, and the limits on recovery.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CLS-1088] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.243-4 [@CL-CLS-1088] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CLS-1088] |
| Applies to | Contracts containing the Changes clause for construction [@CL-CLS-1088] |

The first route is a written change order. The Contracting Officer may at any time, without notice to the sureties, if any, by written order designated or indicated to be a change order, make changes in the work within the general scope of the contract, including the changes the clause lists [@CL-CLS-1088]. The listed changes are changes in the specifications, in the method or manner of performance of the work, in the Government-furnished property or services, and directing acceleration in the performance of the work [@CL-CLS-1137].

The second route is another written or oral order. Any other written or oral order from the Contracting Officer that causes a change shall be treated as a change order under the clause, followed by a proviso [@CL-CLS-1089]. The proviso is that the Contractor gives the Contracting Officer written notice stating the date, circumstances, and source of the order and that the Contractor regards the order as a change order [@CL-CLS-1090]. Except as the clause provides, no order, statement, or conduct of the Contracting Officer shall be treated as a change under the clause or entitle the Contractor to an equitable adjustment [@CL-CLS-1135].

Where a change causes an increase or decrease in the Contractor's cost of, or the time required for, performance of any part of the work, whether or not changed by any such order, the Contracting Officer shall make an equitable adjustment [@CL-CLS-1091]. Except for an adjustment based on defective specifications, no adjustment for any change under the paragraph on other written or oral orders shall be made for any costs incurred more than 20 days before the Contractor gives written notice as required [@CL-CLS-1092]. For defective specifications for which the Government is responsible, the equitable adjustment shall include any increased cost reasonably incurred by the Contractor in attempting to comply with them [@CL-CLS-1136].

The Contractor must assert its right to an adjustment within 30 days after receipt of a written change order under the first route or the furnishing of a written notice under the second route [@CL-CLS-1132]. The Contractor asserts the right by submitting to the Contracting Officer a written statement describing the general nature and amount of proposal, unless the period for doing so is extended by the Government [@CL-CLS-1133]. The prescription note states that the 30-day period may be varied according to agency procedures [@CL-CLS-1134].

No proposal by the Contractor for an equitable adjustment shall be allowed if asserted after final payment under the contract [@CL-CLS-1093].

## How it works

A change reaches the Contractor either through a written change order or through another written or oral order that the Contractor identifies by notice [@CL-CLS-1088; @CL-CLS-1089; @CL-CLS-1090]. The equitable adjustment follows a change that moves cost or time [@CL-CLS-1091]. On the second route, no adjustment is made for costs incurred more than 20 days before the written notice, except for an adjustment based on defective specifications [@CL-CLS-1092]. The Contractor asserts its right within the assertion period [@CL-CLS-1132; @CL-CLS-1133], and a proposal asserted after final payment is not allowed [@CL-CLS-1093].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A construction contractor receives an oral instruction to move a wall. It gives written notice 10 days after the instruction, stating the date, the source and that it regards the instruction as a change order. Costs of 30,000 were incurred in those first 10 days, and 50,000 afterwards. No cost was incurred more than 20 days before the notice, so the pre-notice limit removes nothing, and both amounts can be priced in the proposal. The Contractor submits a written statement of the general nature and amount of its proposal within the assertion period after the notice, and well before final payment.

## Never confuse

A written change order is not the same as another order treated as a change order. The written-notice proviso [@CL-CLS-1089; @CL-CLS-1090] and the pre-notice cost limit [@CL-CLS-1092] are stated for the second route. The accounting for change orders is on [Change order accounting](cls-change-order-accounting.md), and the fixed-price clause is on [Changes, Fixed-Price clause and equitable adjustment](../../contract-finance/funding/ctf-changes-fixed-price-equitable-adjustment.md). The cost-reimbursement version is on [Changes clause for cost-reimbursement contracts](cls-changes-cost-reimbursement.md), the time-and-materials version is on [Changes clause for time-and-materials and labor-hour contracts](cls-changes-time-and-materials-labor-hours.md), and a separate notice route is on [Notification of changes clause](cls-notification-of-changes.md). The claims process is on [Disputes clause, claims and certification](cls-disputes-clause.md).
