## Purpose

Explains when the contracting officer may require separate cost accounts for incurred direct costs of work affected by contract changes, and what those accounts hold.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CLS-0207] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.243-6 [@CL-CLS-0207] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CLS-0207] |
| Applies to | Contracts with the Change Order Accounting clause when estimated change costs exceed $100,000 [@CL-CLS-0207] |

The contracting officer may require change order accounting whenever the estimated cost of a change or series of related changes exceeds $100,000 [@CL-CLS-0207].

For each change or series of related changes, the contractor must maintain separate accounts, by job order or other suitable accounting procedure, of all incurred segregable, direct costs, less allocable credits, of work, both changed and not changed, allocable to the change [@CL-CLS-0208].

## How it works

Once the contracting officer requires change order accounting for a change or series of related changes above the dollar threshold, the contractor keeps separate accounts for that change [@CL-CLS-0207; @CL-CLS-0208]. The accounts cover the incurred segregable direct costs of both changed and not changed work that are allocable to the change [@CL-CLS-0208]. The contractor must maintain the accounts until the parties agree to an equitable adjustment for the changes ordered or the matter is conclusively disposed of in accordance with the Disputes clause [@CL-CLS-0209].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contracting officer issues an unpriced change order estimated to cost 250,000, which exceeds 100,000, and requires change order accounting. The contractor opens a job order that collects the direct labor and direct material incurred on the changed engineering tasks and on the unchanged tasks that are allocable to the change, net of allocable credits. The answer: it keeps that job order open until the parties agree to an equitable adjustment or the matter is conclusively disposed of under the Disputes clause.

## Never confuse

Change order accounting is not the equitable adjustment itself. The clause covers the separate accounts, which the contracting officer may require when the estimated cost of a change or series of related changes exceeds the stated amount [@CL-CLS-0207; @CL-CLS-0208]. How the price is adjusted for a change is covered in [Changes in fixed-price contracts](../../contract-finance/funding/ctf-changes-fixed-price-equitable-adjustment.md).
