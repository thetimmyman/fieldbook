## Purpose

Explains when the contracting officer may require separate cost accounts for incurred direct costs of work affected by contract changes, and what those accounts hold.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CLS-0207] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.243-6 [@CL-CLS-0207] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.243-6 [@CL-CLS-0021] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CLS-0207] |
| Applies to | Contracts with the Change Order Accounting clause, under which the contracting officer may require change order accounting whenever the estimated cost of a change or series of related changes exceeds $100,000 [@CL-CLS-0207] |

The contracting officer may require change order accounting whenever the estimated cost of a change or series of related changes exceeds $100,000 [@CL-CLS-0207].

In the FAR's general definitions, a change order is a written order, signed by the contracting officer, directing the contractor to make a change that the Changes clause authorizes the contracting officer to order without the contractor's consent [@CL-CLS-1497]. The RFO model text of FAR 2.101 defines a change order in the same words as the codified definition, apart from the printed form of the apostrophe [@CL-CLS-1528; @CL-CLS-1497].

For each change or series of related changes, the contractor must maintain separate accounts, by job order or other suitable accounting procedure, of all incurred segregable, direct costs, less allocable credits, of work, both changed and not changed, allocable to the change [@CL-CLS-0208].

## How it works

The clause lets the contracting officer require change order accounting above the dollar threshold [@CL-CLS-0207], and it says the contractor shall maintain separate accounts for each change or series of related changes [@CL-CLS-0208]. The accounts cover the incurred segregable direct costs, less allocable credits, of both changed and not changed work that are allocable to the change [@CL-CLS-0208]. The contractor must maintain the accounts until the parties agree to an equitable adjustment for the changes ordered by the contracting officer or the matter is conclusively disposed of in accordance with the Disputes clause [@CL-CLS-0209].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text from February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. RFO FAR 52.243-6 has the same $100,000 rule for requiring change order accounting [@CL-CLS-0021; @CL-CLS-0207], the same separate-accounts rule [@CL-CLS-0022; @CL-CLS-0208], and the same period for maintaining the accounts [@CL-CLS-0023; @CL-CLS-0209].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contracting officer issues an unpriced change order estimated to cost 250,000, which exceeds 100,000, and requires change order accounting. The contractor opens a job order that collects the direct labor and direct material incurred on the changed engineering tasks and on the unchanged tasks that are allocable to the change, net of allocable credits. The answer: it keeps that job order open until the parties agree to an equitable adjustment or the matter is conclusively disposed of under the Disputes clause.

## Never confuse

Change order accounting is not the equitable adjustment itself. The clause covers the separate accounts, which the contracting officer may require when the estimated cost of a change or series of related changes exceeds the stated amount [@CL-CLS-0207; @CL-CLS-0208]. How the price is adjusted for a change is covered in [Changes in fixed-price contracts](../../contract-finance/funding/ctf-changes-fixed-price-equitable-adjustment.md).
