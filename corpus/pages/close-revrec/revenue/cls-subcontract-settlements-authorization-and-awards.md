## Purpose

Explains how the Government treats a prime contractor's settlements with terminated subcontractors, the amount limit on settling without approval or ratification, and how judgments and arbitration awards enter the prime settlement.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CLS-0920] |
| Codified citation | FAR 49.104, 49.108-3, 49.108-4 and 49.108-5 [@CL-CLS-0912; @CL-CLS-0920; @CL-CLS-0922; @CL-CLS-0923] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CLS-0920] |
| Applies to | Prime contractor settlements arising from the termination of subcontracts [@CL-CLS-0920; @CL-CLS-0922] |

In no event will the Government pay the prime contractor any amount for loss of anticipatory profits or consequential damages resulting from the termination of any subcontract (but see 49.108-5) [@CL-CLS-0920]. The TCO will also determine if the settlement was arrived at in good faith, is reasonable in amount, and is allocable to the terminated portion of the contract (or, if allocable only in part, that the proposed allocation is reasonable) [@CL-CLS-0921].

The TCO may, upon written request, give written authorization to the prime contractor to conclude settlements of subcontracts terminated in whole or in part without approval or ratification when the amount of settlement (see 49.002(d)) is $100,000 or less, if the listed conditions are met [@CL-CLS-0922]. The contractor may make any number of separate settlements with a single subcontractor but shall not divide settlement proposals solely to bring them under an authorization limit [@CL-CLS-0961]. When it is determined that the contractor's procedures are not adequate, or that improper settlements are being made, or when the authority has not been used in the preceding 2 years, the TCO shall revoke the authorization by written notice to the contractor, effective on the date of receipt [@CL-CLS-0962].

## How it works

When a subcontractor obtains a final judgment against a prime contractor, the TCO shall, for the purposes of settling the prime contract, treat the amount of the judgment as a cost of settling with the contractor, to the extent the judgment is properly allocable to the terminated portion of the prime contract, if the listed conditions are met [@CL-CLS-0969; @CL-CLS-0923]. If the conditions in subparagraphs (a)(1) through (5) of the section are not all met, the TCO may allow the contractor the part of the judgment considered fair for settling the subcontract settlement proposal, giving due regard to the policies in this part for settlement of proposals [@CL-CLS-0960]. When a contractor and a subcontractor submit the subcontractor's settlement proposal to arbitration under any applicable law or contract provision, the TCO shall recognize the arbitration award as the cost of settling the proposal of the contractor to the same extent and under the same conditions as in paragraphs (a) and (b) of the section [@CL-CLS-1603; @CL-CLS-0924]. The contractor's own listed duty is to settle outstanding liabilities and proposals arising out of termination of subcontracts, obtaining any approvals or ratifications required by the TCO [@CL-CLS-0912]. For the other duties, see [Prime contractor duties after a notice of termination](cls-prime-contractor-duties-after-termination-notice.md). For the audit side of subcontract settlements received for approval or ratification, see [Audit referral of termination settlement proposals and subcontract settlements](cls-termination-settlement-audit-referral.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

The TCO has authorized a prime contractor, on written request, to settle subcontracts without approval or ratification up to 100,000. The contractor settles subcontract A at 80,000 and subcontract B at 140,000.

Answer: A is within the stated limit, so the authorization can apply if its listed conditions are met. B is above the limit, so the authorization does not cover it. Neither settlement can include anticipatory profits or consequential damages, subject to the judgment and award rules.

## Never confuse

Authority to settle without approval or ratification is a limited authorization given by the TCO on written request when the amount of settlement (see 49.002(d)) is $100,000 or less, and it is not an exemption from the rule that in no event will the Government pay the prime contractor any amount for loss of anticipatory profits or consequential damages resulting from the termination of any subcontract (but see 49.108-5) [@CL-CLS-0922; @CL-CLS-0920]. A final judgment obtained by a subcontractor against a prime contractor shall be treated as a cost of settling with the contractor to the extent properly allocable to the terminated portion of the prime contract if the listed conditions are met, and if the conditions in subparagraphs (a)(1) through (5) are not all met, the TCO may allow the contractor the part of the judgment considered fair for settling the subcontract settlement proposal [@CL-CLS-0969; @CL-CLS-0923; @CL-CLS-0960]. For the settlement bases and the deductions, see [Termination settlement proposals: inventory basis and total-cost basis](../inventory-wip/cls-termination-settlement-proposal-bases.md) and [Termination settlements: deductions, completed end items, and settlement limitation](cls-termination-settlement-deductions-and-limitations.md).
