## Purpose

Explains how the Government treats a prime contractor's settlements with terminated subcontractors, the amount limit on settling without approval or ratification, and how judgments and arbitration awards enter the prime settlement.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CLS-0920] |
| Codified citation | FAR 49.108-3, 49.108-4 and 49.108-5 [@CL-CLS-0920; @CL-CLS-0922; @CL-CLS-0923] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CLS-0920] |
| Applies to | Prime contractor settlements arising from the termination of subcontracts [@CL-CLS-0920; @CL-CLS-0922] |

In no event will the Government pay the prime contractor any amount for loss of anticipatory profits or consequential damages resulting from the termination of any subcontract, but the text points the reader to the section on judgments and arbitration awards, which is covered below [@CL-CLS-0920]. In the same settlement procedure section, the TCO determines if the settlement was arrived at in good faith, is reasonable in amount, and is allocable to the terminated portion of the contract, or, if allocable only in part, that the proposed allocation is reasonable [@CL-CLS-0921].

Upon written request, the TCO may give written authorization to the prime contractor to conclude settlements of subcontracts terminated in whole or in part without approval or ratification when the amount of settlement is $100,000 or less, if the listed conditions are met [@CL-CLS-0922]. The quoted text points to the applicability section for how the amount of settlement is figured, and the TCO is the person named as giving the authorization [@CL-CLS-0922]. The authorization has two further limits. The contractor may make any number of separate settlements with a single subcontractor but shall not divide settlement proposals solely to bring them under an authorization limit [@CL-CLS-0961]. The TCO shall revoke the authorization by written notice, effective on the date of receipt, when it is determined that the contractor's procedures are not adequate or that improper settlements are being made, or when the authority has not been used in the preceding 2 years [@CL-CLS-0962].

## How it works

The section on judgments and arbitration awards addresses the case in which a subcontractor obtains a final judgment against a prime contractor [@CL-CLS-0969]. For such a judgment, the TCO treats the amount as a cost of settling with the contractor, for the purposes of settling the prime contract, to the extent the judgment is properly allocable to the terminated portion of the prime contract, if the listed conditions are met [@CL-CLS-0923]. The section lists those conditions, and this page does not restate them. If those conditions are not all met, the TCO may allow the contractor the part of the judgment considered fair for settling the subcontract settlement proposal, giving due regard to the policies in this part for settlement of proposals [@CL-CLS-0960]. When a subcontractor's settlement proposal goes to arbitration, the TCO recognizes the award as the cost of settling the contractor's proposal to the same extent and under the same conditions as in the judgment paragraphs of that section [@CL-CLS-0924]. The contractor's own listed duty is to settle outstanding liabilities and proposals arising out of termination of subcontracts, obtaining any approvals or ratifications required by the TCO [@CL-CLS-0912]; for the other duties, see [Prime contractor duties after a notice of termination](cls-prime-contractor-duties-after-termination-notice.md). The section names the TCO as the one who determines the good-faith, reasonableness and allocability points [@CL-CLS-0921]. For the audit side of subcontract settlements received for approval or ratification, see [Audit referral of termination settlement proposals and subcontract settlements](cls-termination-settlement-audit-referral.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

The TCO has authorized a prime contractor, on written request, to settle subcontracts without approval or ratification up to 100,000. The contractor settles subcontract A at 80,000 and subcontract B at 140,000.

Answer: A is within the stated limit, so the authorization can apply if its listed conditions are met. B is above the limit, so the authorization does not cover it. Neither settlement can include anticipatory profits or consequential damages, subject to the judgment and award rules.

## Never confuse

Authority to settle without approval or ratification is a limited authorization given by the TCO on written request, and it is not an exemption from the general rule against paying anticipatory profits or consequential damages, which the section qualifies only by pointing to the judgments and arbitration awards section [@CL-CLS-0922; @CL-CLS-0920]. A judgment treated as a cost of settling is allowed to the extent allocable to the terminated portion of the prime contract if the listed conditions are met, and when they are not all met the TCO may allow only the part considered fair [@CL-CLS-0923; @CL-CLS-0960]. For the settlement bases and the deductions, see [Termination settlement proposals: inventory basis and total-cost basis](../inventory-wip/cls-termination-settlement-proposal-bases.md) and [Termination settlements: deductions, completed end items, and settlement limitation](cls-termination-settlement-deductions-and-limitations.md).
