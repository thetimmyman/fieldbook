## Purpose

Shows the courses of action a contracting officer may take instead of terminating a contract for default, and the condition attached to each one.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CLS-0258] |
| Codified citation | FAR 49.402-4 [@CL-CLS-0258; @CL-CLS-0259; @CL-CLS-0260; @CL-CLS-0261] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CLS-0258] |
| Applies to | Courses of action available to the contracting officer in lieu of termination for default [@CL-CLS-0258] |

The courses of action in the section, among others, are available to the contracting officer in lieu of termination for default when they are in the Government's interest [@CL-CLS-0258].

The contracting officer may permit the contractor, the surety or the guarantor to continue performance of the contract under a revised delivery schedule [@CL-CLS-0259].

The contracting officer may permit the contractor to continue performance by a subcontract or other business arrangement with an acceptable third party, provided the rights of the Government are adequately preserved [@CL-CLS-0260].

If the requirement for the supplies and services in the contract no longer exists and the contractor is not liable to the Government for damages as provided in 49.402-7, the contracting officer may execute a no-cost termination settlement agreement, using the formats in 49.603-6 and 49.603-7 as a guide [@CL-CLS-0261].

## How it works

The following courses of action, among others, are available to the contracting officer in lieu of termination for default when in the Government's interest [@CL-CLS-0258].

| Course | Who decides and who acts | Condition |
|---|---|---|
| Revised delivery schedule | The contracting officer may permit it; the contractor, the surety or the guarantor then continues performance [@CL-CLS-0259] | In the Government's interest [@CL-CLS-0258] |
| Subcontract or other business arrangement | The contracting officer may permit it; the contractor then continues performance through an acceptable third party [@CL-CLS-0260] | The rights of the Government are adequately preserved [@CL-CLS-0260]; in the Government's interest [@CL-CLS-0258] |
| No-cost termination settlement agreement | The contracting officer may execute it [@CL-CLS-0261] | The requirement no longer exists and the contractor is not liable to the Government for damages as provided in 49.402-7 [@CL-CLS-0261]; in the Government's interest [@CL-CLS-0258] |

The first two courses let performance continue, either under a revised delivery schedule or through an acceptable third party [@CL-CLS-0259; @CL-CLS-0260]. The third is a no-cost termination settlement agreement [@CL-CLS-0261].

Both conditions of the third course are joined by "and", so both must hold [@CL-CLS-0261]. The proviso on the rights of the Government belongs to the second course [@CL-CLS-0260].

The formats in 49.603-6 and 49.603-7 are named as a guide for the no-cost agreement [@CL-CLS-0261]. They are described on [No-cost settlement agreements for complete and partial terminations and reservations](cls-no-cost-settlement-agreements.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

Two supply contracts, each worth 2,000,000, are late.

On contract A, the requiring activity confirms that the items are no longer needed, and the contractor is not liable for damages as provided in 49.402-7. Both conditions hold. If it is in the Government's interest, the contracting officer may execute a no-cost termination settlement agreement instead of terminating for default [@CL-CLS-0258; @CL-CLS-0261].

On contract B, the requirement continues. The no-cost course is not available on its terms [@CL-CLS-0261]. If it is in the Government's interest, the contracting officer may instead permit the contractor, the surety or the guarantor to continue under a revised delivery schedule, for example one that moves delivery from Day 60 to Day 120 [@CL-CLS-0258; @CL-CLS-0259].

## Never confuse

These courses are alternatives to termination for default [@CL-CLS-0258]. The default termination itself and its effects are on [Termination for default: effect, notices and repurchase against the contractor](cls-termination-for-default-effect-and-repurchase.md). The default clauses are on [Default clauses for fixed-price contracts and when each goes in](cls-fixed-price-default-clauses.md).

The no-cost course applies if the requirement for the supplies and services in the contract no longer exists, and the contractor is not liable to the Government for damages as provided in 49.402-7 [@CL-CLS-0261].

The condition that the rights of the Government are adequately preserved is stated for the subcontract or other business arrangement with an acceptable third party [@CL-CLS-0260].

## Company calibration

Who confirms whether the requirement still exists and whether the contractor is exposed to damages before a no-cost route is proposed. [@Q-004]
