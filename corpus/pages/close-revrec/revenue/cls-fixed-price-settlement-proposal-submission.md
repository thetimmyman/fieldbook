## Purpose

Shows when and how a contractor submits a settlement proposal to the termination contracting officer after a termination, and which standard forms go with it.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CLS-1000] |
| Codified citation | FAR 49.206-1 [@CL-CLS-1000] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CLS-1000] |
| Applies to | Settlement proposals submitted because of a termination, subject to the provisions of the termination clause [@CL-CLS-1000]. |

Subject to the termination clause (the clauses are on [Termination for convenience clauses and when each goes in](cls-termination-for-convenience-clauses.md)), the contractor should promptly submit to the TCO a settlement proposal for the amount claimed because of the termination [@CL-CLS-1000]. The final settlement proposal must be submitted within one year from the effective date of the termination unless the TCO extends the period [@CL-CLS-1001].

The proposal must cover all cost elements, including settlements with subcontractors and any proposed profit [@CL-CLS-1003]. With the consent of the TCO, proposals may be filed in successive steps covering separate portions of the contractor's costs, and each such interim proposal shall include all costs of a particular type except as the TCO may authorize otherwise [@CL-CLS-1002].

## How it works

Settlement proposals must be on the forms prescribed in FAR 49.602 unless the forms are inadequate for a particular contract [@CL-CLS-1004]. The contractor may use the Settlement Proposal (Short Form), SF 1438, when the total proposal is less than $10,000, unless the TCO instructs otherwise [@CL-CLS-1005]. The Schedule of Accounting Information, SF 1439, must be submitted for each termination under a contract for which a settlement proposal is submitted, except when the Standard Form 1438 is used [@CL-CLS-1006]. Although several interim proposals may be submitted, SF 1439 need be submitted only once unless major changes occur in the information after the original form is filed [@CL-CLS-1571]. The inventory basis and the total-cost basis are covered on the proposal-bases card.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contract is terminated and the contractor's whole claim comes to 8,000. That is below the short-form figure of 10,000, so the contractor may use the short form unless the TCO says otherwise, and it files no accounting schedule. A second contractor claims 80,000 in two interim filings, with the TCO's consent. It uses the prescribed forms and files the accounting schedule once, unless major changes occur in that information after the original filing. The second filing covers a separate portion of its costs.
