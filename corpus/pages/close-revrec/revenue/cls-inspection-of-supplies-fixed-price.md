## Purpose

Explains the effect of Government acceptance under the Inspection of Supplies—Fixed-Price clause and the exceptions that prevent acceptance from being conclusive.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CLS-0204] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.246-2 [@CL-CLS-0204] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.246-2 [@CL-CLS-0123] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CLS-0204] |
| Applies to | Contracts that include the Inspection of Supplies—Fixed-Price clause [@CL-CLS-0204] |

Under the inspection clause, acceptance is conclusive, except for latent defects, fraud, gross mistakes amounting to fraud, or as otherwise provided in the contract [@CL-CLS-0204].

The Government must accept or reject supplies as promptly as practicable after delivery, unless otherwise provided in the contract [@CL-CLS-0206].

## How it works

The Government has the right to inspect and test all supplies called for by the contract, to the extent practicable, at all places and times, including the period of manufacture, and in any event before acceptance [@CL-CLS-0303]. Government inspections and tests do not relieve the contractor of responsibility for defects or other failures to meet contract requirements discovered before acceptance [@CL-CLS-0205]. Government failure to inspect and accept or reject the supplies does not relieve the contractor from responsibility, or impose liability on the Government, for nonconforming supplies [@CL-CLS-0304]. Acceptance is conclusive except as the rule states [@CL-CLS-0204].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers must use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified FAR part 52 text, as Class Deviation 2026-O0038 directs, effective February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. RFO FAR 52.246-2 keeps the Government's right to inspect and test all supplies called for by the contract, to the extent practicable, at all places and times, including the period of manufacture, and in any event before acceptance [@CL-CLS-0097; @CL-CLS-0303]. It keeps the duty to accept or reject supplies as promptly as practicable after delivery, unless otherwise provided in the contract [@CL-CLS-0098; @CL-CLS-0206]. It keeps the rule that Government failure to inspect and accept or reject the supplies does not relieve the Contractor from responsibility, or impose liability on the Government, for nonconforming supplies [@CL-CLS-0099; @CL-CLS-0304]. It keeps the rule that inspections and tests by the Government do not relieve the Contractor of responsibility for defects or other failures to meet contract requirements discovered before acceptance [@CL-CLS-0100; @CL-CLS-0205]. It also keeps the rule that acceptance is conclusive, except for latent defects, fraud, gross mistakes amounting to fraud, or as otherwise provided in the contract [@CL-CLS-0123; @CL-CLS-0204].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor delivers 500 units of custom hardware. The Government inspects them and accepts them. Two months later, the Government notices a surface scratch that was plainly visible at delivery. The contract has no other provision on the point, and the scratch involves no fraud and no gross mistake amounting to fraud. The answer: the scratch is not a latent defect, so none of the listed exceptions applies and the acceptance stays conclusive.

## Never confuse

Government inspection is not acceptance. Inspections and tests do not relieve the contractor of responsibility for defects discovered before acceptance [@CL-CLS-0205], whereas acceptance is conclusive except for the listed exceptions [@CL-CLS-0204].

When title passes to the Government is a separate question, covered in [Payments and responsibility for supplies](../revenue/cls-payments-and-responsibility-for-supplies.md).
