## Purpose

Explains what a fixed-price contract with prospective price redetermination provides, when it may be used, and the limitations that must be met before it is used.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CLS-0550] |
| Codified citation | FAR 16.205-1 [@CL-CLS-0550] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CLS-0550] |
| Applies to | Acquisitions of quantity production or services for which a firm fixed price can be negotiated for an initial period but not for later periods [@CL-CLS-0551] |

A fixed-price contract with prospective price redetermination provides for a firm fixed price for an initial period of contract deliveries or performance and prospective redetermination, at a stated time or times during performance, of the price for subsequent periods of performance [@CL-CLS-0550].

The initial period should be the longest period for which it is possible to negotiate a fair and reasonable firm fixed price, and each subsequent pricing period should be at least 12 months [@CL-CLS-0552].

## How it works

The contract may provide for a ceiling price based on evaluation of the uncertainties involved in performance and their possible cost impact [@CL-CLS-0553]. That ceiling price should provide for assumption of a reasonable proportion of the risk by the contractor and, once established, may be adjusted only by operation of contract clauses providing for equitable adjustment or other revision of the contract price under stated circumstances [@CL-CLS-0675].

This contract type shall not be used unless, among other conditions, negotiations have established that the conditions for use of a firm-fixed-price contract are not present and that a fixed-price incentive contract would not be more appropriate [@CL-CLS-0554]. The same list of limitations also names three further items [@CL-CLS-0555]. They are that the contractor's accounting system is adequate for price redetermination, that the prospective pricing periods can be made to conform with operation of that accounting system, and that there is reasonable assurance that price redetermination actions will take place promptly at the specified times [@CL-CLS-0555; @CL-CLS-0193].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor and the buyer agree a fixed-price contract for quantity production over three years. The first 18 months are priced at a firm fixed price of 500,000. Before that period ends, the parties redetermine the price for the next 12-month period and agree on 380,000 from evaluated production data. The answer: the 500,000 firm price covers the first 18 months, and the 380,000 redetermined price applies only to the following 12 months.

## Never confuse

Prospective redetermination is not retroactive redetermination. Under prospective redetermination the price is firm for the initial period and is redetermined for subsequent periods, whereas a fixed-ceiling-price contract with retroactive redetermination redetermines the price within a ceiling after completion of the contract [@CL-CLS-0550; @CL-CLS-0556]. For the retroactive type, see [Fixed-ceiling-price contracts with retroactive price redetermination](cls-fixed-ceiling-price-retroactive-price-redetermination.md). For general contract type context, see [Contract types](../../contract-finance/billing-payment/ctf-contract-types.md).
