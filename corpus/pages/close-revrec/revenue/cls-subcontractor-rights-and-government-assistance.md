## Purpose

Shows where a subcontractor stands after a prime contract is terminated, how a delay in settling a subcontractor proposal affects the prime settlement, and when the Government assists in settling a particular subcontract.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CLS-1258] |
| Codified citation | FAR 49.108-1, 49.108-6 and 49.108-7 [@CL-CLS-1258; @CL-CLS-1261; @CL-CLS-1263] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CLS-1258] |
| Applies to | Subcontractors of a terminated prime contract, and the prime contractor and subcontractors that settle with their immediate subcontractors [@CL-CLS-1258; @CL-CLS-1260] |

Subcontractor's rights. A subcontractor has no contractual rights against the Government upon the termination of a prime contract [@CL-CLS-1258]. It may have rights against the prime contractor or intermediate subcontractor with whom it has contracted [@CL-CLS-1259]. Upon termination of a prime contract, the prime contractor and each subcontractor are responsible for the prompt settlement of the settlement proposals of their immediate subcontractors [@CL-CLS-1260].

Delay. When a prime contractor's inability to settle with a subcontractor delays the settlement of the prime contract, the TCO may settle with the prime contractor [@CL-CLS-1261]. The TCO shall except the subcontractor settlement proposal from the settlement in whole or part and reserve the rights of the Government and the prime contractor with respect to that proposal [@CL-CLS-1262].

Government assistance. In unusual cases the TCO may determine, with the consent of the prime contractor, that it is in the Government's interest to provide assistance to the prime contractor in the settlement of a particular subcontract [@CL-CLS-1263]. In these situations, the Government, the prime contractor and a subcontractor may enter into an agreement covering the settlement of one or more subcontracts [@CL-CLS-1264]. In these settlements, the subcontractor shall be paid through the prime contractor as part of the overall settlement with the prime contractor [@CL-CLS-1265].

Authorization for subcontract settlements and the awards that follow them are handled in [Subcontract settlements: authority, judgments and arbitration awards](cls-subcontract-settlements-authorization-and-awards.md). This page does not restate them.

## How it works

The settlement duty follows the chain of contracts. The subcontractor's rights run to the party it contracted with, and the prime contractor and each subcontractor are responsible for the prompt settlement of the proposals of their immediate subcontractors [@CL-CLS-1258; @CL-CLS-1259; @CL-CLS-1260].

When a stuck subcontractor proposal delays the prime settlement, the TCO may settle with the prime contractor [@CL-CLS-1261]. The section then directs the TCO to except the subcontractor proposal, in whole or part, and to reserve the rights of the Government and the prime contractor on that proposal [@CL-CLS-1262].

Government assistance is the exception. In unusual cases the TCO may determine, with the consent of the prime contractor, that assistance is in the Government's interest [@CL-CLS-1263]. The three parties may then agree on settling one or more subcontracts [@CL-CLS-1264]. The subcontractor is paid through the prime contractor as part of the overall settlement [@CL-CLS-1265]. The section sets no threshold or deadline for these steps.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A prime contract with two subcontractors is terminated. Neither subcontractor has contractual rights against the Government [@CL-CLS-1258]. The prime contractor is responsible for the prompt settlement of the proposals of its immediate subcontractors [@CL-CLS-1260]. Both subcontractors here are immediate subcontractors. The prime contractor settles with the first subcontractor. It cannot settle with the second, and that inability delays the prime settlement. The TCO may then settle with the prime contractor. In that settlement the TCO shall except the second subcontractor's proposal, in whole or part, and reserve the rights of the Government and the prime contractor on it [@CL-CLS-1261; @CL-CLS-1262].

Separately, suppose the TCO determines in an unusual case, with the prime contractor's consent, that assisting in settling the second subcontract is in the Government's interest. The Government, the prime contractor and that subcontractor may agree on its settlement. The subcontractor is then paid through the prime contractor as part of the overall settlement [@CL-CLS-1263; @CL-CLS-1264; @CL-CLS-1265].

## Never confuse

Government assistance in a subcontract settlement is not a direct Government contract right for the subcontractor. The subcontractor has no contractual rights against the Government, and in an assisted settlement it is paid through the prime contractor [@CL-CLS-1258; @CL-CLS-1265]. Assistance is also not the general rule, because the section frames it for unusual cases and with the prime contractor's consent [@CL-CLS-1263]. A prime contractor's inability to settle with a subcontractor, where it delays the prime settlement, does not stop that settlement. The TCO may settle with the prime contractor and shall except the subcontractor proposal [@CL-CLS-1261; @CL-CLS-1262].

Excepting a proposal under the delay rule is a different step from assistance under the assistance rule [@CL-CLS-1262; @CL-CLS-1263]. Assignment of subcontract rights and settlement of a subcontractor's proposal by the TCO are covered in [Terminated subcontracts, assignment of rights and direct settlement](../../contract-finance/billing-payment/ctf-subcontract-termination-and-direct-settlement.md).
