## Purpose

Shows how a convenience termination notice may be given electronically or by letter, and what each notice contains.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CLS-1028] |
| Codified citation | FAR 49.601-1 and 49.601-2 [@CL-CLS-1028; @CL-CLS-1032]; FAR 49.601 [@CL-CLS-1469] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CLS-1028] |
| Applies to | Notices of termination for the Government's convenience. The suggested letter is for supply contracts and may be modified for other contracts and for subcontracts [@CL-CLS-1032]. |

The contracting officer may provide expedited notice of termination by electronic means that includes a requirement for the contractor to confirm receipt [@CL-CLS-1028]. If the contractor does not confirm receipt promptly, the contracting officer shall resend the notice electronically and expedite the letter notice described in FAR 49.601-2 [@CL-CLS-1029]. If confirmation is received and the electronic notice includes all content in FAR 49.601-2, the contracting officer need not send the letter notice [@CL-CLS-1030].

The section suggests a letter notice for use if a contract for supplies is being terminated for convenience, and with appropriate modifications it may be used for other contracts and for subcontracts [@CL-CLS-1032]. The letter shall be sent by certified mail, return receipt requested, or electronically, provided evidence of receipt is received by the contracting officer [@CL-CLS-1033]. If no prior electronic notice was issued, or if no confirmation of an electronic notice was received, the alternate notice that follows the letter notice is used [@CL-CLS-1065].

The section on notice of termination for convenience refers readers to 49.402-3(g) for notice of termination for default [@CL-CLS-1469].

## How it works

The first electronic notice printed in the section is suggested for use if a supply contract is being completely terminated for convenience, and if appropriately modified it may be used for other than supply contracts [@CL-CLS-1064; @CL-CLS-0190]. It tells the contractor to immediately stop all work, terminate subcontracts, and place no further orders, except to the extent (including, where the bracketed text is inserted, what is necessary to complete items not terminated) that it or a subcontractor wishes to retain and continue work-in-process or other materials for its own account [@CL-CLS-1031]. The letter notice tells the contractor to stop all work, make no further shipments, and place no further orders relating to the contract, except for the items the notice goes on to list [@CL-CLS-1034]. Those items are alternatives: the continued portion of the contract, if any, work-in-process or other materials the contractor may wish to retain for its own account, or work-in-process that the Contracting Officer authorizes the contractor to continue [@CL-CLS-1066]. The Contracting Officer may authorize work-in-process to continue for safety precautions, to clear or avoid damage to equipment, to avoid immediate complete spoilage of work-in-process having a definite commercial value, or to prevent any other undue loss to the Government [@CL-CLS-1067]. The letter also tells the contractor to keep adequate records of its compliance with subparagraph (1) showing the date it received the Notice of Termination, the effective date of the termination, and the extent of completion of performance on the effective date [@CL-CLS-1035], and to furnish notice of termination to each immediate subcontractor and supplier that will be affected by the termination, specifying the Government contract number, stating whether the contract has been terminated completely or partially, providing instructions to stop all work, make no further shipments, place no further orders, and terminate all subcontracts under the contract (subject to the exceptions in subparagraph (1)), providing instructions to submit any settlement proposal promptly, and requesting that similar notices and instructions be given to its immediate subcontractors [@CL-CLS-1036; @CL-CLS-0191; @CL-CLS-0192].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contracting officer emails a termination notice for a supply contract and asks the contractor to confirm receipt. The contractor confirms the same day, and the email carried all the content the letter would have carried, so no letter is needed. In a second case no confirmation arrives. The contracting officer resends the email and expedites the letter, and the letter uses the alternate opening paragraph because no confirmation was received.

## Never confuse

The texts printed in these sections are suggested formats, and the electronic route's own test for skipping the letter is that confirmation is received and the electronic notice includes all the content of the letter notice [@CL-CLS-1030]. The rules for what a notice of termination must state, and the termination contracting officer's later duties, are on [Termination authority, the notice of termination and the TCO's duties](cls-termination-authority-notice-and-tco-duties.md). The clauses under which a contract is terminated are on [Termination for convenience clauses and when each goes in](cls-termination-for-convenience-clauses.md), and the submission of the contractor's settlement proposal is on [Submission of settlement proposals after termination of a fixed-price contract](cls-fixed-price-settlement-proposal-submission.md).
