## Purpose

Sets out the framework for cost-reimbursement contracts: the cost ceiling, when they may be used, the acquisition plan, accounting system and resource conditions, and the commercial exclusion.

Cost-reimbursement types of contracts provide for payment of allowable incurred costs, to the extent prescribed in the contract [@CL-CLS-0567].

These contracts establish an estimate of total cost for the purpose of obligating funds and establishing a ceiling that the contractor may not exceed, except at its own risk, without the approval of the contracting officer [@CL-CLS-0680].

## How it works

The contracting officer must use cost-reimbursement contracts only when one of two alternative listed cases applies [@CL-CLS-0568; @CL-CLS-0681]. The first is circumstances that do not allow the agency to define its requirements sufficiently to allow for a fixed-price type contract [@CL-CLS-0568]. The second is uncertainties in contract performance that do not permit costs to be estimated with sufficient accuracy to use any type of fixed-price contract [@CL-CLS-0681]. The contracting officer must document the rationale for the contract type in the written acquisition plan and ensure that the plan is approved and signed at least one level above the contracting officer [@CL-CLS-0682].

A cost-reimbursement contract may be used only when all four listed conditions are met. The first three are that the factors in 16.104 have been considered, a written acquisition plan has been approved and signed at least one level above the contracting officer, and the contractor's accounting system is adequate for determining costs applicable to the contract or order [@CL-CLS-0569; @CL-CLS-0230]. The fourth is that, prior to award of the contract or order, adequate Government resources are available to award and manage a contract other than firm-fixed-priced [@CL-CLS-0570]. This includes appropriate Government surveillance during performance in accordance with 1.602-2, to provide reasonable assurance that efficient methods and effective cost controls are used [@CL-CLS-0683]. The use of cost-reimbursement contracts is prohibited for the acquisition of commercial products and commercial services [@CL-CLS-0571].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor performs an engineering development contract with an estimated total cost of 1,000,000 and an adequate accounting system. Incurred cost reaches 950,000 and the contractor expects the total to reach 1,100,000. The contracting officer approves a higher ceiling of 1,100,000. The answer: cost above the original 1,000,000 is reimbursable once the contracting officer approves it, and before approval the contractor exceeds the ceiling at its own risk.

## Never confuse

Cost-reimbursement types of contracts provide for payment of allowable incurred costs, to the extent prescribed in the contract [@CL-CLS-0567]. They establish an estimate of total cost for the purpose of obligating funds and establishing a ceiling that the contractor may not exceed, except at its own risk, without the approval of the contracting officer [@CL-CLS-0680]. The contracting officer shall use them only in one of the two alternative cases listed in FAR 16.301-2(a) [@CL-CLS-0568; @CL-CLS-0681]. For the notice duty as costs approach the ceiling, see [Limitation of cost](../../contract-finance/funding/ctf-limitation-of-cost.md). For a fixed-price contract that pays for effort, see [Firm-fixed-price, level-of-effort term contracts](cls-firm-fixed-price-level-of-effort.md). For the accounting system criteria, see [Accounting system criteria](../../compliance/business-systems/cmp-accounting-system-criteria.md).
