## Purpose

Shows how a registrant presents trade, related-party and other receivables, and what it separately discloses when receivables include amounts due under long-term contracts: retainage billed but not paid, unbilled amounts, claims and uncertain items, and amounts collectible after one year. This article and other articles of Regulation S-X provide clarification of certain disclosures that must be included in any event in financial statements filed with the Commission [@CL-CLS-0850].

The receivables item requires amounts receivable from customers (trade), from related parties (see section 210.4-08(k)), from underwriters, promoters and employees (other than related parties) which arose in other than the ordinary course of business, and from others to be stated separately [@CL-CLS-0819]. If the aggregate amount of notes receivable exceeds 10 percent of the aggregate amount of receivables, the above information shall be set forth separately, in the balance sheet or in a note thereto, for accounts receivable and notes receivable [@CL-CLS-0820].

If receivables include amounts due under long-term contracts (see section 210.5-02.6(d)), the listed amounts are stated separately in the balance sheet or in a note to the financial statements [@CL-CLS-0821]. One listed amount is balances billed but not paid by customers under retainage provisions in contracts [@CL-CLS-0822]. Another is amounts representing the recognized sales value of performance and such amounts that had not been billed and were not billable to customers at the date of the balance sheet, including a general description of the prerequisites for billing [@CL-CLS-0823]. A third is billed or unbilled amounts representing claims or other similar items subject to uncertainty concerning their determination or ultimate realization, including a description of the nature and status of the principal items comprising such amount [@CL-CLS-0824]. With respect to those three items, the amounts included in each item that are expected to be collected after one year are also stated, and when the amounts of retainage are expected to be collected is also stated by year if practicable [@CL-CLS-0825].

## How it works

The registrant states receivables separately by the four listed categories [@CL-CLS-0819]. It sets forth the information separately for accounts receivable and notes receivable when the aggregate amount of notes receivable exceeds 10 percent of the aggregate amount of receivables [@CL-CLS-0820]. For receivables that include amounts due under long-term contracts, it states separately the retainage, unbilled, and claims or uncertain amounts, and for each item it states the amounts expected to be collected after one year (and by year, if practicable, for retainage) [@CL-CLS-0821; @CL-CLS-0822; @CL-CLS-0823; @CL-CLS-0824; @CL-CLS-0825]. The cross-reference to section 210.5-02.6(d) in the long-term contracts lead-in [@CL-CLS-0821] is covered in [Inventories and long-term contract disclosures under Regulation S-X](../inventory-wip/cls-sx-inventories-long-term-contracts.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A registrant has 1,000 of receivables, of which 150 are notes. The notes exceed 10 percent of receivables, so accounts and notes are shown separately. Within the long-term contract balances, 60 is retainage billed but not paid, 90 is work recognized but not yet billable, and 20 is a claim under uncertainty. The note also states how much of each is expected after one year, and the year in which the retainage is expected.

## Never confuse

The notes test compares the aggregate amount of notes receivable with the aggregate amount of receivables [@CL-CLS-0820]. Amounts representing the recognized sales value of performance that had not been billed and were not billable to customers at the balance sheet date are a separate listed item from billed or unbilled amounts representing claims or other similar items subject to uncertainty [@CL-CLS-0823; @CL-CLS-0824]. For contract payment rules, see [Making payments](../../contract-finance/billing-payment/ctf-making-payments.md).

## Company calibration

How unbilled amounts, retainage and claims are identified and reviewed at close, and what the company's policy is for expected collection after one year. [@Q-027]
