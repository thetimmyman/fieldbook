## Purpose

Shows when the Government may terminate a fixed-price construction contract for default, which delays are excused, the notice a delayed Contractor gives, and when a default termination converts to one for convenience.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CLS-1118] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.249-10 and FAR 52.249-14 [@CL-CLS-1118; @CL-CLS-1122] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.249-10 and RFO FAR 52.249-14 [@CL-CLS-0061; @CL-CLS-0068] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CLS-1118] |
| Applies to | Contracts containing the Default (Fixed-Price Construction) clause, and contracts for which the Excusable Delays clause is prescribed [@CL-CLS-1118; @CL-CLS-1123] |

If the Contractor refuses or fails to prosecute the work or any separable part with the diligence that will insure its completion within the time specified in the contract, including any extension, or fails to complete the work within this time, the Government may, by written notice to the Contractor, terminate the right to proceed with the delayed work or separable part [@CL-CLS-1594; @CL-CLS-1118]. Which clause goes into which fixed-price contract is on [Default clauses for fixed-price contracts and when each goes in](cls-fixed-price-default-clauses.md).

Under the basic fixed-price construction default clause, one listed condition for not terminating the right to proceed or charging damages is that the delay in completing the work arises from unforeseeable causes beyond the control and without the fault or negligence of the Contractor [@CL-CLS-1119]. The clause gives a list of alternative examples of such causes, which begins with acts of God or of the public enemy, acts of the Government in either its sovereign or contractual capacity, acts of another Contractor in the performance of a contract with the Government, fires, floods and epidemics, and goes on to further examples [@CL-CLS-1147]. The second listed condition is that the Contractor, within 10 days from the beginning of any delay (unless extended by the Contracting Officer), notifies the Contracting Officer in writing of the causes of delay [@CL-CLS-1120]. The two conditions are listed together, and the second is introduced by the word "and" [@CL-CLS-1119; @CL-CLS-1120].

The Contracting Officer shall ascertain the facts and the extent of delay [@CL-CLS-0228]. If, in the judgment of the Contracting Officer, the findings of fact warrant such action, the time for completing the work shall be extended, and the findings of the Contracting Officer shall be final and conclusive on the parties but subject to appeal under the Disputes clause [@CL-CLS-1148]. The claims process is on [Disputes clause, claims and certification](cls-disputes-clause.md).

If, after termination of the Contractor's right to proceed, it is determined that the Contractor was not in default or that the delay was excusable, the rights and obligations of the parties will be the same as if the termination had been issued for the convenience of the Government [@CL-CLS-1121].

Except for defaults of subcontractors at any tier, the Contractor shall not be in default because of any failure to perform the contract under its terms if the failure arises from causes beyond the control and without the fault or negligence of the Contractor [@CL-CLS-1122]. The Excusable Delays clause is prescribed for solicitations and contracts for supplies, services, construction, and research and development on a fee basis whenever a cost-reimbursement contract is contemplated, and also for time-and-material contracts and labor-hour contracts [@CL-CLS-1123]. Upon request of the Contractor, the Contracting Officer shall ascertain the facts and extent of the failure [@CL-CLS-1595]. In the basic text of the Excusable Delays clause, if the Contracting Officer determines that any failure to perform results from one or more of the causes the clause lists above, the delivery schedule shall be revised, subject to the rights of the Government under the termination clause of the contract [@CL-CLS-1124]. If the failure to perform is caused by the failure of a subcontractor at any tier to perform or make progress, and the cause of the failure was beyond the control of both the Contractor and subcontractor and without the fault or negligence of either, the Contractor shall not be deemed to be in default, unless all of three conditions apply: the subcontracted supplies or services were obtainable from other sources, the Contracting Officer ordered the Contractor in writing to purchase these supplies or services from the other source, and the Contractor failed to comply reasonably with the order [@CL-CLS-1149; @CL-CLS-0229].

## How it works

Where the Contractor refuses or fails to prosecute the work or any separable part with the diligence that will insure its completion within the time specified, or fails to complete the work within that time, the Government may, by written notice, terminate the right to proceed with the delayed work or separable part [@CL-CLS-1594; @CL-CLS-1118]. The right to proceed is not terminated, and damages are not charged, if the cause condition and the notice condition are both met [@CL-CLS-1119; @CL-CLS-1120]. If a default termination is later found wrong, or the delay excusable, the parties stand as if the termination were for convenience [@CL-CLS-1121]. Under the separate Excusable Delays clause, except for defaults of subcontractors at any tier, the Contractor shall not be in default because of a failure to perform that arises from causes beyond its control and without its fault or negligence, and the delivery schedule shall be revised if the Contracting Officer determines that a failure to perform results from one or more of the causes the clause lists [@CL-CLS-1122; @CL-CLS-1124].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text from February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. RFO FAR 52.249-10 has the same Government right to terminate the right to proceed for failure to complete the work within the time specified in the contract [@CL-CLS-0061; @CL-CLS-1118], the same unforeseeable-cause condition [@CL-CLS-0062; @CL-CLS-1119], and the same first six examples of such causes [@CL-CLS-0063; @CL-CLS-1147], with its list of alternatives going on to quarantine restrictions, strikes, freight embargoes, unusually severe weather, or delays of subcontractors or suppliers at any tier arising from unforeseeable causes beyond the control and without the fault or negligence of both the Contractor and the subcontractors or suppliers [@CL-CLS-0064]. It has the same 10-day notice condition [@CL-CLS-0065; @CL-CLS-1120], the same time extension and finality rule [@CL-CLS-0066; @CL-CLS-1148], and the same rule that a default termination later found wrong or excusable is treated as one for convenience [@CL-CLS-0067; @CL-CLS-1121]. RFO FAR 52.249-14 has the same excusable delays rule [@CL-CLS-0068; @CL-CLS-1122], the same prescription [@CL-CLS-0069; @CL-CLS-1123], and the same delivery schedule revision [@CL-CLS-0070; @CL-CLS-1124]. It has the same subcontractor rule [@CL-CLS-0071; @CL-CLS-0072; @CL-CLS-1149], with three conditions after unless, all of which must apply: that the subcontracted supplies or services were obtainable from other sources, that the Contracting Officer ordered the Contractor in writing to purchase them from the other source, and that the Contractor failed to comply reasonably with the order [@CL-CLS-0073].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A fixed-price construction contract falls behind after a severe flood delays the site. The Contractor notifies the Contracting Officer in writing within the notice period. The Government terminates the right to proceed anyway. A later determination finds the delay excusable, so the parties are treated as if the termination were for convenience, and the Contractor's costs are settled on that basis rather than as default damages.

## Never confuse

If, after termination of the Contractor's right to proceed, it is determined that the Contractor was not in default or that the delay was excusable, the rights and obligations of the parties will be the same as if the termination had been issued for the convenience of the Government [@CL-CLS-1121]. The construction default clause states a 10-day notice by the delayed Contractor, unless extended by the Contracting Officer [@CL-CLS-1120]. The convenience clauses are on [Termination for convenience clauses and when each goes in](cls-termination-for-convenience-clauses.md), and the effects of a default termination are on [Termination for default: effect, notices and repurchase against the contractor](cls-termination-for-default-effect-and-repurchase.md).
