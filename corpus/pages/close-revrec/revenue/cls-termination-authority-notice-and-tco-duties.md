## Purpose

Shows who decides on a termination, what the written notice of termination must state, and what the termination contracting officer does once the notice is out.

## The rule

The contracting officer shall terminate contracts, whether for default or convenience, only when it is in the Government's interest [@CL-CLS-0900]. When the price of the undelivered balance is less than $5,000, the contract should not normally be terminated for convenience but should be permitted to run to completion [@CL-CLS-0972]. The contracting officer shall effect a no-cost settlement instead of issuing a termination notice when it is known that the contractor will accept one, Government property was not furnished, and there are no outstanding payments, debts due the Government, or other contractor obligations [@CL-CLS-0965]. After the contracting officer issues the notice of termination, the termination contracting officer (TCO) is responsible for negotiating any settlement with the contractor, including a no-cost settlement if appropriate [@CL-CLS-0901].

The notice of termination shall state, among its listed items, that the contract is being terminated under the clause that authorizes the termination, the effective date of termination, the extent of termination, and any special instructions [@CL-CLS-0902]. Another listed item is the steps the contractor should take to minimize the impact on personnel, which applies if the termination, together with all other outstanding terminations, will result in a significant reduction in the contractor's work force [@CL-CLS-0903].

## How it works

The section sets out the TCO's duties under a lead-in that ties them to the termination clause and the notice of termination, and the first duty is to direct the action required of the prime contractor [@CL-CLS-0904]. The next listed duty is to examine the prime contractor's settlement proposal and, when appropriate, the settlement proposals of subcontractors [@CL-CLS-0905]. The listed duties then turn to negotiating a settlement and, where a complete settlement cannot be negotiated, to settling by determination the elements that cannot be agreed on [@CL-CLS-0906]. Of the four listed duties, only the two settlement duties carry a timing word, which is promptly [@CL-CLS-0906].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contracting officer decides that a fixed-price supply contract should be terminated for convenience because it is in the Government's interest. The notice states the clause, the effective date, that the remaining units are terminated, and a special instruction to stop ordering material.

The TCO then directs the contractor's wind-down, examines the contractor's settlement proposal of 500,000 and the subcontractor proposals, and negotiates. The parties agree on every element except a 20,000 item. The TCO settles that item by determination.

Answer: the contracting officer decided to terminate and issued the notice, and the TCO owns the negotiation and the determination of the one element not agreed.

## Never confuse

The contracting officer who decides to terminate and issues the notice is a different role from the TCO who then negotiates the settlement [@CL-CLS-0900; @CL-CLS-0901]. A negotiated settlement is also not a settlement by determination: among the TCO's listed duties, the TCO settles by determination the elements that cannot be agreed on if unable to negotiate a complete settlement [@CL-CLS-0906], and the separate section on determinations also covers a settlement proposal that is not submitted within the period the termination clause requires [@CL-CLS-0925]. For when a settlement proposal goes to audit, see [Audit referral of termination settlement proposals and subcontract settlements](cls-termination-settlement-audit-referral.md).
