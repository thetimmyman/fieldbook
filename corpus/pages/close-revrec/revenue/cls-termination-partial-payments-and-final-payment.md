## Purpose

Explains how partial payments on a termination settlement proposal are requested and capped, what happens if they turn out to be too large, and how the final payment and its interest are handled.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CLS-0929] |
| Codified citation | FAR 49.112-1 and 49.112-2 [@CL-CLS-0929; @CL-CLS-0939] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CLS-0929] |
| Applies to | Partial payments: contracts that authorize partial payments on settlement proposals before settlement [@CL-CLS-0929]. Final payment: a settlement agreement or a settlement by determination [@CL-CLS-0939; @CL-CLS-0940] |

If the contract authorizes partial payments on settlement proposals before settlement, a prime contractor may request them on the prescribed form at any time after submission of interim or final settlement proposals [@CL-CLS-0929]. Except for undelivered acceptable finished products, partial payments shall not be made for profit or fee claimed under the terminated portion of the contract [@CL-CLS-0930]. If the reviews and the TCO's examination indicate that the requested payment is proper, reasonable payments may be authorized in the discretion of the TCO up to the listed amounts [@CL-CLS-0931]. One listed limit is 100 percent of the contract price, adjusted for undelivered acceptable items completed before the termination date or later completed with the TCO's approval [@CL-CLS-0932]. Another is 90 percent of the direct cost of termination inventory [@CL-CLS-0933], and another is 90 percent of other allowable costs allocable to the terminated portion and not included in the other listed items [@CL-CLS-0934]. The TCO deducts, among the listed deductions, unliquidated balances of progress and advance payments, with interest, that are allocable to the terminated portion [@CL-CLS-0935]. The total of all partial payments shall not exceed the amount that will, in the opinion of the TCO, become due because of the termination [@CL-CLS-0936].

If the total of partial payments exceeds the amount finally determined due on the settlement proposal, the contractor shall repay the excess to the Government on demand, together with interest [@CL-CLS-0937]. The interest is computed at the rate established by the Secretary of the Treasury, from the date the excess payment was received to the date of repayment [@CL-CLS-0952]. Interest is not charged in every case. One exception is an excess payment attributable to a reduction in the settlement proposal because of retention or other disposition of termination inventory, until 10 days after the retention or disposition or a later date determined by the TCO [@CL-CLS-0938]. A second exception is an overpayment under cost-reimbursement research and development contracts without profit or fee, if the overpayments are repaid to the Government within 30 days after demand [@CL-CLS-0953].

## How it works

The source gives payment events, not accounting entries, so how a finance reader books them is the company's policy and is not established here. A prime contractor may request a partial payment at any time after it submits an interim or final settlement proposal [@CL-CLS-0929]. Any excess is repayable on demand with interest, subject to the two listed exceptions [@CL-CLS-0937; @CL-CLS-0938; @CL-CLS-0953]. After a settlement agreement, the final voucher shows the amount agreed upon, less any portion previously paid [@CL-CLS-0939]. After a settlement by determination, the voucher shows the amount determined due if there is no appeal within the allowed time [@CL-CLS-0940], or the amount finally determined due on the appeal if there is one [@CL-CLS-0954], in each case less any portion previously paid. The Government shall not pay interest on the amount due under a settlement agreement or a settlement by determination, but may pay interest on a successful contractor appeal under the Disputes clause [@CL-CLS-0941]. For how a determination is reached and appealed, see [Settlement by determination under a terminated contract](cls-settlement-by-determination.md). For subcontract settlements, see [Subcontract settlements: authority, judgments and arbitration awards](cls-subcontract-settlements-authorization-and-awards.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contract authorizes partial payments. The contractor submits an interim settlement proposal and receives partial payments that total 600,000 after the TCO's deductions. Later the settlement is agreed at 800,000.

Answer: the final voucher is for 200,000, which is 800,000 less the 600,000 already paid, and the Government pays no interest on it. If the agreed amount had been 550,000, the contractor would repay 50,000 on demand with interest from the date the excess was received, unless one of the listed exceptions applied.

## Never confuse

Partial payments on a settlement proposal are not progress or advance payments, although unliquidated balances of those payments are deducted from a partial payment [@CL-CLS-0935]. Interest is charged on a repaid excess, subject to its exceptions, and is not paid on the final amount due, although it may be paid on a successful contractor appeal [@CL-CLS-0937; @CL-CLS-0941]. For the progress payment mechanics, see [Progress payment liquidation](../../contract-finance/financing/ctf-progress-payment-liquidation.md). For the settlement deductions, see [Termination settlements: deductions, completed end items, and settlement limitation](cls-termination-settlement-deductions-and-limitations.md). For the clause itself, see [Termination for Convenience of the Government (Fixed-Price): settlement](../../contract-finance/billing-payment/ctf-termination-for-convenience-settlement.md).
