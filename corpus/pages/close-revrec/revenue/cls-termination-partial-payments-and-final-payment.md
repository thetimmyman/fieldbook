## Purpose

Explains how partial payments on a termination settlement proposal are requested and capped, what happens if they turn out to be too large, and how the final payment and its interest are handled.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CLS-0929] |
| Codified citation | FAR 49.112-1 and 49.112-2 [@CL-CLS-0929; @CL-CLS-0939] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CLS-0929] |
| Applies to | Partial payments: contracts that authorize partial payments on settlement proposals before settlement [@CL-CLS-0929]. Final payment: a settlement agreement or a settlement by determination [@CL-CLS-0939; @CL-CLS-0940] |

If the contract authorizes partial payments on settlement proposals before settlement, a prime contractor may request them on the prescribed form at any time after submission of interim or final settlement proposals [@CL-CLS-0929]. Except for undelivered acceptable finished products, partial payments shall not be made for profit or fee claimed under the terminated portion of the contract [@CL-CLS-0930]. Before approving any partial payment, the TCO shall obtain any desired accounting, engineering, or other specialized reviews of the data submitted in support of the contractor's settlement proposal [@CL-CLS-0419]. If the reviews and the TCO's examination indicate that the requested payment is proper, reasonable payments may be authorized in the discretion of the TCO up to the five listed amounts [@CL-CLS-0931]. The five amounts are: (1) 100 percent of the contract price, adjusted for undelivered acceptable items completed before the termination date or later completed with the approval of the TCO [@CL-CLS-0932], (2) 100 percent of the amount of any subcontract settlement settled and paid by the prime contractor if the settlement was approved or ratified by the TCO under 49.108-3(c) or was authorized under 49.108-4 [@CL-CLS-0420], (3) 90 percent of the direct cost of termination inventory, including costs of raw materials, purchased parts, supplies, and direct labor [@CL-CLS-0933], (4) 90 percent of other allowable costs (including settlement expense and manufacturing and administrative indirect costs) allocable to the terminated portion of the contract and not included in headings (1), (2), or (3) [@CL-CLS-0934], and (5) 100 percent of partial payments made to subcontractors under this section [@CL-CLS-0421]. In arriving at the amount of any partial payment to be made, the TCO shall deduct from the gross amount of items payable under this section both (1) all unliquidated balances of progress and advance payments (including interest) made to the contractor which are allocable to the terminated portion of the contract [@CL-CLS-0935] and (2) the amounts of all credits arising from the purchase, retention, or sale of property the costs of which are included in the payment request [@CL-CLS-0422]. The total amount of all partial payments made shall not exceed the amount that will, in the opinion of the TCO, become due to the contractor because of the termination [@CL-CLS-0936].

If the total of partial payments exceeds the amount finally determined due on the settlement proposal, the contractor shall repay the excess to the Government on demand, together with interest [@CL-CLS-0937]. The interest shall be computed at the rate established by the Secretary of the Treasury under title 50 of the United States Code Appendix, section 1215(b)(2), from the date the excess payment was received by the contractor to the date of repayment [@CL-CLS-0952]. However, interest will not be charged for any (1) excess payment attributable to a reduction in the settlement proposal because of retention or other disposition of termination inventory, until 10 days after the date of the retention or disposition, or a later date determined by the TCO because of the circumstances [@CL-CLS-0938], or (2) overpayment under cost-reimbursement research and development contracts without profit or fee if the overpayments are repaid to the Government within 30 days after demand [@CL-CLS-0953].

## How it works

A prime contractor may request a partial payment at any time after submission of interim or final settlement proposals [@CL-CLS-0929]. Any excess is repayable on demand with interest, subject to the two listed exceptions [@CL-CLS-0937; @CL-CLS-0938; @CL-CLS-0953]. Upon execution of a settlement agreement, the contractor shall submit a voucher or invoice showing the amount agreed upon, less any portion previously paid, and the TCO shall attach a copy of the settlement agreement to the voucher or invoice and forward the documents to the disbursing officer for payment [@CL-CLS-0939]. After a settlement by determination, if the contractor does not appeal within the time allowed, the contractor shall submit a voucher or invoice showing the amount determined due, less any portion previously paid [@CL-CLS-0940], and if the contractor appeals, the contractor shall submit a voucher or invoice showing the amount finally determined due on the appeal, less any portion previously paid [@CL-CLS-0954]. Pending determination of any appeal, the contractor may submit vouchers or invoices for charges that are not directly involved with the portion being appealed, without prejudice to the rights of either party on the appeal [@CL-CLS-0429]. The Government shall not pay interest on the amount due under a settlement agreement or a settlement by determination, and the Government may, however, pay interest on a successful contractor appeal from a contracting officer's determination under the Disputes clause at 52.233-1 [@CL-CLS-0941]. For how a determination is reached and appealed, see [Settlement by determination under a terminated contract](cls-settlement-by-determination.md). For subcontract settlements, see [Subcontract settlements: authority, judgments and arbitration awards](cls-subcontract-settlements-authorization-and-awards.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contract authorizes partial payments. The contractor submits an interim settlement proposal and receives partial payments that total 600,000 after the TCO's deductions. Later the settlement is agreed at 800,000.

Answer: the final voucher is for 200,000, which is 800,000 less the 600,000 already paid, and the Government pays no interest on it. If the agreed amount had been 550,000, the contractor would repay 50,000 on demand with interest from the date the excess was received, unless one of the listed exceptions applied.

## Never confuse

Partial payments on a settlement proposal are not progress or advance payments, although unliquidated balances of those payments are deducted from a partial payment [@CL-CLS-0935]. Interest is charged on a repaid excess, subject to its exceptions, and is not paid on the final amount due, although it may be paid on a successful contractor appeal [@CL-CLS-0937; @CL-CLS-0941]. For the progress payment mechanics, see [Progress payment liquidation](../../contract-finance/financing/ctf-progress-payment-liquidation.md). For the settlement deductions, see [Termination settlements: deductions, completed end items, and settlement limitation](cls-termination-settlement-deductions-and-limitations.md). For the clause itself, see [Termination for Convenience of the Government (Fixed-Price): settlement](../../contract-finance/billing-payment/ctf-termination-for-convenience-settlement.md).
