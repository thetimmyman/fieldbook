## Purpose

Establishes the standard of fair compensation and business judgment for termination settlements and assigns responsibility for settling unsettled contract changes.

A settlement should compensate the contractor fairly for the work done and the preparations made for the terminated portions of the contract, including a reasonable allowance for profit [@CL-CLS-0500].

The cost principles and procedures in the applicable subpart of part 31 shall, subject to the general principles in 49.201, (a) be used in asserting, negotiating, or determining costs relevant to termination settlements under contracts with other than educational institutions [@CL-CLS-0504], and (b) be a guide for the negotiation of settlements under contracts for experimental, developmental, or research work with educational institutions (but see 31.104) [@CL-CLS-0432].

## How it works

Fair compensation is a matter of judgment and cannot be measured exactly, and the use of business judgment, as distinguished from strict accounting principles, is the heart of a settlement [@CL-CLS-0500; @CL-CLS-0501]. The primary objective is to negotiate a settlement by agreement, and the parties may agree on a total amount without agreeing on or segregating the particular elements of costs or profit [@CL-CLS-0502]. Cost and accounting data may provide guides, but are not rigid measures, and in appropriate cases costs may be estimated, differences compromised, and doubtful questions settled by agreement [@CL-CLS-0503]. Recordkeeping, reporting, and accounting related to the settlement should be kept to a minimum compatible with the reasonable protection of the public interest [@CL-CLS-0650].

Before settlement of a completely terminated contract, the termination contracting officer must obtain from the contracting office a list of all related unsettled contract changes [@CL-CLS-0505]. The termination contracting officer must settle those changes as part of final settlement after obtaining the recommendations of the contracting office [@CL-CLS-0506]. When a contract has been partially terminated, outstanding unsettled contract changes will usually be handled by the contracting officer, who may delegate that function to the termination contracting officer [@CL-CLS-0507].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor incurs direct costs of 400,000 and indirect costs of 80,000 preparing for a terminated manufacturing order. The exact split of effort across preliminary tasks cannot be measured precisely because engineering notes are commingled. The parties use business judgment and agree on a total of 520,000, including profit, without segregating the individual cost elements. The answer: the agreement stands as a total amount, because the parties may agree on a total without segregating the elements of cost or profit.
