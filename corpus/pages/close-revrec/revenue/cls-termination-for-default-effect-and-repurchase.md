## Purpose

Explains what a termination for default means for payment, the cure notice and show cause notice steps, and the excess reprocurement costs the contractor may owe.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CLS-0942]; RFO model text [@CL-CLS-1541] |
| Codified citation | FAR 49.402-1, 49.402-2, 49.402-3 and 49.402-6 [@CL-CLS-0966; @CL-CLS-0942; @CL-CLS-0947; @CL-CLS-0949]; FAR 2.101 [@CL-CLS-1522] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 2.101 [@CL-CLS-1541] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CLS-0942] |
| Applies to | Contracts containing the Default clause at 52.249-8 [@CL-CLS-0966] |

Under contracts containing the Default clause at 52.249-8, the Government has the right, subject to the notice requirements of the clause, to terminate the contract completely or partially for default if the contractor fails to: (a) make delivery of the supplies or perform the services within the time specified in the contract, (b) perform any other provision of the contract, or (c) make progress and that failure endangers performance of the contract [@CL-CLS-0966; @CL-CLS-0194; @CL-CLS-0967].

Under a termination for default, the Government is not liable for the contractor's costs on undelivered work and is entitled to the repayment of advance and progress payments, if any, applicable to that work [@CL-CLS-0942]. The Government may elect, under the Default clause, to require the contractor to transfer title and deliver to the Government completed supplies and manufacturing materials, as directed by the contracting officer [@CL-CLS-0956]. Subject to paragraph (d) of the section, the Government shall pay the contractor the contract price for any completed supplies, and the amount agreed upon by the contracting officer and the contractor for any manufacturing materials, acquired by the Government under the Default clause [@CL-CLS-0943]. Paragraph (d) requires that the Government be protected from overpayment that might result from failure to provide for its potential liability to laborers and material suppliers for lien rights outstanding against the completed supplies or materials after the Government has paid the contractor for them [@CL-CLS-0955]. To accomplish this, before paying for supplies or materials, the contracting officer shall take one or more of the following measures: (1) ascertain whether the payment bonds, if any, furnished by the contractor are adequate to satisfy all lienors' claims or whether it is feasible to obtain similar bonds to cover outstanding liens, (2) require the contractor to furnish appropriate statements from laborers and material suppliers disclaiming any lien rights they may have to the supplies and materials, (3) obtain appropriate agreement by the Government, the contractor, and lienors ensuring release of the Government from any potential liability to the contractor or lienors, (4) withhold from the amount due for the supplies or materials any amount the contracting officer determines necessary to protect the Government's interest, but only if the measures in subparagraphs (d)(1), (2), and (3) cannot be accomplished or are considered inadequate, and (5) take other appropriate action considering the circumstances and the degree of the contractor's solvency [@CL-CLS-0195; @CL-CLS-0196; @CL-CLS-0197; @CL-CLS-0198; @CL-CLS-0199].

The contractor is liable to the Government for any excess costs incurred in acquiring supplies and services similar to those terminated for default (see 49.402-6), and for any other damages, whether or not repurchase is effected (see 49.402-7) [@CL-CLS-0944]. Excess cost may not be charged against the defaulting contractor for more than the undelivered quantity terminated for default, including variations in quantity permitted by the terminated contract [@CL-CLS-0957]. If repurchase is made at a price over the price of the supplies or services terminated, the contracting officer shall, after completion and final payment of the repurchase contract, make a written demand on the contractor for the total amount of the excess, giving consideration to any increases or decreases in other costs such as transportation and discounts [@CL-CLS-0949; @CL-CLS-0970]. If the contractor fails to make payment, the contracting officer shall follow the procedures in subpart 32.6 for collecting contract debts due the Government [@CL-CLS-0418].

In FAR 2.101, termination for default means the exercise of the Government's right to completely or partially terminate a contract because of the contractor's actual or anticipated failure to perform its contractual obligations [@CL-CLS-1522]. In RFO FAR 2.101, termination for default means the exercise of the Government's right to completely or partially terminate a contract because of the contractor's actual or anticipated failure to perform its contractual obligations [@CL-CLS-1541].

## How it works

Subdivisions (a)(1)(ii) and (a)(1)(iii) of the Default clause cover situations when the contractor fails to perform some of the other provisions of the contract (such as not furnishing a required performance bond) or so fails to make progress as to endanger performance of the contract [@CL-CLS-0945]. If the termination is predicated upon this type of failure, the contracting officer shall give the contractor written notice specifying the failure and providing a period of 10 days (or longer period as necessary) in which to cure the failure [@CL-CLS-0946]. Upon expiration of the 10 days (or longer period), the contracting officer may issue a notice of termination for default unless it is determined that the failure to perform has been cured [@CL-CLS-0968]. Subdivision (a)(1)(i) of the Default clause covers situations when the contractor has defaulted by failure to make delivery of the supplies or to perform the services within the specified time, and in those situations no notice of failure or of the possibility of termination for default is required to be sent to the contractor before the actual notice of termination (but see paragraph (e) of the section) [@CL-CLS-0958; @CL-CLS-0959]. However, if the Government has taken any action that might be construed as a waiver of the contract delivery or performance date, the contracting officer shall send a notice to the contractor setting a new date for the contractor to make delivery or complete performance [@CL-CLS-1564], and that notice shall reserve the Government's rights under the Default clause [@CL-CLS-1565]. If termination for default appears appropriate, the contracting officer should, if practicable, notify the contractor in writing of the possibility of the termination, and that notice shall call the contractor's attention to the contractual liabilities if the contract is terminated for default and request the contractor to show cause why the contract should not be terminated for default [@CL-CLS-0947; @CL-CLS-0948].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contract for 1,000 units at 1,000 each is terminated for default after 600 units are delivered and paid. Progress payments of 100,000 relate to the 400 undelivered units. The Government repurchases 400 similar units at 1,300 each.

Answer: the Government is not liable for the contractor's costs on the undelivered units, and the progress payments applicable to that work are repayable. The excess is 300 per unit across 400 units, which is 120,000, and the contracting officer demands it after completion and final payment of the repurchase contract. Other damages may also be owed.

## Never confuse

Under a termination for default, the Government is not liable for the contractor's costs on undelivered work and is entitled to the repayment of advance and progress payments, if any, applicable to that work [@CL-CLS-0942]. For the notice that a termination must state, see [Termination authority, the notice of termination and the TCO's duties](cls-termination-authority-notice-and-tco-duties.md). For the convenience settlement, see [Termination for Convenience of the Government (Fixed-Price): settlement](../../contract-finance/billing-payment/ctf-termination-for-convenience-settlement.md). A cure notice gives a period of 10 days (or longer period as necessary) to cure a failure covered by subdivision (a)(1)(ii) or (a)(1)(iii) [@CL-CLS-0945; @CL-CLS-0946], while a show cause notice requests the contractor to show cause why the contract should not be terminated for default [@CL-CLS-0948]. For repayment of progress payments, see [Progress payment liquidation](../../contract-finance/financing/ctf-progress-payment-liquidation.md).
