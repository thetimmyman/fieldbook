## Purpose

Explains what a termination for default means for payment, the cure notice and show cause notice steps, and the excess reprocurement costs the contractor may owe.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CLS-0942] |
| Codified citation | FAR 49.402-1, 49.402-2, 49.402-3 and 49.402-6 [@CL-CLS-0966; @CL-CLS-0942; @CL-CLS-0947; @CL-CLS-0949]; FAR 2.101 [@CL-CLS-1522] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CLS-0942] |
| Applies to | Contracts containing the Default clause at 52.249-8 [@CL-CLS-0966] |

Under contracts containing the Default clause at 52.249-8, the Government's right to terminate the contract completely or partially for default is subject to the notice requirements of the clause [@CL-CLS-0966]. The section lists three failures: to make delivery of the supplies or perform the services within the time specified in the contract, to perform any other provision of the contract, and to make progress where that failure endangers performance of the contract [@CL-CLS-0967].

Under a termination for default, the Government is not liable for the contractor's costs on undelivered work and is entitled to the repayment of advance and progress payments, if any, applicable to that work [@CL-CLS-0942]. The Government may elect, under the Default clause, to require the contractor to transfer title and deliver to the Government completed supplies and manufacturing materials, as directed by the contracting officer [@CL-CLS-0956]. Subject to the lien protection in the section's paragraph on overpayment, the Government shall pay the contractor the contract price for any completed supplies, and the amount agreed upon by the contracting officer and the contractor for any manufacturing materials, acquired by the Government under the Default clause [@CL-CLS-0943]. That paragraph requires that the Government be protected from overpayment that might result from failure to provide for its potential liability to laborers and material suppliers for lien rights outstanding against the completed supplies or materials after it has paid the contractor for them [@CL-CLS-0955].

The contractor is liable to the Government for any excess costs incurred in acquiring supplies and services similar to those terminated for default, and for any other damages, whether or not repurchase is effected [@CL-CLS-0944]. Excess cost may not be charged against the defaulting contractor for more than the undelivered quantity terminated for default, including variations in quantity permitted by the terminated contract [@CL-CLS-0957]. If repurchase is made at a price over the price of the supplies or services terminated, the contracting officer shall, after completion and final payment of the repurchase contract, make a written demand on the contractor for the total amount of the excess [@CL-CLS-0949]. In making that demand, the contracting officer gives consideration to any increases or decreases in other costs such as transportation and discounts [@CL-CLS-0970].

The FAR definitions section describes termination for default as the exercise of the Government's right to completely or partially terminate a contract because of the contractor's actual or anticipated failure to perform its contractual obligations [@CL-CLS-1522]. The RFO text of FAR 2.101 defines termination for default as the exercise of the Government's right to completely or partially terminate a contract because of the contractor's actual or anticipated failure to perform its contractual obligations, the same in substance as the codified definition [@CL-CLS-1541; @CL-CLS-1522].

## How it works

The section treats a cure notice and a show cause notice as different steps. A cure notice applies to a failure to perform other contract provisions or a failure to make progress that endangers performance [@CL-CLS-0945]. For that kind of failure, the contracting officer gives written notice specifying the failure and a period of 10 days, or a longer period as necessary, to cure it [@CL-CLS-0945; @CL-CLS-0946]. Upon expiration of that period, the contracting officer may issue a notice of termination for default unless it is determined that the failure to perform has been cured [@CL-CLS-0968]. A default by failure to deliver or perform within the specified time is treated differently: no notice of failure or of the possibility of termination for default is required before the actual notice of termination, but the paragraph on show cause notices is cross-referenced [@CL-CLS-0958; @CL-CLS-0959]. If termination for default appears appropriate, the contracting officer should, if practicable, notify the contractor in writing of the possibility of the termination, and that notice calls attention to the contractual liabilities and requests the contractor to show cause why the contract should not be terminated [@CL-CLS-0947; @CL-CLS-0948]. The section states no dollar threshold or fee for these steps.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contract for 1,000 units at 1,000 each is terminated for default after 600 units are delivered and paid. Progress payments of 100,000 relate to the 400 undelivered units. The Government repurchases 400 similar units at 1,300 each.

Answer: the Government is not liable for the contractor's costs on the undelivered units, and the progress payments applicable to that work are repayable. The excess is 300 per unit across 400 units, which is 120,000, and the contracting officer demands it after completion and final payment of the repurchase contract. Other damages may also be owed.

## Never confuse

A termination for default is not a convenience settlement, because under default the Government is not liable for the contractor's costs on undelivered work [@CL-CLS-0942]. For the notice that a termination must state, see [Termination authority, the notice of termination and the TCO's duties](cls-termination-authority-notice-and-tco-duties.md). For the convenience settlement, see [Termination for Convenience of the Government (Fixed-Price): settlement](../../contract-finance/billing-payment/ctf-termination-for-convenience-settlement.md). A cure notice gives a period to cure a listed kind of failure [@CL-CLS-0946], while a show cause notice asks why the contract should not be terminated [@CL-CLS-0948]. For repayment of progress payments, see [Progress payment liquidation](../../contract-finance/financing/ctf-progress-payment-liquidation.md).
