## Purpose

Covers the fixed-ceiling-price contract with retroactive price redetermination: what it provides, when it is appropriate, the ceiling and billing price rules, and the limitations on its use.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CLS-0556] |
| Codified citation | FAR 16.206-1 [@CL-CLS-0556] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CLS-0556] |
| Applies to | Research and development contracts estimated at the simplified acquisition threshold or less [@CL-CLS-0557] |

A fixed-ceiling-price contract with retroactive price redetermination provides for a fixed ceiling price and retroactive price redetermination within the ceiling after completion of the contract [@CL-CLS-0556].

The contract type is appropriate for research and development contracts estimated at the simplified acquisition threshold or less when it is established at the outset that a fair and reasonable firm fixed price cannot be negotiated [@CL-CLS-0557]. The same sentence adds, as a second condition, that it be established at the outset that the amount involved and short performance period make the use of any other fixed-price contract type impracticable [@CL-CLS-0676].

## How it works

A ceiling price shall be negotiated for the contract at a level that reflects a reasonable sharing of risk by the contractor [@CL-CLS-0558]. The established ceiling price may be adjusted only if required by the operation of contract clauses providing for equitable adjustment or other revision of the contract price under stated circumstances [@CL-CLS-0677]. The contract should be awarded only after negotiation of a billing price that is as fair and reasonable as the circumstances permit [@CL-CLS-0559].

This contract type provides the contractor no cost control incentive except the ceiling price [@CL-CLS-0679]. The contracting officer should therefore make clear to the contractor during discussion before award that the contractor's management effectiveness and ingenuity will be considered in retroactively redetermining the price [@CL-CLS-0678].

This contract type shall not be used unless, among other conditions, the contract is for research and development and the estimated cost is the simplified acquisition threshold or less and the contractor's accounting system is adequate for price redetermination [@CL-CLS-0560]. The same list also names reasonable assurance that the price redetermination will take place promptly at the specified time, and approval of its use in writing by the head of the contracting activity (or a higher-level official, if required by agency procedures) [@CL-CLS-0561].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor performs a research and development study under a fixed-ceiling-price contract with retroactive price redetermination. The billing price is 180,000 and the ceiling price is 200,000. After completion the parties redetermine the final price at 190,000. The answer: the final price of 190,000 is within the 200,000 ceiling, so 10,000 is owed above the amounts billed at the billing price.

## Never confuse

Retroactive redetermination is not cost reimbursement. The final price is redetermined within a ceiling after completion, with management effectiveness and ingenuity considered, whereas a cost-reimbursement contract provides for payment of allowable incurred costs to the extent prescribed in the contract [@CL-CLS-0556; @CL-CLS-0678; @CL-CLS-0567]. For the prospective type, see [Fixed-price contracts with prospective price redetermination](cls-fixed-price-prospective-price-redetermination.md). For cost-reimbursement contracts, see [Cost-reimbursement contracts](cls-cost-reimbursement-contracts.md). For payment for supplies, see [Payments and responsibility for supplies](cls-payments-and-responsibility-for-supplies.md).
