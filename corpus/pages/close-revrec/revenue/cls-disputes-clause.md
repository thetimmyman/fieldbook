## Purpose

Shows how the disputes clause defines a claim, when a claim must be certified and what the certification says, how the Contracting Officer decides, what interest runs, and the duty to keep performing.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CLS-1102] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.233-1 [@CL-CLS-1102] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CLS-1102] |
| Applies to | Contracts containing the Disputes clause [@CL-CLS-1102] |

In the disputes clause, a claim means a written demand or written assertion by one of the contracting parties seeking, as a matter of right, the payment of money in a sum certain, the adjustment or interpretation of contract terms, or other relief arising under or relating to the contract [@CL-CLS-1102]. However, a written demand or written assertion by the Contractor seeking the payment of money exceeding $100,000 is not a claim under chapter 71 of title 41 of the United States Code until certified [@CL-CLS-1142]. A voucher, invoice, or other routine request for payment that is not in dispute when submitted is not a claim under that chapter [@CL-CLS-1143]. A claim by the Contractor shall be made in writing and, unless otherwise stated in the contract, submitted to the Contracting Officer for a written decision within 6 years after accrual of the claim [@CL-CLS-1106].

The Contractor shall provide the specified certification when submitting any claim exceeding $100,000 [@CL-CLS-1103]. The certification shall state, among other things, that the claim is made in good faith and that the supporting data are accurate and complete to the best of the certifier's knowledge and belief [@CL-CLS-1104]. The certification text also states that the amount requested accurately reflects the contract adjustment for which the Contractor believes the Government is liable, and that the certifier is authorized to certify the claim on behalf of the Contractor [@CL-CLS-1105].

For Contractor-certified claims over $100,000, the Contracting Officer must, within 60 days, decide the claim or notify the Contractor of the date by which the decision will be made [@CL-CLS-1107]. The Contracting Officer's decision shall be final unless the Contractor appeals or files a suit as provided in the Contract Disputes statute [@CL-CLS-1108]. For Contractor claims of $100,000 or less, the Contracting Officer must, if requested in writing by the Contractor, render a decision within 60 days of the request [@CL-CLS-1141].

The Government shall pay interest on the amount found due and unpaid from the date that the Contracting Officer receives the claim (certified, if required), or from the date that payment otherwise would be due if that date is later, until the date of payment [@CL-CLS-1109].

Under the basic clause, the Contractor shall proceed diligently with performance of the contract, pending final resolution of any request for relief, claim, appeal, or action arising under the contract, and comply with any decision of the Contracting Officer [@CL-CLS-1110].

## How it works

A written demand or assertion of this kind is a claim, although a Contractor demand for payment over the stated amount is not a claim until certified [@CL-CLS-1102; @CL-CLS-1142]. A Contractor claim goes to the Contracting Officer in writing within the stated period, unless the contract states otherwise [@CL-CLS-1106], with the certification when it exceeds the stated amount [@CL-CLS-1103; @CL-CLS-1104; @CL-CLS-1105]. The Contracting Officer decides within the period stated for the size of the claim [@CL-CLS-1107; @CL-CLS-1141], and the decision is final unless appealed or sued on [@CL-CLS-1108]. Interest runs on any amount found due and unpaid [@CL-CLS-1109]. Throughout, the Contractor keeps performing [@CL-CLS-1110].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A Contractor submits a certified written claim for 450,000 after the parties fail to agree on a price adjustment. The Contracting Officer, within 60 days, either decides the claim or sets a date for the decision. The decision finds 300,000 due and unpaid. Interest runs on that amount from the date the claim was received, or from the date payment otherwise would be due if that is later, until payment, and the Contractor keeps performing in the meantime.

## Never confuse

A routine request for payment that is not in dispute is not a claim [@CL-CLS-1143]. Failure to agree to an adjustment is a dispute under this clause in the cost-reimbursement and time-and-materials changes clauses [@CL-CLS-1081; @CL-CLS-1131]. Those clauses are on [Changes clause for cost-reimbursement contracts](cls-changes-cost-reimbursement.md) and [Changes clause for time-and-materials and labor-hour contracts](cls-changes-time-and-materials-labor-hours.md), and the construction version is on [Changes clause for construction contracts](cls-changes-construction.md). The certification applies to a claim over the stated amount [@CL-CLS-1103]. The Contracting Officer's findings on the facts and extent of a delay under the construction default clause are final and conclusive on the parties but subject to appeal under this clause [@CL-CLS-1148], as [Fixed-price construction default and excusable delays](cls-construction-default-and-excusable-delays.md) shows.
