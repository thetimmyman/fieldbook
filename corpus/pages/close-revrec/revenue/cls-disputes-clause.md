## Purpose

Shows how the disputes clause defines a claim, when a claim must be certified and what the certification says, how the Contracting Officer decides, what interest runs, and the duty to keep performing.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CLS-1102] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.233-1 [@CL-CLS-1102] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.233-1 [@CL-CLS-0085] |
| Edition date | MAY 2014 [@CL-CLS-1628] |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CLS-1102] |
| Applies to | Contracts containing the Disputes clause [@CL-CLS-1102] |

In the disputes clause, a claim means a written demand or written assertion by one of the contracting parties seeking, as a matter of right, the payment of money in a sum certain, the adjustment or interpretation of contract terms, or other relief arising under or relating to the contract [@CL-CLS-1102]. However, a written demand or written assertion by the Contractor seeking the payment of money exceeding $100,000 is not a claim under chapter 71 of title 41 of the United States Code until certified [@CL-CLS-1142]. A voucher, invoice, or other routine request for payment that is not in dispute when submitted is not a claim under that chapter [@CL-CLS-1143]. The submission may be converted to a claim under that chapter, by complying with the submission and certification requirements of the clause, if it is disputed either as to liability or amount or is not acted upon in a reasonable time [@CL-CLS-0232]. A claim by the Contractor shall be made in writing and, unless otherwise stated in the contract, submitted to the Contracting Officer for a written decision within 6 years after accrual of the claim [@CL-CLS-1106].

The Contractor shall provide the specified certification when submitting any claim exceeding $100,000 [@CL-CLS-1103]. The certification requirement does not apply to issues in controversy that have not been submitted as all or part of a claim [@CL-CLS-0233]. The certification shall state, among other things, that the claim is made in good faith and that the supporting data are accurate and complete to the best of the certifier's knowledge and belief [@CL-CLS-1104]. The certification text also states that the amount requested accurately reflects the contract adjustment for which the Contractor believes the Government is liable, and that the certifier is authorized to certify the claim on behalf of the Contractor [@CL-CLS-1105].

For Contractor-certified claims over $100,000, the Contracting Officer must, within 60 days, decide the claim or notify the Contractor of the date by which the decision will be made [@CL-CLS-1107]. The Contracting Officer's decision shall be final unless the Contractor appeals or files a suit as provided in the Contract Disputes statute [@CL-CLS-1108]. For Contractor claims of $100,000 or less, the Contracting Officer must, if requested in writing by the Contractor, render a decision within 60 days of the request [@CL-CLS-1141].

The Government shall pay interest on the amount found due and unpaid from the date that the Contracting Officer receives the claim (certified, if required), or from the date that payment otherwise would be due if that date is later, until the date of payment [@CL-CLS-1109]. With regard to claims having defective certifications, as defined in 48 CFR 33.201, interest shall be paid from the date that the Contracting Officer initially receives the claim [@CL-CLS-0234].

Under the basic clause, the Contractor shall proceed diligently with performance of the contract, pending final resolution of any request for relief, claim, appeal, or action arising under the contract, and comply with any decision of the Contracting Officer [@CL-CLS-1110].

## How it works

A written demand or assertion of this kind is a claim, although a Contractor demand for payment over the stated amount is not a claim until certified [@CL-CLS-1102; @CL-CLS-1142]. A Contractor claim goes to the Contracting Officer in writing within the stated period, unless the contract states otherwise [@CL-CLS-1106], with the certification when it exceeds the stated amount [@CL-CLS-1103; @CL-CLS-1104; @CL-CLS-1105]. For a Contractor-certified claim over the stated amount the Contracting Officer must, within 60 days, decide the claim or notify the Contractor of the date by which the decision will be made [@CL-CLS-1107]; for a Contractor claim of that amount or less, the Contracting Officer must, if requested in writing by the Contractor, render a decision within 60 days of the request [@CL-CLS-1141]. The Contracting Officer's decision is final unless the Contractor appeals or files suit [@CL-CLS-1108]. Interest runs on any amount found due and unpaid [@CL-CLS-1109]. Under the basic clause, the Contractor keeps performing, pending final resolution of any request for relief, claim, appeal, or action arising under the contract, and complies with any decision of the Contracting Officer [@CL-CLS-1110].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers must use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified FAR part 52 text, as Class Deviation 2026-O0038 directs, effective February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. RFO FAR 52.233-1 keeps the claim definition, which is now in paragraph (a), including that a written demand or written assertion by the Contractor seeking the payment of money exceeding $100,000 is not a claim under chapter 71 of title 41 of the United States Code until certified [@CL-CLS-0085; @CL-CLS-0086; @CL-CLS-1102; @CL-CLS-1142], and that a voucher, invoice, or other routine request for payment that is not in dispute when submitted is not a claim under that chapter [@CL-CLS-0087; @CL-CLS-1143]. It keeps the rule that a claim by the Contractor must be made in writing and, unless otherwise stated in the contract, submitted to the Contracting Officer for a written decision within 6 years after accrual of the claim [@CL-CLS-0088; @CL-CLS-1106]. It keeps the certification of any claim exceeding $100,000, with the same certification words [@CL-CLS-0089; @CL-CLS-0090; @CL-CLS-0091; @CL-CLS-1103; @CL-CLS-1104; @CL-CLS-1105]. It keeps both 60-day periods: for Contractor claims of $100,000 or less, a decision within 60 days of the request if requested in writing, and for Contractor-certified claims over $100,000, a decision or notice of the decision date within 60 days [@CL-CLS-0092; @CL-CLS-0093; @CL-CLS-1141; @CL-CLS-1107]. It keeps the rule that the Contracting Officer's decision is final unless the Contractor appeals or files a suit as provided in chapter 71 of title 41 of the United States Code [@CL-CLS-0094; @CL-CLS-1108]. It keeps the interest on the amount found due and unpaid from the date the Contracting Officer receives the claim (certified, if required), or from the date payment otherwise would be due if that date is later, until the date of payment [@CL-CLS-0095; @CL-CLS-1109]. Under the basic clause it keeps the Contractor's duty to proceed diligently with performance, pending final resolution of any request for relief, claim, appeal, or action arising under the contract, and to comply with any decision of the Contracting Officer [@CL-CLS-0096; @CL-CLS-1110].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A Contractor submits a certified written claim for 450,000 after the parties fail to agree on a price adjustment. The Contracting Officer, within 60 days, either decides the claim or sets a date for the decision. The decision finds 300,000 due and unpaid. Interest runs on that amount from the date the claim was received, or from the date payment otherwise would be due if that is later, until payment, and the Contractor keeps performing in the meantime.

## Never confuse

A voucher, invoice, or other routine request for payment that is not in dispute when submitted is not a claim under that chapter, but the submission may be converted to a claim if it is disputed either as to liability or amount or is not acted upon in a reasonable time [@CL-CLS-1143; @CL-CLS-0232]. Failure to agree to an adjustment is a dispute under this clause in the cost-reimbursement and time-and-materials changes clauses [@CL-CLS-1081; @CL-CLS-1131]. Those clauses are on [Changes clause for cost-reimbursement contracts](cls-changes-cost-reimbursement.md) and [Changes clause for time-and-materials and labor-hour contracts](cls-changes-time-and-materials-labor-hours.md), and the construction version is on [Changes clause for construction contracts](cls-changes-construction.md). The certification is provided when submitting any claim exceeding the stated amount, and does not apply to issues in controversy that have not been submitted as all or part of a claim [@CL-CLS-1103; @CL-CLS-0233]. The Contracting Officer's findings of fact under the construction default clause are final and conclusive on the parties but subject to appeal under this clause [@CL-CLS-1148], as [Fixed-price construction default and excusable delays](cls-construction-default-and-excusable-delays.md) shows.

Paragraph letters differ between the texts: the codified clause defines a claim in paragraph (c) [@CL-CLS-1102], and the RFO clause defines it in paragraph (a) [@CL-CLS-0085].
