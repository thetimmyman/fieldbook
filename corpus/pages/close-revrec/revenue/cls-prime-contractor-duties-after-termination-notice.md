## Purpose

Lists the duties that the notice and termination clause for a convenience termination generally require of a prime contractor after it receives a notice of termination.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CLS-0907] |
| Codified citation | FAR 49.104 [@CL-CLS-0907] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CLS-0907] |
| Applies to | Contractors that receive a notice of termination, with the convenience-termination duties listed under the notice and clause [@CL-CLS-0907] |

After receipt of the notice of termination, the contractor shall comply with the notice and the termination clause of the contract, except as otherwise directed by the TCO [@CL-CLS-0907]. The notice and clause applicable to convenience terminations generally require the duties listed next [@CL-CLS-0907].

The listed duties include that the contractor stops work immediately on the terminated portion and stops placing subcontracts under it, terminates all subcontracts related to the terminated portion of the prime contract, and immediately advises the TCO of any special circumstances precluding the stoppage of work [@CL-CLS-0908]. If the termination is partial, a listed duty is to perform the continued portion and promptly submit any request for an equitable adjustment of price for the continued portion, supported by evidence of any increase in the cost [@CL-CLS-0909]. Another listed duty is to take necessary or directed action to protect and preserve property in the contractor's possession in which the Government has or may acquire an interest and, as directed by the TCO, deliver it to the Government [@CL-CLS-0910]. Another is to promptly notify the TCO in writing of any legal proceedings growing out of any subcontract or other commitment related to the terminated portion [@CL-CLS-0911]. Another is to settle outstanding liabilities and proposals arising out of termination of subcontracts, obtaining any approvals or ratifications the TCO requires [@CL-CLS-0912]. The last two listed duties are to promptly submit the contractor's own settlement proposal, supported by appropriate schedules, and to dispose of termination inventory as directed or authorized by the TCO [@CL-CLS-0913].

## How it works

Stopping work on the terminated portion and terminating related subcontracts are the first listed duties [@CL-CLS-0908]. The listed duties also include settling outstanding liabilities and proposals arising out of termination of subcontracts, obtaining any approvals or ratifications required by the TCO [@CL-CLS-0912]; promptly submitting the contractor's own settlement proposal, supported by appropriate schedules, and disposing of termination inventory, as directed or authorized by the TCO [@CL-CLS-0913]; and taking necessary or directed action to protect and preserve property in the contractor's possession in which the Government has or may acquire an interest [@CL-CLS-0910]. If the termination is partial, the listed duties include performing the continued portion and promptly submitting any request for an equitable adjustment of price for the continued portion [@CL-CLS-0909]. For how the TCO handles the contractor's settlement proposal and subcontract settlements, see [Subcontract settlements: authority, judgments and arbitration awards](cls-subcontract-settlements-authorization-and-awards.md) and [Audit referral of termination settlement proposals and subcontract settlements](cls-termination-settlement-audit-referral.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A fixed-price contract is terminated in part for convenience. The contractor stops work on the terminated units on receipt of the notice, terminates the matching purchase orders, and tells the TCO that one line is mid-process and cannot be stopped at once. It keeps building the continued units and requests a price adjustment of 30,000 for the continued portion, with cost evidence.

It settles two supplier terminations, with the approvals the TCO requires, and then submits its own settlement proposal with schedules. Answer: the subcontract settlements, the proposal and the inventory disposal drive the close; the price adjustment request is a separate amount for the continued work.

## Never confuse

The contractor's duty is to comply with the notice and the termination clause except as otherwise directed by the TCO, and the listed duties are those the notice and clause generally require [@CL-CLS-0907]. For the settlement proposal and termination inventory, see [Termination settlement proposals: inventory basis and total-cost basis](../inventory-wip/cls-termination-settlement-proposal-bases.md) and [Termination inventory: disposal schedules and government property accounting](../inventory-wip/cls-termination-inventory-schedules-and-property-accounting.md). The duty to stop work immediately applies to the terminated portion of the contract [@CL-CLS-0908], and if the termination is partial the contractor shall perform the continued portion [@CL-CLS-0909]. For the equitable adjustment and partial settlements, see [Partial termination: equitable adjustment and partial settlements](cls-partial-termination-equitable-adjustment-and-settlements.md). For the TCO's duties after the notice, see [Termination authority, the notice of termination and the TCO's duties](cls-termination-authority-notice-and-tco-duties.md). For payments on the settlement proposal, see [Partial payments and final payment under a terminated contract](cls-termination-partial-payments-and-final-payment.md).
