## Purpose

Shows which cost rules apply to price adjustments under the modification pricing clause, what a superseding contract replaces, and what access the Government gets to on-base facilities.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-CLS-1165; @CL-CLS-1166; @CL-CLS-1168] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 252.243-7001, 252.241-7000 and 252.241-7001 [@CL-CLS-1165; @CL-CLS-1166; @CL-CLS-1168] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CLS-1165; @CL-CLS-1166; @CL-CLS-1168] |
| Applies to | Contracts with any of the three clauses, each prescribed at its own DFARS section [@CL-CLS-1165; @CL-CLS-1166; @CL-CLS-1168] |

Under the pricing clause, when costs are a factor in any price adjustment under the contract, the cost principles and procedures in FAR part 31 and DFARS part 231 in effect on the date of the contract apply [@CL-CLS-1165].

The superseding contract clause provides that the contract supersedes an earlier contract, identified by number and date, which provided similar services [@CL-CLS-1166]. Any capital credits accrued to the Government, any remaining credits due to the Government under the connection charge, or any termination liability are transferred to the superseding contract [@CL-CLS-1167]. The clause provides for listing the accrued capital credits by year and delivery point, and the outstanding connection charge credits by month and year with the remaining amount due the Government [@CL-CLS-1216]. It also provides for listing termination liability charges by month and year, showing the monthly facility cost recovered and the remaining facility cost to be recovered [@CL-CLS-1217]. Under the Government access clause, authorized representatives of the Government may have access to the Contractor's on-base facilities upon reasonable notice or in case of emergency [@CL-CLS-1168].

## How it works

The pricing clause names which cost rules apply to a price adjustment, those in effect on the date of the contract [@CL-CLS-1165]. The superseding contract clause moves credits and liability from the earlier contract to this one, in the schedules the clause provides for [@CL-CLS-1167; @CL-CLS-1216; @CL-CLS-1217]. The access clause ties access to reasonable notice or an emergency [@CL-CLS-1168].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contract is signed in a year when a cost principle allows a certain cost. Later the rule changes, and a modification adds work. The contractor prices the added cost under the cost principles and procedures in effect on the date of the contract. For the superseding contract clause, a new service contract replaces an earlier one, and 20,000 of accrued capital credits, listed by year and delivery point in the new contract, move to it.

## Never confuse

The pricing clause selects the cost rules for a price adjustment, and it does not say how a change is accounted or when an adjustment is owed. Change accounting is on [Change Order Accounting clause](cls-change-order-accounting.md), equitable adjustment under the Changes clause is on [Changes, Fixed-Price clause and equitable adjustment](../../contract-finance/funding/ctf-changes-fixed-price-equitable-adjustment.md), requests for equitable adjustment in DoD definitization are on [DoD contract definitization and requests for equitable adjustment](../../contract-finance/financing/ctf-dod-definitization-and-requests-for-equitable-adjustment.md), and the cost rules themselves are on [Cost principles for contracts with commercial organizations](../../compliance/cas-allowability/cmp-cost-principles-commercial-organizations.md) and [The FAR System and agency supplements](../../compliance/regulatory-framework/cmp-far-system-and-agency-supplements.md). The access clause concerns on-base facilities of the Contractor, and it is not a pricing or credit rule [@CL-CLS-1168].
