## Purpose

Shows how a registrant presents net sales and revenues by type, the costs applicable to them, and selling, general and administrative expenses on the face of the statement of comprehensive income, including the combination rule for small classes and the related-party disclosure. This article and other articles of Regulation S-X provide clarification of certain disclosures that must be included in any event in financial statements filed with the Commission [@CL-CLS-0850].

| Applies to | The statement of comprehensive income filed with the Commission [@CL-CLS-0850; @CL-CLS-0839]. For the combination rule, where income is derived from more than one of the subcaptions of the net sales and revenues caption [@CL-CLS-0837] |

The net sales and gross revenues caption requires net sales of tangible products (gross sales less discounts, returns and allowances), operating revenues of public utilities or others, income from rentals, revenues from services and other revenues to be stated separately [@CL-CLS-0839]. The costs and expenses applicable to sales and revenues caption requires the amount of cost of tangible goods sold, operating expenses of public utilities or others, expenses applicable to rental income, cost of services and expenses applicable to other revenues to be stated separately [@CL-CLS-0840]. Amounts earned from transactions with related parties shall be disclosed as required under section 210.4-08(k) [@CL-CLS-0841].

If income is derived from more than one of the subcaptions described under section 210.5-03.1, each class that is not more than 10 percent of the sum of the items may be combined with another class [@CL-CLS-0837]. If these items are combined, related costs and expenses as described under section 210.5-03.2 shall be combined in the same manner [@CL-CLS-0838]. The caption list includes selling, general and administrative expenses (caption 4), provision for doubtful accounts and notes (caption 5), and other general expenses (caption 6), which includes items not normally included in caption 4 and requires any material item to be stated separately [@CL-CLS-0842].

## How it works

The registrant states revenue separately by listed subcaption, with the matching costs and expenses stated separately by listed subcaption [@CL-CLS-0839; @CL-CLS-0840]. A class that is not more than 10 percent of the sum of the items may be combined with another class, and if those items are combined, the related costs and expenses shall be combined in the same manner [@CL-CLS-0837; @CL-CLS-0838]. Selling, general and administrative expenses sit in their own caption, apart from other general expenses [@CL-CLS-0842]. Amounts earned from transactions with related parties shall be disclosed as required under section 210.4-08(k) [@CL-CLS-0841].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A registrant earns 900 from product sales, 80 from services and 20 from rentals, a total of 1,000. Rentals are 2 percent and services are 8 percent of the total. Each is not more than 10 percent, so either may be combined with another class. If rentals are combined into services, the rental expenses are combined with the cost of services in the same way.

## Never confuse

The 10 percent test compares each class of income with the sum of the items under section 210.5-03.1, whereas under the materiality rule an item whose amount is not material need not be separately set forth and the combination of insignificant amounts is permitted [@CL-CLS-0837; @CL-CLS-0816]. For that rule, see [Form, GAAP presumption and materiality under Regulation S-X](../group-bridge/cls-sx-form-gaap-materiality.md). Combining classes on the revenue side requires combining the related costs and expenses in the same manner [@CL-CLS-0838]. For how management explains movement in these lines, see [MD&A results of operations](../../fpa/kpi/fpa-mdna-results-of-operations.md), and for segment information see [Business description by segment](../../fpa/kpi/fpa-business-description-by-segment.md).
