## Purpose

Explains what the Payments clause pays for, namely supplies delivered and accepted and services rendered and accepted, and how title and risk of loss pass between the parties under the Responsibility for Supplies clause.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CLS-0200] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.232-1 [@CL-CLS-0200] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CLS-0200] |
| Applies to | Fixed-price supply contracts, fixed-price service contracts and contracts for nonregulated communication services [@CL-CLS-0300] |

Under the Payments clause, the Government pays the contractor the stipulated contract prices for delivered and accepted supplies or rendered and accepted services upon submission of proper invoices or vouchers, less any deductions provided in the contract [@CL-CLS-0200].

Title to supplies furnished under the contract passes to the Government upon formal acceptance, regardless of when or where physical possession occurs, unless the contract specifically provides for earlier passage of title [@CL-CLS-0201].

## How it works

The clause pays for supplies delivered and accepted or services rendered and accepted, on submission of proper invoices or vouchers [@CL-CLS-0200]. The clause is inserted appropriately modified with respect to payment due date in accordance with agency regulations [@CL-CLS-0300]. Payment timing under the Prompt Payment clause is covered in [Prompt payment](../../contract-finance/billing-payment/ctf-prompt-payment.md).

Unless the contract provides otherwise, risk of loss stays with the contractor until it passes to the Government on delivery to a carrier when transportation is f.o.b. origin [@CL-CLS-0301]. When transportation is f.o.b. destination, it passes on acceptance or delivery at the destination, whichever is later [@CL-CLS-0302]. Supplies that fail to conform so as to give a right of rejection stay at the contractor's risk until cure or acceptance [@CL-CLS-0202]. Under the risk-of-loss paragraph, the contractor is not liable for loss or damage caused by the negligence of Government personnel acting within the scope of their employment [@CL-CLS-0203].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor delivers a batch of widgets priced at 100,000 under a firm-fixed-price supply contract with transportation f.o.b. destination. The widgets arrive on Day 10 and the Government formally accepts them on Day 14. The contractor submits a proper invoice for 100,000 after acceptance. The answer: title passes on formal acceptance on Day 14, and risk of loss passes on the later of acceptance and delivery at the destination, which is also Day 14.

## Never confuse

Physical receipt is not formal acceptance. Title does not pass upon physical delivery to a Government site until formal acceptance occurs, unless the contract specifically provides for earlier passage of title [@CL-CLS-0201].

Title and risk of loss are separate. With f.o.b. origin shipment, risk can pass on delivery to a carrier, before formal acceptance, while title passes on formal acceptance unless the contract provides for earlier passage [@CL-CLS-0201; @CL-CLS-0301].
