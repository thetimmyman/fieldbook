## Purpose

Controls the deductions taken from a termination settlement, the separate invoicing and pricing of completed end items, and the contract price ceiling on the settlement amount.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CLS-0517] |
| Codified citation | FAR 49.204 [@CL-CLS-0517] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CLS-0517] |
| Applies to | Amounts payable to the contractor under a settlement [@CL-CLS-0517] |

From the amount payable to the contractor under a settlement, the termination contracting officer must deduct the agreed price for any part of the termination inventory purchased or retained by the contractor, and the proceeds from any materials sold that have not been paid or credited to the Government [@CL-CLS-0517].

The total amount payable to the contractor for a settlement, before deducting disposal or other credits and exclusive of settlement costs, must not exceed the contract price less payments otherwise made or to be made under the contract [@CL-CLS-0520].

## How it works

A further deduction is the fair value, as determined by the termination contracting officer, of termination inventory that is lost or so damaged as to become undeliverable before transfer of title to the Government or to a buyer [@CL-CLS-0656]. Normal spoilage is excepted, as is inventory for which the Government has expressly assumed the risk of loss [@CL-CLS-0657].

Promptly after the effective date of termination, the termination contracting officer must have all undelivered completed end items inspected and accepted if they comply with the contract requirements, and must determine which accepted end items are to be delivered under the contract [@CL-CLS-0658]. The contractor invoices accepted and delivered end items at the contract price in the usual manner and does not include them in the settlement proposal [@CL-CLS-0518]. When completed end items, though accepted, are not to be delivered under the contract, the contractor includes them in the settlement proposal at the contract price, adjusted for any saving of freight or other charges, together with any credits for their purchase, retention, or sale [@CL-CLS-0519].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A fixed-price contract has a total price of 1,000,000. Before termination the contractor was paid 300,000 for delivered items. It claims 750,000, made up of 700,000 for termination work and 50,000 of settlement costs. The limit applies before disposal credits and excludes settlement costs, so the test is 1,000,000 less 300,000, or 700,000. The answer: the 700,000 of work is at the limit and passes, and the 50,000 of settlement costs sits outside the limit.

## Never confuse

An accepted and delivered end item is invoiced in the usual manner and left out of the settlement proposal, whereas an accepted end item that is not to be delivered goes into the proposal at the contract price [@CL-CLS-0518; @CL-CLS-0519]. The price limit on the settlement is measured before disposal or other credits and excludes settlement costs [@CL-CLS-0520].
