## Purpose

Names each default clause for fixed-price contracts and the contract type and amount that trigger its use.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CLS-1024] |
| Codified citation | FAR 49.504 [@CL-CLS-1024] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CLS-1024] |
| Applies to | Fixed-price contracts, by type: supplies and services, research and development, and construction [@CL-CLS-1024; @CL-CLS-1026; @CL-CLS-1027]. |

For supplies and services, the contracting officer shall insert the clause at 52.249-8 when a fixed-price contract is contemplated and the contract amount is expected to exceed the simplified acquisition threshold [@CL-CLS-1024]. In that paragraph, the contracting officer may use the clause at or below the threshold if appropriate, for example if the acquisition involves items with a history of unsatisfactory quality [@CL-CLS-1025]. If the contract is for transportation or transportation-related services, the contracting officer shall use the clause with its Alternate I [@CL-CLS-1060].

For research and development, the clause at 52.249-9 goes in when a fixed-price contract is contemplated and the contract amount is expected to exceed the simplified acquisition threshold, except those with educational or nonprofit institutions on a no-profit basis [@CL-CLS-1026; @CL-CLS-1059].

For construction, the clause at 52.249-10 goes in when a fixed-price contract is contemplated and the contract amount is expected to exceed the simplified acquisition threshold [@CL-CLS-1027]. If the contract is for dismantling, demolition, or removal of improvements, the contracting officer shall use that clause with its Alternate I [@CL-CLS-1061]. If the contract is to be awarded during a period of national emergency, the contracting officer may use it with one of two alternatives: its Alternate II when a fixed-price contract for construction is contemplated [@CL-CLS-1062], or its Alternate III when a contract for dismantling, demolition, or removal of improvements is contemplated [@CL-CLS-1063; @CL-CLS-1599].

## How it works

Each clause is tied to the kind of work and to the contract amount expected at award [@CL-CLS-1024; @CL-CLS-1026; @CL-CLS-1027]. For research and development, the clause at 52.249-9 is not inserted in those with educational or nonprofit institutions on a no-profit basis [@CL-CLS-1059]. The effects of a default termination are on [Termination for default: effect, notices and repurchase against the contractor](cls-termination-for-default-effect-and-repurchase.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

Assume an illustrative threshold of 250,000. A fixed-price construction contract expected at 900,000 carries the construction default clause. A fixed-price supply order expected at 80,000 is below that threshold, so it need not carry the supplies and services default clause, but the contracting officer may include it if appropriate, for example when the items have a history of unsatisfactory quality.

## Never confuse

The effects of a termination for default are on [Termination for default: effect, notices and repurchase against the contractor](cls-termination-for-default-effect-and-repurchase.md), the convenience clauses are on [Termination for convenience clauses and when each goes in](cls-termination-for-convenience-clauses.md), and a convenience termination is settled as shown on [Termination for Convenience of the Government (Fixed-Price): settlement](../../contract-finance/billing-payment/ctf-termination-for-convenience-settlement.md). Where a surety completes a defaulted construction contract, a separate takeover agreement may apply, and [Surety-takeover agreements](cls-surety-takeover-agreements.md) covers it. The notices that precede a default termination are on [Cost-reimbursement settlement format, subcontract settlement authorization and delinquency notices](cls-cost-reimbursement-settlement-format-and-delinquency.md).
