## Purpose

Sets out the notice procedures that departments and agencies are responsible for establishing, and the notice that a prime contractor is responsible for passing down, when funding levels in an appropriations act terminate or substantially reduce contracts under major defense programs.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-CLS-0295] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 249.7003 [@CL-CLS-0295] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CLS-0295] |
| Applies to | Contracts under major defense programs that funding levels in an appropriations act terminate or substantially reduce [@CL-CLS-0295] |

Paragraph (a) names section 1372 of the National Defense Authorization Act for Fiscal Year 1994 and section 824 of the National Defense Authorization Act for Fiscal Year 1997 [@CL-CLS-0428]. They are intended to help establish benefit eligibility under the Workforce Innovation and Opportunity Act for employees of DoD contractors and subcontractors adversely affected by termination or substantial reductions in major defense programs [@CL-CLS-0294].

Departments and agencies are responsible for establishing procedures to identify which contracts, if any, under major defense programs will be terminated or substantially reduced as a result of the funding levels provided in an appropriations act [@CL-CLS-0295]. They are also responsible for a procedure to provide notice of the anticipated termination or substantial reduction in the funding of affected contracts within 60 days of the enactment of the act [@CL-CLS-0296]. The notice goes directly to the Secretary of Labor and, through the contracting officer, to each prime contractor [@CL-CLS-0296].

When subcontracts have been issued, the prime contractor is responsible for providing notice of the termination or substantial reduction in funding to all first-tier subcontractors with a subcontract valued equal to or greater than $700,000 [@CL-CLS-0297].

The prime contractor is also responsible for requiring that each subcontractor provide such notice to each of its subcontractors for subcontracts valued greater than $150,000 [@CL-CLS-0298]. It is responsible for requiring that each subcontractor impose a similar notice and flowdown requirement in subcontracts valued greater than $150,000 at all tiers [@CL-CLS-0298].

## How it works

| Party | Duty | Deadline or threshold |
|---|---|---|
| Departments and agencies | Establish procedures to identify the affected contracts [@CL-CLS-0295] | Contracts under major defense programs affected by the funding levels in an appropriations act [@CL-CLS-0295] |
| Departments and agencies | Establish a procedure to provide notice directly to the Secretary of Labor and through the contracting officer to each prime contractor [@CL-CLS-0296] | Within 60 days of enactment [@CL-CLS-0296] |
| Contracting officer | The notice to each prime contractor goes through the contracting officer [@CL-CLS-0296] | Within 60 days of enactment [@CL-CLS-0296] |
| Prime contractor | Notify all first-tier subcontractors [@CL-CLS-0297] | Subcontract valued equal to or greater than $700,000 [@CL-CLS-0297] |
| Prime contractor | Require each subcontractor to notify its subcontractors, and to impose a similar notice and flowdown requirement at all tiers [@CL-CLS-0298] | Subcontracts valued greater than $150,000 [@CL-CLS-0298] |

The duty starts with the funding act. Departments and agencies are responsible for establishing procedures to identify affected contracts from the funding levels the act provides [@CL-CLS-0295].

The notice follows on two routes. One runs directly to the Secretary of Labor. The other runs through the contracting officer to each prime contractor [@CL-CLS-0296]. The notice to the Secretary of Labor is the one that goes directly [@CL-CLS-0296].

The two subcontract tests use different words. The first-tier test is "equal to or greater than" $700,000, and the later tests are "greater than" $150,000 [@CL-CLS-0297; @CL-CLS-0298]. A first-tier subcontract of exactly $700,000 meets the first test [@CL-CLS-0297]. A subcontract of exactly $150,000 does not meet the second [@CL-CLS-0298].

The $700,000 test applies to first-tier subcontracts [@CL-CLS-0297]. The $150,000 tests apply to subcontracts that a subcontractor places with its own subcontractors and to subcontracts at all tiers [@CL-CLS-0298].

The contract clause that carries the contractor's own notice steps is covered on [Notification of anticipated contract termination or reduction (DFARS)](cls-dfars-anticipated-termination-notification.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An appropriations act is enacted on Day 0. Its funding levels terminate one contract and substantially reduce another, both under a major defense program. The department's procedure identifies both contracts [@CL-CLS-0295]. It provides notice directly to the Secretary of Labor and, through the contracting officer, to each prime contractor, and it does so by Day 60 [@CL-CLS-0296].

One prime contractor has three first-tier subcontracts valued at 800,000, 700,000 and 600,000. It is responsible for notifying the first two, because each is equal to or greater than 700,000 [@CL-CLS-0297]. The 600,000 subcontract is below that first-tier test, although the requirement in paragraph (c)(2) that each subcontractor notify its own subcontractors valued above 150,000 still refers to each subcontractor [@CL-CLS-0298].

A subcontractor places two subcontracts of its own, valued at 160,000 and 150,000. The prime contractor is responsible for requiring the subcontractor to provide notice for the 160,000 subcontract, which is greater than 150,000 [@CL-CLS-0298]. The 150,000 subcontract does not meet that test, which needs a value greater than 150,000 [@CL-CLS-0298].

## Never confuse

The notice provided under the section concerns the anticipated termination of, or substantial reduction in the funding of, affected contracts [@CL-CLS-0296]. The notice of termination that starts the settlement clock is on [Prime contractor duties after a termination notice](cls-prime-contractor-duties-after-termination-notice.md).

The responsibility to establish the procedure for notice within 60 days sits with departments and agencies, and the subcontractor notice responsibilities sit with the prime contractor [@CL-CLS-0296; @CL-CLS-0297].

The prime contractor is responsible for requiring that each subcontractor impose a similar notice and flowdown requirement in subcontracts valued greater than $150,000 at all tiers [@CL-CLS-0298].
