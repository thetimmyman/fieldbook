## Purpose

Shows why a capital plan for a Government contract starts from the contractor's own assets, and what must be clearly demonstrated before the Government supplies property instead.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-IND-0001; @CL-IND-0007] |
| Codified citation | FAR 45.102 and FAR 45.103 [@CL-IND-0001; @CL-IND-0007] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-IND-0001; @CL-IND-0007] |
| Applies to | Property needed to perform Government contracts [@CL-IND-0001] |

Contractors are ordinarily required to furnish all property necessary to perform Government contracts [@CL-IND-0001]. A contractor's inability or unwillingness to supply its own resources is not sufficient reason for the furnishing or acquisition of property [@CL-IND-0005].

Except for property provided under contracts for repair, maintenance, overhaul, or modification, which is not subject to the requirements of paragraph (b) of FAR 45.102 [@CL-IND-0006], contracting officers shall provide property to contractors only when all four conditions in paragraph (b) are clearly demonstrated [@CL-IND-0002; @CL-IND-0003; @CL-IND-0004]: providing the property is in the Government's best interest [@CL-IND-0002]; the overall benefit to the acquisition significantly outweighs the increased cost of administration, including ultimate property disposal [@CL-IND-0003]; providing the property does not substantially increase the Government's assumption of risk; and Government requirements cannot otherwise be met [@CL-IND-0004].

## How it works

The contractor's inability or unwillingness to supply its own resources is not sufficient reason for the furnishing or acquisition of property [@CL-IND-0005]. Property provided under contracts for repair, maintenance, overhaul, or modification is not subject to the requirements of paragraph (b) of FAR 45.102 [@CL-IND-0006; @CL-IND-0002]. Under FAR 45.103(a), agencies shall allow and encourage contractors to use voluntary consensus standards and industry-leading practices and standards to manage Government property in their possession [@CL-IND-0007], eliminate to the maximum practical extent any competitive advantage a prospective contractor may have by using Government property [@CL-IND-0008], ensure maximum practical reutilization of contractor inventory for government purposes, and require contractors to use Government property already in their possession to the maximum extent practical in performing Government contracts, among other listed duties [@CL-IND-0009]. For the clause that governs property once it is furnished, see [Government Property clause](../../close-revrec/fixed-assets/cls-government-property-clause.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor bids a production line that needs a machine costing 2 million. It says it would rather not buy the machine and asks the Government to supply one. The contracting officer asks whether each of the conditions is clearly demonstrated, and the contractor's preference is not a reason on its own. The answer: the contractor plans to buy the machine and carries the 2 million in its capital plan.

## Never confuse

This card covers the policy that contractors are ordinarily required to furnish all property necessary to perform Government contracts and the conditions and exception for providing property to contractors [@CL-IND-0001; @CL-IND-0002; @CL-IND-0006], while the [Government Property clause](../../close-revrec/fixed-assets/cls-government-property-clause.md) covers the contract clause. The contractor's inability or unwillingness to supply its own resources is not sufficient reason for the furnishing or acquisition of property [@CL-IND-0005].
