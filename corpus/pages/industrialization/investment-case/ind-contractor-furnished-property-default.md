## Purpose

Shows why a capital plan for a Government contract starts from the contractor's own assets, and what must be clearly demonstrated before the Government supplies property instead.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-IND-0001; @CL-IND-0007] |
| Codified citation | FAR 45.102 and FAR 45.103 [@CL-IND-0001; @CL-IND-0007] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-IND-0001; @CL-IND-0007] |
| Applies to | Property needed to perform Government contracts [@CL-IND-0001] |

Contractors are ordinarily required to furnish all property necessary to perform Government contracts [@CL-IND-0001]. A contractor's inability or unwillingness to supply its own resources is not sufficient reason for the furnishing or acquisition of property [@CL-IND-0005].

Contracting officers shall provide property to contractors only when it is clearly demonstrated, first, that providing it is in the Government's best interest [@CL-IND-0002]. The same paragraph lists further conditions that must be clearly demonstrated: the overall benefit to the acquisition significantly outweighs the increased cost of administration, including ultimate property disposal [@CL-IND-0003], and providing the property does not substantially increase the Government's assumption of risk and Government requirements cannot otherwise be met [@CL-IND-0004].

## How it works

The listed conditions are the gate before any property is provided, and a contractor's inability or unwillingness to supply its own resources does not open it [@CL-IND-0005]. Property provided under contracts for repair, maintenance, overhaul, or modification is not subject to the requirements of paragraph (b) of the same section, which is the paragraph that sets those conditions [@CL-IND-0006; @CL-IND-0002]. The general policy section also lists duties for agencies. Agencies shall allow and encourage contractors to use voluntary consensus standards and industry-leading practices to manage Government property in their possession [@CL-IND-0007]. Among the other duties in the same list, agencies shall eliminate to the maximum practical extent any competitive advantage a prospective contractor may have by using Government property [@CL-IND-0008], ensure maximum practical reutilization of contractor inventory for government purposes, and require contractors to use Government property already in their possession to the maximum extent practical in performing Government contracts [@CL-IND-0009]. For the clause that governs property once it is furnished, see [Government Property clause](../../close-revrec/fixed-assets/cls-government-property-clause.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor bids a production line that needs a machine costing 2 million. It says it would rather not buy the machine and asks the Government to supply one. The contracting officer asks whether each of the conditions is clearly demonstrated, and the contractor's preference is not a reason on its own. The answer: the contractor plans to buy the machine and carries the 2 million in its capital plan.

## Never confuse

The default policy is not the contract clause. This card covers whether the Government provides property at all, while the [Government Property clause](../../close-revrec/fixed-assets/cls-government-property-clause.md) covers the contractor's duties for property it holds. A contractor's inability or unwillingness to supply its own resources is not sufficient reason for the furnishing or acquisition of property [@CL-IND-0005].
