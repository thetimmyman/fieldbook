## Purpose

Explains what the Defense Priorities and Allocations System is for, which programs may be eligible for priorities and allocations support, who may place priority ratings, and what a priority rating and a rated order are. The duties that attach once a rated order arrives are on the [rated orders](ind-dpas-rated-orders.md) card.

15 CFR 700.1 states that the part implements the Defense Priorities and Allocations System, which is administered by the Department of Commerce, Bureau of Industry and Security [@CL-IND-0050]. The system implements the priorities and allocations authority of Title I of the Defense Production Act of 1950, as amended, codified at section 4501 and following of title 50 of the United States Code, delegated to the Department of Commerce under Executive Order 13603, including use of the authority to support emergency preparedness activities under Title VI of the disaster relief and emergency assistance statute codified at section 5121 and following of title 42 of the United States Code, and the priorities authority of section 18 of the Selective Service Act of 1948, codified at section 3816 of title 50 of the United States Code, and related statutes, all with respect to industrial resources [@CL-IND-0051; @CL-IND-0052; @CL-IND-0370]. The system establishes procedures for the placement, acceptance and performance of priority rated contracts and orders, other than contracts of employment, and for the allocation of materials, services and facilities for approved programs [@CL-IND-0342].

15 CFR 700.2 says that certain national defense and energy programs, including military, emergency preparedness, homeland security, and critical infrastructure protection and restoration activities, may be eligible for priorities and allocations support, as determined by a Determination Department [@CL-IND-0053]. The Department of Commerce administers the system and may exercise priorities and allocations authority to ensure the timely delivery of industrial resources to meet approved program requirements [@CL-IND-0054]. The Department has delegated authority to place priority ratings on contracts or orders necessary or appropriate to promote the national defense to certain government agencies that issue such contracts or orders [@CL-IND-0055]. Those delegations include authority to authorize recipients of rated orders to place ratings on contracts or orders to contractors, subcontractors and suppliers [@CL-IND-0056]. Schedule I to the part lists the agencies to which the Department has delegated authority, and the Department of Commerce is also listed as an agency for programs where its authorization is necessary to place rated orders [@CL-IND-0343].

## How it works

A priority rating identifies a rated order and consists of a rating symbol, DO or DX, and a program identification symbol [@CL-IND-0057]. Rated orders take precedence over all unrated orders as necessary to meet required delivery dates [@CL-IND-0058]. The rating symbol indicates the level of priority, and among rated orders, DX rated orders take precedence over DO rated orders [@CL-IND-0058]. The program identification symbol indicates which approved program is attributed to the rated order [@CL-IND-0059].

Persons receiving rated orders must give them preferential treatment as required by the part [@CL-IND-0060]. All rated orders must be scheduled to the extent possible to ensure delivery by the required delivery date [@CL-IND-0060]. Persons who receive rated orders must in turn place rated orders with their suppliers for the industrial resources they need to fill the orders [@CL-IND-0337; @CL-IND-0061]. This provision ensures that suppliers will give priority treatment to rated orders from contractor to subcontractor to suppliers throughout the procurement chain [@CL-IND-0061].

A person may place a priority rating on an order only when it is in receipt of a rated order, has been explicitly authorized to do so by the Department of Commerce or a Delegate Agency, or is otherwise permitted to do so by the part [@CL-IND-0062]. Authority to authorize recipients of rated orders to place ratings is part of the delegations described above [@CL-IND-0056]. What a rated order must contain, and how a supplier accepts, rejects and schedules one, are on the [rated orders](ind-dpas-rated-orders.md) card and the [acceptance, rejection and flowdown](ind-dpas-acceptance-rejection-and-flowdown.md) card. The levels of priority are on the [authority and priority ratings](ind-dpas-priority-ratings-and-authority.md) card, and limits on placing rated orders are on the [limits on rated orders](ind-dpas-rated-order-limits-and-rating-authority.md) card. The terms are on the [definitions](ind-dpas-definitions.md) card.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A fabricator holds an unrated order for 400 housings due on day 30. It then receives a DO rated order for 200 of the same housings due on day 20, and the shop cannot meet both dates unless it builds the rated order first. A rated order takes precedence over an unrated order as necessary to meet required delivery dates, so the shop schedules the rated order first and gives it preferential treatment [@CL-IND-0058; @CL-IND-0060]. It needs steel it does not hold, so it places a rated order on its steel supplier [@CL-IND-0061]. It may rate that order because it is in receipt of a rated order [@CL-IND-0062]. Had the shop held only the unrated order, it could rate its steel order only if Commerce or a Delegate Agency had explicitly authorized it or the part otherwise permitted it [@CL-IND-0062].

## Never confuse

The two symbols are not interchangeable: a priority rating consists of the rating symbol (DO or DX), which indicates the level of priority, and a program identification symbol, which indicates which approved program is attributed to the rated order [@CL-IND-0057; @CL-IND-0058; @CL-IND-0059]. A person may place a priority rating on an order only when it is in receipt of a rated order, has been explicitly authorized to do so by the Department of Commerce or a Delegate Agency, or is otherwise permitted to do so by the part [@CL-IND-0062].
