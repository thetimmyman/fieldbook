## Purpose

Shows when a person may ask Commerce for relief from the Defense Priorities and Allocations System regulation, and the steps for an appeal when the request is denied.

## The rule

| Applies to | The part and official actions, except Directive Allocation Orders issued by a temporary final rule [@CL-IND-0198] |

The adjustments or exceptions process applies to the part and to official actions issued under it, except for Directive Allocation Orders issued by a temporary final rule [@CL-IND-0198]. A person may submit a request to the Office of Strategic Industries and Economic Security of the Department of Commerce for an adjustment or exception on listed grounds [@CL-IND-0199]. One listed ground is that a provision of the part or an official action results in an undue or exceptional hardship on that person not suffered generally by others in similar situations and circumstances [@CL-IND-0200]. Another listed ground is that the consequence of following a provision of the part or an official action is contrary to the intent of the Defense Production Act, the Selective Service Act and related statutes, or the part [@CL-IND-0201]. Each request for adjustment or exception must be in writing and contain a complete statement of all the facts and circumstances related to the provision of the part or official action from which adjustment is sought and a full and precise statement of the reasons why relief should be provided [@CL-IND-0220]. The adjustment or exception process for Directive Allocation Orders issued by a temporary final rule is contained in each Directive Allocation Order [@CL-IND-0223].

The Office of Strategic Industries and Economic Security shall respond to requests for adjustment of or exceptions to compliance within 25 days, not including Saturdays, Sundays or Government holidays, of the date of receipt [@CL-IND-0202]. A request for adjustment or exception does not relieve any person from complying with the part or official action in question while the request is being considered, unless such interim relief is granted in writing by the Office [@CL-IND-0203; @CL-IND-9001]. The printed text of the adjustments section was amended, effective August 27, 2026, through August 27, 2027 [@CL-IND-0207].

A person whose request has been denied by the Office of Strategic Industries and Economic Security may appeal to the Assistant Secretary for Export Administration, who shall review and reconsider the denial [@CL-IND-0204]. An appeal of a denied request must be received by the Assistant Secretary for Export Administration no later than 45 days after receipt of a written notice of denial from the Office of Strategic Industries and Economic Security [@CL-IND-0205; @CL-IND-0329]. The decision of the Assistant Secretary for Export Administration shall be made within a reasonable time after receipt of the appeal and shall be the final administrative action [@CL-IND-0206]. After the 45-day appeal period, an appeal may be accepted at the discretion of the Assistant Secretary for Export Administration [@CL-IND-0219]. The submission of an appeal does not relieve any person from the obligation of complying with the part or official action in question while the appeal is being considered, unless such relief is granted in writing by the Assistant Secretary for Export Administration [@CL-IND-0224; @CL-IND-9002].

## How it works

A person writes to the Office on a listed ground, with a complete statement of the facts and the reasons, and keeps complying unless the Office grants interim relief in writing [@CL-IND-0199; @CL-IND-0220; @CL-IND-0203; @CL-IND-9001]. The Office responds within the stated time [@CL-IND-0202]. If it denies the request, the person may appeal [@CL-IND-0204]. The appeal deadline is not absolute, because the Assistant Secretary may accept a later appeal at discretion [@CL-IND-0219]. An appeal does not by itself relieve the person from complying unless the Assistant Secretary grants relief in writing [@CL-IND-0224; @CL-IND-9002]. The printed section also covers what an appeal must contain, an optional informal hearing and how the appeal is decided, which this card does not state. The process for Directive Allocation Orders issued by a temporary final rule is in each such order [@CL-IND-0223]. Allocation orders are on [DPAS: allocation orders and their required elements](ind-dpas-allocation-orders.md), and audits and penalties are on [DPAS: audits, investigations, violations and penalties](ind-dpas-audits-investigations-and-penalties.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A supplier believes a provision causes it an undue hardship that others in similar situations do not suffer. It writes to the Office and keeps filling its orders. The Office denies the request, and the supplier receives the written notice. The answer: the supplier may appeal to the Assistant Secretary, and the appeal should be received within the 45-day period counted from receipt of the notice.

## Never confuse

A request for an adjustment or exception is not an appeal: the request goes to the Office, and the appeal goes to the Assistant Secretary after a denial [@CL-IND-0199; @CL-IND-0204]. Making a request does not suspend the duty to comply, unless the Office grants interim relief in writing [@CL-IND-0203; @CL-IND-9001].
