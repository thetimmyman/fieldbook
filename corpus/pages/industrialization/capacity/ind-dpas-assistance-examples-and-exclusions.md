## Purpose

Shows the situations in which special priorities assistance is usually provided under 15 CFR 700.52, and the situations listed in 15 CFR 700.54 in which it may not be provided [@CL-IND-0261; @CL-IND-0267].

Special priorities assistance may be provided for any reason in support of the regulation and an approved program, but it is usually provided in the situations that paragraph (a) of § 700.52 lists [@CL-IND-0261]. The paragraph points to §§ 700.55, 700.57 and 700.58 and Schedule I for approved programs [@CL-IND-0261]. Section 700.55 is on [the approved program determination card](ind-dpas-approved-program-determination.md) [@CL-IND-0261].

Assistance is provided at the discretion of the Determination Departments, Delegate Agencies and the Department of Commerce, when it is determined that the assistance is warranted to meet the objectives of the regulation [@CL-IND-0266]. The heading of § 700.54 says "will not be provided," while the text says assistance "may not" be provided in the listed examples [@CL-IND-0278; @CL-IND-0267].

## How it works

The usual situations are these. A person is experiencing difficulty in obtaining delivery against a rated order by the required delivery date [@CL-IND-0262]. A person cannot locate a supplier for an item needed to fill a rated order [@CL-IND-0263]. A person is experiencing difficulty in obtaining delivery for an unrated order by the required delivery date [@CL-IND-0263].

Other examples of assistance are ensuring that rated orders receive preferential treatment by suppliers, resolving production or delivery conflicts between various rated orders, and assisting in placing rated orders with suppliers [@CL-IND-0264]. They also include verifying the urgency of rated orders and determining the validity of rated orders [@CL-IND-0265].

Section 700.54 gives examples where assistance may not be provided, in situations when a person is attempting to secure a price advantage, obtain delivery prior to the time required to fill a rated order, or gain competitive advantage [@CL-IND-0267]. The examples also include attempting to disrupt an industry apportionment program in a manner designed to provide the person with an unwarranted share of scarce items, or to overcome a supplier's regularly established terms of sale or conditions of doing business [@CL-IND-0268].

The text says "may not." It does not say assistance will never be provided in these situations [@CL-IND-0267]. The decision is discretionary and rests with the Determination Departments, Delegate Agencies and the Department of Commerce [@CL-IND-0266]. The general provisions on filing a request are on [the special priorities assistance card](ind-dpas-special-priorities-assistance.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A company is short of a coating chemical for a rated order and cannot locate any supplier of it. That is a person who cannot locate a supplier for an item needed to fill a rated order, a situation in which assistance is usually provided [@CL-IND-0263]. Assisting in placing rated orders with suppliers is also an example of assistance [@CL-IND-0264]. Whether to provide it is still the decision of the Determination Departments, Delegate Agencies and the Department of Commerce [@CL-IND-0266].

A second company asks for assistance in obtaining delivery two weeks earlier than its rated order requires, so that it can build inventory. That is an attempt to obtain delivery prior to the time required to fill a rated order, an example where assistance may not be provided [@CL-IND-0267]. The text leaves the decision to the same decision makers [@CL-IND-0266].

## Never confuse

Assistance is not a price or competitive tool. A person who is attempting to secure a price advantage, gain competitive advantage, obtain delivery prior to the time required to fill a rated order, or overcome a supplier's regularly established terms of sale or conditions of doing business is in one of the examples where assistance may not be provided [@CL-IND-0267; @CL-IND-0268].

The usual situations are not a limit on assistance. Assistance may be provided for any reason in support of the regulation and an approved program [@CL-IND-0261].

A request outside the examples where assistance may not be provided is not guaranteed to succeed, because assistance is provided at the discretion of the decision makers when they determine it is warranted [@CL-IND-0266].
