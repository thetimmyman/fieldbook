## Purpose

Explains how a person asks for authority to use a priority rating under 15 CFR 700.51: in general, for production or construction equipment, and in advance of a rated prime contract [@CL-IND-0240; @CL-IND-0246; @CL-IND-0251].

| Applies to | A person who may request authority to use a priority rating in ordering needed items [@CL-IND-0240; @CL-IND-0242] |

A person may request the authority to use a priority rating in ordering needed items if a rated order is likely to be delayed because the person is unable to obtain items not normally rated under the part [@CL-IND-0240]. A person who does not have priority rating authority from a Delegate Agency or the Department of Commerce, and is unable to ensure the timely delivery of industrial resources, may also request that authority [@CL-IND-0242].

Examples of items for which ratings may be authorized include production or construction equipment, computers when not used as production items, and expansion, rebuilding or replacing plant facilities [@CL-IND-0241].

A rating authorization is an official action granting specific priority rating authority that permits a person to place a priority rating on an order for an item, such as an item not normally ratable under this regulation, or that authorizes a person to modify a priority rating on a specific order or series of contracts or orders [@CL-IND-1024; @CL-IND-1025]. To request priority rating authority, the section on rating authorizations points to § 700.51 [@CL-IND-1026].

Under paragraph (b)(1), a request for priority rating authority under the part must be submitted on Form BIS-999 to the Department of Commerce, and the paragraph refers to § 700.50(c) for information on the form [@CL-IND-0243]. Under paragraph (b)(2), a request may be used only to support approved programs [@CL-IND-0244]. The paragraph points to §§ 700.55, 700.57 and 700.58 for information on requesting a determination by the appropriate Determination Department that the request supports a program necessary or appropriate to promote the national defense [@CL-IND-0245]. Section 700.55 is on [the approved program determination card](ind-dpas-approved-program-determination.md) [@CL-IND-0245].

## How it works

Paragraphs (a) and (b) do not say who grants the authority. Paragraph (b)(1) says where the request goes [@CL-IND-0243]. Assistance that includes authorizing ratings is covered on [the assistance card](ind-dpas-special-priorities-assistance.md).

Paragraph (c) covers equipment. A request for priority rating authority for production or construction equipment must be submitted to the appropriate Delegate Agency or the Department of Commerce [@CL-IND-0246]. Requests in support of Department of Defense approved programs should be submitted to the Department of Defense on Department of Defense Form DD 691, and all other requests should be submitted on Form BIS-999 [@CL-IND-0247]. If the Delegate Agency is unable to resolve the problem or authorize the use of a priority rating, it may forward the request to the Department of Commerce for action [@CL-IND-0248]. Paragraph (b)(1) sends a request to the Department of Commerce, and paragraph (c)(1) sends an equipment request to the appropriate Delegate Agency or the Department of Commerce [@CL-IND-0243; @CL-IND-0246]. The text does not say which controls.

When a rating is authorized for procuring production or construction equipment, a rated order may be used either to purchase or to lease such equipment [@CL-IND-0249]. A lease may be taken only from a person engaged in the business of leasing such equipment or from a person willing to lease rather than sell [@CL-IND-0250]. What may not be bought with a rated order is on [the limits card](ind-dpas-rated-order-limits-and-rating-authority.md).

For authority in advance of a rated prime contract, in certain cases and upon specific request, the Department of Commerce, to promote the national defense, may authorize a person to place a priority rating on an order to a supplier in advance of the issuance of a rated prime contract [@CL-IND-0251]. The person requesting advance authority must obtain sponsorship of the request from the appropriate Delegate Agency or from those authorized by Commerce to priority rate the prime contract [@CL-IND-0252]. The person shall also assume any business risk associated with placing rated orders if they have to be cancelled because the rated prime contract is not issued [@CL-IND-0253].

The person must state in the request the words the paragraph gives [@CL-IND-0277]. Those words say that authorizing a rating in advance, and using it with suppliers, in no way commits the Department of Commerce or any other government agency to enter into a contract or order or to expend funds [@CL-IND-0254]. They also say that the Federal Government shall not be liable for any cancellation charges, termination costs or other damages that may accrue if a rated prime contract is not eventually placed, and that this covers the case in which, as a result, the person must subsequently cancel orders placed with the rating [@CL-IND-0255; @CL-IND-0379].

In reviewing a request for advance authority, Commerce will consider, among other things, the criteria the paragraph lists [@CL-IND-0256]. They are the probability that the prime contract will be awarded, the impact of the resulting rated orders on suppliers and on other authorized programs, and whether the contractor is the sole source [@CL-IND-0257]. They also include whether the item has a long lead time and the time period for which the rating is requested [@CL-IND-0258].

Paragraph (d)(4) says Commerce may require periodic reports on the use of the rating authority granted under paragraph (c) of the section [@CL-IND-0259]. The reference is to paragraph (c), as printed.

If a rated prime contract is not issued, the person shall promptly notify all suppliers who have received rated orders under the advance authority that the priority rating on those orders is cancelled [@CL-IND-0260]. The text gives no number of days; the duty is to act promptly [@CL-IND-0260].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A company expects a rated prime contract but has not received it. It needs a part with a 40-week lead time from a supplier, and the company has no authority to place a rating on that order.

It obtains sponsorship from the appropriate Delegate Agency [@CL-IND-0252]. Its request states the words the paragraph requires [@CL-IND-0277]. It submits the request on Form BIS-999 to the Department of Commerce, asking it to authorize a rating on the supplier order in advance of the prime contract [@CL-IND-0243; @CL-IND-0251]. The request supports an approved program, because a request for priority rating authority may be used only to support approved programs [@CL-IND-0244].

Commerce considers, among other things, the probability of award and whether the part has a long lead time [@CL-IND-0256; @CL-IND-0257; @CL-IND-0258]. It authorizes the rating, and the company places the order.

The prime contract is then not issued. The company must promptly notify the supplier that the rating on the order is cancelled [@CL-IND-0260]. The company bears the business risk of cancelling the order, and its request has stated that the Federal Government shall not be liable for cancellation charges [@CL-IND-0253; @CL-IND-0255].

## Never confuse

Rating authority is not a rated order. A rated order is a prime contract, a subcontract or a purchase order in support of an approved program [@CL-IND-0079]. Rating authority is the permission to use a priority rating in ordering items, and under paragraph (b)(2) a request for it may be used only to support approved programs [@CL-IND-0240; @CL-IND-0244].

It is also not a determination that a program is approved. The paragraph points to separate sections for requesting that determination [@CL-IND-0245].

Advance authority does not commit the Government to a contract. The request must say that authorizing the rating and using it with suppliers commits neither Commerce nor any other government agency to enter into a contract or order or to expend funds [@CL-IND-0254].
