## Purpose

Shows what records a person must keep under the Defense Priorities and Allocations System regulation, for how long, and which transactions the part covers.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Not established. |
| Codified citation | Not established. |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Not established. |
| Applies to | Persons with transactions covered by the part or an official action [@CL-IND-0208] |

Persons are required to make and preserve for at least three years accurate and complete records of any transaction covered by the part or an official action [@CL-IND-0208]. Records must be maintained in sufficient detail to permit the determination, upon examination, of whether each transaction complies with the part or any official action, and the part does not specify any particular method or system to be used [@CL-IND-0209]. Records required to be maintained by the part must be made available for examination on demand by duly authorized representatives of the Department of Commerce [@CL-IND-0210]. Persons must also develop, maintain and submit to the Department of Commerce any other records and reports that may be required for the administration of the Defense Production Act, the Selective Service Act and related statutes, and the part [@CL-IND-0221].

The part and all official actions, unless specifically stated otherwise, apply to transactions in any state, territory or possession of the United States and the District of Columbia [@CL-IND-0211]. The part and all official actions apply not only to deliveries to other persons but also to deliveries to affiliates and subsidiaries of a person and deliveries from one branch, division or section of a single entity to another under common ownership or control [@CL-IND-0212].

A person shall not be held liable for damages or penalties for any act or failure to act resulting directly or indirectly from compliance with any provision of the part, or an official action, notwithstanding that such provision or action shall subsequently be declared invalid by judicial or other competent authority [@CL-IND-1033; @CL-IND-1034].

## How it works

Persons must make and preserve, for at least three years, accurate and complete records of any transaction covered by the part or an official action [@CL-IND-0208]. The records must let an examiner decide whether each transaction complies, and the part does not specify any particular method or system [@CL-IND-0209]. Records required to be maintained by the part must be made available for examination on demand by duly authorized representatives of Commerce, as provided in section 700.71 [@CL-IND-0210]. The audit and investigation powers are described on [DPAS: audits, investigations, violations and penalties](ind-dpas-audits-investigations-and-penalties.md). Persons must also develop, maintain and submit to Commerce any other records and reports that may be required for the administration of the Defense Production Act, the Selective Service Act and related statutes, and the part [@CL-IND-0221]. The printed section does not say from which date the retention period runs. It also treats some information as confidential, which this card does not state.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A supplier fills a rated order and keeps the order, its acceptance and its delivery records in a shared drive. Later, a Commerce representative asks to examine them. The answer: the supplier must make the records available on demand, and they must be detailed enough to show whether the transaction complied.

## Never confuse

The retention period is a floor, not a fixed term, because records must be kept for at least three years [@CL-IND-0208]. The part does not prescribe a records system, so the test is whether the records permit the determination of compliance [@CL-IND-0209]. A transfer inside one company is not outside the part when it moves between branches, divisions or sections under common ownership or control [@CL-IND-0212].
