## Purpose

Shows what the DO and DX priority ratings mean, what a rated order must contain, which provision and clause go into rated-order solicitations and contracts, and whom the rules bind.

## The rule

| Field | Value |

| Applies to | Rated orders under the DPAS [@CL-IND-0154] |

The DPAS establishes two levels of priority for rated orders, identified by the rating symbols DO and DX [@CL-IND-0154]. All DO rated orders have equal priority with each other and take preference over unrated orders, and all DX rated orders have equal priority with each other and take preference over DO rated orders and unrated orders [@CL-IND-0134].

A rated order must include the appropriate priority rating (e.g. DO-A1, DX-A2, DO-N1) and a required delivery date or dates, and the words "immediately" or "as soon as possible" do not constitute a delivery date [@CL-IND-0135]. When a requirements contract, basic ordering agreement, prime vendor contract, or similar procurement document bearing a priority rating contains no specific delivery date or dates, but provides for the furnishing of items from time-to-time or within a stated period against specific purchase orders, such as calls, requisitions, and delivery orders, the purchase orders supporting such contracts or agreements must specify a required delivery date or dates and are to be considered as rated as of the date of their receipt by the supplier and not as of the date of the original procurement document [@CL-IND-0338; @CL-IND-0339; @CL-IND-0340]. A rated order must also include the written signature on a manually placed order, or the digital signature or name on an electronically placed order, of an individual authorized to sign rated orders for the person placing the order [@CL-IND-0135; @CL-IND-0136]. A rated order must also include a statement that reads in substance: "This is a rated order certified for national defense use, and you are required to follow all the provisions of the Defense Priorities and Allocations System regulation (15 CFR part 700)." [@CL-IND-0135; @CL-IND-0137] If the rated order is placed for the purpose of emergency preparedness requirements and expedited action is necessary or appropriate to meet these requirements, it must also include a statement that reads: "This rated order is placed for the purpose of emergency preparedness. It must be accepted or rejected within [Insert a time limit no less than the minimum applicable time limit specified in § 700.13(d)(2)]." [@CL-IND-0138; @CL-IND-0341; @CL-IND-0162]

## How it works

The FAR says that the DPAS regulation contains provisions concerning, among other matters, the elements of a rated order, acceptance and rejection of rated orders, preferential scheduling and extension of priority ratings (flowdown) [@CL-IND-0155]. It also says that rated orders shall be placed in accordance with the provisions of the DPAS and that agency heads shall ensure compliance with the DPAS by contracting activities within their agencies [@CL-IND-0141]. A rated order must include the appropriate priority rating, the required delivery date or dates, the signature of an individual authorized to sign rated orders for the person placing the order, and the certification statement for 15 CFR part 700, plus the emergency preparedness statement when applicable [@CL-IND-0135; @CL-IND-0136; @CL-IND-0137; @CL-IND-0138; @CL-IND-0341; @CL-IND-0162]. The duties of a person who receives a rated order are on [DPAS: acceptance, rejection, scheduling and flowdown of rated orders](ind-dpas-acceptance-rejection-and-flowdown.md). Contracting officers shall insert the provision at 52.211-14, Notice of Priority Rating for National Defense, Emergency Preparedness, and Energy Program Use, in solicitations when the contract to be awarded will be a rated order [@CL-IND-0139]. Contracting officers shall insert the clause at 52.211-15, Defense Priority and Allocation Requirements, in contracts that are rated orders [@CL-IND-0140].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A buyer places an order for 500 valves marked DO-A1, with a required delivery date of 15 March, the signature of an authorized individual and the national defense statement. The order has every required element. A second order reads deliver as soon as possible and gives no date. The answer: the first order is a complete rated order, and the second lacks a delivery date.

## Never confuse

All DO rated orders have equal priority with each other and take preference over unrated orders, and all DX rated orders have equal priority with each other and take preference over DO rated orders and unrated orders [@CL-IND-0134]. A rated order must include both the appropriate priority rating and a required delivery date or dates, and the words "immediately" or "as soon as possible" do not constitute a delivery date [@CL-IND-0135]. Contracting officers insert the provision at 52.211-14 in solicitations when the contract to be awarded will be a rated order and the clause at 52.211-15 in contracts that are rated orders, and the DPAS regulation contains the provisions concerning the elements of a rated order [@CL-IND-0139; @CL-IND-0140; @CL-IND-0155].
