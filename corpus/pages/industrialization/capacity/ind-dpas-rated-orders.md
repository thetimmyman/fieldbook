## Purpose

Shows what the DO and DX priority ratings mean, what a rated order must contain, which provision and clause go into rated-order solicitations and contracts, and whom the rules bind.

## The rule

| Applies to | Rated orders under the DPAS [@CL-IND-0154] |

The DPAS establishes two levels of priority for rated orders, identified by the rating symbols DO and DX [@CL-IND-0154]. All DO rated orders have equal priority with each other and take preference over unrated orders, and all DX rated orders have equal priority with each other and take preference over DO rated orders and unrated orders [@CL-IND-0134].

A rated order must carry the appropriate priority rating and program identification symbol and a required delivery date or dates, and the words immediately or as soon as possible do not constitute a delivery date [@CL-IND-0135]. It must also carry the written signature, or the digital signature or name on an electronically placed order, of an individual authorized to sign rated orders for the person placing the order [@CL-IND-0136; @CL-IND-0135]. It must also carry a statement that reads in substance that it is a rated order certified for national defense use and that the recipient is required to follow all the provisions of the Defense Priorities and Allocations System regulation, which the statement names as 15 CFR part 700 [@CL-IND-0137; @CL-IND-0135]. A rated order placed for emergency preparedness requirements, where expedited action is necessary or appropriate, must also include a prescribed statement [@CL-IND-0138]. That statement says the order must be accepted or rejected within a time limit no less than the minimum applicable time limit specified in the acceptance and rejection section [@CL-IND-0162].

## How it works

The FAR says that the DPAS regulation contains provisions concerning, among other matters, the elements of a rated order, acceptance and rejection of rated orders, preferential scheduling and extension of priority ratings (flowdown) [@CL-IND-0155]. It also says that rated orders shall be placed in accordance with the provisions of the DPAS and that agency heads shall ensure compliance with the DPAS by contracting activities within their agencies [@CL-IND-0141]. The element rules speak of the person placing the order [@CL-IND-0136]. The duties of a person who receives a rated order are on [DPAS: acceptance, rejection, scheduling and flowdown of rated orders](ind-dpas-acceptance-rejection-and-flowdown.md), and the printed sections do not define who counts as a person. A buyer builds the order from the required elements [@CL-IND-0135; @CL-IND-0136; @CL-IND-0137]. Contracting officers shall insert the provision Notice of Priority Rating for National Defense, Emergency Preparedness, and Energy Program Use in a solicitation when the contract to be awarded will be a rated order [@CL-IND-0139]. They shall also insert the clause Defense Priority and Allocation Requirements in contracts that are rated orders [@CL-IND-0140].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A buyer places an order for 500 valves marked DO-A1, with a required delivery date of 15 March, the signature of an authorized individual and the national defense statement. The order has every required element. A second order reads deliver as soon as possible and gives no date. The answer: the first order is a complete rated order, and the second lacks a delivery date.

## Never confuse

DO and DX are priority levels between rated orders, and an unrated order sits below both [@CL-IND-0134]. A rating is not a delivery date, because immediately or as soon as possible does not count [@CL-IND-0135]. The FAR provision and clause are inserted by contracting officers, while the order elements come from the DPAS regulation [@CL-IND-0139; @CL-IND-0140; @CL-IND-0155].
