## Purpose

Shows what Commerce may examine under the Defense Priorities and Allocations System regulation and what penalties and remedies follow a violation.

| Applies to | Audits and investigations that check whether the part has been followed [@CL-IND-0193] |

Audits and investigations are official actions involving the examination of books, records, documents, other writings and information to ensure that the Defense Production Act, the Selective Service Act and related statutes, and the part have been properly followed [@CL-IND-0193]. The Department of Commerce may issue administrative subpoenas, which constitute official actions [@CL-IND-0194]. When undertaking an audit, investigation or other inquiry, the Department of Commerce shall define the scope and purpose in the official action given to the person under investigation [@CL-IND-0222], and shall have ascertained that the information sought or other adequate and authoritative data are not available from any Federal or other responsible agency [@CL-IND-0330]. One listed document, a demand for information, requires a person to furnish to a duly authorized representative of the Department of Commerce any information necessary or appropriate to the enforcement or the administration of the Defense Production Act, the Selective Service Act or the part [@CL-IND-0195].

Willful violation of the provisions of Title I or sections 705 or 707 of the Defense Production Act, the priorities provisions of the Selective Service Act and related statutes, or the part is a crime, and upon conviction a person may be punished by fine or imprisonment, or both, as provided in sections 103 and 705 of the Defense Production Act and in section 468(f) of the Selective Service Act [@CL-IND-0196; @CL-IND-0217]. The government may also seek an injunction from a court of appropriate jurisdiction to prohibit the continuance of any violation of, or to enforce compliance with, the Defense Production Act, the part, or an official action [@CL-IND-0197]. No person shall deliver any item if the person knows or has reason to believe that the item will be accepted, redelivered, held, or used in violation of the Defense Production Act, the part, or an official action [@CL-IND-0218]. In such instances, the person must immediately notify the Department of Commerce that, in accordance with the section, delivery has not been made [@CL-IND-9003].

## How it works

An audit or investigation is an official action involving the examination of books, records, documents, other writings and information to ensure that the Defense Production Act, the Selective Service Act and related statutes, and the part have been properly followed [@CL-IND-0193]. When undertaking an audit, investigation or other inquiry, the Department of Commerce defines the scope and purpose in the official action given to the person under investigation and must have ascertained that the information sought or other adequate and authoritative data are not available from any Federal or other responsible agency [@CL-IND-0222; @CL-IND-0330]. Commerce may issue administrative subpoenas and demands for information [@CL-IND-0194; @CL-IND-0195]. The records a person must keep and make available are on [DPAS: records, retention and where the part applies](ind-dpas-records-and-applicability.md). Relief from a requirement of the part is on [DPAS: adjustments, exceptions and appeals](ind-dpas-adjustments-exceptions-and-appeals.md). Willful violation of the provisions of Title I or sections 705 or 707 of the Defense Production Act, the priorities provisions of the Selective Service Act and related statutes, or the part is a crime punishable on conviction by fine or imprisonment, or both, as provided in sections 103 and 705 of the Defense Production Act and in section 468(f) of the Selective Service Act, and the government may also seek an injunction from a court of appropriate jurisdiction [@CL-IND-0196; @CL-IND-0217; @CL-IND-0197].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

Commerce serves a supplier with a demand for information asking for its records of rated orders. The supplier must furnish the information that is necessary or appropriate to the enforcement or administration of the rules. The answer: the demand is an official action. A willful violation found later could lead on conviction to a fine, imprisonment or both.

## Never confuse
