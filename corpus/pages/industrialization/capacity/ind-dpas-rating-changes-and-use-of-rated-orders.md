## Purpose

Shows how a priority rating on a rated order is changed or cancelled, and how a contractor uses its own rating to order the supplies it needs.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|

| Applies to | The priority rating on a rated order [@CL-IND-0169] |

The priority rating on a rated order may be changed or cancelled by an official action of the Department of Commerce or by written notification from the person who placed the rated order, including a Delegate Agency [@CL-IND-0169]. An amendment to a rated order that significantly alters a supplier's original production or delivery schedule constitutes a new rated order as of the date of its receipt, and the supplier must accept or reject the amended order [@CL-IND-0170]. A person must cancel any rated orders that the person or a predecessor in interest has placed with suppliers, or cancel the priority ratings on those orders, if the person no longer needs the items in those orders to fill a rated order [@CL-IND-0171]. A person adding a rating to an unrated order, or changing or cancelling a priority rating, must promptly notify all suppliers to whom the order was sent of the addition, change or cancellation [@CL-IND-0225]. If an unrated order is amended so as to make it a rated order, or a DO rating is changed to a DX rating, the supplier must give the appropriate preferential treatment to the order as of the date the change is received by the supplier [@CL-IND-0226]. The following amendments to a rated order do not constitute a new rated order: a change in shipping destination; a reduction in the total amount of the order; and an increase in the total amount of the order which has negligible impact upon deliveries [@CL-IND-0215]. Nor do a minor variation in size or design before the start of production, or a change agreed upon between the supplier and the customer [@CL-IND-0228].

Among the things a person must use rated orders to obtain are items that will be physically incorporated into other items to fill rated orders, including the portion of such items normally consumed or converted into scrap or by-products in the course of processing [@CL-IND-0172]. One listed thing a person must use rated orders to obtain is MRO needed to produce the finished items to fill rated orders, and the priority rating used for MRO must contain the program identification symbol H7 along with the rating symbol on the customer's rated order [@CL-IND-0173]. A person may use a rated order to replace inventoried items, including finished items, that were used to fill rated orders, subject to listed conditions, one of which is that the order be placed within 90 days of the date of use of the inventory [@CL-IND-0174]. Another listed condition is that the order use a DO rating symbol and the program identification symbol on the customer's rated order, and a DX rating symbol may not be used even if the inventory was used to fill a DX rated order [@CL-IND-0175]. A person is not required to place a priority rating on an order for less than $125,000, or one half of the Simplified Acquisition Threshold, whichever amount is greater, provided that delivery can be obtained in a timely fashion without the use of the priority rating [@CL-IND-0213; @CL-IND-0214].

## How it works

A rating may be changed or cancelled by an official action of Commerce or by written notification from the person who placed the order, including a Delegate Agency [@CL-IND-0169]. An amendment that significantly alters the supplier's production or delivery schedule makes a new rated order unless it is one of the listed exceptions, such as a change the supplier and customer agree [@CL-IND-0228], and the supplier handles it under the acceptance and rejection rules on [DPAS: acceptance, rejection, scheduling and flowdown of rated orders](ind-dpas-acceptance-rejection-and-flowdown.md) [@CL-IND-0170]. A contractor passes its rating down by placing rated orders for the items and MRO it needs to fill a rated order [@CL-IND-0172; @CL-IND-0173]. When it no longer needs those items, it must cancel the orders or their ratings [@CL-IND-0171]. What may not be bought with a rated order is on [DPAS: limits on rated orders and requests for rating authority](ind-dpas-rated-order-limits-and-rating-authority.md). The printed sections also set rules on combining rated orders and on minimum commercially procurable quantities, which this card does not state.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor holds a DO-A3 rated order for 100 gearboxes and orders bearings for them with a DO-A3 rated order. It also orders maintenance supplies with a DO-H7 rating. The customer then cancels its order. The answer: the contractor no longer needs the items, so it must cancel the bearing order and the maintenance order, or their ratings.

## Never confuse

Changing a rating is not the same as amending an order: an amendment that significantly alters the supplier's original production or delivery schedule constitutes a new rated order [@CL-IND-0170], while a change in shipping destination, a reduction in the total amount of the order or a change agreed upon between the supplier and the customer does not [@CL-IND-0215; @CL-IND-0228]. A rated order that replaces used inventory is also not the same as one placed for MRO: the MRO order carries the program identification symbol H7 with the rating symbol on the customer's rated order [@CL-IND-0173], while the replacement order must use a DO rating symbol even if the inventory filled a DX rated order [@CL-IND-0175].
