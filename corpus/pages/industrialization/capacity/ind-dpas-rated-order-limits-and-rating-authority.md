## Purpose

Shows what may not be bought with a rated order, and how priority rating authority is requested in the one case that the printed sections describe.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|

| Applies to | Persons placing rated orders [@CL-IND-0176] |

A person may not place a rated order unless the person is in receipt of a rated order, has been explicitly authorized to do so by the Department of Commerce or a Delegate Agency, or is otherwise permitted to do so by the part [@CL-IND-0176]. Rated orders may not be used to obtain delivery on a date earlier than needed, or a greater quantity of the item than needed except to obtain a minimum procurable quantity [@CL-IND-0177]. Separate rated orders may not be placed solely for obtaining minimum procurable quantities on each order if the minimum procurable quantity would be sufficient to cover more than one rated order [@CL-IND-0227]. One listed thing rated orders may not be used to obtain is items in advance of the receipt of a rated order, except as specifically authorized by the Department of Commerce [@CL-IND-0178].

One listed group of items for which rated orders may not be used, unless specific priority rating authority has been obtained from a Delegate Agency or the Department of Commerce, is items for plant improvement, expansion or construction, unless they will be physically incorporated into a construction project covered by a rated order [@CL-IND-0179]. The same list, which applies unless specific priority rating authority has been obtained from a Delegate Agency or the Department of Commerce, also includes production or construction equipment or items to be used for the manufacture of production equipment [@CL-IND-0180; @CL-IND-0179]. Separately, no person may place a rated order to obtain listed items, one of which is copper raw materials, unless such order is authorized by an official action of the Department of Commerce [@CL-IND-0181].

For projects believed to maximize domestic energy supplies, a person may request priority rating authority for scarce, critical and essential supplies of materials, equipment and services [@CL-IND-0182]. The text adds, in parentheses after the word services, a scope related to the production of materials or equipment, or the installation, repair or maintenance of equipment, and the request is made by submitting it to the Department of Energy [@CL-IND-0183]. If the Department of Energy notifies the Department of Commerce that the project maximizes domestic energy supplies and that the materials, equipment or services are critical and essential, the Department of Commerce will determine whether the items in question are scarce and, if they are scarce, whether there is a need to use the priorities authority [@CL-IND-0184; @CL-IND-0216]. After its analysis, the Department of Commerce will advise the Department of Energy whether the two findings, on scarcity and on the need to use the priorities authority, have been satisfied [@CL-IND-0216; @CL-IND-0185], and if they are satisfied it will authorize the Department of Energy to grant the use of a priority rating to the applicant [@CL-IND-0185].

Section 101(c) of the Defense Production Act authorizes the use of priority ratings for projects which maximize domestic energy supplies [@CL-IND-1015]. Projects which maximize domestic energy supplies include those which maintain or further domestic energy exploration, production, refining, and transportation; maintain or further the conservation of energy; or are involved in the construction or maintenance of energy facilities [@CL-IND-1016].

## How it works

The starting point is that a person may not place a rated order unless the person received one, was explicitly authorized to do so, or is otherwise permitted to do so by the part [@CL-IND-0176]. The listed limits then narrow what may be rated, and some items need specific authority first [@CL-IND-0179]. For energy projects, the request goes to the Department of Energy, and Commerce then authorizes that department to grant the rating once the findings are satisfied [@CL-IND-0183; @CL-IND-0185]. The printed sections do not establish a general procedure for requesting priority rating authority for other items; the line on advance authorization points to another section that this card's source text does not include [@CL-IND-0178]. The printed section also lists further items for which rated orders may not be used, which this card does not state.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A shop holds a rated order for 200 housings. It wants to buy a new press for its plant, and it also wants its steel delivered a month before it is needed. The answer: it may not use the rated order for the press unless it has specific priority rating authority, and it may not use the rated order to get delivery earlier than needed.

## Never confuse

The limits on what may be rated are not the same as the energy request route. The energy route is a request to the Department of Energy for authority on projects believed to maximize domestic energy supplies [@CL-IND-0182; @CL-IND-0183]. The copper rule is also separate from the plant improvement and equipment group: it applies notwithstanding any authorization or requirement elsewhere in the part and turns on an official action of Commerce [@CL-IND-0181], while the plant improvement and equipment group turns on specific priority rating authority from a Delegate Agency or Commerce [@CL-IND-0179].
