## Purpose

Explains where a person sends the written request under 15 CFR 700.55 for a determination that a program is necessary or appropriate to promote the national defense [@CL-IND-0270].

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Not established. |
| Codified citation | Not established. |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Not established. |
| Applies to | A person requesting priority rating authority or requiring assistance in obtaining rated items under the part, for the activities that each paragraph lists [@CL-IND-0372; @CL-IND-0374; @CL-IND-0377] |

Each paragraph says that such a person should submit a request for a determination in writing [@CL-IND-0372; @CL-IND-0375; @CL-IND-0377]. The determination sought is that the request supports a program that is necessary or appropriate to promote the national defense, which the section calls an approved program [@CL-IND-0270]. The text says "should," not "must."

The section has three paragraphs, one for each category of activity within the United States [@CL-IND-0269; @CL-IND-0272; @CL-IND-0274]. Each paragraph names a different office to receive the request [@CL-IND-0271; @CL-IND-0273; @CL-IND-0275].

## How it works

Paragraph (a) covers homeland security, emergency preparedness, and critical infrastructure protection and restoration related activities [@CL-IND-0372]. The heading of paragraph (a) says "emergency response," where the sentence says "emergency preparedness" [@CL-IND-0269; @CL-IND-0372]. The request goes to the Office of Policy and Program Analysis, Federal Emergency Management Agency, Department of Homeland Security, at the postal address the paragraph prints [@CL-IND-0271]. The same sentence also gives a telephone number, a fax number, an email address and a website [@CL-IND-0373].

Paragraph (b) covers military production and construction, military use of civil transportation, stockpiles managed by the Department of Defense, space, and directly related activities [@CL-IND-0374; @CL-IND-0375]. The request goes to the Department of Defense DPA Title I Lead in the Office of the Assistant Secretary of Defense for Industrial Base Policy, at the postal address the paragraph prints [@CL-IND-0273]. The sentence also gives a telephone number, a fax number, an email address and a website [@CL-IND-0376].

Paragraph (c) covers energy production and construction, distribution and use, and directly related activities [@CL-IND-0377]. The request goes to the Department of Energy, Office of Cybersecurity, Energy Security, and Emergency Response, at the postal address the paragraph prints [@CL-IND-0275]. The sentence also gives a telephone number and an email address [@CL-IND-0378].

Each paragraph's heading adds "within the United States" [@CL-IND-0269; @CL-IND-0272; @CL-IND-0274].

This section names the office that receives the request. It does not say in terms who makes the determination, although § 700.51(b)(2) refers to a determination by the appropriate Determination Department [@CL-IND-0245]. Determination Departments are defined on [the definitions card](ind-dpas-definitions.md). The text sets no deadline and no fee.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A company wants authority to use a priority rating for a machine tool that supports a military production program within the United States.

Paragraph (b) covers military production and construction [@CL-IND-0374]. The company writes a request for a determination, stating that the request supports a program necessary or appropriate to promote the national defense [@CL-IND-0375; @CL-IND-0270]. It sends the request to the Department of Defense DPA Title I Lead at the postal address the paragraph prints [@CL-IND-0273].

Under § 700.51(b)(2), the company's own request for priority rating authority may be used only to support approved programs [@CL-IND-0244]. The section does not set a time for the determination.

A second company runs a pipeline project. It sends its request to the Department of Energy office that paragraph (c) names, not to the Defense Department office [@CL-IND-0377; @CL-IND-0275].

## Never confuse

An approved program determination is neither a priority rating nor rating authority. The determination sought is that the request supports a program necessary or appropriate to promote the national defense [@CL-IND-0270]. A request for rating authority is separate and may be used only to support approved programs [@CL-IND-0244]. See [the rating authority card](ind-dpas-priority-rating-authority-requests.md).

Each category names its own office, so a request for one category should go to the office that its paragraph names [@CL-IND-0271; @CL-IND-0273; @CL-IND-0275].
