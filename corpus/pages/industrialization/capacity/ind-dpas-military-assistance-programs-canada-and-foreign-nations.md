## Purpose

Shows how priority ratings and informal help work for military assistance programs that involve Canada, other foreign nations or international organizations, and which office takes each request.

## The rule

| Applies to | Persons in Canada, in other foreign nations and in international organizations that need priority ratings for items bought in the United States, and persons in the United States that need informal help abroad [@CL-IND-0279; @CL-IND-0290; @CL-IND-0280; @CL-IND-0291] |

15 CFR 700.56 provides for authorizing priority ratings to persons in Canada to obtain items in the United States in support of approved programs [@CL-IND-0279]. 15 CFR 700.57 provides the same for persons in other foreign nations and international organizations [@CL-IND-0290]. Priority ratings have no legal authority outside the United States, so each section also says how a person in the United States may get informal assistance abroad, in Canada or in foreign nations that are signatories to bilateral security of supply arrangements with the Department of Defense [@CL-IND-0280; @CL-IND-0291]. The second section lists those nations as Australia, Estonia, Denmark, Finland, Israel, Italy, Japan, Latvia, Lithuania, The Netherlands, Norway, Republic of Korea, Singapore, Spain, Sweden and the United Kingdom, and says a website gives the current list [@CL-IND-0292; @CL-IND-0320].

Canada. The section says the joint United States-Canadian military arrangements for the defense of North America and the integrated nature of the two countries' defense industries require close coordination and the establishment of a means to provide mutual assistance to the defense industries in both countries [@CL-IND-0322; @CL-IND-0325]. The Department of Commerce coordinates with Public Works and Government Services Canada on all matters of mutual concern relating to the administration of this part [@CL-IND-0281]. A person in the United States ordering defense items in Canada for an approved program should tell the Canadian supplier that the items fill a rated order [@CL-IND-0282]. The supplier should also be told that, if it or its vendor needs production materials from the United States to fill the order, the supplier or vendor should contact Public Works and Government Services Canada for authority to place rated orders in the United States, and the paragraph gives that office's postal address, telephone, fax and email [@CL-IND-0283]. A person in Canada producing defense items for the Canadian government may also obtain priority rating authority for items bought in the United States by applying to Public Works and Government Services Canada, Acquisitions Branch, Business Management Directorate, in accordance with its procedures [@CL-IND-0284; @CL-IND-0285]. A person in Canada needing special priorities assistance in obtaining defense items in the United States may apply to the same office, and Public Works and Government Services Canada will forward appropriate requests to the Department of Commerce [@CL-IND-0286; @CL-IND-0287]. A person in the United States needing help to obtain items in Canada must submit a request on Form BIS-999 to the Office of Strategic Industries and Economic Security, and the Department of Commerce will forward appropriate requests to Public Works and Government Services Canada [@CL-IND-0288; @CL-IND-0289].

Other nations and international organizations. A person in a foreign nation other than Canada, or in an international organization, that needs help to obtain items in the United States or priority rating authority for items bought in the United States should submit a request to the Department of Defense DPA Title I Lead in the Office of the Assistant Secretary of Defense for Industrial Base Policy [@CL-IND-0293; @CL-IND-0294]. If a United States Government agency is acquiring the end product, the request should go to the Lead through the United States contract administration representative [@CL-IND-0295]. If a foreign nation or international organization is acquiring it, the request must be sponsored before it goes to the Lead by the government of the foreign nation or the international organization that will use the end product [@CL-IND-0296]. If the Department of Defense endorses the request, it will be forwarded to the Department of Commerce for appropriate action [@CL-IND-0297].

Signatory nations. The bilateral arrangements allow the Department of Defense to request priority delivery for its contracts, subcontracts and orders from companies in those countries [@CL-IND-0321]. The paragraph repeats the list of those nations [@CL-IND-0323]. A person in the United States needing priority delivery of a contract, subcontract or order in such a nation should contact the Department of Defense DPA Title I Lead for assistance [@CL-IND-0298; @CL-IND-0299]. A person in such a nation should request assistance under paragraph (b)(1) of the same section, which is the route in the previous paragraph [@CL-IND-0300].

## How it works

For Canada, a request for special priorities assistance from a person in Canada may be made to Public Works and Government Services Canada, which will forward appropriate requests to Commerce, while a request from a person in the United States for assistance in obtaining items in Canada must go to Commerce on Form BIS-999, and Commerce will forward appropriate requests to Public Works and Government Services Canada [@CL-IND-0286; @CL-IND-0287; @CL-IND-0288; @CL-IND-0289]. Priority rating authority for a person in Canada is sought from Public Works and Government Services Canada in accordance with its procedures [@CL-IND-0284; @CL-IND-0285]. For other nations, the request goes to the Department of Defense Lead, through the contract administration representative or after sponsorship, and if the Department of Defense endorses it, the request will be forwarded to Commerce for appropriate action; the text does not say what happens to a request that is not endorsed [@CL-IND-0295; @CL-IND-0296; @CL-IND-0297]. The Department of Defense decides whether to endorse, and the text does not state the test for endorsement. The sections state no deadline or dollar threshold. Most duties are stated as "should", and the sponsorship duty and the Form BIS-999 duty are stated as "must" [@CL-IND-0296; @CL-IND-0288].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A United States prime contractor orders 300 defense assemblies from a Canadian supplier for an approved program. It tells the supplier that the order fills a rated order [@CL-IND-0282]. The supplier needs 400 units of a material made in the United States, so it contacts Public Works and Government Services Canada for authority to place rated orders in the United States [@CL-IND-0283]. The prime also needs help to obtain a part from a Canadian vendor, so it submits Form BIS-999 to the Office of Strategic Industries and Economic Security, which forwards appropriate requests to Public Works and Government Services Canada [@CL-IND-0288; @CL-IND-0289].

In a second case, a firm in a foreign nation other than Canada wants priority rating authority for 200 pumps that its own government is buying. The end product is being acquired by a foreign nation, so its government must sponsor the request before it is submitted to the Department of Defense Lead [@CL-IND-0293; @CL-IND-0294; @CL-IND-0296]. If the Department of Defense endorses the request, it will be forwarded to Commerce for appropriate action, and the text does not say what happens if it does not [@CL-IND-0297].

## Never confuse

Informal help in Canada or in a signatory nation is not legal authority to rate orders there, because priority ratings have no legal authority outside the United States [@CL-IND-0280; @CL-IND-0291]. The route for other nations does not cover Canada, which has its own section [@CL-IND-0293; @CL-IND-0279]. Critical infrastructure requests from foreign nations follow a separate route, on [DPAS critical infrastructure assistance](ind-dpas-critical-infrastructure-assistance-foreign-nations.md).
