## Purpose

Shows how priority ratings and informal help work for military assistance programs that involve Canada, other foreign nations or international organizations, and which office takes each request.

| Applies to | Persons in Canada, in other foreign nations and in international organizations that need priority ratings for items bought in the United States, and persons in the United States that need informal help abroad [@CL-IND-0279; @CL-IND-0290; @CL-IND-0280; @CL-IND-0291] |

To promote military assistance to Canada, 15 CFR 700.56 provides for authorizing priority ratings to persons in Canada to obtain items in the United States in support of approved programs [@CL-IND-0279]. To promote military assistance to foreign nations and international organizations (for example, the North Atlantic Treaty Organization or the United Nations), 15 CFR 700.57 provides for authorizing priority ratings to persons in foreign nations or international organizations to obtain items in the United States in support of approved programs [@CL-IND-0333; @CL-IND-0290]. Although priority ratings have no legal authority outside of the United States, these sections also provide information on how persons in the United States may obtain informal assistance in Canada and in foreign nations that are signatories to bilateral security of supply arrangements with the Department of Defense [@CL-IND-0280; @CL-IND-0291]. Those foreign nations are Australia, Estonia, Denmark, Finland, Israel, Italy, Japan, Latvia, Lithuania, The Netherlands, Norway, Republic of Korea, Singapore, Spain, Sweden and the United Kingdom, and the Defense Industrial Base Policy website has the current list of Security of Supply signatories [@CL-IND-0292; @CL-IND-0320].

Canada. The joint U.S.-Canadian military arrangements for the defense of North America and the integrated nature of the U.S. and Canadian defense industries require close coordination and the establishment of a means to provide mutual assistance to the defense industries in both countries [@CL-IND-0322; @CL-IND-0325]. The Department of Commerce coordinates with Public Works and Government Services Canada on all matters of mutual concern relating to the administration of this part [@CL-IND-0281]. Any person in the United States ordering defense items in Canada in support of an approved program should inform the Canadian supplier that the items being ordered are to be used to fill a rated order [@CL-IND-0282]. The Canadian supplier should be informed that if production materials are needed from the United States by the supplier or the supplier's vendor to fill the order, the supplier or vendor should contact Public Works and Government Services Canada for authority to place rated orders in the United States [@CL-IND-0283]. Any person in Canada producing defense items for the Canadian government may also obtain priority rating authority for items to be purchased in the United States by applying to Public Works and Government Services Canada, Acquisitions Branch, Business Management Directorate, in accordance with its procedures [@CL-IND-0284; @CL-IND-0285]. A person in Canada needing special priorities assistance in obtaining defense items in the United States may apply to Public Works and Government Services Canada, Acquisitions Branch, Business Management Directorate, and Public Works and Government Services Canada will forward appropriate requests to the Department of Commerce [@CL-IND-0286; @CL-IND-0287]. Any person in the United States requiring assistance in obtaining items in Canada must submit a request to the Office of Strategic Industries and Economic Security on Form BIS-999, and the Department of Commerce will forward appropriate requests to Public Works and Government Services Canada [@CL-IND-0288; @CL-IND-0289].

Other nations and international organizations. Any person in a foreign nation other than Canada, or any person in an international organization, requiring assistance in obtaining items in the United States or priority rating authority for items to be purchased in the United States, should submit a request for such assistance or priority rating authority to the Department of Defense DPA Title I Lead, Office of the Assistant Secretary of Defense for Industrial Base Policy, 3330 Defense Pentagon, Room 3B854, Washington, DC 20301 [@CL-IND-0293; @CL-IND-0294]. If the end product is being acquired by a U.S. Government agency, the request should be submitted to the Department of Defense DPA Title I Lead through the U.S. contract administration representative [@CL-IND-0295]. If the end product is being acquired by a foreign nation or international organization, the request must be sponsored prior to its submission to the Department of Defense DPA Title I Lead by the government of the foreign nation or the international organization that will use the end product [@CL-IND-0296]. If the Department of Defense endorses the request, it will be forwarded to the Department of Commerce for appropriate action [@CL-IND-0297].

Signatory nations. The Department of Defense has entered into bilateral security of supply arrangements with Australia, Estonia, Denmark, Finland, Israel, Italy, Japan, Latvia, Lithuania, The Netherlands, Norway, Republic of Korea, Singapore, Spain, Sweden and the United Kingdom that allow the Department of Defense to request the priority delivery for Department of Defense contracts, subcontracts, and orders from companies in these countries [@CL-IND-0321; @CL-IND-0323]. Any person in the United States requiring assistance in obtaining the priority delivery of a contract, subcontract, or order in these countries to support an approved program should contact the Department of Defense DPA Title I Lead for assistance [@CL-IND-0298; @CL-IND-0299]. Persons in Australia, Estonia, Denmark, Finland, Israel, Italy, Japan, Latvia, Lithuania, The Netherlands, Norway, Republic of Korea, Singapore, Spain, Sweden and the United Kingdom should request assistance in accordance with § 700.57(b)(1) [@CL-IND-0300].

## How it works

For Canada, a person in Canada needing special priorities assistance in obtaining defense items in the United States may apply to Public Works and Government Services Canada, Acquisitions Branch, Business Management Directorate, which will forward appropriate requests to the Department of Commerce, while a person in the United States requiring assistance in obtaining items in Canada must submit a request to the Office of Strategic Industries and Economic Security on Form BIS-999, and the Department of Commerce will forward appropriate requests to Public Works and Government Services Canada [@CL-IND-0286; @CL-IND-0287; @CL-IND-0288; @CL-IND-0289]. A person in Canada producing defense items for the Canadian government may obtain priority rating authority for items to be purchased in the United States by applying to Public Works and Government Services Canada, Acquisitions Branch, Business Management Directorate, in accordance with its procedures [@CL-IND-0284; @CL-IND-0285]. For a foreign nation other than Canada or an international organization, the request should go to the Department of Defense DPA Title I Lead, through the U.S. contract administration representative if the end product is being acquired by a U.S. Government agency or after sponsorship by the government of the foreign nation or the international organization that will use the end product if the end product is being acquired by a foreign nation or international organization, and if the Department of Defense endorses the request, it will be forwarded to the Department of Commerce for appropriate action [@CL-IND-0293; @CL-IND-0294; @CL-IND-0295; @CL-IND-0296; @CL-IND-0297].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A United States prime contractor orders 300 defense assemblies from a Canadian supplier for an approved program. It tells the supplier that the order fills a rated order [@CL-IND-0282]. The supplier needs 400 units of a material made in the United States, so it contacts Public Works and Government Services Canada for authority to place rated orders in the United States [@CL-IND-0283]. The prime also needs help to obtain a part from a Canadian vendor, so it submits Form BIS-999 to the Office of Strategic Industries and Economic Security, which forwards appropriate requests to Public Works and Government Services Canada [@CL-IND-0288; @CL-IND-0289].

In a second case, a firm in a foreign nation other than Canada wants priority rating authority for 200 pumps that its own government is buying. The end product is being acquired by a foreign nation, so its government must sponsor the request before it is submitted to the Department of Defense Lead [@CL-IND-0293; @CL-IND-0294; @CL-IND-0296]. If the Department of Defense endorses the request, it will be forwarded to Commerce for appropriate action [@CL-IND-0297].

## Never confuse

Priority ratings have no legal authority outside of the United States; the two sections instead route informal assistance requests: 15 CFR 700.56 and 700.57 provide information on how persons in the United States may obtain informal assistance in Canada and in the foreign nations that are signatories to bilateral security of supply arrangements with the Department of Defense [@CL-IND-0280; @CL-IND-0291]. The two sections do not reach the same people: 15 CFR 700.56 applies to military assistance to Canada, whereas 15 CFR 700.57 applies to a person in a foreign nation other than Canada or in an international organization [@CL-IND-0279; @CL-IND-0293]. Critical infrastructure assistance to foreign nations and international organizations is on [DPAS critical infrastructure assistance](ind-dpas-critical-infrastructure-assistance-foreign-nations.md).
