## Purpose

Shows when a supplier must accept or may reject a rated order, the response deadlines, how rated orders are scheduled, and how the rating passes down to suppliers.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Not established. |
| Codified citation | Not established. |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Not established. |
| Applies to | Persons who receive rated orders [@CL-IND-0142] |

Except as otherwise specified in the section, a person shall accept every rated order received and must fill such orders regardless of any other rated or unrated orders that have been accepted [@CL-IND-0142]. Unless otherwise directed by Commerce, a person shall not accept a rated order for delivery on a specific date if unable to fill the order by that date, but must inform the customer of the earliest date on which delivery can be made and offer to accept the order [@CL-IND-0143]. Unless otherwise directed by Commerce, rated orders may be rejected in the listed cases as long as a supplier does not discriminate among customers, including when the person placing the order is unwilling or unable to meet regularly established terms of sale or payment [@CL-IND-0144].

Except as provided in paragraph (d)(2) of that section, a person must accept or reject a rated order in writing or in electronic format within fifteen (15) working days after receipt of a DO rated order and within ten (10) working days after receipt of a DX rated order [@CL-IND-0145]. That paragraph covers a rated order placed for emergency preparedness requirements, where expedited action is necessary or appropriate and the order includes the prescribed statement; the person must accept or reject that order [@CL-IND-0156]. The minimum times for acceptance or rejection that such orders may specify are six (6) hours after receipt where the order is issued by an authorized person in response to a hazard that has occurred [@CL-IND-0146; @CL-IND-0156], or twelve (12) hours after receipt where the order is issued by an authorized person to prepare for an imminent hazard [@CL-IND-0147; @CL-IND-0156].

A person must schedule operations, including the acquisition of all needed production items, in a timely manner to satisfy the delivery requirements of each rated order [@CL-IND-0157]. DO rated orders must be given production preference over unrated orders, if necessary to meet required delivery dates, even if this requires the diversion of items being processed or ready for delivery against unrated orders, and DX rated orders must likewise be given preference over DO rated orders [@CL-IND-0148]. The required elements of a rated order must be included on each successive order placed to obtain industrial resources needed to fill a customer's rated order [@CL-IND-0149]. A person must use rated orders with suppliers to obtain industrial resources needed to fill a rated order [@CL-IND-0159].

## How it works

In the printed sections the duties fall on a person who receives a rated order [@CL-IND-0142]. The FAR points to the DPAS regulation for acceptance and rejection of rated orders, preferential scheduling and extension of priority ratings (flowdown) [@CL-IND-0155]. A supplier starts from acceptance, rejects only in the mandatory or optional cases, and answers within the DO or DX deadline [@CL-IND-0142; @CL-IND-0143; @CL-IND-0144; @CL-IND-0145]. If it accepts, it schedules operations to satisfy the delivery requirements of the rated order, with DX preferred over DO and DO over unrated work where needed [@CL-IND-0157; @CL-IND-0148]. It then places rated orders with its own suppliers carrying the required elements, and the inclusion of the rating continues from contractor to subcontractor to supplier throughout the entire supply chain [@CL-IND-0149; @CL-IND-0159; @CL-IND-0158]. What a rated order must contain is on [Priorities and allocations: DO and DX rated orders](ind-dpas-rated-orders.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A shop receives a DO rated order calling for delivery on 30 June, but it can only deliver on 15 July. It must not accept the 30 June date. It must tell the customer that 15 July is the earliest date and offer to accept the order on that basis. It must respond in writing or electronically within 15 working days of receipt. A DX rated order would need a response within 10 working days.

## Never confuse

Mandatory rejection, where the supplier cannot meet the date, is not optional rejection, which covers listed cases such as unmet terms of sale or payment [@CL-IND-0143; @CL-IND-0144]. A supplier using the optional cases may not discriminate among customers [@CL-IND-0144]. Passing the rating down is not optional: the required elements must be included on each successive order [@CL-IND-0149].
