## Purpose

Shows when a supplier must accept or may reject a rated order, the response deadlines, how rated orders are scheduled, and how the rating passes down to suppliers.

## The rule

| Applies to | Persons who receive rated orders [@CL-IND-0142] |

Except as otherwise specified in the section, a person shall accept every rated order received and must fill such orders regardless of any other rated or unrated orders that have been accepted [@CL-IND-0142]. Unless otherwise directed by Commerce, a person shall not accept a rated order for delivery on a specific date if unable to fill the order by that date, must inform the customer of the earliest date on which delivery can be made and offer to accept the order on the basis of that date, and scheduling conflicts with previously accepted lower rated or unrated orders are not sufficient reason for rejection under the section [@CL-IND-0143; @CL-IND-0334]. Unless otherwise directed by Commerce, rated orders may be rejected in any of the listed cases as long as a supplier does not discriminate among customers, including if the person placing the order is unwilling or unable to meet regularly established terms of sale or payment [@CL-IND-0144].

Except as provided in paragraph (d)(2) of the section, a person must accept or reject a rated order in writing (hard copy), or in electronic format, within fifteen (15) working days after receipt of a DO rated order and within ten (10) working days after receipt of a DX rated order [@CL-IND-0145]. Under paragraph (d)(2), if a rated order is placed for the purpose of emergency preparedness requirements and expedited action is necessary or appropriate to meet these requirements and the order includes the statement set forth in § 700.12(b), a person must accept or reject the rated order and transmit the acceptance or rejection in writing or in an electronic format within the time specified in the rated order [@CL-IND-0156; @CL-IND-0336]. The minimum times for acceptance or rejection that such orders may specify are six (6) hours after receipt if the order is issued by an authorized person in response to a hazard that has occurred [@CL-IND-0146; @CL-IND-0156], or twelve (12) hours after receipt if the order is issued by an authorized person to prepare for an imminent hazard [@CL-IND-0147; @CL-IND-0156].

A person must schedule operations, including the acquisition of all needed production items, in a timely manner to satisfy the delivery requirements of each rated order [@CL-IND-0157]. Modifying production or delivery schedules is necessary only when required delivery dates for rated orders cannot otherwise be met [@CL-IND-0049]. DO rated orders must be given production preference over unrated orders, if necessary to meet required delivery dates, even if this requires the diversion of items being processed or ready for delivery against unrated orders, and DX rated orders must be given preference over DO rated orders and unrated orders, if necessary to meet required delivery dates, even if this requires the diversion of items being processed or ready for delivery against DO rated orders or unrated orders [@CL-IND-0148; @CL-IND-9000]. The required elements of a rated order outlined in § 700.12 must be included on each successive order placed to obtain industrial resources needed to fill a customer's rated order [@CL-IND-0149]. A person must use rated orders with suppliers to obtain industrial resources needed to fill a rated order, must include all elements of a rated order outlined in section 700.12 on the rated order, and must use the priority rating indicated on the customer's rated order, except as otherwise provided in the part (see § 700.17) or as directed by the Department of Commerce [@CL-IND-0159; @CL-IND-0326].

## How it works

The FAR points to the DPAS regulation for the rules on acceptance and rejection of rated orders, preferential scheduling, and extension of priority ratings (flowdown) from contractors to subcontractors and suppliers [@CL-IND-0155]. Except as otherwise specified in 15 CFR 700.13, a person who receives a rated order accepts it, rejects it in the mandatory or optional cases unless otherwise directed by Commerce, and answers in writing (hard copy) or in electronic format within the DO, DX or emergency preparedness deadline [@CL-IND-0142; @CL-IND-0143; @CL-IND-0144; @CL-IND-0145; @CL-IND-0156; @CL-IND-0336]. The person schedules operations, including the acquisition of all needed production items, in a timely manner to satisfy the delivery requirements of each rated order, modifying production or delivery schedules only when required delivery dates for rated orders cannot otherwise be met, and gives DX rated orders preference over DO rated orders and unrated orders and DO rated orders preference over unrated orders if necessary to meet required delivery dates [@CL-IND-0157; @CL-IND-0049; @CL-IND-0148; @CL-IND-9000]. The person uses rated orders with suppliers to obtain industrial resources needed to fill a rated order, includes the required elements outlined in § 700.12 on each successive order, and uses the priority rating indicated on the customer's rated order except as otherwise provided in the part (see § 700.17) or as directed by the Department of Commerce, and this continues from contractor to subcontractor to supplier throughout the entire supply chain [@CL-IND-0149; @CL-IND-0158; @CL-IND-0159; @CL-IND-0326]. What a rated order must contain is on [Priorities and allocations: DO and DX rated orders](ind-dpas-rated-orders.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A shop receives a DO rated order calling for delivery on 30 June, but it can only deliver on 15 July. It must not accept the 30 June date. It must tell the customer that 15 July is the earliest date and offer to accept the order on that basis. It must respond in writing or electronically within 15 working days of receipt. A DX rated order would need a response within 10 working days.

## Never confuse

Mandatory and optional rejection are not the same posture: under mandatory rejection, unless otherwise directed by Commerce, a person shall not accept a rated order for delivery on a specific date if unable to fill the order by that date, and must inform the customer of the earliest date on which delivery can be made and offer to accept the order on the basis of that date [@CL-IND-0143; @CL-IND-0334]. Under optional rejection, unless otherwise directed by Commerce, rated orders may be rejected in any of the listed cases as long as a supplier does not discriminate among customers, including if the person placing the order is unwilling or unable to meet regularly established terms of sale or payment [@CL-IND-0144]. Under extension of priority ratings, a person must use rated orders with suppliers to obtain industrial resources needed to fill a rated order and must include the required elements of a rated order outlined in § 700.12 on each successive order [@CL-IND-0149; @CL-IND-0159; @CL-IND-0326].
