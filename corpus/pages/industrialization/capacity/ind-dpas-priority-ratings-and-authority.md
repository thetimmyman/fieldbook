## Purpose

Shows who may assign priority ratings under the Defense Priorities and Allocations System regulation, what the DO and DX levels mean, and which categories of items the part does not apply to unless otherwise agreed to by the concerned agencies.

The Department of Commerce has authorized the Delegate Agencies to assign priority ratings to orders for industrial resources needed for use in approved programs [@CL-IND-0163]. The regulation establishes two levels of priority, identified by the rating symbols DO and DX [@CL-IND-0165]. All DO rated orders have equal priority with each other and take preference over unrated orders, and all DX rated orders have equal priority with each other and take preference over DO rated orders and unrated orders [@CL-IND-0166].

A priority rating consists of the rating symbol (DO and DX) and the program identification symbol, such as A1, A7, or N1, and program identification symbols do not in themselves connote any priority [@CL-IND-0165; @CL-IND-0168]. A Directive issued by Commerce takes preference over any DX rated order, DO rated order or unrated order, as stipulated in the Directive [@CL-IND-0167].

## How it works

The Department of Commerce has authorized the Delegate Agencies to assign priority ratings to orders for industrial resources needed for use in approved programs [@CL-IND-0163]. A priority rating consists of the rating symbol (DO and DX) and the program identification symbol, and program identification symbols do not in themselves connote any priority [@CL-IND-0165; @CL-IND-0168]. Unless otherwise agreed to by the concerned agencies, the provisions of the part are not applicable to listed items, including food resources, food resource facilities, livestock resources, veterinary resources, plant health resources, and the domestic distribution of farm equipment and commercial fertilizer [@CL-IND-0164]. ## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A supplier holds three open orders: one marked DX-A1, one marked DO-A1 and one with no rating. The answer: the DX order takes preference over the DO order and the unrated order, and the DO order takes preference over the unrated order. The A1 part names the program and adds no priority.

## Never confuse

The regulation establishes two levels of priority for rated orders, identified by the rating symbols DO and DX, whereas program identification symbols do not in themselves connote any priority [@CL-IND-0165; @CL-IND-0168]. All DO rated orders take preference over unrated orders and all DX rated orders take preference over DO rated orders and unrated orders, while a Directive issued by Commerce takes preference over any DX rated order, DO rated order or unrated order, as stipulated in the Directive [@CL-IND-0166; @CL-IND-0167].
