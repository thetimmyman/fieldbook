## Purpose

Shows who may assign priority ratings under the Defense Priorities and Allocations System regulation, what the DO and DX levels mean, and what limits the printed sections place on the part.

## The rule

| Applies to | Rated orders under the DPAS regulation [@CL-IND-0165] |

The Department of Commerce has authorized the Delegate Agencies to assign priority ratings to orders for industrial resources needed for use in approved programs [@CL-IND-0163]. The regulation establishes two levels of priority, identified by the rating symbols DO and DX [@CL-IND-0165]. All DO rated orders have equal priority with each other and take preference over unrated orders, and all DX rated orders have equal priority with each other and take preference over DO rated orders and unrated orders [@CL-IND-0166].

A priority rating consists of the rating symbol, DO or DX, and the program identification symbol, and program identification symbols do not in themselves connote any priority [@CL-IND-0168]. A Directive issued by Commerce takes preference over any DX rated order, DO rated order or unrated order, as stipulated in the Directive [@CL-IND-0167].

## How it works

Under the printed text, Commerce has authorized the Delegate Agencies to assign priority ratings to orders for industrial resources needed for use in approved programs [@CL-IND-0163]. The rating symbol sets the priority level, and the program identification symbol does not in itself connote any priority [@CL-IND-0168]. The printed sections do not say which agencies are Delegate Agencies. Unless otherwise agreed to by the concerned agencies, the part is not applicable to listed categories, one of which is food resources and related items, and the section lists further categories [@CL-IND-0164]. What a rated order must contain, and the FAR provision and clause for it, are on [Priorities and allocations: DO and DX rated orders](ind-dpas-rated-orders.md). How a rating is changed or cancelled and used with suppliers is on [DPAS: changing ratings and using rated orders with suppliers](ind-dpas-rating-changes-and-use-of-rated-orders.md), what may not be bought with a rated order is on [DPAS: limits on rated orders and requests for rating authority](ind-dpas-rated-order-limits-and-rating-authority.md), and what an allocation order must contain is on [DPAS: allocation orders and their required elements](ind-dpas-allocation-orders.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A supplier holds three open orders: one marked DX-A1, one marked DO-A1 and one with no rating. The answer: the DX order takes preference over the DO order and the unrated order, and the DO order takes preference over the unrated order. The A1 part names the program and adds no priority.

## Never confuse

The DO and DX symbols set priority between rated orders, while the program identification symbol names the program and does not in itself connote any priority [@CL-IND-0168]. A DX rating does not outrank a Directive issued by Commerce, because a Directive takes preference over any DX rated order, DO rated order or unrated order, as stipulated in the Directive [@CL-IND-0167].
