## Purpose

Shows how Commerce prepares to use its allocations authority and what each allocation order must contain.

| Applies to | Allocation orders under the DPAS regulation [@CL-IND-0188] |

Before using its allocations authority to address a supply problem within its resource jurisdiction, the Department of Commerce will develop a plan with listed contents [@CL-IND-0186]. Three listed contents of the plan are a statement of the specific objectives of the allocation action, a list of the materials, services or facilities to be allocated, and a list or description of the sources subject to the action [@CL-IND-0187].

Allocation orders may be issued directly to the affected persons, by constructive notice through publication in the Federal Register, or by publication of a temporary final rule in the Federal Register [@CL-IND-0188]. Every allocation order must include a detailed description of the required allocation action, including its relationship to previously or subsequently received DX rated orders, DO rated orders and unrated orders [@CL-IND-0189]. One listed element of every allocation order is specific start and end calendar dates for each required allocation action [@CL-IND-0190]. An allocation order issued directly to affected persons must include a statement that reads in substance that it is an allocation order certified for national defense use [@CL-IND-0191].

The printed section on the elements of an allocation order was revised, effective August 27, 2026, through August 27, 2027 [@CL-IND-0192]. Check the current text of that section before relying on this list.

## How it works

The source text for this card does not define an allocation order in one sentence; it describes the plan Commerce develops first and the elements each order must include [@CL-IND-0186; @CL-IND-0189]. Commerce plans the action, then issues orders in one of three ways [@CL-IND-0188]. Each order must describe the action and its relationship to rated and unrated orders, and give start and end dates [@CL-IND-0189; @CL-IND-0190]. The printed section sets further elements for each way of issuing an order, and this card states only the direct-issue statement [@CL-IND-0191]. The duties of a person who receives a rated order are on [Priorities and allocations: DO and DX rated orders](ind-dpas-rated-orders.md), and the DO and DX levels are on [DPAS: authority, priority ratings and the DO and DX levels](ind-dpas-priority-ratings-and-authority.md). Relief from an order is on [DPAS: adjustments, exceptions and appeals](ind-dpas-adjustments-exceptions-and-appeals.md), which excludes Directive Allocation Orders issued by a temporary final rule [@CL-IND-0198].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

Commerce issues an allocation order directly to one supplier. The order describes the required action, relates it to DX, DO and unrated orders, gives a start date of 1 March and an end date of 30 June, and carries the national defense statement. The answer: the order has the elements that apply to every order and the statement required for orders issued directly.

## Never confuse

An allocation order is not a rated order. An allocation order comes from Commerce's allocations authority, which Commerce plans before use [@CL-IND-0186], and it must say how it relates to DX rated, DO rated and unrated orders [@CL-IND-0189].
