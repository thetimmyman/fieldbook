## Purpose

Shows how Commerce prepares to use its allocations authority and what each allocation order must contain.

## The rule

| Applies to | Allocation orders issued under the DPAS regulation [@CL-IND-0188] |

Before using its allocations authority to address a supply problem within its resource jurisdiction, the Department of Commerce will develop a plan that includes a statement of the specific objectives of the allocation action, a list of the materials, services, or facilities to be allocated, and a list or description of the sources of the materials, services, or facilities that will be subject to the allocation action [@CL-IND-0186; @CL-IND-0187].

Allocation orders may be issued directly to the affected persons, by constructive notice to the parties through publication in the Federal Register, or by publication of a temporary final rule in the Federal Register [@CL-IND-0188]. Every allocation order must include a detailed description of the required allocation action, including its relationship to previously or subsequently received DX rated orders, DO rated orders and unrated orders, and specific start and end calendar dates for each required allocation action [@CL-IND-0189; @CL-IND-0190]. An allocation order issued directly to affected persons must include a statement that reads in substance: "This is an allocation order certified for national defense use. [Insert the name of the person receiving the order] is required to comply with this order, in accordance with the provisions of the Defense Priorities and Allocations System regulation (15 CFR part 700)." [@CL-IND-0191; @CL-IND-0331]

An allocation order may be changed or cancelled by an official action from the Department of Commerce [@CL-IND-1017]. Notice of such changes or cancellations may be provided directly to persons to whom the order being cancelled or modified applies, or constructive notice may be provided by publication in the Federal Register [@CL-IND-1018].

At 91 FR 50705, August 6, 2026, § 700.34 was revised, effective August 27, 2026, through August 27, 2027 [@CL-IND-0192].

## How it works

Before using its allocations authority to address a supply problem within its resource jurisdiction, the Department of Commerce develops a plan with the listed contents, and then issues allocation orders directly to the affected persons, by constructive notice to the parties through publication in the Federal Register, or by publication of a temporary final rule in the Federal Register [@CL-IND-0186; @CL-IND-0187; @CL-IND-0188]. Every allocation order must include a detailed description of the required allocation action, including its relationship to previously or subsequently received DX rated orders, DO rated orders and unrated orders, and specific start and end calendar dates for each required allocation action, and an order issued directly to affected persons must include the compliance statement for 15 CFR part 700 [@CL-IND-0189; @CL-IND-0190; @CL-IND-0191; @CL-IND-0331]. An allocation order may be changed or cancelled by an official action from the Department of Commerce, with notice provided directly to persons to whom the order being cancelled or modified applies or by constructive notice through publication in the Federal Register [@CL-IND-1017; @CL-IND-1018]. The elements of a rated order are on [Priorities and allocations: DO and DX rated orders](ind-dpas-rated-orders.md), and the DO and DX levels are on [DPAS: authority, priority ratings and the DO and DX levels](ind-dpas-priority-ratings-and-authority.md). Relief from an order is on [DPAS: adjustments, exceptions and appeals](ind-dpas-adjustments-exceptions-and-appeals.md), which applies to the part and to official actions issued under the part except for Directive Allocation Orders issued by a temporary final rule [@CL-IND-0198].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

Commerce issues an allocation order directly to one supplier. The order describes the required action, relates it to DX, DO and unrated orders, gives a start date of 1 March and an end date of 30 June, and carries the national defense statement. The answer: the order has the elements that apply to every order and the statement required for orders issued directly.

## Never confuse

The plan comes before the orders rather than after them: before using its allocations authority to address a supply problem within its resource jurisdiction, the Department of Commerce develops a plan, and every allocation order must include a detailed description of the required allocation action, including its relationship to previously or subsequently received DX rated orders, DO rated orders and unrated orders [@CL-IND-0186; @CL-IND-0189].
