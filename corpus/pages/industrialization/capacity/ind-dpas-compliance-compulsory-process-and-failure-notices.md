## Purpose

Shows what compliance actions the Department of Commerce may take under the priority and allocation rules, when it may seek compulsory process, and what a written notice of failure to comply says.

## The rule

| Applies to | Compliance actions taken for the enforcement or administration of the Defense Production Act, the Selective Service Act and related statutes, this part or an official action [@CL-IND-0309] |

General provisions. Compliance actions may be taken for any reason necessary or appropriate to the enforcement or administration of the Defense Production Act, the Selective Service Act and related statutes, this part or an official action [@CL-IND-0309]. Such actions include audits, investigations or other inquiries [@CL-IND-0310]. A willful violation of any of the provisions of Title I or section 705 of the Defense Production Act, of the provisions of this part, or of an official action of the Department of Commerce is a criminal act, punishable as provided in sections 103 and 705 of the Defense Production Act [@CL-IND-0311].

Compulsory process. If a person refuses to permit a duly authorized representative of the Department of Commerce to have access to any premises or source of information necessary to the administration or enforcement of the Defense Production Act or this part, the Department of Commerce may seek compulsory process [@CL-IND-0312]. Compulsory process means the institution of appropriate legal action, including ex parte application for an inspection warrant or its equivalent, in any forum of appropriate jurisdiction [@CL-IND-0313]. Compulsory process may be sought in advance of an audit, investigation or other inquiry if, in the judgment of the Director of the Office of Strategic Industries and Economic Security, U.S. Department of Commerce, in consultation with the Chief Counsel for Industry and Security, U.S. Department of Commerce, there is reason to believe that a person will refuse to permit an audit, investigation or other inquiry, or that other circumstances exist which make such process desirable or necessary [@CL-IND-0314; @CL-IND-0315].

Notification of failure to comply. At the conclusion of an audit, investigation or other inquiry, or at any other time, the Department of Commerce may inform the person in writing where compliance with the requirements of the Defense Production Act, the Selective Service Act and related statutes, or this part was not met [@CL-IND-0316]. In cases where the Department of Commerce determines that failure to comply with the provisions of the Defense Production Act, the Selective Service Act and related statutes, or this part was inadvertent, the person may be informed in writing of the particulars involved and the corrective action to be taken [@CL-IND-0317; @CL-IND-0318]. Failure to take corrective action may then be construed as a willful violation of the Defense Production Act, this part or an official action [@CL-IND-0319].

Compliance conflicts. If compliance with any provision of the Defense Production Act, the Selective Service Act and related statutes, this regulation, or an official action would prevent a person from filling a rated order or from complying with another provision of the Defense Production Act, this regulation, or an official action, the person must immediately notify the Department of Commerce for resolution of the conflict [@CL-IND-1031; @CL-IND-1032].

## How it works

The Department of Commerce may take compliance actions, including audits, investigations or other inquiries, for any reason necessary or appropriate to the enforcement or administration of the Defense Production Act, the Selective Service Act and related statutes, this part or an official action [@CL-IND-0309; @CL-IND-0310]. When access to any premises or source of information necessary to the administration or enforcement of the Defense Production Act or this part is refused, it may seek compulsory process, which means the institution of appropriate legal action, including ex parte application for an inspection warrant or its equivalent, in any forum of appropriate jurisdiction [@CL-IND-0312; @CL-IND-0313]. Compulsory process may be sought in advance of an audit, investigation or other inquiry if, in the judgment of the Director of the Office of Strategic Industries and Economic Security, U.S. Department of Commerce, in consultation with the Chief Counsel for Industry and Security, U.S. Department of Commerce, there is reason to believe that a person will refuse to permit an audit, investigation or other inquiry, or that other circumstances exist which make such process desirable or necessary [@CL-IND-0314; @CL-IND-0315]. In cases where the Department of Commerce determines that failure to comply with the provisions of the Defense Production Act, the Selective Service Act and related statutes, or this part was inadvertent, the person may be informed in writing of the particulars involved and the corrective action to be taken, and failure to take corrective action may then be construed as a willful violation [@CL-IND-0317; @CL-IND-0318; @CL-IND-0319].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A supplier refuses a duly authorized Commerce representative access to its order log, a source of information necessary to the administration or enforcement of the Defense Production Act or this part. Commerce may seek compulsory process, for example an ex parte application for an inspection warrant in a forum of appropriate jurisdiction [@CL-IND-0312; @CL-IND-0313]. Later, Commerce determines that 2 order-log entries were not rated as required and that the failure was inadvertent. It may inform the supplier in writing of the particulars and the corrective action to be taken [@CL-IND-0317; @CL-IND-0318]. If the supplier then takes no corrective action, that failure may be construed as a willful violation [@CL-IND-0319], and a willful violation is a criminal act, punishable as provided in sections 103 and 705 of the Defense Production Act [@CL-IND-0311].

## Never confuse

Under notification of failure to comply, the Department of Commerce may inform the person in writing where compliance with the requirements of the Defense Production Act, the Selective Service Act and related statutes, or this part was not met, whereas compulsory process means the institution of appropriate legal action, including ex parte application for an inspection warrant or its equivalent, in any forum of appropriate jurisdiction [@CL-IND-0316; @CL-IND-0313]. A willful violation of any of the provisions of Title I or section 705 of the Defense Production Act, of the provisions of this part, or of an official action of the Department of Commerce is a criminal act, punishable as provided in sections 103 and 705 of the Defense Production Act, and when the Department of Commerce informs a person in writing of the particulars and corrective action for an inadvertent failure to comply, failure to take corrective action may then be construed as a willful violation of the Defense Production Act, this part or an official action [@CL-IND-0311; @CL-IND-0317; @CL-IND-0318; @CL-IND-0319].
