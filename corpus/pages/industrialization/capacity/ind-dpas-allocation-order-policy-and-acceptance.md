## Purpose

Sets out when allocation orders may be used, what must happen before the Department of Commerce may control the general distribution of a material in the civilian market, the types of allocation order, and the duties that fall on a person that receives one. The rated order mechanics are on the [rated orders](ind-dpas-rated-orders.md) card.

15 CFR 700.30 states that allocation orders will be used only when there is insufficient supply of a material, service or facility to satisfy national defense requirements through the use of the priorities authority [@CL-IND-0083], or when the use of the priorities authority would cause a severe and prolonged disruption in the supply of materials, services or facilities available to support normal United States economic activities [@CL-IND-0084]. They will not be used to ration materials or services at the retail level [@CL-IND-0084]. When used, they will be distributed equitably among the suppliers of the materials, services or facilities being allocated, and will not require any person to relinquish a disproportionate share of the civilian market [@CL-IND-0085].

15 CFR 700.32 applies to controlling the general distribution of a material in the civilian market, and no allocation action by the Department of Commerce may be used for that purpose unless the conditions of paragraphs (a), (b) and (c) of the section are met [@CL-IND-0086]. Under paragraph (a), the Secretary has made a written finding that the material is a scarce and critical material essential to the national defense, and that the requirements of the national defense for the material cannot otherwise be met without creating a significant dislocation of the normal distribution of the material in the civilian market to such a degree as to create appreciable hardship [@CL-IND-0087; @CL-IND-0088]. Under paragraph (b), the Secretary has submitted the finding for the President's approval through the Assistant to the President and National Security Advisor and the Assistant to the President for Homeland Security and Counterterrorism [@CL-IND-0089]. Under paragraph (c), the President has approved the finding [@CL-IND-0089]. In the section, Secretary means the Secretary of Commerce or his or her designee [@CL-IND-0344].

15 CFR 700.33 lists three types of allocation orders available for communicating allocation actions [@CL-IND-0090]. A set-aside is an official action that requires a person to reserve materials, services or facilities capacity in anticipation of the receipt of rated orders [@CL-IND-0090]. A directive is an official action that requires a person to take or refrain from taking certain actions in accordance with its provisions [@CL-IND-0091]. For example, a directive can require a person to stop or reduce production of an item, prohibit the use of selected materials, services or facilities, or divert the use of materials, services or facilities from one purpose to another [@CL-IND-0345]. An allotment is an official action that specifies the maximum quantity of a material, service or facility authorized for a specific use to promote the national defense [@CL-IND-0092].

## How it works

The duties in 15 CFR 700.35 fall on a person that receives an allocation order [@CL-IND-0093]. Except as otherwise specified in the section, that person shall accept and comply with every allocation order received [@CL-IND-0093]. The person shall not discriminate against an allocation order in any manner, such as by charging higher prices for materials, services or facilities covered by the order [@CL-IND-0094]. Discrimination also includes imposing terms and conditions for contracts and orders involving allocated materials, services or facilities that differ from the person's terms and conditions for contracts and orders for those materials, services or facilities before the person received the allocation order [@CL-IND-0095].

If a person is unable to comply fully with the required actions specified in an allocation order, the person must notify the Office of Strategic Industries and Economic Security immediately [@CL-IND-0096]. The person must also explain the extent to which compliance is possible and give the reasons why full compliance is not possible [@CL-IND-0097]. If the notification is given verbally, written or electronic confirmation must be provided within one working day [@CL-IND-0098]. A working day is any day that the recipient of an order is open for business [@CL-IND-0082]. The notification does not release the person from complying with the order to the fullest extent possible, until the Department of Commerce notifies the person that the order has been changed or cancelled [@CL-IND-0099].

Each type of allocation order is an official action [@CL-IND-0090; @CL-IND-0091; @CL-IND-0092]. The part defines an official action as an action taken by the Department of Commerce under the authority of the Defense Production Act, the Selective Service Act and related statutes, and the part, and adds that such actions include the issuance of set-asides, allotments and directives [@CL-IND-0074; @CL-IND-0075]. The terms are on the [definitions](ind-dpas-definitions.md) card. The acceptance and rejection rules for rated orders are on the [acceptance, rejection and flowdown](ind-dpas-acceptance-rejection-and-flowdown.md) card, and requests for relief are on the [adjustments, exceptions and appeals](ind-dpas-adjustments-exceptions-and-appeals.md) card.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A supplier receives a directive to divert half of its coating line to a defense program for 90 days. A directive can require a person to divert the use of facilities from one purpose to another [@CL-IND-0345]. The supplier accepts the directive, as the section requires except as the section otherwise specifies [@CL-IND-0093]. It can meet two thirds of the diversion but not all of it. It telephones the Office of Strategic Industries and Economic Security the same day, says that it can divert two thirds, and gives the reasons it cannot divert the rest [@CL-IND-0096; @CL-IND-0097]. It sends written confirmation on the next working day, which is within one working day [@CL-IND-0098]. It keeps the achieved diversion in place and continues to comply to the fullest extent possible until Commerce notifies it that the order has been changed or cancelled [@CL-IND-0099]. Charging a higher price for the diverted capacity than it charged before the directive would discriminate against the order [@CL-IND-0094].

## Never confuse

A rated order is a prime contract, a subcontract or a purchase order in support of an approved program issued in accordance with the provisions of the part, whereas an allocation order is an official action to control the distribution of materials, services or facilities for a purpose deemed necessary or appropriate to promote the national defense [@CL-IND-0079; @CL-IND-0064]. A set-aside is a type of allocation order that requires a person to reserve materials, services or facilities capacity in anticipation of the receipt of rated orders [@CL-IND-0090; @CL-IND-0081]. 15 CFR 700.32 states that no allocation action by the Department of Commerce may be used to control the general distribution of a material in the civilian market unless the conditions of paragraphs (a), (b) and (c) of that section are met [@CL-IND-0086]. 15 CFR 700.30 states when allocation orders will be used and how they will be distributed, and 15 CFR 700.35 states the acceptance, non-discrimination and immediate notification duties of a person that receives an allocation order [@CL-IND-0083; @CL-IND-0084; @CL-IND-0085; @CL-IND-0093; @CL-IND-0094; @CL-IND-0096].
