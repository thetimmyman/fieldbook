## Purpose

Shows what counts as a multiyear contract, how long it may run, and who must decide before one is used.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR and codified DFARS [@CL-IND-0100; @CL-IND-0161] |
| Codified citation | FAR 17.103; FAR 17.105-1; DFARS 217.172 [@CL-IND-0100; @CL-IND-0101; @CL-IND-0102; @CL-IND-0161] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-IND-0100] |
| Applies to | Contracts for supplies or services for more than 1, but not more than 5, program years [@CL-IND-0100] |

A multiyear contract is a contract for the purchase of supplies or services for more than 1, but not more than 5, program years [@CL-IND-0100].

Outside DoD, NASA and the Coast Guard, the contracting officer may enter into a multiyear contract if the head of the contracting activity determines, among other things, that the need for the supplies or services is reasonably firm and continuing over the period of the contract [@CL-IND-0101]. The text adds a second determination about the best interests of the United States, which this card does not draft.

For DoD, NASA and the Coast Guard, the head of the agency may enter into a multiyear contract for supplies if the listed conditions are met, the first of which is that the use of such a contract will result in significant savings of the total estimated costs of carrying out the program through annual contracts [@CL-IND-0102]. The DFARS section on multiyear contracts for supplies adds the condition that the use of such a contract will promote the national security of the United States [@CL-IND-0161]. This card does not draft the other DFARS conditions.

## How it works

The approval route depends on the agency. Outside DoD, NASA and the Coast Guard, the head of the contracting activity makes the determination that lets the contracting officer enter into the contract [@CL-IND-0101]. For DoD, NASA and the Coast Guard, the head of the agency may enter into a multiyear contract for supplies if the listed conditions are met [@CL-IND-0102]. Those conditions concern savings, a minimum need that stays substantially unchanged, a stable design with technical risks that are not excessive, a reasonable expectation of funding at a level to avoid cancellation, and realistic estimates [@CL-IND-0102; @CL-IND-0103; @CL-IND-0104; @CL-IND-0105; @CL-IND-0106]. Cancellation ceilings are on [Multiyear cancellation ceilings and nonrecurring costs](ind-multiyear-cancellation-ceilings.md), and notice to Congress before award is on [Multiyear contracts: congressional notification and advance procurement](ind-multiyear-congressional-notification-and-advance-procurement.md). Funding for contracts that run into later fiscal years is a separate topic; see [Contracts conditioned on availability of funds](../../fpa/backlog/fpa-funds-availability-conditioned-contracts.md) and [Contracts crossing fiscal years](../../fpa/forecast-vintage/fpa-contracts-crossing-fiscal-years.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A DoD agency wants to buy a seal kit over four program years. The head of the agency finds that the contract will save about 6 percent against annual contracts, that the minimum need is expected to hold, and that the design is stable. The head of the agency also expects to request funding each year, the cost estimates are realistic, and the use of the contract promotes the national security of the United States. Four program years is more than 1 and not more than 5, and each listed condition is met. The answer: the head of the agency may enter into the multiyear contract for supplies. If the design were still changing, the stable-design condition would not be met.

## Never confuse

The savings condition compares a multiyear contract with carrying out the program through annual contracts [@CL-IND-0102]. Meeting the conditions is also not the same as having funds for every year: the funding condition is a reasonable expectation that the head of the agency will request funding at a level to avoid cancellation [@CL-IND-0105; @CL-IND-0102].
