## Purpose

Shows what counts as a multiyear contract, how long it may run, and who must decide before one is used.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR and codified DFARS [@CL-IND-0100; @CL-IND-0161] |
| Codified citation | FAR 17.103; FAR 17.105-1; DFARS 217.172 [@CL-IND-0100; @CL-IND-0101; @CL-IND-0102; @CL-IND-0161] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-IND-0100] |
| Applies to | Contracts for supplies or services for more than 1, but not more than 5, program years [@CL-IND-0100] |

A multiyear contract is a contract for the purchase of supplies or services for more than 1, but not more than 5, program years [@CL-IND-0100].

Except for DoD, NASA, and the Coast Guard, the contracting officer may enter into a multiyear contract if the head of the contracting activity determines both that the need for the supplies or services is reasonably firm and continuing over the period of the contract and that a multiyear contract will serve the best interests of the United States by encouraging full and open competition or promoting economy in administration, performance, and operation of the agency's programs [@CL-IND-0101; @CL-IND-0409].

For DoD, NASA, and the Coast Guard, the head of the agency may enter into a multiyear contract for supplies if all five conditions in FAR 17.105-1(b) are met, the first of which is that the use of such a contract will result in significant savings of the total estimated costs of carrying out the program through annual contracts [@CL-IND-0102; @CL-IND-0103; @CL-IND-0104; @CL-IND-0105; @CL-IND-0106]. Under DFARS 217.172(b), the head of the agency may enter into a multiyear contract for supplies if, in addition to the conditions listed in FAR 17.105-1(b), the use of such a contract will promote the national security of the United States [@CL-IND-0161].

## How it works

Except for DoD, NASA, and the Coast Guard, the contracting officer may enter into a multiyear contract if the head of the contracting activity makes both required determinations under FAR 17.105-1(a) [@CL-IND-0101; @CL-IND-0409]. For DoD, NASA, and the Coast Guard, the head of the agency may enter into a multiyear contract for supplies if all five conditions in FAR 17.105-1(b) are met: the use of such a contract will result in significant savings of the total estimated costs of carrying out the program through annual contracts [@CL-IND-0102]; the minimum need to be purchased is expected to remain substantially unchanged during the contemplated contract period in terms of production rate, procurement rate, and total quantities [@CL-IND-0103]; there is a stable design for the supplies to be acquired, and the technical risks associated with such supplies are not excessive [@CL-IND-0104]; there is a reasonable expectation that, throughout the contemplated contract period, the head of the agency will request funding for the contract at a level to avoid contract cancellation [@CL-IND-0105]; and the estimates of both the cost of the contract and the cost avoidance through the use of a multiyear contract are realistic [@CL-IND-0106]. Cancellation ceilings are on [Multiyear cancellation ceilings and nonrecurring costs](ind-multiyear-cancellation-ceilings.md), and notice to Congress before award is on [Multiyear contracts: congressional notification and advance procurement](ind-multiyear-congressional-notification-and-advance-procurement.md). Funding for contracts that run into later fiscal years is a separate topic; see [Contracts conditioned on availability of funds](../../fpa/backlog/fpa-funds-availability-conditioned-contracts.md) and [Contracts crossing fiscal years](../../fpa/forecast-vintage/fpa-contracts-crossing-fiscal-years.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A DoD agency wants to buy a seal kit over four program years. The head of the agency finds that the contract will save about 6 percent against annual contracts, that the minimum need is expected to hold, and that the design is stable. The head of the agency also expects to request funding each year, the cost estimates are realistic, and the use of the contract promotes the national security of the United States. Four program years is more than 1 and not more than 5, and each listed condition is met. The answer: the head of the agency may enter into the multiyear contract for supplies. If the design were still changing, the stable-design condition would not be met.

## Never confuse

For DoD, NASA, and the Coast Guard, the savings condition is that the use of such a contract will result in significant savings of the total estimated costs of carrying out the program through annual contracts [@CL-IND-0102], and the funding condition is that there is a reasonable expectation that, throughout the contemplated contract period, the head of the agency will request funding for the contract at a level to avoid contract cancellation [@CL-IND-0105]. If funds are not appropriated to support the succeeding years' requirements, the agency must cancel the contract [@CL-IND-0410].
