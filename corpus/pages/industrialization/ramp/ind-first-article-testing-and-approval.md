## Purpose

Shows when first article testing and approval may be appropriate, when it is normally not required, and when it may be waived.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-IND-0124] |
| Codified citation | FAR 9.303; FAR 9.304; FAR 9.306 [@CL-IND-0124; @CL-IND-0125; @CL-IND-0127; @CL-IND-0128] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-IND-0124] |
| Applies to | Contracts where a first article testing and approval requirement is considered [@CL-IND-0124] |

Testing and approval may be appropriate when, as one listed circumstance, the contractor has not previously furnished the product to the Government [@CL-IND-0124]. Another listed circumstance concerns a contractor that previously furnished the product to the Government, but there have been subsequent changes in processes or specifications, production has been discontinued for an extended period of time, or the product acquired under a previous contract developed a problem during its life [@CL-IND-0125; @CL-IND-0124]. The text also lists the case where the product is described by a performance specification, and the case where it is essential to have an approved first article to serve as a manufacturing standard [@CL-IND-0126; @CL-IND-0124].

Normally, testing and approval is not required in contracts for research or development, for products requiring qualification before award, or for products normally sold in the commercial market, among the listed exceptions [@CL-IND-0127]. The text lists one further exception, for products covered by complete and detailed technical specifications, unless the requirements are so novel or exacting that it is questionable whether the products would meet the requirements without testing and approval [@CL-IND-0127; @CL-IND-1083].

A solicitation containing a testing and approval requirement shall inform offerors that the requirement may be waived when supplies identical or similar to those called for have previously been delivered by the offeror and accepted by the Government [@CL-IND-0160; @CL-IND-0128].

FAR 2.101 defines a first article as a preproduction model, initial production sample, test sample, first lot, pilot lot, or pilot models [@CL-IND-1047]. The RFO model text of FAR 2.101 defines a first article in the same words as the codified definition [@CL-IND-1065; @CL-IND-1047]. It defines first article testing as testing and evaluating the first article for conformance with specified contract requirements before or in the initial stage of production [@CL-IND-1048]. The RFO model text of FAR 2.101 defines first article testing in the same words as the codified definition [@CL-IND-1066; @CL-IND-1048].

## How it works

Use is a judgment: testing and approval may be appropriate in the listed circumstances [@CL-IND-0124; @CL-IND-0125; @CL-IND-0126], and it is normally not required in the listed contract types [@CL-IND-0127]. The solicitation tells offerors that the requirement may be waived when identical or similar supplies were previously delivered by the offeror and accepted by the Government [@CL-IND-0160; @CL-IND-0128]. Who bears the cost of materials and production before approval, and how approval is decided, are on [Contractor risk before first article approval and related procedures](ind-first-article-contractor-risk-and-procedures.md). Acceptance of production supplies is covered separately in [Inspection of Supplies—Fixed-Price clause](../../close-revrec/revenue/cls-inspection-of-supplies-fixed-price.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A buyer solicits a new bracket that supplier A has never furnished to the Government. First article testing and approval may be appropriate, because the contractor has not previously furnished the product. Supplier B bids a catalog valve normally sold in the commercial market, so testing and approval is normally not required for that contract. Supplier C delivered identical brackets that the Government accepted, and the solicitation told offerors the requirement may be waived in that case. The answer: A may be tested, B is normally not, and C may be waived.

## Never confuse

An exception is not a waiver. Exceptions describe contracts where testing and approval is normally not required [@CL-IND-0127], while the waiver notice goes in a solicitation that contains a testing and approval requirement and covers an offeror that has previously delivered identical or similar supplies that the Government accepted [@CL-IND-0160; @CL-IND-0128].
