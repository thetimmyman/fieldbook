## Purpose

Shows how a multiyear cancellation ceiling is built, what it may include, how it falls over the contract, and what the DoD rules limit.

## The rule

| Applies to | Multiyear contracts that carry a cancellation ceiling [@CL-IND-0107] |

The cancellation ceiling is the maximum cancellation charge that the contractor can receive in the event of cancellation [@CL-IND-0107]. The cancellation charge is the amount of unrecovered costs which would have been recouped through amortization over the full term of the contract, including the term canceled [@CL-IND-0107].

Cancellation ceilings must exclude amounts for requirements included in prior program years, and the contracting officer shall reduce the cancellation ceiling for each program year in direct proportion to the remaining requirements subject to cancellation [@CL-IND-0108]. The FAR solicitation rule for multiyear contracts states that the cancellation ceiling shall not be an evaluation factor [@CL-IND-0110].

## How it works

In determining cancellation ceilings, the contracting officer must estimate reasonable preproduction or startup, labor learning, and other nonrecurring costs to be incurred by an average prime contractor or subcontractor, being costs which would be applicable to, and which normally would be amortized over, the items or services to be furnished under the multiyear requirements [@CL-IND-0150; @CL-IND-0335]. Nonrecurring costs include, where applicable, plant or equipment relocation or rearrangement, special tooling and special test equipment, preproduction engineering, initial rework, initial spoilage and pilot runs, among other items [@CL-IND-0109]. The contracting officer reduces the ceiling for each program year in direct proportion to the requirements still subject to cancellation [@CL-IND-0108].

The FAR section headed special procedures applicable to DoD, NASA and the Coast Guard says that contracts awarded under the multiyear procedure shall be firm-fixed-price, fixed-price with economic price adjustment, or fixed-price incentive [@CL-IND-0152; @CL-IND-0111]. Under the same heading, including recurring costs in cancellation ceilings is an exception to normal contract financing arrangements and requires approval by the agency head [@CL-IND-0152; @CL-IND-0112]. The DFARS lists, among its conditions for entering a multiyear contract for supplies, that cancellation provisions do not include consideration of recurring manufacturing costs of the contractor for unfunded units [@CL-IND-0122; @CL-IND-0123]. The FAR rule on recurring costs in cancellation ceilings and the DFARS condition are stated separately, and neither printed section refers to the other, so this card does not reconcile them [@CL-IND-0112; @CL-IND-0123].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A four-year multiyear contract is priced at 100 million. Nonrecurring costs are estimated at 8 million, and the four program years carry 40, 30, 20 and 10 percent of the requirements. The ceilings for the later program years are 4.8 million, 2.4 million and 0.8 million. Each is 8 million times the share of requirements still subject to cancellation (60, 30 and 10 percent), so the ceiling falls as the contract is performed. The ceiling would not be used to rank offers.

## Never confuse

The ceiling is a cap on what the contractor can receive on cancellation, and it is not an evaluation factor [@CL-IND-0107; @CL-IND-0110]. The dollar level at which a ceiling requires notice to Congress is on [Multiyear contracts: congressional notification and advance procurement](ind-multiyear-congressional-notification-and-advance-procurement.md), the approval conditions for a multiyear contract are on [Multiyear contracting](ind-multiyear-contracting.md), and termination cost rules are in [Termination costs](../../close-revrec/inventory-wip/cls-termination-costs.md).

Whether the learning curve and the cost history behind the nonrecurring cost estimates match the program's actual startup costs. [@Q-046]
