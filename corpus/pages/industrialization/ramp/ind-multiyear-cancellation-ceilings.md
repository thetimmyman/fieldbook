## Purpose

Shows how a multiyear cancellation ceiling is built, what it may include, how it falls over the contract, and what the DoD rules limit.

## The rule

The cancellation ceiling is the maximum cancellation charge that the contractor can receive in the event of cancellation [@CL-IND-0107]. The cancellation charge is the amount of unrecovered costs which would have been recouped through amortization over the full term of the contract, including the term canceled [@CL-IND-0107].

All program years except the first are subject to cancellation, and for each program year subject to cancellation, the contracting officer shall establish a cancellation ceiling [@CL-IND-0402]. Cancellation ceilings must exclude amounts for requirements included in prior program years, and the contracting officer shall reduce the cancellation ceiling for each program year in direct proportion to the remaining requirements subject to cancellation [@CL-IND-0108]. Under FAR 17.106-2(g), the cancellation ceiling shall not be an evaluation factor [@CL-IND-0110].

## How it works

In determining cancellation ceilings, the contracting officer must estimate reasonable preproduction or startup, labor learning, and other nonrecurring costs to be incurred by an average prime contractor or subcontractor, which would be applicable to, and which normally would be amortized over, the items or services to be furnished under the multiyear requirements [@CL-IND-0150; @CL-IND-0335]. Nonrecurring costs include such costs, where applicable, as plant or equipment relocation or rearrangement, special tooling and special test equipment, preproduction engineering, initial rework, initial spoilage, pilot runs, allocable portions of the costs of facilities to be acquired or established for the conduct of the work, costs incurred for the assembly, training, and transportation to and from the job site of a specialized work force, and unrealized labor learning [@CL-IND-0109; @CL-IND-0403]. They shall not include any costs of labor or materials, or other expenses, except as indicated in FAR 17.106-1(c)(2), which might be incurred for performance of subsequent program year requirements [@CL-IND-0404]. The contracting officer shall reduce the cancellation ceiling for each program year in direct proportion to the remaining requirements subject to cancellation [@CL-IND-0108].

Under FAR 17.106-3, headed Special procedures applicable to DoD, NASA, and the Coast Guard, contracts awarded under the multiyear procedure shall be firm-fixed-price, fixed-price with economic price adjustment, or fixed-price incentive [@CL-IND-0152; @CL-IND-0111]. Under the same section, the inclusion of recurring costs in cancellation ceilings is an exception to normal contract financing arrangements and requires approval by the agency head [@CL-IND-0152; @CL-IND-0112]. Under DFARS 217.172(e), the head of the agency shall not enter into a multiyear contract unless, among other required conditions, cancellation provisions in the contract do not include consideration of recurring manufacturing costs of the contractor associated with the production of unfunded units to be delivered under the contract [@CL-IND-0122; @CL-IND-0123].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A four-year multiyear contract is priced at 100 million. Nonrecurring costs are estimated at 8 million, and the four program years carry 40, 30, 20 and 10 percent of the requirements. The ceilings for the later program years are 4.8 million, 2.4 million and 0.8 million. Each is 8 million times the share of requirements still subject to cancellation (60, 30 and 10 percent), so the ceiling falls as the contract is performed. The ceiling would not be used to rank offers.

## Never confuse

The cancellation ceiling is the maximum cancellation charge that the contractor can receive in the event of cancellation, and it shall not be an evaluation factor [@CL-IND-0107; @CL-IND-0110]. The dollar level at which a ceiling requires notice to Congress is on [Multiyear contracts: congressional notification and advance procurement](ind-multiyear-congressional-notification-and-advance-procurement.md), the approval conditions for a multiyear contract are on [Multiyear contracting](ind-multiyear-contracting.md), and termination cost rules are in [Termination costs](../../close-revrec/inventory-wip/cls-termination-costs.md).
