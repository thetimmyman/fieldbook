## Purpose

Shows who bears the cost of materials and production before first article approval, how a contracting officer can authorize early spending, and what the solicitation and administration steps are.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-IND-0129] |
| Codified citation | FAR 9.305; FAR 9.306; FAR 9.307 [@CL-IND-0129; @CL-IND-0130; @CL-IND-0131; @CL-IND-0132; @CL-IND-0133] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-IND-0129] |
| Applies to | Contracts with a first article testing and approval requirement [@CL-IND-0129] |

Before first article approval, the acquisition of materials or components, or commencement of production, is normally at the sole risk of the contractor [@CL-IND-0129]. To minimize this risk, the contracting officer shall provide sufficient time in the delivery schedule for acquisition of materials and components, and for production after receipt of first article approval [@CL-IND-0153].

When Government requirements preclude giving that time, the contracting officer may, before approval of the first article, authorize the contractor to acquire specific materials or components or commence production to the extent essential to meet the delivery schedule [@CL-IND-0130; @CL-IND-0153]. Costs incurred based on that authorization are allocable to the contract for progress payments and for termination settlements if the contract is terminated for the convenience of the Government [@CL-IND-0131; @CL-IND-0130].

## How it works

Risk sits with the contractor by default until approval [@CL-IND-0129]. The contracting officer is to build enough time into the delivery schedule, and can authorize early acquisition or production to the extent essential to meet the delivery schedule when requirements preclude that time [@CL-IND-0153; @CL-IND-0130]. Costs incurred on that authorization are allocable for progress payments and termination settlements [@CL-IND-0131]. Where the contractor is to be responsible for the first article approval testing, a solicitation containing a testing and approval requirement shall provide the performance or other characteristics that the first article must meet for approval, among other listed items [@CL-IND-0132]. The Government laboratory or other activity responsible for first article testing or evaluation shall inform the contracting office whether to approve, conditionally approve, or disapprove the first article [@CL-IND-0133]. The contracting officer shall then notify the contractor of the action taken and furnish a copy of the notice to the contract administration office [@CL-IND-9006]. When testing and approval is used, and when it may be waived, are on [First article testing and approval](ind-first-article-testing-and-approval.md). Progress payment inventory and termination costs are covered on [Government title to progress payment inventory](../../close-revrec/inventory-wip/cls-government-title-progress-payment-inventory.md) and [Termination costs](../../close-revrec/inventory-wip/cls-termination-costs.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A delivery schedule leaves no time to buy long-lead material after first article approval. The contracting officer authorizes the contractor to buy 2 million of specific material before approval, to the extent essential to meet the delivery schedule. The answer: without that authorization, the purchase is normally at the contractor's sole risk. With it, the 2 million is allocable to the contract for progress payments and, if the contract is terminated for the convenience of the Government, for termination settlements.

## Never confuse

Authorization to buy early is not first article approval: the responsible activity informs the contracting office whether to approve, conditionally approve, or disapprove, and the contracting officer then notifies the contractor of the action taken [@CL-IND-0133; @CL-IND-9006]. The text grants allocability for progress payments and termination settlements [@CL-IND-0131]; the printed sections do not say what happens to those costs if the first article is disapproved.
