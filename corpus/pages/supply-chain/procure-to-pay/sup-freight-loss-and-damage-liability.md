## Purpose

Sets out the liability standard for freight lost or damaged in the movement, the household goods notice deadline and indemnity rate, and how the household goods clause differs from the clause for other freight.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-SUP-2308] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.247-22 and FAR 52.247-23 [@CL-SUP-2308; @CL-SUP-2310] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.247-22 [@CL-SUP-0382]; RFO FAR 52.247-23 [@CL-SUP-0383; @CL-SUP-0384] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-2308] |
| Applies to | The freight clause in solicitations and contracts for the transportation of freight other than household goods [@CL-SUP-2309]; the household goods clause, which the prescription directs to be inserted [@CL-SUP-2352] |

Except when loss or damage arises out of causes beyond the contractor's control and without its fault or negligence, the contractor assumes full liability for all goods lost or damaged in the movement covered by the contract [@CL-SUP-2308]. That is the freight clause, which is inserted in solicitations and contracts for the transportation of freight other than household goods [@CL-SUP-2309].

The household goods clause uses the same exception. Except when loss or damage arises out of causes beyond the contractor's control and without its fault or negligence, the contractor is liable to the owner for loss of or damage to any article while it is in a circumstance that the paragraph lists [@CL-SUP-2310]. The listed circumstances are an article being packed, picked up, loaded, transported, delivered, unloaded or unpacked, stored in transit, or serviced (appliances, etc.) by a third person hired by the contractor to perform the servicing [@CL-SUP-2311].

The contractor is liable for loss or damage discovered by the owner if written notice of it is dispatched to the contractor not later than 75 days following the date of delivery [@CL-SUP-2312]. The contractor indemnifies the owner of the goods at a rate, stated in cents per pound per article, that the clause leaves blank for completion [@CL-SUP-2313]. That rate is a bracketed fill-in and is specific to each contract [@CL-SUP-2313].

## How it works

The freight clause puts full liability on the contractor for goods lost or damaged in the movement, with one exception: causes beyond its control and without its fault or negligence [@CL-SUP-2308]. Both conditions of the exception must be met, because the clause joins them with the word and [@CL-SUP-2308]. The household goods clause owes the liability to the owner while the article is in any one of the listed circumstances: being packed, picked up, loaded, transported, delivered, unloaded or unpacked, stored in transit, or serviced by a third person the contractor hired to do the servicing [@CL-SUP-2310; @CL-SUP-2311]. It adds a notice rule, which looks to the dispatch of written notice within 75 days of delivery [@CL-SUP-2312], and an indemnity rate per pound per article [@CL-SUP-2313].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text under Class Deviation 2026-O0038, effective February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. RFO FAR 52.247-22 keeps the rule that, except when loss and/or damage arises out of causes beyond the control and without the fault or negligence of the contractor, the contractor assumes full liability for any and all goods lost and/or damaged in the movement covered by the contract [@CL-SUP-0382; @CL-SUP-2308]. RFO FAR 52.247-23(a) keeps the same exception, with the contractor liable to the owner for loss of or damage to any article while it is in a circumstance that the paragraph lists [@CL-SUP-0383; @CL-SUP-2310], the listed circumstances being an article packed, picked up, loaded, transported, delivered, unloaded or unpacked, stored in transit, or serviced (appliances, etc.) by a third person hired by the contractor to perform the servicing [@CL-SUP-0384; @CL-SUP-2311]. Paragraph (b) keeps the notice rule, under which the contractor is liable for loss or damage discovered by the owner if written notice of it is dispatched to the contractor not later than 75 days following the date of delivery [@CL-SUP-0385; @CL-SUP-2312], and paragraph (c) keeps the indemnity rate, in cents per pound per article, that the clause leaves blank for completion [@CL-SUP-0386; @CL-SUP-2313].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor moves four pallets of general freight and a driver mishandles the load, damaging two pallets. The contractor assumes full liability, because the damage did not arise from a cause beyond its control. Had a flood beyond the contractor's control destroyed the pallets, without its fault or negligence, the exception would apply.

A household goods article weighing 50 pounds is delivered, and the owner finds it cracked 60 days later. The owner sends written notice on day 70, which is not later than 75 days after delivery, so the notice rule is met. The contract rate in the blank is 60 cents per pound per article, so the indemnity for that article is 3,000 cents, or 30 dollars.

## Never confuse

Liability for the goods is not liability for injury to people or damage to other property from the contractor's vehicles or equipment, which sits under a different clause [@CL-SUP-2303; @CL-SUP-2308]. The freight clause covers freight other than household goods [@CL-SUP-2309]. The household goods clause adds a notice rule and an indemnity rate [@CL-SUP-2312; @CL-SUP-2313]. The DFARS has its own liability clauses, covered on a separate card.
