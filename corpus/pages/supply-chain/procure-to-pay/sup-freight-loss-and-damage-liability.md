## Purpose

Sets out the liability standard for freight lost or damaged in the movement, the household goods notice deadline and indemnity rate, and how the household goods clause differs from the clause for other freight.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-SUP-2308] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.247-22 and FAR 52.247-23 [@CL-SUP-2308; @CL-SUP-2310] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.247-22 [@CL-SUP-0382]; RFO FAR 52.247-23 [@CL-SUP-0383; @CL-SUP-0384] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-2308] |
| Applies to | The freight clause in solicitations and contracts for the transportation of freight other than household goods [@CL-SUP-2309]; the household goods clause, which the prescription directs to be inserted [@CL-SUP-2352] |

Except when loss and/or damage arises out of causes beyond the control and without the fault or negligence of the contractor, the contractor shall assume full liability for any and all goods lost and/or damaged in the movement covered by the contract [@CL-SUP-2308]. That is the freight clause, which is inserted in solicitations and contracts for the transportation of freight other than household goods [@CL-SUP-2309].

Under the household goods clause, except when loss and/or damage arise out of causes beyond the control and without the fault or negligence of the contractor, the contractor shall be liable to the owner for the loss of and/or damage to any article while being in one of three circumstances [@CL-SUP-2310]: packed, picked up, loaded, transported, delivered, unloaded or unpacked; stored in transit; or serviced (appliances, etc.) by a third person hired by the contractor to perform the servicing [@CL-SUP-2311].

The contractor shall be liable for loss and/or damage discovered by the owner if written notice of it is dispatched to the contractor not later than 75 days following the date of delivery [@CL-SUP-2312]. The contractor shall indemnify the owner of the goods at a rate of ___ cents per pound per article, a rate the clause leaves blank for completion [@CL-SUP-2313].

## How it works

The freight clause puts full liability on the contractor for goods lost and/or damaged in the movement, with one exception [@CL-SUP-2308]. The exception covers loss and/or damage arising out of causes that are both beyond the control and without the fault or negligence of the contractor [@CL-SUP-2308]. The household goods clause, except when loss and/or damage arise out of causes beyond the control and without the fault or negligence of the contractor, makes the contractor liable to the owner while the article is in any one of the listed circumstances: packed, picked up, loaded, transported, delivered, unloaded or unpacked; stored in transit; or serviced by a third person the contractor hired to do the servicing [@CL-SUP-2310; @CL-SUP-2311]. Its paragraph (b) makes the contractor liable for loss and/or damage discovered by the owner if written notice is dispatched to the contractor not later than 75 days following the date of delivery [@CL-SUP-2312], and its paragraph (c) has the contractor indemnify the owner of the goods at a rate of ___ cents per pound per article [@CL-SUP-2313].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text under Class Deviation 2026-O0038, effective February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. RFO FAR 52.247-22 provides that, except when loss and/or damage arises out of causes beyond the control and without the fault or negligence of the contractor, the contractor shall assume full liability for any and all goods lost and/or damaged in the movement covered by the contract [@CL-SUP-0382]. RFO FAR 52.247-23(a) provides that, except when loss and/or damage arise out of causes beyond the control and without the fault or negligence of the contractor, the contractor shall be liable to the owner for the loss of and/or damage to any article while being [@CL-SUP-0383] packed, picked up, loaded, transported, delivered, unloaded or unpacked; stored in transit; or serviced (appliances, etc.) by a third person hired by the contractor to perform the servicing [@CL-SUP-0384]. Paragraph (b) provides that the contractor shall be liable for loss and/or damage discovered by the owner if written notice of it is dispatched to the contractor not later than 75 days following the date of delivery [@CL-SUP-0385], and paragraph (c) provides that the contractor shall indemnify the owner of the goods at a rate of ___ cents per pound per article [@CL-SUP-0386].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor moves four pallets of general freight and a driver mishandles the load, damaging two pallets. The contractor assumes full liability, because the damage did not arise from a cause beyond its control. Had a flood beyond the contractor's control destroyed the pallets, without its fault or negligence, the exception would apply.

A household goods article weighing 50 pounds is delivered, and the owner finds it cracked 60 days later. The owner sends written notice on day 70, which is not later than 75 days after delivery, so the notice rule is met. The contract rate in the blank is 60 cents per pound per article, so the indemnity for that article is 3,000 cents, or 30 dollars.

## Never confuse

Responsibility for all damage or injury to persons or property occasioned through the use, maintenance and operation of the contractor's vehicles or other equipment by, or the action of, the contractor or its employees and agents is assumed under FAR 52.247-21(a) [@CL-SUP-2303], unlike the clause on goods lost and/or damaged in the movement [@CL-SUP-2308], where the freight clause covers freight other than household goods [@CL-SUP-2309] and the household goods clause has a notice rule and an indemnity rate [@CL-SUP-2312; @CL-SUP-2313].
