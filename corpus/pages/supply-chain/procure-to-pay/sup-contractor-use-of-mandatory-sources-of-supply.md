## Purpose

Shows which sources the contractor must buy from for Government use, what it must do when a mandatory source cannot deliver, and where price, delivery and payment fit.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-SUP-2944] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.208-9 [@CL-SUP-2944] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-2944] |
| Applies to | Contracts where the schedule names mandatory sources for supplies or services provided for Government use [@CL-SUP-2946] |

Certain supplies or services to be provided under the contract for use by the Government are required by law to be obtained from nonprofit agencies participating in the program operated by the Committee for Purchase From People Who Are Blind or Severely Disabled [@CL-SUP-2944]. Additionally, certain of these supplies are available from the Defense Logistics Agency, the General Services Administration or the Department of Veterans Affairs [@CL-SUP-2945]. The contractor obtains mandatory supplies or services from the specific sources indicated in the contract schedule [@CL-SUP-2946]. Those sources are contract-specific.

The contractor immediately notifies the Contracting Officer if a mandatory source is unable to provide the supplies or services by the time required, or if the quality of what it provides is unsatisfactory [@CL-SUP-2947]. The contractor does not purchase the supplies or services from other sources until the Contracting Officer has notified it that the Committee or an AbilityOne central nonprofit agency has authorized purchase from other sources [@CL-SUP-2948].

Price and delivery information for mandatory supplies obtained through the distribution facilities of the Defense Logistics Agency, the General Services Administration or the Department of Veterans Affairs is available from the Contracting Officer [@CL-SUP-2987]. For mandatory supplies or services not available from those three agencies, price and delivery information is available from the appropriate central nonprofit agency [@CL-SUP-2988]. Payments are made directly to the source making delivery [@CL-SUP-2989]. The clause also lists points of contact for AbilityOne central nonprofit agencies, with an address and telephone number for each; the clause text has them [@CL-SUP-2990].

## How it works

The schedule is the operative list: the law names the nonprofit agency program, and the contractor is bound to the specific sources the schedule indicates [@CL-SUP-2944; @CL-SUP-2946].

A delivery or quality failure opens a notification duty rather than a free choice. The release to another source comes only after the Contracting Officer reports that the Committee or a central nonprofit agency has authorized it [@CL-SUP-2947; @CL-SUP-2948].

Where to ask about price and delivery depends on the route: the Contracting Officer for supplies obtained through the three agencies' distribution facilities, and the appropriate central nonprofit agency for mandatory items not available from them [@CL-SUP-2987; @CL-SUP-2988].

Payments go directly to the source making delivery [@CL-SUP-2989].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

The schedule lists a custodial supply item to be bought from a central nonprofit agency, which is not available from the three distribution agencies, so the contractor gets price and delivery information from that agency. The agency says it cannot ship for eight weeks, which is later than the delivery time required. The contractor notifies the Contracting Officer the same day. It does not buy elsewhere until the Contracting Officer reports that the Committee or an AbilityOne central nonprofit agency has authorized purchase from other sources. After that notice it buys 5,000 units from another supplier and pays that supplier directly.

## Never confuse

A mandatory source is not a preferred one: the contractor may not switch to another source on its own, and may buy elsewhere only after the Contracting Officer has notified it of the authorization [@CL-SUP-2946; @CL-SUP-2948]. Certain of these supplies are also available from the Defense Logistics Agency, the General Services Administration or the Department of Veterans Affairs, which changes where price and delivery information comes from and not who the mandatory source is [@CL-SUP-2945; @CL-SUP-2987; @CL-SUP-2988]. Buying practice beyond this clause sits with [Contractor purchasing system](sup-contractor-purchasing-system.md).

## Company calibration

Whether each Government-use purchase is checked against the mandatory sources in the schedule before a buyer places the order. [@Q-022]
