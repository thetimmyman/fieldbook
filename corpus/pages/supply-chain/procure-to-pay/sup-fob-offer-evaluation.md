## Purpose

Shows how the Government compares f.o.b. origin and f.o.b. destination offers and what shipping point data an offeror gives.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-SUP-1995; @CL-SUP-1998; @CL-SUP-1999] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.247-45 to 52.247-47 [@CL-SUP-1995; @CL-SUP-1998; @CL-SUP-1999] |
| RFO citation | No RFO equivalent for FAR 52.247-45, FAR 52.247-46 and FAR 52.247-47: the RFO text lists each as [Reserved] [@CL-SUP-0273; @CL-SUP-0274; @CL-SUP-0275] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-1995; @CL-SUP-1998; @CL-SUP-1999] |
| Applies to | Solicitations that include the f.o.b. origin and f.o.b. destination evaluation provisions [@CL-SUP-1995; @CL-SUP-1998; @CL-SUP-1999] |

Where offers are invited on both bases, the Government will award on the basis the Contracting Officer determines to be most advantageous [@CL-SUP-1995]. An offer on only one basis is acceptable and is evaluated only on that basis [@CL-SUP-1996]. For f.o.b. origin offers, the Government normally uses land methods by regulated common carrier within the contiguous United States and considers only these methods to establish the cost of transportation [@CL-SUP-1997], measured between the offeror's shipping point and the destination (tentative or firm, whichever is applicable) in the contiguous United States [@CL-SUP-2085]. That transportation cost is added to the offer price to determine the Government's overall cost [@CL-SUP-1998]. When methods other than land transportation are appropriate, the evaluation provision is modified accordingly [@CL-SUP-2087]. The Government uses tentative destinations only for evaluation and may use any other means of transportation or any other destination at the time of shipment [@CL-SUP-2086].

If an offeror designates more than one shipping point or plant and does not state the quantity per point before bid opening, the offer is evaluated on delivery of the entire quantity [@CL-SUP-1999] from the point or plant where transportation cost is most favorable to the Government [@CL-SUP-2000]. If the offeror fails to indicate any shipping point or plant before bid opening (or the closing date for receipt of offers), the offer is evaluated on delivery from the plant at which the contract will be performed, as indicated in the offer [@CL-SUP-2088]. If no plant is indicated, it is evaluated on delivery from the contractor's business address indicated in the offer [@CL-SUP-2089]. If the offeror uses a different shipping point than the one the Government used to evaluate offers, any increase in transportation cost is the contractor's and any savings revert to the Government [@CL-SUP-2001].

## How it works

Evaluation puts offers on one footing by adding the transportation cost to an f.o.b. origin offer price [@CL-SUP-1998]. The offeror's own data decides which shipping point is used. The most-favorable-point rule applies when the offeror designates more than one point and does not state quantities per point before bid opening [@CL-SUP-1999; @CL-SUP-2000], and the plant or business-address rules apply when no shipping point is indicated [@CL-SUP-2088; @CL-SUP-2089]. If the contractor ships from a different point than the one used in evaluation, it bears any increase in transportation cost [@CL-SUP-2001].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text from February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. The RFO text lists FAR 52.247-45 [@CL-SUP-0273], FAR 52.247-46 [@CL-SUP-0274] and FAR 52.247-47 [@CL-SUP-0275] as [Reserved].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

Offer A is f.o.b. origin at 1,000 per lot from plant X, and the land common carrier cost from plant X to the tentative destination in the contiguous United States is 60, so it is evaluated at 1,060. Offer B is f.o.b. destination at 1,050 and is evaluated on that basis. Offer B has the lower evaluated cost. Now suppose offer A had designated two plants without stating quantities before bid opening. The Government would evaluate the entire quantity from the plant with the most favorable transportation cost. If the contractor then ships from the other plant and transportation costs 15 more, the contractor bears the 15. If it costs 10 less, the 10 reverts to the Government. A third offeror indicates no shipping point or plant before bid opening, but its offer says the contract will be performed at plant Z, so the Government evaluates delivery from plant Z. At shipment the Government may still use a destination other than the tentative one. This example follows the codified provisions, not the RFO text, which lists FAR 52.247-45, FAR 52.247-46 and FAR 52.247-47 as [Reserved] [@CL-SUP-0273; @CL-SUP-0274; @CL-SUP-0275].

## Never confuse

The shipping point used in evaluation is not the same thing as the shipping point the contractor uses. If they differ, the contractor bears any increase in transportation cost and any savings revert to the Government [@CL-SUP-2001]. A tentative destination is used only for evaluation, and the Government may use any other destination at shipment [@CL-SUP-2086]. The codified text prints provisions on evaluating f.o.b. origin and f.o.b. destination offers, on the shipping point used in evaluation and on evaluation of f.o.b. origin offers [@CL-SUP-1995; @CL-SUP-1999; @CL-SUP-1998], while the RFO text lists all three numbers as [Reserved] [@CL-SUP-0273; @CL-SUP-0274; @CL-SUP-0275].

## Company calibration

Confirm which shipping points the company offers from and whether it states quantities per point before bid opening. [@Q-022]
