## Purpose

Sets out the two import delivery terms, where delivery happens in each, the duties, fees and landing charges the contractor pays, and when its responsibility for loss or damage ends.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-SUP-2343] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.247-39 and FAR 52.247-40 [@CL-SUP-2343; @CL-SUP-2346] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.247-39 [@CL-SUP-0401]; RFO part 52 lists 52.247-40 as reserved [@CL-SUP-0407] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-2343] |
| Applies to | Solicitations and contracts when the delivery term is f.o.b. inland point, country of importation [@CL-SUP-2349]; or ex dock, pier, or warehouse, port of importation [@CL-SUP-2350] |

F.o.b. inland point, country of importation, means free of expense to the Government, on board the indicated type of conveyance of the carrier, delivered to the specified inland point where the consignee's facility is located [@CL-SUP-2343]. The clause is inserted in solicitations and contracts when that is the delivery term [@CL-SUP-2349]. The contractor packs and marks the shipment to comply with contract specifications or, in the absence of specifications, prepares it for ocean transportation in conformance with carrier requirements to protect the goods [@CL-SUP-2386]. It delivers the shipment, in or on the inland carrier's conveyance, in good order and condition to the specified inland point where the consignee's facility is located [@CL-SUP-2387]. It pays and bears all applicable charges incurred up to the point of delivery, including transportation costs, export, import or other fees or taxes, costs of landing, wharfage costs, customs duties and costs of certificates of origin [@CL-SUP-2344]. Those charges also include consular invoices and other documents that may be required for importation [@CL-SUP-2391]. The contractor is responsible for loss of or damage to the goods until their arrival on or in the carrier's conveyance at the specified inland point [@CL-SUP-2345].

Ex dock, pier, or warehouse, port of importation, means free of expense to the Government delivered on the designated dock or pier or in the warehouse at the specified port of importation [@CL-SUP-2346]. The clause is inserted in solicitations and contracts when that is the delivery term [@CL-SUP-2350]. The contractor packs and marks the shipment to comply with contract specifications or, in the absence of specifications, prepares it for ocean transportation in conformance with carrier requirements to protect the goods [@CL-SUP-2388]. It delivers the shipment in good order and condition [@CL-SUP-2389]. It pays and bears all charges up to the point of delivery specified in the contract, including transportation costs, export, import or other fees or taxes, and costs of wharfage and landing if any [@CL-SUP-2347]. Those charges also include customs duties and costs of certificates of origin, consular invoices, or other documents that may be required for exportation or importation [@CL-SUP-2392]. The contractor is responsible for loss of or damage to the goods occurring before delivery of the shipment to the point of delivery specified in the contract [@CL-SUP-2348].

## How it works

Under both terms the contractor pays charges up to the point of delivery, and it answers for loss or damage up to that point [@CL-SUP-2344; @CL-SUP-2345; @CL-SUP-2347; @CL-SUP-2348]. The two terms differ in where that point is. Under the inland point term it is the specified inland point where the consignee's facility is located [@CL-SUP-2343]. Under the ex dock term it is the designated dock or pier or the warehouse at the specified port of importation [@CL-SUP-2346]. Both clauses put export, import or other fees or taxes and customs duties among the charges the contractor pays [@CL-SUP-2344; @CL-SUP-2392; @CL-SUP-2347].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text from February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. The RFO inland point clause keeps the inland point rules described above. Its prescription says to insert the clause in solicitations and contracts when the delivery term is f.o.b. inland point, country of importation [@CL-SUP-0400; @CL-SUP-2349]. It defines the term as free of expense to the Government, on board the indicated type of conveyance of the carrier, delivered to the specified inland point where the consignee's facility is located [@CL-SUP-0401; @CL-SUP-2343]. Paragraph (b) provides that the Contractor shall do what paragraphs (b)(1) through (b)(3) list [@CL-SUP-9120]. The Contractor packs and marks the shipment to comply with contract specifications or, in the absence of specifications, prepares it for ocean transportation in conformance with carrier requirements to protect the goods [@CL-SUP-0402; @CL-SUP-2386], and delivers it, in or on the inland carrier's conveyance, in good order and condition to the specified inland point [@CL-SUP-0403; @CL-SUP-2387]. The Contractor pays and bears all applicable charges incurred up to the point of delivery, including transportation costs; export, import, or other fees or taxes; costs of landing; wharfage costs; customs duties and costs of certificates of origin; consular invoices; and other documents that may be required for importation [@CL-SUP-0404; @CL-SUP-0405; @CL-SUP-2344; @CL-SUP-2391], and is responsible for any loss of and/or damage to the goods until their arrival on or in the carrier's conveyance at the specified inland point [@CL-SUP-0406; @CL-SUP-2345]. The RFO part 52 text lists section 52.247-40, the ex dock clause, as [Reserved] [@CL-SUP-0407]; the ex dock rules described above come from the codified clause text [@CL-SUP-2346].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An import shipment incurs 2,000 dollars of customs duties, 300 dollars of landing costs and 200 dollars of wharfage at the port, and then 900 dollars of transportation inland to the consignee's facility.

Under the inland point term, the contractor pays all of those charges, 3,400 dollars in total, because they are incurred up to the point of delivery at the consignee's facility. If the goods are damaged on the inland leg before they arrive on the carrier's conveyance at the inland point, the loss is the contractor's.

Under the ex dock term, the point of delivery is the designated dock at the port. The contractor pays the duties, landing and wharfage, 2,500 dollars in total, and the 900 dollars of inland transportation is a charge after that point and not a charge up to it. If the goods are damaged on the dock before they are delivered there, the loss is the contractor's. That ex dock paragraph applies the codified clause text; the RFO text lists 52.247-40 as [Reserved] [@CL-SUP-0407].

## Never confuse

Ex dock, pier or warehouse ends at the port of importation, while f.o.b. inland point runs inland to the consignee's facility [@CL-SUP-2346; @CL-SUP-2343]. The inland point term is not the domestic f.o.b. destination term, which has its own card, and neither import term is an export term: the export terms end at a vessel or at the point of exportation, on the card for them. In RFO part 52, 52.247-39 defines the inland point term [@CL-SUP-0401], while 52.247-40 is [Reserved] [@CL-SUP-0407] and the codified 52.247-40 defines the ex dock term [@CL-SUP-2346].

## Company calibration

Confirm which import delivery term each order carries, the delivery point specified in each contract, and that duties, landing charges and inland transportation are priced up to that point. [@Q-004]
