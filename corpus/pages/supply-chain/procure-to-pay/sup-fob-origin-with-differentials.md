## Purpose

Sets out how an f.o.b. origin delivery term works with priced differentials for mode, vehicle type or delivery place, what the contractor does at loading, what its bill of lading shows, and what loss or damage it answers for.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-SUP-2324] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.247-33 [@CL-SUP-2324] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-2324] |
| Applies to | Contracts containing the F.o.b. Origin, With Differentials clause [@CL-SUP-2324] |

F.o.b. origin, with differentials, means delivery free of expense to the Government on board the indicated type of conveyance of the carrier (or of the Government, if specified) at a designated point in the city, county and State from which the shipments will be made [@CL-SUP-2324], a point from which line-haul transportation service (as distinguished from switching, local drayage or other terminal service) will begin [@CL-SUP-2356]. It also covers delivery to, and placement on, the carrier's wharf (at shipside, within reach of the ship's loading tackle, when the shipping point is within a port area having water transportation service) or the carrier's freight station [@CL-SUP-2357], and delivery to a United States Postal Service facility [@CL-SUP-2358]. If stated in the solicitation, it also covers delivery to any Government-designated point located within the same city or commercial zone as the f.o.b. origin point specified in the contract [@CL-SUP-2359].

Differentials for mode of transportation, type of vehicle or place of delivery, as indicated in the contractor's offer, may be added to the contract price [@CL-SUP-2325]. They are expressed as a rate in cents for each 100 pounds of the supplies, for one or more of the options under the clause that the Government may specify at the time of shipment [@CL-SUP-2326]. The clause provides a blank for the offeror's differentials in cents per hundredweight, for optional modes, vehicle types, transportation within a mode, or places of delivery that the Government specifies at the time of shipment and that are not included in the f.o.b. origin price the offeror indicates in the Schedule [@CL-SUP-2371]. The blank is specific to each contract [@CL-SUP-2371].

If, at the time of shipment, the Government specifies a mode of transportation, type of vehicle or place of delivery for which the offeror set a differential, the contractor includes the total of those differential costs [@CL-SUP-2328]. That total is the applicable differential multiplied by the actual weight, and the contractor includes it as a separate reimbursable item on its invoice for the supplies [@CL-SUP-2372]. The Government has the option of performing or arranging, at its own expense, any transportation from the contractor's shipping plant or point to the carrier's facility at the time of shipment, and whenever it exercises that option it makes no reimbursement based on a quoted differential [@CL-SUP-2329].

The contractor packs and marks the shipment to comply with contract specification or, in the absence of specifications, prepares it in conformance with carrier requirements to protect the goods and to ensure assessment of the lowest applicable transportation charge [@CL-SUP-2360]. It orders specified carrier equipment when the Government requests it or, if none is specified, orders appropriate carrier equipment not in excess of capacity to accommodate the shipment [@CL-SUP-2361]. It delivers the shipment in good order and condition to the carrier and, when it loads a carload or truckload shipment, loads, stows, trims, blocks and braces it on or in the carrier's conveyance as carrier rules and regulations require [@CL-SUP-2332]. It distributes the copies of the bill of lading, or other transportation receipts, as the ordering agency directs [@CL-SUP-2367].

The contractor completes the Government bill of lading supplied by the ordering agency or, when none is supplied, prepares a commercial bill of lading or other transportation receipt [@CL-SUP-2362]. The bill of lading shows a description of the shipment under the governing freight classification or tariff (or Government rate tender) under which lowest freight rates are applicable, and the seals affixed to the conveyance with their serial numbers or other identification [@CL-SUP-2363]. It also shows the lengths and capacities of cars or trucks ordered and furnished, and other pertinent information required to effect prompt delivery to the consignee, including name, delivery address, postal address and ZIP code of consignee, and routing [@CL-SUP-2364]. It shows special instructions or annotations requested by the ordering agency, for example that the shipment is the property of the Government and that the Government will reimburse the freight charges paid to the carrier [@CL-SUP-2365], and the signature of the carrier's agent and the date the shipment is received by the carrier [@CL-SUP-2366].

The contractor is responsible for loss of or damage to the goods occurring before delivery to the carrier and for loss or damage resulting from improper packing and marking [@CL-SUP-2330]. It is also responsible for loss or damage resulting from improper loading, stowing, trimming, blocking or bracing of the shipment, if the contractor loaded it on or in the carrier's conveyance [@CL-SUP-2331].

## How it works

The clause says it may be advantageous to the offeror to submit f.o.b. origin prices that include only the lowest cost to the contractor for loading at its plant or most favorable shipping point [@CL-SUP-2368]. The cost beyond that point of bringing the supplies to the place of delivery, and of loading, blocking and bracing on the vehicle the Government specifies at shipment, may exceed that lowest cost when the offeror ships for its own account [@CL-SUP-2369]. Accordingly, the offeror may indicate differentials that may be added to the offered price [@CL-SUP-2370]. The Government considers the differentials when it evaluates offers, to determine the lowest overall cost to the Government [@CL-SUP-2327]. At shipment the differential for an option the Government specifies is billed as a separate invoice item, and if the Government performs or arranges the plant-to-carrier transportation itself, it reimburses nothing based on a quoted differential [@CL-SUP-2372; @CL-SUP-2329].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An offer prices 20,000 pounds of supplies at 10,000 dollars f.o.b. origin, with a differential of 300 cents for each 100 pounds for flatbed truckload. The Government considers that differential when it evaluates offers. At shipment the Government specifies a flatbed, and the actual weight is 20,000 pounds, which is 200 units of 100 pounds. The differential cost is 200 times 300 cents, or 600 dollars, which the contractor shows as a separate reimbursable item on the invoice next to the 10,000 dollars for the supplies. Had the Government instead arranged the haul from the plant to the carrier's facility at its own expense, it would reimburse nothing based on the quoted differential.

On the same shipment, if the contractor loads the truck itself and poor bracing lets the load shift and break, the loss results from improper bracing by the contractor, and the contractor is responsible. A pallet the contractor's forklift drops in the plant yard before the carrier receives it is also the contractor's responsibility.

## Never confuse

A differential is quoted in the offer [@CL-SUP-2325]. It is billed through the invoice when the Government specifies at shipment the mode, vehicle or place that it prices [@CL-SUP-2328; @CL-SUP-2372]. The f.o.b. origin variants (contractor's facility, freight allowed and freight prepaid) and plain f.o.b. origin are separate clauses, covered on their own cards.
