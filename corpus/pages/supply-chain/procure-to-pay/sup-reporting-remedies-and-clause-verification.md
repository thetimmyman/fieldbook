## Purpose

Shows what the agency does with executive compensation and service contract reports, what a contractor must correct or explain, and what the contracting officer must do when a report fails to meet the requirements or is not submitted on time.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-SUP-3322] |
| Codified citation | FAR 4.1401, 4.1402 and 4.1704 [@CL-SUP-4117; @CL-SUP-4118; @CL-SUP-3322; @CL-SUP-3391] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-3322] |
| Applies to | Contractors with the executive compensation and first-tier subcontract award reporting clause or the service contract reporting clauses [@CL-SUP-3383; @CL-SUP-3391; @CL-SUP-3392] |

FAR subpart 4.14 applies to all contracts with a value of $40,000 or more, and nothing in the subpart requires the disclosure of classified information [@CL-SUP-4117]. Reporting of subcontract information will be limited to the first-tier subcontractor [@CL-SUP-4118].

For the executive compensation and first-tier subcontract award reporting clause, FAR 52.204-10, agencies shall ensure that contractors comply with its reporting requirements [@CL-SUP-3383]. Agencies shall review contractor reports on a quarterly basis to ensure the information is consistent with contract information [@CL-SUP-3322]. The agency is not required to address data for which it would not normally have supporting information, such as the compensation information required of contractors and first-tier subcontractors [@CL-SUP-3384]. However, the agency shall inform the contractor of any inconsistencies with the contract information and require that the contractor correct the report, or provide a reasonable explanation as to why it believes the information is correct [@CL-SUP-9712; @CL-SUP-3385]. Agencies may review the reports at the FSRS website [@CL-SUP-3386].

When contracting officers report the contract action to the Federal Procurement Data System (FPDS) in accordance with FAR subpart 4.6, certain data will pre-populate from FPDS to assist contractors in completing and submitting their reports [@CL-SUP-3387]. If the contractor finds FPDS data in error when it completes the subcontract report, the contractor should notify the Government contracting officer, who is responsible for correcting the data in FPDS [@CL-SUP-3388]. Contracts reported using the generic entity identifier allowed at FAR 4.605(c)(2) will interfere with the contractor's ability to comply with this reporting requirement, because the data will not pre-populate from FPDS [@CL-SUP-3389].

If the contractor fails to comply with the reporting requirements, the contracting officer shall exercise appropriate contractual remedies [@CL-SUP-3323]. In addition, the contracting officer shall make the contractor's failure to comply with the reporting requirements a part of the contractor's performance information under FAR subpart 42.15 [@CL-SUP-3390]. There is a reporting exception in FAR 52.204-10(g) for contractors and subcontractors who had gross income in the previous tax year under $300,000 [@CL-SUP-3324]. Under that paragraph, a contractor in the previous tax year with gross income from all sources under $300,000 is exempt from the requirement to report subcontractor awards [@CL-CMP-3545], and a contractor does not need to report awards for a subcontractor that had gross income from all sources under $300,000 in the previous tax year [@CL-CMP-3586].

For service contract reporting, the contracting officer shall ensure that the clause at FAR 52.204-14, Service Reporting Requirement, is included in solicitations, contracts, and orders as prescribed at FAR 4.1705, for other than indefinite-delivery contracts [@CL-SUP-3391]. For indefinite-delivery contracts, the contracting officer who awarded the contract shall ensure that FAR 52.204-15, Service Contract Reporting Requirements for Indefinite-Delivery Contracts, is included in solicitations and contracts as prescribed at FAR 4.1705 [@CL-SUP-3392]. The contracting officer at the order level shall verify the clause's inclusion in the contract [@CL-SUP-3325]. If the contractor fails to submit a report in a timely manner, the contracting officer shall exercise appropriate contractual remedies [@CL-SUP-3393], and in addition, the contracting officer shall make the contractor's failure to comply with the reporting requirements a part of the contractor's performance information under FAR subpart 42.15 [@CL-SUP-3394].

## How it works

Agencies shall review contractor reports on a quarterly basis to ensure the information is consistent with contract information; the agency is not required to address data for which the agency would not normally have supporting information, such as the compensation information required of contractors and first-tier subcontractors, however, the agency shall inform the contractor of any inconsistencies with the contract information and require that the contractor correct the report, or provide a reasonable explanation as to why it believes the information is correct [@CL-SUP-3322; @CL-SUP-3384; @CL-SUP-9712; @CL-SUP-3385]. If the contractor finds FPDS data in error when it completes the subcontract report, the contractor should notify the Government contracting officer, who is responsible for correcting the data in FPDS [@CL-SUP-3388]. When a contractor fails to comply with the reporting requirements or fails to submit a report in a timely manner, the contracting officer shall exercise appropriate contractual remedies and shall make the contractor's failure to comply with the reporting requirements a part of the contractor's performance information under FAR subpart 42.15 [@CL-SUP-3323; @CL-SUP-3390; @CL-SUP-3393; @CL-SUP-3394].

There is a reporting exception in FAR 52.204-10(g) for contractors and subcontractors who had gross income in the previous tax year under $300,000: a contractor in the previous tax year with gross income from all sources under $300,000 is exempt from the requirement to report subcontractor awards, and a contractor does not need to report awards for a subcontractor that had gross income from all sources under $300,000 in the previous tax year [@CL-SUP-3324; @CL-CMP-3545; @CL-CMP-3586]. For indefinite-delivery contracts, the contracting officer who awarded the contract shall ensure that FAR 52.204-15 is included in solicitations and contracts as prescribed at FAR 4.1705, and the contracting officer at the order level shall verify the clause's inclusion in the contract [@CL-SUP-3392; @CL-SUP-3325]. The two reports and their clauses are described on [Executive compensation and subcontract award reporting](../../compliance/regulatory-framework/cmp-executive-compensation-and-subcontract-award-reporting.md), [Service contract reporting](../../compliance/regulatory-framework/cmp-service-contract-reporting.md) and [Service contract reporting for indefinite-delivery contracts](../../compliance/regulatory-framework/cmp-service-contract-reporting-indefinite-delivery.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor under a contract with the executive compensation clause files a subcontract report whose award amount differs from the contract information the agency holds. In its quarterly review the agency must inform the contractor of the inconsistency, and the contractor corrects the report or gives a reasonable explanation [@CL-SUP-3322; @CL-SUP-9712; @CL-SUP-3385]. The agency need not address the compensation data [@CL-SUP-3384]. A second contractor fails to submit a required executive compensation report, so the contracting officer exercises appropriate contractual remedies and records the failure in its performance information [@CL-SUP-3323; @CL-SUP-3390].

An indefinite-delivery contract for services is being awarded, and the service reporting clause is prescribed for it at FAR 4.1705. The contracting officer who awards it must ensure that FAR 52.204-15 is included in the solicitation and contract as prescribed [@CL-SUP-3392], and the contracting officer at the order level later verifies the clause's inclusion in the contract [@CL-SUP-3325]. A contractor with gross income from all sources of 250,000 in the previous tax year is under the 300,000 gross-income exception [@CL-SUP-3324], so under the executive compensation clause it is exempt from the requirement to report subcontractor awards [@CL-CMP-3545].

## Never confuse

For FAR 52.204-10, agencies shall ensure that contractors comply with its reporting requirements, whereas for service contract reporting the contracting officer shall ensure that FAR 52.204-14 is included in solicitations, contracts, and orders for other than indefinite-delivery contracts and that FAR 52.204-15 is included in solicitations and contracts for indefinite-delivery contracts as prescribed at FAR 4.1705 [@CL-SUP-3383; @CL-SUP-3391; @CL-SUP-3392]. Contracts reported using the generic entity identifier allowed at FAR 4.605(c)(2) will interfere with the contractor's ability to comply with the FAR 4.1402 reporting requirement, because the data will not pre-populate from FPDS, whereas FAR 52.204-10(g) provides a reporting exception for contractors and subcontractors who had gross income in the previous tax year under $300,000 [@CL-SUP-3389; @CL-SUP-3324].
