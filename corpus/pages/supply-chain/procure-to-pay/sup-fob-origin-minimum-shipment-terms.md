## Purpose

Shows what the contractor must load or guarantee under f.o.b. origin minimum shipment terms and the price adjustment when shipping characteristics differ.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-SUP-2002; @CL-SUP-2006; @CL-SUP-2010] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.247-59 to 52.247-61 [@CL-SUP-2002; @CL-SUP-2006; @CL-SUP-2010] |
| RFO citation | No RFO equivalent for FAR 52.247-59, FAR 52.247-60 and FAR 52.247-61, which RFO part 52 lists as [Reserved] [@CL-SUP-0321; @CL-SUP-0322; @CL-SUP-0323] |
| Edition date | APR 1984 [@CL-SUP-9813] |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-2002; @CL-SUP-2006; @CL-SUP-2010] |
| Applies to | Solicitations and contracts containing the carload and truckload, guaranteed shipping characteristics or minimum size of shipments clause [@CL-SUP-2002; @CL-SUP-2006; @CL-SUP-2010] |

Under the carload and truckload clause, the contractor agrees that shipment shall be made in carload or truckload lots when the quantity to be delivered to any one destination in any delivery period pursuant to the contract schedule of deliveries is sufficient to constitute a carload or truckload shipment [@CL-SUP-2002], except as the Contracting Officer may otherwise permit or direct in writing [@CL-SUP-2003]. For evaluation purposes, the agreed weight of a carload or truckload is the highest applicable minimum weight that will result in the lowest freight rate (or per car charge) [@CL-SUP-2090], on file or published in common carrier tariffs or tenders as of the date of bid opening (or the closing date for receipt of proposals) [@CL-SUP-2091]. For actual delivery, the agreed weight is the highest applicable minimum weight that will result in the lowest possible freight rate (or per car charge) on file or published as of the date of shipment [@CL-SUP-2092]. If the total weight of a scheduled quantity to a destination is less than the highest carload or truckload minimum weight used for evaluation, the contractor ships that quantity in one shipment [@CL-SUP-2004]. The contractor shall be liable to the Government for any increased costs to the Government resulting from failure to comply with those requirements [@CL-SUP-2005]. The clause is inserted in solicitations and contracts when it is contemplated that they may result in f.o.b. origin contracts with shipments in carloads or truckloads [@CL-SUP-2093].

Under the guaranteed shipping characteristics clause, the offeror is requested to complete shipping data for each separately packed part or component, and that data is used to determine transportation costs for evaluation [@CL-SUP-2006]. For each separately packed part or component, the data includes the type of container and the shipping configuration [@CL-SUP-2102]. It also asks for the size of the container, the number of items per container and the gross weight of the container and contents [@CL-SUP-2188], and for whether the shipment is palletized or skidded, the number of containers per pallet or skid, the weight of the empty pallet bottom or skid and sides, and the size of the pallet or skid and contents [@CL-SUP-2189]. It asks for the number of containers or pallets or skids per railcar, with the size and the type of railcar [@CL-SUP-2295], and per trailer, with the size of the trailer in feet and the type of trailer [@CL-SUP-2296]. A footnote on the railcar and trailer counts refers to the number of complete units (line item) to be shipped in carrier's equipment [@CL-SUP-2297]. If the offeror does not furnish enough data for the Government to determine the item shipping costs [@CL-SUP-2100], evaluation is based on the characteristics submitted by the offeror whose offer produces the highest transportation costs or, in the absence thereof, on the Contracting Officer's best estimate [@CL-SUP-2101]. After evaluation but before award the Government completes the rate used in evaluation, the tender or tariff and the item [@CL-SUP-2103]. The price is reduced if the item shipping costs, based on the actual shipping characteristics, exceed those used for evaluation [@CL-SUP-2007; @CL-SUP-2008]. The reduction equals the difference between the transportation costs actually incurred and the costs that would have been incurred if the evaluated characteristics had been accurate [@CL-SUP-2008].

Under the minimum size of shipments clause, shipment will be made in carload and truckload lots when the quantity to be delivered to any one destination in any delivery period pursuant to the contract schedule of deliveries is sufficient to constitute a carload or truckload shipment [@CL-SUP-2094], except as the Contracting Officer may otherwise permit or direct in writing [@CL-SUP-2095]. The agreed weight is the highest applicable minimum weight that will result in the lowest freight rate (or per car charge) on file or published as of the date of shipment [@CL-SUP-2096]. If the total weight of a scheduled quantity to a destination is less than the highest carload or truckload minimum weight, the contractor ships that quantity in one shipment [@CL-SUP-2097], and shall be liable to the Government for any increased costs to the Government resulting from failure to comply with those requirements [@CL-SUP-2098]. That liability does not attach if supplies are outsized or of such nature that they cannot be loaded at the highest minimum weight bracket [@CL-SUP-2010]. The clause is inserted in solicitations and contracts when volume rates may apply [@CL-SUP-2099].

## How it works

The carload and truckload clause sets the duty to ship in full lots, fixes the agreed weight at evaluation and at shipment, and puts the cost of failure on the contractor [@CL-SUP-2004; @CL-SUP-2005; @CL-SUP-2090; @CL-SUP-2092]. The guaranteed characteristics clause reduces the price when actual shipping costs exceed those used in evaluation [@CL-SUP-2007; @CL-SUP-2008], and the data is used only to evaluate offers and to establish that liability [@CL-SUP-2104]. The minimum size clause fixes the agreed weight at the date of shipment and excuses liability for outsized supplies [@CL-SUP-2096; @CL-SUP-2010].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text under Class Deviation 2026-O0038, effective February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. The RFO text prints FAR 52.247-59 as [Reserved] [@CL-SUP-0321], where the codified text has the carload and truckload clause [@CL-SUP-2002]. It prints FAR 52.247-60 as [Reserved] [@CL-SUP-0322], where the codified text has the guaranteed shipping characteristics provision [@CL-SUP-2006]. It prints FAR 52.247-61 as [Reserved] [@CL-SUP-0323], where the codified text has the minimum size of shipments clause [@CL-SUP-2094].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

Under a carload and truckload clause, the lowest published freight rate on the bid opening date applies at a minimum weight of 30,000 pounds, the highest applicable minimum weight giving the lowest rate, so 30,000 pounds is the agreed weight for evaluation. A scheduled quantity to one destination weighs 25,000 pounds, which is less than 30,000, so the contractor must ship it in one shipment. If it splits the quantity into two shipments and the Government pays 200 more freight as a result, the contractor is liable for the 200. Under a minimum size of shipments clause in another contract, the highest minimum weight on the shipment date is 30,000 pounds and the contractor splits a 25,000 pound scheduled quantity. If the supplies are outsized and cannot be loaded at the highest minimum weight bracket, the liability does not attach. Separately, the offeror gave shipping data under the guaranteed characteristics clause, including the railcar and trailer counts with the size and type of each, and the evaluated item shipping cost was 400. The actual shipping characteristics produce an actual cost of 550. The contract price is reduced by 150, the difference between the actual and the evaluated transportation cost.

## Never confuse

The codified text has carload and truckload, guaranteed shipping characteristics and minimum size of shipments clauses at FAR 52.247-59, FAR 52.247-60 and FAR 52.247-61 [@CL-SUP-2002; @CL-SUP-2006; @CL-SUP-2094], but RFO FAR part 52 prints those three section numbers as [Reserved] [@CL-SUP-0321; @CL-SUP-0322; @CL-SUP-0323]. The guaranteed shipping characteristics requested in subparagraph (a)(1) of the clause do not establish actual transportation requirements, which are specified elsewhere in the solicitation [@CL-SUP-2009]. They will be used only for the purpose of evaluating offers and establishing any liability of the successful offeror for increased transportation costs resulting from actual shipping characteristics which differ from those used for evaluation [@CL-SUP-2104].

The f.o.b. origin delivery term and its variants are covered in [F.o.b. origin delivery term](sup-fob-origin-delivery-term.md) and [F.o.b. origin variants: contractor's facility, freight allowed, freight prepaid](sup-fob-origin-variants.md).
