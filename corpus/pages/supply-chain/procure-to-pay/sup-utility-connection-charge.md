## Purpose

Shows how the connection charge for new utility connection facilities is computed, paid, credited back and settled when the contract ends early, including the Alternate I version for a nonrefundable charge.

### The charge

In consideration of the contractor furnishing and installing at its expense the new connection facilities, the Government pays the contractor a connection charge [@CL-SUP-2689]. The payment is in the form of progress payments, advance payments or a lump sum, as agreed to by the parties and as permitted by applicable law [@CL-SUP-2747]. The total amount payable is either the estimated cost less the agreed salvage value or the actual cost less the salvage value, whichever is less, and the contract fills in the estimated cost and the salvage value [@CL-SUP-2690]. As a condition precedent to final payment, the contractor must execute a release of any claims against the Government arising under or by virtue of the installation [@CL-SUP-2691].

### Ownership, operation and maintenance

The new facilities remain the contractor's property notwithstanding the Government's payment of a connection charge [@CL-SUP-2692]. They are at all times during the life of the contract or any renewals operated, maintained and repaired by the contractor at its expense [@CL-SUP-2748]. All taxes and other charges on them, and all liability arising out of their construction, operation, maintenance or repair, are the contractor's obligation [@CL-SUP-2749].

### Credits

The contractor allows the Government, on each monthly bill for service furnished to the service location, a credit of the percentage the contract inserts of each bill as rendered, until the credits accumulated equal the connection charge [@CL-SUP-2693]. The contractor may at any time allow a credit up to 100 percent of each bill [@CL-SUP-2694].

The next two duties arise where the contractor serves a customer other than the Government before any termination of the contract but after completion of the facilities [@CL-SUP-2750]. The contractor must then promptly notify the Government in writing, whether the Government is being served simultaneously, intermittently or not at all [@CL-SUP-2695]. Unless the parties otherwise agree in writing at that time, the contractor must promptly accelerate the credits, up to 100 percent of each monthly bill [@CL-SUP-2751]. The acceleration continues until the contractor has refunded the amount that reflects the Government's connection costs for the portion of the facilities used in serving others [@CL-SUP-2752].

If the contractor terminates the contract or defaults in performance before a connection charge paid by the Government is fully credited, the contractor pays the Government an amount equal to the uncredited balance as of the date of the termination or default [@CL-SUP-2696].

### Termination by the Government before completion

The Government reserves the right to terminate the contract at any time before completion of the facilities for which it is to pay a connection charge [@CL-SUP-2753]. If it does, the contractor is paid the cost of any work accomplished, including direct and indirect costs reasonably allocable to the completed work before termination, plus the cost of removal, less the salvage value [@CL-SUP-2754].

### Termination by the Government after completion

One condition for the contractor's options is that the Government terminates the contract after completion of the facilities for which it has paid a connection charge but before the contractor has credited the charge in full [@CL-SUP-2755]. In that case the contractor has the options the clause then lists [@CL-SUP-2780].

The first option is to retain the facilities in place for the number of months the contract inserts after the Government's notice of termination, on the conditions that follow [@CL-SUP-2756]. If during that period the contractor serves any other customer by means of the facilities, the contractor must, instead of allowing credits, pay the Government installments during that period [@CL-SUP-2757]. The installments are in like amount, manner and extent as the credit under the credit paragraph before the termination [@CL-SUP-2779]. Immediately after that period the contractor must promptly pay the Government the uncredited balance of the connection charge in full [@CL-SUP-2758].

The second option is to remove the facilities at the contractor's own expense within the number of months the contract inserts after the effective date of the termination by the Government [@CL-SUP-2759]. If the contractor elects to remove the facilities, the Government has the option of purchasing them at the agreed salvage value set forth in the clause [@CL-SUP-2760]. The contractor must, at the direction of the Government, leave in place facilities located on Government property that the Government elects to purchase at the agreed salvage value [@CL-SUP-2761].

### Alternate I

Where the Contracting Officer determines that a nonrefundable charge is to be paid and no credits are due the Government, Alternate I directs deleting paragraphs (c) and (e), renumbering paragraph (d) as (c) and adding a replacement paragraph (d) [@CL-SUP-2762]. Under Alternate I, where the Government terminates the contract after completion of the facilities for which it is to pay a connection charge, the contractor has the options the replacement paragraph lists [@CL-SUP-2763].

Under Alternate I, the first option is to retain the facilities in place for the number of months the contract inserts after the Government's notice of termination [@CL-SUP-2764]. If the parties have not agreed on terms for retention in place beyond the inserted number of months, the contractor must remove the facilities under the removal option [@CL-SUP-2765]. The second option is to remove the facilities at the contractor's own expense within the number of months the contract inserts after the effective date of the termination by the Government [@CL-SUP-2766]. If the contractor elects to remove them, the Government has the option of purchasing them at the agreed salvage value set forth in the clause [@CL-SUP-2775]. The contractor must, at the direction of the Government, leave in place facilities located on Government property that the Government elects to purchase at the agreed salvage value [@CL-SUP-2776].

## How it works

The contractor builds and keeps the facilities, and the Government pays a connection charge that is capped at the lower of the estimated cost less salvage value and the actual cost less salvage value [@CL-SUP-2689; @CL-SUP-2690; @CL-SUP-2692]. Under the basic clause, the credits recover the charge from the monthly bills at the inserted percentage, and the contractor may raise the credit to 100 percent of a bill at any time [@CL-SUP-2693; @CL-SUP-2694]. In the basic clause, use of the facilities by another customer triggers a written notice and an acceleration of credits [@CL-SUP-2695; @CL-SUP-2751; @CL-SUP-2752]. Termination or default by the contractor, termination by the Government before completion and termination by the Government after completion each have their own paragraph in the basic clause [@CL-SUP-2696; @CL-SUP-2753; @CL-SUP-2755]. Where a nonrefundable charge is paid and no credits are due, Alternate I deletes the credit paragraph and the basic termination-after-completion paragraph and adds a replacement termination-after-completion paragraph [@CL-SUP-2762; @CL-SUP-2763].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

The estimated cost is 100,000, the actual cost is 90,000, and the agreed salvage value is 10,000. The estimated cost less salvage is 90,000, and the actual cost less salvage is 80,000, so the charge is the lower figure, 80,000. The contractor releases its claims before final payment. The contract inserts a credit of 10 percent of each monthly bill, so a bill of 5,000 carries a credit of 500. After completion, and before any termination, the contractor begins to serve another customer through part of the facilities. It promptly notifies the Government in writing. Unless the parties agree otherwise in writing, it accelerates the credit up to 100 percent of each bill. If it credits the full 100 percent, which is 5,000 on a bill of that size, the 20,000 that reflects the Government's connection costs for the part of the facilities used to serve others is refunded after 4 such bills. In a separate case, the contractor defaults when credits total 30,000, and it pays the Government the uncredited balance as of the date of default, which is 80,000 less 30,000, or 50,000.

## Never confuse

Paying a connection charge does not transfer the facilities, which remain the contractor's property [@CL-SUP-2692]. A connection charge is also not the nonrefundable, nonrecurring service charge, which is a separate charge that may be in addition to or in lieu of a connection charge [@CL-SUP-2705]. Termination liability is a separate clause with its own computation; see [Utility termination liability and nonrefundable service charge](sup-utility-termination-liability-and-service-charge.md).
