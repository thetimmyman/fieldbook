## Purpose

Shows how the desired and required time of delivery clause separates the schedule the Government wants from the schedule an offer must meet, how the date of award or of receipt of the notice of award fixes the delivery period, and what each of the three alternates changes.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-SUP-2552] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.211-9 [@CL-SUP-2552] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |

| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-2552] |

The clause states two schedules in paragraph (a). The desired delivery schedule sets out the delivery the Government desires as item numbers and quantities to be delivered within a number of days after the date of the contract [@CL-SUP-2552]. The required delivery schedule is stated in the same form [@CL-SUP-2604]. The Contracting Officer inserts the details of both schedules and any assumed date, so every bracketed blank is contract-specific [@CL-SUP-2552; @CL-SUP-2604; @CL-SUP-2561].

An offeror that is unable to meet the desired delivery schedule may, without prejudicing evaluation of its offer, propose a delivery schedule [@CL-SUP-2553], and the clause provides a schedule for that proposal, listing item number, quantity and days after the date of contract [@CL-SUP-2605]. The proposed delivery schedule must not extend the delivery period beyond the time for delivery in the Government's required delivery schedule [@CL-SUP-2554].

An offer that proposes delivery of a quantity under terms or conditions that will not clearly fall within the applicable required delivery period is considered nonresponsive and rejected [@CL-SUP-2555]. If the offeror proposes no other delivery schedule, the desired delivery schedule applies [@CL-SUP-2556].

## How it works

The required schedule is the outer limit and the desired schedule is the default [@CL-SUP-2554; @CL-SUP-2556]. Proposing later delivery does not prejudice the evaluation, provided the proposal does not extend delivery beyond the required schedule [@CL-SUP-2553; @CL-SUP-2554], while an offer that will not clearly fall within the required period is rejected [@CL-SUP-2555].

Paragraph (b) of the basic clause directs attention to the Contract Award provision of the solicitation, under which a written award or acceptance of offer mailed or otherwise furnished to the successful offeror results in a binding contract [@CL-SUP-2606]. The Government will mail or otherwise furnish the award or notice of award not later than the day the award is dated [@CL-SUP-2607], so the offeror shall compute the time available for performance beginning with the actual date of award, rather than the date the written notice of award is received through the ordinary mails [@CL-SUP-2557].

Under the basic clause, the Government still evaluates an offer that measures delivery from the Contractor's date of receipt of the contract or notice of award, by adding five calendar days for delivery of the award through the ordinary mails [@CL-SUP-2558]. If the solicitation states that the contract or notice of award will be transmitted electronically, it adds one working day instead [@CL-SUP-2559]. A working day excludes weekends and United States Federal holidays [@CL-SUP-2608]. If the offered delivery date, computed that way, is later than the required delivery date, the offer is considered nonresponsive and rejected [@CL-SUP-2560].

Each alternate substitutes for paragraph (b) of the basic clause or, in Alternate III, allows it to be deleted, and sets the heading of the third column of paragraph (a) [@CL-SUP-2609; @CL-SUP-2613; @CL-SUP-2617]. Alternate I applies where the delivery schedule is expressed in specific calendar dates or specific periods and is based on an assumed date of award, and the contracting officer may then substitute its paragraph (b) for paragraph (b) of the basic clause [@CL-SUP-2609]. The time may be expressed by substituting on or before, during the months, or not sooner than and later than as headings for the third column of paragraph (a) [@CL-SUP-2610]. The Alternate I paragraph states that the delivery dates or specific periods are based on the assumption that the Government will make award by a stated date, and each delivery date is extended by the number of calendar days after that date that the contract is in fact awarded [@CL-SUP-2561]. It keeps the direction to the Contract Award provision [@CL-SUP-2611] and the instruction that the offeror shall compute the time available from the actual date of award rather than the date the notice is received through the ordinary mails [@CL-SUP-2612].

Alternate II applies where the delivery schedule is expressed in specific calendar dates or specific periods and is based on an assumed date the contractor receives notice of award, and the contracting officer may then substitute its paragraph (b) [@CL-SUP-2613]. The heading of the third column of paragraph (a) may be within days after the date of receipt of a written notice of award [@CL-SUP-2614]. Each delivery date is extended by the number of calendar days after the assumed date that the Contractor receives notice of award [@CL-SUP-2615], provided that the Contractor promptly acknowledges receipt of notice of award [@CL-SUP-2616].

Alternate III applies where the delivery schedule is to be based on the actual date the contractor receives a written notice of award, and the contracting officer may then delete paragraph (b) of the basic clause [@CL-SUP-2617]. The same heading, within days after the date of receipt of a written notice of award, may be used for the third column of paragraph (a) [@CL-SUP-2618].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

The Government desires 500 units within 30 days and requires them within 60 days after the date of contract. Offeror A proposes 500 units within 45 days. That is later than desired but inside the required period, so it is allowed without prejudice to its evaluation. Offeror B proposes 400 units within 45 days and 100 units within 75 days. The 100 units fall outside the required period, so the offer is rejected as nonresponsive. Offeror C proposes no other schedule, so the desired schedule of 30 days applies.

Under the basic clause, Offeror D measures delivery from receipt of the contract and proposes 52 days. The Government adds 5 calendar days for the ordinary mails, so the computed delivery date is 57 days after award, which is not later than the required 60 days, and the offer is not rejected on that ground. Offeror E proposes 56 days on the same basis. The computed 61 days is later than the required 60 days, so the offer is rejected.

Where Alternate II is used with notice of award assumed by 1 June and the Contractor receives notice on 5 June and promptly acknowledges receipt, each delivery date moves 4 calendar days later, so a delivery date of 1 September becomes 5 September.

## Never confuse

The desired schedule is not a ceiling and the required schedule is not a target. A proposal later than the desired schedule is allowed without prejudice to the evaluation [@CL-SUP-2553], but an offer that reaches past the required period is rejected rather than merely less attractive [@CL-SUP-2554; @CL-SUP-2555].

An alternate changes the event that the delivery dates are tied to, namely an assumed award date, an assumed date of receipt of notice of award, or actual receipt of a written notice of award [@CL-SUP-2609; @CL-SUP-2613; @CL-SUP-2617]. It does not substitute for the paragraph (a) limit that the proposed schedule must not extend delivery beyond the required delivery schedule [@CL-SUP-2554].

## Company calibration

Confirm which schedule in each solicitation is desired and which is required, how each offered date was computed, and which alternate paragraph the contract uses [@Q-022].
