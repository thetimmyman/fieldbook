## Purpose

Explains how transit arrangements are evaluated, how an offeror proposes to use transit privilege credits, who pays and who bears risk on the shipment, and how the quoted rate affects the evaluated price.

### Transit Arrangements

The Transit Arrangements provision is inserted in solicitations when benefits may accrue to the Government because transit arrangements may apply [@CL-SUP-2458]. The lowest appropriate common carrier transportation costs, including the offeror's through transit rates and charges when applicable, from the offeror's shipping points, via the transit point, to the ultimate destination will be used in evaluating offers [@CL-SUP-2459]. The provision has blanks for the transit points and the destinations, which are specific to each solicitation [@CL-SUP-2525].

### Transportation Transit Privilege Credits

The Transportation Transit Privilege Credits clause is inserted in solicitations and contracts when supplies are of such a nature, or it is the custom of the trade, that offerors may have potential transit credits available and the Government may reduce transportation costs through the use of transit credits [@CL-SUP-2460]. An offeror that has established transit privileges with regulated common carriers, applicable to the supplies when shipped from the original source, is invited to propose using those credits for shipping the supplies to the designated Government destinations [@CL-SUP-2461].

The offeror ships the supplies under commercial bills of lading and pays all remaining transportation charges connected with the shipment, subject to reimbursement by the Government in an amount equal to the remaining charges but not exceeding the amount quoted by the offeror [@CL-SUP-2462]. After loading on the carrier's equipment and acceptance by the carrier, shipments under paid commercial bills of lading move for the account of and at the risk of the Government [@CL-SUP-2463]. That rule does not hold if, pursuant to the Changes clause, the office administering the contract directs use of Government bills of lading [@CL-SUP-2526].

The amount the offeror quotes represents the transportation costs in cents per 100 pounds (freight rate) for full carload or truckload shipments of the supplies from the offeror's original source, via the offeror's transit plant or point, to the Government destinations [@CL-SUP-2527]. The quoted amount includes the carrier's transit privilege charge, less the applicable transit credit, which is the amount (rate) initially paid to the carrier for shipment from the original source to the offeror's transit plant or point [@CL-SUP-2528].

The rate per CWT quoted will be used by the Government to evaluate the offered f.o.b. origin price unless a lower rate is applicable on the date of bid opening (or the closing date specified for receipt of offers) [@CL-SUP-2464]. To have the offer evaluated on the quoted rate, the offeror must insert the remaining transportation charges that it agrees to pay, including any transit charges and subject to Government reimbursement as the clause explains, to the destinations listed in the Schedule [@CL-SUP-2529]. The clause provides a blank headed rate per CWT in cents to destination, which the offeror completes for each solicitation [@CL-SUP-2530].

## How it works

An offeror with a transit privilege proposes the credit-adjusted rate, ships on its own commercial bills of lading, pays the remaining charges, and is reimbursed up to its quoted amount [@CL-SUP-2461; @CL-SUP-2462]. The quoted rate is net of the transit credit [@CL-SUP-2528], and the Government uses it to evaluate the offered f.o.b. origin price unless a lower rate applies on the date of bid opening or the closing date for offers [@CL-SUP-2464].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text from February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. The RFO text of FAR 52.247-56 keeps the insertion of the Transit Arrangements provision in solicitations when benefits may accrue to the Government because transit arrangements may apply [@CL-SUP-0639; @CL-SUP-2458]. It keeps the evaluation of offers on the lowest appropriate common carrier transportation costs, including the offeror's through transit rates and charges when applicable, via the transit point to the ultimate destination [@CL-SUP-0640; @CL-SUP-2459], and the blanks for the transit points and the destinations [@CL-SUP-0641; @CL-SUP-2525]. The RFO text prints FAR 52.247-57 as [Reserved] [@CL-SUP-0642]. The transit privilege credits rules described above are the codified FAR 52.247-57 [@CL-SUP-2460].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An offeror holds a transit privilege and quotes a rate of 50 cents per 100 pounds for a full carload of 40,000 pounds, from its original source via its transit plant to the Government destination, after deducting the transit credit. On the date of bid opening a lower rate of 45 cents applies, so the Government evaluates the offered f.o.b. origin price with 45 cents. The quoted amount for the load is 200. The contractor ships under a commercial bill of lading and pays 180 of remaining charges, and the Government reimburses 180. Had the remaining charges been 230, the reimbursement would stop at the 200 quoted. Once the carrier accepts the loaded car, it moves at the Government's risk, unless the administering office has directed Government bills of lading under the Changes clause.

## Never confuse

A transit credit is not a discount that the offeror keeps. The credit is netted out of the quoted rate, and the Government reimburses only the remaining charges, up to the amount quoted [@CL-SUP-2528; @CL-SUP-2462]. Transit arrangements, which evaluate the through rate via the transit point, also differ from transit privilege credits, where the offeror ships under commercial bills of lading and is reimbursed [@CL-SUP-2459; @CL-SUP-2462]. For stopping in transit and multiple shipments, see [Charges for multiple shipments and stopping in transit](sup-charges-multiple-shipments-and-stopping-in-transit.md). The codified text has both the Transit Arrangements provision and the Transportation Transit Privilege Credits clause [@CL-SUP-2458; @CL-SUP-2460], but the RFO text keeps the transit arrangements provision as FAR 52.247-56 and prints FAR 52.247-57 as [Reserved] [@CL-SUP-0639; @CL-SUP-0642].

## Company calibration

Whether the company holds transit privileges it can apply, and what shipping and reimbursement commitments it would make by quoting a rate, since those shape the near-term shipping plan. [@Q-022]
