## Purpose

Shows who evaluates a request for consent to a subcontract and the questions the contracting officer considers.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-SUP-0505] |
| Codified citation | FAR 44.202-1 and FAR 44.202-2 [@CL-SUP-0505; @CL-SUP-0508] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-0505] |
| Applies to | Proposed subcontracts for which the contracting officer reviews the contractor's notification and supporting data for consent [@CL-SUP-0506] |

The cognizant administrative contracting officer is responsible for consent to subcontracts, except when the contracting officer retains the contract for administration or withholds the consent responsibility from delegation [@CL-SUP-0505].

The contracting officer responsible for consent shall review the contractor's notification and supporting data to ensure that the proposed subcontract is appropriate for the risks involved and consistent with current policy and sound business judgment [@CL-SUP-0506]. Designation of specific subcontractors during contract negotiations does not in itself satisfy the requirements for advance notification or consent under the Subcontracts clause [@CL-SUP-0507].

## How it works

The contracting officer responsible for consent must, at a minimum, review the request and supporting data and consider a list of questions, the first being whether the decision to subcontract is consistent with the contractor's approved make-or-buy program, if any [@CL-SUP-0508]. The questions also ask whether the selection of the supplies, equipment, or services is technically justified [@CL-SUP-0654], whether adequate price competition was obtained or its absence properly justified [@CL-SUP-0655], and whether the contractor has a sound basis for selecting and determining the responsibility of the subcontractor [@CL-SUP-0656]. Another question is whether the contractor performed adequate cost or price analysis or price comparisons and obtained certified cost or pricing data and data other than certified cost or pricing data [@CL-SUP-0657]. Particularly careful and thorough consideration is necessary when any one of four alternative situations arises. The first is that the prime contractor's purchasing system or performance is inadequate [@CL-SUP-0509]. The second is that close working relationships or ownership affiliations between the prime and subcontractor may preclude free competition or result in higher prices [@CL-SUP-9713]. The third is that subcontracts are proposed for award on a non-competitive basis, at prices that appear unreasonable, or at prices higher than those offered to the Government in comparable circumstances, and the fourth is that subcontracts are proposed on a cost-reimbursement, time-and-materials, or labor-hour basis [@CL-SUP-9714]. The defense supplement adds the question whether, where other than lowest price is the basis for subcontractor selection, the contractor has adequately substantiated the selection as offering the greatest value to the Government [@CL-SUP-0510].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A prime contractor under a defense contract asks for consent to place a subcontract with a supplier it chose on best value, not lowest price. The supplier's name appeared in a list of intended subcontractors during negotiations. The contracting officer asks whether the contractor substantiated the greatest-value choice, whether price competition was adequate or its absence justified, and whether the contractor analysed cost or price.

Answer: the contracting officer needs those questions answered before consent. The earlier naming of the supplier does not by itself satisfy the consent requirement.

## Never confuse

Naming a subcontractor during negotiations is not consent. It does not in itself satisfy the requirements for advance notification or consent [@CL-SUP-0507]. If, in the opinion of the contracting officer, those requirements were satisfied for certain subcontracts evaluated during negotiations, the contracting officer shall identify them in paragraph (j) of the clause [@CL-SUP-0653]. The clause itself is covered on the [Subcontracts: consent and advance notification](sup-subcontracts-consent-advance-notification.md) card, and make-or-buy programs on the [Make-or-buy programs](../sourcing-evidence/sup-make-or-buy-programs.md) card.

## Company calibration

Confirm whether subcontract packages carry the support the contracting officer looks for: price analysis, competition, responsibility and make-or-buy fit. [@Q-040; @Q-041]
