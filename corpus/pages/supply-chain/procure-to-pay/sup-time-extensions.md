## Purpose

Shows how a change order grants time for a contract change and how that extension interacts with the completion dates and the liquidated damages computation.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-SUP-2575] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.211-13 [@CL-SUP-2575] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.211-13 [@CL-SUP-0635; @CL-SUP-0636; @CL-SUP-0637] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-2575] |
| Applies to | Not established. |

Time extensions for contract changes depend upon the extent, if any, by which the changes cause delay in the completion of the various elements of construction [@CL-SUP-2575].

The change order granting the time extension may provide that the contract completion date is extended only for those specific elements related to the changed work, and that the remaining completion dates for all other portions of the work are not altered [@CL-SUP-2576]. The change order may also provide an equitable readjustment of liquidated damages under the new completion schedule [@CL-SUP-2577].

## How it works

The extension depends on the delay that the change causes to the elements of construction, not on the fact of a change alone [@CL-SUP-2575]. Because the change order may limit the extension to the elements related to the changed work, the other portions of the work may keep their own completion dates [@CL-SUP-2576]. The same change order may readjust liquidated damages to the new completion schedule, so the new completion schedule and an equitable readjustment of liquidated damages can be set in the same order [@CL-SUP-2577].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text from February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. The RFO text of FAR 52.211-13 keeps the rule that time extensions for contract changes depend upon the extent, if any, by which the changes cause delay in the completion of the various elements of construction [@CL-SUP-0635; @CL-SUP-2575]. It keeps the change order's option to extend the completion date only for the specific elements related to the changed work, leaving the remaining completion dates for all other portions of the work unaltered [@CL-SUP-0636; @CL-SUP-2576], and its option to provide an equitable readjustment of liquidated damages under the new completion schedule [@CL-SUP-0637; @CL-SUP-2577].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A change order adds work to one element of a construction contract and delays that element by 12 days, while the other elements are not delayed. The change order provides that the completion date is extended by 12 days for that element only and that the other completion dates are not altered, and it provides an equitable readjustment of liquidated damages under the new completion schedule. The clause permits each of these provisions but does not require them.

## Never confuse

An extension of time is not an automatic consequence of a change. It depends on the extent, if any, of the delay the change causes to the elements of construction [@CL-SUP-2575], and the change order may confine it to the elements related to the changed work instead of moving every completion date [@CL-SUP-2576].
