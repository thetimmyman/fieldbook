## Purpose

Shows what an offeror describes about the freight classification of its supplies, how the Government uses that description, and the loading, blocking and bracing standards the contractor follows for carload rail shipments.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-SUP-2450; @CL-SUP-2454] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.247-53 and FAR 52.247-58 [@CL-SUP-2450; @CL-SUP-2454] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.247-53 [@CL-SUP-0368]; RFO FAR 52.247-58 [@CL-SUP-0373] |
| Edition date | FAR 52.247-53: Apr 1984 [@CL-SUP-0368]; FAR 52.247-58: Apr 1984 [@CL-SUP-0373] |

### Freight classification description

The Freight Classification Description provision is inserted in solicitations when the supplies being acquired are new to the supply system, nonstandard, or modifications of previously shipped items, and different freight classifications may apply [@CL-SUP-2450]. Offerors are requested to indicate the full Uniform Freight Classification (rail) description, or the National Motor Freight Classification description, applicable to the supplies, the same as the offeror uses for commercial shipment [@CL-SUP-2451].

The description should include the packing of the commodity, the container material, unusual shipping dimensions and other conditions affecting traffic descriptions [@CL-SUP-2452]. At the end of the provision, the offeror describes the commodity for freight classification purposes [@CL-SUP-2522]. The Government will use the offerors' descriptions and other available information to determine the classification description most appropriate and advantageous to the Government [@CL-SUP-2521]. The offeror understands that shipments on any f.o.b. origin contract awarded as a result of the solicitation will be made in conformity with the shipping classification description specified by the Government, which may be different from the description the offeror furnished [@CL-SUP-2453].

### Loading, blocking and bracing

The Loading, Blocking, and Bracing of Freight Car Shipments clause is inserted in solicitations and contracts when supplies may be shipped in carload lots by rail [@CL-SUP-2454]. Upon receipt of shipping instructions as provided in the contract, the supplies to be included in any carload shipment by rail must be loaded, blocked and braced by the contractor [@CL-SUP-2455] in accordance with the standards that the Association of American Railroads has published and that are effective at the time of shipment [@CL-SUP-2523].

Where the Association of American Railroads has published no such standards, the shipment must be loaded, blocked and braced in accordance with standards the shipper established, evidenced by written acceptance of an authorized carrier representative [@CL-SUP-2456]. The contractor shall be liable for payment of any damage to any supplies caused by the failure to load, block and brace in accordance with acceptable standards set forth in the clause [@CL-SUP-2457]. A copy of the appropriate pamphlet of the Association of American Railroads may be obtained from that Association [@CL-SUP-2524].

## How it works

The offeror indicates its description in its offer [@CL-SUP-2451]. The Government will use the offerors' descriptions as well as other information available to determine the classification description most appropriate and advantageous to the Government [@CL-SUP-2521], and shipments on any f.o.b. origin contract awarded will be made in conformity with the shipping classification description specified by the Government, which may be different from the description the offeror furnished [@CL-SUP-2453]. For carload rail shipments the loading clause applies: on receipt of shipping instructions as provided in the contract, the contractor must load, block and brace the supplies in accordance with the standards published by the Association of American Railroads and effective at the time of shipment [@CL-SUP-2455; @CL-SUP-2523] or, where the Association has published no such standards, in accordance with standards established by the shipper as evidenced by written acceptance of an authorized representative of the carrier [@CL-SUP-2456]. The contractor shall be liable for payment of any damage to any supplies caused by the failure to load, block and brace in accordance with acceptable standards [@CL-SUP-2457].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text under Class Deviation 2026-O0038, effective February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. RFO FAR 52.247-53 requests that offerors indicate the full Uniform Freight Classification (rail) description, or the National Motor Freight Classification description, applicable to the supplies, the same as the offeror uses for commercial shipment [@CL-SUP-0368]. It says the description should include the packing of the commodity, the container material, unusual shipping dimensions and other conditions affecting traffic descriptions [@CL-SUP-0369], and that the Government will use the descriptions as well as other information available to determine the classification description most appropriate and advantageous to the Government [@CL-SUP-0370]. It has the offeror understand that shipments on any f.o.b. origin contract awarded as a result of the solicitation will be made in conformity with the shipping classification description specified by the Government, which may be different from the classification description furnished [@CL-SUP-0371], and it ends with the offeror's description of the commodity for freight classification purposes [@CL-SUP-0372]. RFO FAR 52.247-58(a) provides that, upon receipt of shipping instructions as provided in the contract, the supplies to be included in any carload shipment by rail shall be loaded, blocked and braced by the Contractor [@CL-SUP-0373] in accordance with the standards published by the Association of American Railroads and effective at the time of shipment [@CL-SUP-0374]. Paragraph (b) provides that shipments for which the Association has published no such standards shall be loaded, blocked and braced in accordance with standards established by the shipper as evidenced by written acceptance of an authorized representative of the carrier [@CL-SUP-0375]. Paragraph (c) provides that the Contractor shall be liable for payment of any damage to any supplies caused by the failure to load, block and brace in accordance with acceptable standards set forth in the clause [@CL-SUP-0376], and paragraph (d) provides that a copy of the appropriate pamphlet of the Association may be obtained from that Association [@CL-SUP-0377].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An offeror ships pumps in wooden crates and states that packing and container material in its description. The Government specifies a different classification description for the awarded f.o.b. origin contract, and the contractor ships in conformity with the Government's description. On a carload rail shipment the Association of American Railroads has published a standard for this kind of load, and the contractor receives shipping instructions. It braces one car below that standard, the load shifts, and the supplies in that car are damaged by 12,000. The contractor pays for the damage because the failure to brace to the standard caused it. On a second shipment for which the Association has published no standard, the contractor loads to the shipper's standard that the carrier's representative accepted in writing.

## Never confuse

The classification description covers the commodity, its packing and its shipping conditions [@CL-SUP-2452], and shipments under an awarded f.o.b. origin contract will be made in conformity with the description the Government specifies, which may be different from the one the offeror furnished [@CL-SUP-2453], unlike loading, blocking and bracing, which is a duty the contractor performs on receipt of shipping instructions, with liability for payment of any damage caused by the failure to load, block and brace in accordance with acceptable standards [@CL-SUP-2455; @CL-SUP-2457]. For liability for loss and damage in carriage generally, see [Freight loss and damage liability](sup-freight-loss-and-damage-liability.md).

## Company calibration

Whether the description the company bids with matches the classification the Government applies, and whether its loading records can show which standard was used for each carload shipment. [@Q-021]
