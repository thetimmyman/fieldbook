## Purpose

Shows what an offeror describes about the freight classification of its supplies, how the Government uses that description, and the loading, blocking and bracing standards the contractor follows for carload rail shipments.

### Freight classification description

The Freight Classification Description provision is inserted in solicitations when the supplies being acquired are new to the supply system, nonstandard, or modifications of previously shipped items, and different freight classifications may apply [@CL-SUP-2450]. Offerors are requested to indicate the full Uniform Freight Classification (rail) description, or the National Motor Freight Classification description, applicable to the supplies, the same as the offeror uses for commercial shipment [@CL-SUP-2451].

The description should include the packing of the commodity, the container material, unusual shipping dimensions and other conditions affecting traffic descriptions [@CL-SUP-2452]. The offeror writes it in the blank at the end of the provision, so it is specific to each offer [@CL-SUP-2522]. The Government will use the offerors' descriptions and other available information to determine the classification description most appropriate and advantageous to the Government [@CL-SUP-2521]. The offeror understands that shipments on any f.o.b. origin contract awarded as a result of the solicitation will be made in conformity with the shipping classification description specified by the Government, which may be different from the description the offeror furnished [@CL-SUP-2453].

### Loading, blocking and bracing

The Loading, Blocking, and Bracing of Freight Car Shipments clause is inserted in solicitations and contracts when supplies may be shipped in carload lots by rail [@CL-SUP-2454]. Upon receipt of shipping instructions as provided in the contract, the contractor loads, blocks and braces the supplies to be included in any carload shipment by rail [@CL-SUP-2455]. It does so in accordance with the standards that the Association of American Railroads has published and that are effective at the time of shipment [@CL-SUP-2523].

Where the Association of American Railroads has published no such standards, the shipment is loaded, blocked and braced to standards the shipper established, evidenced by written acceptance of an authorized carrier representative [@CL-SUP-2456]. The contractor is liable for payment of damage to supplies caused by failure to load, block and brace to acceptable standards [@CL-SUP-2457]. A copy of the appropriate pamphlet of the Association of American Railroads may be obtained from that Association [@CL-SUP-2524].

## How it works

The offeror's description reaches the Government at the offer stage, and the Government may substitute its own description for the awarded f.o.b. origin contract [@CL-SUP-2451; @CL-SUP-2521; @CL-SUP-2453]. After award, the loading clause takes over for carload rail shipments: the contractor loads on receipt of shipping instructions, to the published standard or else to the shipper's standard that the carrier's authorized representative has accepted in writing, and it pays for damage that the failure to meet the standard causes [@CL-SUP-2455; @CL-SUP-2523; @CL-SUP-2456; @CL-SUP-2457].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text under Class Deviation 2026-O0038, effective February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. RFO FAR 52.247-53 keeps the request that offerors indicate the full Uniform Freight Classification (rail) description, or the National Motor Freight Classification description, applicable to the supplies, the same as the offeror uses for commercial shipment [@CL-SUP-0368; @CL-SUP-2451]. It keeps the statement that the description should include the packing of the commodity, the container material, unusual shipping dimensions and other conditions affecting traffic descriptions [@CL-SUP-0369; @CL-SUP-2452], and that the Government will use the descriptions and other information available to determine the classification description most appropriate and advantageous to the Government [@CL-SUP-0370; @CL-SUP-2521]. It keeps the offeror's understanding that shipments on any f.o.b. origin contract awarded as a result of the solicitation will be made in conformity with the shipping classification description specified by the Government, which may be different from the description the offeror furnished [@CL-SUP-0371; @CL-SUP-2453], and it keeps the blank in which the offeror describes the commodity [@CL-SUP-0372; @CL-SUP-2522]. RFO FAR 52.247-58(a) keeps the duty that, upon receipt of shipping instructions as provided in the contract, the contractor loads, blocks and braces the supplies to be included in any carload shipment by rail [@CL-SUP-0373; @CL-SUP-2455], to the standards the Association of American Railroads has published and that are effective at the time of shipment [@CL-SUP-0374; @CL-SUP-2523]. Paragraph (b) keeps the rule that, where the Association has published no such standards, the shipment is loaded, blocked and braced to standards the shipper established, as evidenced by written acceptance of an authorized carrier representative [@CL-SUP-0375; @CL-SUP-2456]. Paragraph (c) keeps the contractor's liability for payment of damage to supplies caused by failure to load, block and brace to acceptable standards [@CL-SUP-0376; @CL-SUP-2457], and paragraph (d) keeps the statement that a copy of the appropriate pamphlet may be obtained from the Association [@CL-SUP-0377; @CL-SUP-2524].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An offeror ships pumps in wooden crates and states that packing and container material in its description. The Government specifies a different classification description for the awarded f.o.b. origin contract, and the contractor ships in conformity with the Government's description. On a carload rail shipment the Association of American Railroads has published a standard for this kind of load, and the contractor receives shipping instructions. It braces one car below that standard, the load shifts, and the supplies in that car are damaged by 12,000. The contractor pays for the damage because the failure to brace to the standard caused it. On a second shipment for which the Association has published no standard, the contractor loads to the shipper's standard that the carrier's representative accepted in writing.

## Never confuse

The classification description is not a price or a packing order. It describes the commodity, its packing and its shipping conditions, and the Government may specify a different description for shipments under the awarded origin contract [@CL-SUP-2452; @CL-SUP-2453]. Loading, blocking and bracing is a separate physical duty that the contractor performs on receipt of shipping instructions, with liability if the standard is missed [@CL-SUP-2455; @CL-SUP-2457]. For liability for loss and damage in carriage generally, see [Freight loss and damage liability](sup-freight-loss-and-damage-liability.md).

## Company calibration

Whether the description the company bids with matches the classification the Government applies, and whether its loading records can show which standard was used for each carload shipment. [@Q-021]
