## Purpose

Shows who furnishes, owns and maintains the utility facilities, what the Government grants and may restrict on its premises, how service is metered, tested and billed, and how far the contractor's duty of continuity reaches.

### Facilities

At its expense, unless the contract provides otherwise, the contractor furnishes, installs, operates and maintains all facilities required to furnish the service, and measures the service at the delivery point specified in the Service Specifications [@CL-SUP-2672]. Title to those facilities stays with the contractor, which is responsible for their loss or damage, except that the Government is responsible to the extent its negligent acts or omissions caused the loss or damage [@CL-SUP-2673].

Notwithstanding other terms of the clause, the contractor must obtain approval from the Contracting Officer before any equipment installation, construction or removal [@CL-SUP-2716].

### Permit, taxes and access

The Government grants the contractor, free of any rental or similar charge but subject to the limitations in the contract, a revocable permit or license to enter the service location for any proper purpose under the contract [@CL-SUP-2717]. The permit or license includes use of the site or sites the parties agree on for installing, operating, maintaining and repairing the contractor's facilities that must be located on Government premises [@CL-SUP-2718]. All applicable taxes and other charges on those facilities, and all of the contractor's liability in constructing, operating, maintaining and repairing them, are the contractor's obligation [@CL-SUP-2719].

Authorized representatives of the contractor are allowed access to the facilities on Government premises at reasonable times to perform the contractor's obligations regarding those facilities [@CL-SUP-2720]. The Government may limit or restrict that access in any manner it considers necessary, for example for national security or public safety [@CL-SUP-2721].

### Removal at the end

Unless the contract specifies otherwise, the contractor must, at its expense, remove the facilities and restore Government premises to their original condition as near as practicable within a reasonable time after the Government terminates the contract [@CL-SUP-2722]. Where the termination is due to the fault of the contractor, the Government may, at its option, keep the facilities in place for a reasonable time while it attempts to obtain comparable service elsewhere [@CL-SUP-2723].

### Metering and billing

All service is measured by suitable metering equipment of standard manufacture, which the contractor furnishes, installs, maintains, repairs, calibrates and reads at its expense [@CL-SUP-2674]. When more than a single meter is installed at a service location, the readings may be billed conjunctively, if appropriate [@CL-SUP-2725]. If a meter fails to register or registers incorrectly, the parties must agree on the length of time of the malfunction and the quantity of service delivered during that time [@CL-SUP-2726], and an appropriate adjustment must be made to the next invoice [@CL-SUP-2727]. A meter that registers not more than the percentage the contract inserts slow or fast is deemed correct [@CL-SUP-2728].

The contractor reads all meters at periodic intervals of approximately 30 days, or in accordance with the policy of the cognizant regulatory body or applicable bylaws [@CL-SUP-2675]. All billings based on meter readings of fewer than the number of days the contract inserts are prorated accordingly [@CL-SUP-2729].

### Meter tests

The contractor must, at its expense, periodically inspect and test the meters it installed at intervals not exceeding the number of years the contract inserts [@CL-SUP-2730]. The Government has the right to have representation during the inspection and test [@CL-SUP-2731]. At the written request of the Contracting Officer, the contractor must make additional tests of any or all of those meters in the presence of Government representatives [@CL-SUP-2732]. The Government bears the cost of those additional tests if the percentage of errors found is not more than the percentage the contract inserts, slow or fast [@CL-SUP-2733]. No meter may be placed in service or allowed to remain in service that has an error in registration in excess of the percentage the contract inserts under normal operating conditions [@CL-SUP-2734].

Each percentage, number of days, number of years and number of hours in these two clauses is a blank that the contract fills in [@CL-SUP-2728; @CL-SUP-2729; @CL-SUP-2730; @CL-SUP-2733; @CL-SUP-2734; @CL-SUP-2737].

### Change in volume or character

The Contracting Officer must give the contractor reasonable notice of any material changes anticipated in the volume or characteristics of the utility service required at each location [@CL-SUP-2735].

### Continuity of service

The contractor must use reasonable diligence to provide a regular and uninterrupted supply of service at each service location [@CL-SUP-2676]. It is not liable for damages, breach of contract or otherwise to the Government for failure, suspension, diminution or other variations of service occasioned by any cause beyond its control [@CL-SUP-2677]. Such causes include, but are not limited to, acts of God or of the public enemy, fires, floods, earthquakes or other catastrophe, strikes, and failure or breakdown of transmission or other facilities [@CL-SUP-2736]. If any such failure, suspension, diminution or other variation of service aggregates more than the number of hours the contract inserts during a billing period, an equitable adjustment is made in the monthly billing, including the minimum monthly charge [@CL-SUP-2737].

## How it works

Under the facilities clause the contractor carries the plant: it furnishes, operates and maintains the facilities, holds title, pays the taxes and bears loss or damage, except to the extent Government negligence caused it [@CL-SUP-2672; @CL-SUP-2673; @CL-SUP-2719]. The Government's side is to approve equipment work, to grant the permit, and to limit access where it considers that necessary [@CL-SUP-2716; @CL-SUP-2717; @CL-SUP-2721]. The service provisions turn delivered service into a bill: the contractor's meters measure it, readings are billed, and meter errors are corrected on the next invoice [@CL-SUP-2674; @CL-SUP-2727]. A cause beyond the contractor's control brings no liability in damages, and the billing adjustment applies once the inserted number of hours is passed [@CL-SUP-2677; @CL-SUP-2737].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A site has one meter that the contractor installed and reads about every 30 days. The contract inserts 10 hours for interruptions and 2 percent in each of the meter accuracy blanks. A storm, an act of God, interrupts service for 40 hours in one billing period. The contractor is not liable for damages or breach, but 40 hours is more than 10, so an equitable adjustment is made in that month's billing, including the minimum monthly charge. Later a test finds a meter registering 1 percent fast. That is within 2 percent, so the meter is deemed correct and stays in service. A Government crew negligently cuts a contractor line on Government premises while moving equipment. The Government is responsible for that damage to the extent its negligence caused it, and title to the line stays with the contractor.

## Never confuse

Ownership of the facilities is not ownership of the service: title to the facilities stays with the contractor, and the contractor measures the service at the delivery point [@CL-SUP-2672; @CL-SUP-2673]. A service interruption is not by itself a breach, because the contractor is not liable for damages where a cause beyond its control occasions it, yet it is not free of billing consequences once the inserted number of hours is passed [@CL-SUP-2677; @CL-SUP-2737].
