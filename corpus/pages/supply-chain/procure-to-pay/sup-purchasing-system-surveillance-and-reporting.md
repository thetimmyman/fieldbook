## Purpose

Shows how the administrative contracting officer keeps watch on a contractor's purchasing program, what the officer may say about a subcontractor's system, and who receives review reports.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-SUP-0522] |
| Codified citation | FAR 44.304, FAR 44.306 and FAR 44.307 [@CL-SUP-0522; @CL-SUP-0524; @CL-SUP-0526] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-0522] |
| Applies to | A contractor's purchasing program under surveillance by the administrative contracting officer [@CL-SUP-0522] |

The administrative contracting officer shall maintain a sufficient level of surveillance to ensure that the contractor is effectively managing its purchasing program [@CL-SUP-0522].

Surveillance shall be accomplished in accordance with a plan developed by the administrative contracting officer with the assistance of subcontracting, audit, pricing, technical, or other specialists as necessary [@CL-SUP-0662].

## How it works

The plan should cover pertinent phases of a contractor's purchasing system (preaward, postaward, performance, and contract completion) and pertinent operations that affect the contractor's purchasing and subcontracting [@CL-SUP-0663]. It should also provide for reviewing the effectiveness of the contractor's corrective actions taken as a result of previous Government recommendations [@CL-SUP-0664]. Duplicative reviews of the same areas by CPSR and other surveillance monitors should be avoided [@CL-SUP-0523]. Upon request, the officer may inform a contractor that the purchasing system of a proposed subcontractor has been approved or disapproved, but shall caution that the Government will not keep the contractor advised of any changes in the approval status [@CL-SUP-0524]. If the proposed subcontractor's purchasing system has not been reviewed, the contractor shall be so advised [@CL-SUP-0525]. The officer shall distribute copies of CPSR reports, approval notifications, and improvement recommendations to at least the cognizant contract audit office [@CL-SUP-0526], and the contractor is also among the recipients, except that furnishing copies of the contractor's response is optional [@CL-SUP-0665].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A prime contractor planning a subcontract asks the administrative contracting officer whether a prospective supplier's purchasing system is approved. The officer replies that it is approved and adds that the Government will not keep the prime contractor advised of changes. For a second supplier whose system was never reviewed, the officer says so.

Answer: both replies follow the rule. The first carries the caution and the second tells the contractor the system has not been reviewed.

## Never confuse

Surveillance is not a CPSR. Surveillance follows a plan and keeps a sufficient level of oversight, and duplicative reviews of the same areas by CPSR and other monitors should be avoided [@CL-SUP-0662; @CL-SUP-0523]. A disclosed approval status is not a guarantee that the status stays current, because the Government will not keep the contractor advised of changes [@CL-SUP-0524]. System approval is covered on the [Contractor purchasing system](sup-contractor-purchasing-system.md) card.

## Company calibration

Confirm whether corrective-action commitments from earlier reviews are tracked so they can be shown during surveillance. [@Q-040]
