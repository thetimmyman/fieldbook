## Purpose

Shows how the two liquidated damages clauses fix a daily amount, when that amount accrues and stops, and how it sits alongside termination for default and excess costs of repurchase.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-SUP-2568] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.211-11 and FAR 52.211-12 [@CL-SUP-2568; @CL-SUP-2572] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.211-11 [@CL-SUP-0421]; RFO FAR 52.211-12 [@CL-SUP-0425] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-2568] |
| Applies to | Not established. |

Under the clause for supplies, services, or research and development, if the Contractor fails to deliver the supplies or perform the services within the time specified in the contract, the Contractor shall, in place of actual damages, pay the Government liquidated damages of the amount inserted per calendar day of delay [@CL-SUP-2568; @CL-SUP-9709]. If the Government terminates the contract in whole or in part under the Default—Fixed-Price Supply and Service clause, the Contractor is liable for liquidated damages accruing until the Government reasonably obtains delivery or performance of similar supplies or services [@CL-SUP-2569]. These liquidated damages are in addition to excess costs of repurchase under the Termination clause [@CL-SUP-2570]. The Contractor will not be charged with liquidated damages when the delay in delivery or performance is beyond its control and without its fault or negligence as defined in the Default—Fixed-Price Supply and Service clause in the contract [@CL-SUP-2571].

Under the construction clause, if the Contractor fails to complete the work within the time specified in the contract, the Contractor shall pay liquidated damages to the Government in the inserted amount for each calendar day of delay until the work is completed or accepted [@CL-SUP-2572]. If the Government terminates the Contractor's right to proceed, liquidated damages continue to accrue until the work is completed [@CL-SUP-2573]. These liquidated damages are in addition to excess costs of repurchase under the Termination clause [@CL-SUP-2574].

## How it works

Both clauses state the amount per calendar day of delay, and the Contracting Officer inserts the amount [@CL-SUP-2568; @CL-SUP-9709; @CL-SUP-2572]. In the supplies clause the amount is paid in place of actual damages [@CL-SUP-2568], and if the Government terminates the contract in whole or in part under the Default clause, liquidated damages accrue until the Government reasonably obtains delivery or performance of similar supplies or services [@CL-SUP-2569]. In the construction clause the amount is payable for each calendar day of delay until the work is completed or accepted [@CL-SUP-2572], and if the Government terminates the Contractor's right to proceed, liquidated damages continue to accrue until the work is completed [@CL-SUP-2573].

Each clause states that these liquidated damages are in addition to excess costs of repurchase under the Termination clause [@CL-SUP-2570; @CL-SUP-2574].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text from February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. In RFO FAR 52.211-11, if the Contractor fails to deliver the supplies or perform the services within the time specified in the contract, the Contractor shall, in place of actual damages, pay the Government liquidated damages of the inserted amount per calendar day of delay [@CL-SUP-0421]. If the Government terminates the contract in whole or in part under the Default-Fixed-Price Supply and Service clause, the Contractor is liable for liquidated damages accruing until the Government reasonably obtains delivery or performance of similar supplies or services [@CL-SUP-0422]. These liquidated damages are in addition to excess costs of repurchase under the Termination clause [@CL-SUP-0423]. The Contractor will not be charged with liquidated damages when the delay is beyond the control and without the fault or negligence of the Contractor as defined in the Default-Fixed-Price Supply and Service clause in the contract [@CL-SUP-0424]. In RFO FAR 52.211-12, if the Contractor fails to complete the work within the time specified in the contract, the Contractor shall pay liquidated damages to the Government in the inserted amount for each calendar day of delay until the work is completed or accepted [@CL-SUP-0425]. If the Government terminates the Contractor's right to proceed, liquidated damages will continue to accrue until the work is completed [@CL-SUP-0426]. These liquidated damages are in addition to excess costs of repurchase under the Termination clause [@CL-SUP-0427]. The RFO text prescribes 52.211-11 in 11.403(a) [@CL-SUP-9121] and 52.211-12 in 11.403(b) [@CL-SUP-9123]; the codified text prescribes them in 11.503(a) [@CL-SUP-9122] and 11.503(b) [@CL-SUP-9124].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A supply contract sets liquidated damages at 500 per calendar day. The Contractor misses the delivery date, and the delay is within its control. The Government terminates the contract under the Default—Fixed-Price Supply and Service clause and buys similar supplies elsewhere. The Government reasonably obtains the similar supplies 20 calendar days after the specified delivery date, so liquidated damages are 20 times 500, or 10,000, in place of actual damages. The Government pays 8,000 more than the contract price for the repurchased supplies, and that excess cost is charged under the Termination clause in addition to the 10,000.

Had the same delay been beyond the Contractor's control and without its fault or negligence, as defined in the Default clause, no liquidated damages would be charged under the supplies clause.

A construction contract sets liquidated damages at 1,000 per calendar day. The Government terminates the Contractor's right to proceed, and the work is completed 40 calendar days after the time specified in the contract. Liquidated damages continue to accrue until the work is completed, so they are 40 times 1,000, or 40,000, in addition to any excess costs of repurchase under the Termination clause.

## Never confuse

Under both clauses, liquidated damages are charged in addition to excess costs of repurchase under the Termination clause [@CL-SUP-2570; @CL-SUP-2574]. Under the supplies clause the daily amount is paid in place of actual damages [@CL-SUP-2568]. After a termination under the Default clause, supplies liquidated damages accrue until similar supplies or services are reasonably obtained [@CL-SUP-2569], and for construction, after a termination of the right to proceed, they continue until the work is completed [@CL-SUP-2573].
