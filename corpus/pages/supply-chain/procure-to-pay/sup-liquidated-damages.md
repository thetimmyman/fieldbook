## Purpose

Shows how the two liquidated damages clauses fix a daily amount, when that amount accrues and stops, and how it sits alongside termination for default and excess costs of repurchase.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-SUP-2568] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.211-11 and FAR 52.211-12 [@CL-SUP-2568; @CL-SUP-2572] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.211-11 [@CL-SUP-0421]; RFO FAR 52.211-12 [@CL-SUP-0425] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-2568] |
| Applies to | Not established. |

Under the clause for supplies, services, or research and development, if the Contractor fails to deliver the supplies or perform the services within the time specified in the contract, it pays the Government liquidated damages of the amount inserted per calendar day of delay, in place of actual damages [@CL-SUP-2568; @CL-SUP-9709]. If the Government terminates the contract in whole or in part under the Default—Fixed-Price Supply and Service clause, the Contractor is liable for liquidated damages accruing until the Government reasonably obtains delivery or performance of similar supplies or services [@CL-SUP-2569]. These liquidated damages are in addition to excess costs of repurchase under the Termination clause [@CL-SUP-2570]. The Contractor is not charged when the delay in delivery or performance is beyond its control and without its fault or negligence as defined in the Default—Fixed-Price Supply and Service clause in the contract [@CL-SUP-2571].

Under the construction clause, if the Contractor fails to complete the work within the time specified in the contract, it pays liquidated damages to the Government in the inserted amount for each calendar day of delay until the work is completed or accepted [@CL-SUP-2572]. If the Government terminates the Contractor's right to proceed, liquidated damages continue to accrue until the work is completed [@CL-SUP-2573]. These liquidated damages are in addition to excess costs of repurchase under the Termination clause [@CL-SUP-2574].

## How it works

Both clauses state the amount per calendar day, and the Contracting Officer inserts the amount, so it is a contract-specific blank [@CL-SUP-2568; @CL-SUP-9709; @CL-SUP-2572]. In the supplies clause the amount stands in place of actual damages [@CL-SUP-2568], and after a termination under the Default clause it keeps accruing until the Government reasonably obtains similar supplies or services [@CL-SUP-2569]. In the construction clause the amount runs until the work is completed or accepted [@CL-SUP-2572], and a termination of the right to proceed does not stop it before the work is completed [@CL-SUP-2573].

The daily amount does not replace excess costs of repurchase: each clause states that the liquidated damages are in addition to those costs under the Termination clause [@CL-SUP-2570; @CL-SUP-2574].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text from February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. Section 52.211-11 of the RFO text keeps the supplies, services, or research and development rules: if the Contractor fails to deliver the supplies or perform the services within the time specified in the contract, it pays the Government liquidated damages, in place of actual damages, of the amount the Contracting Officer inserts per calendar day of delay [@CL-SUP-0421; @CL-SUP-2568; @CL-SUP-9709]; if the Government terminates the contract in whole or in part under the Default-Fixed-Price Supply and Service clause, the Contractor is liable for liquidated damages accruing until the Government reasonably obtains delivery or performance of similar supplies or services [@CL-SUP-0422; @CL-SUP-2569]; these liquidated damages are in addition to excess costs of repurchase under the Termination clause [@CL-SUP-0423; @CL-SUP-2570]; and the Contractor will not be charged when the delay is beyond the control and without the fault or negligence of the Contractor as defined in the Default-Fixed-Price Supply and Service clause in the contract [@CL-SUP-0424; @CL-SUP-2571]. Section 52.211-12 keeps the construction rules: if the Contractor fails to complete the work within the time specified in the contract, it pays liquidated damages in the amount the Contracting Officer inserts for each calendar day of delay until the work is completed or accepted [@CL-SUP-0425; @CL-SUP-2572]; if the Government terminates the Contractor's right to proceed, liquidated damages continue to accrue until the work is completed [@CL-SUP-0426; @CL-SUP-2573]; and these liquidated damages are in addition to excess costs of repurchase under the Termination clause [@CL-SUP-0427; @CL-SUP-2574]. The prescriptions differ: the RFO text prescribes 52.211-11 in 11.403(a) [@CL-SUP-9121], where the codified text prescribes it in 11.503(a) [@CL-SUP-9122], and prescribes 52.211-12 in 11.403(b) [@CL-SUP-9123], where the codified text prescribes it in 11.503(b) [@CL-SUP-9124].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A supply contract sets liquidated damages at 500 per calendar day. The Contractor misses the delivery date, and the delay is within its control. The Government terminates the contract under the Default—Fixed-Price Supply and Service clause and buys similar supplies elsewhere. The Government reasonably obtains the similar supplies 20 calendar days after the specified delivery date, so liquidated damages are 20 times 500, or 10,000, in place of actual damages. The Government pays 8,000 more than the contract price for the repurchased supplies, and that excess cost is charged under the Termination clause in addition to the 10,000.

Had the same delay been beyond the Contractor's control and without its fault or negligence, as defined in the Default clause, no liquidated damages would be charged under the supplies clause.

A construction contract sets liquidated damages at 1,000 per calendar day. The Government terminates the Contractor's right to proceed, and the work is completed 40 calendar days after the time specified in the contract. Liquidated damages continue to accrue until the work is completed, so they are 40 times 1,000, or 40,000, in addition to any excess costs of repurchase under the Termination clause.

## Never confuse

Liquidated damages are not the Government's repurchase costs. The daily amount stands in place of actual damages for late delivery or performance under the supplies clause [@CL-SUP-2568], and under both clauses it is charged in addition to excess costs of repurchase under the Termination clause [@CL-SUP-2570; @CL-SUP-2574]. A termination does not end the daily charge: it runs until similar supplies or services are reasonably obtained [@CL-SUP-2569], or, for construction, until the work is completed [@CL-SUP-2573].
