## Purpose

Shows the value engineering duties of an architect-engineer contractor, what it submits after award, what each value engineering proposal must contain, and why the contractor is paid for the effort but takes no part of the savings.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-SUP-3113] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.248-2 [@CL-SUP-3113] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.248-2 [@CL-SUP-2644] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-3113] |
| Applies to | Contracts that include the architect-engineer value engineering clause [@CL-SUP-3113] |

The contractor performs value engineering services, submits progress reports as the Schedule specifies, and submits to the contracting officer any resulting value engineering proposals [@CL-SUP-3113]. The services include value engineering evaluation and review and study of design documents immediately following completion of the 35 percent design stage, or at such stages as the contracting officer may direct [@CL-SUP-3114]. The activities are performed concurrently with, and without delay to, the schedule set forth in the contract [@CL-SUP-3115]. Each separately priced line item for value engineering services defines the scope of work specifically and may include studies of items other than design documents [@CL-SUP-3191]. The contractor is paid as the contract specifies for the effort and does not share in savings that may result from the Government's acceptance and use of proposals [@CL-SUP-3116].

Value engineering is an organized effort to analyze the functions of systems, equipment, facilities, services and supplies to achieve the essential functions at the lowest life cycle cost consistent with required performance, reliability, quality and safety [@CL-SUP-3193]. Life cycle cost is the sum of all costs over the useful life of a building, system or product, including the cost of design, construction, acquisition, operation, maintenance and salvage (resale) value, if any [@CL-SUP-3192]. In this clause a value engineering proposal, in connection with an architect-engineer contract, is a change proposal developed by employees of the Federal Government or contractor value engineering personnel under contract to an agency to provide value engineering services for the contract or program [@CL-SUP-3117].

The FAR's general definitions say that, under paragraph (1) of the definition, architect-engineer services, as defined in section 1102 of title 40 of the United States Code, include professional services of an architectural or engineering nature, as defined by State law, if applicable, that are required to be performed or approved by a person licensed, registered, or certified to provide those services [@CL-SUP-9544]. The definition's other two paragraphs are not summarized here.

After award, the contractor provides the Government with a fee breakdown schedule for the value engineering services included in the contract schedule [@CL-SUP-3194]. It submits, for the contracting officer's approval, a list of team members and their resumes representing the engineering disciplines required to complete the study effort, and evidence of the team leader's qualifications and engineering discipline [@CL-SUP-3195]. Later changes or substitutions to the approved team go to the contracting officer in writing for approval [@CL-SUP-3196]. The team leader is responsible for prestudy work assembly, and edits, reproduces and signs the final report and each proposal [@CL-SUP-3197]. All proposals, even if submitted earlier as an individual submission, are contained in the final report [@CL-SUP-3198].

As a minimum, the contractor includes the following in each proposal [@CL-SUP-3199]:

- A description of the difference between the existing and the proposed design, the comparative advantages and disadvantages of each, a justification when an item's function is being altered, the effect of the change on system or facility performance, and any pertinent objective test data [@CL-SUP-3118].
- A list and analysis of the design criteria or specifications that must be changed if the proposal is accepted [@CL-SUP-3200].
- A separate detailed estimate of the impact on project cost of each proposal, if accepted and implemented by the Government [@CL-SUP-3119].
- A description and estimate of the costs the Government may incur in implementing the proposal, such as design change cost and test and evaluation cost [@CL-SUP-3120].
- A prediction of any effects the proposed change may have on life cycle cost [@CL-SUP-3201].
- The effect the proposal will have on design or construction schedules [@CL-SUP-3202].

Approved proposals are implemented by bilateral modification to the contract [@CL-SUP-3121].

## How it works

After award the contractor files the fee breakdown schedule and gets the study team approved, and the study follows the design documents at the stage the clause sets or the contracting officer directs [@CL-SUP-3194; @CL-SUP-3195; @CL-SUP-3114]. The team leader gathers the work and signs a final report that holds every proposal, each with the minimum content [@CL-SUP-3197; @CL-SUP-3198; @CL-SUP-3199]. Acceptance is implemented by bilateral modification to the contract [@CL-SUP-3121]. Payment follows the contract for the effort, and no savings share follows an accepted proposal [@CL-SUP-3116].

Class Deviation 2026-O0038 directs DoD contracting officers to use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text, effective February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. RFO FAR 52.248-2 keeps these rules: the contractor performs value engineering services, submits progress reports as specified in the Schedule, and submits to the contracting officer any resulting proposals [@CL-SUP-2644] [@CL-SUP-3113]. The services include evaluation and review and study of design documents immediately following completion of the 35 percent design stage or at such stages as the contracting officer may direct [@CL-SUP-2645] [@CL-SUP-3114]. The contractor is paid as the contract specifies for the effort but does not share in savings which may result from the Government's acceptance and use of proposals [@CL-SUP-2646] [@CL-SUP-3116]. Approved proposals are implemented by bilateral modification to the contract [@CL-SUP-2647] [@CL-SUP-3121].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An architect-engineer contract has a separately priced line item of 40,000 for value engineering services, and the line item states the scope of the study. After award the contractor gives the Government a fee breakdown for the 40,000 and a list of team members with resumes and the team leader's qualifications. The design reaches 35 percent complete, and the study starts immediately after. The team leader signs a final report that holds 3 proposals, one of which was sent earlier on its own. One proposal changes the mechanical plant and cuts the estimated project cost by 250,000. It also describes the Government's own implementation cost of 15,000 for design change and testing, and the effect on the design schedule. The Government accepts it by bilateral modification. The answer: the contractor is paid the 40,000 as the contract specifies, takes no share of the savings from the 250,000, and the change is implemented by bilateral modification.

## Never confuse

A proposal here is a change proposal, not the study that produced it [@CL-SUP-3117]. Sharing is also the difference from the construction clause: the construction contractor shares in instant contract savings, while this contractor is paid for the work and takes no share of the savings an accepted proposal produces [@CL-SUP-3122; @CL-SUP-3116]. The general value engineering card, for other contracts, is [value engineering general](../../program-finance/evms/prg-far-value-engineering-general.md).
