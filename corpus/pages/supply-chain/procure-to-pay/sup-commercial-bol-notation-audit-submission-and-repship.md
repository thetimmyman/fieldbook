## Purpose

Sets out three related shipping duties: what the contractor writes on commercial shipping documents, which transportation documents go to a prepayment audit, and when a prepaid notice of shipment and a report of shipment go to the transportation officer.

| Codified citation | FAR 52.247-1, 52.247-67 and 52.247-68 [@CL-SUP-2011; @CL-SUP-2014; @CL-SUP-2017] |

When the contracting officer authorizes supplies to be shipped on a commercial bill of lading and the contractor will be reimbursed those transportation costs as direct allowable costs, the contractor ensures before shipment that the commercial shipping documents carry one of the two notations in the clause [@CL-SUP-2141; @CL-SUP-2011]. The paragraph (a) notation is the one to use if the Government is shown as the consignor or the consignee, and it says the actual total transportation charges paid to the carrier by the consignor or consignee are assignable to, and shall be reimbursed by, the Government [@CL-SUP-2142; @CL-SUP-2012]. The paragraph (b) notation is the one to use if the Government is not shown as the consignor or the consignee, and it says the actual total transportation charges paid to the carrier by the consignor or consignee shall be reimbursed by the Government pursuant to a stated cost-reimbursement contract number and that this may be confirmed by contacting the contract administration office listed in the contract [@CL-SUP-2143; @CL-SUP-2013].

## How it works

The contractor submits, for prepayment audit, the transportation documents for freight charges the United States will assume that were paid by the contractor under a cost-reimbursement contract and by a first-tier subcontractor under a cost-reimbursement subcontract thereunder [@CL-SUP-2014]. The contracting officer fills in the address for the submission [@CL-SUP-2144]. A cost-reimbursement contractor submits for audit only those bills of lading with freight shipment charges exceeding $100, and bills under $100 are retained on-site by the contractor and made available for on-site audits [@CL-SUP-2015]. The exception applies only to freight shipment bills and is not intended to apply to bills and invoices for any other transportation services [@CL-SUP-2016].

Unless the contracting officer directs otherwise, the contractor sends a prepaid notice of shipment to the consignee transportation officer in one of two alternatives [@CL-SUP-2017; @CL-SUP-3858]. The first alternative is all shipments of classified material, protected sensitive and protected controlled material, the listed explosives and poisons, and radioactive materials requiring the use of a III bar label [@CL-SUP-2018]. The second alternative is a truckload or carload shipment of supplies weighing 20,000 pounds or more, or a lighter shipment that occupies the full visible capacity of a railway car or motor vehicle [@CL-SUP-2019]. The second alternative applies when the shipment is given to any carrier for transportation to a domestic destination other than a port for export [@CL-SUP-2020]. A domestic destination is a destination within the contiguous United States or, if shipment originates in Alaska or Hawaii, a destination in Alaska or Hawaii, respectively [@CL-SUP-2145]. Unless the contracting officer directs otherwise, the contractor also transmits the notice by rapid means so that the consignee transportation officer receives it at least 24 hours before the shipment arrives [@CL-SUP-2017; @CL-SUP-2021], and sends the receiving transportation officer the bill of lading or a letter or other document with the information the clause shows, prominently identified as a Report of Shipment or REPSHIP FOR T.O. [@CL-SUP-2017; @CL-SUP-2146].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text from February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. RFO FAR 52.247-1 keeps the notation duty: when the Contracting Officer authorizes supplies to be shipped on a commercial bill of lading and the Contractor will be reimbursed these transportation costs as direct allowable costs, the Contractor shall ensure before shipment is made that the commercial shipping documents are annotated with either of the following notations, as appropriate [@CL-SUP-0085; @CL-SUP-0086; @CL-SUP-2011]. It keeps the paragraph (a) notation, which is the one to use if the Government is shown as the consignor or the consignee, and which says the actual total transportation charges paid to the carrier(s) by the consignor or consignee are assignable to, and shall be reimbursed by, the Government [@CL-SUP-0087; @CL-SUP-0088; @CL-SUP-2142; @CL-SUP-2012]. It keeps the paragraph (b) notation, which is the one to use if the Government is not shown as the consignor or the consignee, and which says the actual total transportation charges paid to the carrier(s) by the consignor or consignee shall be reimbursed by the Government pursuant to a stated cost-reimbursement contract number and that this may be confirmed by contacting the contract administration office listed in the contract [@CL-SUP-0089; @CL-SUP-0090; @CL-SUP-0091; @CL-SUP-2143; @CL-SUP-2013]. RFO FAR 52.247-67 keeps the audit duty for transportation documents on which the United States will assume freight charges that were paid by the Contractor under a cost-reimbursement contract and by a first-tier subcontractor under a cost-reimbursement subcontract thereunder [@CL-SUP-0092; @CL-SUP-0093; @CL-SUP-2014]. It keeps the rule that cost-reimbursement Contractors only submit for audit those bills of lading with freight shipment charges exceeding $100, and that bills under $100 are retained on-site and made available for on-site audits [@CL-SUP-0094; @CL-SUP-2015], and the limit of that exception to freight shipment bills, not bills and invoices for any other transportation services [@CL-SUP-0095; @CL-SUP-2016]. It keeps the address that the Contracting Officer fills in [@CL-SUP-0096; @CL-SUP-2144]. RFO FAR 52.247-68 keeps the prepaid notice of shipment to the consignee transportation officer, unless the Contracting Officer directs otherwise [@CL-SUP-0098; @CL-SUP-2017], in one of two alternatives [@CL-SUP-3859; @CL-SUP-3858]: all shipments of the listed classified, protected, explosive, poison and radioactive material [@CL-SUP-0099; @CL-SUP-2018], or a truckload or carload shipment of supplies weighing 20,000 pounds or more, or a lighter shipment that occupies the full visible capacity of a railway car or motor vehicle [@CL-SUP-0100; @CL-SUP-2019], when it is given to any carrier (common, contract, or private) for transportation to a domestic destination other than a port for export [@CL-SUP-0111; @CL-SUP-2020]. It keeps the definition of a domestic destination [@CL-SUP-0097; @CL-SUP-2145] and, unless the Contracting Officer directs otherwise [@CL-SUP-0098; @CL-SUP-2017], the notice transmitted by rapid means to be received by the consignee transportation officer at least 24 hours before the arrival of the shipment [@CL-SUP-0112; @CL-SUP-2021], and the bill of lading or letter or other document identified as a Report of Shipment or REPSHIP FOR T.O. [@CL-SUP-0113; @CL-SUP-2146].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A cost-reimbursement contractor ships supplies on a commercial bill of lading that the contracting officer authorized, and the Government is shown as the consignee, so the shipping documents carry the paragraph (a) notation before the shipment moves. The contractor pays $4,000 of freight on that bill, which exceeds $100, so the bill goes to the audit address the contracting officer filled in. A $60 freight bill from the same week is under $100 and stays on site for on-site audit. The shipment is a 21,000-pound truckload bound for a depot in the contiguous United States, which meets the 20,000-pound trigger, so unless the contracting officer directs otherwise a prepaid notice goes by rapid means to the consignee transportation officer. If the truck is due at 14:00 on a Thursday, the notice must be received no later than 14:00 on the Wednesday before. The bill of lading, or a letter identified as REPSHIP FOR T.O., also goes to the receiving transportation officer.

## Never confuse

The notation is made on the commercial shipping documents before shipment when the contracting officer authorizes the commercial bill of lading and the costs are reimbursed as direct allowable costs [@CL-SUP-2141; @CL-SUP-2011], unlike the audit submission, which covers the documents for freight charges the United States will assume and goes to the address the contracting officer fills in [@CL-SUP-2014; @CL-SUP-2144].
