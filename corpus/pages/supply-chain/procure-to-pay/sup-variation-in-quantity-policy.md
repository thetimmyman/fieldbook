## Purpose

Shows the quantity tolerance a fixed-price supply contract may allow, how excess deliveries are handled, when a construction quantity variation moves the price or the completion date, and which clause goes into each kind of contract.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-SUP-3306] |
| Codified citation | FAR 11.701, 11.702 and 11.703 [@CL-SUP-3306; @CL-SUP-3309; @CL-SUP-3311] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-3306] |
| Applies to | Fixed-price supply contracts, services that involve the furnishing of supplies, and construction contracts with unit-priced items [@CL-SUP-3306; @CL-SUP-3311; @CL-SUP-3309] |

A fixed-price supply contract may authorize Government acceptance of a variation in the quantity of items called for if the variation is caused by conditions of loading, shipping or packing, or by allowances in manufacturing processes [@CL-SUP-3306]. Any permissible variation must be stated as a percentage and may be an increase, a decrease or a combination of both, but contracts for subsistence items may use other applicable terms of variation in quantity [@CL-SUP-3358].

There should be no standard or usual variation percentage [@CL-SUP-3359]. The overrun or underrun permitted in each contract should be based upon the normal commercial practices of a particular industry for a particular item, and the permitted percentage should be no larger than is necessary to afford a contractor reasonable protection [@CL-SUP-3360]. The permissible variation may not exceed plus or minus 10 percent unless a different limitation is established in agency regulations [@CL-SUP-3307]. Consideration must be given to the quantity to which the percentage variation applies, and when delivery will be made to multiple destinations and the variation is to apply to the item quantity for each destination, this requirement must be stated in the contract [@CL-SUP-3361].

Contractors are responsible for delivery of the specified quantity of items in a fixed-price contract, within allowable variations, if any [@CL-SUP-3362]. The contract may include the clause at FAR 52.211-17, Delivery of Excess Quantities, to provide that excess quantities of items totaling up to $250 in value may be retained without compensating the contractor [@CL-SUP-3308]. The clause may also provide that excess quantities totaling over $250 in value may, at the Government's option, be either returned at the contractor's expense or retained and paid for at the contract unit price [@CL-SUP-3363].

Construction contracts may authorize a variation in estimated quantities of unit-priced items [@CL-SUP-3364]. When the variation between the estimated and actual quantity of a unit-priced item is more than plus or minus 15 percent, an equitable adjustment in the contract price must be made upon the demand of either the Government or the contractor [@CL-SUP-3309]. The contractor may request an extension of time if the variation increases the time necessary for completion, and the contracting officer must receive the request in writing within 10 days from the beginning of the period of delay [@CL-SUP-3310]. The contracting officer may extend that 10-day limit before the date of final settlement of the contract [@CL-SUP-3365], and must ascertain the facts and make any adjustment for extending the completion date that the findings justify [@CL-SUP-3366].

## How it works

The contracting officer must insert the clause at FAR 52.211-16, Variation in Quantity, in solicitations and contracts if authorizing a variation in quantity in fixed-price contracts for supplies or for services that involve the furnishing of supplies [@CL-SUP-3311]. The contracting officer may insert the clause at FAR 52.211-17, Delivery of Excess Quantities, when a fixed-price supply contract is contemplated [@CL-SUP-3367]. The contracting officer must insert the clause at FAR 52.211-18, Variation in Estimated Quantity, when a fixed-price construction contract is contemplated that authorizes a variation in the estimated quantity of unit-priced items [@CL-SUP-3312].

The supply rule is a tolerance around the stated quantity, while the construction rule is a price adjustment trigger when the actual quantity of a unit-priced item departs from the estimate by more than plus or minus 15 percent [@CL-SUP-3306; @CL-SUP-3309]. The time relief on construction has its own written-request deadline, which the contracting officer may extend before final settlement [@CL-SUP-3310; @CL-SUP-3365]. The clause mechanics are set out in [Variation in quantity and excess deliveries](../procure-to-pay/sup-variation-in-quantity-and-excess-deliveries.md) and [Variation in estimated quantity](../procure-to-pay/sup-variation-in-estimated-quantity.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A fixed-price supply contract for 10,000 units authorizes a variation stated as plus or minus 10 percent, so 9,000 to 11,000 units are within the allowable variation [@CL-SUP-3358; @CL-SUP-3307]. The contract includes the Delivery of Excess Quantities clause. A shipment of 11,180 units is 180 units over the allowable maximum, and at 2 each the excess is worth 360. That is over 250, so the Government may, at its option, return the excess at the contractor's expense or keep it and pay 360 at the contract unit price [@CL-SUP-3363]. Had the excess been worth 250 or less, the Government could have kept it without compensating the contractor [@CL-SUP-3308].

On a fixed-price construction line item estimated at 4,000 cubic yards, the actual quantity is 4,700, a variation of 17.5 percent. That is more than plus or minus 15 percent, so either party may demand an equitable adjustment in the contract price [@CL-SUP-3309]. Had the actual quantity been 4,500, a variation of 12.5 percent, this trigger would not have been met [@CL-SUP-3309]. If the added quantity increases the time needed for completion, the contractor's written request for more time must reach the contracting officer within 10 days from the beginning of the period of delay, a limit the contracting officer may extend before final settlement [@CL-SUP-3310; @CL-SUP-3365].

## Never confuse

Variation in quantity is a tolerance that a fixed-price supply contract may itself authorize, stated as a percentage [@CL-SUP-3306; @CL-SUP-3358]. It is also not an excess-delivery rule: a delivery above the contract requirements plus any allowable variation is the case that the Delivery of Excess Quantities clause, which the contract may include, addresses [@CL-SUP-3395; @CL-SUP-3308; @CL-SUP-3367].

The invitation to comment on economic purchase quantities, and the option to purchase leased or rented equipment, are covered in [Economic purchase quantity for supplies and option to purchase equipment](../../estimating/pricing/est-economic-purchase-quantity-and-equipment-option.md).
