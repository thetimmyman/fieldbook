## Purpose

Shows which variations in quantity the Government accepts, how the contract states the percentage limits and what they apply to, and how quantities above the allowance are kept, returned or paid for.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-SUP-2578] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.211-16 and FAR 52.211-17 [@CL-SUP-2578; @CL-SUP-2581] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-2578] |

A variation in the quantity of any item called for by the contract will not be accepted unless conditions of loading, shipping or packing, or allowances in manufacturing processes, caused it, and then only to the extent, if any, specified in paragraph (b) of the clause [@CL-SUP-2578]. That paragraph limits the permissible variation to a percent increase and a percent decrease that the Contracting Officer inserts [@CL-SUP-2579]; the percentages are contract-specific blanks.

The percentages apply to the designation the Contracting Officer inserts in the blank, such as the total contract quantity, item 1 only, or each quantity specified in the delivery schedule [@CL-SUP-2580]. The designation may also be the total item quantity for each destination or the total quantity of each item without regard to destination [@CL-SUP-2621].

The Contractor is responsible for the delivery of each item quantity within allowable variations, if any [@CL-SUP-2581].

## How it works

If the Contractor delivers and the Government receives quantities of an item in excess of the quantity called for, after considering any allowable variation in quantity, the excess quantities are treated as delivered for the convenience of the Contractor [@CL-SUP-2582]. The Government may retain excess quantities up to 250 dollars in value without compensating the Contractor, and the Contractor waives all right, title or interests in them [@CL-SUP-2583]. Quantities in excess of 250 dollars are, at the option of the Government, either returned at the Contractor's expense or retained and paid for by the Government at the contract unit price [@CL-SUP-2584].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contract calls for 10,000 units at a contract unit price of 10, with a permitted increase of 2 percent applied to the total contract quantity, so up to 10,200 units fall within the allowance. Assume the extra units come from allowances in the manufacturing process.

The Contractor delivers 10,350 units, and the Government receives them. The 150 units above the allowance are excess quantities treated as delivered for the convenience of the Contractor. They are worth 1,500 at the unit price, which is more than 250, so the Government chooses whether to return them at the Contractor's expense or to retain them and pay for them at the contract unit price.

Had the Contractor delivered 10,220 units, the 20 units above the allowance would be worth 200, which is not more than 250. The Government may retain them without compensating the Contractor, and the Contractor waives all right, title or interests in them.

## Never confuse

The permitted percentage variation is not the same as an excess quantity. A variation is accepted only if loading, shipping, packing or manufacturing allowances caused it, and only to the extent of the percentages [@CL-SUP-2578; @CL-SUP-2579]. A quantity above the contract quantity after considering any allowable variation is an excess, which the Government may keep without payment up to 250 dollars in value or, above that value, return at the Contractor's expense or keep and pay for at the unit price [@CL-SUP-2582; @CL-SUP-2583; @CL-SUP-2584].

## Company calibration

Confirm how received quantities are reconciled against the contract quantity and the inserted designation before any excess is accepted or credited [@Q-021].
