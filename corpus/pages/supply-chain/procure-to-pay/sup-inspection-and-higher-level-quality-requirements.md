## Purpose

Shows who inspects under the contractor inspection clause, what the Government may inspect and evaluate under the short-form research and development clause, and which quality standard binds, and which subcontracts carry it, under the higher-level quality clause.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-SUP-3105; @CL-SUP-3107; @CL-SUP-3109] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.246-1, FAR 52.246-9 and FAR 52.246-11 [@CL-SUP-3105; @CL-SUP-3107; @CL-SUP-3109] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.246-1 [@CL-SUP-0408]; RFO FAR 52.246-9 [@CL-SUP-0410]; RFO FAR 52.246-11 [@CL-SUP-0412] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-3105; @CL-SUP-3107; @CL-SUP-3109] |
| Applies to | Contracts that include one or more of the three clauses, each rule below applying only where its own clause is included [@CL-SUP-3105; @CL-SUP-3107; @CL-SUP-3109] |

Contractor Inspection Requirements: the contractor is responsible for performing or having performed all inspections and tests necessary to substantiate that the supplies or services furnished under the contract conform to contract requirements, including any applicable technical requirements for specified manufacturers' parts [@CL-SUP-3105]. This clause takes precedence over any Government inspection and testing required in the contract's specifications, except for specialized inspections or tests specified to be performed solely by the Government [@CL-SUP-3106].

Inspection of Research and Development (Short Form): the Government has the right to inspect and evaluate the work performed or being performed under the contract, and the premises where the work is being performed, at all reasonable times and in a manner that will not unduly delay the work [@CL-SUP-3107]. If the Government inspects or evaluates on the premises of the contractor or a subcontractor, the contractor furnishes, and requires subcontractors to furnish, all reasonable facilities and assistance for the safe and convenient performance of these duties [@CL-SUP-3108].

Higher-Level Contract Quality Requirement: the contractor complies with the higher-level quality standard or standards listed in the clause [@CL-SUP-3109]. The contracting officer inserts the title, number, date and tailoring, if any, of those standards, so the listed standards are contract-specific [@CL-SUP-3190]. The contractor includes the applicable requirements of the listed standards, and the requirement to flow down such standards as applicable, in lower-tier subcontracts of the two kinds described next [@CL-SUP-3110]. The first kind is any subcontract for critical and complex items, a term for which the clause cross-refers to FAR 46.203(b) and (c) [@CL-SUP-3111]. The second kind is a subcontract whose technical requirements require control of such things as design, work operations, in-process control, testing and inspection, or attention to such factors as organization, planning, work instructions, documentation control and advanced metrology [@CL-SUP-3112].

## How it works

Under the contractor inspection clause, inspection is a contractor duty: the contractor is responsible for performing or having performed the inspections and tests necessary to substantiate that the supplies or services conform [@CL-SUP-3105]. The Government inspection and testing that the specifications require gives way to that clause, except for specialized inspections or tests that the specifications reserve solely to the Government [@CL-SUP-3106]. The short-form research and development clause instead states a Government right to inspect and evaluate, with a duty on the contractor to furnish facilities and assistance when the Government does so on its premises or a subcontractor's [@CL-SUP-3107; @CL-SUP-3108]. The higher-level quality clause adds a listed standard that the contractor meets itself and carries into the subcontracts described above [@CL-SUP-3109; @CL-SUP-3110]. This card does not restate acceptance, rejection or correction rules; they sit in the inspection clauses for each contract type, such as those on the linked cards.

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text from February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. Section 52.246-1 of the RFO text keeps the rule that the Contractor is responsible for performing or having performed all inspections and tests necessary to substantiate that the supplies or services furnished under the contract conform to contract requirements, including any applicable technical requirements for specified manufacturers' parts [@CL-SUP-0408; @CL-SUP-3105], and that the clause takes precedence over any Government inspection and testing required in the contract's specifications, except for specialized inspections or tests specified to be performed solely by the Government [@CL-SUP-0409; @CL-SUP-3106]. Section 52.246-9 keeps the Government's right to inspect and evaluate the work performed or being performed under the contract, and the premises where the work is being performed, at all reasonable times and in a manner that will not unduly delay the work [@CL-SUP-0410; @CL-SUP-3107], and the Contractor's duty, if the Government performs inspection or evaluation on the premises of the Contractor or a subcontractor, to furnish and require subcontractors to furnish all reasonable facilities and assistance for the safe and convenient performance of these duties [@CL-SUP-0411; @CL-SUP-3108]. Section 52.246-11 keeps the rule that the Contractor complies with the higher-level quality standard or standards listed in the clause [@CL-SUP-0412; @CL-SUP-3109], with the Contracting Officer inserting the title, number, date, and tailoring, if any [@CL-SUP-0413; @CL-SUP-3190]. It also keeps the requirement to include applicable requirements of those standards, and the requirement to flow down such standards as applicable, in lower-tier subcontracts for critical and complex items, with the cross-reference to 46.203(b) and (c), or whose technical requirements require control of such things as design, work operations, in-process control, testing, and inspection, or attention to such factors as organization, planning, work instructions, documentation control, and advanced metrology [@CL-SUP-0414; @CL-SUP-0415; @CL-SUP-0416; @CL-SUP-3110; @CL-SUP-3111; @CL-SUP-3112].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A supply contract that includes the contractor inspection clause calls for 500 machined housings. The specifications call for a Government test of the housings and reserve one pressure test to the Government alone. The contractor runs its own dimension and material tests on all 500 and keeps the records. The answer: the contractor is responsible for the inspections and tests needed to substantiate conformance, and the clause takes precedence over the Government test in the specifications, except for the pressure test that the specifications reserve solely to the Government.

A research and development contract includes the short form, and a Government evaluator visits a subcontractor's site. The answer: the Government may inspect and evaluate at reasonable times without unduly delaying the work, and the contractor furnishes reasonable facilities and assistance for safe and convenient performance and requires the subcontractor to do the same.

A third contract lists a synthetic Standard X in the higher-level quality clause. A subcontract to make a critical and complex item must carry the applicable requirements of Standard X and the flow-down requirement. A subcontract for another item carries them as well if its technical requirements require control of testing and inspection, but the contractor does not choose the standard, because the contracting officer inserts it.

## Never confuse

The standard is not supplied by the higher-level quality clause itself: the contracting officer inserts it, and the contractor's obligation is to meet the listed standard and flow it down [@CL-SUP-3190; @CL-SUP-3109; @CL-SUP-3110]. The Government's right to inspect and evaluate under the short form is also not the contractor's inspection duty under the contractor inspection clause: they sit in different clauses, and each applies only where its clause is in the contract [@CL-SUP-3105; @CL-SUP-3107].
