## Purpose

Explains the authority and permits that an offeror or contractor must hold under the permits clause and the relocation capability clause, and the site inspection that the inspection provision urges offerors to make.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-SUP-2193] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.247-2, FAR 52.247-3 and FAR 52.247-4 [@CL-SUP-2191; @CL-SUP-2193; @CL-SUP-2201] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.247-2 [@CL-SUP-0643; @CL-SUP-0644; @CL-SUP-0645; @CL-SUP-0646; @CL-SUP-0647]; No RFO equivalent for FAR 52.247-3 or FAR 52.247-4, which the RFO text prints as [Reserved] [@CL-SUP-0648; @CL-SUP-0649] |

| Applies to | The permits clause as prescribed in FAR 47.207-1(a) [@CL-SUP-2241]; the relocation capability clause in solicitations and contracts for transportation or transportation-related services when a Federal office is relocated [@CL-SUP-2202]; the inspection provision in solicitations for transportation or transportation-related services when it is desired for offerors to inspect sites [@CL-SUP-2247] |

The permits clause is inserted as prescribed in FAR 47.207-1(a) [@CL-SUP-2241]. This page does not set out that prescription. In paragraph (a), the offeror marks whether it holds authorization from the Federal Highway Administration or another cognizant regulatory body and, if it does, gives the regulatory body's name and the authorization number [@CL-SUP-2242]. The offeror must furnish the Government copies of the authorization, if requested, before moving the material under any contract awarded [@CL-SUP-2191]. The offeror must also obtain and maintain, at its own expense, any permits, franchises, licenses and other authorities issued by State and local governments [@CL-SUP-2192].

The relocation capability clause is inserted in solicitations and contracts for transportation or transportation-related services when a Federal office is relocated, to ensure that offerors are capable of performing interstate or intrastate moving contracts involving the relocation of Federal offices [@CL-SUP-2202]. Where the contractor performs the move as a carrier within the borders of more than one State, including the District of Columbia (an interstate move), it must have obtained and hold appropriate and current operating authority from the Federal Motor Carrier Safety Administration [@CL-SUP-2193].

The inspection provision is inserted in solicitations for transportation or transportation-related services when it is desired for offerors to inspect the shipping, receiving or other sites to ensure realistic bids [@CL-SUP-2247]. It urges offerors to inspect the shipping and receiving facilities where services are to be performed and to satisfy themselves about all general and local conditions that may affect the cost of contract performance [@CL-SUP-2201]. The provision says offerors are urged to inspect; it does not say they must.

## How it works

Paragraph (b) of the relocation clause covers an intrastate move that the contractor is to perform as a carrier wholly within the borders of one State or the District of Columbia [@CL-SUP-2243]. When that State or the District of Columbia requires it, the contractor must have obtained and hold appropriate and current operating authority from that jurisdiction in the form of a certificate, permit or equivalent license to operate [@CL-SUP-2194]. If the State or the District of Columbia requires no authority to operate, the contractor as carrier must maintain facilities, equipment and a business address within the jurisdiction in which the move is to take place [@CL-SUP-2195].

A Commercial Zone alternative applies if the move is to originate or terminate, or both, within an area of one State or the District of Columbia that is part of a recognized Commercial Zone (the clause cites Subpart B of 49 CFR part 372) whose boundaries encompass portions of more than one State or the District of Columbia [@CL-SUP-2244]. In that case it is sufficient for the contractor as carrier to maintain facilities, equipment and a business address within the Commercial Zone and to hold appropriate operating authority, if required, from the jurisdiction within which it maintains them [@CL-SUP-2196].

If the move will not be performed by the contractor as carrier, it must be performed for the contractor by a carrier operating under a subcontract with the contractor [@CL-SUP-2197]. In the subcontracted case, the contractor is not subject to the requirements of paragraphs (a) and (b) but is responsible for requiring and ensuring that the subcontractor carrier complies with those requirements in every respect [@CL-SUP-2198].

The contractor must be in compliance with the applicable requirements of the clause at least 14 days before the date on which performance is to commence under the terms specified, with an exception for a short period between award and the start of performance [@CL-SUP-2199]. Where the period from the date of award to the date performance is to commence is less than 28 days, the contractor must comply with the applicable requirements midway between the time of award and the time performance commences [@CL-SUP-2200].

Alternate I applies if a Federal office move is intrastate and the contracting officer determines that it is in the Government's interest not to apply the requirements for holding or obtaining State authority to operate within the State, and to maintain a facility within the State or Commercial zone [@CL-SUP-2245]. Paragraph (b) of the basic clause is then deleted, the remaining paragraphs are redesignated, and in the new paragraph (b) the words "paragraphs (a) and (b) above" are replaced with "paragraph (a) above" [@CL-SUP-2246].

Several entries are contract-specific fill-ins. Paragraph (b) of the inspection provision lists the scheduled site visits by location, date and time, and its paragraph (c) names whom offerors may contact, by name and telephone number; both are completed for each solicitation, and this page does not state them [@CL-SUP-2248; @CL-SUP-2249].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text from February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. The RFO text of FAR 52.247-2 keeps the insertion of the permits clause as prescribed in FAR 47.207-1(a) [@CL-SUP-0643; @CL-SUP-2241]. It keeps paragraph (a), in which the offeror marks whether it holds authorization from the Federal Highway Administration or another cognizant regulatory body [@CL-SUP-0644; @CL-SUP-2242], and gives the regulatory body's name and the authorization number if authorization is held [@CL-SUP-0645; @CL-SUP-2242]. It keeps the duty to furnish the Government copies of the authorization, if requested, before moving the material under any contract awarded [@CL-SUP-0646; @CL-SUP-2191], and the duty to obtain and maintain, at the offeror's expense, any permits, franchises, licenses and other authorities issued by State and local governments [@CL-SUP-0647; @CL-SUP-2192]. The RFO text prints FAR 52.247-3 as [Reserved] [@CL-SUP-0648] and prints FAR 52.247-4 as [Reserved] [@CL-SUP-0649]. The relocation capability clause and the inspection provision described above are the codified FAR 52.247-3 and FAR 52.247-4 [@CL-SUP-2202; @CL-SUP-2247].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A Federal office relocation contract is awarded, and performance is to begin 20 days after award. Fewer than 28 days separate award from the start, so the contractor must comply midway, on day 10 after award, and the 14 day lead time does not apply. Had performance been due to begin 60 days after award, the contractor would have had to comply at least 14 days before the start, by day 46.

The move runs from one State into another and the contractor will carry it, so it must hold current operating authority from the Federal Motor Carrier Safety Administration. If it instead subcontracts the haul to a carrier, the contractor is not itself subject to paragraphs (a) and (b), but it must require and ensure that the subcontractor carrier meets them in every respect. If that carrier's authority has lapsed, the contractor has not ensured compliance.

A second move is wholly inside one State that requires no authority to operate. The contractor, as carrier, keeps facilities, equipment and a business address in that State. If the contracting officer applied Alternate I and found it in the Government's interest not to apply the State authority and facility requirements, paragraph (b) would drop out of the clause.

## Never confuse

The authorization in paragraph (a) of the permits clause is not the State and local permits in its paragraph (b): the offeror states the authorization it holds, if any, and must obtain and maintain the permits, franchises, licenses and other authorities at its own expense [@CL-SUP-2242; @CL-SUP-2192].

The permits clause asks the offeror to state its authorization from the Federal Highway Administration or another cognizant regulatory body [@CL-SUP-2242]. The relocation capability clause requires, for an interstate move that the contractor performs as a carrier, authority from the Federal Motor Carrier Safety Administration, and it applies where a Federal office is relocated [@CL-SUP-2193; @CL-SUP-2202].

The inspection provision concerns conditions that may affect the cost of contract performance, not the authority an offeror must hold [@CL-SUP-2201]. The permits clause has an RFO counterpart in FAR 52.247-2 [@CL-SUP-0643; @CL-SUP-2241], but the codified relocation capability clause and inspection provision at FAR 52.247-3 and FAR 52.247-4 [@CL-SUP-2202; @CL-SUP-2247] print as [Reserved] in the RFO text [@CL-SUP-0648; @CL-SUP-0649].

The familiarization with conditions clause and the financial statement provision are covered in [Familiarization with conditions and the financial statement](sup-familiarization-with-conditions-and-financial-statement.md).
