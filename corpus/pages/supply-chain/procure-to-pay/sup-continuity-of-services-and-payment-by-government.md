## Purpose

Shows the transition duties a services contract can impose at expiration, and the payment, title and release rules for dismantling, demolition or removal work where the Government pays the contractor.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-SUP-2918] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.237-3 and FAR 52.237-4 [@CL-SUP-2918; @CL-SUP-2923] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.237-3 [@CL-SUP-0117] and RFO FAR 52.237-4 [@CL-SUP-0127] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-2918] |
| Applies to | Contracts that carry the continuity clause, and solicitations and contracts solely for dismantling, demolition or removal of improvements where the contracting officer determines that the Government will pay the contractor in addition to any title [@CL-SUP-2918; @CL-SUP-2923; @CL-SUP-3005] |

The continuity clause starts from a recognition by the contractor that the services under the contract are vital to the Government and must be continued without interruption, and that on expiration a successor, either the Government or another contractor, may continue them [@CL-SUP-2991]. The contractor agrees to furnish phase-in training and to exercise its best efforts and cooperation to effect an orderly and efficient transition to a successor [@CL-SUP-2918].

On the Contracting Officer's written notice, the contractor furnishes phase-in, phase-out services for up to 90 days after the contract expires and negotiates in good faith a plan with a successor to determine the nature and extent of the services required [@CL-SUP-2919]. The plan specifies a training program and a date for transferring responsibilities for each division of work described in the plan, and it is subject to the Contracting Officer's approval [@CL-SUP-2992]. The contractor provides sufficient experienced personnel during the phase-in, phase-out period to maintain the services at the required level of proficiency [@CL-SUP-2993].

The contractor allows as many personnel as practicable to remain on the job to help the successor maintain the continuity and consistency of the services [@CL-SUP-2920]. It also discloses necessary personnel records and allows the successor to conduct on-site interviews with these employees [@CL-SUP-2994]. If selected employees are agreeable to the change, the contractor releases them at a mutually agreeable date and negotiates transfer of their earned fringe benefits to the successor [@CL-SUP-2921]. The contractor is reimbursed for all reasonable phase-in, phase-out costs, meaning costs incurred within the agreed period after contract expiration that result from phase-in, phase-out operations, and for a fee (profit) not to exceed a pro rata portion of the fee (profit) under the contract [@CL-SUP-2922].

The payment clause is inserted in solicitations and contracts solely for dismantling, demolition or removal of improvements [@CL-SUP-2923], whenever the contracting officer determines that the Government shall make payment to the contractor in addition to any title to property that the contractor may receive under the contract [@CL-SUP-3005]. Under paragraph (a), the Government pays the contractor an amount that is filled in, in full consideration of the performance of the work called for in the Schedule if Alternate I is used and in partial consideration otherwise [@CL-SUP-2995].

The Government makes progress payments monthly as the work proceeds, or at more frequent intervals as the Contracting Officer determines, on estimates the Contracting Officer approves [@CL-SUP-2924]. Except as paragraph (c) provides, the Contracting Officer retains 10 percent of the estimated payment until final completion and acceptance of the contract work [@CL-SUP-2925]. If the Contracting Officer finds satisfactory progress during a payment period, the Contracting Officer may authorize that payment in full without retaining a percentage [@CL-SUP-2996]. On completion and acceptance of a unit or division whose price is stated separately, the Contracting Officer may authorize full payment for it without retaining a percentage [@CL-SUP-2997]. When the work is substantially completed, the Contracting Officer retains an amount considered adequate for the protection of the Government and, at the Contracting Officer's discretion, may release all or a portion of any excess amount [@CL-SUP-2926].

Under paragraph (d) of the basic clause, the contractor receives title to all property to be dismantled or demolished that is not specifically designated as retained by the Government [@CL-SUP-2927]. Title vests in the contractor immediately upon the Government's issuing the notice of award, or, if a performance bond is to be furnished after award, upon the Government's issuance of a notice to proceed with the work [@CL-SUP-2998]. The Government is not responsible for the condition of, or any loss or damage to, the property [@CL-SUP-2999]. If the contractor does not wish to remove any of the property from the site, the Contracting Officer may, on written request, grant permission to leave it on the premises [@CL-SUP-3000]. As a condition of that permission, the contractor agrees to waive any right, title, claim or interest in and to the property [@CL-SUP-3001].

On completion and acceptance of all work and receipt of a properly executed voucher, the Government makes final payment of the amount due [@CL-SUP-2928]. If requested, the contractor releases all claims against the Government arising under the contract, other than any claims it specifically excepts, in stated amounts, from the release [@CL-SUP-3002]. If the contracting officer determines that the Government shall retain all material resulting from the dismantling or demolition work, Alternate I deletes paragraph (d) from the basic clause and renumbers the remaining paragraphs [@CL-SUP-3003].

## How it works

The contractor agrees to furnish phase-in training and to exercise its best efforts and cooperation to effect an orderly and efficient transition to a successor [@CL-SUP-2918], and furnishes the phase-in, phase-out services and negotiates the plan upon the Contracting Officer's written notice [@CL-SUP-2919].

The services run for up to 90 days after expiration, under a plan that names a training program and a transfer date for each division of work and that the Contracting Officer must approve [@CL-SUP-2919; @CL-SUP-2992].

The contractor allows as many personnel as practicable to remain on the job, shares necessary personnel records, allows on-site interviews, and releases employees who agree to the change at a mutually agreeable date with their earned fringe benefits negotiated across to the successor [@CL-SUP-2920; @CL-SUP-2994; @CL-SUP-2921].

The contractor is paid for the phase-in, phase-out work: reasonable costs incurred in the agreed period after expiration, plus a fee (profit) not to exceed a pro rata portion of the fee (profit) under the contract [@CL-SUP-2922].

Payment under the demolition clause runs as the work proceeds. Except as paragraph (c) provides, the Contracting Officer retains 10 percent of the estimated payment until final completion and acceptance of the contract work [@CL-SUP-2925]. The Contracting Officer may authorize full payment without retaining a percentage for a period of satisfactory progress or for a unit or division whose price is stated separately [@CL-SUP-2996; @CL-SUP-2997], and when the work is substantially completed retains an amount considered adequate for the protection of the Government [@CL-SUP-2926].

Title passes to the contractor with the property to be dismantled, and the Government is not responsible for the condition of, or any loss or damage to, the property. If the contractor does not wish to remove any of the property from the site, the Contracting Officer may, upon written request, grant permission to leave it on the premises, and as a condition the contractor agrees to waive any right, title, claim, or interest in and to the property [@CL-SUP-2927; @CL-SUP-2998; @CL-SUP-2999; @CL-SUP-3000; @CL-SUP-3001].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text from February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. RFO FAR 52.237-3 keeps the contractor's recognition that the services under the contract are vital to the Government and must be continued without interruption and that, upon contract expiration, a successor, either the Government or another contractor, may continue them [@CL-SUP-0117; @CL-SUP-2991], and its agreement to furnish phase-in training and to exercise its best efforts and cooperation to effect an orderly and efficient transition to a successor [@CL-SUP-0118; @CL-SUP-2918]. It keeps the duty, upon the Contracting Officer's written notice, to furnish phase-in, phase-out services for up to 90 days after the contract expires and to negotiate in good faith a plan with a successor [@CL-SUP-0119; @CL-SUP-2919], the plan's training program and transfer date for each division of work, subject to the Contracting Officer's approval [@CL-SUP-0120; @CL-SUP-2992], and the duty to provide sufficient experienced personnel to keep the services at the required level of proficiency [@CL-SUP-0121; @CL-SUP-2993]. It keeps the rules that the contractor allows as many personnel as practicable to remain on the job [@CL-SUP-0122; @CL-SUP-2920], discloses necessary personnel records and allows on-site interviews [@CL-SUP-0123; @CL-SUP-2994], and, if selected employees are agreeable to the change, releases them at a mutually agreeable date and negotiates transfer of their earned fringe benefits to the successor [@CL-SUP-0124; @CL-SUP-2921]. It keeps the reimbursement of all reasonable phase-in, phase-out costs incurred within the agreed period after contract expiration, plus a fee (profit) not to exceed a pro rata portion of the fee (profit) under the contract [@CL-SUP-0125; @CL-SUP-2922]. The RFO prescription line for FAR 52.237-4 cites 37.701-4(a) where the codified line cites 37.304(a) [@CL-SUP-9033; @CL-SUP-9034], and says the clause goes in solicitations and contracts solely for dismantling, demolition, or removal of improvements whenever the contracting officer determines that the Government shall make payment to the contractor in addition to any title to property that the contractor may receive [@CL-SUP-0126]. RFO FAR 52.237-4 keeps paragraph (a), under which the Government pays a filled-in amount, with the word full inserted if Alternate I is used and partial otherwise [@CL-SUP-0127; @CL-SUP-2995]. It keeps the monthly progress payments, or payments at more frequent intervals as the Contracting Officer determines, on estimates the Contracting Officer approves [@CL-SUP-0128; @CL-SUP-2924], the authority to pay in full without retaining a percentage if satisfactory progress was achieved during the payment period [@CL-SUP-0130; @CL-SUP-2996] or on completion and acceptance of a separately priced unit or division [@CL-SUP-0131; @CL-SUP-2997], and the paragraph (c) retention of an amount considered adequate for the protection of the Government at substantial completion, with release of all or a portion of any excess at the Contracting Officer's discretion [@CL-SUP-0132; @CL-SUP-2926]. The RFO text states the same rule on retaining 10 percent of the estimated payment until final completion and acceptance of the contract work, except as paragraph (c) provides, but it says "paragraph (c) of this clause" where the codified text says "paragraph (c) below" [@CL-SUP-0129; @CL-SUP-2925]. It keeps the title rules in paragraph (d): the contractor receives title to property to be dismantled or demolished that the Government has not specifically designated as retained [@CL-SUP-0133; @CL-SUP-2927], and title vests upon the notice of award or, if a performance bond is to be furnished after award, upon the notice to proceed [@CL-SUP-0134; @CL-SUP-2998]. The Government is not responsible for the condition of, or any loss or damage to, the property [@CL-SUP-0135; @CL-SUP-2999], the Contracting Officer may, upon written request, let the contractor leave property the contractor does not wish to remove [@CL-SUP-0136; @CL-SUP-3000], and as a condition the contractor agrees to waive any right, title, claim, or interest in the property [@CL-SUP-0137; @CL-SUP-3001]. It keeps the final payment on completion and acceptance of all work and receipt of a properly executed voucher [@CL-SUP-0138; @CL-SUP-2928], the release of claims if requested, other than any claims specifically excepted in stated amounts [@CL-SUP-0139; @CL-SUP-3002], and Alternate I, which deletes paragraph (d) and renumbers the remaining paragraphs if the contracting officer determines that the Government shall retain all material resulting from the dismantling or demolition work [@CL-SUP-0140; @CL-SUP-3003].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

Continuity of services: a 12-month services contract with a total fee of 120,000 is about to expire. The Contracting Officer gives written notice, and the contractor furnishes phase-out services for 45 days after expiry, which is within the 90-day limit. The contractor negotiates a plan with the successor that names a training program and a transfer date for each division of work, and the Contracting Officer approves it. Employees who agree to the change are released on a mutually agreeable date. The contractor's reasonable phase-out costs in the agreed period are 30,000. Taking a 360-day term and a pro rata portion by time, the fee may not exceed 120,000 times 45 over 360, which is 15,000, so the largest total reimbursement is 45,000.

Payment by the Government: a demolition contract where the Contracting Officer has decided the Government will pay in addition to title, and the basic clause is used, so the word partial is inserted, paragraph (d) applies, and the filled-in amount is 200,000. The first monthly estimate approved is 40,000, so 10 percent, which is 4,000, is retained and 36,000 is paid. The Contracting Officer finds satisfactory progress in the second month and authorizes the 50,000 estimate in full. At substantial completion the Contracting Officer retains 3,000 as adequate and releases the other 1,000 of the retained 4,000. The contractor holds title to the property to be dismantled that the Government did not designate as retained, and the Government is not responsible for its condition. The contractor asks in writing to leave some scrap on the premises, the Contracting Officer agrees, and the contractor waives any interest in that scrap. On completion, acceptance and a properly executed voucher, the Government pays the final amount due, and the contractor releases claims if asked, excepting any it lists in stated amounts.

## Never confuse

The continuity clause concerns the transition of the services under the contract to a successor [@CL-SUP-2918; @CL-SUP-2991], and the payment clause is inserted in solicitations and contracts solely for dismantling, demolition or removal of improvements [@CL-SUP-2923]. Under the payment clause, the Contracting Officer retains 10 percent of the estimated payment until final completion and acceptance, except as paragraph (c) provides [@CL-SUP-2925], and may authorize full payment without retaining a percentage for satisfactory progress or for a separately priced unit or division [@CL-SUP-2996; @CL-SUP-2997]. The title rules are in paragraph (d) of the basic clause, and Alternate I deletes paragraph (d) if the contracting officer determines that the Government shall retain all material resulting from the dismantling or demolition work [@CL-SUP-2927; @CL-SUP-3003]. General progress payment mechanics sit on [Progress payments](../../contract-finance/financing/ctf-progress-payments.md).
