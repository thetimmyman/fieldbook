## Purpose

Explains when a contractor must use a United States-flag air carrier for international air transportation, and what it puts on its vouchers when it uses another carrier.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-SUP-2022] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.247-63 [@CL-SUP-2022] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.247-63 [@CL-SUP-0714; @CL-SUP-0715; @CL-SUP-0716; @CL-SUP-0717; @CL-SUP-0718; @CL-SUP-0719; @CL-SUP-0720; @CL-SUP-0721; @CL-SUP-0722; @CL-SUP-0723] |

If available, the contractor, in performing work under the contract, shall use U.S.-flag carriers for international air transportation of personnel (and their personal effects) or property [@CL-SUP-2023]. The clause recites that the Fly America Act (section 40118 of title 49 of the United States Code) requires that all Federal agencies and Government contractors and subcontractors use U.S.-flag air carriers for United States Government-financed international air transportation of personnel (and their personal effects) or property, to the extent that service by those carriers is available [@CL-SUP-9716; @CL-SUP-2024].

## How it works

International air transportation means transportation by air between a place in the United States and a place outside it, or between two places both outside the United States [@CL-SUP-2022]. In the clause, United States means the 50 States, the District of Columbia and outlying areas [@CL-SUP-2147]. A United States-flag air carrier is an entity granted authority to provide air transportation in the form of a certificate of public convenience and necessity under section 41102 of title 49 of the United States Code [@CL-SUP-2148].

In the event that the contractor selects a carrier other than a U.S.-flag air carrier for international air transportation, the contractor shall include a statement on vouchers involving such transportation essentially in the form set out in the clause [@CL-SUP-2025]. The statement states that international air transportation by a U.S.-flag air carrier was not available or that it was necessary to use foreign-flag air carrier service for the stated reasons [@CL-SUP-2149]. The clause also recites that the Act requires the General Services Administration to issue regulations that, in the absence of satisfactory proof of the necessity for foreign-flag air transportation, disallow expenditures from funds, appropriated or otherwise established for the account of the United States, for international air transportation secured aboard a foreign-flag air carrier [@CL-SUP-9717; @CL-SUP-2150] if a U.S.-flag air carrier is available to provide such services [@CL-SUP-2183].

The contractor shall include the substance of the clause, including paragraph (e), in each subcontract or purchase under the contract that may involve international air transportation [@CL-SUP-2026].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text from February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. RFO FAR 52.247-63 keeps the definition of international air transportation [@CL-SUP-0714; @CL-SUP-2022], of United States [@CL-SUP-0715; @CL-SUP-2147] and of a U.S.-flag air carrier [@CL-SUP-0716; @CL-SUP-2148]. It keeps the statement that all Federal agencies and Government contractors and subcontractors use U.S.-flag air carriers for U.S. Government-financed international air transportation, to the extent that service by those carriers is available [@CL-SUP-0717; @CL-SUP-2024], and the recital that regulations are to disallow expenditures absent satisfactory proof of necessity [@CL-SUP-0718; @CL-SUP-2150], if a U.S.-flag air carrier is available to provide such services [@CL-SUP-0719; @CL-SUP-2183]. It keeps the duty, if available, to use U.S.-flag carriers for international air transportation of personnel (and their personal effects) or property in performing work under the contract [@CL-SUP-0720; @CL-SUP-2023]. It keeps the voucher statement duty when the contractor selects another carrier [@CL-SUP-0721; @CL-SUP-2025], with the statement that U.S.-flag service was not available or that foreign-flag service was necessary, for stated reasons [@CL-SUP-0722; @CL-SUP-2149], and the flowdown of the substance of the clause, including paragraph (e), to each subcontract or purchase that may involve international air transportation [@CL-SUP-0723; @CL-SUP-2026].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor sends two engineers from a plant in Ohio to a supplier in Germany, with tickets costing 12,000 charged to the contract. That is international air transportation, because it runs between a place in the United States and a place outside it. A United States-flag carrier is available on the route, so the contractor books it. A second trip runs from Germany to Poland. Both places are outside the United States, so it is still international air transportation and the same duty applies. On that trip the contractor selects a foreign-flag carrier, so the voucher carries the statement with the contractor's reasons. If those reasons are not satisfactory proof of the necessity for foreign-flag service while a United States-flag carrier was available, the expenditure is exposed to disallowance.

## Never confuse

If available, the contractor, in performing work under the contract, shall use U.S.-flag carriers for international air transportation of personnel (and their personal effects) or property—meaning transportation by air between a place in the United States and a place outside the United States or between two places both of which are outside the United States [@CL-SUP-2022; @CL-SUP-2023]—unlike foreign-flag air carrier service, for which the voucher statement must state that U.S.-flag air carrier service was not available or that foreign-flag service was necessary for the stated reasons, because without satisfactory proof of necessity the General Services Administration regulations disallow expenditures for foreign-flag air transportation when a U.S.-flag air carrier is available [@CL-SUP-2149; @CL-SUP-9717; @CL-SUP-2150; @CL-SUP-2183].

## Company calibration

How the company books international air travel and shipments, where the statement for each voucher is kept, and who confirms that the clause is in each contract. [@Q-004]
