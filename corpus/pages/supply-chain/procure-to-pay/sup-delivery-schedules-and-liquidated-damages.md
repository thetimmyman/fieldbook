## Purpose

Shows how a delivery or performance schedule is stated and evaluated, how the date of the contract and the notice of award fix the period, and when liquidated damages may be used, limited and reduced or waived.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-SUP-3294] |
| Codified citation | FAR 11.401, 11.403 and 11.501 [@CL-SUP-3294; @CL-SUP-3295; @CL-SUP-3301] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-3294] |
| Applies to | Solicitations and contracts that state a delivery or performance schedule [@CL-SUP-3294] |

The time of delivery or performance is an essential contract element and must be clearly stated in solicitations, and contracting officers must ensure that delivery or performance schedules are realistic and meet the requirements of the acquisition [@CL-SUP-3294]. Schedules that are unnecessarily short or difficult to attain tend to restrict competition, are inconsistent with small business policies, and may result in higher contract prices [@CL-SUP-3343]. Solicitations must, except when clearly unnecessary, inform bidders or offerors of the basis on which their bids or proposals will be evaluated with respect to time of delivery or performance [@CL-SUP-3344].

The contracting officer may express a schedule as a specific calendar date, or as a specific period from the date of the contract, meaning the date of award or acceptance by the Government or the effective date shown in the contract [@CL-SUP-3295]. The contracting officer may also express it as a specific period from the date the contractor receives the notice of award or acceptance, including notice by receipt of a contract document executed by the Government [@CL-SUP-3296], or as a specific time for delivery after the contractor receives each individual order, as in indefinite delivery type contracts and GSA schedules [@CL-SUP-3297]. The time specified for contract performance should not be curtailed to the prejudice of the contractor because of delay by the Government in giving notice of award [@CL-SUP-3346].

If the schedule is based on the date of the contract, the contracting officer must mail or otherwise furnish the contract, notice of award, acceptance of proposal or other contract document to the contractor not later than the date of the contract [@CL-SUP-3347]. If the schedule is based on the date the contractor receives the notice of award, or is expressed in specific calendar dates on the assumption that notice will be received by a specified date [@CL-SUP-3348], the contracting officer must send the document by certified mail, return receipt requested, or by another method that provides evidence of the date of receipt [@CL-SUP-3349].

In an invitation for bids, where the schedule is based on the date of the contract and a bid offers delivery based on the date the contractor receives the contract or notice of award, the contracting officer must evaluate the bid by adding 5 calendar days, which represents the normal time for arrival through ordinary mail [@CL-SUP-3298; @CL-SUP-4077]. If the contract or notice of award will be transmitted electronically, the solicitation must say so and the contracting officer evaluates the delivery schedule, based on the date of contract receipt or notice of award, by adding one working day [@CL-SUP-3299]. A working day excludes weekends and United States Federal holidays [@CL-SUP-3350]. If the offered delivery date computed with mailing or transmittal time is later than the delivery date required by the invitation for bids, the bid is considered nonresponsive and rejected [@CL-SUP-3300]. If award is made, the delivery date is the number of days offered in the bid after the contractor actually receives the notice of award [@CL-SUP-3351].

Before using a liquidated damages clause, the contracting officer must consider the potential impact on pricing, competition and contract administration [@CL-SUP-3352]. If timely delivery or performance is unusually important to the Government, liquidated damages clauses may be used [@CL-SUP-3345]. A clause may be used only when the time of delivery or timely performance is so important that the Government may reasonably expect to suffer damage if delivery or performance is delinquent [@CL-SUP-3301], and the extent or amount of the damage would be difficult or impossible to estimate accurately or prove [@CL-SUP-3353].

Liquidated damages are not punitive and are not negative performance incentives, and they are used to compensate the Government for probable damages [@CL-SUP-3354]. The rate must be a reasonable forecast of just compensation for the harm caused by late delivery or untimely performance of the particular contract [@CL-SUP-3302]. A maximum amount or a maximum period for assessing liquidated damages is to be used if these limits reflect the maximum probable damage to the Government [@CL-SUP-3303]. The contracting officer may use more than one rate when the contracting officer expects the probable damage to change over the period of performance [@CL-SUP-3304].

## How it works

A schedule may run from the date of the contract [@CL-SUP-3295], from the date the contractor receives the notice of award or acceptance [@CL-SUP-3296], or from the contractor's receipt of each individual order [@CL-SUP-3297]. For a schedule based on the date of the contract, the contracting officer mails or otherwise furnishes the contract document not later than the date of the contract [@CL-SUP-3347]. For a schedule based on the date the contractor receives the notice of award, or expressed in specific calendar dates on the assumption that notice will be received by a specified date, the contracting officer sends the document by certified mail, return receipt requested, or by another method that will provide evidence of the date of receipt [@CL-SUP-3348; @CL-SUP-3349].

The contracting officer must take all reasonable steps to mitigate liquidated damages [@CL-SUP-3355]. If the contract contains a liquidated damages clause and the contracting officer is considering terminating for default, the contracting officer should seek expeditiously to obtain performance by the contractor or terminate the contract and repurchase [@CL-SUP-3356]. Prompt contracting officer action will prevent excessive loss to defaulting contractors and protect the interests of the Government [@CL-SUP-3357].

The head of the agency may reduce or waive the amount of liquidated damages assessed under a contract if the Commissioner, Financial Management Service, or designee approves [@CL-SUP-3305]. The clause wording itself sits in [Time of delivery](../procure-to-pay/sup-time-of-delivery.md) and [Liquidated damages](../procure-to-pay/sup-liquidated-damages.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An invitation for bids requires 1,000 units 60 days after the date of the contract, so the schedule is based on the date of the contract. One bid offers delivery 55 days after the bidder receives the notice of award. The contracting officer adds 5 calendar days [@CL-SUP-3298], which makes 60 days, so the computed date is not later than the required date and the bid is not rejected on that ground [@CL-SUP-3300]. A second bid offers 58 days after receipt of the notice of award, which computes to 63 days, later than the required 60 days, so that bid is nonresponsive and rejected [@CL-SUP-3298; @CL-SUP-3300]. If the invitation had stated that the notice of award would be transmitted electronically, it would have had to say so, and the contracting officer would have evaluated the delivery schedule by adding one working day [@CL-SUP-3299].

For the same buy, the contracting officer has considered the effect on pricing, competition and contract administration, and finds that timely delivery is unusually important, so that the Government may reasonably expect damage if delivery is late, and that the damage would be hard to estimate [@CL-SUP-3352; @CL-SUP-3345; @CL-SUP-3301; @CL-SUP-3353]. The clause sets 200 per calendar day as a reasonable forecast of the harm, with a ceiling of 4,000 because that reflects the maximum probable damage [@CL-SUP-3302; @CL-SUP-3303]. If the contractor later asks for relief, the head of the agency may reduce or waive the assessment if the Commissioner, Financial Management Service, or designee approves [@CL-SUP-3305].

## Never confuse

Liquidated damages are not punitive and are not negative performance incentives [@CL-SUP-3354]. The rate must be a reasonable forecast of just compensation for the harm caused by late delivery or untimely performance of the particular contract [@CL-SUP-3302], the contracting officer must take all reasonable steps to mitigate liquidated damages [@CL-SUP-3355], and the head of the agency may reduce or waive the amount assessed if the Commissioner, Financial Management Service, or designee approves [@CL-SUP-3305]. The time of delivery or performance is an essential contract element that must be clearly stated in solicitations [@CL-SUP-3294], and if timely delivery or performance is unusually important to the Government, liquidated damages clauses may be used [@CL-SUP-3345].
