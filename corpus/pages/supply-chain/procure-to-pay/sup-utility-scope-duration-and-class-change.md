## Purpose

Explains the period, type of service and tariff basis of a utility service contract, the minimum monthly charge, and the rate schedule that applies when the class of service changes.

### Scope and duration

The contract inserts a period of service [@CL-SUP-2778] and a type of utility service [@CL-SUP-2666]. The contractor agrees to furnish and the Government agrees to purchase that service in accordance with the applicable tariffs, rules and regulations as approved by the applicable governing regulatory body and as set forth in the contract [@CL-SUP-2666]. The period and the type of service are blanks that each contract fills in.

Neither the contractor nor the Government is under any obligation to continue any service under the terms and conditions of the contract beyond the expiration date [@CL-SUP-2667]. The contractor must provide the Government with one complete set of the rates, terms and conditions of service in effect as of the date of the contract and any subsequently approved rates [@CL-SUP-2668].

The contractor is paid at the applicable rate under the tariff, and the Government is liable for the minimum monthly charge, if any, that the contract specifies, from the period in which service is first furnished and for the term of the contract [@CL-SUP-2669]. Any minimum monthly charge is equitably prorated for the periods in which commencement and termination of the contract become effective [@CL-SUP-2713].

### Change in class of service

If the class of service changes, the service is provided at the contractor's lowest available rate schedule applicable to the class of service furnished [@CL-SUP-2670]. Where the contractor does not have approved rate schedules for the services on file with the regulatory body, no clause in the contract precludes the parties from negotiating a rate schedule for the class of service furnished [@CL-SUP-2671].

## How it works

The contract fills in the period and the type of service, and service is furnished under the applicable tariffs, rules and regulations as approved by the regulatory body and as set forth in the contract [@CL-SUP-2778; @CL-SUP-2666]. Any minimum monthly charge runs from the period in which service is first furnished and for the term of the contract, and it is prorated for the periods in which the contract starts and ends [@CL-SUP-2669; @CL-SUP-2713]. A class change moves the service to the contractor's lowest available schedule for the class furnished, and the contract does not stop the parties from negotiating a schedule where none is on file [@CL-SUP-2670; @CL-SUP-2671]. Neither side must continue service after the expiration date [@CL-SUP-2667].

Under the RFO text, the DoD part 52 class deviation directs DoD contracting officers to use the revised FAR part 52 in place of the codified part 52 text, effective February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. The following rules appear in the RFO text of these clauses. The contract inserts the period of service [@CL-SUP-2409; @CL-SUP-2778], and for that period the contractor agrees to furnish and the Government agrees to purchase the type of utility service that the contract inserts in accordance with the applicable tariffs, rules and regulations as approved by the applicable governing regulatory body and as set forth in the contract [@CL-SUP-9184; @CL-SUP-2410; @CL-SUP-2666]. Neither the contractor nor the Government is obligated to continue service under the contract's terms beyond the expiration date [@CL-SUP-2411; @CL-SUP-2667]. The contractor is paid at the applicable rate under the tariff, and the Government is liable for any minimum monthly charge specified in the contract, from the period service is first furnished and for the contract term [@CL-SUP-2412; @CL-SUP-2669], and any minimum monthly charge is equitably prorated for the periods in which commencement and termination of the contract become effective [@CL-SUP-2413; @CL-SUP-2713]. When the class of service changes, the service is provided at the contractor's lowest available rate schedule applicable to the class of service furnished [@CL-SUP-2414; @CL-SUP-2670]. Where the contractor has no approved rate schedules for those services on file with the regulatory body, no clause in the contract precludes the parties from negotiating a rate schedule for the class of service furnished [@CL-SUP-2415; @CL-SUP-2671].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contract runs for 12 months at a stated minimum monthly charge of 4,000. Service starts on the sixteenth day of a 30-day month. The first month is equitably prorated by days, so 15 of 30 days gives a minimum of 2,000. Each later full month carries the full minimum of 4,000, even in a month of low use, and the month in which the term ends is prorated the same way if it is partial. The contractor gives the Government one complete set of the rates, terms and conditions in effect at the contract date and any subsequently approved rates. In month six the site changes its class of service. The contractor bills the new class at its lowest available rate schedule for that class. If the contractor has no approved schedule on file for the new class, the parties may negotiate one. When the term ends, neither side must continue service.

## Never confuse

The class-of-service clause says which schedule applies after the class changes [@CL-SUP-2670]. A change in rates or terms and conditions follows a separate path; see [Rate changes for regulated and unregulated utility service](sup-utility-rate-changes-regulated-and-unregulated.md).
