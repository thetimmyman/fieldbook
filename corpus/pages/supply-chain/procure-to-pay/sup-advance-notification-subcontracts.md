## Purpose

Shows when a contractor on a cost-reimbursement contract must tell the contracting officer about a subcontract before it is awarded.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-SUP-3839] |
| Codified citation | FAR 44.201-2 [@CL-SUP-3839] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-3839] |
| Applies to | Contractors under cost-reimbursement contracts [@CL-SUP-3839] |

Under cost-reimbursement contracts, the contractor is required by statute to notify the contracting officer as the section sets out [@CL-SUP-3839]. The section gives one rule for the Department of Defense, the Coast Guard and the National Aeronautics and Space Administration, and another for other civilian agencies [@CL-SUP-3840; @CL-SUP-3842].

Paragraph (a) covers the Department of Defense, the Coast Guard and the National Aeronautics and Space Administration. Unless the contractor maintains an approved purchasing system, a statute in title 10 of the United States Code requires notification before the award of any cost-plus-fixed-fee subcontract [@CL-SUP-3840]. The same notification, subject to the same exception for a contractor that maintains an approved purchasing system, is required before the award of any fixed-price subcontract that exceeds the greater of the simplified acquisition threshold or five percent of the total estimated cost of the contract [@CL-SUP-3841].

Paragraph (b) covers civilian agencies other than the Coast Guard and the National Aeronautics and Space Administration. Even if the contractor has an approved purchasing system, a statute in title 41 of the United States Code requires notification before the award of any cost-plus-fixed-fee subcontract [@CL-SUP-3842]. The same notification, even if the contractor has an approved purchasing system, is required before the award of any fixed-price subcontract that exceeds either the simplified acquisition threshold or five percent of the total estimated cost of the contract. The section does not say which of the two governs [@CL-SUP-3843].

The section does not say how to notify, what the notice contains or how far ahead of award to give it. Those details are not established here.

## How it works

The duty falls on the contractor, and the notice goes to the contracting officer before award [@CL-SUP-3839; @CL-SUP-3840]. Which paragraph applies depends on the agency awarding the contract, because the section sorts contracts by agency [@CL-SUP-3840; @CL-SUP-3842]. For the first group, an approved purchasing system removes the duty. For the second group, the duty applies despite an approved system [@CL-SUP-3840; @CL-SUP-3842]. The threshold is stated for the fixed-price subcontract, and its percentage is measured against the total estimated cost of the contract [@CL-SUP-3841; @CL-SUP-3843]. How a purchasing system comes to be approved is in [purchasing system approval and withdrawal](sup-purchasing-system-approval-and-withdrawal.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A civilian agency, other than the Coast Guard and the National Aeronautics and Space Administration, awards a cost-reimbursement contract with a total estimated cost of 4,000,000. The contractor has an approved purchasing system. For this example the simplified acquisition threshold is 250,000. The contractor plans a fixed-price subcontract of 400,000. Five percent of 4,000,000 is 200,000. The subcontract exceeds both 200,000 and 250,000. Paragraph (b) applies even with an approved system, so the contractor must notify the contracting officer before award [@CL-SUP-3839; @CL-SUP-3842; @CL-SUP-3843]. Had the contract been a Department of Defense contract and the contractor maintained an approved purchasing system, paragraph (a) would not require this notification [@CL-SUP-3840; @CL-SUP-3841].

## Never confuse

Advance notification is not consent. Notification informs the contracting officer before award, and the section frames it as a statutory duty [@CL-SUP-3839; @CL-SUP-3840; @CL-SUP-3842]. Consent is a separate requirement that the consent section sets in the cases it describes [@CL-SUP-3830; @CL-SUP-3833]. See [consent to subcontract](sup-consent-to-subcontract.md) and [consent and advance notification](sup-subcontracts-consent-advance-notification.md).

The approved purchasing system has opposite effects in the two paragraphs. It removes the duty in paragraph (a) and does not remove it in paragraph (b) [@CL-SUP-3840; @CL-SUP-3842].

## Company calibration

Whether each cost-reimbursement contract sits with a defense or a civilian agency, and whether the purchasing system is approved. [@Q-040]
