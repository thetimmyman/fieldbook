## Purpose

Walks a shipment through receipt, loading at the tailgate and the two return paths that follow a failed delivery.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-SUP-2228] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.247-14, FAR 52.247-15 and FAR 52.247-16 [@CL-SUP-2228; @CL-SUP-2229; @CL-SUP-2232] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.247-15 [@CL-SUP-0485; @CL-SUP-0486; @CL-SUP-0487; @CL-SUP-0488]; no RFO equivalent for FAR 52.247-14 or FAR 52.247-16 [@CL-SUP-0483; @CL-SUP-0484] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-2228; @CL-SUP-2229; @CL-SUP-2232] |
| Applies to | The receipt clause in solicitations and contracts for transportation or transportation-related services [@CL-SUP-2227]; the loading and unloading clause when the contractor is responsible for loading and unloading shipments [@CL-SUP-2264]; the returning undelivered freight clause when the contractor is responsible for returning undelivered freight [@CL-SUP-2266] |

The receipt clause is inserted in solicitations and contracts for transportation or transportation-related services [@CL-SUP-2227]. The contractor shall diligently count and examine all goods tendered for shipment, receipt for them, and make an appropriate written exception for any goods not in apparent good order [@CL-SUP-2228].

The loading and unloading clause is inserted in solicitations and contracts for transportation or transportation-related services when the contractor is responsible for loading and unloading shipments [@CL-SUP-2264]. Unless the contract specifies store-door or inside delivery, the contractor shall load and unload shipments at no additional expense to the Government [@CL-SUP-2229]. The Government or its agent will place or receive freight at the tailgate of the contractor's vehicle [@CL-SUP-2265]. Tailgate delivery, for purposes of the clause, is defined as that which enables a forklift truck or similar equipment, with operator only, to place or remove cargo from the tailgate of the contractor's vehicle [@CL-SUP-2230]. If loading is the contractor's responsibility, the contractor shall perform all shoring, blocking, and bracing, and shall provide dunnage at the contractor's expense [@CL-SUP-2231].

The returning undelivered freight clause is inserted in solicitations and contracts for transportation or transportation-related services when the contractor is responsible for returning undelivered freight [@CL-SUP-2266].

## How it works

When a shipment cannot be delivered through no fault of the contractor, the contractor shall contact the shipper for disposition instructions [@CL-SUP-2232]. If the shipment is ordered returned to the origin point, the charges for the return trip shall be the same as the charges for the outbound trip [@CL-SUP-2233]. The shipper will maintain a record of the goods that, through no fault of the contractor, could not be delivered and are returned to the shipper [@CL-SUP-2267]. If, at a future date, the returned goods are determined to be related to a claim against the contractor, the claim will be adjusted accordingly [@CL-SUP-2268].

When a shipment cannot be delivered due to the fault of the contractor, the contractor shall return the shipment to the origin point at no charge to the Government [@CL-SUP-2234]. Any charges incurred for redelivery, which are in excess of the charges that would have been incurred under the contract, shall be for the contractor's account in accordance with the Default clause of the contract [@CL-SUP-2235].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text from February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. RFO FAR 52.247-15 keeps the loading rules: unless the contract specifies store-door or inside delivery, the contractor shall load and unload shipments at no additional expense to the Government [@CL-SUP-0485; @CL-SUP-2229]. It keeps the rule that the Government or its agent will place or receive freight at the tailgate of the contractor's vehicle [@CL-SUP-0486; @CL-SUP-2265]. It keeps the definition of tailgate delivery as that which enables a forklift truck or similar equipment, with operator only, to place or remove cargo from the tailgate of the contractor's vehicle [@CL-SUP-0487; @CL-SUP-2230]. It keeps the duty, when loading is the contractor's responsibility, to perform all shoring, blocking, and bracing and to provide dunnage at the contractor's expense [@CL-SUP-0488; @CL-SUP-2231]. RFO FAR 52.247-14 is marked [Reserved] [@CL-SUP-0483], and RFO FAR 52.247-16 is marked [Reserved] [@CL-SUP-0484].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

Thirty crates arrive. The contractor counts them, finds 2 crushed cartons and notes the exception in writing when it receipts for the goods.

Loading is the contractor's responsibility, so it loads at the tailgate, shores, blocks and braces the load and supplies dunnage at its own expense.

At the destination the consignee refuses delivery through no fault of the contractor, so the contractor contacts the shipper, which orders the goods returned. The outbound charge was 400, so the return trip is charged at 400, and the shipper keeps a record of the returned goods. Had the contractor's own fault prevented delivery, the return would have cost the Government nothing. If redelivery then cost 700 against 400 under the contract, the extra 300 would be for the contractor's account under the Default clause.

## Never confuse

Tailgate delivery, for purposes of the loading and unloading clause, is defined as that which enables a forklift truck or similar equipment, with operator only, to place or remove cargo from the tailgate of the contractor's vehicle [@CL-SUP-2230]. Unless the contract specifies store-door or inside delivery, the contractor shall load and unload shipments at no additional expense to the Government [@CL-SUP-2229].

When a shipment cannot be delivered through no fault of the contractor and is ordered returned to the origin point, the charges for the return trip shall be the same as the charges for the outbound trip, whereas when a shipment cannot be delivered due to the fault of the contractor, the contractor shall return the shipment to the origin point at no charge to the Government [@CL-SUP-2232; @CL-SUP-2233; @CL-SUP-2234].

Codified FAR 52.247-14 sets out the receipt of shipment clause [@CL-SUP-2228] and codified FAR 52.247-16 sets out the returning undelivered freight clause [@CL-SUP-2232], while RFO FAR 52.247-14 and RFO FAR 52.247-16 are marked [Reserved] [@CL-SUP-0483; @CL-SUP-0484].
