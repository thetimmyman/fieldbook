## Purpose

Walks a shipment through receipt, loading at the tailgate and the two return paths that follow a failed delivery.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-SUP-2228] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.247-14, FAR 52.247-15 and FAR 52.247-16 [@CL-SUP-2228; @CL-SUP-2229; @CL-SUP-2232] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.247-15 [@CL-SUP-0485; @CL-SUP-0486; @CL-SUP-0487; @CL-SUP-0488]; no RFO equivalent for FAR 52.247-14 or FAR 52.247-16 [@CL-SUP-0483; @CL-SUP-0484] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-2228; @CL-SUP-2229; @CL-SUP-2232] |
| Applies to | The receipt clause in solicitations and contracts for transportation or transportation-related services [@CL-SUP-2227]; the loading and unloading clause when the contractor is responsible for loading and unloading shipments [@CL-SUP-2264]; the returning undelivered freight clause when the contractor is responsible for returning undelivered freight [@CL-SUP-2266] |

The receipt clause is inserted in solicitations and contracts for transportation or transportation-related services [@CL-SUP-2227]. The contractor must diligently count and examine all goods tendered for shipment, receipt for them, and make an appropriate written exception for any goods not in apparent good order [@CL-SUP-2228].

The loading and unloading clause is inserted in solicitations and contracts for transportation or transportation-related services when the contractor is responsible for loading and unloading shipments [@CL-SUP-2264]. Unless the contract specifies store-door or inside delivery, the contractor must load and unload shipments at no additional expense to the Government [@CL-SUP-2229]. The Government or its agent will place or receive freight at the tailgate of the contractor's vehicle [@CL-SUP-2265]. Tailgate delivery means that a forklift truck or similar equipment, with an operator only, can place or remove cargo from the tailgate of the contractor's vehicle [@CL-SUP-2230]. If loading is the contractor's responsibility, the contractor must perform all shoring, blocking and bracing and must provide dunnage at its own expense [@CL-SUP-2231].

The returning undelivered freight clause is inserted in solicitations and contracts for transportation or transportation-related services when the contractor is responsible for returning undelivered freight [@CL-SUP-2266].

## How it works

When a shipment cannot be delivered through no fault of the contractor, the contractor must contact the shipper for disposition instructions [@CL-SUP-2232]. If the shipment is ordered returned to the origin point, the charges for the return trip must be the same as the charges for the outbound trip [@CL-SUP-2233]. The shipper must maintain a record of the goods that, through no fault of the contractor, could not be delivered and are returned to the shipper [@CL-SUP-2267]. If, at a future date, the returned goods are determined to be related to a claim against the contractor, the claim will be adjusted accordingly [@CL-SUP-2268].

When the contractor's own fault prevents delivery, the contractor must return the shipment to the origin point at no charge to the Government [@CL-SUP-2234]. Redelivery charges in excess of the charges that would have been incurred under the contract are for the contractor's account under the Default clause [@CL-SUP-2235].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text from February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. RFO FAR 52.247-15 keeps the loading rules: unless the contract specifies store-door or inside delivery, the contractor loads and unloads shipments at no additional expense to the Government [@CL-SUP-0485; @CL-SUP-2229]. It keeps the rule that the Government or its agent places or receives freight at the tailgate of the contractor's vehicle [@CL-SUP-0486; @CL-SUP-2265]. It keeps the definition of tailgate delivery by a forklift truck or similar equipment, with an operator only [@CL-SUP-0487; @CL-SUP-2230]. It keeps the duty, when loading is the contractor's responsibility, to perform all shoring, blocking and bracing and to provide dunnage at the contractor's expense [@CL-SUP-0488; @CL-SUP-2231]. The RFO text prints FAR 52.247-14 as reserved with no clause text [@CL-SUP-0483], so the receipt clause described above has no text under that number [@CL-SUP-2228; @CL-SUP-0483]. It prints FAR 52.247-16 as reserved with no clause text [@CL-SUP-0484], so the two return paths described above have no text under that number [@CL-SUP-2232; @CL-SUP-2234; @CL-SUP-0484].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

Thirty crates arrive. The contractor counts them, finds 2 crushed cartons and notes the exception in writing when it receipts for the goods. This receipt step follows the codified text; the RFO text prints FAR 52.247-14 as reserved [@CL-SUP-0483].

Loading is the contractor's responsibility, so it loads at the tailgate, shores, blocks and braces the load and supplies dunnage at its own expense.

At the destination the consignee refuses delivery through no fault of the contractor, so the contractor contacts the shipper, which orders the goods returned. The outbound charge was 400, so the return trip is charged at 400, and the shipper keeps a record of the returned goods. Had the contractor's own fault prevented delivery, the return would have cost the Government nothing. If redelivery then cost 700 against 400 under the contract, the extra 300 would be for the contractor's account under the Default clause. The return steps in this paragraph follow the codified text; the RFO text prints FAR 52.247-16 as reserved [@CL-SUP-0484].

## Never confuse

Tailgate delivery is defined by equipment: a forklift truck or similar equipment, with an operator only, can place or remove cargo from the tailgate [@CL-SUP-2230]. The contractor loads and unloads at no additional expense unless the contract specifies store-door or inside delivery [@CL-SUP-2229]. The clause text does not define store-door or inside delivery.

A return that is not the contractor's fault carries the same charges as the outbound trip, while a return caused by the contractor's fault carries no charge to the Government [@CL-SUP-2233; @CL-SUP-2234].

The codified text prints the receipt clause at FAR 52.247-14 [@CL-SUP-2228] and the returning undelivered freight clause at FAR 52.247-16 [@CL-SUP-2232], but the RFO text prints both numbers as reserved [@CL-SUP-0483; @CL-SUP-0484].
