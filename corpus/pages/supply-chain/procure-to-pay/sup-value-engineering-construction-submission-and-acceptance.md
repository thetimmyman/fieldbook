## Purpose

Shows what makes a construction value engineering change proposal, what it must contain, where it goes, how the contracting officer accepts or rejects it, and how the contractor protects its data.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-SUP-3122] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.248-3 [@CL-SUP-3122] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.248-3 [@CL-SUP-2648] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-3122] |
| Applies to | Contracts that include the construction value engineering clause, basic or with Alternate I [@CL-SUP-3122; @CL-SUP-3223] |

Under the construction value engineering clause, the contractor is encouraged to develop, prepare and submit value engineering change proposals voluntarily, and shares in any instant contract savings realized from accepted proposals in accordance with the sharing paragraph [@CL-SUP-3122]. A value engineering change proposal is a proposal that requires a change to the instant contract to implement and results in reducing the contract price or estimated cost without impairing essential functions or characteristics [@CL-SUP-3123]. A proposal is not one if it involves a change in deliverable end item quantities only, or to the contract type only [@CL-SUP-3124]. Alternate I deletes the collateral savings paragraph and redesignates the remaining paragraphs, as the [sharing card](sup-value-engineering-construction-sharing.md) covers [@CL-SUP-3223].

As a minimum, the contractor includes in each proposal the information described in subparagraphs (1) through (7) of the preparation paragraph [@CL-SUP-3207]. If the proposed change is affected by contractually required configuration management or similar procedures, the instructions in those procedures on format, identification and priority assignment govern preparation of the proposal [@CL-SUP-3208]. The seven items are:

- A description of the difference between the existing contract requirement and that proposed, the comparative advantages and disadvantages of each, a justification when an item's function or characteristics are being altered, and the effect of the change on the end item's performance [@CL-SUP-3209].
- A list and analysis of the contract requirements that must be changed if the proposal is accepted, including any suggested specification revisions [@CL-SUP-3210].
- A separate, detailed cost estimate for the affected portions of the existing contract requirement and for the proposal [@CL-SUP-3211]. The cost reduction takes into account the contractor's allowable development and implementation costs, including any amount attributable to subcontracts [@CL-SUP-3212].
- A description and estimate of the costs the Government may incur in implementing the proposal, such as test and evaluation and operating and support costs [@CL-SUP-3213].
- A prediction of any effects the proposed change would have on collateral costs to the agency [@CL-SUP-3214].
- A statement of the time by which a contract modification accepting the proposal must be issued to achieve the maximum cost reduction, noting any effect on the contract completion time or delivery schedule [@CL-SUP-3215].
- Identification of any previous submissions of the proposal, including the dates submitted, the agencies and contract numbers involved, and previous Government actions, if known [@CL-SUP-3216].

The contractor submits proposals to the Resident Engineer at the worksite, with a copy to the contracting officer [@CL-SUP-3125]. The contracting officer notifies the contractor of the status of a proposal within 45 calendar days after the contracting office receives it [@CL-SUP-3126]. If additional time is required, the contracting officer notifies the contractor within the 45-day period and gives the reason for the delay and the expected date of the decision [@CL-SUP-3217]. The Government processes proposals expeditiously, but it is not liable for any delay in acting upon a proposal [@CL-SUP-3218].

If a proposal is not accepted, the contracting officer notifies the contractor in writing, explaining the reasons for rejection [@CL-SUP-3127]. The contractor may withdraw a proposal, in whole or in part, at any time before the Government accepts it [@CL-SUP-3219]. The contracting officer may require written notification before the contractor undertakes significant expenditures for proposal effort [@CL-SUP-3220].

A proposal may be accepted, in whole or in part, by the contracting officer's award of a modification to the contract citing the clause [@CL-SUP-3128]. The contracting officer may accept it, even though no agreement on price reduction has been reached, by issuing the contractor a notice to proceed with the change [@CL-SUP-3129]. Until a notice to proceed is issued or a modification applies the proposal, the contractor performs in accordance with the existing contract [@CL-SUP-3221]. The decision to accept or reject all or part of any proposal is a unilateral decision made solely at the discretion of the contracting officer [@CL-SUP-3130].

The contractor may restrict the Government's right to use any part of a proposal or its supporting data by marking the prescribed legend on the affected parts [@CL-SUP-3224]. The legend says that the data, furnished under the clause, are not to be disclosed outside the Government or duplicated, used or disclosed, in whole or in part, for any purpose other than to evaluate a proposal submitted under the clause [@CL-SUP-3225]. The legend's restriction does not limit the Government's right to use information in the data if it has been obtained or is otherwise available from the contractor or another source without limitations [@CL-SUP-3226]. If a proposal is accepted, the contractor grants the Government unlimited rights in the proposal and supporting data, except for data qualifying and submitted as limited rights technical data [@CL-SUP-3227]. For that limited rights data, the Government has the rights specified in the modification implementing the proposal and marks the data appropriately [@CL-SUP-3228]. The terms unlimited rights and limited rights are defined in part 27 of the Federal Acquisition Regulation [@CL-SUP-3229].

## How it works

The contractor is encouraged to develop, prepare and submit value engineering change proposals voluntarily, includes as a minimum in each proposal the information described in subparagraphs (1) through (7) of the preparation paragraph, and submits proposals to the Resident Engineer at the worksite with a copy to the contracting officer [@CL-SUP-3122; @CL-SUP-3207; @CL-SUP-3125]. The contracting officer notifies the contractor of the status of any proposal within 45 calendar days after the contracting office receives it, and if additional time is required, notifies the contractor within the 45-day period and provides the reason for the delay and the expected date of the contracting officer's decision [@CL-SUP-3126; @CL-SUP-3217]. A proposal may be accepted, in whole or in part, by the contracting officer's award of a modification to the contract citing the clause, or the contracting officer may accept a proposal even though no agreement on price reduction has been reached by issuing the contractor a notice to proceed with the change, and until a notice to proceed is issued or a contract modification applies a proposal to the contract, the contractor performs in accordance with the existing contract [@CL-SUP-3128; @CL-SUP-3129; @CL-SUP-3221]. The decision to accept or reject all or part of any proposal is a unilateral decision made solely at the discretion of the contracting officer, and if a proposal is not accepted the contracting officer notifies the contractor in writing, explaining the reasons for rejection [@CL-SUP-3130; @CL-SUP-3127]. The sharing of savings, including Alternate I, is on the [sharing card](sup-value-engineering-construction-sharing.md).

Class Deviation 2026-O0038 directs DoD contracting officers to use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text, effective February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. In RFO FAR 52.248-3, the contractor is encouraged to develop, prepare and submit proposals voluntarily, and shares in any instant contract savings realized from accepted proposals in accordance with paragraph (f) of the clause [@CL-SUP-2648] [@CL-SUP-3122]. The contractor includes, as a minimum, in each proposal the information described in paragraphs (c)(1) through (7) of the clause [@CL-SUP-9240] [@CL-SUP-3207]. If the proposed change is affected by contractually required configuration management or similar procedures, the instructions in those procedures relating to format, identification and priority assignment govern preparation of the proposal [@CL-SUP-9241] [@CL-SUP-3208]. The cost reduction associated with a proposal takes into account the contractor's allowable development and implementation costs, including any amount attributable to subcontracts under paragraph (h) of the clause [@CL-SUP-9242] [@CL-SUP-3212]. A proposal requires a change to the instant contract to implement and results in reducing the contract price or estimated cost without impairing essential functions or characteristics [@CL-SUP-2655] [@CL-SUP-3123], and it is not a proposal if it involves a change in deliverable end item quantities only, or to the contract type only [@CL-SUP-2656] [@CL-SUP-3124]. The contractor submits proposals to the Resident Engineer at the worksite, with a copy to the contracting officer [@CL-SUP-2657] [@CL-SUP-3125]. The contracting officer notifies the contractor of the status of a proposal within 45 calendar days after the contracting office receives it [@CL-SUP-2658] [@CL-SUP-3126], and if additional time is required, notifies the contractor within that period and gives the reason for the delay and the expected date of the decision [@CL-SUP-2659] [@CL-SUP-3217]. If a proposal is not accepted, the contracting officer notifies the contractor in writing, explaining the reasons for rejection, and the contractor may withdraw a proposal, in whole or in part, at any time before the Government accepts it [@CL-SUP-2660] [@CL-SUP-3127] [@CL-SUP-3219]. The contracting officer may accept a proposal, even though no agreement on price reduction has been reached, by issuing a notice to proceed with the change [@CL-SUP-2821] [@CL-SUP-3129]. The decision to accept or reject all or part of any proposal is a unilateral decision made solely at the discretion of the contracting officer [@CL-SUP-2822] [@CL-SUP-3130]. The legend says the data, furnished under the clause, are not to be disclosed outside the Government or duplicated, used or disclosed, in whole or in part, for any purpose other than to evaluate a proposal submitted under the clause [@CL-SUP-2823] [@CL-SUP-3225], and its restriction does not limit the Government's right to use information in the data if it has been obtained or is otherwise available from the contractor or another source without limitations [@CL-SUP-2824] [@CL-SUP-3226]. If a proposal is accepted, the contractor grants the Government unlimited rights in the proposal and supporting data, except for data qualifying and submitted as limited rights technical data [@CL-SUP-2825] [@CL-SUP-3227], for which the Government has the rights specified in the modification implementing the proposal and marks the data appropriately [@CL-SUP-2826] [@CL-SUP-3228]. When the head of the contracting activity determines that the cost of calculating and tracking collateral savings will exceed the benefits to be derived in a construction contract, Alternate I deletes paragraph (g), the collateral savings paragraph, from the basic clause and redesignates the remaining paragraphs [@CL-SUP-2654] [@CL-SUP-3223].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A site contractor on a fixed-price construction contract proposes a different wall assembly. The proposal includes all seven items, including the time by which a modification must be issued to get the full cost reduction. The contractor sends it to the Resident Engineer at the worksite with a copy to the contracting officer. On day 40 after the contracting office receives it, the contracting officer tells the contractor the reason for the delay and the expected decision date. The answer: that notice falls within the 45 days, and the Government is not liable for the delay. The contracting officer then issues a notice to proceed before the price reduction is agreed. The answer: until that notice to proceed or a modification applies the proposal, the contractor builds the existing wall assembly, and from the notice it makes the change.

A second proposal only cuts the number of units delivered. The answer: it is not a value engineering change proposal, because it changes deliverable end item quantities only. A third proposal is not accepted. The answer: the contracting officer explains the reasons in writing, and the contractor could have withdrawn it at any time before acceptance.

The contractor marks the pricing detail of the wall proposal with the legend. The answer: the Government may use it only to evaluate the proposal, but the restriction does not limit use of information the Government obtained from another source without limitations. If the proposal is accepted, the Government gets unlimited rights, except in data qualifying and submitted as limited rights technical data.

## Never confuse

Any value engineering change proposal may be accepted, in whole or in part, by the contracting officer's award of a modification to the contract citing the clause, and the decision to accept or reject all or part of any proposal is a unilateral decision made solely at the discretion of the contracting officer [@CL-SUP-3128; @CL-SUP-3130]. Until a notice to proceed is issued or a contract modification applies a proposal to the contract, the contractor performs in accordance with the existing contract [@CL-SUP-3221]. Instant contract savings and collateral savings, which are the money side, sit in the [sharing card](sup-value-engineering-construction-sharing.md). The value engineering clause for other contracts is covered at [value engineering general](../../program-finance/evms/prg-far-value-engineering-general.md) and [value engineering sharing](../../program-finance/evms/prg-far-value-engineering-sharing.md).
