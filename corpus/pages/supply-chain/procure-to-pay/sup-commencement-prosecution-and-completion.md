## Purpose

Shows the three duties the construction clause places on the Contractor, namely start, diligence and completion, and how Alternate I moves a calendar completion date when the notice to proceed is late.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-SUP-2562] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.211-10 [@CL-SUP-2562] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-2562] |
| Applies to | Fixed-price construction solicitations and contracts [@CL-SUP-2562] |

The clause is inserted in solicitations and contracts when a fixed-price construction contract is contemplated, and it may be changed to accommodate the issuance of orders under indefinite-delivery contracts for construction [@CL-SUP-2562]. The Contractor must commence work within the number of calendar days that the Contracting Officer inserts, counted after the date the Contractor receives the notice to proceed [@CL-SUP-2563]. It must prosecute the work diligently [@CL-SUP-2564]. It must complete the entire work ready for use not later than the stated time, and the time stated for completion includes final cleanup of the premises [@CL-SUP-2565]. The number of days and the completion time are contract-specific blanks [@CL-SUP-2563; @CL-SUP-2565].

The Contracting Officer specifies the time for completion either as a number of days after the date the contractor receives the notice to proceed or as a calendar date [@CL-SUP-2619].

## How it works

The Contractor is required to do all three things: commence work on time, prosecute the work diligently, and complete the entire work ready for use by the stated time [@CL-SUP-2563; @CL-SUP-2564; @CL-SUP-2565].

Alternate I applies where the completion date is expressed as a specific calendar date computed on the basis of the contractor receiving the notice to proceed by a certain day, and it adds a paragraph to the basic clause [@CL-SUP-2620]. That paragraph states that the completion date is based on the assumption that the successful offeror will receive the notice to proceed by a stated date, and the completion date is extended by the number of calendar days after that date that the Contractor receives the notice to proceed [@CL-SUP-2566]. The extension does not apply to the extent that the delay in issuance of the notice to proceed results from the failure of the Contractor to execute the contract and give the required performance and payment bonds within the time specified in the offer [@CL-SUP-2567].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A construction contract uses Alternate I and sets completion for 31 March, assuming the Contractor receives the notice to proceed by 1 March. The notice actually arrives on 11 March, and none of the delay comes from a failure to execute the contract or to give the required bonds. The completion date moves 10 calendar days, to 10 April.

Suppose instead that 4 of those 10 days of delay result from the Contractor's failure to execute the contract and give the required performance and payment bonds within the time specified in the offer. The extension does not apply to that part, so the completion date moves only 6 calendar days, to 6 April.

## Never confuse

Commencement is not completion. A late notice to proceed moves the completion date only where Alternate I is used, and not to the extent that the delay came from the Contractor's own failure to execute the contract and give the required performance and payment bonds in time [@CL-SUP-2620; @CL-SUP-2567]. The duty to prosecute the work diligently is separate from the duty to complete by the stated time [@CL-SUP-2564; @CL-SUP-2565].
