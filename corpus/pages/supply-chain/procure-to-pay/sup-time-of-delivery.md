## Purpose

Shows how the time of delivery clause states the required delivery schedule, how offers that propose a different schedule are treated, how the date of award or of receipt of the notice of award fixes the delivery period, and what each of the three alternates changes.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-SUP-2541] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.211-8 [@CL-SUP-2541] |
| RFO citation | No RFO equivalent; RFO FAR 52.211-8 is marked [Reserved] [@CL-SUP-0638] |
| Edition date | Not established. |

Paragraph (a) states delivery as a required delivery schedule that lists each item number, the quantity, and the within-days-after-date-of-contract period within which the Government requires delivery to be made; the Contracting Officer inserts the specific details [@CL-SUP-2541], and under Alternate I inserts the date by which the Government assumes it will make award [@CL-SUP-2551].

The clause also provides a schedule for the offeror's proposed delivery, listing item number, quantity and days after the date of contract [@CL-SUP-2591]. Offers that propose delivery of each quantity within the applicable delivery period are evaluated equally as regards time of delivery [@CL-SUP-2542], and an offer whose delivery will not clearly fall within the applicable required delivery period is considered nonresponsive and rejected [@CL-SUP-2543]. When an offeror offers an earlier delivery schedule than required, the Government reserves the right to award under either the required delivery schedule or the proposed delivery schedule [@CL-SUP-2544]. If the offeror proposes no other delivery schedule, the required delivery schedule applies [@CL-SUP-2545].

## How it works

Paragraph (b) of the basic clause directs attention to the Contract Award provision of the solicitation, under which a written award or acceptance of offer mailed, or otherwise furnished to the successful offeror, results in a binding contract [@CL-SUP-2592]. The Government will mail or otherwise furnish the award or notice of award not later than the day award is dated [@CL-SUP-2593], so the offeror should compute the time available for performance beginning with the actual date of award, rather than the date the written notice of award is received through the ordinary mails [@CL-SUP-2546].

Under the basic clause, the Government still evaluates an offer that measures delivery from the Contractor's date of receipt of the contract or notice of award, by adding one of two alternatives. The first is five calendar days for delivery of the award through the ordinary mails [@CL-SUP-2547]. The second is one working day, if the solicitation states that the contract or notice of award will be transmitted electronically [@CL-SUP-2548]. A working day excludes weekends and United States Federal holidays [@CL-SUP-2549]. If the offered delivery date, computed that way, is later than the required delivery date, the offer is considered nonresponsive and rejected [@CL-SUP-2550].

Each alternate substitutes for paragraph (b) of the basic clause or, in Alternate III, allows it to be deleted, and provides a substitute heading for the third column of paragraph (a) [@CL-SUP-2594; @CL-SUP-2595; @CL-SUP-2598; @CL-SUP-2599; @CL-SUP-2602; @CL-SUP-2603]. Alternate I applies where the delivery schedule is expressed in terms of specific calendar dates or specific periods and is based on an assumed date of award, and the contracting officer may then substitute its paragraph (b) for paragraph (b) of the basic clause [@CL-SUP-2594]. The time may be expressed by substituting one of three alternative headings for the third column of paragraph (a): "on or before"; "during the months __________"; or "not sooner than __________ or later than ___________" [@CL-SUP-2595]. The Alternate I paragraph states that the delivery dates or specific periods are based on the assumption that the Government will make award by a date inserted by the Contracting Officer, and each delivery date in the delivery schedule shall be extended by the number of calendar days after the above date that the contract is in fact awarded [@CL-SUP-2551]. It keeps the direction to the Contract Award provision [@CL-SUP-2596] and the instruction that the offeror compute the time available from the actual date of award rather than the date the written notice of award is received through the ordinary mails [@CL-SUP-2597].

Alternate II applies where the delivery schedule is expressed in terms of specific calendar dates or specific periods and is based on an assumed date the contractor will receive notice of award, and the contracting officer may then substitute its paragraph (b) for paragraph (b) of the basic clause [@CL-SUP-2598]. The time may be expressed by substituting "within days after the date of receipt of a written notice of award" as the heading for the third column of paragraph (a) [@CL-SUP-2599]. The delivery dates or specific periods are based on the assumption that the successful offeror will receive notice of award by a date inserted by the Contracting Officer, and each delivery date in the delivery schedule shall be extended by the number of calendar days after the above date that the Contractor receives notice of award [@CL-SUP-2600], provided that the Contractor promptly acknowledges receipt of notice of award [@CL-SUP-2601].

Alternate III applies where the delivery schedule is to be based on the actual date the contractor receives a written notice of award, and the contracting officer may then delete paragraph (b) of the basic clause [@CL-SUP-2602]. The same heading, within days after the date of receipt of a written notice of award, may be used for the third column of paragraph (a) [@CL-SUP-2603].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text from February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. RFO FAR 52.211-8 is marked [Reserved] [@CL-SUP-0638]. The clause and its three alternates described above are in codified FAR 52.211-8 [@CL-SUP-2541].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An invitation requires 2,000 units within 90 days after the date of contract. Offeror A promises 2,000 units within 75 days. That is earlier than required, so the Government may award under either the required schedule or the proposed schedule. Offeror B promises 1,500 units within 60 days and the other 500 units within 100 days. The 500 units do not fall within the required period, so the offer is rejected as nonresponsive.

Under the basic clause, Offeror C measures delivery from receipt of the contract and proposes 88 days. The Government adds 5 calendar days for the ordinary mails, so the computed delivery date is 93 days after award, later than the required 90 days, and the offer is rejected. Had Offeror C proposed 85 days, the computed 90 days would not be later than the required delivery date, and the offer would not be rejected on that ground.

Where Alternate I is used with an assumed award date of 1 June and the contract is in fact awarded on 8 June, each delivery date moves 7 calendar days later, so a delivery date of 1 September becomes 8 September.

## Never confuse

Offers that propose delivery of each quantity within the applicable delivery period specified in the required delivery schedule will be evaluated equally as regards time of delivery [@CL-SUP-2542]. When an offeror offers an earlier delivery schedule than that required, the Government reserves the right to award under either the required delivery schedule or the proposed delivery schedule [@CL-SUP-2544]. Offers that propose delivery that will not clearly fall within the applicable required delivery period specified in the required delivery schedule will be considered nonresponsive and rejected [@CL-SUP-2543].

Alternates I, II, and III apply where the delivery schedule is based on an assumed date of award, an assumed date the contractor will receive notice of award, or the actual date the contractor receives a written notice of award, respectively [@CL-SUP-2594; @CL-SUP-2598; @CL-SUP-2602], whereas paragraph (a) of the clause sets out the required delivery schedule and provides that offers proposing delivery that will not clearly fall within the applicable required delivery period will be considered nonresponsive and rejected [@CL-SUP-2541; @CL-SUP-2543]. Codified FAR 52.211-8 states a required delivery schedule and rejects as nonresponsive an offer that will not clearly fall within the required delivery period [@CL-SUP-2541; @CL-SUP-2543], while RFO FAR 52.211-8 is marked [Reserved] [@CL-SUP-0638].
