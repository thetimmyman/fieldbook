## Purpose

Shows how the time of delivery clause states the required delivery schedule, how offers that propose a different schedule are treated, how the date of award or of receipt of the notice of award fixes the delivery period, and what each of the three alternates changes.

## The rule

|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-SUP-2541] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.211-8 [@CL-SUP-2541] |

Paragraph (a) states delivery as a required delivery schedule that lists each item number, the quantity, and the days after the date of the contract within which delivery is required; the Contracting Officer inserts the specific details [@CL-SUP-2541]. Every bracketed blank in the clause and its alternates, including the schedule details and any assumed date, is contract-specific and is filled in when the clause is used [@CL-SUP-2541; @CL-SUP-2551].

The clause also provides a schedule for the offeror's proposed delivery, listing item number, quantity and days after the date of contract [@CL-SUP-2591]. Offers that propose delivery of each quantity within the applicable delivery period are evaluated equally as regards time of delivery [@CL-SUP-2542], and an offer whose delivery will not clearly fall within the applicable required delivery period is considered nonresponsive and rejected [@CL-SUP-2543]. When an offeror offers an earlier delivery schedule than required, the Government reserves the right to award under either the required delivery schedule or the proposed delivery schedule [@CL-SUP-2544]. If the offeror proposes no other delivery schedule, the required delivery schedule applies [@CL-SUP-2545].

## How it works

Paragraph (b) of the basic clause directs attention to the Contract Award provision of the solicitation, under which a written award or acceptance of offer mailed, or otherwise furnished to the successful offeror, results in a binding contract [@CL-SUP-2592]. The Government will mail or otherwise furnish the award or notice of award not later than the day award is dated [@CL-SUP-2593], so the offeror should compute the time available for performance beginning with the actual date of award, rather than the date the written notice of award is received through the ordinary mails [@CL-SUP-2546].

Under the basic clause, the Government still evaluates an offer that measures delivery from the Contractor's date of receipt of the contract or notice of award, by adding five calendar days for delivery of the award through the ordinary mails [@CL-SUP-2547]. If the solicitation states that the contract or notice of award will be transmitted electronically, it adds one working day instead [@CL-SUP-2548]. A working day excludes weekends and United States Federal holidays [@CL-SUP-2549]. If the offered delivery date, computed that way, is later than the required delivery date, the offer is considered nonresponsive and rejected [@CL-SUP-2550].

Each alternate substitutes for paragraph (b) of the basic clause or, in Alternate III, allows it to be deleted, and sets the heading of the third column of paragraph (a) [@CL-SUP-2594; @CL-SUP-2598; @CL-SUP-2602]. Alternate I applies where the delivery schedule is expressed in specific calendar dates or specific periods and is based on an assumed date of award, and the contracting officer may then substitute its paragraph (b) for paragraph (b) of the basic clause [@CL-SUP-2594]. The time may be expressed by substituting on or before, during the months, or not sooner than and later than as headings for the third column of paragraph (a) [@CL-SUP-2595]. The Alternate I paragraph states that the delivery dates or specific periods are based on the assumption that the Government will make award by a stated date, and each delivery date is extended by the number of calendar days after that date that the contract is in fact awarded [@CL-SUP-2551]. It keeps the direction to the Contract Award provision [@CL-SUP-2596] and the instruction that the offeror compute the time available from the actual date of award rather than the date the notice is received through the ordinary mails [@CL-SUP-2597].

Alternate II applies where the delivery schedule is expressed in specific calendar dates or specific periods and is based on an assumed date the contractor will receive notice of award, and the contracting officer may then substitute its paragraph (b) [@CL-SUP-2598]. The heading of the third column of paragraph (a) may be within days after the date of receipt of a written notice of award [@CL-SUP-2599]. Each delivery date is extended by the number of calendar days after the assumed date that the Contractor receives notice of award [@CL-SUP-2600], provided that the Contractor promptly acknowledges receipt of notice of award [@CL-SUP-2601].

Alternate III applies where the delivery schedule is to be based on the actual date the contractor receives a written notice of award, and the contracting officer may then delete paragraph (b) of the basic clause [@CL-SUP-2602]. The same heading, within days after the date of receipt of a written notice of award, may be used for the third column of paragraph (a) [@CL-SUP-2603].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An invitation requires 2,000 units within 90 days after the date of contract. Offeror A promises 2,000 units within 75 days. That is earlier than required, so the Government may award under either the required schedule or the proposed schedule. Offeror B promises 1,500 units within 60 days and the other 500 units within 100 days. The 500 units do not fall within the required period, so the offer is rejected as nonresponsive.

Under the basic clause, Offeror C measures delivery from receipt of the contract and proposes 88 days. The Government adds 5 calendar days for the ordinary mails, so the computed delivery date is 93 days after award, later than the required 90 days, and the offer is rejected. Had Offeror C proposed 85 days, the computed 90 days would not be later than the required delivery date, and the offer would not be rejected on that ground.

Where Alternate I is used with an assumed award date of 1 June and the contract is in fact awarded on 8 June, each delivery date moves 7 calendar days later, so a delivery date of 1 September becomes 8 September.

## Never confuse

An earlier delivery offer is not automatically the better offer. Offers that deliver within the required period are evaluated equally as regards time of delivery [@CL-SUP-2542], so an earlier schedule gives the Government an option rather than a preference [@CL-SUP-2544], while an offer that does not clearly fall within the required period is rejected rather than ranked lower [@CL-SUP-2543].

An alternate changes the event that the delivery dates are tied to, namely an assumed award date, an assumed date of receipt of notice of award, or actual receipt of a written notice of award [@CL-SUP-2594; @CL-SUP-2598; @CL-SUP-2602]. It does not substitute for the paragraph (a) rule that an offer that does not clearly fall within the required delivery period is rejected [@CL-SUP-2543].

## Company calibration

Confirm, for each delivery schedule in force, which date the periods run from and which alternate paragraph the contract uses [@Q-022].
