## Purpose

Shows how a contracting officer orders changes to drawings and specifications, how a contractor reports a differing site condition and gets an adjustment, and how an architect-engineer contractor keeps to the subcontractors and consultants agreed in negotiations.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-SUP-3091; @CL-SUP-3097] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.243-5 and FAR 52.244-4 [@CL-SUP-3091; @CL-SUP-3097] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.243-5 [@CL-SUP-0018; @CL-SUP-0019; @CL-SUP-0020; @CL-SUP-0021; @CL-SUP-0022; @CL-SUP-0023]; RFO FAR 52.244-4 [@CL-SUP-0024; @CL-SUP-0025] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-3091; @CL-SUP-3097] |
| Applies to | Contracts that include the changes and changed conditions clause, or the architect-engineer subcontractors clause [@CL-SUP-3091; @CL-SUP-3097] |

Under the Changes and Changed Conditions clause, the contracting officer may order changes in the drawings and specifications, in writing and within the general scope of the contract [@CL-SUP-3091]. The contractor must promptly notify the contracting officer, in writing, of subsurface or latent physical conditions that differ materially from those the contract indicates, or of unknown unusual physical conditions at the site, before proceeding with the work [@CL-SUP-3092].

If changes or conditions increase or decrease the cost of, or the time required for, the work, the contracting officer makes an equitable adjustment upon the contractor's proposal [@CL-SUP-3093]. The contractor must submit the proposal for adjustment before final payment under the contract [@CL-SUP-3094]. For a condition, the contracting officer makes no equitable adjustment unless the contractor has submitted, and the contracting officer has received, the required written notice, or the contracting officer waives the written notice [@CL-SUP-3095]. Failure to agree to any adjustment is a dispute under the Disputes clause [@CL-SUP-3096].

Under the Subcontractors and Outside Associates and Consultants clause for architect-engineer services, the subcontractors, associates and consultants that the contractor requires for the covered services are limited to the individuals or firms specifically identified and agreed to during negotiations [@CL-SUP-3097]. The contractor must obtain the contracting officer's written consent before substituting any of them [@CL-SUP-3098].

## How it works

A change starts with a written order from the contracting officer, and a differing condition starts with the contractor's written notice [@CL-SUP-3091; @CL-SUP-3092]. In both cases the equitable adjustment follows where cost or time changes, and on the contractor's proposal submitted before final payment [@CL-SUP-3093; @CL-SUP-3094]. The written-notice limit applies to an adjustment for a condition: without notice received, or a waiver, there is no adjustment for that condition [@CL-SUP-3095]. The limit is worded for conditions only, and it does not mention ordered changes [@CL-SUP-3095]. If the parties cannot agree on an adjustment, the disagreement is a dispute under the Disputes clause [@CL-SUP-3096].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text from February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. RFO FAR 52.243-5(a) says the contracting officer may order changes in the drawings and specifications within the scope of the contract in writing [@CL-SUP-0018], where the codified paragraph says within the general scope of the contract [@CL-SUP-3091]. RFO paragraph (b) keeps the rule that the contractor promptly notifies the contracting officer, in writing, of subsurface or latent physical conditions differing materially from those indicated in the contract or unknown unusual physical conditions at the site before proceeding with the work [@CL-SUP-0019] [@CL-SUP-3092]. RFO paragraph (c) says that, if changes under paragraph (a) of this clause or conditions under paragraph (b) of this clause increase or decrease the cost of, or time required for performing the work, the contracting officer makes an equitable adjustment (see paragraph (d) of this clause) upon submittal of a proposal [@CL-SUP-0020], where the codified paragraph gives the same rule without the words of this clause [@CL-SUP-3093]. RFO paragraph (c) keeps the rule that the contractor submits the proposal for adjustment before final payment under the contract [@CL-SUP-0021] [@CL-SUP-3094]. RFO paragraph (d) says that the contracting officer makes no equitable adjustment under paragraph (b) of this clause unless the contractor has submitted the required written notice and the contracting officer has received the notice, or the contracting officer waives the requirement for written notice [@CL-SUP-0022], where the codified paragraph words the first condition as the contractor having submitted, and the contracting officer having received, the required written notice, and refers to the written notice in the waiver [@CL-SUP-3095]. RFO paragraph (e) keeps the rule that failure to agree to any adjustment is a dispute under the Disputes clause [@CL-SUP-0023] [@CL-SUP-3096]. RFO FAR 52.244-4 keeps the limit of subcontractors, associates and consultants to individuals or firms specifically identified and agreed to during negotiations [@CL-SUP-0024] [@CL-SUP-3097], and the requirement for the contracting officer's written consent before any substitution [@CL-SUP-0025] [@CL-SUP-3098]. The RFO text of FAR 52.243-5 gives FAR 43.305(e) as its prescription [@CL-SUP-9000], where the codified text gives FAR 43.205(e) [@CL-SUP-9003]. The RFO text of FAR 52.244-4 gives FAR 44.201-5(b) as its prescription [@CL-SUP-9001], where the codified text gives FAR 44.204(b) [@CL-SUP-9004].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A construction contract is priced at 2,000,000. During excavation the crew meets rock that the drawings do not show. The contractor writes to the contracting officer that week, before starting that part of the work, and the contracting officer receives the notice. Thirty days later the contractor submits a proposal for 90,000 of extra cost and 12 extra days, long before final payment. The answer: cost and time rose, the written notice came before the work and was received, and the proposal came before final payment, so the contracting officer makes an equitable adjustment for the cost and time the parties agree. If they cannot agree on the figures, the disagreement is a dispute under the Disputes clause.

A second condition, an unmarked buried tank, is found. The crew works on and the contractor never sends written notice. The answer: the contracting officer makes no equitable adjustment for that condition unless the contracting officer waives the written notice, and the contractor also failed to notify before proceeding with the work.

On a separate architect-engineer contract, the team agreed in negotiations includes one structural consultant. The firm leaves the project and the contractor wants to use another. The answer: the contractor needs the contracting officer's written consent before making the substitution.

## Never confuse

An ordered change is not a differing site condition. A change begins with a written direction from the contracting officer within the general scope, while a condition begins in the field and puts a duty on the contractor to notify before proceeding [@CL-SUP-3091; @CL-SUP-3092]. The consent in the architect-engineer clause is also not the general subcontract consent clause: it fixes the named team and requires written consent for a substitution [@CL-SUP-3097; @CL-SUP-3098]. Related cards are [construction changes](../../close-revrec/revenue/cls-changes-construction.md), [notification of changes](../../close-revrec/revenue/cls-notification-of-changes.md), [the Disputes clause](../../close-revrec/revenue/cls-disputes-clause.md) and [subcontract consent and advance notification](sup-subcontracts-consent-advance-notification.md). The codified paragraph (a) lets the contracting officer order changes in writing within the general scope of the contract [@CL-SUP-3091], while the RFO paragraph (a) says within the scope of the contract [@CL-SUP-0018].
