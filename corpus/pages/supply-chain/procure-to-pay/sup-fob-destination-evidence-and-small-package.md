## Purpose

Shows what evidence of shipment the contractor keeps under f.o.b. destination and how prepaid small package freight is billed under f.o.b. origin.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-SUP-1986; @CL-SUP-1991] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.247-48 and 52.247-65 [@CL-SUP-1986; @CL-SUP-1991] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.247-48 [@CL-SUP-0246; @CL-SUP-0247; @CL-SUP-0248; @CL-SUP-0249; @CL-SUP-0250]. No RFO equivalent for FAR 52.247-65: the RFO text lists it as [Reserved] [@CL-SUP-0251] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-1986; @CL-SUP-1991] |
| Applies to | Contracts containing the evidence of shipment clause or the prepaid small package clause [@CL-SUP-1986; @CL-SUP-1991] |

If a contract is awarded on an f.o.b. destination basis, the contractor shall not submit an invoice until the supplies covered have been shipped to the destination [@CL-SUP-1986]. It must retain, and make available to the Government for review as necessary, evidence of shipment documentation for 3 years after final payment [@CL-SUP-1987]. For a common carrier the evidence is a signed copy of the commercial bill of lading, indicating the carrier's intent to ship the supplies to the destination [@CL-SUP-1988]. For parcel post it is a copy of the certificate of mailing, and for other transport a copy of the delivery document showing receipt at the destination [@CL-SUP-1989]. The contractor does not have to submit that documentation with its invoice [@CL-SUP-1990].

When the Contracting Officer authorizes it, f.o.b. origin freight shipments without a security classification move on prepaid commercial bills of lading or other shipping documents to domestic destinations, including air and water terminals [@CL-SUP-1991]. Individual shipment weight is governed by carrier restrictions but may not exceed 150 pounds by any form of commercial air or 1,000 pounds by other commercial carriers [@CL-SUP-1992]. The Government will reimburse the contractor for reasonable freight charges [@CL-SUP-1993]. Transportation charges are billed as a separate item on the invoice for each shipment, and a copy of the bill of lading, shipment receipt or freight bill accompanies the invoice unless the contract specifies otherwise [@CL-SUP-1994].

## How it works

Under the evidence clause, if the contract is awarded on an f.o.b. destination basis, the contractor shall not submit an invoice for payment until the supplies covered by the invoice have been shipped to the destination [@CL-SUP-1986], and shall retain the evidence of shipment documentation for a period of 3 years after final payment and make it available to the Government for review as necessary [@CL-SUP-1987].

For small package shipments the contractor annotates the commercial bill of lading as the contract's commercial bill of lading notations clause requires [@CL-SUP-2080] and consolidates prepaid shipments under procedures set by the cognizant transportation office [@CL-SUP-2081]. It may combine Government prepaid shipments with its own commercial shipments for delivery to one or more consignees, and the Government reimburses its pro rata share of the total freight costs [@CL-SUP-2082]. It provides a copy of the commercial bill of lading promptly to each consignee, and may not divide quantities into mailable lots to avoid movement by other modes of transportation [@CL-SUP-2083]. The Government processes loss and damage claims [@CL-SUP-2084].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text from February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. If an RFO contract is awarded on an f.o.b. destination basis, RFO FAR 52.247-48 provides that the Contractor shall not submit an invoice for payment until the supplies covered by the invoice have been shipped to the destination [@CL-SUP-0246], and shall retain, and make available to the Government for review as necessary, the evidence of shipment documentation for a period of 3 years after final payment under the contract [@CL-SUP-0247]. If transportation is accomplished by common carrier the documentation is a signed copy of the commercial bill of lading indicating the carrier's intent to ship the supplies to the destination specified in the contract [@CL-SUP-0248]; if by parcel post, a copy of the certificate of mailing; and if by other than common carrier or parcel post, a copy of the delivery document showing receipt at the destination specified in the contract [@CL-SUP-0249]. The Contractor is not required to submit evidence of shipment documentation with its invoice [@CL-SUP-0250]. The RFO text lists FAR 52.247-65, the small package clause, as [Reserved] [@CL-SUP-0251].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contract is f.o.b. destination. The contractor ships 500 units by common carrier. It may not invoice until the supplies have shipped to the destination. It keeps the signed commercial bill of lading showing the carrier's intent to ship to the contract destination, and does not send it with the invoice. Final payment is made in year 4, so the contractor keeps the document through year 7. Separately, a contract is f.o.b. origin and the Contracting Officer has authorized small package shipments. The contractor sends an unclassified 30 pound package to a domestic destination by commercial air on a prepaid commercial bill of lading, well under the 150 pound air limit, and annotates the bill of lading as the notations clause requires. The freight is 45. The Government reimburses the 45 as reasonable freight. The invoice shows the 45 as its own line, a copy of the freight bill accompanies the invoice, and the Government, not the contractor, processes any loss claim. The small package part of this example follows the codified clause, not the RFO text, which lists FAR 52.247-65 as [Reserved] [@CL-SUP-0251].

## Never confuse

For transportation by common carrier, the signed copy of the commercial bill of lading indicates the carrier's intent to ship the supplies to the destination specified in the contract [@CL-SUP-1988]. For transportation by other than common carrier or parcel post, the document is a copy of the delivery document showing receipt at the destination specified in the contract [@CL-SUP-1989]. The codified text lets the Contracting Officer authorize prepaid small package freight shipments [@CL-SUP-1991], unlike the RFO text, which lists FAR 52.247-65 as [Reserved] [@CL-SUP-0251].
