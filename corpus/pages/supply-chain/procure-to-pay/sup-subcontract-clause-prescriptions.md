## Purpose

Shows when the contracting officer must or may put each subcontract clause that this section covers into a solicitation or contract, and the thresholds that decide it.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-SUP-3844] |
| Codified citation | FAR 44.204 [@CL-SUP-3844] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-3844] |
| Applies to | Solicitations and contracts of the types listed in the prescriptions [@CL-SUP-3844] |

The contracting officer decides what goes into the solicitation or contract, and the section directs insertion with "shall" in two prescriptions and permits it with "may" in one [@CL-SUP-3844; @CL-SUP-3850; @CL-SUP-3851]. This section gives three prescriptions. It does not prescribe the clause at 52.244-6, which FAR 44.403 covers [@CL-SUP-3857].

The Subcontracts clause. The contracting officer shall insert the clause at 52.244-2, Subcontracts, in solicitations and contracts when contemplating any one of five alternatives. The first is a cost-reimbursement contract [@CL-SUP-3844]. The second is a letter contract that exceeds the simplified acquisition threshold [@CL-SUP-3845]. The third is a fixed-price contract that exceeds the simplified acquisition threshold and under which unpriced contract actions, including unpriced modifications or unpriced delivery orders, are anticipated [@CL-SUP-3846]. The fourth is a time-and-materials contract that exceeds the simplified acquisition threshold, and the fifth is a labor-hour contract that exceeds it [@CL-SUP-3847]. The cost-reimbursement trigger has no dollar threshold [@CL-SUP-3844]. If a cost-reimbursement contract is contemplated for a civilian agency other than the Coast Guard and the National Aeronautics and Space Administration, the officer shall use the clause with its Alternate I [@CL-SUP-3848]. Use of the clause is not required in either of two cases: fixed-price architect-engineer contracts, or contracts for mortuary services, refuse services, or shipment and storage of personal property, when an agency-prescribed clause on approval of subcontractors' facilities is required [@CL-SUP-3849].

The architect-engineer clause. The contracting officer may insert the clause at 52.244-4, Subcontractors and Outside Associates and Consultants (Architect-Engineer Services), in architect-engineer contracts [@CL-SUP-3850].

The competition clause. When contracting by negotiation, the contracting officer shall insert the clause at 52.244-5, Competition in Subcontracting, in solicitations and contracts when the contract amount is expected to exceed the simplified acquisition threshold, unless an exception below applies [@CL-SUP-3851]. The officer does not insert it if either of two exceptions applies: a firm-fixed-price contract awarded on the basis of adequate price competition, or whose prices are set by law or regulation, is contemplated, or a time-and-materials, labor-hour or architect-engineer contract is contemplated [@CL-SUP-3852].

The commercial products and commercial services clause. This section does not prescribe it. Under FAR 44.403, the contracting officer shall insert the clause at 52.244-6, Subcontracts for Commercial Products and Commercial Services, in solicitations and contracts other than those for commercial products or commercial services, and no dollar threshold is stated there [@CL-SUP-3857]. See [subcontracts for commercial products and commercial services](../sourcing-evidence/sup-subcontracts-commercial-products-and-services.md).

## How it works

Each prescription turns on the contract type, and most also turn on the simplified acquisition threshold [@CL-SUP-3845; @CL-SUP-3846; @CL-SUP-3847; @CL-SUP-3851]. The contracting officer applies them when contemplating the contract. Two prescriptions direct insertion and one permits it. The Subcontracts clause and the competition clause are inserted by direction, with the stated exclusions and exceptions. The architect-engineer clause may be inserted [@CL-SUP-3844; @CL-SUP-3851; @CL-SUP-3850]. The consent rule is separate; see [consent to subcontract](sup-consent-to-subcontract.md) and [consent and advance notification](sup-subcontracts-consent-advance-notification.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A defense agency negotiates a time-and-materials contract expected to exceed the simplified acquisition threshold. The contracting officer inserts the Subcontracts clause, because the contract is a time-and-materials contract that exceeds that threshold [@CL-SUP-3847]. The officer does not insert the Competition in Subcontracting clause, because a time-and-materials contract is contemplated [@CL-SUP-3852]. Had the contract been cost-reimbursement at a civilian agency other than the Coast Guard and the National Aeronautics and Space Administration, the officer would use the Subcontracts clause with its Alternate I [@CL-SUP-3848].

## Never confuse

The clause prescription is not the consent rule. The prescription decides which clause goes into the solicitation or contract. The consent rule decides when a subcontract needs consent [@CL-SUP-3844; @CL-SUP-3830; @CL-SUP-3833]. See [consent to subcontract](sup-consent-to-subcontract.md).

A permitted clause is not a required one. The architect-engineer clause may be inserted, and the Subcontracts clause is directed for the listed contract types [@CL-SUP-3850; @CL-SUP-3844]. An architect-engineer contract also does not always escape the Subcontracts clause: the exclusion covers fixed-price architect-engineer contracts, and a cost-reimbursement contract triggers the clause on its own terms [@CL-SUP-3849; @CL-SUP-3844]. The competition clause is not inserted when an architect-engineer contract is contemplated [@CL-SUP-3852].
