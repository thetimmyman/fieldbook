## Purpose

Explains the share of ocean cargo that must move on privately owned United States-flag commercial vessels, when that share applies, the bill of lading copies that show it, and how the clause flows down and where it does not apply.

| Codified citation | FAR 52.247-64 [@CL-SUP-2027] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |

| Applies to | Equipment, materials or commodities that may be transported by ocean vessel and are acquired for a United States Government agency account, furnished to or for the account of a foreign nation in the cases the clause lists, or acquired with advance of funds, loans or guaranties made by or on behalf of the United States [@CL-SUP-2151; @CL-SUP-2152; @CL-SUP-2182] |

The clause directs the contractor to use privately owned United States-flag commercial vessels to ship at least 50 percent of the gross tonnage involved under the contract, computed separately for dry bulk carriers, dry cargo liners and tankers [@CL-SUP-2027]. The clause states that the Cargo Preference Act of 1954 requires Federal departments and agencies to transport in such vessels at least 50 percent of the gross tonnage of equipment, materials or commodities that may be transported in ocean vessels, subject to the exceptions in paragraph (e) [@CL-SUP-2153; @CL-SUP-2033]. The contractor's duty applies whenever it ships equipment, materials or commodities under the conditions of paragraph (a), to the extent that such vessels are available at rates that are fair and reasonable [@CL-SUP-2028].

## How it works

The paragraph (a) conditions are met when cargo that may be transported by ocean vessel, located within or outside the United States, is acquired for a United States Government agency account [@CL-SUP-2151]; is furnished to or for the account of a foreign nation without provision for reimbursement, or furnished for the account of a foreign nation in connection with which the United States advances funds or credits or guarantees the convertibility of foreign currencies [@CL-SUP-2152]; or is acquired with advance of funds, loans or guaranties made by or on behalf of the United States [@CL-SUP-2182].

For each shipment the contractor submits one legible copy of a rated on-board ocean bill of lading to both the contracting officer and the Office of Cargo Preference of the Maritime Administration [@CL-SUP-2029]. Subcontractor bills of lading are submitted through the prime contractor [@CL-SUP-2030]. The copies are due within 20 working days of the date of loading for shipments originating in the United States and within 30 working days for shipments originating outside the United States [@CL-SUP-2031]. Each copy carries the sponsoring United States Government agency, the vessel name, the flag of registry, the date and port of loading and the port of final discharge [@CL-SUP-2159], and the commodity description, the gross weight in pounds and cubic feet if available, and the total ocean freight revenue in United States dollars [@CL-SUP-2160].

The contractor inserts the substance of the clause, including the flowdown paragraph, in all subcontracts and purchase orders under the contract, except those described in paragraph (e)(4) [@CL-SUP-2032]. The paragraph (a) requirement does not apply to cargoes carried in vessels as required or authorized by law or treaty [@CL-SUP-2033], to ocean transportation between foreign countries of supplies purchased with foreign currencies made available, or derived from funds made available, under the Foreign Assistance Act of 1961 [@CL-SUP-2035], or to shipments of classified supplies when the classification prohibits the use of non-Government vessels [@CL-SUP-2034]. The exception for subcontracts and purchase orders for commercial products or commercial services does not hold where the contract is a contract or agreement for ocean transportation services or a construction contract [@CL-SUP-2036], where the supplies are items the contractor is reselling or distributing to the Government without adding value [@CL-SUP-2154]; the clause says that, generally, the contractor does not add value to the items when it subcontracts items for f.o.b. destination shipment [@CL-SUP-2110], or where the supplies are shipped in direct support of United States military contingency operations, exercises, or forces deployed in connection with United Nations or North Atlantic Treaty Organization humanitarian or peacekeeping operations [@CL-SUP-2155].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contract moves 10,000 tons of dry bulk cargo and 4,000 tons of tanker cargo, all acquired for a United States Government agency account, so the paragraph (a) conditions are met. The 50 percent floor is computed separately for each carrier type: 5,000 tons for dry bulk and 2,000 tons for tankers. The contractor books 6,000 tons of dry bulk on privately owned United States-flag vessels, which clears the dry bulk floor, but only 1,500 tons of tanker cargo, which is 37.5 percent and does not clear the tanker floor. The dry bulk surplus does not cover the tanker shortfall because the shares are computed separately, and the shortfall is permitted only to the extent that such tankers were not available at fair and reasonable rates. A dry bulk shipment loads in the United States, so a rated on-board bill of lading copy with the listed information goes to the contracting officer and the Office of Cargo Preference within 20 working days of loading; a shipment loading outside the United States would have 30 working days. A purchase order for commercial products that the contractor resells to the Government without adding value does not fall in the commercial products exception, so the substance of the clause goes into it. Generally, the contractor adds no value when it subcontracts those items for f.o.b. destination shipment, so a purchase order of that kind is usually in the same position.

## Never confuse

The basic clause sets a share of tonnage by carrier type, not an order to use only those vessels [@CL-SUP-2027]. Alternate I substitutes its own paragraphs (a) and (b) for those of the basic clause and has the contractor use privately owned United States-flag commercial vessels, and no others, for the ocean transportation of any supplies under the contract, except as provided in paragraphs (b) and (e) [@CL-SUP-2156; @CL-SUP-2157]. Under Alternate I, if privately owned United States-flag commercial vessels are not available for timely shipment at fair and reasonable rates, the contractor notifies the contracting officer and requests either authorization to ship in foreign-flag vessels or designation of available United States-flag vessels [@CL-SUP-2106], and if the contracting officer authorizes foreign-flag shipment in writing, the contract price is equitably adjusted to reflect the difference in cost between shipping in privately owned United States-flag commercial vessels and in foreign-flag vessels [@CL-SUP-2107]. Alternate II substitutes its own paragraph (e) for paragraph (e) of the basic clause [@CL-SUP-2158]. Under Alternate II, the exception for subcontracts and purchase orders under the contract for commercial products or commercial services does not hold where the supplies are items the contractor is reselling or distributing to the Government without adding value [@CL-SUP-2108], or are shipments in direct support of United States military contingency operations, exercises, or forces deployed in connection with United Nations or North Atlantic Treaty Organization humanitarian or peacekeeping operations [@CL-SUP-2109]; the printed paragraph (e)(4) of Alternate II names only those two conditions [@CL-SUP-2108; @CL-SUP-2109]. The clause actually in the contract decides which version applies.
