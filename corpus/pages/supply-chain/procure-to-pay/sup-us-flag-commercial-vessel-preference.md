## Purpose

Explains the share of ocean cargo that must move on privately owned United States-flag commercial vessels, when that share applies, the bill of lading copies that show it, and how the clause flows down and where it does not apply.

The clause directs the contractor to use privately owned U.S.-flag commercial vessels to ship at least 50 percent of the gross tonnage involved under the contract (computed separately for dry bulk carriers, dry cargo liners, and tankers) [@CL-SUP-2027]. Except as provided in paragraph (e) of the clause, the Cargo Preference Act of 1954 (section 1241(b) of the appendix to title 46 of the United States Code) requires that Federal departments and agencies shall transport in privately owned U.S.-flag commercial vessels at least 50 percent of the gross tonnage of equipment, materials, or commodities that may be transported in ocean vessels (computed separately for dry bulk carriers, dry cargo liners, and tankers) when the conditions in paragraph (a) apply [@CL-SUP-0043; @CL-SUP-0044; @CL-SUP-2153; @CL-SUP-2033]. The contractor's duty applies whenever shipping any equipment, materials, or commodities under the conditions set forth in paragraph (a) of the clause, to the extent that such vessels are available at rates that are fair and reasonable for privately owned U.S.-flag commercial vessels [@CL-SUP-2028].

## How it works

The paragraph (a) conditions are met when cargo that may be transported by ocean vessel, located within or outside the United States, meets any one of four alternatives. It is acquired for a United States Government agency account [@CL-SUP-2151]; it is furnished to or for the account of a foreign nation without provision for reimbursement; it is furnished for the account of a foreign nation in connection with which the United States advances funds or credits or guarantees the convertibility of foreign currencies [@CL-SUP-2152]; or it is acquired with advance of funds, loans or guaranties made by or on behalf of the United States [@CL-SUP-2182].

For each shipment the contractor submits one legible copy of a rated on-board ocean bill of lading to both the contracting officer and the Office of Cargo Preference of the Maritime Administration [@CL-SUP-2029]. Subcontractor bills of lading are submitted through the prime contractor [@CL-SUP-2030]. The copies are due within 20 working days of the date of loading for shipments originating in the United States and within 30 working days for shipments originating outside the United States [@CL-SUP-2031]. Each copy carries the sponsoring United States Government agency, the vessel name, the flag of registry, the date and port of loading and the port of final discharge [@CL-SUP-2159], and the commodity description, the gross weight in pounds and cubic feet if available, and the total ocean freight revenue in United States dollars [@CL-SUP-2160].

The contractor inserts the substance of the clause, including the flowdown paragraph, in all subcontracts and purchase orders under the contract, except those described in paragraph (e)(4) [@CL-SUP-2032]. The paragraph (a) requirement does not apply to cargoes carried in vessels as required or authorized by law or treaty [@CL-SUP-2033], to ocean transportation between foreign countries of supplies purchased with foreign currencies made available, or derived from funds made available, under the Foreign Assistance Act of 1961 [@CL-SUP-2035], or to shipments of classified supplies when the classification prohibits the use of non-Government vessels [@CL-SUP-2034]. The exception for subcontracts and purchase orders for commercial products or commercial services does not hold in any one of these alternative cases: where the contract is a contract or agreement for ocean transportation services or a construction contract [@CL-SUP-2036], where the supplies are items the contractor is reselling or distributing to the Government without adding value [@CL-SUP-2154]; the clause says that, generally, the contractor does not add value to the items when it subcontracts items for f.o.b. destination shipment [@CL-SUP-2110], or where the supplies are shipped in direct support of United States military contingency operations, exercises, or forces deployed in connection with United Nations or North Atlantic Treaty Organization humanitarian or peacekeeping operations [@CL-SUP-2155].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text from February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. RFO FAR 52.247-64(a) says that, except as provided in paragraph (e), the Cargo Preference Act of 1954 (cited as section 55305 of title 46 of the United States Code) requires Federal departments and agencies to transport in privately owned U.S.-flag commercial vessels at least 50 percent of the gross tonnage, where the codified paragraph cites section 1241(b) of the appendix to that title [@CL-SUP-0724; @CL-SUP-0043; @CL-SUP-2153]. It keeps the scope of that tonnage as equipment, materials or commodities that may be transported in ocean vessels, computed separately for dry bulk carriers, dry cargo liners and tankers [@CL-SUP-0725; @CL-SUP-0044; @CL-SUP-2153]. It keeps the paragraph (a) conditions for cargo acquired for a U.S. Government agency account [@CL-SUP-0726; @CL-SUP-2151], furnished to or for the account of a foreign nation [@CL-SUP-0727; @CL-SUP-2152], or acquired with advance of funds, loans or guaranties made by or on behalf of the United States [@CL-SUP-0728; @CL-SUP-2182]. It keeps the Contractor's duty to ship at least 50 percent of the gross tonnage involved under the contract, computed separately for those carrier types [@CL-SUP-0729; @CL-SUP-2027], whenever shipping under the paragraph (a) conditions, to the extent that such vessels are available at rates that are fair and reasonable for privately owned U.S.-flag commercial vessels [@CL-SUP-0730; @CL-SUP-2028]. It keeps the submission of one legible copy of a rated on-board ocean bill of lading for each shipment to both the Contracting Officer and the Office of Cargo Preference [@CL-SUP-0731; @CL-SUP-2029], with subcontractor bills of lading submitted through the Prime Contractor [@CL-SUP-0732; @CL-SUP-2030], within 20 working days of loading for shipments originating in the United States or 30 working days for shipments originating outside it [@CL-SUP-0733; @CL-SUP-2031], and the listed contents of each copy [@CL-SUP-0734; @CL-SUP-2159] [@CL-SUP-0735; @CL-SUP-2160]. It keeps the flowdown of the substance of the clause, including paragraph (d), in all subcontracts or purchase orders except those described in paragraph (e)(4) [@CL-SUP-0736; @CL-SUP-2032]. It keeps the exceptions for cargoes carried in vessels as required or authorized by law or treaty [@CL-SUP-0737; @CL-SUP-2033], ocean transportation between foreign countries of supplies purchased with the listed foreign currencies or funds [@CL-SUP-0738; @CL-SUP-2035], and shipments of classified supplies when the classification prohibits the use of non-Government vessels [@CL-SUP-0739; @CL-SUP-2034]. It keeps the exception for subcontracts or purchase orders for commercial products or commercial services unless the contract is for ocean transportation services or is a construction contract [@CL-SUP-0740; @CL-SUP-2036], or the supplies are items the Contractor is reselling or distributing to the Government without adding value [@CL-SUP-0741; @CL-SUP-2154], with the statement that, generally, the Contractor does not add value when it subcontracts items for f.o.b. destination shipment [@CL-SUP-0742; @CL-SUP-2110], or are shipped in direct support of U.S. military contingency operations, exercises or forces deployed in connection with United Nations or North Atlantic Treaty Organization humanitarian or peacekeeping operations [@CL-SUP-0743; @CL-SUP-2155].

RFO Alternate I keeps the substitution of its paragraphs (a) and (b) for those of the basic clause [@CL-SUP-0744; @CL-SUP-2156], the use of privately owned U.S.-flag commercial vessels, and no others, except as provided in paragraphs (b) and (e) [@CL-SUP-0745; @CL-SUP-0045; @CL-SUP-2157], the notice to the Contracting Officer and request for authorization to ship in foreign-flag vessels or designation of available U.S.-flag vessels when such vessels are not available for timely shipment at fair and reasonable rates [@CL-SUP-0746; @CL-SUP-0747; @CL-SUP-2106], and the equitable adjustment of the contract price if the Contracting Officer authorizes foreign-flag shipment in writing [@CL-SUP-0748; @CL-SUP-2107]. RFO Alternate II keeps the substitution of its paragraph (e) for paragraph (e) of the basic clause [@CL-SUP-0749; @CL-SUP-2158], the commercial products and commercial services exception unless the supplies are items the Contractor is reselling or distributing to the Government without adding value [@CL-SUP-0808; @CL-SUP-2108]. RFO Alternate II also says that, generally, the Contractor does not add value to the items when it subcontracts items for f.o.b. destination shipment [@CL-SUP-9210]. It keeps the same exclusion for shipments in direct support of the listed U.S. military operations [@CL-SUP-0809; @CL-SUP-2109].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contract moves 10,000 tons of dry bulk cargo and 4,000 tons of tanker cargo, all acquired for a United States Government agency account, so the paragraph (a) conditions are met. The 50 percent floor is computed separately for each carrier type: 5,000 tons for dry bulk and 2,000 tons for tankers. The contractor books 6,000 tons of dry bulk on privately owned United States-flag vessels, which clears the dry bulk floor, but only 1,500 tons of tanker cargo, which is 37.5 percent and does not clear the tanker floor. The dry bulk surplus does not cover the tanker shortfall because the shares are computed separately, and the shortfall is permitted only to the extent that such tankers were not available at fair and reasonable rates. A dry bulk shipment loads in the United States, so a rated on-board bill of lading copy with the listed information goes to the contracting officer and the Office of Cargo Preference within 20 working days of loading; a shipment loading outside the United States would have 30 working days. A purchase order for commercial products that the contractor resells to the Government without adding value does not fall in the commercial products exception, so the substance of the clause goes into it. Generally, the contractor adds no value when it subcontracts those items for f.o.b. destination shipment, so a purchase order of that kind is usually in the same position.

## Never confuse

The basic clause requires the contractor to use privately owned U.S.-flag commercial vessels to ship at least 50 percent of the gross tonnage involved under the contract (computed separately for dry bulk carriers, dry cargo liners, and tankers) [@CL-SUP-2027]. Alternate I substitutes its own paragraphs (a) and (b) for those of the basic clause and requires that, except as provided in paragraphs (b) and (e) of the clause, the contractor shall use privately owned U.S.-flag commercial vessels, and no others, in the ocean transportation of any supplies to be furnished under the contract [@CL-SUP-2156; @CL-SUP-0045; @CL-SUP-2157]. Under Alternate I, if such vessels are not available for timely shipment at rates that are fair and reasonable for privately owned U.S.-flag commercial vessels, the contractor shall notify the Contracting Officer and request in writing authorization to ship in foreign-flag vessels or designation of available U.S.-flag vessels [@CL-SUP-2106], and if the contractor is authorized in writing by the Contracting Officer to ship the supplies in foreign-flag vessels, the contract price shall be equitably adjusted to reflect the difference in costs of shipping the supplies in privately owned U.S.-flag commercial vessels and in foreign-flag vessels [@CL-SUP-2107]. Alternate II substitutes its own paragraph (e) for paragraph (e) of the basic clause [@CL-SUP-2158]. Under Alternate II, the requirement in paragraph (a) does not apply to subcontracts or purchase orders under the contract for commercial products or commercial services, unless the supplies being transported are items the contractor is reselling or distributing to the Government without adding value [@CL-SUP-2108], or are shipped in direct support of U.S. military contingency operations, exercises, or forces deployed in connection with United Nations or North Atlantic Treaty Organization humanitarian or peacekeeping operations [@CL-SUP-2109]. Codified FAR 52.247-64(a) cites the Cargo Preference Act of 1954 as section 1241(b) of the appendix to title 46 of the United States Code [@CL-SUP-0043], while RFO FAR 52.247-64(a) cites it as section 55305 of title 46 of the United States Code [@CL-SUP-0724].
