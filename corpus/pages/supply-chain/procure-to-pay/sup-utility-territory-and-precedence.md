## Purpose

Shows what an offeror of electric service represents about State law on electric utility service, and which document controls when the contract and incorporated rate material disagree.

### Territory compliance representation

Section 8093 of Public Law 100-202 generally requires purchases of electricity by any department, agency, or instrumentality of the United States to be consistent with State law governing the provision of electric utility service [@CL-SUP-2661]. That State law includes State utility commission rulings and electric utility franchises or service territories established under State statute, State regulation, or State-approved territorial agreements [@CL-SUP-2711].

By signing the offer, the offeror represents that the offer to sell electricity is consistent with Section 8093 [@CL-SUP-2662]. On request of the Contracting Officer, the offeror must submit supporting legal and factual rationale for the representation [@CL-SUP-2663].

### Order of precedence

Where the terms of the contract, including the specifications, are inconsistent with a rate schedule, rider or exhibit incorporated in the contract by reference or otherwise, or with any of the contractor's rules and regulations, the terms of the contract control [@CL-SUP-2664].

## How it works

By signing the offer, the offeror represents that the offer to sell electricity is consistent with Section 8093 of Public Law 100-202, and on request of the Contracting Officer the offeror must submit supporting legal and factual rationale for the representation [@CL-SUP-2662; @CL-SUP-2663]. Under the order of precedence clause, where the terms of the contract, including the specifications, are inconsistent with a rate schedule, rider or exhibit incorporated in the contract by reference or otherwise, or with any of the contractor's rules and regulations, the terms of the contract control [@CL-SUP-2664].

Under the RFO text, the DoD part 52 class deviation directs DoD contracting officers to use the revised FAR part 52 in place of the codified part 52 text, effective February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. In RFO FAR 52.241-1 and 52.241-2, Section 8093 of Public Law 100-202 generally requires purchases of electricity by any department, agency, or instrumentality of the United States to be consistent with State law governing the provision of electric utility service [@CL-SUP-2533; @CL-SUP-2661]. That State law includes State utility commission rulings and electric utility franchises or service territories established under State statute, State regulation, or State-approved territorial agreements [@CL-SUP-9188; @CL-SUP-2711]. By signing the offer, the offeror represents that the offer to sell electricity is consistent with Section 8093 of Public Law 100-202 [@CL-SUP-2534; @CL-SUP-2662]. On request of the Contracting Officer, the offeror must submit the supporting legal and factual rationale for that representation [@CL-SUP-2535; @CL-SUP-2663]. Where the terms of the contract, including the specifications, are inconsistent with an incorporated rate schedule, rider or exhibit, or with the contractor's rules and regulations, the contract terms control [@CL-SUP-2536; @CL-SUP-2664].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An offeror signs an offer to sell electricity. The Contracting Officer later asks for the legal and factual rationale behind the representation, and the offeror must supply it. After award, the contract specifications say that service is measured at a named delivery point. The contractor's incorporated rate schedule says that service is measured at a point the contractor chooses. The two are inconsistent, so the specifications control and service is measured at the named delivery point. A conflicting line in the contractor's own rules yields in the same way.

## Never confuse

By signing the offer, the offeror represents that the offer to sell electricity is consistent with Section 8093 of Public Law 100-202, unlike the order of precedence clause, under which the terms of the contract control where the terms of the contract, including the specifications, are inconsistent with a rate schedule, rider or exhibit incorporated in the contract by reference or otherwise, or with any of the contractor's rules and regulations [@CL-SUP-2662; @CL-SUP-2664]. The clauses on rate changes for regulated and unregulated utility service are covered in [Rate changes for regulated and unregulated utility service](sup-utility-rate-changes-regulated-and-unregulated.md).
