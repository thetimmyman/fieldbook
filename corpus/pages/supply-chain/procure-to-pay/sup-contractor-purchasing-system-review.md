## Purpose

Shows the objective of a contractor purchasing system review, the sales levels that lead to a decision to review, and what the review looks at.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR and DFARS [@CL-SUP-0516; @CL-SUP-0521] |
| Codified citation | FAR 44.301, FAR 44.302, DFARS 244.301, DFARS 244.302 and DFARS 244.303 [@CL-SUP-0516; @CL-SUP-0659; @CL-SUP-9476; @CL-SUP-0521; @CL-SUP-9477; @CL-SUP-9478] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-0516] |
| Applies to | Contractors whose sales to the Government are expected to exceed the review level [@CL-SUP-0659; @CL-SUP-0521] |

The objective of a contractor purchasing system review is to evaluate the efficiency and effectiveness with which the contractor spends Government funds and complies with Government policy when subcontracting [@CL-SUP-0516].

Under the FAR, where a contractor's sales to the Government are expected to exceed $25 million during the next 12 months, a review is performed to determine if a CPSR is needed [@CL-SUP-0659]. The sales counted exclude competitively awarded firm-fixed-price and competitively awarded fixed-price with economic price adjustment contracts and sales of commercial products and commercial services under part 12 [@CL-SUP-0658]. The head of the agency responsible for contract administration may raise or lower the $25 million review level if it is considered to be in the Government's best interest [@CL-SUP-0518]. Under the defense supplement, in lieu of the FAR threshold, the administrative contracting officer shall determine the need for a CPSR if a contractor's sales to the Government are expected to exceed $50 million during the next 12 months [@CL-SUP-0521].

The defense supplement section on the objective says the administrative contracting officer is solely responsible for initiating reviews of the contractor's purchasing systems, but other organizations may request that the administrative contracting officer initiate such reviews [@CL-SUP-9476]. The defense supplement section on the extent of review says also to review the adequacy of rationale documenting commercial product or commercial service determinations, to ensure compliance with the definition of commercial product or commercial service in FAR 2.101 [@CL-SUP-9477]. It also says to review the adequacy of the contractor's counterfeit electronic part detection and avoidance system under clause 252.246-7007, Contractor Counterfeit Electronic Part Detection and Avoidance System [@CL-SUP-9478]. The text of those two review items names no official.

## How it works

The administrative contracting officer determines the need for a CPSR based on, but not limited to, the contractor's past performance and the volume, complexity and dollar value of subcontracts [@CL-SUP-0517]. Once an initial determination has been made, the officer shall determine at least every 3 years whether a purchasing system review is necessary [@CL-SUP-0519]. The considerations listed for consent evaluation of particular subcontracts also shall be used to evaluate the contractor's purchasing system, including its policies, procedures, and performance [@CL-SUP-0520]. Special attention is given to the results of market research and the degree of price competition obtained, among other listed matters [@CL-SUP-0661]. Unless segregation of subcontracts is impracticable, the evaluation does not include subcontracts awarded exclusively in support of competitively awarded firm-fixed-price or fixed-price with economic price adjustment Government contracts, among other listed categories [@CL-SUP-0660]. What follows a finding of weaknesses is on the [Contractor purchasing system](sup-contractor-purchasing-system.md) and [Business system withholds](../../compliance/business-systems/cmp-business-system-withholds.md) cards.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor expects 60 million in sales to the Government over the next 12 months, none of it in the excluded categories. It performs mainly defense work.

Answer: under the defense supplement, which applies in lieu of the FAR threshold, the sales exceed 50 million, so the administrative contracting officer determines the need for a CPSR [@CL-SUP-0521]. The FAR level of 25 million is not the trigger here.

## Never confuse

The review level is a trigger for deciding whether a CPSR is needed [@CL-SUP-0659]. The FAR level and the defense level are different numbers, and the defense number applies in lieu of the FAR threshold [@CL-SUP-0518; @CL-SUP-0521].
