## Purpose

Shows when a prime contractor needs customer consent before placing a subcontract, with and without an approved purchasing system, and what the contracting officer may require.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-SUP-3830] |
| Codified citation | FAR 44.201-1 [@CL-SUP-3830] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-3830] |
| Applies to | Subcontract consent under prime contracts, whether or not the contractor has an approved purchasing system [@CL-SUP-3830; @CL-SUP-3833] |

The FAR's general definitions say consent to subcontract means the contracting officer's written consent for the prime contractor to enter into a particular subcontract [@CL-SUP-9539]. The first sentence of the RFO model text definition of consent to subcontract in FAR 2.101 reads the same as the codified first sentence, apart from the printed form of the apostrophe [@CL-SUP-9592; @CL-SUP-9539].

Paragraph (a) covers a contractor that has an approved purchasing system. Consent is required for subcontracts that the contracting officer specifically identifies in the contract's subcontracts clause [@CL-SUP-3830]. The contracting officer may also require consent to a subcontract if the officer has determined that an individual consent action is required to protect the Government adequately because of the subcontract's type, complexity or value, or because it needs special surveillance [@CL-SUP-3831]. These can be subcontracts for critical systems, subsystems, components or services [@CL-SUP-3856]. Subcontracts may be identified by subcontract number or by class of items, such as engines on a prime contract for airframes [@CL-SUP-3832].

Paragraph (b) covers a contractor that does not have an approved purchasing system. Consent to subcontract is required for cost-reimbursement, time-and-materials, labor-hour or letter contracts [@CL-SUP-3833]. The same sentence adds unpriced actions, including unpriced modifications and unpriced delivery orders, under fixed-price contracts that exceed the simplified acquisition threshold. For those actions it lists cost-reimbursement, time-and-materials and labor-hour subcontracts, and fixed-price subcontracts that exceed a stated amount [@CL-SUP-3834]. For the Department of Defense, the Coast Guard and the National Aeronautics and Space Administration, that amount is the greater of the simplified acquisition threshold or five percent of the total estimated cost of the contract [@CL-SUP-3835]. For other civilian agencies, the text says either the simplified acquisition threshold or five percent of the total estimated cost of the contract, and does not say which of the two governs [@CL-SUP-3836].

Paragraph (c) says consent may be required for subcontracts under prime contracts for architect-engineer services. It does not say who may require it [@CL-SUP-3837]. Paragraph (d) says the contracting officer's written authorization for the contractor to purchase from Government sources constitutes consent [@CL-SUP-3838].

## How it works

The first question is whether the contractor has an approved purchasing system. Paragraph (a) applies if it does, and paragraph (b) applies if it does not [@CL-SUP-3830; @CL-SUP-3833]. This section does not say who approves a purchasing system; see [the purchasing system definitions](sup-purchasing-system-scope-and-definitions.md).

With an approved system, the contracting officer decides which subcontracts need consent. The officer names them in the subcontracts clause, by number or by class of items, or makes an individual determination of need [@CL-SUP-3830; @CL-SUP-3831; @CL-SUP-3832]. Without an approved system, the rule turns on the contract type, on unpriced actions under fixed-price contracts and on the subcontract type. A fixed-price subcontract is tested by whether it exceeds a stated amount [@CL-SUP-3834]. For the Department of Defense, the Coast Guard and the National Aeronautics and Space Administration, that amount is the greater of the simplified acquisition threshold or five percent of the total estimated cost of the contract [@CL-SUP-3835]. For other civilian agencies, the text gives either figure and does not say which governs [@CL-SUP-3836].

This section does not state a deadline or how to request consent. For those details see [consent and advance notification](sup-subcontracts-consent-advance-notification.md). What consent does not decide is in [consent limitations](sup-consent-limitations-and-dod-requirements.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A Department of Defense prime contract is fixed-price, and the contractor has no approved purchasing system. For this example the simplified acquisition threshold is 300,000, and the total estimated cost of the contract is 8,000,000. Five percent of 8,000,000 is 400,000, so the greater of the two figures is 400,000 [@CL-SUP-3835]. Under an unpriced delivery order, the contractor plans two fixed-price subcontracts, one of 500,000 and one of 300,000. The first exceeds 400,000, so consent is required for it [@CL-SUP-3834; @CL-SUP-3835]. The second does not exceed 400,000, so the fixed-price subcontract amount does not require consent for it. The prime contract is fixed-price, which is not on paragraph (b)'s list of contract types, and the second subcontract is not a cost-reimbursement, time-and-materials or labor-hour subcontract, so paragraph (b) gives no other route to consent for it [@CL-SUP-3833; @CL-SUP-3834]. The contract's estimated cost of 8,000,000 exceeds the simplified acquisition threshold of 300,000, so the unpriced-action rule applies to it [@CL-SUP-3834]. If the contractor had an approved purchasing system, paragraph (a) would apply instead, and consent would turn on the subcontracts the contracting officer identifies or individually requires [@CL-SUP-3830; @CL-SUP-3831].

## Never confuse

Consent to subcontract is not advance notification. Advance notification is a separate statutory duty of the contractor, under cost-reimbursement contracts, to notify the contracting officer before award of the subcontracts the section lists [@CL-SUP-3839; @CL-SUP-3840; @CL-SUP-3842]. The two have different triggers; see [advance notification of subcontracts](sup-advance-notification-subcontracts.md). Consent is also not the clause prescription: the prescription decides when the Subcontracts clause goes into a solicitation or contract, and this section decides when consent is required [@CL-SUP-3844; @CL-SUP-3830; @CL-SUP-3833]. See [clause prescriptions](sup-subcontract-clause-prescriptions.md).

Without an approved purchasing system, the fixed-price subcontract threshold is not one number for every agency. It is the greater of two figures for the Department of Defense, the Coast Guard and the National Aeronautics and Space Administration. For other civilian agencies the text says either figure [@CL-SUP-3835; @CL-SUP-3836].
