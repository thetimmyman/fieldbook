## Purpose

Sets out what an acceptable purchasing system must do, how the government tests it, and what follows when material weaknesses are found.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-SUP-0101] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 252.244-7001 [@CL-SUP-0101] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |

| Applies to | Contracts carrying the Contractor Purchasing System Administration clause [@CL-SUP-0101] |

The contractor must establish and maintain an acceptable purchasing system, and failure to maintain one may result in disapproval of the system by the contracting officer and/or withholding of payments [@CL-SUP-0101].

Acceptable means compliance with the system criteria in the clause [@CL-SUP-0110].

## How it works

The system spans purchasing and subcontracting, including make-or-buy decisions, vendor selection, analysis of quoted prices, price negotiation, placing and administering orders, and expediting delivery of materials [@CL-SUP-0102].

A material weakness is a deficiency or combination of deficiencies in the internal control over information in contractor business systems such that there is a reasonable possibility that a material misstatement of such information will not be prevented, or detected and corrected, on a timely basis [@CL-SUP-0103].

The contracting officer gives a written initial determination of any material weaknesses, and the contractor shall respond within 30 days [@CL-SUP-0106] and, if it disagrees, state its rationale in writing [@CL-SUP-0282]. The contracting officer then notifies the contractor in writing of the final determination concerning remaining material weaknesses and the adequacy of any proposed or completed corrective action [@CL-SUP-0280], and system disapproval if one or more material weaknesses remain [@CL-SUP-0281].

The contractor then has 45 days from receipt either to correct the weaknesses or to submit an acceptable corrective action plan with milestones [@CL-SUP-0107].

Disapproval at the final determination brings withheld payments where the Contractor Business Systems clause is in the contract [@CL-SUP-0108].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

After award, a contractor changes its subcontract effort so that it reaches 80 percent of the total cost of the work to be performed under the contract. Its procedures produce a timely written notice to the contracting officer that identifies the revised subcontract cost and includes verification that the contractor will provide added value. The 70 percent threshold is exceeded, so the notice is triggered.

## Never confuse

This is not a subcontract consent decision, and it is not a price reasonableness finding on one purchase.

The clause tests the buying process against the system criteria, one of which is having procedures to timely notify the contracting officer in writing when subcontract effort changes after award to exceed 70 percent of the total cost of the work [@CL-SUP-0104]. That notification must identify the revised cost of the subcontract effort and include verification that the contractor will provide added value [@CL-SUP-0105].

Where Alternate I is used, the listed paragraphs reach only contractors subject to the Cost Accounting Standards [@CL-SUP-0109].
