## Purpose

Sets out what an acceptable purchasing system must do, how the government tests it, and what follows when material weaknesses are found.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-SUP-0101] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 252.244-7001 [@CL-SUP-0101] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-0101] |
| Applies to | Contracts carrying the Contractor Purchasing System Administration clause [@CL-SUP-0101] |

The contractor must establish and maintain an acceptable purchasing system, and failure to maintain one may result in disapproval of the system by the contracting officer and/or withholding of payments [@CL-SUP-0101].

Acceptable means compliance with the system criteria in the clause [@CL-SUP-0110].

## How it works

The system spans purchasing and subcontracting, including make-or-buy decisions, vendor selection, analysis of quoted prices, price negotiation, placing and administering orders, and expediting delivery of materials [@CL-SUP-0102].

A material weakness is a deficiency or combination of deficiencies in the internal control over information in contractor business systems such that there is a reasonable possibility that a material misstatement of such information will not be prevented, or detected and corrected, on a timely basis [@CL-SUP-0103]. A reasonable possibility exists when the likelihood of an event occurring is either probable or more than remote but less than likely [@CL-SUP-3861].

The contracting officer gives a written initial determination of any material weaknesses, and the contractor shall respond within 30 days [@CL-SUP-0106] and, if it disagrees, state its rationale in writing [@CL-SUP-0282]. The contracting officer then notifies the contractor in writing of the final determination concerning remaining material weaknesses and the adequacy of any proposed or completed corrective action [@CL-SUP-0280], and system disapproval, if the contracting officer determines that one or more material weaknesses remain [@CL-SUP-0281].

If the contractor receives the contracting officer's final determination of material weaknesses [@CL-SUP-9698], it has 45 days from receipt of the final determination either to correct the material weaknesses or to submit an acceptable corrective action plan showing milestones and actions to eliminate the weaknesses [@CL-SUP-0107].

If the contracting officer makes a final determination to disapprove the contractor's purchasing system and the contract includes the Contractor Business Systems clause at DFARS 252.242-7005, the contracting officer will withhold payments in accordance with that clause [@CL-SUP-0108].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

After award, a contractor changes its subcontract effort so that it reaches 80 percent of the total cost of the work to be performed under the contract. Its procedures produce a timely written notice to the contracting officer that identifies the revised subcontract cost and includes verification that the contractor will provide added value. The 70 percent threshold is exceeded, so the notice is triggered.

## Never confuse

An acceptable purchasing system is one that complies with the system criteria in paragraph (c) of the clause [@CL-SUP-0110]. One of the criteria is having procedures to timely notify the contracting officer in writing if the contractor changes the amount of subcontract effort after award so that it exceeds 70 percent of the total cost of the work to be performed under the contract, task order or delivery order [@CL-SUP-0104], or, as the other alternative, if any subcontractor changes the amount of lower-tier subcontractor effort after award so that it exceeds 70 percent of the total cost of the work to be performed under its subcontract [@CL-SUP-3862]. For the contractor's change, the notification must identify the revised cost of the subcontract effort and include verification that the contractor will provide added value [@CL-SUP-0105].

Where Alternate I is used, paragraphs (a) through (f) of the clause do not apply unless the contractor is subject to the Cost Accounting Standards under chapter 15 of title 41, United States Code, as implemented at 48 CFR 9903.201-1 [@CL-SUP-0109].
