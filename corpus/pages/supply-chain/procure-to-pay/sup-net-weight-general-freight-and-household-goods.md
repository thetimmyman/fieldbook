## Purpose

Shows how net weight is found, which weight certificates the contractor must attach to the invoice, and how part loads under a household goods move are weighed.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-SUP-2216] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.247-10 and FAR 52.247-11 [@CL-SUP-2216; @CL-SUP-2218] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.247-10 [@CL-SUP-0472; @CL-SUP-0473; @CL-SUP-0474] and RFO FAR 52.247-11 [@CL-SUP-0475; @CL-SUP-0476; @CL-SUP-0477; @CL-SUP-0478; @CL-SUP-0479; @CL-SUP-0480] |
| Edition date | FAR 52.247-10: APR 1984 [@CL-SUP-9820]; FAR 52.247-11: APR 1984 [@CL-SUP-9821] |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-2216; @CL-SUP-2218] |
| Applies to | The general freight clause where the weight of shipments of freight other than household goods or office furniture is not known at the time of shipment and the contractor is responsible for determining the net weight [@CL-SUP-2215]; the household goods and office furniture clause in contracts for transportation or transportation-related services when movements of Government employees' household goods or relocations of Government offices are involved [@CL-SUP-2254] |

Under the general freight clause, the net weight of the shipment is found by deducting the tare weight of the vehicle from its gross weight, and the tare weight is determined by having the empty vehicle with a full tank of fuel weighed by a certified weighmaster on a certified scale [@CL-SUP-2216]. The gross weight is determined by having the loaded vehicle with a full tank of fuel weighed by a certified weighmaster on a certified scale [@CL-SUP-2216; @CL-SUP-2253]. The contractor shall attach the original copies of the empty and loaded weight certificates to the invoice for services [@CL-SUP-2217].

Under the household goods and office furniture clause, the net weight of the shipment is found by deducting the tare weight of the vehicle, which is determined by having a certified weighmaster weigh on a certified scale the empty vehicle with all blankets, pads, chains, dollies, hand trucks and all other necessary equipment inside it [@CL-SUP-2218]. The gross weight, from which the tare weight is deducted, is determined by having a certified weighmaster weigh on a certified scale the fully loaded vehicle before arrival at destination [@CL-SUP-2218; @CL-SUP-2255]. The contractor shall attach the original copy of each weight certificate to the invoice for services [@CL-SUP-2220].

## How it works

Under both clauses, net weight is the gross weight less the tare weight [@CL-SUP-2216; @CL-SUP-2218; @CL-SUP-2255]. Under the household goods and office furniture clause, the net weight of the first part load shall be determined in the same manner as specified for a full load [@CL-SUP-2256]. The net weight of the second part load shall be determined by using as the tare weight the gross weight of the vehicle containing the first part load and deducting such weight from the new gross weight [@CL-SUP-2219]. The new gross weight is determined by having the loaded vehicle weighed again, in the same manner as specified for the full load [@CL-SUP-2219; @CL-SUP-2257]. The same procedure applies for each succeeding part load [@CL-SUP-2258].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text from February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. RFO FAR 52.247-10 keeps the general freight rule that net weight is the gross weight less the tare weight, with the tare weight determined by having the empty vehicle with a full tank of fuel weighed by a certified weighmaster on a certified scale and the gross weight determined by having the loaded vehicle with a full tank of fuel weighed by a certified weighmaster on a certified scale [@CL-SUP-0472; @CL-SUP-0473; @CL-SUP-2216; @CL-SUP-2253]. It keeps the requirement to attach the original copies of the empty and loaded weight certificates to the invoice for services [@CL-SUP-0474; @CL-SUP-2217]. RFO FAR 52.247-11 keeps the household goods and office furniture rule that the net weight is obtained by deducting the tare weight of the vehicle (determined by having a certified weighmaster weigh on a certified scale the empty vehicle with all blankets, pads, chains, dollies, hand trucks, and all other necessary equipment inside it) from the gross weight (determined by having a certified weighmaster weigh on a certified scale the fully loaded vehicle before arrival at destination) [@CL-SUP-0475; @CL-SUP-0476; @CL-SUP-2218; @CL-SUP-2255]. It keeps the part load rules: the net weight of the first part load shall be determined in the same manner as specified for a full load, the net weight of the second part load shall be determined by using as the tare weight the gross weight of the vehicle containing the first part load and deducting such weight from the new gross weight (determined by having the loaded vehicle weighed again, in the same manner as specified for the full load), and the same procedure shall apply for each succeeding part load [@CL-SUP-0477; @CL-SUP-0478; @CL-SUP-0479; @CL-SUP-2256; @CL-SUP-2219; @CL-SUP-2257; @CL-SUP-2258]. It keeps the requirement to attach the original copy of each weight certificate to the invoice for services [@CL-SUP-0480; @CL-SUP-2220].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A general freight load weighs 40,000 pounds loaded, and the empty vehicle with a full tank of fuel weighs 30,000 pounds, both weighed by a certified weighmaster on a certified scale. The net weight is 10,000 pounds, and the contractor attaches the original copies of both weight certificates to the invoice.

A household goods vehicle weighs 30,000 pounds empty with its blankets, pads, chains, dollies and hand trucks inside. With the first part load it weighs 42,000 pounds, so the first part load nets 12,000 pounds. For the second part load, 42,000 pounds becomes the tare weight. The loaded vehicle is weighed again at 50,000 pounds, so the second part load nets 8,000 pounds. The contractor attaches the original copy of each weight certificate to the invoice.

## Never confuse

Under the agreed weight clause, the shipping activity marks the weight of the shipment on the shipping document and the contractor accepts that weight for determining the appropriate rate or charge [@CL-SUP-2212], unlike under these net weight clauses, where the net weight is obtained by deducting the tare weight of the vehicle from its gross weight [@CL-SUP-2216; @CL-SUP-2218; @CL-SUP-2255]. For general freight, the tare weight is determined by having the empty vehicle with a full tank of fuel weighed by a certified weighmaster on a certified scale, and for household goods and office furniture, the tare weight is determined by having a certified weighmaster weigh on a certified scale the empty vehicle with all blankets, pads, chains, dollies, hand trucks and all other necessary equipment inside it [@CL-SUP-2216; @CL-SUP-2218]. The general freight clause requires the contractor to attach the original copies of the empty and loaded weight certificates to the invoice for services, and the household goods and office furniture clause requires the contractor to attach the original copy of each weight certificate to the invoice for services [@CL-SUP-2217; @CL-SUP-2220].
