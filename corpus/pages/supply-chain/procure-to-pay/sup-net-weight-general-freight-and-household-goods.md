## Purpose

Shows how net weight is found, which weight certificates the contractor must attach to the invoice, and how part loads under a household goods move are weighed.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-SUP-2216] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.247-10 and FAR 52.247-11 [@CL-SUP-2216; @CL-SUP-2218] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-2216; @CL-SUP-2218] |
| Applies to | The general freight clause where the weight of shipments of freight other than household goods or office furniture is not known at the time of shipment and the contractor is responsible for determining the net weight [@CL-SUP-2215]; the household goods and office furniture clause in contracts for transportation or transportation-related services when movements of Government employees' household goods or relocations of Government offices are involved [@CL-SUP-2254] |

Under the general freight clause, the net weight of the shipment is found by deducting the tare weight of the vehicle from its gross weight, and the tare weight is determined by having the empty vehicle with a full tank of fuel weighed by a certified weighmaster on a certified scale [@CL-SUP-2216]. The gross weight is determined by having the loaded vehicle with a full tank of fuel weighed by a certified weighmaster on a certified scale [@CL-SUP-2253]. The contractor must attach the original copies of the empty and loaded weight certificates to the invoice for services [@CL-SUP-2217].

Under the household goods and office furniture clause, the net weight of the shipment is found by deducting the tare weight of the vehicle, which is determined by having a certified weighmaster weigh on a certified scale the empty vehicle with all blankets, pads, chains, dollies, hand trucks and all other necessary equipment inside it [@CL-SUP-2218]. The gross weight, from which the tare weight is deducted, is determined by having a certified weighmaster weigh on a certified scale the fully loaded vehicle before arrival at destination [@CL-SUP-2255]. The contractor must attach the original copy of each weight certificate to the invoice for services [@CL-SUP-2220].

## How it works

For a full load, net weight is the gross weight less the tare weight [@CL-SUP-2216; @CL-SUP-2218; @CL-SUP-2255]. The net weight of the first part load is determined in the same manner as for a full load [@CL-SUP-2256]. For a second part load, the gross weight of the vehicle containing the first part load is the tare weight, and it is deducted from the new gross weight [@CL-SUP-2219]. The new gross weight is determined by having the loaded vehicle weighed again, in the same manner as for the full load [@CL-SUP-2257]. The same procedure applies for each succeeding part load [@CL-SUP-2258].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A general freight load weighs 40,000 pounds loaded, and the empty vehicle with a full tank of fuel weighs 30,000 pounds, both weighed by a certified weighmaster on a certified scale. The net weight is 10,000 pounds, and the contractor attaches the original copies of both weight certificates to the invoice.

A household goods vehicle weighs 30,000 pounds empty with its blankets, pads, chains, dollies and hand trucks inside. With the first part load it weighs 42,000 pounds, so the first part load nets 12,000 pounds. For the second part load, 42,000 pounds becomes the tare weight. The loaded vehicle is weighed again at 50,000 pounds, so the second part load nets 8,000 pounds. The contractor attaches the original copy of each weight certificate to the invoice.

## Never confuse

Net weight is not the agreed weight: under the agreed weight clause the shipping activity's weight on the shipping document is accepted [@CL-SUP-2212], while under these clauses the net weight is found by weighing the vehicle [@CL-SUP-2216; @CL-SUP-2218].

Tare weight differs between the two clauses: for general freight it is the empty vehicle with a full tank of fuel, and for household goods and office furniture it is the empty vehicle with all blankets, pads, chains, dollies, hand trucks and all other necessary equipment inside it [@CL-SUP-2216; @CL-SUP-2218]. The general freight clause calls for the original copies of the empty and loaded weight certificates, and the household goods clause for the original copy of each weight certificate [@CL-SUP-2217; @CL-SUP-2220].
