## Purpose

Shows how instant contract savings and collateral savings are worked out and shared on an accepted construction value engineering change proposal, including the collateral savings cap, Alternate I and the subcontract rules.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-SUP-3131] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.248-3 and its Alternate I [@CL-SUP-3131; @CL-SUP-3223] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-3131] |
| Applies to | Contracts that include the construction value engineering clause, basic or with Alternate I [@CL-SUP-3131; @CL-SUP-3223] |

Instant contract savings are the estimated reduction in Contractor cost of performance resulting from acceptance of the proposal, minus allowable Contractor development and implementation costs, including subcontractors' development and implementation costs [@CL-SUP-3133]. The contractor's development and implementation costs are those it incurs on a proposal specifically in developing, testing, preparing and submitting it, as well as those it incurs to make the contractual changes required by Government acceptance [@CL-SUP-3204]. Government costs are agency costs that result directly from developing and implementing the proposal, such as any net increases in the cost of testing, operations, maintenance and logistic support [@CL-SUP-3205]. They do not include the normal administrative costs of processing the proposal [@CL-SUP-3206].

The contractor shares in any instant contract savings realized from accepted proposals [@CL-SUP-3122]. The Government's share of savings is found by subtracting Government costs from instant contract savings and multiplying the result by 45 percent for fixed-price contracts or 75 percent for cost-reimbursement contracts [@CL-SUP-3131]. The sharing paragraph states the Government's percentage [@CL-SUP-3131]. Payment of any share due the contractor is authorized by a modification to the contract that accepts the proposal [@CL-SUP-3222]. The modification reduces the contract price or estimated cost by the amount of instant contract savings and provides the contractor's share of savings by adding the amount calculated to the contract price or fee [@CL-SUP-3132].

Collateral costs are agency costs of operation, maintenance, logistic support or Government-furnished property [@CL-SUP-3203]. Collateral savings are measurable net reductions resulting from a proposal in the agency's overall projected collateral costs, exclusive of acquisition savings, whether or not the acquisition cost changes [@CL-SUP-3134]. Under the basic clause, on acceptance the contracting officer increases the instant contract amount by 20 percent of any projected collateral savings determined to be realized in a typical year of use, after subtracting any Government costs not previously offset [@CL-SUP-3135]. The contractor's share of collateral savings will not exceed the contract's firm-fixed-price or estimated cost at the time the proposal is accepted, or $100,000, whichever is greater [@CL-SUP-3136]. The contracting officer is the sole determiner of the amount of collateral savings [@CL-SUP-3137].

Alternate I applies in a construction contract when the head of the contracting activity determines that the cost of calculating and tracking collateral savings will exceed the benefits to be derived [@CL-SUP-3223]. It deletes the collateral savings paragraph from the basic clause and redesignates the remaining paragraphs [@CL-SUP-3223].

The contractor includes an appropriate value engineering clause in any subcontract of $90,000 or more, and may include one in subcontracts of lesser value [@CL-SUP-3138]. In computing any adjustment in the contract's price under the sharing paragraph, the contractor's allowable development and implementation costs include any subcontractor's allowable such costs clearly resulting from a proposal the Government accepted under this contract [@CL-SUP-3139]. They exclude any value engineering incentive payments to a subcontractor [@CL-SUP-3230]. The contractor may choose any arrangement for subcontractor incentive payments, provided those payments do not reduce the Government's share of the savings resulting from the proposal [@CL-SUP-3140].

## How it works

Start from the contractor's estimated cost reduction, subtract allowable development and implementation costs, including subcontractors' allowable costs from a proposal accepted under this contract and without any subcontractor incentive payment, and the result is instant contract savings [@CL-SUP-3133; @CL-SUP-3139; @CL-SUP-3230]. Subtract Government costs, which leave out normal administrative processing, and apply the Government's percentage for the contract type [@CL-SUP-3205; @CL-SUP-3206; @CL-SUP-3131]. The accepting modification then cuts the price or estimated cost by the instant contract savings and adds the contractor's share [@CL-SUP-3222; @CL-SUP-3132]. Collateral savings are a separate addition under the basic clause, and Alternate I removes that paragraph [@CL-SUP-3135; @CL-SUP-3223].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A fixed-price construction contract is priced at 1,500,000. A proposal is accepted. The contractor estimates that it cuts its cost of performance by 230,000. Its own development and implementation costs are 22,000, and a subcontractor's allowable costs from the same proposal, accepted under this contract, are 8,000, so allowable costs are 30,000. A separate 5,000 incentive payment to the subcontractor is not counted. Instant contract savings are 230,000 minus 30,000, which is 200,000.

Government costs are a 20,000 net increase in testing cost. The Government's 4,000 of normal administrative cost for processing is not a Government cost. The base is 200,000 minus 20,000, which is 180,000. On a fixed-price contract the Government's share is 45 percent of 180,000, which is 81,000. The modification cuts the price by 200,000 to 1,300,000 and adds the contractor's share, the amount calculated [@CL-SUP-3132]. The clause gives a percentage only for the Government's share, so this example does not compute the contractor's share of instant contract savings or a final price. On a cost-reimbursement contract, the Government's share would be 75 percent of 180,000, which is 135,000.

The contracting officer determines projected collateral savings of 50,000 for a typical year, and assume no Government costs remain that were not previously offset. The addition is 20 percent of 50,000, which is 10,000. The cap is the greater of the 1,500,000 price at acceptance or 100,000, so the cap does not bite. On a firm-fixed-price contract of 40,000 with determined collateral savings of 600,000 and no Government costs left to subtract, 20 percent is 120,000, and the cap is the greater of 40,000 or 100,000, which is 100,000, so the contractor's share of collateral savings stops at 100,000. Under Alternate I, paragraph (g) is deleted, so neither the collateral addition nor its cap would apply.

For subcontracts, a 120,000 subcontract must carry a value engineering clause, and a 60,000 subcontract may.

## Never confuse

The percentage in the clause is the Government's share of savings, not the contractor's [@CL-SUP-3131]. Instant contract savings are also not the gross price reduction, because they are net of allowable development and implementation costs, including subcontractors' [@CL-SUP-3133]. Collateral savings are not instant contract savings: they are reductions in the agency's own collateral costs, added separately and capped [@CL-SUP-3134; @CL-SUP-3135; @CL-SUP-3136]. The value engineering clause for other contracts has its own sharing rules, which are on [value engineering sharing](../../program-finance/evms/prg-far-value-engineering-sharing.md) and [value engineering general](../../program-finance/evms/prg-far-value-engineering-general.md).

## Company calibration

Who computes the instant and collateral savings figures, how the company works out the contractor's share of instant contract savings, because the sharing paragraph states only the Government's percentage, who approves subcontract incentive arrangements, and where the accepted modifications are held.
