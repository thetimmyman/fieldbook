## Purpose

Explains the annual report a contractor files on training to prevent human trafficking under the clause for United States-flag air carriers, what the report counts, and when it is due.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-SUP-2037] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.247-69 [@CL-SUP-2037] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.247-69 [@CL-SUP-0009; @CL-SUP-0010; @CL-SUP-0011; @CL-SUP-0012; @CL-SUP-0013; @CL-SUP-0014; @CL-SUP-0015] |
| Edition date | Jan 2025 [@CL-SUP-0009] |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-2037] |
| Applies to | Contracts that include FAR 52.247-69, the clause titled as a reporting requirement for United States-flag air carriers regarding training to prevent human trafficking [@CL-SUP-2161] |

The contractor provides the annual report by October 30th, by email, to the agencies the clause names [@CL-SUP-2037]. The annual report shall include information from the preceding Government fiscal year (October 1 through September 30) regarding the items described next [@CL-SUP-2038].

## How it works

The report states the number of personnel trained in the detection and reporting of potential human trafficking, including the training required under section 44734(a)(4) of title 49, United States Code [@CL-SUP-2039]. It states the number of notifications of potential human trafficking victims received from staff or other passengers [@CL-SUP-2040]. For each such notification it states whether the contractor notified the Global Human Trafficking Hotline, another comparable hotline, or law enforcement at the relevant airport [@CL-SUP-2041]. Where the contractor made a notification, it gives the date of the notification and the method [@CL-SUP-2042]. The clause says the personnel trained should include flight attendants, ticket counter agents, gate agents and other air carrier workers whose jobs require regular interaction with passengers [@CL-SUP-2162].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text from February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. RFO FAR 52.247-69 keeps the annual report rule: the contractor provides the annual report by October 30th, via email, to the agencies the paragraph lists [@CL-SUP-0009] [@CL-SUP-2037]. The RFO annual report shall include information from the preceding Government fiscal year (October 1 through September 30) regarding the items listed in the paragraph [@CL-SUP-0010] [@CL-SUP-2038]. It keeps the number of personnel trained in the detection and reporting of potential human trafficking, including the training required under section 44734(a)(4) of title 49, United States Code [@CL-SUP-0011] [@CL-SUP-2039], and the number of notifications of potential human trafficking victims received from staff or other passengers [@CL-SUP-0012] [@CL-SUP-2040]. It keeps the statement of whether the contractor notified the Global Human Trafficking Hotline, another comparable hotline, or law enforcement at the relevant airport for each such notification [@CL-SUP-0013] [@CL-SUP-2041], and, if the contractor made a notification, the date and the method of notification [@CL-SUP-0014] [@CL-SUP-2042]. RFO paragraph (c) keeps the rule, given in accordance with two statutes the paragraph names [@CL-SUP-9002], that the personnel trained should include flight attendants, ticket counter agents, gate agents and other air carrier workers whose jobs require regular interaction with passengers [@CL-SUP-0015] [@CL-SUP-2162].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A carrier's report is due by October 30th and covers the fiscal year that ended the previous September 30, which began on October 1 a year earlier. During that year the carrier trained 250 flight attendants, ticket counter agents and gate agents and 30 other workers who regularly interact with passengers, so the report states 280 personnel trained. It received 4 notifications of potential victims from staff or other passengers, so the report states 4. For 3 of them the carrier sent a text to the Global Human Trafficking Hotline, and for each of those 3 the report gives the date of the text and the method, text to Hotline. For the fourth the report states that no notification to a hotline or law enforcement was made, and it gives no date or method.

## Never confuse

The report states whether a hotline or law enforcement was notified and, where one was, the date and method [@CL-SUP-2041; @CL-SUP-2042], unlike the training count, which covers personnel trained in the detection and reporting of potential human trafficking, including the training required under section 44734(a)(4) of title 49, United States Code [@CL-SUP-2039], where the clause says the personnel trained should include the listed categories of workers [@CL-SUP-2162].
