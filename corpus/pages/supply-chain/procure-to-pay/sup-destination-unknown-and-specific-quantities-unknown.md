## Purpose

Shows how a solicitation handles evaluation when the delivery destinations are tentative, when they are unknown and transportation cost is left out, or when the destinations are known but the quantity for each is not.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-SUP-2436; @CL-SUP-2438; @CL-SUP-2440] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.247-49, FAR 52.247-50 and FAR 52.247-62 [@CL-SUP-2436; @CL-SUP-2438; @CL-SUP-2440] |
| RFO citation | No RFO equivalent [@CL-SUP-0170; @CL-SUP-0171; @CL-SUP-0172] |

### Destination Unknown

The Destination Unknown provision is inserted in solicitations when destinations are tentative and only for the purpose of evaluating offers [@CL-SUP-2436]. For the purpose of evaluating offers and for no other purpose, the final destinations for the supplies are considered to be those that the solicitation lists after the words "as follows" [@CL-SUP-2437].

### No Evaluation of Transportation Costs

The No Evaluation of Transportation Costs provision is inserted in solicitations when exact destinations are not known and it is impractical to establish tentative or general delivery places for the purpose of evaluating transportation costs [@CL-SUP-2438]. Under it, the costs of transporting supplies to be delivered under the contract will not be an evaluation factor for award [@CL-SUP-2439].

### Specific Quantities Unknown

The Specific Quantities Unknown clause is inserted in solicitations and contracts when total requirements and the destinations to which shipments will be made are known, but the specific quantity to be shipped to each destination cannot be predetermined [@CL-SUP-2440]. The clause protects the interests of both the Government and the contractor during the course of the performance of the contract [@CL-SUP-2488].

For the purpose of evaluating f.o.b. destination offers, the Government estimates that the quantity specified will be shipped to the destinations indicated [@CL-SUP-2441]. If the quantity shipped to a destination varies from the quantity estimated, and if the variation results in a change in the transportation costs, appropriate adjustment shall be made [@CL-SUP-2442].

## How it works

The Destination Unknown provision lists the final destinations after the words "as follows" [@CL-SUP-2437], and paragraph (a) of the Specific Quantities Unknown clause has a table headed estimated quantity and destination [@CL-SUP-2489]. Under the first provision the final destinations are considered to be those listed for the purpose of evaluating offers and for no other purpose [@CL-SUP-2437]. Under the No Evaluation of Transportation Costs provision, the costs of transporting supplies to be delivered under the contract will not be an evaluation factor for award [@CL-SUP-2439]. Under the clause, the Government estimates the quantity shipped to the destinations indicated for the purpose of evaluating f.o.b. destination offers, and appropriate adjustment shall be made if the quantity shipped to each destination varies from the estimate and the variation changes the transportation costs [@CL-SUP-2441; @CL-SUP-2442].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text from February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. The RFO text prints FAR 52.247-49 as [Reserved] [@CL-SUP-0170], FAR 52.247-50 as [Reserved] [@CL-SUP-0171] and FAR 52.247-62 as [Reserved] [@CL-SUP-0172]. The codified text prints the provisions and the clause under those numbers [@CL-SUP-2436; @CL-SUP-2438; @CL-SUP-2440].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

Case one: a solicitation lists one depot as the tentative destination. Offers are evaluated as if the supplies go there, and that listing serves no other purpose. Case two: two offers are priced at 100,000 and 102,000, and the solicitation carries the No Evaluation of Transportation Costs provision. Freight of 5,000 on the first and 1,000 on the second is not evaluated, so the 100,000 offer is lower. Case three: a solicitation for 10,000 units estimates 6,000 to a first depot and 4,000 to a second, and f.o.b. destination offers are evaluated on those figures. If 7,000 units actually go to the first depot and transportation costs rise by 3,000, an appropriate adjustment is made for the change.

## Never confuse

Destination Unknown is inserted when destinations are tentative and only for the purpose of evaluating offers [@CL-SUP-2436], and the listed destinations are considered the final destinations for evaluating offers and for no other purpose [@CL-SUP-2437]. No Evaluation of Transportation Costs is inserted when exact destinations are not known and it is impractical to establish tentative or general delivery places for the purpose of evaluating transportation costs [@CL-SUP-2438], and transportation costs will not be an evaluation factor for award [@CL-SUP-2439]. Specific Quantities Unknown is inserted when total requirements and destinations are known but the specific quantity to be shipped to each destination cannot be predetermined [@CL-SUP-2440], and the Government estimates the quantity shipped to each destination indicated for evaluating f.o.b. destination offers [@CL-SUP-2441]. For how f.o.b. offers are evaluated generally, see [F.o.b. offer evaluation](sup-fob-offer-evaluation.md).

The codified FAR 52.247-49, 52.247-50 and 52.247-62 carry the three rules described above [@CL-SUP-2436; @CL-SUP-2438; @CL-SUP-2440]; the RFO text prints each of those numbers as [Reserved] [@CL-SUP-0170; @CL-SUP-0171; @CL-SUP-0172].

## Company calibration

Which of the three situations a solicitation uses and who confirms that its clause applies, because each changes how the offer is priced and evaluated. [@Q-004]
