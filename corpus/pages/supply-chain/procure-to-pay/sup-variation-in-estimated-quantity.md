## Purpose

Shows when a move away from an estimated quantity of a unit-priced item brings an equitable price adjustment, how that adjustment is measured, and how a resulting time extension is requested and decided.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-SUP-2585] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.211-18 [@CL-SUP-2585] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.211-18 [Reserved] [@CL-SUP-2827] |
| Edition date | APR 1984 [@CL-SUP-9848] |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-2585] |
| Applies to | Fixed-price construction solicitations and contracts that authorize a variation in the estimated quantity of unit-priced items [@CL-SUP-2585] |

If the quantity of a unit-priced item is an estimated quantity and the actual quantity varies more than 15 percent above or below the estimated quantity, an equitable adjustment in the contract price is made upon demand of either party [@CL-SUP-2586].

The equitable adjustment is based upon any increase or decrease in costs due solely to the variation above 115 percent or below 85 percent of the estimated quantity [@CL-SUP-2587].

If the quantity variation causes an increase in the time necessary for completion, the Contractor may request, in writing, an extension of time, to be received by the Contracting Officer within 10 days from the beginning of the delay [@CL-SUP-2589]. The request may instead be received within such further period as the Contracting Officer may grant before the date of final settlement of the contract [@CL-SUP-2590]. On receipt of a written request, the Contracting Officer ascertains the facts and makes an adjustment for extending the completion date as, in the Contracting Officer's judgement, is justified [@CL-SUP-2588].

## How it works

If the quantity of a unit-priced item is an estimated quantity and the actual quantity of the unit-priced item varies more than 15 percent above or below the estimated quantity, an equitable adjustment in the contract price is made upon demand of either party [@CL-SUP-2586]. The equitable adjustment is based upon any increase or decrease in costs due solely to the variation above 115 percent or below 85 percent of the estimated quantity [@CL-SUP-2587].

If the quantity variation is such as to cause an increase in the time necessary for completion, the Contractor may request, in writing, an extension of time, to be received by the Contracting Officer within 10 days from the beginning of the delay, or within such further period as may be granted by the Contracting Officer before the date of final settlement of the contract [@CL-SUP-2589; @CL-SUP-2590]. Upon the receipt of a written request for an extension, the Contracting Officer ascertains the facts and makes an adjustment for extending the completion date as, in the judgement of the Contracting Officer, is justified [@CL-SUP-2588].

Class Deviation 2026-O0038 directs DoD contracting officers to use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text, effective February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. RFO FAR 52.211-18 is marked [Reserved] [@CL-SUP-2827], whereas codified FAR 52.211-18 sets out the Variation in Estimated Quantity clause [@CL-SUP-2585; @CL-SUP-2586; @CL-SUP-2587; @CL-SUP-2589].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A unit-priced item is estimated at 4,000 units. If the actual quantity is 4,800, the increase is 20 percent, which is more than 15 percent, so either party may demand an equitable adjustment. The adjustment is based on the cost effect of the 200 units above 4,600, which is 115 percent of the estimate. The first 600 units of the increase carry no adjustment.

If the actual quantity is 4,600, the increase is exactly 15 percent, which is not more than 15 percent, so the clause gives no right to an adjustment. If the actual quantity is 3,000, the quantity is 75 percent of the estimate and the decrease is more than 15 percent. The adjustment is based on the cost effect of the 400 units below 3,400, which is 85 percent of the estimate.

If the extra quantity also lengthens the work, the Contractor requests an extension in writing. If the delay begins on 1 June, the Contracting Officer must receive the request within 10 days unless the Contracting Officer grants a further period before final settlement. The Contracting Officer then ascertains the facts and adjusts the completion date as is justified in the Contracting Officer's judgement.

## Never confuse

If the quantity of a unit-priced item in this contract is an estimated quantity and the actual quantity of the unit-priced item varies more than 15 percent above or below the estimated quantity, an equitable adjustment in the contract price is made upon demand of either party, based upon any increase or decrease in costs due solely to the variation above 115 percent or below 85 percent of the estimated quantity [@CL-SUP-2586; @CL-SUP-2587]. Under the Variation in Quantity clause, a variation in the quantity of any item called for by the contract will not be accepted unless the variation has been caused by conditions of loading, shipping, or packing, or allowances in manufacturing processes, and then only to the extent, if any, specified in paragraph (b) of that clause [@CL-SUP-2578].
