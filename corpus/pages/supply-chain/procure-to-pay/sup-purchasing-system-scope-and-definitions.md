## Purpose

Explains what the FAR part on subcontracting policies covers and the definitions it uses for contractors, subcontracts and approved purchasing systems.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-SUP-0500] |
| Codified citation | FAR 44.000 and FAR 44.101 [@CL-SUP-0500; @CL-SUP-0501] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-0500] |
| Applies to | Consent to subcontracts, advance notification of subcontracts, and review of contractors' purchasing systems, except prime contracts for commercial products or commercial services acquired under part 12 [@CL-SUP-0711; @CL-SUP-0500] |

The FAR part on subcontracting prescribes policies and procedures for consent to subcontracts or advance notification of subcontracts, and for review, evaluation, and approval of contractors' purchasing systems [@CL-SUP-0711].

The consent and advance notification requirements of the subpart on consent to subcontracts are not applicable to prime contracts for commercial products or commercial services acquired under part 12 of the FAR [@CL-SUP-0500]. An approved purchasing system is a contractor's purchasing system that has been reviewed and approved in accordance with that part [@CL-SUP-0501].

## How it works

A contractor purchasing system review is the complete evaluation of a contractor's purchasing of material and services, subcontracting, and subcontract management from development of the requirement through completion of subcontract performance [@CL-SUP-0502]. A subcontract is any contract, as defined in subpart 2.1 of the FAR, entered into by a subcontractor to furnish supplies or services for performance of a prime contract or a subcontract, and it includes but is not limited to purchase orders, and changes and modifications to purchase orders [@CL-SUP-0651]. A contractor can be the total contractor organization or a separate entity of it, such as an affiliate, division, or plant, that performs its own purchasing [@CL-SUP-0652]. The defense supplement defines the purchasing system and an acceptable purchasing system in the terms the clause uses, so those definitions are covered on the [Contractor purchasing system](sup-contractor-purchasing-system.md) card [@CL-SUP-0503; @CL-SUP-0504].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A company has two plants, and each plant places its own orders with suppliers. A change to a purchase order at one plant goes to a supplier. The prime contract is for commercial products acquired under part 12.

Answer: each plant that performs its own purchasing can be the contractor for the purposes of the review. The purchase order change is itself a subcontract. The consent and advance notification requirements do not apply to this prime contract.

## Never confuse

A purchasing system review is not a consent review of one subcontract. The review is the complete evaluation of the contractor's purchasing, subcontracting and subcontract management [@CL-SUP-0502], while the consent decision looks at a proposed subcontract and the contractor's notification for it [@CL-SUP-0506]. Consent is covered on the [Subcontracts: consent and advance notification](sup-subcontracts-consent-advance-notification.md) card.
