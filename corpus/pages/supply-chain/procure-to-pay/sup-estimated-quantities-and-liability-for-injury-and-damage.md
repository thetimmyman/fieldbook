## Purpose

Explains how a transportation solicitation uses estimated shipment quantities or weights to evaluate offers, and what responsibility, insurance and indemnity the contractor carries for personal injury and property damage while performing.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-SUP-2301] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.247-20 and FAR 52.247-21 [@CL-SUP-2301; @CL-SUP-2302] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.247-21 [@CL-SUP-0198; @CL-SUP-0199; @CL-SUP-0200; @CL-SUP-0210; @CL-SUP-0211; @CL-SUP-0212]. No RFO equivalent for FAR 52.247-20: the RFO text lists it as [Reserved] [@CL-SUP-0197] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-2301] |
| Applies to | The estimates provision in solicitations for transportation or transportation-related services when quantities or weights between each origin and destination are not known [@CL-SUP-2351]; the liability clause in solicitations and contracts for transportation or transportation-related services [@CL-SUP-2302] |

The estimates provision is inserted in solicitations for transportation or transportation-related services when quantities or weights of shipments between each origin and destination are not known, and it states an estimated quantity or weight for each origin and destination pair [@CL-SUP-2351]. For the purpose of evaluating offers, and for no other purpose, those estimated quantities or weights are considered the quantities or weights to be shipped between each origin and destination listed [@CL-SUP-2301]. The origins, destinations and estimates are entries in the provision's table and are specific to each solicitation [@CL-SUP-2351].

The contractor assumes responsibility for all damage or injury to persons or property occasioned through the use, maintenance and operation of its vehicles or other equipment by, or the action of, the contractor or its employees and agents [@CL-SUP-2303]. At its own expense, the contractor must maintain adequate public liability and property damage insurance during the continuance of the contract, insuring it against all claims for injury or damage [@CL-SUP-2304]. It must also maintain workers' compensation and other legally required insurance for its own employees and agents [@CL-SUP-2305].

The Government is in no event liable or responsible for damage or injury to any person or property occasioned through the use, maintenance or operation of any vehicle or other equipment by, or the action of, the contractor or its employees and agents in performing under the contract [@CL-SUP-2306]. The Government is also to be indemnified and saved harmless against claims for damage or injury occasioned in that way [@CL-SUP-2307].

## How it works

The solicitation states an estimated quantity or weight for each origin and destination pair, and evaluation treats those figures as the quantities or weights to be shipped [@CL-SUP-2351; @CL-SUP-2301]. The provision allows no other use of the estimates [@CL-SUP-2301]. For liability, the contractor answers for damage or injury arising from its own vehicles, equipment, employees and agents, insures against claims for injury or damage, and insures its own employees and agents under workers' compensation and other legally required cover [@CL-SUP-2303; @CL-SUP-2304; @CL-SUP-2305]. The Government is not liable or responsible for that damage or injury, and it is to be indemnified and saved harmless against claims for damage or injury in such cases; the quoted text does not say who indemnifies it [@CL-SUP-2306; @CL-SUP-2307].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text from February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. The RFO text lists FAR 52.247-20, the estimates provision, as [Reserved] [@CL-SUP-0197]. RFO FAR 52.247-21 keeps the liability rules described above: the contractor's responsibility for damage or injury [@CL-SUP-2303; @CL-SUP-0199], its public liability and property damage insurance [@CL-SUP-2304; @CL-SUP-0200], its workers' compensation and other legally required insurance [@CL-SUP-2305; @CL-SUP-0210], and the Government's non-liability and indemnity [@CL-SUP-2306; @CL-SUP-2307; @CL-SUP-0211; @CL-SUP-0212].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A solicitation lists two lanes with estimated weights of 40,000 pounds and 10,000 pounds. An offeror quotes 2 dollars per 100 pounds on both lanes. Evaluation applies the estimates, so the offer is scored on 50,000 pounds in total, which is 500 hundredweight, and its evaluated price is 1,000 dollars. The estimates serve evaluation only and no other purpose. This part of the example follows the codified estimates provision, not the RFO text, which lists FAR 52.247-20 as [Reserved] [@CL-SUP-0197].

Later, while performing, a contractor's truck backs into a customer's fence and the repair costs 3,000 dollars. The contractor is responsible for that damage because it arose from the use of its vehicle by its employee. The Government is not liable or responsible, and the contractor holds the insurance the clause requires, including workers' compensation for its own driver.

## Never confuse

This evaluation provision is not the contract clause on estimated weights or quantities, which the linked card covers. This provision confines the estimates to evaluating offers [@CL-SUP-2301]. Liability for personal injury and property damage from the contractor's vehicles and equipment is also a different matter from liability for loss of or damage to the freight, which the freight liability clauses cover [@CL-SUP-2303; @CL-SUP-2308].

The codified text prints an estimates provision at FAR 52.247-20 that confines the estimates to evaluating offers [@CL-SUP-2301], while the RFO text lists that number as [Reserved] [@CL-SUP-0197].

## Company calibration

Confirm which awards carry the estimates provision and the liability clause, whether the contractor's insurance meets the clause, and where the estimates stated in each solicitation are kept. [@Q-004]
