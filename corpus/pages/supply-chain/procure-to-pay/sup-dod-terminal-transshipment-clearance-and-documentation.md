## Purpose

Explains the Export Release and the movement data a contractor must obtain and mark before cargo goes to a Department of Defense air or water terminal for transshipment overseas.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-SUP-2043] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.247-52 [@CL-SUP-2043] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.247-52 [@CL-SUP-0173] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-2043] |
| Applies to | Solicitations and contracts under which shipments will be consigned to Department of Defense air or water terminal transshipment points [@CL-SUP-2172], unless clearance and documentation requirements have been expressly delegated to the contractor [@CL-SUP-2043] |

The clause is inserted in solicitations and contracts when shipments will be consigned to Department of Defense air or water terminal transshipment points [@CL-SUP-2172]. Shipments to water or air ports for transshipment to overseas destinations are subject to its requirements unless clearance and documentation requirements have been expressly delegated to the contractor [@CL-SUP-2043]. At least 10 days before shipping cargo to a water port, the contractor obtains an Export Release from the Government transportation office for each shipment weighing 10,000 pounds or more [@CL-SUP-2044]. For a water port, a release is also needed for a shipment weighing less than 10,000 pounds in these cases: classified cargo (TOP SECRET, SECRET or CONFIDENTIAL), cargo that requires exclusive use of a motor vehicle, and cargo that will occupy the full visible capacity of a railway car or motor vehicle [@CL-SUP-2045]; cargo that is less than a carload or truckload but will be tendered as one, and cargo going to an ammunition outloading port for water shipment [@CL-SUP-2163]; cargo consisting of narcotics, perishable biological material or vehicles to be offered for driveaway service [@CL-SUP-2164]; and explosives, ammunition, poisons or other dangerous articles in the classes the clause names, and radioactive material as defined in 49 CFR 173.403, class 7 [@CL-SUP-2165].

## How it works

The contractor is cautioned not to order railway cars or motor vehicles for loading until an Export Release has been received [@CL-SUP-2046]. If the contracting officer directs delivery within a shorter period than 10 days, the contractor advises the transportation office of the date on which the cargo will be ready for shipment [@CL-SUP-2166].

Separately, at least 5 days before shipping cargo to either a water port or an air port, regardless of weight, security classification or commodity description, the contractor gives the Government transportation office the information the clause lists [@CL-SUP-2047]. The clause has the contractor furnish the listed items when applying for the Export Release in paragraph (a) or when providing information for preparation of the Transportation Control and Movement Document in accordance with paragraph (d) [@CL-SUP-9699]: the proposed shipment dates, the number and type of containers, the gross weight and cube, and the number of cars or trucks involved [@CL-SUP-2167], together with the Transportation Control Numbers required for marking and, for items classified as dangerous substances, the proper shipping name specified in 46 CFR 146.05 [@CL-SUP-2168].

The contractor annotates all movement documents, Government or commercial bills of lading or other delivery documents, with the Transportation Control Number and the Consignor Code of the activity directing the shipment [@CL-SUP-2048], and with the Consignee Code and the Transportation Priority for each shipment unit [@CL-SUP-2169]. The annotation also carries the Export Release Number and valid shipping period, if stated, and if the period has expired the contractor must request a renewal [@CL-SUP-2049], and the cubic foot measurement of each shipment unit [@CL-SUP-2170]. Annotations go in the Description of Articles space, except that on Government bills of lading the Export Release number and shipping period go in the space entitled Route Order/Release No. [@CL-SUP-2171]. The contractor mails a copy of the bill of lading or other movement document to the transshipment point and gives a copy to the carrier for presentation at the transshipment point with delivery of the shipment [@CL-SUP-2050].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text from February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. RFO FAR 52.247-52 keeps the scope: all shipments to water or air ports for transshipment to overseas destinations are subject to its requirements unless clearance and documentation requirements have been expressly delegated to the Contractor [@CL-SUP-0173; @CL-SUP-2043], and the clause goes in solicitations and contracts when shipments will be consigned to DoD air or water terminal transshipment points [@CL-SUP-0174; @CL-SUP-2172]. It keeps the Export Release rule: at least 10 days before shipping cargo to a water port, for each shipment weighing 10,000 pounds or more [@CL-SUP-0175; @CL-SUP-2044], and for a lighter shipment if the cargo is classified, needs exclusive use of a motor vehicle or will occupy the full visible capacity of a railway car or motor vehicle [@CL-SUP-0176; @CL-SUP-2045], is less than a carload or truckload but will be tendered as one, or goes to an ammunition outloading port [@CL-SUP-0177; @CL-SUP-2163], consists of narcotics, perishable biological material or driveaway vehicles [@CL-SUP-0178; @CL-SUP-2164], or consists of the listed dangerous articles or radioactive material as defined in 49 CFR 173.403, class 7 [@CL-SUP-0179; @CL-SUP-2165]. It keeps the caution not to order railway cars or motor vehicles for loading until an Export Release has been received [@CL-SUP-0180; @CL-SUP-2046], the advice to the transportation office of the ready date if the Contracting Officer directs delivery within a shorter period than 10 days [@CL-SUP-0181; @CL-SUP-2166], and the information due at least 5 days before shipping cargo to either a water port or an air port, regardless of weight, security classification or commodity description [@CL-SUP-0182; @CL-SUP-2047], to permit preparation of a Transportation Control and Movement Document [@CL-SUP-9031; @CL-SUP-9032]. The information list keeps the shipment dates, containers, gross weight and cube, and number of cars or trucks [@CL-SUP-0183; @CL-SUP-0184; @CL-SUP-2167], and the Transportation Control Numbers required for marking [@CL-SUP-0185; @CL-SUP-2168]. The RFO paragraph (e)(6) says the proper shipping name for items classified as dangerous substances is the one specified in 49 CFR 172.101, where the codified paragraph names 46 CFR 146.05 [@CL-SUP-0186; @CL-SUP-2168]. It keeps the annotation of movement documents with the Transportation Control Number and the Consignor Code of the activity directing the shipment [@CL-SUP-0187; @CL-SUP-0188; @CL-SUP-2048], the Consignee Code and Transportation Priority for each shipment unit [@CL-SUP-0189; @CL-SUP-2169], the Export Release Number and valid shipping period, if stated, with a renewal request if the period has expired [@CL-SUP-0190; @CL-SUP-2049], and the cubic foot measurement of each shipment unit [@CL-SUP-0191; @CL-SUP-2170]. It keeps the Description of Articles space, except that on Government bills of lading the Export Release number and shipping period go in the space entitled Route Order/Release No. [@CL-SUP-0192; @CL-SUP-2171], and the mailing of a copy of the bill of lading or other movement document to the transshipment point and a copy to the carrier for presentation with delivery of the shipment [@CL-SUP-0193; @CL-SUP-2050].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor ships a 12,000-pound lot of ordinary parts to a water port for transshipment overseas. The lot weighs more than 10,000 pounds, so the contractor needs the Export Release at least 10 days before shipping. With a ship date of day 30, the release must be in hand by day 20, and the contractor does not order railway cars until it arrives. The movement data go to the transportation office at least 5 days before shipping, by day 25. A 3,000-pound lot of ordinary parts going to the same port needs no Export Release, assuming none of the listed cargo conditions applies, but its data are still due 5 days before shipping. A 3,000-pound lot of perishable biological material going to that port does need a release. A 12,000-pound lot going to an air port needs no Export Release, because the release rule covers water ports, but its data are still due 5 days before shipping. If the contracting officer directs delivery of the first lot within 8 days, the contractor tells the transportation office when the cargo will be ready. The bill of lading is annotated with the control number, the consignor and consignee codes, the priority, the release number and its shipping period, and the cubic measurement, and copies go to the terminal and to the carrier.

## Never confuse

An Export Release is not the movement document. The release is the clearance obtained from the Government transportation office, while the movement document is the bill of lading or delivery document that the contractor annotates and copies to the terminal [@CL-SUP-2044; @CL-SUP-2048]. The 10-day release rule covers water ports, while the 5-day information rule covers water and air ports [@CL-SUP-2044; @CL-SUP-2047].

For the proper shipping name of items classified as dangerous substances, the codified clause names 46 CFR 146.05 [@CL-SUP-2168], while the RFO text names 49 CFR 172.101 [@CL-SUP-0186].
