## Purpose

Shows what a services solicitation asks offerors to do about visiting the site, and what the contractor owes when its work damages Government buildings, equipment or vegetation.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-SUP-2911] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.237-1 and FAR 52.237-2 [@CL-SUP-2911; @CL-SUP-2914] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-2911] |
| Applies to | Solicitations and contracts for services performed on a Government installation, unless the work is construction [@CL-SUP-2911; @CL-SUP-2914] |

The site visit instrument is a provision, not a contract clause. It is inserted in solicitations for services to be performed on Government installations, unless the solicitation is for construction [@CL-SUP-2911]. Offerors or quoters are urged and expected to inspect the site where services are to be performed and to satisfy themselves about all general and local conditions that may affect the cost of contract performance, to the extent that the information is reasonably obtainable [@CL-SUP-2912]. In no event is a failure to inspect the site grounds for a claim after contract award [@CL-SUP-2913].

The protection instrument is a clause. It is inserted in solicitations and contracts for services to be performed on Government installations, unless a construction contract is contemplated [@CL-SUP-2914]. The contractor must use reasonable care to avoid damaging existing buildings, equipment and vegetation on the Government installation [@CL-SUP-2915]. If the contractor's failure to use reasonable care causes damage to that property, the contractor must replace or repair the damage at no expense to the Government, as the Contracting Officer directs [@CL-SUP-2916]. If the contractor fails or refuses to make the repair or replacement, it is liable for the cost, which may be deducted from the contract price [@CL-SUP-2917].

## How it works

The provision puts the site inspection on the offeror. It speaks of urging and expecting the inspection, and it limits what an offeror can expect to learn to information that is reasonably obtainable [@CL-SUP-2912].

An offeror who skips the inspection cannot use that omission as grounds for a claim after award [@CL-SUP-2913].

The clause sets a care standard rather than a ban: the duty is to use reasonable care to avoid damage [@CL-SUP-2915].

The repair or replacement duty arises only when the contractor's failure to use reasonable care caused the damage, and it is carried out at no expense to the Government as the Contracting Officer directs [@CL-SUP-2916].

If the contractor fails or refuses to repair or replace, the cost becomes the contractor's liability and may be deducted from the contract price [@CL-SUP-2917].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A cleaning contract is for services at a warehouse on a Government installation, and the solicitation was not for construction. An offeror priced the work without visiting the site. After award it finds the loading dock slower to reach than it assumed and asks for more money. The provision gives no ground for that claim, because failure to inspect is not grounds for a claim after award.

During performance, careless handling of a pallet jack by the contractor's crew chips a door frame. That is damage caused by a failure to use reasonable care, so the Contracting Officer directs the contractor to repair it at no expense to the Government. The contractor refuses. The repair costs 4,800, the contractor is liable for that cost, and the Government deducts 4,800 from the contract price.

## Never confuse

The site visit provision is a pre-award expectation with no post-award claim behind it, while the protection clause is a performance duty with a cost attached [@CL-SUP-2912; @CL-SUP-2913; @CL-SUP-2917]. Neither instrument is prescribed where the work is construction, so do not read a construction solicitation as carrying the site visit bar or the protection duty [@CL-SUP-2911; @CL-SUP-2914]. The protection clause applies to damage caused by a failure to use reasonable care, not to every loss on the installation [@CL-SUP-2916].
