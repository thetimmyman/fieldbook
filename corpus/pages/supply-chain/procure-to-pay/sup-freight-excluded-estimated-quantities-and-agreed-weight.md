## Purpose

Sets out what a transportation contract leaves out of scope, how little an estimated volume promises, and where the agreed weight of general freight comes from.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-SUP-2212] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.247-7, FAR 52.247-8 and FAR 52.247-9 [@CL-SUP-2213; @CL-SUP-2210; @CL-SUP-2212] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.247-8 [@CL-SUP-0380; @CL-SUP-0381]; No RFO equivalent for FAR 52.247-7 and FAR 52.247-9, which RFO part 52 lists as [Reserved] [@CL-SUP-0378; @CL-SUP-0379] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-2213; @CL-SUP-2210; @CL-SUP-2212] |
| Applies to | The freight excluded clause when any commodities or types of shipments have been identified for exclusion [@CL-SUP-2251]; the estimated weights or quantities clause when weights or quantities are estimates [@CL-SUP-2209]; the agreed weight clause when the shipping activity determines the weight of shipments of freight other than household goods or office furniture [@CL-SUP-2252] |

The freight excluded clause is inserted, substantially as printed, in solicitations and contracts for transportation or transportation-related services when any commodities or types of shipments have been identified for exclusion [@CL-SUP-2251]. Shipments that can be more advantageously or economically moved by parcel post or small package carrier are excluded from the scope of the contract [@CL-SUP-2213]. The clause's list of excluded shipments also includes shipments of unusual value, explosives and other dangerous articles, household goods, bulk commodities and commodities injurious or contaminating to other freight, and shipments the Government may elect to move in Government vehicles [@CL-SUP-2214].

The estimated weights or quantities clause is inserted in solicitations and contracts for transportation or transportation-related services when weights or quantities are estimates [@CL-SUP-2209]. An estimated weight or quantity is not a guarantee of the actual weight or quantity, because the Government does not guarantee any particular volume of traffic described in the contract [@CL-SUP-2210]. To the extent services are required as described in the contract and in accordance with its terms, orders for those services will be placed with the contractor [@CL-SUP-2211].

The agreed weight clause is inserted in solicitations and contracts for transportation or transportation-related services when the shipping activity determines the weight of shipments of freight other than household goods or office furniture [@CL-SUP-2252]. The shipping activity determines the weight of each shipment, the weight is shown on the covering shipping document, and the contractor accepts it as the agreed weight [@CL-SUP-2212].

## How it works

A shipment on the excluded list is outside the scope of the contract [@CL-SUP-2213; @CL-SUP-2214]. Because the clause is inserted substantially as printed, the list in an actual contract may differ from the printed list [@CL-SUP-2251]. For traffic that stays in scope, the estimated volume is not a guarantee, but services required as described in the contract are ordered from this contractor [@CL-SUP-2210; @CL-SUP-2211]. Where the shipping activity determines the weight, the weight on the covering shipping document is the agreed weight [@CL-SUP-2212].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text under Class Deviation 2026-O0038, effective February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. RFO FAR 52.247-8 keeps the rule that estimated weights or quantities are not a guarantee of actual weights or quantities, because the Government does not guarantee any particular volume of traffic described in the contract [@CL-SUP-0380; @CL-SUP-2210], and that, to the extent services are required as described in the contract and in accordance with its terms, orders for those services will be placed with the contractor [@CL-SUP-0381; @CL-SUP-2211]. The RFO text prints FAR 52.247-7 as [Reserved] [@CL-SUP-0378], where the codified text has the freight excluded clause [@CL-SUP-2213], and prints FAR 52.247-9 as [Reserved] [@CL-SUP-0379], where the codified text has the agreed weight clause [@CL-SUP-2212].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A solicitation estimates 500 shipments a year, and only 200 turn out to be required under the contract's terms. The estimate did not guarantee 500, and the orders for the 200 required services are placed with this contractor. A 30 pound parcel that a small package carrier can move more economically is excluded from the scope of the contract if the contract carries the freight excluded clause. For one shipment that stays in scope, the shipping activity records 12,000 pounds on the covering shipping document, and the contractor accepts 12,000 pounds as the agreed weight.

## Never confuse

The codified agreed weight clause has the shipping activity determine the weight of each shipment [@CL-SUP-2212], and the codified freight excluded clause excludes shipments that can be more advantageously or economically moved by parcel post or small package carrier [@CL-SUP-2213], but the RFO text prints FAR 52.247-9 and FAR 52.247-7 as [Reserved] [@CL-SUP-0379; @CL-SUP-0378]. An estimate that is not guaranteed is not an empty contract, because orders for required services still go to the contractor [@CL-SUP-2211]. Agreed weight is not net weight: under the agreed weight clause the shipping activity determines the weight of each shipment [@CL-SUP-2212], while the general freight net weight clause is inserted when the weight is not known at the time of shipment and the contractor is responsible for determining the net weight [@CL-SUP-2215], and it has the net weight determined by deducting the tare weight of the vehicle from the gross weight of the vehicle [@CL-SUP-2216].
