## Purpose

Explains how export offers made on different delivery bases are compared on one measure, the laid down cost to the Government at the overseas port of discharge, which costs are added, how ports of loading are chosen and nominated, and what the three alternates change.

## The rule

|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-SUP-2443; @CL-SUP-2490] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.247-51 [@CL-SUP-2443; @CL-SUP-2490] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-2443; @CL-SUP-2490] |
| Applies to | Solicitations that include the Evaluation of Export Offers provision [@CL-SUP-2490] |

The provision is prescribed by FAR 47.305-6(e) [@CL-SUP-2490]. Its paragraphs fall into three groups: the charges used in evaluation, the two delivery bases and their evaluation formulas, and the ports of loading and the price basis the offeror states. The table of ports in paragraph (d) is completed for each solicitation, while the place of delivery blank, any additional nominated ports and the price basis choices are for the offeror to complete [@CL-SUP-2500; @CL-SUP-2532; @CL-SUP-2509; @CL-SUP-2510].

### Charges used in evaluation

Paragraph (a) of the basic provision deals with port handling and ocean charges other than at Department of Defense (DOD) water terminals [@CL-SUP-2491]. It refers to port handling and ocean charges in tariffs on file with the Bureau of Domestic Regulation, the Federal Maritime Commission or other appropriate regulatory authorities as of the date of bid opening (or the closing date specified for receipt of offers) [@CL-SUP-2449]. Those charges, if they will be effective for the date of the expected initial shipment, will be used in the evaluation of offers [@CL-SUP-2492].

### F.o.b. origin with transportation under a Government bill of lading

Paragraph (b) covers f.o.b. origin offers with transportation under a Government bill of lading [@CL-SUP-2493]. Offers shall be evaluated and awards made on the basis of the lowest laid down cost to the Government at the overseas port of discharge, through methods and ports compatible with the required delivery dates and the transportation conditions known at the time of evaluation [@CL-SUP-2443]. The evaluation includes, in addition to the f.o.b. origin price of the item, the inland transportation costs from the point of origin in the United States to the port of loading, the port handling charges at the port of loading, and ocean shipping costs [@CL-SUP-2444], meaning ocean shipping costs from the United States port of loading to the overseas port of discharge [@CL-SUP-2494].

Offers are evaluated on the basis of shipment through one of the ports set forth in paragraph (d) to the overseas port of discharge, through the port that will result in the lowest cost to the Government [@CL-SUP-2496]. The Government may designate the mode of routing of shipment and may load from other than those ports specified for evaluation purposes [@CL-SUP-2495]. Ports of loading are considered destinations within the meaning of f.o.b. destination as used in the F.o.b. Origin clause of the contract [@CL-SUP-2445].

### F.o.b. port of loading with inspection and acceptance at origin

Paragraph (c) covers f.o.b. port of loading offers with inspection and acceptance at origin [@CL-SUP-2497]. Offers shall be evaluated on the basis of the lowest laid down cost to the Government at the overseas port of discharge via methods compatible with required delivery dates and conditions affecting transportation known at the time of evaluation [@CL-SUP-2498]. In that evaluation, in addition to the price to the United States port of loading, the port handling charges at the port of loading and the ocean shipping cost from the port of loading to the overseas port of discharge are included [@CL-SUP-2446].

Unless offers apply only to f.o.b. origin delivery under Government bills of lading, offerors must designate at least one of the listed ports of loading as their place of delivery [@CL-SUP-2447]. Failure to designate at least one port as the point to which the contractor will deliver may render the offer nonresponsive [@CL-SUP-2448].

### Ports of loading and the price basis

Paragraph (d) lists the terminals the Government uses in evaluating offers and, for a DOD water terminal, sets out the ocean and port handling charges for the information of offerors [@CL-SUP-2499]. The table has columns for ports or terminals of loading and for combined ocean and port handling charges to a country, with a unit of measure such as metric ton, measurement ton or cubic foot [@CL-SUP-2500].

The Government considers the ports named in paragraph (d) appropriate for the solicitation because of their compatibility with methods and facilities required to handle the cargo and types of vessels and to meet the required overseas delivery dates [@CL-SUP-2501]. Offerors may nominate additional ports of loading that the offeror considers to be more favorable to the Government [@CL-SUP-2502]. Paragraph (e) has a blank for the additional United States ports the offeror nominates, if any [@CL-SUP-2509].

The Government may disregard the nominated ports if, after considering the quantity and nature of the supplies concerned, the requisite cargo handling capability, the available sailings on United States-flag vessels and other pertinent transportation factors, it determines that use of the nominated ports is not compatible with the required overseas delivery date [@CL-SUP-2503; @CL-SUP-2504]. United States Great Lakes ports of loading may be considered in evaluating offers only for items scheduled for delivery during the ice-free or navigable period proclaimed by the authorities of the St. Lawrence Seaway [@CL-SUP-2505], and the provision gives the normal period as between April 15 and November 30 annually [@CL-SUP-2506]. All ports named, including nominated ports determined to be eligible, are considered in evaluating all offers received in order to establish the lowest laid down cost to the Government at the overseas port of discharge [@CL-SUP-2507]. All determinations are based on the availability of ocean services by United States-flag vessels only [@CL-SUP-2508].

In paragraph (f) the offeror indicates whether prices are based on paragraph (b), f.o.b. origin with transportation by Government bill of lading to a port listed in paragraph (d), or on paragraph (c), f.o.b. destination at a port listed in paragraph (d) [@CL-SUP-2510]. The choices also include paragraph (e), f.o.b. origin with transportation by Government bill of lading to a nominated port, and/or paragraph (e), f.o.b. destination at a nominated port [@CL-SUP-2511].

### Alternates

When the CONUS ports of export are DOD water terminals, Alternate I deletes paragraph (a) of the basic provision and substitutes its own paragraph (a) [@CL-SUP-2512]. Under Alternate I the port handling and ocean charges are set forth in paragraph (d) for the information of offerors and are current as of the time of issuance of the solicitation [@CL-SUP-2513]. For evaluation of offers under Alternate I, the Government uses the port handling and ocean charges made available by the Directorate of International Traffic, Military Surface Deployment and Distribution Command rate information letters [@CL-SUP-2514]. The letters used are those on file as of the date of bid opening (or the closing date specified for receipt of offers) and effective for the date of the expected initial shipment [@CL-SUP-2515].

When offers are solicited on an f.o.b. origin only basis, Alternate II deletes paragraphs (c) and (f) of the basic provision without redesignating the ensuing paragraphs [@CL-SUP-2516].

When offers are solicited on an f.o.b. destination only basis, Alternate III deletes paragraph (b) of the basic provision without redesignating the ensuing paragraphs [@CL-SUP-2517]. Alternate III also deletes subparagraph (c)(2) and paragraph (f) and substitutes new ones [@CL-SUP-2518]. Under Alternate III offerors must designate at least one of the ports of loading listed in paragraph (d) as their place of delivery, and failure to designate at least one may render the offer nonresponsive [@CL-SUP-2519]. Under Alternate III offerors indicate whether prices are based on paragraph (c), f.o.b. destination at a port listed in paragraph (d), or on paragraph (e), f.o.b. destination at a port nominated in paragraph (e) [@CL-SUP-2520].

## How it works

Every offer is reduced to one measure, the lowest laid down cost to the Government at the overseas port of discharge, so that an offer made at origin and an offer made at a port of loading can be ranked against each other [@CL-SUP-2443; @CL-SUP-2498]. An origin offer carries the inland leg, port handling and ocean costs on top of its price [@CL-SUP-2444; @CL-SUP-2494]. A port of loading offer carries port handling and ocean costs on top of its price to the port [@CL-SUP-2446]. The port and ocean figures come from tariffs on file at bid opening or the closing date for offers that are effective for the expected initial shipment, or under Alternate I for the DOD water terminals from the rate information letters on file at that date [@CL-SUP-2449; @CL-SUP-2492; @CL-SUP-2515]. Alternate II and Alternate III remove the paragraphs for the basis that the solicitation does not use [@CL-SUP-2516; @CL-SUP-2517].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

Two offers compete for one overseas port of discharge, and the solicitation uses the basic provision. The origin offer prices the item at 110,000, with 8,000 of inland freight, 2,000 of port handling at the port of loading and 14,000 of ocean shipping, for a laid down cost of 134,000. The port of loading offer prices the item delivered to the port of loading at 115,000, with 2,000 of port handling and 14,000 of ocean shipping, for a laid down cost of 131,000. The port of loading offer is lower on laid down cost even though its item price is higher, so it wins. A third offer is not limited to f.o.b. origin delivery under Government bills of lading and names none of the listed ports of loading as its place of delivery. That offer may be rendered nonresponsive.

## Never confuse

For f.o.b. origin offers, laid down cost is not the item price: the comparison starts from the item price and adds the inland leg, port handling and ocean costs, so a lower item price can still lose; for f.o.b. port of loading offers, it starts from the price to the port and adds port handling and ocean costs [@CL-SUP-2443; @CL-SUP-2444; @CL-SUP-2446]. The two delivery bases are also not the same: on the origin basis ports of loading are treated as destinations, and on the port basis the offeror itself designates the port of loading as its place of delivery [@CL-SUP-2445; @CL-SUP-2447]. For the general method of evaluating f.o.b. offers, see [F.o.b. offer evaluation](sup-fob-offer-evaluation.md).
