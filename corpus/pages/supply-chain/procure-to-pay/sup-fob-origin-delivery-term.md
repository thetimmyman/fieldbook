## Purpose

Shows where the contractor delivers under an f.o.b. origin term, what it bears, when its responsibility for loss applies and what the bill of lading must show.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-SUP-1961] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.247-29 [@CL-SUP-1961] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.247-29 [@CL-SUP-0297; @CL-SUP-0310] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-1961] |
| Applies to | Contracts containing the F.o.b. Origin clause [@CL-SUP-1961] |

F.o.b. origin means delivery, free of expense to the Government, in one of these alternative ways: on board the carrier's conveyance (or the Government's, if specified) at a designated point in the city, county and State of shipment [@CL-SUP-1961], a point from which line-haul transportation service (as distinguished from switching, local drayage or other terminal service) will begin [@CL-SUP-2061]. Another alternative is delivery to and placement on the carrier's wharf (at shipside, within reach of the ship's loading tackle, when the shipping point is within a port area having water transportation service) or the carrier's freight station [@CL-SUP-1962]. Another is delivery to a United States Postal Service facility [@CL-SUP-1962; @CL-SUP-9704]. If the solicitation says so, a further alternative is delivery to a Government designated point in the same city or commercial zone as the f.o.b. origin point specified in the contract [@CL-SUP-9704; @CL-SUP-1963].

The contractor packs and marks the shipment to the contract specifications or, without specifications, to carrier requirements to protect the goods and to ensure assessment of the lowest applicable transportation charge [@CL-SUP-1964]. It orders the carrier equipment the Government specifies when asked or, if none is specified, appropriate carrier equipment not in excess of the capacity needed [@CL-SUP-2062]. It delivers the shipment to the carrier in good order and condition and, when it loads a carload or truckload, loads, stows, trims, blocks and braces it as carrier rules require [@CL-SUP-1965].

Risk of loss under this term sits with the contractor until delivery to the carrier. The contractor answers for loss or damage occurring before delivery to the carrier and for loss or damage resulting from improper packing and marking [@CL-SUP-1966]. When the contractor loaded the shipment on or in the carrier's conveyance, it also answers for loss or damage resulting from improper loading, stowing, trimming, blocking or bracing [@CL-SUP-1967].

## How it works

The contractor completes the Government bill of lading supplied by the ordering agency or, when none is supplied, prepares a commercial bill of lading or other transportation receipt [@CL-SUP-1968]. The bill of lading shows a description of the shipment under the governing freight classification or tariff (or Government rate tender) under which lowest freight rates are applicable and the seals on the conveyance with their serial numbers or other identification [@CL-SUP-1969], the lengths and capacities of the cars or trucks ordered and furnished and the consignee delivery information [@CL-SUP-2063], and the carrier's agent's signature with the date the carrier received the shipment [@CL-SUP-2065]. It also carries any special instructions or annotations the ordering agency requests for commercial bills of lading, for example that the shipment is Government property and that freight charges paid to the carrier will be reimbursed by the Government [@CL-SUP-2064]. The contractor distributes the copies as the ordering agency directs [@CL-SUP-1970].

These duties are performed at the plant where supplies are finally inspected and accepted unless carrier shipping facilities are not available there [@CL-SUP-1971], in which case they are performed at the point in the same or nearest city where the specified carrier's facilities are available, subject to the qualifications that follow [@CL-SUP-1972; @CL-SUP-9700]. A shipping plant in Alaska or Hawaii adds a rule: supplies listed for shipment outside Alaska or Hawaii go to the port of loading in that State, as specified in the contract, at the contractor's expense [@CL-SUP-1973], and to that extent the contract is f.o.b. destination [@CL-SUP-2066]. If the plant is in Hawaii and the contract requires delivery by container service, the contractor instead delivers the supplies at its own expense to the container yard [@CL-SUP-2067], in the same or nearest city where seavan container service is available [@CL-SUP-2105].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text under Class Deviation 2026-O0038, effective February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. RFO paragraph (a) keeps the meaning of f.o.b. origin as delivery, free of expense to the Government, in one of several alternative ways, the first being on board the indicated type of conveyance of the carrier (or of the Government, if specified) at a designated point in the city, county and State from which the shipment will be made [@CL-SUP-0297; @CL-SUP-1961], a point from which line-haul transportation service (as distinguished from switching, local drayage or other terminal service) will begin [@CL-SUP-0298; @CL-SUP-2061]. It keeps, as other alternatives, delivery to and placement on the carrier's wharf (at shipside, within reach of the ship's loading tackle, when the shipping point is within a port area having water transportation service) or the carrier's freight station, or delivery to a United States Postal Service facility [@CL-SUP-0299; @CL-SUP-1962; @CL-SUP-9704], or, if the solicitation says so, delivery to a Government designated point within the same city or commercial zone as the f.o.b. origin point specified in the contract [@CL-SUP-0304; @CL-SUP-1963]. RFO FAR 52.247-29(b) keeps the contractor's duties to pack and mark the shipment to the contract specifications or, without specifications, to carrier requirements [@CL-SUP-0305; @CL-SUP-1964], to order the specified carrier equipment or, if none is specified, appropriate equipment not in excess of the capacity needed [@CL-SUP-0306; @CL-SUP-2062], and to deliver the shipment to the carrier in good order and condition and, when it loads a carload or truckload, to load, stow, trim, block and/or brace it as carrier rules and regulations require [@CL-SUP-0307; @CL-SUP-1965]. It keeps the contractor's responsibility for loss or damage occurring before delivery to the carrier or resulting from improper packing and marking [@CL-SUP-0308; @CL-SUP-1966], and for loss or damage resulting from improper loading, stowing, trimming, blocking and/or bracing if the contractor loaded the shipment on or in the carrier's conveyance [@CL-SUP-0309; @CL-SUP-1967]. It keeps the duty to complete the Government bill of lading supplied by the ordering agency or, when none is supplied, to prepare a commercial bill of lading or other transportation receipt [@CL-SUP-0310; @CL-SUP-1968], showing the freight description and the seals [@CL-SUP-0311; @CL-SUP-1969], the lengths and capacities of the cars or trucks and the consignee delivery information [@CL-SUP-0312; @CL-SUP-2063], the ordering agency's annotations [@CL-SUP-0313; @CL-SUP-2064], and the carrier's agent's signature and the date the carrier received the shipment, with the copies distributed as the ordering agency directs [@CL-SUP-0314; @CL-SUP-2065; @CL-SUP-1970]. RFO FAR 52.247-29(c) keeps the place of performance: the plant where the supplies are finally inspected and accepted, unless the facilities for shipment by carrier's equipment are not available at the contractor's plant [@CL-SUP-0315; @CL-SUP-1971], in which case the point in the same or nearest city where the specified carrier's facilities are available, subject to the qualifications that follow [@CL-SUP-0316; @CL-SUP-1972; @CL-SUP-9700]. It keeps the Alaska and Hawaii rule: a shipping plant in either State delivers the supplies listed for shipment outside Alaska or Hawaii to the port of loading in that State, as specified in the contract, at the contractor's expense [@CL-SUP-0317; @CL-SUP-1973], and to that extent the contract is f.o.b. destination [@CL-SUP-0318; @CL-SUP-2066]. RFO paragraph (c)(2) applies notwithstanding paragraph (c)(1) of the clause: if the contractor's shipping plant is in Hawaii and the contract requires delivery by container service, the contractor delivers the supplies at its own expense [@CL-SUP-0319; @CL-SUP-2067] to the container yard in the same or nearest city where seavan container service is available [@CL-SUP-0320; @CL-SUP-2105]. The RFO text calls the cross-referenced paragraph "paragraph (c)(1)" where the codified text calls it "subparagraph (c)(1)" [@CL-SUP-0319; @CL-SUP-2067].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contract is f.o.b. origin at the contractor's plant, with carrier equipment not specified by the Government. The contractor orders a truck of suitable size, packs and marks 100 crates to the contract specifications, and loads and braces them itself as carrier rules require. One crate is dropped and broken during loading, before the carrier has the shipment. That loss is the contractor's, because it happened before delivery to the carrier. After the carrier signs for the load, 5 crates are found crushed at the consignee because they were braced badly at loading. That loss is also the contractor's, because it resulted from improper loading by the contractor, which loaded them. Damage after delivery to the carrier that has nothing to do with packing, marking or loading is outside the contractor loss responsibilities in this clause. No Government bill of lading was supplied, so the contractor prepares a commercial bill of lading. It shows the shipment description under the governing tariff, the seal numbers, the length and capacity of the truck ordered and furnished, the consignee's name and delivery address, the annotation the ordering agency asked for, and the carrier's agent's signature and the date the carrier received the shipment. The contractor then sends the copies where the ordering agency directs.

## Never confuse

F.o.b. origin is not f.o.b. destination. Under f.o.b. destination the contractor pays and bears all charges to the specified point of delivery [@CL-SUP-1983], is responsible for loss before the consignee receives the shipment there [@CL-SUP-1982], and prepares and distributes commercial bills of lading [@CL-SUP-2076]. Under f.o.b. origin the contractor completes the Government bill of lading when one is supplied [@CL-SUP-1968]. The clause text states who answers for loss or damage; it does not say when title passes.

The minimum shipment terms for carload and truckload, guaranteed shipping characteristics and minimum size of shipments are covered in [F.o.b. origin minimum shipment terms: carload, guaranteed characteristics, minimum size](sup-fob-origin-minimum-shipment-terms.md).

## Company calibration

Confirm which delivery term each contract carries, who loads at each shipping point, and who supplies the Government bill of lading. [@Q-022]
