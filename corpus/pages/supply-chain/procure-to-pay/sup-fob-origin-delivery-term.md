## Purpose

Shows where the contractor delivers under an f.o.b. origin term, what it bears, when its responsibility for loss applies and what the bill of lading must show.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-SUP-1961] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.247-29 [@CL-SUP-1961] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.247-29 [@CL-SUP-0297; @CL-SUP-0310] |
| Edition date | Feb 2006 [@CL-SUP-0297] |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-1961] |
| Applies to | Contracts containing the F.o.b. Origin clause [@CL-SUP-1961] |

F.o.b. origin means delivery, free of expense to the Government, in one of these alternative ways: on board the carrier's conveyance (or the Government's, if specified) at a designated point in the city, county and State of shipment [@CL-SUP-1961], a point from which line-haul transportation service (as distinguished from switching, local drayage or other terminal service) will begin [@CL-SUP-2061]. Another alternative is delivery to and placement on the carrier's wharf (at shipside, within reach of the ship's loading tackle, when the shipping point is within a port area having water transportation service) or the carrier's freight station [@CL-SUP-1962]. Another is delivery to a United States Postal Service facility [@CL-SUP-1962; @CL-SUP-9704]. If the solicitation says so, a further alternative is delivery to a Government designated point in the same city or commercial zone as the f.o.b. origin point specified in the contract [@CL-SUP-9704; @CL-SUP-1963].

Under paragraph (b), the contractor shall pack and mark the shipment to comply with contract specifications or, in the absence of specifications, prepare it in conformance with carrier requirements to protect the goods and to ensure assessment of the lowest applicable transportation charge [@CL-SUP-3872; @CL-SUP-1964]. It shall order specified carrier equipment when requested by the Government or, if not specified, order appropriate carrier equipment not in excess of capacity to accommodate the shipment [@CL-SUP-2062]. It shall deliver the shipment in good order and condition to the carrier, and load, stow, trim, block, and/or brace a carload or truckload shipment (when loaded by the Contractor) on or in the carrier's conveyance as required by carrier rules and regulations [@CL-SUP-1965].

The contractor shall be responsible for any loss of or damage to the goods occurring before delivery to the carrier, resulting from improper packing and marking [@CL-SUP-1966], or resulting from improper loading, stowing, trimming, blocking, and/or bracing of the shipment, if loaded by the Contractor on or in the carrier's conveyance [@CL-SUP-1967].

## How it works

The contractor shall complete the Government bill of lading supplied by the ordering agency or, when a Government bill of lading is not supplied, prepare a commercial bill of lading or other transportation receipt [@CL-SUP-1968]. The bill of lading shows a description of the shipment under the governing freight classification or tariff (or Government rate tender) under which lowest freight rates are applicable and the seals on the conveyance with their serial numbers or other identification [@CL-SUP-1969], the lengths and capacities of the cars or trucks ordered and furnished and other pertinent information required to effect prompt delivery to the consignee, including name, delivery address, postal address and ZIP code of the consignee, routing, etc. [@CL-SUP-2063], and the carrier's agent's signature with the date the carrier received the shipment [@CL-SUP-2065]. It also carries any special instructions or annotations the ordering agency requests for commercial bills of lading, for example that the shipment is Government property and that freight charges paid to the carrier will be reimbursed by the Government [@CL-SUP-2064]. The contractor shall distribute the copies of the bill of lading, or other transportation receipts, as directed by the ordering agency [@CL-SUP-1970].

These Contractor responsibilities are specified for performance at the plant or plants at which the supplies are to be finally inspected and accepted, unless the facilities for shipment by carrier's equipment are not available at the Contractor's plant [@CL-SUP-1971], in which case the responsibilities shall be performed f.o.b. the point or points in the same or nearest city where the specified carrier's facilities are available, subject to the following qualifications [@CL-SUP-1972; @CL-SUP-9700]. A shipping plant in Alaska or Hawaii adds a rule: supplies listed for shipment outside Alaska or Hawaii go to the port of loading in that State, as specified in the contract, at the contractor's expense [@CL-SUP-1973], and to that extent the contract is f.o.b. destination [@CL-SUP-2066]. Notwithstanding subparagraph (c)(1), if the plant is in Hawaii and the contract requires delivery by container service, the contractor shall deliver the supplies at its own expense to the container yard [@CL-SUP-2067], in the same or nearest city where seavan container service is available [@CL-SUP-2105].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text under Class Deviation 2026-O0038, effective February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. RFO paragraph (a) defines f.o.b. origin as free of expense to the Government delivered on board the indicated type of conveyance of the carrier (or of the Government, if specified) at a designated point in the city, county, and State from which the shipment will be made [@CL-SUP-0297], and from which line-haul transportation service (as distinguished from switching, local drayage, or other terminal service) will begin [@CL-SUP-0298]; to, and placed on, the carrier's wharf (at shipside, within reach of the ship's loading tackle, when the shipping point is within a port area having water transportation service) or the carrier's freight station, or to a U.S. Postal Service facility [@CL-SUP-0299]; or, if stated in the solicitation, to any Government designated point located within the same city or commercial zone as the f.o.b. origin point specified in the contract [@CL-SUP-0304]. RFO paragraph (b) covers packing and marking [@CL-SUP-0305], carrier equipment [@CL-SUP-0306], delivery of the shipment to the carrier and loading [@CL-SUP-0307], responsibility for loss or damage [@CL-SUP-0308; @CL-SUP-0309], and the bill of lading, including completion of the Government bill of lading supplied by the ordering agency or, when none is supplied, preparation of a commercial bill of lading or other transportation receipt [@CL-SUP-0310], the freight description and seals [@CL-SUP-0311], the lengths and capacities of the cars or trucks and delivery information for the consignee [@CL-SUP-0312], the ordering agency's special instructions or annotations [@CL-SUP-0313], and the carrier's agent's signature and the date the shipment is received by the carrier, with distribution of the copies as directed by the ordering agency [@CL-SUP-0314]. Under RFO paragraph (c), the Contractor responsibilities are specified for performance at the plant or plants at which the supplies are to be finally inspected and accepted, unless the facilities for shipment by carrier's equipment are not available at the Contractor's plant [@CL-SUP-0315], in which case the responsibilities shall be performed f.o.b. the point or points in the same or nearest city where the specified carrier's facilities are available, subject to the following qualifications [@CL-SUP-0316]. If the Contractor's shipping plant is located in Alaska or Hawaii, the Contractor shall deliver the supplies listed for shipment outside Alaska or Hawaii to the port of loading in Alaska or Hawaii, respectively, as specified in the contract, at Contractor's expense [@CL-SUP-0317], and to that extent the contract shall be "f.o.b. destination" [@CL-SUP-0318]. RFO paragraph (c)(2) begins "Notwithstanding paragraph (c)(1)": if the Contractor's shipping plant is located in Hawaii and the contract requires delivery by container service, the Contractor shall deliver the supplies, at the Contractor's expense [@CL-SUP-0319], to the container yard in the same or nearest city where seavan container service is available [@CL-SUP-0320]. The codified text reads "Notwithstanding subparagraph (c)(1)" at that point [@CL-SUP-2067].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contract is f.o.b. origin at the contractor's plant, with carrier equipment not specified by the Government. The contractor orders a truck of suitable size, packs and marks 100 crates to the contract specifications, and loads and braces them itself as carrier rules require. One crate is dropped and broken during loading, before the carrier has the shipment. That loss is the contractor's, because it happened before delivery to the carrier. After the carrier signs for the load, 5 crates are found crushed at the consignee because they were braced badly at loading. That loss is also the contractor's, because it resulted from improper loading by the contractor, which loaded them. Damage after delivery to the carrier that has nothing to do with packing, marking or loading is outside the contractor loss responsibilities in this clause. No Government bill of lading was supplied, so the contractor prepares a commercial bill of lading. It shows the shipment description under the governing tariff, the seal numbers, the length and capacity of the truck ordered and furnished, the consignee's name and delivery address, the annotation the ordering agency asked for, and the carrier's agent's signature and the date the carrier received the shipment. The contractor then sends the copies where the ordering agency directs.

## Never confuse

For f.o.b. destination, under FAR 52.247-34 the contractor shall pay and bear all charges to the specified point of delivery [@CL-SUP-3870; @CL-SUP-1983], be responsible for any loss of or damage to the goods occurring before receipt of the shipment by the consignee at the delivery point specified in the contract [@CL-SUP-1982], and prepare and distribute commercial bills of lading [@CL-SUP-2076], unlike f.o.b. origin, where under FAR 52.247-29 the contractor shall complete the Government bill of lading supplied by the ordering agency or, when a Government bill of lading is not supplied, prepare a commercial bill of lading or other transportation receipt [@CL-SUP-3872; @CL-SUP-1968]. The minimum shipment terms for carload and truckload, guaranteed shipping characteristics and minimum size of shipments are covered in [F.o.b. origin minimum shipment terms: carload, guaranteed characteristics, minimum size](sup-fob-origin-minimum-shipment-terms.md).

## Company calibration

Confirm which delivery term each contract carries, who loads at each shipping point, and who supplies the Government bill of lading. [@Q-022]
