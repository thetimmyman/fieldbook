## Purpose

Contrasts how a change in rates or terms and conditions of utility service takes effect when a regulatory body regulates the service and when it does not, including the notices or requests and the rate cap in each case.

## The rule

| Codified citation | FAR 52.241-7 and 52.241-8 [@CL-SUP-2678; @CL-SUP-2684] |

| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-2678] |
| Applies to | Contracts where the clauses are inserted as prescribed, the first to the extent a regulatory body regulates the service and the second to the extent it does not [@CL-SUP-2743; @CL-SUP-2746; @CL-SUP-2678; @CL-SUP-2684] |

### Regulated services

The regulated-services clause applies to the extent services furnished under the contract are subject to regulation by a regulatory body [@CL-SUP-2678]. The contractor agrees to give written notice, to the recipient that the contract inserts, of the filing of an application for change in rates or terms and conditions of service, concurrently with the filing, and of any changes pending with the regulatory body as of the date of contract award [@CL-SUP-2679]. The notice must fully describe the proposed change [@CL-SUP-2739].

If the regulatory body approves any changes during the term of the contract, the contractor must forward a copy to the Contracting Officer within 15 days after the effective date [@CL-SUP-2680]. The contractor continues furnishing service in accordance with the amended tariff, and the Government pays for service at the higher or lower rates as of the date the rates are made effective [@CL-SUP-2681].

The contractor agrees that throughout the life of the contract the applicable published and unpublished rate schedules will not exceed the lowest cost published and unpublished rate schedules available to any other customers of the same class under similar conditions of use and service [@CL-SUP-2682].

If the regulatory body promulgates a regulation on matters other than rates that affects the contract, the contractor must immediately provide a copy to the Contracting Officer [@CL-SUP-2740]. The Government is not bound to accept any new regulation inconsistent with Federal laws or regulations [@CL-SUP-2741].

Changes to rates or terms and conditions of service are made part of the contract by contract modification unless the contract specifies otherwise, and the effective date is the effective date set by the regulatory body [@CL-SUP-2683]. Any factors not governed by the regulatory body have an effective date as agreed to by the parties [@CL-SUP-2742].

### Unregulated services

The unregulated-services clause applies to the extent services furnished under the contract are not subject to regulation by a regulatory body [@CL-SUP-2684]. After the date that the contract inserts, either party may request a change in rates or terms and conditions of service, unless the contract provides otherwise [@CL-SUP-2685]. On receipt of a written request that details and gives the reasons for the proposed changes, both parties agree to enter into negotiations concerning them [@CL-SUP-2744].

The effective date of any change is as agreed to by the parties [@CL-SUP-2686]. The contractor agrees that throughout the life of the contract the negotiated rates will not exceed the published and unpublished rates charged to any other customer of the same class under similar terms and conditions of use and service [@CL-SUP-2745].

If the parties fail to agree on a change after a reasonable period of time, the failure is a dispute under the Disputes clause [@CL-SUP-2687]. Changes to rates, terms or conditions that result from the negotiations are made part of the contract by contract modification [@CL-SUP-2688].

## How it works

The regulated-services clause applies to the extent services furnished under the contract are subject to regulation by a regulatory body, and the unregulated-services clause applies to the extent services furnished under the contract are not subject to regulation by a regulatory body [@CL-SUP-2678; @CL-SUP-2684]. For regulated service, the contractor agrees to give written notice, to the recipient the contract inserts, of the filing of an application for change in rates or terms and conditions of service concurrently with the filing and of any changes pending with the regulatory body as of the date of contract award, forwards a copy of any changes approved during the term of the contract to the Contracting Officer within 15 days after the effective date, and continues furnishing service in accordance with the amended tariff while the Government pays for service at the higher or lower rates as of the date the rates are made effective [@CL-SUP-2679; @CL-SUP-2680; @CL-SUP-2681]. For unregulated service, on receipt of a written request that details and gives the reasons for the proposed changes, both parties agree to enter into negotiations concerning them, and the effective date of any change is as agreed to by the parties [@CL-SUP-2744; @CL-SUP-2686]. For regulated service, changes to rates or terms and conditions of service are made part of the contract by contract modification unless the contract specifies otherwise, with the effective date set by the regulatory body, and for unregulated service, changes to rates, terms or conditions resulting from the negotiations are made part of the contract by contract modification [@CL-SUP-2683; @CL-SUP-2688]. Throughout the life of the contract, the applicable published and unpublished rate schedules for regulated service will not exceed the lowest cost published and unpublished rate schedules available to any other customers of the same class under similar conditions of use and service, and the negotiated rates for unregulated service will not exceed the published and unpublished rates charged to any other customer of the same class under similar terms and conditions of use and service [@CL-SUP-2682; @CL-SUP-2745].

Under the RFO text, the DoD part 52 class deviation directs DoD contracting officers to use the revised FAR part 52 in place of the codified part 52 text, effective February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. In RFO FAR 52.241-7 and 52.241-8, the regulated-services clause applies to the extent the services furnished under the contract are subject to regulation by a regulatory body [@CL-SUP-2398; @CL-SUP-2678]. The contractor agrees to give written notice, to the recipient that the contract inserts, of the filing of an application for change in rates or terms and conditions of service, concurrently with the filing, and of any changes pending with the regulatory body as of the date of contract award [@CL-SUP-2399; @CL-SUP-2679]. If, during the term of the contract, the regulatory body having jurisdiction approves any changes, the contractor must forward a copy to the Contracting Officer within 15 days after the effective date [@CL-SUP-2400; @CL-SUP-2680], and the contractor continues furnishing service under the amended tariff while the Government pays the higher or lower rates from the date the rates are made effective [@CL-SUP-2401; @CL-SUP-2681]. Changes to rates or terms and conditions are made part of the contract by contract modification unless the contract specifies otherwise, and the effective date of the change is the effective date set by the regulatory body [@CL-SUP-2402; @CL-SUP-2683]. Any factors not governed by the regulatory body have an effective date as agreed to by the parties [@CL-SUP-9187; @CL-SUP-2742]. The contractor agrees that throughout the life of the contract the applicable published and unpublished rate schedules will not exceed the lowest cost published and unpublished rate schedules available to any other customers of the same class under similar conditions of use and service [@CL-SUP-2403; @CL-SUP-2682]. The unregulated-services clause applies to the extent the services furnished under the contract are not subject to regulation by a regulatory body [@CL-SUP-2404; @CL-SUP-2684]. On receipt of a written request that details and gives the reasons for the proposed changes, both parties agree to enter into negotiations concerning them [@CL-SUP-2405; @CL-SUP-2744], and the effective date of any change shall be as agreed to by the parties [@CL-SUP-2406; @CL-SUP-2686]. The contractor agrees that throughout the life of the contract the negotiated rates will not exceed the published and unpublished rates charged to any other customer of the same class under similar terms and conditions of use and service [@CL-SUP-2407; @CL-SUP-2745]. Changes to rates, terms or conditions resulting from the negotiations are made part of the contract by contract modification [@CL-SUP-2408; @CL-SUP-2688].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

Site A is served under regulated rates. The utility files a rate application, and the contractor gives written notice to the recipient the contract names at the same time as the filing, fully describing the change. The regulatory body approves the change effective 1 July. The old rate is 0.10 per unit and the new rate is 0.12, and no cheaper schedule is available to other customers of the same class under similar conditions of use and service, so the cap on the rate schedule is met. The Government pays 0.12 per unit as of 1 July, and the contractor forwards a copy of the approved change by 16 July, which is within 15 days after the effective date. A modification records the change. The same regulatory body also issues a non-rate regulation that affects the contract. The contractor immediately gives the Contracting Officer a copy, and the Government is not bound to accept it if it is inconsistent with Federal laws or regulations.

Site B is unregulated. The contract inserts 1 January as the date after which either party may ask for a change. In March one party sends a written request with its reasons, and both parties negotiate. Other customers of the same class pay 0.115 under similar terms and conditions, so the negotiated rate may not exceed 0.115. The parties agree on 0.11 starting 1 October, and a modification records it. If they had not agreed after a reasonable period, the failure would be a dispute under the Disputes clause.

## Never confuse

Under the regulated-services clause, changes to rates or terms and conditions of service are made part of the contract by contract modification unless the contract specifies otherwise, the effective date of the change is the effective date set by the regulatory body, and any factors not governed by the regulatory body have an effective date as agreed to by the parties [@CL-SUP-2683; @CL-SUP-2742]. Under the unregulated-services clause, after the date the contract inserts, either party may request a change in rates or terms and conditions of service unless the contract provides otherwise, both parties agree to enter into negotiations on receipt of a written request that details and gives the reasons for the proposed changes, and if the parties fail to agree on a change after a reasonable period of time, the failure is a dispute under the Disputes clause [@CL-SUP-2685; @CL-SUP-2744; @CL-SUP-2687]. A change in class of service follows its own clause; see [Utility service scope, duration and change in class of service](sup-utility-scope-duration-and-class-change.md).

The electric service territory representation and the order of precedence clause are covered in [Electric service territory representation and order of precedence](sup-utility-territory-and-precedence.md).
