## Purpose

Shows what a contractor owes when it performs installation operation services with Government property that the Government furnishes as it stands, including replacement of lost property and disposal of scrap.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-SUP-3187] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.245-2 [@CL-SUP-3187] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.245-2 [@CL-SUP-0390] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-3187] |
| Applies to | Contracts that include the Government property installation operation services clause [@CL-SUP-3187] |

The Government property listed in paragraph (e) of the clause is furnished to the contractor in an "as-is, where is" condition [@CL-SUP-3187]. The Government makes no warranty regarding the suitability for use of the property specified in the contract [@CL-SUP-3099]. The contractor is to be afforded the opportunity to inspect the property as the solicitation specifies [@CL-SUP-3186]. Paragraph (e) introduces the list of property provided under the clause [@CL-SUP-3189], and the no-warranty sentence refers to the Government property specified in the contract [@CL-SUP-3099].

The Government bears no responsibility for repair or replacement of any lost Government property [@CL-SUP-3188]. If any or all of the property is lost or becomes no longer usable, the contractor is responsible for replacing it at the contractor's expense [@CL-SUP-3100]. The contractor has title to all replacement property and continues to be responsible for contract performance [@CL-SUP-3101].

Unless the contracting officer determines otherwise, the Government abandons all rights and title to unserviceable and scrap property resulting from contract performance [@CL-SUP-3102]. On notification to the contracting officer, the contractor removes that property from the Government premises and disposes of it at the contractor's expense [@CL-SUP-3103]. Except as the clause provides, Government property furnished under the contract is governed by the Government Property clause of the contract [@CL-SUP-3104].

## How it works

The property is furnished as it stands with no suitability warranty, and the contractor is afforded the opportunity to inspect it as the solicitation specifies [@CL-SUP-3187; @CL-SUP-3099; @CL-SUP-3186]. Replacement is the contractor's responsibility rather than a Government repair: the contractor bears the expense, takes title to the replacement property and keeps the performance obligation [@CL-SUP-3188; @CL-SUP-3100; @CL-SUP-3101]. Scrap and unserviceable property leaves Government ownership by default, and the contractor carries the cost of removing it and disposing of it [@CL-SUP-3102; @CL-SUP-3103].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text from February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. Section 52.245-2 of the RFO text keeps the rule that the Government property listed in paragraph (e) is furnished to the Contractor in an "as-is, where is" condition [@CL-SUP-0390; @CL-SUP-3187]. It also keeps the rules that the Government makes no warranty regarding the suitability for use of the Government property specified in the contract and that the Contractor is afforded the opportunity to inspect the Government property as specified in the solicitation [@CL-SUP-0391; @CL-SUP-3099; @CL-SUP-3186]. It keeps the rules that the Government bears no responsibility for repair or replacement of any lost Government property and that, if any or all of the property is lost or becomes no longer usable, the Contractor is responsible for replacement at Contractor expense [@CL-SUP-0392; @CL-SUP-3188; @CL-SUP-3100], and that the Contractor has title to all replacement property and continues to be responsible for contract performance [@CL-SUP-0393; @CL-SUP-3101]. It keeps the rule that, unless the Contracting Officer determines otherwise, the Government abandons all rights and title to unserviceable and scrap property resulting from contract performance [@CL-SUP-0394; @CL-SUP-3102], and that on notification to the Contracting Officer the Contractor removes it from the Government premises and disposes of it at Contractor expense [@CL-SUP-0395; @CL-SUP-3103]. It keeps the rule that, except as the clause provides, Government property furnished under the contract is governed by the Government Property clause of the contract [@CL-SUP-0396; @CL-SUP-3104], and paragraph (e) still introduces the list of Government property provided under the clause [@CL-SUP-0397; @CL-SUP-3189].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An installation operation services contract lists 4 pumps in paragraph (e), furnished as they stand. The contractor inspected them as the solicitation allowed. Two fail and are no longer usable, and replacements cost 30,000. The answer: the Government bears no responsibility for repair or replacement, and because the pumps are no longer usable the contractor is responsible for replacing them at its own expense, 30,000, holds title to the replacements and keeps performing.

During the work the contractor also produces unserviceable parts and scrap. The contracting officer makes no contrary determination. The answer: the Government abandons its rights and title to that property, and after notifying the contracting officer the contractor removes it from the premises and disposes of it at its own cost. If the contracting officer had determined otherwise, the abandonment would not apply to that property.

## Never confuse

This is not the general Government Property clause. That clause governs the Government property furnished under the contract, except as the installation operation services clause provides otherwise [@CL-SUP-3104]. The title that this clause gives the contractor is title to replacement property, and the listed property is furnished as it stands without a suitability warranty [@CL-SUP-3101; @CL-SUP-3099]. Compare [the Government property clause](../../close-revrec/fixed-assets/cls-government-property-clause.md), [responsibility and liability for Government property](../../close-revrec/fixed-assets/cls-responsibility-liability-government-property.md) and [title to Government-furnished and contractor-acquired property](../../close-revrec/fixed-assets/cls-title-to-government-furnished-and-contractor-acquired-property.md).

## Company calibration

Whether each installation operation services contract lists its Government property in paragraph (e), and who signs off on replacement purchases and on scrapping. [@Q-022]
