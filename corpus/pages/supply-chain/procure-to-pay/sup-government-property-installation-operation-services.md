## Purpose

Shows what a contractor owes when it performs installation operation services with Government property that the Government furnishes as it stands, including replacement of lost property and disposal of scrap.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-SUP-3187] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.245-2 [@CL-SUP-3187] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.245-2 [@CL-SUP-0390] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-3187] |
| Applies to | Contracts that include the Government property installation operation services clause [@CL-SUP-3187] |

The Government property listed in paragraph (e) of the clause is furnished to the contractor in an "as-is, where is" condition [@CL-SUP-3187]. The Government makes no warranty regarding the suitability for use of the property specified in the contract [@CL-SUP-3099]. The contractor shall be afforded the opportunity to inspect the Government property as specified in the solicitation [@CL-SUP-3186]. Paragraph (e) introduces the list of Government property provided under the clause [@CL-SUP-3189].

The Government bears no responsibility for repair or replacement of any lost Government property [@CL-SUP-3188]. If any or all of the property is lost or becomes no longer usable, the contractor shall be responsible for replacement of the property at the contractor's expense [@CL-SUP-3100]. The contractor shall have title to all replacement property and shall continue to be responsible for contract performance [@CL-SUP-3101].

Unless the contracting officer determines otherwise, the Government abandons all rights and title to unserviceable and scrap property resulting from contract performance [@CL-SUP-3102]. On notification to the contracting officer, the contractor shall remove that property from the Government premises and dispose of it at the contractor's expense [@CL-SUP-3103]. Except as provided in the clause, Government property furnished under the contract shall be governed by the Government Property clause of the contract [@CL-SUP-3104].

## How it works

The property listed in paragraph (e) is furnished in an "as-is, where is" condition, the Government makes no warranty regarding its suitability for use, and the contractor shall be afforded the opportunity to inspect it as specified in the solicitation [@CL-SUP-3187; @CL-SUP-3099; @CL-SUP-3186]. The Government bears no responsibility for repair or replacement of any lost Government property [@CL-SUP-3188]; if any or all of it is lost or becomes no longer usable, the contractor shall be responsible for replacement at its expense, shall have title to all replacement property and shall continue to be responsible for contract performance [@CL-SUP-3100; @CL-SUP-3101]. Unless the contracting officer determines otherwise, the Government abandons all rights and title to unserviceable and scrap property resulting from contract performance [@CL-SUP-3102], and on notification to the contracting officer the contractor shall remove it from the Government premises and dispose of it at contractor expense [@CL-SUP-3103].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text from February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. RFO FAR 52.245-2(a) provides that the Government property listed in paragraph (e) is furnished to the Contractor in an "as-is, where is" condition [@CL-SUP-0390]. It also provides that the Government makes no warranty regarding the suitability for use of the Government property specified in the contract and that the Contractor shall be afforded the opportunity to inspect the Government property as specified in the solicitation [@CL-SUP-0391]. Paragraph (b) provides that the Government bears no responsibility for repair or replacement of any lost Government property and that, if any or all of the Government property is lost or becomes no longer usable, the Contractor shall be responsible for replacement of the property at Contractor expense [@CL-SUP-0392], and that the Contractor shall have title to all replacement property and shall continue to be responsible for contract performance [@CL-SUP-0393]. Paragraph (c) provides that, unless the Contracting Officer determines otherwise, the Government abandons all rights and title to unserviceable and scrap property resulting from contract performance [@CL-SUP-0394], and that upon notification to the Contracting Officer the Contractor shall remove such property from the Government premises and dispose of it at Contractor expense [@CL-SUP-0395]. Paragraph (d) provides that, except as provided in the clause, Government property furnished under the contract shall be governed by the Government Property clause of the contract [@CL-SUP-0396], and paragraph (e) introduces the list of Government property provided under the clause [@CL-SUP-0397].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An installation operation services contract lists 4 pumps in paragraph (e), furnished as they stand. The contractor inspected them as the solicitation allowed. Two fail and are no longer usable, and replacements cost 30,000. The answer: the Government bears no responsibility for repair or replacement, and because the pumps are no longer usable the contractor is responsible for replacing them at its own expense, 30,000, holds title to the replacements and keeps performing.

During the work the contractor also produces unserviceable parts and scrap. The contracting officer makes no contrary determination. The answer: the Government abandons its rights and title to that property, and after notifying the contracting officer the contractor removes it from the premises and disposes of it at its own cost. If the contracting officer had determined otherwise, the abandonment would not apply to that property.

## Never confuse

The Government Property clause of the contract governs Government property furnished under the contract, except as provided in the installation operation services clause [@CL-SUP-3104]. Under the installation operation services clause the contractor shall have title to all replacement property [@CL-SUP-3101], and the listed property is furnished in an "as-is, where is" condition with no Government warranty regarding suitability for use [@CL-SUP-3187; @CL-SUP-3099]. Compare [the Government property clause](../../close-revrec/fixed-assets/cls-government-property-clause.md), [responsibility and liability for Government property](../../close-revrec/fixed-assets/cls-responsibility-liability-government-property.md) and [title to Government-furnished and contractor-acquired property](../../close-revrec/fixed-assets/cls-title-to-government-furnished-and-contractor-acquired-property.md).

## Company calibration

Whether each installation operation services contract lists its Government property in paragraph (e), and who signs off on replacement purchases and on scrapping. [@Q-022]
