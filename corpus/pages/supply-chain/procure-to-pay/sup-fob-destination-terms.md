## Purpose

Shows what the contractor pays under f.o.b. destination, where delivery happens and until when the contractor answers for loss.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-SUP-1978; @CL-SUP-1984] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.247-34 and 52.247-35 [@CL-SUP-1978; @CL-SUP-1984] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-1978; @CL-SUP-1984] |
| Applies to | Contracts containing the f.o.b. destination or f.o.b. destination, within consignee's premises clause [@CL-SUP-1978; @CL-SUP-1984] |

F.o.b. destination means delivery, free of expense to the Government, on board the carrier's conveyance at a specified delivery point where the consignee's facility is located [@CL-SUP-1978]. Supplies go to the consignee's wharf (if the destination is a port city and the supplies are for export), warehouse unloading platform or receiving dock, at the contractor's expense [@CL-SUP-1979]. The contractor must pay and bear all charges to the specified point of delivery [@CL-SUP-1983].

The carrier used changes the exact point and who unloads. If a rail carrier is used, supplies are delivered to the specified unloading platform of the consignee [@CL-SUP-2072]. If a motor carrier (including piggyback) is used, supplies are delivered to the truck tailgate at the unloading platform of the consignee, except when they meet the requirements of Item 568 of the National Motor Freight Classification for heavy or bulky freight [@CL-SUP-2073]. For those heavy or bulky supplies, unloading, including movement to the tailgate, is performed by the consignee, with assistance from the truck driver if requested [@CL-SUP-2074]. If the contractor uses a rail carrier or freight forwarder for less than carload shipments, it must ensure that the carrier will furnish tailgate delivery, when required, if transfer to truck is required to complete delivery to the consignee [@CL-SUP-2075].

The contractor must be responsible for loss or damage occurring before the consignee receives the shipment at the delivery point [@CL-SUP-1982]. The Government is not liable for delivery, storage, demurrage, accessorial or other charges before actual delivery (or constructive placement as defined in carrier tariffs) [@CL-SUP-1980], except for charges caused by an act or order of the Government acting in its contractual capacity [@CL-SUP-1981].

The contractor prepares and distributes commercial bills of lading [@CL-SUP-2076] and furnishes a delivery schedule and designates the mode of delivering carrier [@CL-SUP-2077]. It packs and marks the shipment to comply with contract specifications or, in the absence of specifications, prepares it in conformance with carrier requirements [@CL-SUP-2140], and delivers it in good order and condition to the point of delivery specified in the contract [@CL-SUP-2184]. The within-premises variant means delivery laid down within the doors of the consignee's premises, including specific rooms if so specified [@CL-SUP-1984], with the same responsibility for loss before receipt by the consignee [@CL-SUP-1985], the same duty to pay and bear all charges to the specified point of delivery [@CL-SUP-2078] the same duty to prepare and distribute commercial bills of lading [@CL-SUP-2079], and the same duties to pack and mark the shipment [@CL-SUP-2185], to deliver it in good order and condition to the point of delivery specified in the contract [@CL-SUP-2186], and to furnish a delivery schedule and designate the mode of delivering carrier [@CL-SUP-2187].

## How it works

Delivery happens at the consignee, not at the carrier. Under plain f.o.b. destination the delivery point is the consignee's dock or platform, or the truck tailgate at the unloading platform for a motor carrier [@CL-SUP-1979; @CL-SUP-2073]. Under the within-premises variant it is inside the consignee's doors [@CL-SUP-1984]. In both, the contractor answers for loss until the consignee receives the shipment [@CL-SUP-1982; @CL-SUP-1985], and it bears the charges to the point of delivery [@CL-SUP-1983; @CL-SUP-2078].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contract is f.o.b. destination to a consignee's receiving dock. The contractor ships 200 cartons by motor carrier, prepares and distributes the commercial bill of lading, furnishes a delivery schedule and designates the mode of delivering carrier. The carrier's freight charge is 2,000. The truck waits and 300 of demurrage builds up before actual delivery. In transit, 10 cartons are crushed. The consignee receives the shipment at the dock, with delivery made to the truck tailgate at the unloading platform. The contractor bears the 2,000 freight and the 300 demurrage, and it is responsible for the 10 crushed cartons, because they were damaged before the consignee received the shipment. If the cartons were heavy or bulky freight meeting Item 568, the consignee would do the unloading, with the driver's help if asked. If the demurrage had been caused by an act or order of the Government acting in its contractual capacity, such as a Government order holding the truck, the Government would bear that 300. Under the within-premises variant, the contractor would deliver and lay down the cartons within the doors of the consignee's premises, in a specific room if the contract says so, and the same loss rule would apply until receipt.

## Never confuse

F.o.b. destination is not f.o.b. origin. Under f.o.b. origin the contractor is responsible for loss before delivery to the carrier and for loss from improper packing and marking [@CL-SUP-1966], and from improper loading when it loaded the shipment [@CL-SUP-1967], and it delivers on board the carrier's conveyance at the shipping point [@CL-SUP-1961].
