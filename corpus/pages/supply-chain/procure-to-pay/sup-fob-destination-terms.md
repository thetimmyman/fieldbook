## Purpose

Shows what the contractor pays under f.o.b. destination, where delivery happens and until when the contractor answers for loss.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-SUP-1978; @CL-SUP-1984] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.247-34 and 52.247-35 [@CL-SUP-1978; @CL-SUP-1984] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.247-34 [@CL-SUP-0252; @CL-SUP-0253; @CL-SUP-0254; @CL-SUP-0255; @CL-SUP-0256; @CL-SUP-0257; @CL-SUP-0258; @CL-SUP-0259; @CL-SUP-0260; @CL-SUP-0261; @CL-SUP-0262; @CL-SUP-0263; @CL-SUP-0264; @CL-SUP-0265]; RFO FAR 52.247-35 [@CL-SUP-0266; @CL-SUP-0267; @CL-SUP-0268; @CL-SUP-0269; @CL-SUP-0270; @CL-SUP-0271; @CL-SUP-0272] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-1978; @CL-SUP-1984] |
| Applies to | Contracts containing the f.o.b. destination or f.o.b. destination, within consignee's premises clause [@CL-SUP-1978; @CL-SUP-1984] |

F.o.b. destination means delivery, free of expense to the Government, on board the carrier's conveyance at a specified delivery point where the consignee's facility is located [@CL-SUP-1978]. Supplies go to the consignee's wharf (if the destination is a port city and the supplies are for export), warehouse unloading platform or receiving dock, at the contractor's expense [@CL-SUP-1979]. Under paragraph (b), the contractor shall pay and bear all charges to the specified point of delivery [@CL-SUP-3870; @CL-SUP-1983].

If a rail carrier is used, supplies are delivered to the specified unloading platform of the consignee [@CL-SUP-2072]. If a motor carrier (including piggyback) is used, supplies are delivered to the truck tailgate at the unloading platform of the consignee, except when they meet the requirements of Item 568 of the National Motor Freight Classification for heavy or bulky freight [@CL-SUP-2073]. For those heavy or bulky supplies, unloading, including movement to the tailgate, is performed by the consignee, with assistance from the truck driver if requested [@CL-SUP-2074]. If the contractor uses a rail carrier or freight forwarder for less than carload shipments, it must ensure that the carrier will furnish tailgate delivery, when required, if transfer to truck is required to complete delivery to the consignee [@CL-SUP-2075].

The contractor shall be responsible for any loss of or damage to the goods occurring before receipt of the shipment by the consignee at the delivery point specified in the contract [@CL-SUP-1982]. The Government shall not be liable for any delivery, storage, demurrage, accessorial, or other charges involved before the actual delivery (or constructive placement as defined in carrier tariffs) or the supplies to the destination [@CL-SUP-1980; @CL-SUP-9060], unless such charges are caused by an act or order of the Government acting in its contractual capacity [@CL-SUP-1981].

The contractor shall prepare and distribute commercial bills of lading [@CL-SUP-2076] and furnish a delivery schedule and designate the mode of delivering carrier [@CL-SUP-2077]. It shall pack and mark the shipment to comply with contract specifications or, in the absence of specifications, prepare it in conformance with carrier requirements [@CL-SUP-2140], and deliver it in good order and condition to the point of delivery specified in the contract [@CL-SUP-2184]. The within-premises variant means delivery laid down within the doors of the consignee's premises, including specific rooms if so specified [@CL-SUP-1984], with the same responsibility for loss before receipt by the consignee [@CL-SUP-1985], the same duty to pay and bear all charges to the specified point of delivery [@CL-SUP-3871; @CL-SUP-2078], the same duty to prepare and distribute commercial bills of lading [@CL-SUP-2079], and the same duties to pack and mark the shipment [@CL-SUP-2185], to deliver it in good order and condition to the point of delivery specified in the contract [@CL-SUP-2186], and to furnish a delivery schedule and designate the mode of delivering carrier [@CL-SUP-2187].

## How it works

Under plain f.o.b. destination, supplies shall be delivered to the destination consignee's wharf, warehouse unloading platform or receiving dock [@CL-SUP-1979], or, if a motor carrier is used, to the truck tailgate at the unloading platform of the consignee, except for Item 568 supplies [@CL-SUP-2073]. Under the within-premises variant, supplies are delivered and laid down within the doors of the consignee's premises [@CL-SUP-1984]. In both clauses, the contractor shall be responsible for loss or damage before receipt of the shipment by the consignee at the delivery point specified in the contract [@CL-SUP-1982; @CL-SUP-1985], and shall pay and bear all charges to the specified point of delivery [@CL-SUP-1983; @CL-SUP-2078].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text from February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. For f.o.b. destination, RFO FAR 52.247-34 defines the term as free of expense to the Government, on board the carrier's conveyance, at a specified delivery point where the consignee's facility is located [@CL-SUP-0252], and provides that supplies shall be delivered to the destination consignee's wharf (if destination is a port city and supplies are for export), warehouse unloading platform, or receiving dock, at the expense of the Contractor [@CL-SUP-0253]. The Government shall not be liable for any delivery, storage, demurrage, accessorial, or other charges involved before the actual delivery (or "constructive placement" as defined in carrier tariffs) of the supplies to the destination [@CL-SUP-0254], unless such charges are caused by an act or order of the Government acting in its contractual capacity [@CL-SUP-0255]; the codified sentence reads "or the supplies to the destination" at that point [@CL-SUP-9060]. If rail carrier is used, supplies shall be delivered to the specified unloading platform of the consignee [@CL-SUP-0256]. If motor carrier (including "piggyback") is used, supplies shall be delivered to truck tailgate at the unloading platform of the consignee, except when the supplies delivered meet the requirements of Item 568 of the National Motor Freight Classification for "heavy or bulky freight" [@CL-SUP-0257]. Unloading of supplies meeting Item 568 (including movement to the tailgate) shall be performed by the consignee, with assistance from the truck driver, if requested [@CL-SUP-0258]. If the contractor uses rail carrier or freight forwarder for less than carload shipments, the contractor shall ensure that the carrier will furnish tailgate delivery, when required, if transfer to truck is required to complete delivery to consignee [@CL-SUP-0259]. Paragraph (b) of RFO FAR 52.247-34 covers packing and marking [@CL-SUP-0260], commercial bills of lading [@CL-SUP-0261], delivery to the point of delivery specified in the contract [@CL-SUP-0262], responsibility for loss or damage before receipt by the consignee at the delivery point specified in the contract [@CL-SUP-0263], a delivery schedule and designation of the mode of delivering carrier [@CL-SUP-0264], and payment of all charges to the specified point of delivery [@CL-SUP-0265]. For f.o.b. destination, within consignee's premises, RFO FAR 52.247-35 defines the term as free of expense to the Government delivered and laid down within the doors of the consignee's premises, including delivery to specific rooms within a building if so specified [@CL-SUP-0266]. Its paragraph (b) covers packing and marking [@CL-SUP-0267], commercial bills of lading [@CL-SUP-0268], delivery to the point of delivery specified in the contract [@CL-SUP-0269], responsibility for loss or damage before receipt by the consignee [@CL-SUP-0270], a delivery schedule and designation of the mode of delivering carrier [@CL-SUP-0271], and payment of all charges to the specified point of delivery [@CL-SUP-0272].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contract is f.o.b. destination to a consignee's receiving dock. The contractor ships 200 cartons by motor carrier, prepares and distributes the commercial bill of lading, furnishes a delivery schedule and designates the mode of delivering carrier. The carrier's freight charge is 2,000. The truck waits and 300 of demurrage builds up before actual delivery. In transit, 10 cartons are crushed. The consignee receives the shipment at the dock, with delivery made to the truck tailgate at the unloading platform. The contractor bears the 2,000 freight and the 300 demurrage, and it is responsible for the 10 crushed cartons, because they were damaged before the consignee received the shipment. If the cartons were heavy or bulky freight meeting Item 568, the consignee would do the unloading, with the driver's help if asked. If the demurrage had been caused by an act or order of the Government acting in its contractual capacity, such as a Government order holding the truck, the Government would bear that 300. Under the within-premises variant, the contractor would deliver and lay down the cartons within the doors of the consignee's premises, in a specific room if the contract says so, and the same loss rule would apply until receipt.

## Never confuse

F.o.b. destination means, in part, free of expense to the Government, on board the carrier's conveyance, at a specified delivery point where the consignee's facility is located [@CL-SUP-1978], unlike f.o.b. destination, within consignee's premises, which means free of expense to the Government delivered and laid down within the doors of the consignee's premises, including delivery to specific rooms within a building if so specified [@CL-SUP-1984]. The f.o.b. origin term has its own page, [F.o.b. origin delivery term](sup-fob-origin-delivery-term.md).
