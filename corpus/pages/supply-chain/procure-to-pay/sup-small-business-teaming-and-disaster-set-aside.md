## Purpose

Shows who may offer under the small business teaming provision, what makes a Small Business Teaming Arrangement, and who may offer under a disaster or emergency area set-aside.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-SUP-2956] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.207-6 and FAR 52.226-4 [@CL-SUP-2956; @CL-SUP-2960] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.207-6 [@CL-SUP-0544; @CL-SUP-0496; @CL-SUP-0497; @CL-SUP-0498; @CL-SUP-0499; @CL-SUP-0540; @CL-SUP-0541; @CL-SUP-0542; @CL-SUP-0543] and RFO FAR 52.226-4 [@CL-SUP-0545; @CL-SUP-0546] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-2956] |
| Applies to | Solicitations that carry the small business teaming provision, which is titled for multiple-award contracts, and contracts that carry the disaster or emergency area set-aside clause [@CL-SUP-2974; @CL-SUP-2960] |

The teaming provision defines a Small Business Teaming Arrangement as an arrangement where two or more small business concerns have formed a joint venture, or where a small business offeror agrees with one or more other small business concerns to have them act as its subcontractors under a specified Government contract [@CL-SUP-2956]. Its title marks it as a provision for multiple-award contracts [@CL-SUP-2974].

An arrangement between the offeror and its small business subcontractors exists through a written agreement between the parties [@CL-SUP-3004]. That agreement is specifically referred to as a Small Business Teaming Arrangement and sets forth the different responsibilities, roles and percentages or other allocations of work as it relates to the acquisition [@CL-SUP-2957].

The definition then adds the mentor-protégé cases. For civilian agencies, an arrangement may include two business concerns in a mentor-protégé relationship when both the mentor and the protégé are small, or the protégé is small and the concerns have received an exception to affiliation under 13 CFR 121.103(h)(3)(ii) or (iii) [@CL-SUP-2971]. For DoD, it may include two business concerns in a mentor-protégé relationship in the Department of Defense Mentor-Protege Program when both are small, and under that program there is no exception to joint venture size affiliation for offers received from teaming arrangements [@CL-SUP-2972; @CL-SUP-2958]. The provision also refers the reader to 13 CFR 121.103(b)(9) for the exception to affiliation for offers received from Small Business Teaming Arrangements in a solicitation for a bundled contract with a reserve [@CL-SUP-2973].

The Government is soliciting and will consider offers from any responsible source, including responsible small business concerns and offers from Small Business Teaming Arrangements or joint ventures of small business concerns [@CL-SUP-2959].

The disaster or emergency area clause solicits offers only from businesses residing or primarily doing business in an area that the Contracting Officer fills in with definite geographic boundaries, and offers received from other businesses are not considered [@CL-SUP-2960]. This set-aside is in addition to any small business set-aside contained in the contract [@CL-SUP-2975].

## How it works

Under the teaming provision, an arrangement between the offeror and its small business subcontractors exists through a written agreement between the parties that is specifically referred to as a Small Business Teaming Arrangement and sets forth the different responsibilities, roles, and percentages (or other allocations) of work as it relates to the acquisition [@CL-SUP-3004; @CL-SUP-2957]. A Small Business Teaming Arrangement also covers an arrangement where two or more small business concerns have formed a joint venture, as well as an arrangement where a small business offeror agrees with one or more other small business concerns to have them act as its subcontractors under a specified Government contract [@CL-SUP-2956; @CL-SUP-3004].

For civilian agencies, a Small Business Teaming Arrangement may include two business concerns in a mentor-protégé relationship when both the mentor and the protégé are small or the protégé is small and the concerns have received an exception to affiliation pursuant to 13 CFR 121.103(h)(3)(ii) or (iii) [@CL-SUP-2971]. For DoD, a Small Business Teaming Arrangement may include two business concerns in a mentor-protégé relationship in the Department of Defense Mentor-Protege Program (see section 4902 of title 10 of the United States Code) when both the mentor and the protégé are small, and there is no exception to joint venture size affiliation for offers received from teaming arrangements under the Department of Defense Mentor-Protege Program [@CL-SUP-2972; @CL-SUP-2958].

The Government is soliciting and will consider offers from any responsible source, including responsible small business concerns and offers from Small Business Teaming Arrangements or joint ventures of small business concerns [@CL-SUP-2959].

Under the disaster or emergency area set-aside clause, offers are solicited only from businesses residing or primarily doing business in the area identified by the Contracting Officer with definite geographic boundaries, and offers received from other businesses shall not be considered [@CL-SUP-2960]. This set-aside is in addition to any small business set-aside contained in the contract [@CL-SUP-2975].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text from February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. RFO FAR 52.207-6 keeps the provision's title for multiple-award contracts [@CL-SUP-0544; @CL-SUP-2974] and the definition of a Small Business Teaming Arrangement as a joint venture of two or more small business concerns or an agreement under which a small business offeror has other small business concerns act as its subcontractors under a specified Government contract [@CL-SUP-0496; @CL-SUP-2956]. It keeps the rule that an arrangement between the offeror and its small business subcontractors exists through a written agreement [@CL-SUP-0497; @CL-SUP-3004] that is specifically referred to as a Small Business Teaming Arrangement and sets forth the different responsibilities, roles and percentages or other allocations of work [@CL-SUP-0498; @CL-SUP-2957]. It keeps the civilian agency mentor-protégé case [@CL-SUP-0499; @CL-SUP-2971], the DoD mentor-protégé case [@CL-SUP-0540; @CL-SUP-2972] and the statement that there is no exception to joint venture size affiliation under the DoD program [@CL-SUP-0541; @CL-SUP-2958]. It keeps the reference to 13 CFR 121.103(b)(9) for bundled contracts with a reserve [@CL-SUP-0542; @CL-SUP-2973] and the statement that the Government will consider offers from any responsible source, including Small Business Teaming Arrangements and joint ventures of small business concerns [@CL-SUP-0543; @CL-SUP-2959]. RFO FAR 52.226-4 keeps the limit that offers are solicited only from businesses residing or primarily doing business in the area the Contracting Officer fills in, and offers from other businesses are not considered [@CL-SUP-0545; @CL-SUP-2960]. It keeps the rule that the set-aside is in addition to any small business set-aside in the contract [@CL-SUP-0546; @CL-SUP-2975].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A small business offeror agrees with one other small business concern that the other will act as its subcontractor under a specified contract. They sign a written agreement titled Small Business Teaming Arrangement that names each firm's responsibilities and roles and a 60/40 split of the work. The Government considers their offer on the multiple-award solicitation, because it will consider offers from any responsible source, including Small Business Teaming Arrangements.

A separate solicitation carries the disaster or emergency area set-aside, with the area filled in by the Contracting Officer, and also carries a small business set-aside. It draws three offers from businesses in the area and one from outside the area. The outside offer is not considered, and the three local offers are still subject to the small business set-aside.
