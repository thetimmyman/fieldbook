## Purpose

Shows how long a debarment usually runs, how it may be extended or reduced, and how improper conduct is imputed among individuals, the contractor and joint venture participants. It also shows that the scope of suspension follows the scope of debarment.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-SUP-0780] |
| Codified citation | FAR 9.406-4, FAR 9.406-5 and FAR 9.407-5 [@CL-SUP-0780; @CL-SUP-0784; @CL-SUP-4152] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-0780] |
| Applies to | Debarment actions, and the individuals and joint venture participants associated with a debarred contractor [@CL-SUP-0780; @CL-SUP-0784; @CL-SUP-0786] |

Debarment shall be for a period commensurate with the seriousness of the cause(s), and generally should not exceed 3 years, except for the listed exceptions [@CL-SUP-0780]. Debarment for violation of the Drug-Free Workplace provisions of chapter 81 of title 41 of the United States Code may be for a period not to exceed 5 years [@CL-SUP-0801]. Debarments under paragraph (b)(2) of the section on causes for debarment shall be for 1 year unless extended under the extension paragraph of the section on the period of debarment [@CL-SUP-0802]. Debarments under paragraph (b)(1)(vii) of the section on causes for debarment shall be for a period of not less than 2 years, inclusive of any suspension period, if suspension precedes a debarment [@CL-SUP-0803]. If suspension precedes a debarment, the suspension period shall be considered in determining the debarment period [@CL-SUP-0804]. The suspending and debarring official may extend the debarment for an additional period if that official determines that an extension is necessary to protect the Government's interest [@CL-SUP-0781], but a debarment may not be extended solely on the basis of the facts and circumstances upon which the initial debarment action was based [@CL-SUP-0782].

The official may reduce the period or extent of debarment, upon the contractor's request supported by documentation, for reasons such as those listed in the FAR [@CL-SUP-0783].

## How it works

Imputation runs in three directions. Fraudulent, criminal, or other seriously improper conduct of any officer, director, shareholder, partner, employee, or other individual associated with a contractor may be imputed to the contractor [@CL-SUP-0806]. That conduct may be imputed when it occurred in connection with the individual's performance of duties for or on behalf of the contractor, or with the contractor's knowledge, approval, or acquiescence [@CL-SUP-0784]. Fraudulent, criminal, or other seriously improper conduct of a contractor may be imputed to any officer, director, shareholder, partner, employee, or other individual associated with the contractor who participated in, knew of, or had reason to know of the contractor's conduct [@CL-SUP-0785]. Fraudulent, criminal, or other seriously improper conduct of one contractor participating in a joint venture or similar arrangement may be imputed to other participating contractors if the conduct occurred for or on behalf of the joint venture or similar arrangement [@CL-SUP-0807], or with the knowledge, approval, or acquiescence of these contractors [@CL-SUP-0786].

Suspension follows the same scope. The scope of suspension shall be the same as that for debarment (see 9.406-5), except that the procedures of 9.407-3 shall be used in imposing suspension [@CL-SUP-4152].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor is debarred for a cause with no special period rule, and the official sets a period of 2 years. During the period the contractor asks for a reduction and files documents showing newly discovered material evidence. Separately, an employee of one joint venture partner commits fraud for the joint venture.

Answer: the official may reduce the period or extent of the debarment on that request. The employee's conduct may be imputed to the other partner if it occurred for or on behalf of the joint venture, or with that partner's knowledge, approval, or acquiescence.

## Never confuse

An extension is not automatic. It needs a determination that it is necessary to protect the Government's interest, and the same facts alone cannot support it [@CL-SUP-0781; @CL-SUP-0782]. Imputation is also not automatic: each direction turns on its stated conditions [@CL-SUP-0784; @CL-SUP-0785; @CL-SUP-0786]. The causes themselves are on the [Causes for debarment](sup-causes-for-debarment.md) card, and the effect of listing is on the [SAM exclusion records and the effect of listing](sup-sam-exclusion-records-and-effect-of-listing.md) card.
