## Purpose

Shows which photovoltaic devices must be domestic, U.S.-made or qualifying, the price exception for foreign devices, and what the offeror certifies.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-SUP-1466; @CL-SUP-1469] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 252.225-7017 [@CL-SUP-1466] and DFARS 252.225-7018 [@CL-SUP-1469] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-1466] |
| Applies to | Contracts with the clause, and offers with the certificate provision, by estimated value of the photovoltaic devices [@CL-SUP-1466; @CL-SUP-1469] |

The clause keys each restriction to the estimated value of the photovoltaic devices that the Contractor specified in its offer in the certificate provision, and then lists value bands [@CL-SUP-1466]. Above the micro-purchase threshold but below $100,000, the Contractor shall utilize only domestic photovoltaic devices unless its offer specified qualifying country or other foreign photovoltaic devices in paragraph (d)(2) of the certificate provision [@CL-SUP-1467; @CL-SUP-1466]. At $100,000 or more but less than $102,280, the Contractor shall utilize under the contract only domestic photovoltaic devices, unless in its offer it specified utilization of the kinds that the clause goes on to list [@CL-SUP-1511]. The exception in that band names these alternatives: Free Trade Agreement country photovoltaic devices (other than Bahraini, Korean, Moroccan, Panamanian, or Peruvian photovoltaic devices), qualifying country photovoltaic devices, or other foreign photovoltaic devices, in paragraph (d)(4) of the certificate provision [@CL-SUP-1512]. At $100,000 or more but less than $174,000, the Contractor shall utilize under the contract only domestic photovoltaic devices, unless in its offer it specified utilization of the kinds that the clause goes on to list [@CL-SUP-1513]. The exception in that band names these alternatives: Free Trade Agreement country photovoltaic devices (other than Bahraini, Moroccan, Panamanian, or Peruvian photovoltaic devices), qualifying country photovoltaic devices, or other foreign photovoltaic devices, in paragraph (d)(5) of the certificate provision [@CL-SUP-1514]. If the Contractor certified in its offer that it will utilize a Free Trade Agreement country photovoltaic device (other than a Bahraini, Moroccan, Panamanian, or Peruvian photovoltaic device) or a qualifying country photovoltaic device, the clause then states what the Contractor shall utilize [@CL-SUP-1515]. A conditional sentence of the restriction paragraph provides that the Contractor shall utilize a Free Trade Agreement country photovoltaic device (other than a Bahraini, Moroccan, Panamanian, or Peruvian photovoltaic device) or a qualifying country photovoltaic device or, at the Contractor's option, a domestic photovoltaic device [@CL-SUP-1516]. At $174,000 or more, the Contractor shall utilize under the contract only U.S.-made, designated country, or qualifying country photovoltaic devices [@CL-SUP-1468]. As printed, the band of $100,000 or more but less than $102,280 [@CL-SUP-1511] and the band of $100,000 or more but less than $174,000 [@CL-SUP-1513] both begin at $100,000, so values from $100,000 up to $102,280 fall in both bands, and the printed text gives no rule for choosing between them. The two bands also differ in their lists: Korean photovoltaic devices are excluded from the Free Trade Agreement kinds in the first band and are not excluded in the second [@CL-SUP-1512; @CL-SUP-1514]. The source text does not establish the dollar amount of the micro-purchase threshold.

The certificate provision says its restrictions apply depending on the estimated aggregate value of photovoltaic devices to be utilized under a resultant contract [@CL-SUP-1469]. At $174,000 or more, the Government will consider only offers that utilize U.S.-made, qualifying country, or designated country photovoltaic devices [@CL-SUP-1470]. Above the micro-purchase threshold but below $174,000, the Government will not accept an offer specifying the use of other foreign photovoltaic devices in the listed paragraphs of the same provision [@CL-SUP-1471], and that restriction does not hold where the Offeror documents to the satisfaction of the Contracting Officer that the price of the foreign photovoltaic device plus 50 percent is less than the price of a comparable domestic photovoltaic device [@CL-SUP-1472]. One listed certification paragraph says that, at $174,000 or more, the Offeror certifies that each photovoltaic device to be used in performance of the contract is a U.S.-made, designated country, or qualifying country photovoltaic device [@CL-SUP-1473].

## How it works

The clause and the certificate both turn on the estimated value of the photovoltaic devices: the clause on the value the Contractor specified in its offer [@CL-SUP-1466], and the certificate on the estimated aggregate value [@CL-SUP-1469]. At the top band both texts allow only U.S.-made, designated country and qualifying country devices [@CL-SUP-1468; @CL-SUP-1470]. Below $174,000 and above the micro-purchase threshold, the certificate provision says the Government will not accept an offer specifying other foreign devices in the listed paragraphs, and that restriction does not hold where the price test is met and documented [@CL-SUP-1471; @CL-SUP-1472]. The restriction paragraph of the clause prints two middle bands that overlap, as the rule above sets out [@CL-SUP-1511; @CL-SUP-1513].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An offer states that the estimated value of the photovoltaic devices is 300,000. That is at or above the clause's top band value of 174,000, so only U.S.-made, designated country or qualifying country devices are allowed, and the offeror certifies one of those.

A second offer states an estimated value of 150,000, which is above the micro-purchase threshold and below 174,000. It proposes other foreign devices that cost 120 against a comparable domestic device at 190. The foreign price plus 50 percent is 180, which is less than 190, so the price test is met if the offeror documents it to the satisfaction of the Contracting Officer.

## Never confuse

The lower bands of the restriction paragraph name domestic photovoltaic devices, and the top band names U.S.-made, designated country or qualifying country devices [@CL-SUP-1467; @CL-SUP-1468]. This clause covers photovoltaic devices and is not the DoD Buy American clause, which covers end products; see [DoD Buy American definitions and delivery rule](sup-dfars-buy-american-definitions-and-delivery-rule.md). The trade agreements clause also covers end products, as covered on [Trade agreements clause and certificate](sup-dfars-trade-agreements-clause-and-certificate.md). The qualifying country sources clause is a separate subcontracting rule, covered on [Qualifying country subcontractors and reports of performance outside the United States and Canada](sup-dfars-qualifying-country-subcontractors-and-performance-reports.md).
