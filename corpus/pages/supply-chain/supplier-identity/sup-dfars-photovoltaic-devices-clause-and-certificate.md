## Purpose

Shows which photovoltaic devices must be domestic, U.S.-made or qualifying, the price exception for foreign devices, and what the offeror certifies.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-SUP-1466; @CL-SUP-1469] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 252.225-7017 [@CL-SUP-1466] and DFARS 252.225-7018 [@CL-SUP-1469] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | DFARS 252.225-7017: MAR 2024 [@CL-SUP-9935]; DFARS 252.225-7018: MAR 2024 [@CL-SUP-9936] |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-1466] |
| Applies to | Contracts with the clause, and offers with the certificate provision, by estimated value of the photovoltaic devices [@CL-SUP-1466; @CL-SUP-1469] |

The clause keys each restriction to the estimated value of the photovoltaic devices that the Contractor specified in its offer in the certificate provision, and then lists value bands [@CL-SUP-1466]. Above the micro-purchase threshold but below $100,000, the Contractor shall utilize only domestic photovoltaic devices unless its offer specified qualifying country or other foreign photovoltaic devices in paragraph (d)(2) of the certificate provision [@CL-SUP-1467; @CL-SUP-1466]. At $100,000 or more but less than $102,280, the Contractor shall utilize under the contract only domestic photovoltaic devices, unless, in its offer, it specified utilization of one of these alternatives: Free Trade Agreement country photovoltaic devices (other than Bahraini, Korean, Moroccan, Panamanian, or Peruvian photovoltaic devices), qualifying country photovoltaic devices, or other foreign photovoltaic devices in paragraph (d)(4) of the certificate provision [@CL-SUP-1511; @CL-SUP-1512]. At $100,000 or more but less than $174,000, the Contractor shall utilize under the contract only domestic photovoltaic devices, unless, in its offer, it specified utilization of one of these alternatives: Free Trade Agreement country photovoltaic devices (other than Bahraini, Moroccan, Panamanian, or Peruvian photovoltaic devices), qualifying country photovoltaic devices, or other foreign photovoltaic devices in paragraph (d)(5) of the certificate provision [@CL-SUP-1513; @CL-SUP-1514]. In that band, if the Contractor certified in its offer that it will utilize a Free Trade Agreement country photovoltaic device (other than a Bahraini, Moroccan, Panamanian, or Peruvian photovoltaic device) or a qualifying country photovoltaic device, then the Contractor shall utilize a Free Trade Agreement country photovoltaic device (other than a Bahraini, Moroccan, Panamanian, or Peruvian photovoltaic device) or a qualifying country photovoltaic device; or, at the Contractor's option, a domestic photovoltaic device [@CL-SUP-1515; @CL-SUP-1516]. At $174,000 or more, the Contractor shall utilize under the contract only U.S.-made, designated country, or qualifying country photovoltaic devices [@CL-SUP-1468]. The band of $100,000 or more but less than $102,280 [@CL-SUP-1511] and the band of $100,000 or more but less than $174,000 [@CL-SUP-1513] both begin at $100,000, so values from $100,000 up to $102,280 fall within both bands. The list in the first band excludes Bahraini, Korean, Moroccan, Panamanian, and Peruvian devices from the Free Trade Agreement kinds [@CL-SUP-1512], and the list in the second band excludes Bahraini, Moroccan, Panamanian, and Peruvian devices [@CL-SUP-1514].

The restrictions in the certificate provision apply depending on the estimated aggregate value of photovoltaic devices to be utilized under a resultant contract [@CL-SUP-1469]. At $174,000 or more, the Government will consider only offers that utilize U.S.-made, qualifying country, or designated country photovoltaic devices [@CL-SUP-1470]. Above the micro-purchase threshold but below $174,000, the Government will not accept an offer specifying the use of other foreign photovoltaic devices in paragraph (d)(2)(ii), (d)(3)(ii), (d)(4)(ii), or (d)(5)(ii) of the provision [@CL-SUP-1471], unless the Offeror documents to the satisfaction of the Contracting Officer that the price of the foreign photovoltaic device plus 50 percent is less than the price of a comparable domestic photovoltaic device [@CL-SUP-1472]. One listed certification paragraph says that, at $174,000 or more, the Offeror certifies that each photovoltaic device to be used in performance of the contract is a U.S.-made, designated country, or qualifying country photovoltaic device [@CL-SUP-1473].

## How it works

The clause and the certificate both turn on the estimated value of the photovoltaic devices: the clause on the value the Contractor specified in its offer [@CL-SUP-1466], and the certificate on the estimated aggregate value [@CL-SUP-1469]. At $174,000 or more, the Contractor shall utilize under the contract only U.S.-made, designated country, or qualifying country photovoltaic devices [@CL-SUP-1468], and the Government will consider only offers that utilize U.S.-made, qualifying country, or designated country photovoltaic devices [@CL-SUP-1470]. Above the micro-purchase threshold but below $174,000, the Government will not accept an offer specifying the use of other foreign photovoltaic devices in the listed paragraphs, unless the Offeror documents to the satisfaction of the Contracting Officer that the price of the foreign photovoltaic device plus 50 percent is less than the price of a comparable domestic photovoltaic device [@CL-SUP-1471; @CL-SUP-1472]. The clause has two middle bands that both begin at $100,000 [@CL-SUP-1511; @CL-SUP-1513].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An offer states that the estimated value of the photovoltaic devices is 300,000. That is at or above the clause's top band value of 174,000, so only U.S.-made, designated country or qualifying country devices are allowed, and the offeror certifies one of those.

A second offer states an estimated value of 150,000, which is above the micro-purchase threshold and below 174,000. It proposes other foreign devices that cost 120 against a comparable domestic device at 190. The foreign price plus 50 percent is 180, which is less than 190, so the price test is met if the offeror documents it to the satisfaction of the Contracting Officer.

## Never confuse

The lower bands of the restriction paragraph name domestic photovoltaic devices unless specified foreign alternatives are certified, unlike the top band at $174,000 or more, which names U.S.-made, designated country or qualifying country devices [@CL-SUP-1467; @CL-SUP-1511; @CL-SUP-1513; @CL-SUP-1468]. For the DoD Buy American clause, see [DoD Buy American definitions and delivery rule](sup-dfars-buy-american-definitions-and-delivery-rule.md). For the trade agreements clause, see [Trade agreements clause and certificate](sup-dfars-trade-agreements-clause-and-certificate.md). For the qualifying country sources clause, see [Qualifying country subcontractors and reports of performance outside the United States and Canada](sup-dfars-qualifying-country-subcontractors-and-reports.md).
