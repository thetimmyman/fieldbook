## Purpose

Shows the notice steps, the effect of approval or disapproval and the cost rules in the two first article approval clauses.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-SUP-1028; @CL-SUP-1030] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.209-3 [@CL-SUP-1028; @CL-SUP-1029; @CL-SUP-1031; @CL-SUP-1032; @CL-SUP-1033; @CL-SUP-1541; @CL-SUP-1457; @CL-SUP-1035; @CL-SUP-1036; @CL-SUP-1066; @CL-SUP-1067; @CL-SUP-1068; @CL-SUP-1376; @CL-SUP-1075] and FAR 52.209-4 [@CL-SUP-1030; @CL-SUP-1034; @CL-SUP-1542; @CL-SUP-1456] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.209-3 [@CL-SUP-3653; @CL-SUP-3654]; RFO FAR 52.209-4 [@CL-SUP-3667; @CL-SUP-3668] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-1028] |
| Applies to | Contracts that include the contractor-testing or government-testing first article clause [@CL-SUP-1028; @CL-SUP-1030] |

In the contractor-testing clause, the Contractor shall notify the Contracting Officer in writing before first article tests begin, of the time and location of the testing so that the Government may witness the tests [@CL-SUP-1028]. The Contracting Officer then notifies the Contractor in writing of the conditional approval, approval, or disapproval of the first article after the Government receives the test report [@CL-SUP-1029]. The government-testing clause has the same notice after the Government receives the first article [@CL-SUP-1030].

In the contractor-testing clause, a notice of conditional approval shall state any further action required of the Contractor, and a notice of disapproval shall cite reasons for the disapproval [@CL-SUP-0080]. A notice of conditional approval or approval does not relieve the Contractor from complying with all requirements of the specifications and all other terms and conditions of the contract [@CL-SUP-1031]. Under the same clause, if the Contractor fails to deliver any first article report on time, or the Contracting Officer disapproves any first article, the Contractor is deemed to have failed to make delivery within the meaning of the Default clause [@CL-SUP-1032].

## How it works

On cost, the contractor-testing clause starts from a disapproved first article: upon Government request the Contractor repeats any or all first article tests [@CL-SUP-1457]. After each request for additional tests, the Contractor makes any necessary changes, modifications, or repairs to the first article or selects another first article for testing [@CL-SUP-1033]. All costs related to these tests are borne by the Contractor, including any and all costs for additional tests following a disapproval [@CL-SUP-1541]. The government-testing clause has the same structure: if the first article is disapproved, the Contractor submits an additional first article upon Government request [@CL-SUP-1456], and after each request the Contractor makes any necessary changes, modifications, or repairs to the first article or selects another first article for testing [@CL-SUP-1034]. All costs related to these tests are borne by the Contractor, including any and all costs for additional tests following a disapproval [@CL-SUP-1542]. In the basic contractor-testing clause, before first article approval the acquisition of materials or components for, or the start of production of, the balance of the contract quantity is at the sole risk of the Contractor [@CL-SUP-1035]. Before first article approval, the costs of those activities are not allocable to the contract for progress payments or for termination settlements if the contract is terminated for the convenience of the Government [@CL-SUP-1036]. Alternate II of the contractor-testing clause lets the Contracting Officer, before first article approval and by written authorization, authorize the Contractor to acquire specific materials or components or to commence production to the extent essential to meet the delivery schedules [@CL-SUP-1066]. Under Alternate II, until first article approval is granted, only costs for the first article and costs incurred under the written authorization are allocable to the contract for progress payments or for termination settlements if the contract is terminated for the convenience of the Government [@CL-SUP-1067]. In the contractor-testing clause, if the Government does not act within the time specified in paragraph (b) or (c) of the clause, the Contracting Officer shall, upon timely written request from the Contractor, equitably adjust under the Changes clause the delivery or performance dates [@CL-SUP-1068]. That adjustment covers the delivery or performance dates and/or the contract price, and any other contractual term affected by the delay [@CL-SUP-1376]. The same clause says the Government may waive the requirement for first article approval test where supplies identical or similar to those called for in the schedule have been previously furnished by the offeror or contractor and have been accepted by the Government, and the offeror or contractor may request a waiver [@CL-SUP-1075; @CL-SUP-0081].

In RFO FAR part 52, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text from February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760; @CL-CMP-9762]. RFO FAR 52.209-3(a) keeps the written notice before first article tests of the time and location of the testing so that the Government may witness the tests [@CL-SUP-3653; @CL-SUP-1028]. RFO FAR 52.209-3(b) says the Contracting Officer will notify the Contractor in writing of the conditional approval, approval, or disapproval where the codified text says shall notify [@CL-SUP-3654; @CL-SUP-1029], and RFO FAR 52.209-4(b) says the same for the government-testing clause [@CL-SUP-3667; @CL-SUP-1030]. RFO FAR 52.209-3(b) keeps the rule that a notice of conditional approval or approval does not relieve the Contractor from complying with the specifications and other terms [@CL-SUP-3655; @CL-SUP-1031], and 52.209-3(d) keeps the deemed failure to make delivery within the meaning of the Default clause [@CL-SUP-3656; @CL-SUP-1032]. On cost, 52.209-3(c) keeps the repeat of tests on Government request after a disapproval [@CL-SUP-3657; @CL-SUP-1457], the changes or new first article after each request [@CL-SUP-3658; @CL-SUP-1033], and the Contractor's cost for the tests including additional tests following a disapproval [@CL-SUP-3659; @CL-SUP-1541]. RFO FAR 52.209-4(c) keeps the additional first article on Government request, the changes after each request and the same cost rule, and says if the Government disapproves the first article where the codified text says if the first article is disapproved [@CL-SUP-3668; @CL-SUP-3669; @CL-SUP-3670; @CL-SUP-1456; @CL-SUP-1034; @CL-SUP-1542]. RFO FAR 52.209-3(g) keeps the sole risk of the Contractor before approval [@CL-SUP-3660; @CL-SUP-1035] and the rule that those costs are not allocable to progress payments or termination settlements [@CL-SUP-3661; @CL-SUP-1036]. RFO FAR 52.209-3 Alternate II keeps the written authorization by the Contracting Officer and the allocable costs [@CL-SUP-3662; @CL-SUP-3663; @CL-SUP-1066; @CL-SUP-1067]. RFO FAR 52.209-3(f) says the Contracting Officer will, where the codified text says shall, equitably adjust the delivery or performance dates and/or the contract price and any other affected term [@CL-SUP-3664; @CL-SUP-3665; @CL-SUP-1068; @CL-SUP-1376], and 52.209-3(h) keeps the waiver where identical or similar supplies were previously furnished and accepted [@CL-SUP-3666; @CL-SUP-1075].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A supplier is told by the contract to run first article tests itself. The first article is disapproved and the Government asks for a retest. The supplier has already bought material for the full lot. The answer: because the Government requested a retest after the disapproval, the supplier bears the retest costs, the early material purchase is at its own risk, and those costs are not allocable to progress payments before approval under the basic clause.
