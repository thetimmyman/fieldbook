## Purpose

Shows who is a covered employee, what the Contractor must do to screen, prevent and report personal conflicts of interest, and which subcontracts carry the clause.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-SUP-1007] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.203-16 [@CL-SUP-1007; @CL-SUP-1008; @CL-SUP-1010; @CL-SUP-1011; @CL-SUP-1057; @CL-SUP-1544; @CL-SUP-1539] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.203-16 [@CL-SUP-3747] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-1007] |
| Applies to | The Contractor, with respect to its covered employees [@CL-SUP-1007; @CL-SUP-1008] |

A covered employee is an individual who performs an acquisition function closely associated with inherently governmental functions and is either an employee of the contractor or a subcontractor that is a self-employed individual treated as a covered employee of the contractor because there is no employer to whom such an individual could submit the required disclosures [@CL-SUP-1007; @CL-SUP-9746].

Under its listed requirements, the Contractor shall have procedures in place to screen covered employees for potential personal conflicts of interest, and the first listed means is obtaining and maintaining from each covered employee, when the employee is initially assigned to the task under the contract, a disclosure of interests [@CL-SUP-1008]. That disclosure is of interests that might be affected by the task to which the employee has been assigned, as follows: financial interests of the covered employee, of close family members, or of other members of the covered employee's household; other employment or financial relationships of the covered employee, including seeking or negotiating for prospective employment or business; and gifts, including travel [@CL-SUP-1544; @CL-SUP-9743; @CL-SUP-9744]. The second listed means, which the clause joins to the first with "and" so that both apply, is requiring each covered employee to update the disclosure statement whenever the employee's personal or financial circumstances change in such a way that a new personal conflict of interest might occur because of the task the covered employee is performing [@CL-SUP-1539; @CL-SUP-9744]. One listed duty of the Contractor for each covered employee is to prevent personal conflicts of interest, including not assigning or allowing a covered employee to perform any task under the contract for which the Contractor has identified a personal conflict of interest for the employee that the Contractor or employee cannot satisfactorily prevent or mitigate in consultation with the contracting agency [@CL-SUP-1009; @CL-SUP-9745].

## How it works

The screening procedures and the duty to prevent conflicts are two of the Contractor's listed requirements [@CL-SUP-1008; @CL-SUP-1009]. One further listed duty is to report to the Contracting Officer any personal conflict-of-interest violation by a covered employee as soon as it is identified, with a description of the violation and the proposed actions the Contractor will take in response [@CL-SUP-1010]. The Contractor shall include the substance of the clause, including its subcontracts paragraph, in subcontracts that meet two conditions, the first being that they exceed the simplified acquisition threshold as defined in FAR 2.101 on the date of subcontract award [@CL-SUP-1011]. The second listed condition is that subcontractor employees will perform acquisition functions closely associated with inherently governmental functions [@CL-SUP-1011; @CL-SUP-1012], as distinct from performance only by a self-employed individual [@CL-SUP-1540]. In exceptional circumstances, if the Contractor cannot satisfactorily prevent a personal conflict of interest as required by the prevention paragraph, the Contractor may submit a request through the Contracting Officer to the Head of the Contracting Activity for the items the clause then lists [@CL-SUP-1057]. The request may be for agreement to a plan to mitigate the personal conflict of interest, or for a waiver of the requirement [@CL-SUP-1058]. The clause text lists further duties, which this card does not draft.

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text under Class Deviation 2026-O0038, effective February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. The RFO text of FAR 52.203-16 keeps the definition of covered employee [@CL-SUP-3747; @CL-SUP-3748; @CL-SUP-1007]. It keeps the screening requirements in paragraph (b)(1), including the initial disclosure of interests and the update whenever circumstances change [@CL-SUP-3749; @CL-SUP-3750; @CL-SUP-3751; @CL-SUP-1008; @CL-SUP-1544; @CL-SUP-1539]. It keeps the duty to prevent personal conflicts of interest in paragraph (b)(2)(i) [@CL-SUP-3752; @CL-SUP-1009], and that duty includes not assigning or allowing a covered employee to perform any task under the contract for which the Contractor has identified a personal conflict of interest for the employee that the Contractor or employee cannot satisfactorily prevent or mitigate in consultation with the contracting agency [@CL-SUP-3753]. For each covered employee, paragraph (b)(2) also requires the Contractor to prohibit use of non-public information accessed through performance of a Government contract for personal gain, and to obtain a signed non-disclosure agreement prohibiting its disclosure [@CL-SUP-9396]. It keeps the duty to report a violation in paragraph (b)(6) [@CL-SUP-3754; @CL-SUP-1010]. It keeps the exceptional-circumstances request in paragraph (c)(1) [@CL-SUP-3755; @CL-SUP-3756; @CL-SUP-1057; @CL-SUP-1058]. It keeps the flowdown rule in paragraph (d), including the simplified acquisition threshold condition and the condition on acquisition functions [@CL-SUP-3757; @CL-SUP-3758; @CL-SUP-1011; @CL-SUP-1012; @CL-SUP-1540].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A support contractor assigns an analyst to review contract proposals for an agency. The analyst is a covered employee. The contractor collects the analyst's disclosure at assignment. Later the analyst's spouse buys shares in a bidder. The answer: the contractor treats this as a possible new conflict, and if a violation is identified it reports the violation and its proposed response to the Contracting Officer.
