## Purpose

Shows what the offeror represents under the overseas construction and overseas architect-engineer provisions, how a United States firm is defined, and how offers are treated.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-SUP-1811; @CL-SUP-1813] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 252.236-7010 [@CL-SUP-1811; @CL-SUP-1844]; DFARS 252.236-7011 [@CL-SUP-1813] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-1811; @CL-SUP-1813] |
| Applies to | Solicitations that include either provision [@CL-SUP-1812; @CL-SUP-1814]; the construction provision is prescribed in DFARS 236.570(c)(1) [@CL-SUP-1844] |

In the construction provision, offers from firms that do not qualify as United States firms will be evaluated by adding 20 percent to the offer [@CL-SUP-1811]. The offeror states whether it is, or is not, a United States firm [@CL-SUP-1812].

In the architect-engineer provision, military construction appropriations acts restrict award of a contract resulting from the solicitation to a United States firm or a joint venture of United States and host nation firms [@CL-SUP-1813]. By submitting its offer, the offeror confirms that it is such a firm or joint venture [@CL-SUP-1814].

## How it works

A United States firm in the construction provision is a firm incorporated in the United States whose corporate headquarters are in the United States [@CL-SUP-1808]. It must also meet all of the remaining conditions, which are joined by "and": the firm has filed corporate and employment tax returns in the United States for a minimum of 2 years (if required), has filed State and Federal income tax returns (if required) for 2 years, and has paid any taxes due as a result of these filings [@CL-SUP-1809]; and the firm employs United States citizens in key management positions [@CL-SUP-1810; @CL-SUP-9737]. In the architect-engineer provision, the second condition is that the firm has filed corporate and employment tax returns in the United States for a minimum of 12 years (if required), has filed State and Federal income tax returns (if required) for 2 years, and has paid any taxes due as a result of these filings [@CL-SUP-1815].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

Assume the construction provision applies. Two offers are received for an overseas construction contract. Offer A is from a firm that is not a United States firm and is priced at 10,000,000. Offer B is from a United States firm and is priced at 11,000,000.

Evaluation adds 20 percent to offer A, so it is evaluated at 12,000,000. Offer B is the lower evaluated offer.

## Never confuse

The construction provision requires a minimum of 2 years of corporate and employment tax returns, if required [@CL-SUP-1809], and the architect-engineer provision a minimum of 12 years, if required [@CL-SUP-1815]. Under the construction provision, offers from firms that do not qualify as United States firms will be evaluated by adding 20 percent to the offer [@CL-SUP-1811], while under the architect-engineer provision military construction appropriations acts restrict award of the contract to a United States firm or a joint venture of United States and host nation firms [@CL-SUP-1813]. Under the Kwajalein provision, offers from firms that do not qualify as United States firms or Marshallese firms will be evaluated by adding 20 percent to the offer, unless application of the factor would not result in award to a United States firm [@CL-SUP-1817]; see [Kwajalein Atoll evaluation preference and competition opportunity for American steel](sup-dfars-kwajalein-preference-and-american-steel.md).
