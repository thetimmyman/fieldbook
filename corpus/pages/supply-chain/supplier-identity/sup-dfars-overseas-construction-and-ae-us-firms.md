## Purpose

Shows what the offeror represents under the overseas construction and overseas architect-engineer provisions, how a United States firm is defined, and how offers are treated.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-SUP-1811; @CL-SUP-1813] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 252.236-7010 [@CL-SUP-1811; @CL-SUP-1844]; DFARS 252.236-7011 [@CL-SUP-1813] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-1811; @CL-SUP-1813] |
| Applies to | Solicitations that include either provision [@CL-SUP-1812; @CL-SUP-1814]; the construction provision text says it is prescribed in a DFARS section it names [@CL-SUP-1844] |

In the construction provision, offers from firms that do not qualify as United States firms will be evaluated by adding 20 percent to the offer [@CL-SUP-1811]. The offeror states whether it is, or is not, a United States firm [@CL-SUP-1812].

In the architect-engineer provision, military construction appropriations acts restrict award of a contract resulting from the solicitation to a United States firm or a joint venture of United States and host nation firms [@CL-SUP-1813]. By submitting its offer, the offeror confirms that it is such a firm or joint venture [@CL-SUP-1814].

## How it works

A United States firm in the construction provision is a firm incorporated in the United States whose corporate headquarters are in the United States [@CL-SUP-1808]. It must also meet the tax filing and payment condition, which prints a minimum of 2 years of corporate and employment tax returns, if required [@CL-SUP-1809], and employ United States citizens in key management positions [@CL-SUP-1810]. The architect-engineer provision uses the same kind of definition but, as printed, a minimum of 12 years of corporate and employment tax returns [@CL-SUP-1815].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

Assume the construction provision applies. Two offers are received for an overseas construction contract. Offer A is from a firm that is not a United States firm and is priced at 10,000,000. Offer B is from a United States firm and is priced at 11,000,000.

Evaluation adds 20 percent to offer A, so it is evaluated at 12,000,000. Offer B is the lower evaluated offer.

## Never confuse

The two provisions use almost the same firm definition, but as printed the construction provision requires a minimum of 2 years of corporate and employment tax filing, if required [@CL-SUP-1809], and the architect-engineer provision a minimum of 12 years, if required [@CL-SUP-1815]. The construction provision adds a percentage to offers from other firms [@CL-SUP-1811], while the architect-engineer provision restricts the award [@CL-SUP-1813]. The Kwajalein provision also evaluates offers by adding a percentage and recognises Marshallese firms [@CL-SUP-1816]; see [Kwajalein Atoll evaluation preference and competition opportunity for American steel](sup-dfars-kwajalein-preference-and-american-steel.md).
