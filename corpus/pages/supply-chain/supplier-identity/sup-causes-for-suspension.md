## Purpose

Shows the offenses and conduct for which the suspending and debarring official may suspend a contractor upon adequate evidence.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-SUP-0776] |
| Codified citation | FAR 9.407-1(b)(1) and FAR 9.407-2 [@CL-SUP-0926; @CL-SUP-0776; @CL-SUP-0779] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-0776] |
| Applies to | Contractors suspected, upon adequate evidence, of the listed offenses or conduct [@CL-SUP-0776; @CL-SUP-0779] |

The suspending and debarring official may suspend a contractor suspected, upon adequate evidence, of any one of the listed offenses or conduct, the first being commission of fraud or a criminal offense in connection with obtaining, attempting to obtain, or performing a public contract or subcontract [@CL-SUP-0776].

The official may upon adequate evidence also suspend a contractor for any other cause of so serious or compelling a nature that it affects the present responsibility of a Government contractor or subcontractor [@CL-SUP-0779].

The FAR's general definitions say adequate evidence means information sufficient to support the reasonable belief that a particular act or omission has occurred [@CL-SUP-9536]. The RFO model text of FAR 2.101 defines adequate evidence in the same words as the codified definition [@CL-SUP-9591; @CL-SUP-9536].

## How it works

The listed causes include violation of Federal or State antitrust statutes relating to the submission of offers, and embezzlement, theft, forgery, bribery, falsification or destruction of records, false statements, tax evasion, violating Federal criminal tax laws, or receiving stolen property [@CL-SUP-0776; @CL-SUP-0777]. Indictment for any of the causes in paragraph (a) of the section constitutes adequate evidence for suspension [@CL-SUP-0778]. Suspension is a serious action to be imposed on the basis of adequate evidence, pending the completion of an investigation or legal proceedings, when it has been determined that immediate action is necessary to protect the Government's interest [@CL-SUP-0926].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor's owner is indicted for bribery of a purchasing official. No trial has taken place.

Answer: the indictment is adequate evidence for suspension, and the official may suspend the contractor pending completion of the legal proceedings when it has been determined that immediate action is necessary to protect the Government's interest.

## Never confuse

Suspension is imposed on adequate evidence of a suspected cause [@CL-SUP-0776], and indictment for a cause in paragraph (a) constitutes adequate evidence [@CL-SUP-0778]. Debarment on a listed offense rests on a conviction or civil judgment [@CL-SUP-0766]; see the [Causes for debarment](sup-causes-for-debarment.md) card. The effect of either listing is on the [SAM exclusion records and the effect of listing](sup-sam-exclusion-records-and-effect-of-listing.md) card.
