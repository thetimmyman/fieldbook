## Purpose

Shows the OFAC purchase prohibition and its flowdown to subcontracts, and when supplies may be accorded duty-free entry and what the contractor must do.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-SUP-1262; @CL-SUP-1265] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.225-13 [@CL-SUP-1262]; FAR 52.225-8 [@CL-SUP-1265] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-1262; @CL-SUP-1265] |
| Applies to | Contracts that include either clause [@CL-SUP-1262; @CL-SUP-1265] |

Except as authorized by the Office of Foreign Assets Control (OFAC) in the Department of the Treasury, the contractor shall not acquire supplies or services for use in performing the contract [@CL-SUP-1263]. The prohibited acquisitions are any supplies or services that any proclamation, Executive order, or statute administered by OFAC, or OFAC's implementing regulations, would prohibit as a transaction by a person subject to the jurisdiction of the United States [@CL-SUP-1262]. The contractor shall insert the clause, including its paragraph (c), in all subcontracts [@CL-SUP-1264].

For supplies not identified in the Schedule to be accorded duty-free entry, the listed procedures apply except as provided in paragraph (d) of the duty-free entry clause or elsewhere in the contract [@CL-SUP-1265]. The contractor shall claim duty-free entry only for supplies to be delivered to the Government under the contract, either as end products or incorporated into end products [@CL-SUP-1268].

## How it works

The first clause is a prohibition with one route around it, which is OFAC authorization [@CL-SUP-1263; @CL-SUP-1262]. The second clause is a notice process. The contractor notifies the Contracting Officer in writing of any purchase of foreign supplies, including raw materials, components, and intermediate assemblies, in excess of $20,000 [@CL-SUP-1266]. The notice duty covers foreign supplies that are to be imported into the customs territory of the United States for delivery to the Government under the contract, either as end products or for incorporation into end products [@CL-SUP-1295]. The notice is due at least 20 calendar days before the importation [@CL-SUP-1267]. Except as otherwise approved by the Contracting Officer, the contract price is reduced by, or the allowable cost does not include, the amount of duty that would be payable if the supplies were not entered duty-free [@CL-SUP-1296].

The contractor is not required to give the notice if two listed conditions are met. The first is that the supplies are identical in nature to items purchased by the contractor or any subcontractor in connection with its commercial business [@CL-SUP-1293]. The second is that segregation of these supplies to ensure use only on Government contracts containing duty-free entry provisions is not economical or feasible [@CL-SUP-1294].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor plans to import a foreign component for 30,000 for delivery under the contract as part of an end product. The component is not identified in the Schedule for duty-free entry. It is not identical to anything the contractor buys for its commercial business, so the commercial-business exception does not apply. The purchase is above the notice amount, so the contractor must notify the Contracting Officer in writing. The import is planned for day 60, so the notice is due no later than day 40. If the Contracting Officer accords duty-free entry and does not approve otherwise, the contract price is reduced by the duty that would otherwise be payable.

## Never confuse

The two clauses address different things. One restricts acquisitions that OFAC measures prohibit, and the other sets out notice and price procedures for duty-free entry [@CL-SUP-1262; @CL-SUP-1265]. For the purchasing system that passes clauses to subcontracts, see [Contractor Purchasing System Administration clause](../procure-to-pay/sup-contractor-purchasing-system.md).
