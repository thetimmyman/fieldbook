## Purpose

Shows the OFAC purchase prohibition and its flowdown to subcontracts, and when supplies may be accorded duty-free entry and what the contractor must do.

## The rule

|---|---|

| Codified citation | FAR 52.225-13 [@CL-SUP-1262]; FAR 52.225-8 [@CL-SUP-1265] |

Except as authorized by the Office of Foreign Assets Control (OFAC) in the Department of the Treasury, the contractor shall not acquire supplies or services for use in performing the contract [@CL-SUP-1263]. The prohibited acquisitions are any supplies or services that any proclamation, Executive order, or statute administered by OFAC, or OFAC's implementing regulations, would prohibit as a transaction by a person subject to the jurisdiction of the United States [@CL-SUP-1262]. The contractor shall insert the clause, including its paragraph (c), in all subcontracts [@CL-SUP-1264].

For supplies not identified in the Schedule to be accorded duty-free entry, the listed procedures apply except as provided in paragraph (d) of the duty-free entry clause or elsewhere in the contract [@CL-SUP-1265]. The contractor shall claim duty-free entry only for supplies to be delivered to the Government under the contract, either as end products or incorporated into end products [@CL-SUP-1268]. The contractor shall also pay duty on supplies, or any portion of them, other than scrap, salvage, or competitive sale authorized by the Contracting Officer, diverted to nongovernmental use [@CL-SUP-4034].

## How it works

Under the first clause, except as authorized by OFAC, the contractor shall not acquire for use in the performance of the contract any supplies or services if any proclamation, Executive order, or statute administered by OFAC, or its implementing regulations, would prohibit such a transaction by a person subject to the jurisdiction of the United States [@CL-SUP-1263; @CL-SUP-1262]. Under the duty-free entry clause, the contractor shall notify the Contracting Officer in writing of any purchase of foreign supplies (including, without limitation, raw materials, components, and intermediate assemblies) in excess of $20,000 [@CL-SUP-1266]. The notice duty covers foreign supplies that are to be imported into the customs territory of the United States for delivery to the Government under the contract, either as end products or for incorporation into end products [@CL-SUP-1295]. The notice is due at least 20 calendar days before the importation [@CL-SUP-1267]. Except as otherwise approved by the Contracting Officer, the contract price is reduced by, or the allowable cost does not include, the amount of duty that would be payable if the supplies were not entered duty-free [@CL-SUP-1296].

The contractor is not required to give the notice for foreign supplies if the supplies are identical in nature to items purchased by the contractor or any subcontractor in connection with its commercial business [@CL-SUP-1293], and segregation of these supplies to ensure use only on Government contracts containing duty-free entry provisions is not economical or feasible [@CL-SUP-1294].

In RFO FAR part 52, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text under Class Deviation 2026-O0038, effective February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760; @CL-CMP-9762]. RFO FAR part 52 lists FAR 52.225-13 as [Reserved] [@CL-SUP-3717]. In RFO FAR part 52, the OFAC prohibition is in paragraph (d)(2)(i) of FAR 52.240-91, which keeps the OFAC exception and the scope of the prohibited supplies and services [@CL-SUP-3718; @CL-SUP-3719; @CL-SUP-1263; @CL-SUP-1262]. RFO FAR 52.240-91(j) says the Contractor shall insert the substance of the clause, including paragraph (j) but excluding subparagraphs (d)(1) and (i)(1), in all subcontracts and other contractual instruments, including subcontracts for acquiring commercial products or commercial services [@CL-SUP-3720]. RFO FAR 52.225-8 keeps the notice rules in paragraph (c), including the $20,000 amount, the import scope, the 20 calendar days and the price reduction [@CL-SUP-3721; @CL-SUP-3722; @CL-SUP-3723; @CL-SUP-3724; @CL-SUP-3725; @CL-SUP-1265; @CL-SUP-1266; @CL-SUP-1295; @CL-SUP-1267; @CL-SUP-1296]. It keeps the exception in paragraph (d) [@CL-SUP-3726; @CL-SUP-3727; @CL-SUP-1293; @CL-SUP-1294] and the duty-free claim limit in paragraph (e) [@CL-SUP-3728; @CL-SUP-1268]. Paragraph (e) also requires the Contractor to pay duty on supplies, or any portion of them, diverted to nongovernmental use, other than scrap, salvage, or competitive sale authorized by the Contracting Officer [@CL-SUP-9397].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor plans to import a foreign component for 30,000 for delivery under the contract as part of an end product. The component is not identified in the Schedule for duty-free entry. It is not identical to anything the contractor buys for its commercial business, so the commercial-business exception does not apply. The purchase is above the notice amount, so the contractor must notify the Contracting Officer in writing. The import is planned for day 60, so the notice is due no later than day 40. If the Contracting Officer accords duty-free entry and does not approve otherwise, the contract price is reduced by the duty that would otherwise be payable.

## Never confuse

One clause restricts acquisitions that OFAC measures prohibit, and the other sets out notice and price procedures for duty-free entry [@CL-SUP-1262; @CL-SUP-1265]. The codified clause has the contractor insert the clause, including its paragraph (c), in all subcontracts [@CL-SUP-1264], unlike RFO FAR 52.240-91(j), which has the contractor insert the substance of the clause, including paragraph (j) but excluding subparagraphs (d)(1) and (i)(1), in all subcontracts and other contractual instruments, including subcontracts for acquiring commercial products or commercial services [@CL-SUP-3720] (see also [Contractor Purchasing System Administration clause](../procure-to-pay/sup-contractor-purchasing-system.md)).
