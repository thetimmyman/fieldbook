## Purpose

Shows what the DoD Buy American and Balance of Payments Program clause counts as a domestic end product and as a qualifying country end product, how the component test works, what the contractor must deliver, and how the alternates change the clause.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-SUP-1311] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 252.225-7001 [@CL-SUP-1311] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-1311] |
| Applies to | All line items in the contract, unless otherwise specified [@CL-SUP-1313] |

The Contractor shall deliver only domestic end products unless, in its offer, it specified delivery of other end products in the Buy American certificate provision of the solicitation [@CL-SUP-1311]. If the Contractor certified in its offer that it will deliver a qualifying country end product, it shall deliver a qualifying country end product or, at its option, a domestic end product [@CL-SUP-1312].

For an end product that does not consist wholly or predominantly of iron or steel, one listed way to be a domestic end product is to be an unmanufactured end product mined or produced in the United States [@CL-SUP-1301]. In the same definition, one listed condition for an end product manufactured in the United States is a component-cost test of 60 percent of the cost of all its components, except that the percentage will be 65 percent in the cases that the rest of the sentence states [@CL-SUP-1302]. The stepped percentages of 65 percent for items delivered in calendar years 2024 through 2028 and 75 percent for items delivered starting in calendar year 2029 do not apply where an alternate percentage is established for a contract in accordance with FAR 25.101(d) [@CL-SUP-1303]. In the basic clause, that sentence also refers, alongside an alternate percentage, to an award made before January 1, 2030, for a foreign end product that exceeds 55 percent domestic content, and it points to a DFARS paragraph for that case [@CL-SUP-1351]. One listed alternative in the definition is that the end product is a COTS item [@CL-SUP-1352].

An end product that consists wholly or predominantly of iron or steel is, by one listed alternative, a domestic end product if it is manufactured in the United States and the cost of iron and steel not produced in the United States or a qualifying country is less than 5 percent of the cost of all the components [@CL-SUP-1305]. Predominantly of iron or steel means that the cost of the iron and steel content exceeds 50 percent of the total cost of all the end product's components [@CL-SUP-1306].

The clause has three alternates. Alternate I adds the South Caucasus and Central and South Asian state definitions to paragraph (a) and uses different paragraphs (b) and (c) than the basic clause [@CL-SUP-1354]. Under Alternate I, a Contractor that certified in its offer a qualifying country end product or an SC/CASA state end product shall deliver a qualifying country end product, an SC/CASA state end product, or, at its option, a domestic end product [@CL-SUP-1355]. Alternate II puts in the definitions of domestic end product and qualifying country end product the domestic content threshold that applies to the entire contract period of performance [@CL-SUP-1356]. Alternate II fixes the component-cost percentage for the entire period of performance by the year of award, at 60 percent for a contract awarded in calendar year 2023, 65 percent for calendar years 2024 through 2028 and 75 percent for calendar year 2029 or later [@CL-SUP-1357]. Alternate III includes the domestic content threshold that applies to the entire contract period of performance, adds the South Caucasus and Central and South Asian state definitions to paragraph (a), and uses different paragraphs (b) and (c) than the basic clause [@CL-SUP-1358].

## How it works

A component is an article, material, or supply incorporated directly into an end product [@CL-SUP-1307], and the cost of components includes transportation costs to the place of incorporation and U.S. duty, with components of unknown origin treated as foreign [@CL-SUP-1304]. A qualifying country is a country with a reciprocal defense procurement memorandum of understanding or international agreement with the United States [@CL-SUP-1308], and the clause then lists the qualifying countries by name. A qualifying country end product is an unmanufactured end product mined or produced in a qualifying country, or one manufactured in a qualifying country if a listed condition is met [@CL-SUP-1309]. The first listed condition is a cost test on listed types of components [@CL-SUP-1323], which include components from a qualifying country and components from the United States [@CL-SUP-1310]. The cost test uses the same stepped percentages as the domestic test, unless an alternate percentage is established for a contract [@CL-SUP-1321]. The definition also lists, as an alternative to the cost test, that the end product is a COTS item [@CL-SUP-1353].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A product is manufactured in the United States and is not predominantly iron or steel. Its components cost 1,000 in total. Components from the United States cost 450 and components from a qualifying country cost 250, so the two groups together are 70 percent of the cost of all components. Assume the percentage that applies to the delivery year is 65 percent. The product passes the component test and is a domestic end product.

## Never confuse

A qualifying country end product is not a domestic end product. The contract calls for domestic end products unless the offer certified otherwise, and an offeror that certified a qualifying country end product may still deliver a domestic one at its option [@CL-SUP-1312].

The FAR clause for supplies has its own domestic end product definition and delivery rule, covered on [Buy American clause for supplies](sup-buy-american-supplies-clause.md). Where the two differ, the DoD component test counts qualifying country components together with United States components [@CL-SUP-1302], while the FAR card's component test counts the cost of components mined, produced, or manufactured in the United States [@CL-SUP-1231]. The DoD delivery rule also gives a contractor that certified a qualifying country end product a choice between that end product and a domestic one [@CL-SUP-1312], while the FAR delivery rule speaks of domestic end products except to the extent the offer specified foreign end products [@CL-SUP-1235].

The offeror's side of this clause is on [DoD Buy American clause: exceptions, certificate and evaluation](sup-dfars-buy-american-exceptions-certificate-and-evaluation.md). The [DoD specialty metals restriction](sup-dfars-specialty-metals-restriction.md), the [DoD bearings clause](sup-dfars-bearings-and-communist-chinese-military-companies.md), the [DoD domestic commodities preference](sup-dfars-domestic-commodities-preference.md) and the [DoD trade agreements clause](sup-dfars-trade-agreements-clause-and-certificate.md) are separate clauses with their own tests, and the component test here does not decide them.
