## Purpose

Shows the exceptions to the Buy American and Balance of Payments Program component test, the certificate the offeror gives, and how the Government evaluates offers. It does not repeat the definitions and delivery rule on the first card for this clause.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-SUP-1314; @CL-SUP-1318] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 252.225-7001 and DFARS 252.225-7000 [@CL-SUP-1314; @CL-SUP-1318] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-1314] |
| Applies to | Line items subject to the Buy American and Balance of Payments Program basic clause of the solicitation [@CL-SUP-1319] |

The component test of the Buy American statute is waived for an end product that is a commercially available off-the-shelf item [@CL-SUP-1314]. A component is considered to have been mined, produced, or manufactured in the United States, regardless of its source in fact, if the end product is manufactured in the United States and the component is of a class or kind for which the Government has made one of the listed determinations [@CL-SUP-1315]. The two determinations listed for that rule are that sufficient and reasonably available commercial quantities of a satisfactory quality are not mined, produced, or manufactured in the United States, or that it is inconsistent with the public interest to apply the restrictions of the Buy American statute [@CL-SUP-1316]. The contract price does not include duty for end products or components for which the Contractor will claim duty-free entry [@CL-SUP-1317].

Under the certificate provision the Government will evaluate offers in accordance with the policies and procedures of DFARS Part 225 [@CL-SUP-1318]. It will also evaluate offers of qualifying country end products without regard to the restrictions of the Buy American statute or the Balance of Payments Program [@CL-SUP-1359].

## How it works

The offeror makes the listed certifications for all line items subject to the basic clause [@CL-SUP-1319]. One listed certification is that each end product, except those listed elsewhere in the provision, is a domestic end product, and that each domestic end product listed separately contains a critical component or a critical item [@CL-SUP-1320]. The provision also has paragraphs for listing qualifying country end products and other foreign end products with their countries of origin, which this card does not set out. For foreign end products that do not consist wholly or predominantly of iron or steel, the offeror also indicates whether they exceed 55 percent domestic content, except for those that are COTS items [@CL-SUP-1322].

The clause has an Alternate I, an Alternate II and an Alternate III. Alternate I adds the South Caucasus and Central and South Asian state definitions to paragraph (a) and uses different paragraphs (b) and (c) than the basic clause [@CL-SUP-1354]. Alternates II and III put the domestic content threshold for the entire contract period of performance into the definitions [@CL-SUP-1356; @CL-SUP-1358]. The provision has an Alternate I that adds the same state definitions and replaces the term qualifying country end products in two paragraphs [@CL-SUP-1360]. This card covers the basic text of each.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An offeror bids on three line items. Two are domestic end products. The third is made in a qualifying country, so the offeror lists it with its country of origin. The Government evaluates that third item without applying the Buy American restrictions. If the offeror wins that line, it delivers the qualifying country end product or, at its option, a domestic end product.

## Never confuse

The certificate provision sits in the solicitation and the clause sits in the contract. The provision collects the offeror's certification and states how the Government evaluates offers, while the clause sets what is delivered, as the first card for this clause shows on [DoD Buy American clause: definitions and delivery rule](sup-dfars-buy-american-definitions-and-delivery-rule.md).

The FAR has its own supplies clause and its own certificates, covered on [Buy American clause for supplies](sup-buy-american-supplies-clause.md) and [Buy American certificates in offers](sup-buy-american-certificates.md). Where the DoD provision differs, it names DFARS Part 225 for the evaluation policies and procedures [@CL-SUP-1318], while the FAR certificate names part 25 of the Federal Acquisition Regulation [@CL-SUP-1239]. The DoD provision also states that offers of qualifying country end products are evaluated without regard to the restrictions of the Buy American statute or the Balance of Payments Program [@CL-SUP-1359].

The waiver of the component test for a commercially available off-the-shelf end product is also different from the exception for such items in the [DoD specialty metals restriction](sup-dfars-specialty-metals-restriction.md), which has its own carve-outs. The [DoD trade agreements clause and certificate](sup-dfars-trade-agreements-clause-and-certificate.md) are a separate pair with their own evaluation rule.
