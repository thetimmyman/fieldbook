## Purpose

Shows the melted or produced rule in the specialty metals clause that names only the United States and its outlying areas, which metals it covers, and what an offeror certifies in the commercial derivative military article provision.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-SUP-1696] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 252.225-7008 [@CL-SUP-1696] and DFARS 252.225-7010 [@CL-SUP-1701] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-1696] |
| Applies to | Contracts with the clause where DFARS 225.7003-5(a)(1) prescribes it [@CL-SUP-1764], and solicitations with the provision for offerors that list commercial derivative military articles [@CL-SUP-1701] |

Any specialty metal delivered under the contract shall be melted or produced in the United States or its outlying areas [@CL-SUP-1696]. The clause text names only those places as the allowed origin [@CL-SUP-1696]. In the clause, produce means atomization, sputtering, or final consolidation of non-melt derived metal powders [@CL-SUP-1746].

The clause definition lists steel whose maximum alloy content exceeds the manganese, silicon or copper limits it sets [@CL-SUP-1697]. The same steel item also lists steel containing more than 0.25 percent of any of the named elements [@CL-SUP-1698]. The definition also lists nickel, iron-nickel and cobalt alloys above set alloying content [@CL-SUP-1699], and titanium and titanium alloys, and zirconium and zirconium alloys [@CL-SUP-1700].

In the provision, the offeror lists any commercial derivative military articles it intends to deliver using the alternative compliance, and its designation of an item is subject to Government review and approval [@CL-SUP-1701; @CL-SUP-1747].

## How it works

The restriction sits in the clause and attaches to specialty metal delivered under the contract [@CL-SUP-1696]. The definition decides which steels and alloys count as specialty metal [@CL-SUP-1697; @CL-SUP-1698; @CL-SUP-1699; @CL-SUP-1700]. If the offeror listed any commercial derivative military articles, it certifies, on the footing that it is awarded a contract and the Government approves the designation, that it and its subcontractors will demonstrate purchase agreements for domestically melted or produced specialty metal in the required form for use during contract performance [@CL-SUP-1702; @CL-SUP-1703]. The amount is not less than the contractor's good faith estimate of the greater of two amounts, one equal to 120 percent of the metal needed for the article [@CL-SUP-1704] and the other equal to 50 percent of the metal to be purchased in the period for the article and the related commercial article [@CL-SUP-1705]. The metal needed includes metal in any incorporated item, including commercially available off-the-shelf items [@CL-SUP-1748].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

Assume an offeror lists one commercial derivative military article, and the Government approves the listing. The article needs 100 kilograms of specialty metal, counting the metal in an incorporated off-the-shelf item, so the first amount is 120 kilograms. The offeror and its subcontractors will buy 300 kilograms of specialty metal in the period for the article and the related commercial article, so the second amount is 150 kilograms. The greater amount is 150 kilograms, so the purchase agreements must cover at least 150 kilograms of domestically melted or produced metal, on the good faith estimate.

## Never confuse

This card covers the clause that names only the United States and its outlying areas [@CL-SUP-1696]. The separate DFARS clause on articles containing specialty metals has its own title; see [Restriction on acquisition of articles containing specialty metals](sup-dfars-specialty-metals-restriction.md). The magnet, tantalum and tungsten restriction is another origin rule; see [Restriction on acquisition of certain magnets, tantalum and tungsten](sup-dfars-magnets-tantalum-tungsten-restriction.md). Anchor chain, forgings and steel plate have their own restrictions; see [Anchor chain, forgings and steel plate restrictions](sup-dfars-anchor-chain-forgings-and-steel-plate-restrictions.md). The engine and vessel component clauses allow a list of countries instead of one place [@CL-SUP-1732; @CL-SUP-1734]; see [Diesel engines, vessel components and overseas contingency operation fuel](sup-dfars-diesel-engines-tao-vessel-components-and-ocs-fuel.md).

Listing an article does not make it a commercial derivative military article, because the certification depends on the Government approving the designation [@CL-SUP-1702; @CL-SUP-1747].
