## Purpose

Shows the melted or produced rule in the specialty metals clause that names only the United States and its outlying areas, which metals it covers, and what an offeror certifies in the commercial derivative military article provision.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-SUP-1696] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 252.225-7008 [@CL-SUP-1696] and DFARS 252.225-7010 [@CL-SUP-1701] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-1696] |
| Applies to | Contracts with the clause where DFARS 225.7003-5(a)(1) prescribes it [@CL-SUP-1764], and solicitations with the provision for offerors that list commercial derivative military articles [@CL-SUP-1701] |

Any specialty metal delivered under the contract shall be melted or produced in the United States or its outlying areas [@CL-SUP-1696]. In the clause, produce means atomization, sputtering, or final consolidation of non-melt derived metal powders [@CL-SUP-1746].

In the clause definition of specialty metal, one alternative is steel whose maximum alloy content exceeds one or more of the manganese, silicon or copper limits it sets [@CL-SUP-1697]. The same steel item has a second alternative, joined by "or": steel containing more than 0.25 percent of any of the named elements [@CL-SUP-1698]. The definition also lists, as further alternatives, nickel or iron-nickel alloys or cobalt alloys above set alloying content [@CL-SUP-1699], titanium and titanium alloys, or zirconium and zirconium alloys [@CL-SUP-1700].

In the provision, the offeror lists any commercial derivative military articles it intends to deliver under any resulting contract using the alternative compliance for commercial derivative military articles, as specified in paragraph (d) of the clause of the solicitation entitled "Restriction on Acquisition of Certain Articles Containing Specialty Metals" (DFARS 252.225-7009), and its designation of an item as a "commercial derivative military article" is subject to Government review and approval in accordance with paragraph (c) of the provision [@CL-SUP-1701; @CL-SUP-0066; @CL-SUP-1747].

## How it works

The restriction sits in the clause and attaches to specialty metal delivered under the contract [@CL-SUP-1696]. The definition decides which steels and alloys count as specialty metal [@CL-SUP-1697; @CL-SUP-1698; @CL-SUP-1699; @CL-SUP-1700]. If the offeror has listed any commercial derivative military articles, it certifies that, if awarded a contract and if the Government approves the designation of the listed items as commercial derivative military articles, the offeror and its subcontractors will demonstrate that, individually or collectively, they have entered into a contractual agreement or agreements to purchase an amount of domestically melted or produced specialty metal in the required form, for use during the period of contract performance in the production of each commercial derivative military article and the related commercial article [@CL-SUP-1702; @CL-SUP-0067; @CL-SUP-1703; @CL-SUP-4059]. The amount is not less than the contractor's good faith estimate of the greater of two amounts: an amount equivalent to 120 percent of the amount of specialty metal that is required to carry out the production of the commercial derivative military article, including the work performed under each subcontract [@CL-SUP-1704; @CL-SUP-0068], or an amount equivalent to 50 percent of the amount of specialty metal that will be purchased by the contractor and its subcontractors for use during such period in the production of the commercial derivative military article and the related commercial article [@CL-SUP-1705]. For the purposes of the provision, the amount of specialty metal that is required to carry out the production of the commercial derivative military article includes specialty metal contained in any item, including commercially available off-the-shelf items, incorporated into such commercial derivative military articles [@CL-SUP-0069; @CL-SUP-1748].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

Assume an offeror lists one commercial derivative military article, and the Government approves the listing. The article needs 100 kilograms of specialty metal, counting the metal in an incorporated off-the-shelf item, so the first amount is 120 kilograms. The offeror and its subcontractors will buy 300 kilograms of specialty metal in the period for the article and the related commercial article, so the second amount is 150 kilograms. The greater amount is 150 kilograms, so the purchase agreements must cover at least 150 kilograms of domestically melted or produced metal, on the good faith estimate.

## Never confuse

The engine and vessel component clauses allow the United States, Australia, Canada, New Zealand, or the United Kingdom [@CL-SUP-1732; @CL-SUP-1734]; see [Diesel engines, vessel components and overseas contingency operation fuel](sup-dfars-diesel-engines-tao-vessel-components-and-ocs-fuel.md) (and see [Restriction on acquisition of articles containing specialty metals](sup-dfars-specialty-metals-restriction.md), [Restriction on acquisition of certain magnets, tantalum and tungsten](sup-dfars-magnets-tantalum-tungsten-restriction.md), and [Anchor chain, forgings and steel plate restrictions](sup-dfars-anchor-chain-forgings-and-steel-plate-restrictions.md)).

The offeror's designation of an item as a commercial derivative military article is subject to Government review and approval, and the certification applies if the offeror is awarded a contract and the Government approves the designation of the listed items as commercial derivative military articles [@CL-SUP-1747; @CL-SUP-1702; @CL-SUP-0067].
