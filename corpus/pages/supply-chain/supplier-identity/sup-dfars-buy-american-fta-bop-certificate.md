## Purpose

Shows what an offeror certifies in the DoD Buy American, Free Trade Agreements and Balance of Payments Program certificate, how it identifies end products that are not domestic, and how the Government evaluates offers. It names each alternate in one sentence and leaves the definitions to the clause card.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-SUP-1847] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 252.225-7035 [@CL-SUP-1847] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-1847] |
| Applies to | Line items subject to the Buy American, Free Trade Agreements and Balance of Payments Program basic clause of the solicitation [@CL-SUP-1850; @CL-SUP-1848] |

For all line items subject to the basic clause of the solicitation, the Offeror certifies that each end product, except the end products it identifies as not domestic, is a domestic end product [@CL-SUP-1850], and it identifies all end products that are not domestic end products [@CL-SUP-1910]. The Offeror lists the line item numbers of domestic end products that contain a critical component or a critical item, with a reference to section 25.105 of the Federal Acquisition Regulation, and certifies that each such domestic end product contains a critical component or a critical item [@CL-SUP-1911; @CL-SUP-1851]. It further certifies that components of unknown origin are considered to have been mined, produced, or manufactured outside the United States or a qualifying country [@CL-SUP-1852].

The Government will evaluate offers in accordance with the policies and procedures of part 225 of the Defense Federal Acquisition Regulation Supplement [@CL-SUP-1847]. For line items subject to the basic clause, it will evaluate offers of qualifying country end products, or Free Trade Agreement country end products other than Bahraini, Moroccan, Panamanian or Peruvian end products, without regard to the restrictions of the Buy American or the Balance of Payments Program [@CL-SUP-1848; @CL-SUP-1849].

## How it works

The terms the provision defines in paragraph (a) have the meanings given in the basic clause of the solicitation [@CL-SUP-1922]. The Offeror shall identify all end products that are not domestic end products [@CL-SUP-1910]. The provision gives three places for them [@CL-SUP-1853; @CL-SUP-1854; @CL-SUP-1855]. The first lists, by line item number and country of origin, the supplies certified as qualifying country end products, except Australian ones [@CL-SUP-1853]. The second lists the supplies certified as Free Trade Agreement country end products other than Bahraini, Moroccan, Panamanian or Peruvian end products [@CL-SUP-1854]. The third lists other foreign end products, including end products manufactured in the United States that do not qualify as domestic end products [@CL-SUP-1855]. For certain foreign end products in the third place, those that do not consist wholly or predominantly of iron or steel or a combination of both, the Offeror also indicates whether they exceed 55 percent domestic content, except those that are COTS items [@CL-SUP-1856], and it selects no where the percentage is unknown [@CL-SUP-1857].

### Alternates

The provision also prints Alternates I to V [@CL-SUP-1858; @CL-SUP-1859; @CL-SUP-1860; @CL-SUP-1912; @CL-SUP-1913]. Alternate I evaluates offers of qualifying country end products without regard to those restrictions [@CL-SUP-1858]. Alternate II adds South Caucasus/Central and South Asian (SC/CASA) state and SC/CASA state end product to paragraph (a) and uses different paragraphs (b)(2) and (c)(2)(i) than the basic provision [@CL-SUP-1859]. Alternate III evaluates offers of qualifying country end products or SC/CASA state end products without regard to them [@CL-SUP-1860]. Alternate IV is the provision prescribed at paragraph (9)(v), adds Korean end product to paragraph (a), and uses Free Trade Agreement country end products other than Bahraini, Korean, Moroccan, Panamanian or Peruvian end products in paragraphs (b)(2) and (c)(2)(ii) [@CL-SUP-1912]. Alternate V evaluates qualifying country, SC/CASA state and Free Trade Agreement end products other than Bahraini, Korean, Moroccan, Panamanian or Peruvian end products without regard to the restrictions of the Buy American statute or the Balance of Payments Program [@CL-SUP-1913; @CL-SUP-3993].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An offer covers four line items under the basic provision. Line item one is made in the United States from mostly domestic components, so the offeror certifies it as a domestic end product. Line item two is made in Germany, a qualifying country, so the offeror lists it with its country of origin in the qualifying country list. Line item three is made in Mexico, so the offeror lists it in the Free Trade Agreement country list. Line item four is made in India. It is on neither list, so the offeror lists it as an other foreign end product; assume it is not mainly iron or steel, is not a COTS item and has an unknown domestic content, so the offeror selects no for exceeding 55 percent. The Government evaluates line items two and three without regard to the restrictions of the Buy American or the Balance of Payments Program.

## Never confuse

The Offeror certifies that each end product, except the end products it identifies as not domestic, is a domestic end product [@CL-SUP-1850; @CL-SUP-1910], and the certificate keeps qualifying country end products and Free Trade Agreement country end products in separate lists [@CL-SUP-1853; @CL-SUP-1854], unlike the basic clause, which sets the delivery duty and definitions on the [basic clause card](sup-dfars-buy-american-fta-bop-clause.md) (for the changes each alternate makes to the clause, see the [alternates card](sup-dfars-buy-american-fta-bop-clause-alternates.md)). For the certificate provision for the DoD clause without Free Trade Agreements, see [DoD Buy American and Balance of Payments Program clause: exceptions, certificate and evaluation of offers](sup-dfars-buy-american-exceptions-certificate-and-evaluation.md). For the FAR certificates, see [Buy American certificates in offers](sup-buy-american-certificates.md), and for the DoD trade agreements pair, see [DoD trade agreements clause and certificate](sup-dfars-trade-agreements-clause-and-certificate.md).
