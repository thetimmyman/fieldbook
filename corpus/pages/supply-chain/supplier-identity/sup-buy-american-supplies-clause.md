## Purpose

Shows what the Buy American supplies clause requires a contractor to deliver, how it defines a domestic end product, a component and a COTS item, and the exception to the delivery rule that the offer can create.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-SUP-1226] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.225-1 [@CL-SUP-1226] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-1226] |
| Applies to | Contracts where the clause is inserted as prescribed in FAR 25.1101(a)(1) [@CL-SUP-1226] |

The contractor shall deliver only domestic end products, except to the extent that it specified delivery of foreign end products in the solicitation provision entitled Buy American Certificate [@CL-SUP-1235]. That offer-side provision is covered on [Buy American certificates in offers](sup-buy-american-certificates.md).

The domestic content test of the Buy American statute is waived for an end product that is a COTS item, except that for an end product that consists wholly or predominantly of iron or steel or a combination of both, the test is applied only to the iron and steel content of the end product, excluding COTS fasteners [@CL-SUP-1234].

A COTS item is, among other listed conditions, an item of supply, including construction material, that is a commercial product as defined in FAR 2.101 [@CL-SUP-1227]. Two further listed conditions are that it is sold in substantial quantities in the commercial marketplace and that it is offered to the Government, at any tier, without modification and in the same form in which it is sold in the commercial marketplace [@CL-SUP-1228]. A COTS item does not include bulk cargo, such as agricultural products and petroleum products [@CL-SUP-1276].

## How it works

A component is an article, material, or supply incorporated directly into an end product [@CL-SUP-1229]. For an end product that does not consist wholly or predominantly of iron or steel or a combination of both, a domestic end product is either an unmanufactured end product mined or produced in the United States or an end product manufactured in the United States that meets a listed condition [@CL-SUP-1230]. One listed condition is a component cost test: the cost of components mined, produced, or manufactured in the United States must exceed 60 percent of the cost of all components, and the percentage is 65 percent for items delivered in calendar years 2024 through 2028 and 75 percent for items delivered starting in calendar year 2029 [@CL-SUP-1231]. Another listed alternative is that the end product is a COTS item [@CL-SUP-1277]. In the definition of domestic end product, components of unknown origin are treated as foreign, and foreign components of a class or kind that the agency finds are not available in sufficient and reasonably available commercial quantities of a satisfactory quality are treated as domestic [@CL-SUP-1233].

For an end product that consists wholly or predominantly of iron or steel or a combination of both, a domestic end product is one manufactured in the United States if the cost of foreign iron and steel constitutes less than 5 percent of the cost of all the components used in the end product [@CL-SUP-1232]. Produced in the United States, for iron or steel, means that all manufacturing processes must take place in the United States, from the initial melting stage through the application of coatings, except metallurgical processes involving refinement of steel additives [@CL-SUP-1278]. Predominantly of iron or steel or a combination of both means that the cost of the iron and steel content exceeds 50 percent of the total cost of all its components [@CL-SUP-1279]. Alternate I of the clause substitutes a sentence for the first sentence of paragraph (1)(ii)(A) of the definition of domestic end product, and that sentence leaves the percentage for the contracting officer to insert [@CL-SUP-1280].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor delivers a manufactured end product in calendar year 2025. The end product is not iron or steel and is not a COTS item. All its components are of known origin, and none is covered by an agency finding of limited availability. Its components cost 100 in total, and 62 of that is for components made in the United States. The percentage in force for deliveries in 2025 is 65, so 62 does not exceed it. The end product is not a domestic end product on the component test, so the contractor may deliver it only if the offer specified delivery of foreign end products in the Buy American Certificate.

## Never confuse

A COTS item is not just any commercial product. Being a commercial product is only the first listed condition, and the item must also meet the sales condition and the unmodified-form condition [@CL-SUP-1227; @CL-SUP-1228]. Bulk cargo such as agricultural products and petroleum products is not a COTS item [@CL-SUP-1276]. For commercial products in subcontracts, see [Subcontracts for commercial products and commercial services](../sourcing-evidence/sup-subcontracts-commercial-products-and-services.md).

This clause is not the Trade Agreements clause or the Free Trade Agreements clause. The Trade Agreements clause delivery rule speaks of U.S.-made or designated country end products rather than domestic end products [@CL-SUP-1253], so see [Trade Agreements clause](sup-trade-agreements-clause.md). The Free Trade Agreements and Israeli Trade Act clause adds trade agreement end products to the delivery choices [@CL-SUP-1249], so see [Buy American, Free Trade Agreements and Israeli Trade Act clause](sup-buy-american-fta-israeli-clause.md). For construction work, the construction materials clause has its own definitions [@CL-SUP-1291], so see [Buy American for construction materials](sup-buy-american-construction-materials.md).
