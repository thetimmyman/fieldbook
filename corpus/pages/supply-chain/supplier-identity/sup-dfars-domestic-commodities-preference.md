## Purpose

Shows which items DoD must receive as grown, reprocessed, reused or produced in the United States under the domestic commodities clause, and the exceptions the clause states.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-SUP-1335] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 252.225-7012 [@CL-SUP-1335] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-1335] |
| Applies to | Items in the listed categories delivered under the contract, as end products or components [@CL-SUP-1335] |

The Contractor shall deliver under the contract only such of the listed items, as end products or components, that have been grown, reprocessed, reused, or produced in the United States, and food is the first listed item [@CL-SUP-1335]. One listed item is clothing and the materials and components of clothing, other than sensors, electronics, or other items added to, and not normally associated with, clothing and the materials and components of clothing [@CL-SUP-1336]. Other listed items include cotton and other natural fiber products, woven silk or woven silk blends, spun silk yarn for cartridge cloth, synthetic fabric and coated synthetic fabric, and canvas products [@CL-SUP-1337]. Among its listed exceptions, the clause does not apply to items listed in section 25.104(a) of the Federal Acquisition Regulation, or to other items for which the Government has determined that a satisfactory quality and sufficient quantity cannot be acquired as and when needed at United States market prices [@CL-SUP-1338]. One listed exception covers incidental amounts of cotton, other natural fibers, or wool incorporated in an end product when their estimated value is not more than 10 percent of the total price of the end product, among other listed conditions [@CL-SUP-1339]. A second condition of that exception is that the estimated value of the cotton, other natural fibers, or wool does not exceed the threshold at DFARS 225.7002-2(a) [@CL-SUP-1371].

## How it works

The duty falls on what is delivered, whether as an end product or as a component [@CL-SUP-1335]. One exception covers items listed in section 25.104(a) of the Federal Acquisition Regulation or other items the Government has determined cannot be acquired as and when needed at United States market prices [@CL-SUP-1338]. Another covers incidental amounts of cotton, other natural fibers, or wool incorporated in an end product when their estimated value is not more than 10 percent of the total price of the end product, among other listed conditions [@CL-SUP-1339; @CL-SUP-1371].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor delivers an end product that contains a small amount of cotton worth 5 on a total end product price of 100. The cotton is 5 percent of the price, so the percentage condition of the incidental amounts exception is met. The contractor must still confirm the other condition, which refers to a DFARS threshold, before relying on the exception.

## Never confuse

The delivery rule of this clause concerns where the listed items were grown, reprocessed, reused, or produced [@CL-SUP-1335]. The 10 percent limit is part of one exception for incidental amounts of fiber and is measured against the total price of the end product [@CL-SUP-1339]. The [DoD Buy American clause](sup-dfars-buy-american-definitions-and-delivery-rule.md) has its own condition on the cost of components for a domestic end product manufactured in the United States [@CL-SUP-1302]. For where specialty metal was melted or produced, see the [DoD specialty metals restriction](sup-dfars-specialty-metals-restriction.md).
