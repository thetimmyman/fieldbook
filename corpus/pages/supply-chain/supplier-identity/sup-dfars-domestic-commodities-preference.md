## Purpose

Shows which items DoD must receive as grown, reprocessed, reused or produced in the United States under the domestic commodities clause, and the exceptions the clause states.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-SUP-1335] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 252.225-7012 [@CL-SUP-1335] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-1335] |
| Applies to | Items in the listed categories delivered under the contract, as end products or components [@CL-SUP-1335] |

The Contractor shall deliver under the contract only such of the listed items, as end products or components, that have been grown, reprocessed, reused, or produced in the United States, and food is the first listed item [@CL-SUP-1335]. One listed item is clothing and the materials and components of clothing, other than sensors, electronics, or other items added to and not normally associated with clothing [@CL-SUP-1336]. Other listed items include cotton and other natural fiber products, woven silk or woven silk blends, spun silk yarn for cartridge cloth, synthetic fabric and coated synthetic fabric, and canvas products [@CL-SUP-1337]. The list has further items that this card does not set out. Among its listed exceptions, the clause does not apply to items listed in section 25.104(a) of the Federal Acquisition Regulation, or to other items for which the Government has determined that a satisfactory quality and sufficient quantity cannot be acquired as and when needed at market prices in the United States [@CL-SUP-1338]. One listed exception covers incidental amounts of cotton, other natural fibers, or wool incorporated in an end product when their estimated value is not more than 10 percent of the total price of the end product, among other listed conditions [@CL-SUP-1339]. A second condition of that exception is that the estimated value of the cotton, other natural fibers, or wool does not exceed the threshold at DFARS 225.7002-2(a) [@CL-SUP-1371]. The clause text does not state that threshold. The clause also lists further exceptions and special rules for fish, shellfish and seafood that this card does not set out.

## How it works

The duty falls on what is delivered, whether as an end product or as a component [@CL-SUP-1335]. The exceptions work by category of item [@CL-SUP-1338] or by quantity of fiber relative to the end product price [@CL-SUP-1339]. The clause adds a definition of qualifying country and other definitions, which this card does not repeat.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor delivers an end product that contains a small amount of cotton worth 5 on a total end product price of 100. The cotton is 5 percent of the price, so the percentage condition of the incidental amounts exception is met. The contractor must still confirm the other condition, which refers to a DFARS threshold, before relying on the exception.

## Never confuse

The delivery rule of this clause asks where listed items were grown, reprocessed, reused or produced, and it has no component-cost test. The only percentage on this card is the price-based limit in one exception for incidental amounts of fiber [@CL-SUP-1339]. The component test in the [DoD Buy American clause](sup-dfars-buy-american-definitions-and-delivery-rule.md) is a cost test on components [@CL-SUP-1302]. The [DoD specialty metals restriction](sup-dfars-specialty-metals-restriction.md) is a separate clause that looks at where specialty metal was melted or produced.
