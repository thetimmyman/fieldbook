## Purpose

Shows when a contractor may not subcontract with a debarred, suspended or proposed-for-debarment party, and the disclosure, notice and flowdown steps around it.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-SUP-0558] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.209-6 [@CL-SUP-0558]; FAR 2.101 [@CL-SUP-9581; @CL-SUP-9582; @CL-SUP-9583] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.209-6 [@CL-SUP-3638; @CL-SUP-3639] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-0558] |
| Applies to | Contracts with the clause inserted as prescribed in FAR 9.409, other than for subcontracts for commercially available off-the-shelf items [@CL-SUP-0689; @CL-SUP-0690] |

Under paragraph (b), the contractor shall not enter into any subcontract, in excess of the threshold specified in FAR 9.405-2(b) on the date of subcontract award, with a contractor that is debarred, suspended, or proposed for debarment by any executive agency unless there is a compelling reason to do so [@CL-SUP-0558]. The prohibition does not apply to a subcontract for a commercially available off-the-shelf item [@CL-SUP-0690].

The codified FAR definitions section defines a commercially available off-the-shelf (COTS) item as any item of supply (including construction material) that is a commercial product as defined in paragraph (1) of the definition of commercial product [@CL-SUP-9581], is sold in substantial quantities in the commercial marketplace, and is offered to the Government, under a contract or subcontract at any tier, without modification, in the same form in which it is sold in the commercial marketplace [@CL-SUP-9582]. A COTS item does not include bulk cargo, as defined in section 40102(4) of title 46 of the United States Code, such as agricultural products and petroleum products [@CL-SUP-9583].

## How it works

The contractor shall require each proposed subcontractor whose subcontract will exceed the threshold specified in FAR 9.405-2(b) on the date of subcontract award, other than a subcontractor providing a commercially available off-the-shelf item, to disclose to the contractor, in writing [@CL-SUP-0559], whether as of the time of award the subcontractor or its principals is or is not debarred, suspended, proposed for debarment, or voluntarily excluded by the Federal Government [@CL-SUP-0691]. The contractor shall notify the contracting officer, in writing, before entering into a subcontract with a party, other than a subcontractor providing a commercially available off-the-shelf item, that is debarred, suspended, proposed for debarment, or voluntarily excluded [@CL-SUP-0560]. The notification is given by a corporate officer or a designee of the contractor [@CL-SUP-0682]. The notice must include the name of the subcontractor and the contractor's knowledge of the reasons for the listing with an exclusion in SAM [@CL-SUP-0692], and the compelling reasons for doing business with the subcontractor despite the listing [@CL-SUP-0712]. It must also include the systems and procedures the contractor has established to ensure that it is fully protecting the Government's interests when dealing with the subcontractor in view of the specific basis for the party's exclusion [@CL-SUP-0693]. Unless the prime contract is for the acquisition of commercial products or commercial services, the contractor shall include the requirements of the clause in each subcontract that meets the conditions in paragraph (e) [@CL-SUP-0561], that is, each subcontract that exceeds the threshold and is not for commercially available off-the-shelf items [@CL-SUP-0694].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text from February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. RFO 52.209-6(b) bars a subcontract above the threshold in FAR 9.405-2(b) on the date of subcontract award with a contractor that is debarred, suspended, or proposed for debarment by any executive agency unless a compelling reason exists to do so [@CL-SUP-3638]. The substance is the same as the codified sentence, but the wording differs: the RFO text says "unless a compelling reason exists" where the codified sentence says "unless there is a compelling reason" [@CL-SUP-3638; @CL-SUP-0558]. RFO 52.209-6(b) keeps the exception for a commercially available off-the-shelf item [@CL-SUP-3639; @CL-SUP-0690]. Paragraph (c) keeps the written disclosure by each proposed subcontractor above the threshold, other than a provider of a commercially available off-the-shelf item [@CL-SUP-3640; @CL-SUP-3641; @CL-SUP-0559; @CL-SUP-0691]. Paragraph (d) keeps the written notice to the contracting officer by a corporate officer or a designee before entering into a subcontract with a party, other than a subcontractor providing a commercially available off-the-shelf item, that is debarred, suspended, proposed for debarment or voluntarily excluded [@CL-SUP-3642; @CL-SUP-3643; @CL-SUP-0560; @CL-SUP-0682], and keeps the notice contents: the subcontractor's name and the contractor's knowledge of the reasons for the listing [@CL-SUP-3644; @CL-SUP-0692], the compelling reasons [@CL-SUP-3645; @CL-SUP-0712], and the systems and procedures [@CL-SUP-3646; @CL-SUP-0693]. Paragraph (e) keeps the flowdown, unless the prime contract is for commercial products or commercial services, to each subcontract that exceeds the threshold and is not for commercially available off-the-shelf items [@CL-SUP-3647; @CL-SUP-3648; @CL-SUP-0561; @CL-SUP-0694].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A prime contractor plans a 150,000 subcontract for technical services, which is above the threshold. The proposed supplier discloses in writing that it was recently proposed for debarment. The contractor has a compelling technical reason to proceed.

Answer: the contractor may enter the subcontract because it has a compelling reason. A corporate officer or designee must first notify the contracting officer in writing, giving the supplier's name, what the contractor knows of the reasons, the compelling reasons, and the protective systems and procedures.

## Never confuse

The prohibition and the notice duty cover different groups: the prohibition names parties that are debarred, suspended, or proposed for debarment [@CL-SUP-0558], unlike the disclosure and notice steps, which also reach voluntarily excluded parties [@CL-SUP-0691; @CL-SUP-0560]. A compelling reason is an exception to the prohibition, alongside the exception for a subcontract for a commercially available off-the-shelf item [@CL-SUP-0558; @CL-SUP-0690], but the written notice must still be given before entering into a subcontract with a listed party, other than a subcontractor providing a commercially available off-the-shelf item [@CL-SUP-0560]. The written disclosure asks whether the subcontractor or its principals are excluded [@CL-SUP-0691], but the prohibition applies when the subcontractor itself is debarred, suspended, or proposed for debarment [@CL-SUP-0558], and the notice also when it is voluntarily excluded [@CL-SUP-0560]. Consent and purchasing system topics are on the [Subcontracts: consent and advance notification](../procure-to-pay/sup-subcontracts-consent-advance-notification.md) and [Contractor purchasing system](../procure-to-pay/sup-contractor-purchasing-system.md) cards.
