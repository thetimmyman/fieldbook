## Purpose

Shows when a contractor may not subcontract with a debarred, suspended or proposed-for-debarment party, and the disclosure, notice and flowdown steps around it.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-SUP-0558] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.209-6 [@CL-SUP-0558] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-0558] |
| Applies to | Contracts with the clause inserted as prescribed in FAR 9.409, other than for subcontracts for commercially available off-the-shelf items [@CL-SUP-0689; @CL-SUP-0690] |

Under paragraph (b), the contractor shall not enter into any subcontract, in excess of the threshold specified in FAR 9.405-2(b) on the date of subcontract award, with a contractor that is debarred, suspended, or proposed for debarment by any executive agency unless there is a compelling reason to do so [@CL-SUP-0558]. The prohibition does not apply to a subcontract for a commercially available off-the-shelf item [@CL-SUP-0690].

## How it works

The contractor shall require each proposed subcontractor whose subcontract will exceed the threshold specified in FAR 9.405-2(b) on the date of subcontract award, other than a subcontractor providing a commercially available off-the-shelf item, to disclose to the contractor, in writing [@CL-SUP-0559], whether as of the time of award the subcontractor or its principals is or is not debarred, suspended, proposed for debarment, or voluntarily excluded by the Federal Government [@CL-SUP-0691]. The contractor shall notify the contracting officer, in writing, before entering into a subcontract with a party, other than a subcontractor providing a commercially available off-the-shelf item, that is debarred, suspended, proposed for debarment, or voluntarily excluded [@CL-SUP-0560]. The notification is given by a corporate officer or a designee of the contractor [@CL-SUP-0682]. The notice must include the name of the subcontractor, the contractor's knowledge of the reasons for the listing with an exclusion in SAM, and the compelling reasons for doing business with the subcontractor despite the listing [@CL-SUP-0692]. It must also include the systems and procedures the contractor has established to protect the Government's interests when dealing with the subcontractor [@CL-SUP-0693]. Unless the prime contract is for the acquisition of commercial products or commercial services, the contractor shall include the requirements of the clause in each subcontract that meets the conditions in paragraph (e) [@CL-SUP-0561], that is, each subcontract that exceeds the threshold and is not for commercially available off-the-shelf items [@CL-SUP-0694].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A prime contractor plans a 150,000 subcontract for technical services, which is above the threshold. The proposed supplier discloses in writing that its principal was recently proposed for debarment. The contractor has a compelling technical reason to proceed.

Answer: the contractor may enter the subcontract only because it has a compelling reason. A corporate officer or designee must first notify the contracting officer in writing, giving the supplier's name, what the contractor knows of the reasons, the compelling reasons, and the protective systems and procedures.

## Never confuse

The prohibition and the notice duty cover different groups. The prohibition names parties that are debarred, suspended, or proposed for debarment [@CL-SUP-0558], while the disclosure and notice steps also reach voluntarily excluded parties [@CL-SUP-0691; @CL-SUP-0560]. A compelling reason lets the contractor proceed only after the written notice [@CL-SUP-0558; @CL-SUP-0560]. Consent and purchasing system topics are on the [Subcontracts: consent and advance notification](../procure-to-pay/sup-subcontracts-consent-advance-notification.md) and [Contractor purchasing system](../procure-to-pay/sup-contractor-purchasing-system.md) cards.
