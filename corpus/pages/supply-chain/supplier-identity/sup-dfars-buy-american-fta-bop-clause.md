## Purpose

Shows the key definitions in the basic DoD Buy American, Free Trade Agreements and Balance of Payments Program clause, which end products the Contractor may deliver, and the domestic content thresholds.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-SUP-1861] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 252.225-7036 [@CL-SUP-1861] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-1861] |
| Applies to | All items in the Schedule, unless otherwise specified [@CL-SUP-1902] |

Under the basic clause the Contractor shall deliver only domestic end products unless, in its offer, it specified delivery of qualifying country end products, Free Trade Agreement country end products other than Bahraini, Moroccan, Panamanian or Peruvian end products, or other foreign end products [@CL-SUP-3994; @CL-SUP-1861]. The second delivery sentence applies if the Contractor certified in its offer that it will deliver a qualifying country end product or such a Free Trade Agreement country end product [@CL-SUP-1862]. It requires the Contractor to deliver a qualifying country end product, such a Free Trade Agreement country end product or, at its option, a domestic end product [@CL-SUP-1937; @CL-SUP-1863].

The contract price does not include duty for end products or components for which the Contractor will claim duty-free entry [@CL-SUP-1901].

## How it works

### Definitions

A foreign end product is an end product other than a domestic end product [@CL-SUP-1900]. For an end product that does not consist wholly or predominantly of iron or steel or a combination of both, a domestic end product is an unmanufactured end product mined or produced in the United States, or an end product manufactured in the United States if listed conditions are met [@CL-SUP-1897]. An alternative to the component cost condition is that the end product is a COTS item [@CL-SUP-1925].

An end product that consists wholly or predominantly of iron or steel or a combination of both is, by one listed alternative, a domestic end product if it is manufactured in the United States and the cost of iron and steel not produced in the United States or a qualifying country constitutes less than 5 percent of the cost of all the components [@CL-SUP-1867]. Iron or steel is produced in the United States or a qualifying country only if all its manufacturing processes take place in the United States or a qualifying country, except metallurgical processes involving refinement of steel additives [@CL-SUP-1929]. The cost of iron and steel not produced there includes, but is not limited to, the cost of iron or steel mill products, castings or forgings not produced there [@CL-SUP-1930]. The mill products, castings or forgings counted are those used in the manufacture of the end product, and the cost also includes a good faith estimate of the cost of all iron or steel components not produced there, excluding COTS fasteners; iron or steel components of unknown origin are treated as foreign [@CL-SUP-1931]. Predominantly of iron or steel means that the cost of the iron and steel content exceeds 50 percent of the total cost of all its components [@CL-SUP-1868]. For that definition, the cost of iron and steel is the cost of the iron or steel mill products, castings or forgings used in the manufacture of the product plus a good faith estimate of the cost of iron or steel components, excluding COTS fasteners [@CL-SUP-1932].

A qualifying country is a country with a reciprocal defense procurement memorandum of understanding or international agreement with the United States in which both countries agree to remove barriers to purchases of supplies produced in the other country [@CL-SUP-1898]. The definition also covers services performed by sources of the other country, and requires the memorandum or agreement to comply, where applicable, with section 36 of the Arms Export Control Act and with section 2457 of title 10 of the United States Code [@CL-SUP-1928]. The clause then lists the qualifying countries by name, from Australia to the United Kingdom of Great Britain and Northern Ireland [@CL-SUP-1899]. It defines a Free Trade Agreement country as Australia, Bahrain, Chile, Colombia, Costa Rica, Dominican Republic, El Salvador, Guatemala, Honduras, Korea (Republic of), Mexico, Morocco, Nicaragua, Panama, Peru, or Singapore [@CL-SUP-1869].

### Domestic content thresholds

For an end product that does not consist wholly or predominantly of iron or steel or a combination of both, one condition for an end product manufactured in the United States is that the cost of its qualifying country components and its components mined, produced, or manufactured in the United States exceeds 60 percent of the cost of all its components, except that the percentage will be 65 percent in the cases the rest of the sentence states [@CL-SUP-1864]. The percentage will be 65 percent for items delivered in calendar years 2024 through 2028 and 75 percent for items delivered starting in calendar year 2029, unless an alternate percentage is established for a contract in accordance with DFARS 225.101(d) [@CL-SUP-1865]. The same sentence refers to an award made before January 1, 2030, for a foreign end product that exceeds 55 percent domestic content [@CL-SUP-1866]. The cost of components includes transportation costs to the place of incorporation into the end product and United States duty, whether or not a duty-free entry certificate is issued [@CL-SUP-1923]. Components of unknown origin are treated as foreign [@CL-SUP-1924]. A component is considered to have been mined, produced, or manufactured in the United States if the end product in which it is incorporated is manufactured in the United States and the component is of a class or kind for which the Government has made one of the determinations that the definition lists [@CL-SUP-1926]. The listed determinations are that sufficient and reasonably available commercial quantities of a satisfactory quality are not mined, produced, or manufactured in the United States, or that it is inconsistent with the public interest to apply the restrictions of the Buy American statute [@CL-SUP-1927].

One listed condition for an end product manufactured in a qualifying country is that the cost of the listed types of components exceeds 60 percent of the cost of all its components, except that the percentage will be 65 percent in the cases the rest of the sentence states [@CL-SUP-1870]. The types counted include components mined, produced, or manufactured in a qualifying country and components mined, produced, or manufactured in the United States [@CL-SUP-1933], and also components of foreign origin of a class or kind for which the Government has determined that sufficient and reasonably available commercial quantities of a satisfactory quality are not mined, produced, or manufactured in the United States, with components of unknown origin treated as foreign [@CL-SUP-1934]. The percentage will be 65 percent for items delivered in calendar years 2024 through 2028 and 75 percent for items delivered starting in calendar year 2029, unless an alternate percentage is established for a contract [@CL-SUP-1935]. The alternative to that condition is that the end product is a COTS item [@CL-SUP-1936].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A product is made in the United States and is not mainly iron or steel. Its components cost 1,000 in total, counting transportation costs to the place of incorporation and United States duty. Assume the origin of every component is known and the product is not a COTS item. Components from the United States cost 500 and components from a qualifying country cost 200, so together they are 70 percent of the cost of all components. Assume the percentage for the delivery year is 65 percent. The product passes the component test and is a domestic end product. If the offer had instead specified a qualifying country end product and the contractor certified it, the contractor would deliver a qualifying country end product or, at its option, a domestic end product.

## Never confuse

The delivery paragraph names qualifying country end products and Free Trade Agreement country end products separately [@CL-SUP-1861]. Australia appears in both the list of qualifying countries and the definition of Free Trade Agreement country [@CL-SUP-1899; @CL-SUP-1869]. The carve-out for Bahraini, Moroccan, Panamanian and Peruvian end products is stated in the Free Trade Agreement country end product group [@CL-SUP-1861]. For the alternates, see the [alternates card](sup-dfars-buy-american-fta-bop-clause-alternates.md); for the offeror's side, see the [certificate card](sup-dfars-buy-american-fta-bop-certificate.md).

For the older DoD Buy American clause without Free Trade Agreements, see [DoD Buy American and Balance of Payments Program clause: definitions and delivery rule](sup-dfars-buy-american-definitions-and-delivery-rule.md) and [its exceptions, certificate and evaluation card](sup-dfars-buy-american-exceptions-certificate-and-evaluation.md). For the FAR clause with the Israeli Trade Act, see [Buy American, Free Trade Agreements and Israeli Trade Act clause](sup-buy-american-fta-israeli-clause.md), and for the DoD trade agreements clause, see [DoD trade agreements clause and certificate](sup-dfars-trade-agreements-clause-and-certificate.md). For claiming duty-free entry, see [Duty-free entry clause](sup-dfars-duty-free-entry.md).
