## Purpose

Shows how a proposed debarment is weighed and decided: the general rules, the mitigating factors, the contractor's response, fact-finding and the timing of the decision.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-SUP-0917] |
| Codified citation | FAR 9.406-1 and FAR 9.406-3 [@CL-SUP-0917; @CL-SUP-0921] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-0917] |
| Applies to | Debarment proposals decided by the suspending and debarring official [@CL-SUP-0923; @CL-SUP-0920] |

The existence of a cause for debarment does not necessarily require debarment, and the seriousness of the acts or omissions and any remedial measures, mitigating factors or aggravating factors should be considered in making any debarment decision [@CL-SUP-0917]. If a cause for debarment exists, the contractor has the burden of demonstrating, to the satisfaction of the suspending and debarring official, its present responsibility and that debarment is not necessary [@CL-SUP-0920]. The causes are on the [Causes for debarment](sup-causes-for-debarment.md) card and the period and scope are on the [Period and scope of debarment](sup-debarment-period-and-scope.md) card; neither is restated here.

The deadlines and the standard of proof in the printed sections are these. The notice of proposed debarment states that the contractor may submit information and argument in opposition within 30 days after receipt of the notice [@CL-SUP-0921; @CL-SUP-0982]. If no suspension is in effect, the decision shall be made within 45 days from the date that the official administrative record is closed, unless the suspending and debarring official extends this period for good cause [@CL-SUP-0924]. That sentence sits in the paragraph that provides for a decision on the administrative record in actions based upon a conviction or civil judgment, or in which there is no genuine dispute over material facts [@CL-SUP-0923]. The official record closes upon the expiration of the contractor's time to submit information and argument in opposition, including any extensions [@CL-SUP-0925]. In any action in which the proposed debarment is not based upon a conviction or civil judgment, the cause for debarment must be established by a preponderance of the evidence [@CL-SUP-0983].

In debarment actions in which additional proceedings are necessary as to disputed material facts, written findings of fact shall be prepared and the suspending and debarring official shall base the decision on the facts as found [@CL-SUP-0984]. The printed section sets no number of days for the decision in those actions.

## How it works

Before arriving at any debarment decision, the suspending and debarring official should consider factors such as those in the list that follows [@CL-SUP-0918]. One listed factor is whether the contractor had effective standards of conduct and internal control systems in place at the time of the activity that constitutes cause for debarment, or had adopted such procedures before any Government investigation of that activity [@CL-SUP-0919].

The notice of proposed debarment shall state, among other listed items, that debarment is being considered and the reasons for the proposed debarment in terms sufficient to put the contractor on notice of the conduct or transactions upon which it is based [@CL-SUP-0982]. In actions not based upon a conviction or civil judgment, if the contractor's submission in opposition raises a genuine dispute over facts material to the proposed debarment, agencies shall also afford the contractor an opportunity to appear with counsel [@CL-SUP-0922]. In actions based upon a conviction or civil judgment, or in which there is no genuine dispute over material facts, the suspending and debarring official shall make a decision on the basis of all the information in the administrative record, including any submission made by the contractor [@CL-SUP-0923].

If the suspending and debarring official decides to impose debarment, the contractor and any affiliates involved shall be given prompt notice, using the notice procedures cross-referenced there, that refers to the notice of proposed debarment, among other listed items [@CL-SUP-0985]. If debarment is not imposed, the official shall promptly notify the contractor and any affiliates involved, using the notice procedures cross-referenced there [@CL-SUP-0986].

The paragraph on administrative agreements and the paragraph on voluntary exclusions each provide that the suspending and debarring official is responsible for the timely and accurate submission of documentation reflecting the agreement or exclusion, and that the submission should be made within 3 working days [@CL-SUP-0987; @CL-SUP-0988].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor receives a notice of proposed debarment. It files a written response on day 20 with proof of an internal control program adopted before any investigation. Its time to respond ends on day 30, and the record closes then. No suspension is in effect and there is no genuine dispute over facts.

Answer: the official decides within 45 days after day 30, which is by day 75, unless the official extends the period for good cause.

## Never confuse

A notice of proposed debarment invites the contractor's opposition within 30 days after receipt [@CL-SUP-0921], while a suspended contractor is invited to respond following the imposition of suspension [@CL-SUP-0928]. A cause for debarment is not itself the decision; the official weighs the factors above [@CL-SUP-0917]. Suspension has its own causes on the [Causes for suspension](sup-causes-for-suspension.md) card, and its procedures are on the [Suspension: general rules, procedures and the decision](sup-suspension-decision-process.md) card.
