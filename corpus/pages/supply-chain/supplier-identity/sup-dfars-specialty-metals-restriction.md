## Purpose

Shows the melted or produced rule for specialty metals in items delivered to DoD, the main exceptions, the alternative for commercial derivative military articles, and the flowdown to subcontracts.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-SUP-1324] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 252.225-7009 [@CL-SUP-1324] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-1324] |
| Applies to | Items delivered under the contract that incorporate specialty metals [@CL-SUP-1324] |

Except as provided in the exceptions paragraph, any specialty metals incorporated in items delivered under the contract shall be melted or produced in the United States, its outlying areas, or a qualifying country [@CL-SUP-1324]. The restriction does not apply to electronic components, which are the first listed exception [@CL-SUP-1325]. One listed exception covers commercially available off-the-shelf items other than the listed carve-outs, the first of which is specialty metal mill products that have not been incorporated into COTS end items, subsystems, assemblies, or components [@CL-SUP-1327].

Another listed exception is for end items containing a minimal amount of otherwise noncompliant specialty metals, meaning specialty metals not melted or produced in the United States, an outlying area, or a qualifying country and not covered by one of the other exceptions in the exceptions paragraph [@CL-SUP-1361]. The minimal content exception applies if the total weight of the noncompliant specialty metals does not exceed 2 percent of the total weight of all specialty metals in the end item, as estimated in good faith by the Contractor, and it does not apply to high performance magnets containing specialty metals [@CL-SUP-1326].

As an alternative to the compliance required by the restriction paragraph, the Contractor may purchase an amount of domestically melted or produced specialty metals in the required form, for use during the period of contract performance in the production of the commercial derivative military article and the related commercial article, if both of the conditions that the same paragraph lists are met [@CL-SUP-1362; @CL-SUP-9738]. One condition is that the Contracting Officer has notified the Contractor of the items to be delivered that the Government has determined to meet the definition of commercial derivative military article [@CL-SUP-1363]. Another condition is that, for each item the Government has determined to meet the definition, the Contractor has certified in the solicitation provision entitled Commercial Derivative Military Article—Specialty Metals Compliance Certificate that it and its subcontractors will enter into a contractual agreement or agreements to purchase an amount of domestically melted or produced specialty metal in the required form, for use during the period of contract performance in the production of each commercial derivative military article and the related commercial article [@CL-SUP-1364; @CL-SUP-9739; @CL-SUP-9740]. The amount of domestically melted or produced specialty metal under the certified agreements is to be not less than the Contractor's good faith estimate of the greater of two amounts, the first being an amount equivalent to 120 percent of the specialty metal required to produce the commercial derivative military article, including work under each subcontract [@CL-SUP-1365]. The second of the two amounts is an amount equivalent to 50 percent of the specialty metal that the Contractor and its subcontractors will purchase during the period for the commercial derivative military article and the related commercial article [@CL-SUP-1366]. For the purposes of this alternative, the amount of specialty metal that is required to carry out production of the commercial derivative military article includes specialty metal contained in any item, including COTS items [@CL-SUP-0065].

## How it works

The restriction attaches to the specialty metal in each delivered item, and the allowed places are the United States, its outlying areas or a qualifying country [@CL-SUP-1324]. The exceptions remove whole categories of items, such as electronic components and commercially available off-the-shelf items with carve-outs, or small quantities by weight [@CL-SUP-1325; @CL-SUP-1327; @CL-SUP-1326]. The Contractor inserts the definitions, restriction and exceptions paragraphs, with the flowdown paragraph itself, in subcontracts, including subcontracts for commercial products, that are for items containing specialty metals; in doing so it modifies paragraph (c)(6) only as necessary to facilitate management of the minimal content exception at the prime contract level (the minimal content exception does not apply to specialty metals contained in high-performance magnets), and does not further alter the clause other than to identify the appropriate parties [@CL-SUP-1328; @CL-SUP-9669; @CL-SUP-9670]. When flowing the clause down, the Contractor excludes and reserves the commercial derivative military article paragraph [@CL-SUP-1367].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An end item holds 200 kilograms of specialty metals in total. Three kilograms of that metal was melted outside the United States, its outlying areas and every qualifying country, and no other exception covers it. The three kilograms are 1.5 percent of the total, and the end item contains no high performance magnets. The minimal content exception covers the item, because 1.5 percent is under the 2 percent limit.

## Never confuse

The restriction on melting and producing samarium-cobalt magnets is in addition to any applicable restrictions on melting of specialty metals if this clause is included in the contract [@CL-SUP-1370], as covered on [Restriction on acquisition of certain magnets, tantalum and tungsten](sup-dfars-magnets-tantalum-tungsten-restriction.md) (see also [DoD Buy American clause](sup-dfars-buy-american-definitions-and-delivery-rule.md) and [Preference for certain domestic commodities](sup-dfars-domestic-commodities-preference.md)). If a contract includes this clause, all bearings that contain specialty metals must meet its requirements [@CL-SUP-1373], as covered on [Ball and roller bearings and items from Communist Chinese military companies](sup-dfars-bearings-and-communist-chinese-military-companies.md) (see also [Subcontracts for commercial products and commercial services](../sourcing-evidence/sup-subcontracts-commercial-products-and-services.md) and [Contractor purchasing system](../procure-to-pay/sup-contractor-purchasing-system.md)).
