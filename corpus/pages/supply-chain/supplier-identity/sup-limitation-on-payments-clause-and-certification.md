## Purpose

Shows what an offeror certifies and discloses about payments to influence a Federal award, what the Contractor may not pay for with appropriated funds, and how the duty reaches subcontractors.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-SUP-1001; @CL-SUP-1004] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.203-11 [@CL-SUP-1001; @CL-SUP-1002; @CL-SUP-1056] and FAR 52.203-12 [@CL-SUP-1004; @CL-SUP-1006; @CL-SUP-1053] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-1001] |
| Applies to | Offerors, for the provision, and the Contractor, for the clause [@CL-SUP-1001; @CL-SUP-1004] |

By signing its offer, the offeror certifies to the best of its knowledge and belief that no Federal appropriated funds have been paid or will be paid to any person for influencing or attempting to influence an officer or employee of any agency, among other persons the provision lists [@CL-SUP-1001]. In the clause, the Contractor shall not use appropriated funds to pay any person for influencing or attempting to influence the listed Government and Congressional personnel in connection with the award of this contract [@CL-SUP-1004]. The clause also bars such payments in connection with the extension, continuation, renewal, amendment, or modification of the contract [@CL-SUP-1051].

If any registrants under the Lobbying Disclosure Act of 1995 have made a lobbying contact on behalf of the offeror with respect to this contract, the offeror must complete and submit OMB Standard Form LLL with its offer to provide the name of the registrants [@CL-SUP-1002]. The offeror need not report regularly employed officers or employees of the offeror to whom payments of reasonable compensation were made [@CL-SUP-1003]. In the clause, regularly employed is defined, for an officer or employee of a person requesting or receiving a Federal contract, as employed by that person for at least 130 working days within 1 year immediately preceding the date of the submission that initiates agency consideration [@CL-SUP-1052].

## How it works

The provision is the offer-stage step: a signed certification, plus a disclosure form when registered lobbyists have made a contact for the offeror. Submission of the certification and disclosure in the provision is a prerequisite for making or entering into the contract, as imposed by section 1352 of title 31 of the United States Code [@CL-SUP-1056]. The clause carries the prohibition into performance. Any person who makes an expenditure prohibited under the clause, or who fails to file or amend the disclosure the clause requires, shall be subject to civil penalties as provided for by section 1352 of title 31 of the United States Code [@CL-SUP-1053]. An imposition of a civil penalty under the clause does not prevent the Government from seeking any other remedy that may be applicable [@CL-SUP-1054]. For subcontracts, the Contractor shall include the substance of the clause in any subcontract that exceeds the threshold specified in FAR 3.808 on the date of subcontract award [@CL-SUP-1006]. The Contractor or subcontractor that awards a subcontract shall retain the declaration required from each person requesting or receiving a subcontract under this contract that exceeds that threshold [@CL-SUP-1055]. The source does not establish the amount of that threshold, because the clause points to FAR 3.808 without stating a figure [@CL-SUP-1006]. The prime Contractor shall, at the end of the calendar quarter in which a subcontractor submits its disclosure form, submit to the Contracting Officer within 30 days a copy of all disclosures [@CL-SUP-1005].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A company submits an offer and learns that a registered lobbyist contacted an agency official about the solicitation on its behalf. It completes the disclosure form and submits it with the offer. After award, it plans a subcontract that is above the threshold named in the clause. The answer: the prime obtains a declaration from the subcontractor, includes the substance of the clause in the subcontract, and forwards a copy of the disclosure form to the Contracting Officer after the quarter in which the subcontractor submits it.

## Never confuse

The prohibition on paying for influence with appropriated funds is a separate matter from the cost-allowability rule for lobbying costs. For allowability, see [Lobbying and political activity costs](../../compliance/cas-allowability/cmp-lobbying-and-political-activity-costs.md). For the FAR subpart that prescribes these texts, see [Limitation on payments to influence Federal transactions](../../compliance/cas-allowability/cmp-limitation-on-payments-to-influence-federal-transactions.md).
