## Purpose

Shows the domestic construction material rule, the exceptions and the cost comparison, and when an offeror should request a determination.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-SUP-1255; @CL-SUP-1260] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.225-9 [@CL-SUP-1255]; FAR 52.225-10 [@CL-SUP-1260] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.225-9 [@CL-SUP-3565; @CL-SUP-3566; @CL-SUP-3567; @CL-SUP-3568; @CL-SUP-3569; @CL-SUP-3570; @CL-SUP-3571; @CL-SUP-3572; @CL-SUP-3573; @CL-SUP-3574]; RFO FAR 52.225-10 [@CL-SUP-3575; @CL-SUP-3576; @CL-SUP-3577; @CL-SUP-3578] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-1255; @CL-SUP-1260] |
| Applies to | Contracts that include the clause [@CL-SUP-1255]; solicitations that include the provision [@CL-SUP-1260] |

The contractor shall use only domestic construction material in performing the contract, except as provided in paragraphs (b)(2) and (b)(3) of the clause [@CL-SUP-1255]. The requirement does not apply to information technology that is a commercial product or to the construction materials or components listed by the Government in the clause [@CL-SUP-1289].

For construction material that does not consist wholly or predominantly of iron or steel or a combination of both, domestic construction material is either an unmanufactured construction material mined or produced in the United States or a construction material manufactured in the United States that meets a listed condition [@CL-SUP-1291]. One listed condition is that the cost of its components mined, produced, or manufactured in the United States exceeds 60 percent of the cost of all its components, with the percentage raised to 65 percent for items delivered in calendar years 2024 through 2028 and to 75 percent for items delivered starting in calendar year 2029 [@CL-SUP-1254]. Components of foreign origin of the same class or kind for which nonavailability determinations have been made are treated as domestic, and components of unknown origin are treated as foreign [@CL-SUP-3974].

An offeror requesting a determination that the Buy American statute is inapplicable should submit the request to the Contracting Officer in time to allow a determination before submission of offers [@CL-SUP-1260]. Alternate I of the provision instead has the offeror submit the request with its offer [@CL-SUP-1292]. An offeror that has not requested a determination before submitting its offer, or has not received a response to a previous request, shall include the information and supporting data in the offer [@CL-SUP-1290].

## How it works

The Contracting Officer may add other foreign construction material to the list of excepted materials if the Government determines, among other listed grounds, that the cost of domestic construction material would be unreasonable [@CL-SUP-1256]. Another listed ground is that applying the restriction of the Buy American statute to a particular construction material would be impracticable or inconsistent with the public interest [@CL-SUP-1288]. A third listed ground, joined to the second by the word or [@CL-SUP-3975], is that the construction material is not mined, produced, or manufactured in the United States in sufficient and reasonably available commercial quantities of a satisfactory quality [@CL-SUP-1259].

One listed unreasonable-cost paragraph applies to domestic construction material that is not a critical item or does not contain critical components [@CL-SUP-1257], and one listed test in it treats the cost as unreasonable when it exceeds the cost of foreign material by more than 20 percent [@CL-SUP-1258]. A listed test for a domestic construction material that is a critical item or contains critical components treats the cost as unreasonable when it exceeds the cost of foreign material by more than 20 percent plus the additional preference factor identified for the critical item [@CL-SUP-1287]. Under the notice provision, the Government will evaluate an offer requesting exception, based on claimed unreasonable cost, by adding to the offered price the appropriate percentage of the cost of such foreign construction material, as specified in paragraph (b)(3)(i) of the clause at FAR 52.225-9 [@CL-SUP-1261; @CL-SUP-9364].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text from February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. RFO FAR 52.225-9 keeps the rule that the contractor shall use only domestic construction material in performing the contract, except as provided in paragraphs (b)(2) and (b)(3) of the clause [@CL-SUP-3565; @CL-SUP-1255]. It keeps the exception for information technology that is a commercial product and for the construction materials or components listed by the Government in the clause [@CL-SUP-3566; @CL-SUP-1289]. It keeps the definition of domestic construction material for material that does not consist wholly or predominantly of iron or steel or a combination of both [@CL-SUP-3567; @CL-SUP-1291], including the 60 percent component cost condition with its 65 percent and 75 percent steps [@CL-SUP-3568; @CL-SUP-1254]. It keeps the grounds on which the Contracting Officer may add foreign construction material to the list: that the cost of domestic construction material would be unreasonable [@CL-SUP-3569; @CL-SUP-1256], that applying the Buy American statute restriction to a particular construction material would be impracticable or inconsistent with the public interest [@CL-SUP-3570; @CL-SUP-1288], and that the construction material is not mined, produced, or manufactured in the United States in sufficient and reasonably available commercial quantities of a satisfactory quality [@CL-SUP-3571; @CL-SUP-1259]. It keeps the unreasonable-cost test of more than 20 percent [@CL-SUP-3572; @CL-SUP-1258] and, for a domestic construction material that is a critical item or contains critical components, the test of more than 20 percent plus the additional preference factor [@CL-SUP-3573; @CL-SUP-3574; @CL-SUP-1287]. RFO FAR 52.225-10 keeps the rule that an offeror requesting a determination should submit the request to the Contracting Officer in time to allow a determination before submission of offers [@CL-SUP-3575; @CL-SUP-1260], and the rule that an offeror that has not requested a determination before submitting its offer, or has not received a response to a previous request, shall include the information and supporting data in the offer [@CL-SUP-3576; @CL-SUP-1290]. Its Alternate I keeps the rule that the request is submitted with the offer [@CL-SUP-3577; @CL-SUP-1292]. It keeps the evaluation of an offer requesting exception, based on claimed unreasonable cost, by adding to the offered price the appropriate percentage of the cost of such foreign construction material [@CL-SUP-3578; @CL-SUP-1261], as specified in paragraph (b)(3)(i) of the clause at FAR 52.225-9 [@CL-SUP-9363; @CL-SUP-9364].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An offeror wants to use a foreign pipe. Neither the pipe nor the domestic pipe is a critical item or contains critical components. Delivered to the site with any duty, the foreign pipe costs 100 and the domestic pipe costs 125. The domestic pipe costs 25 percent more than the foreign pipe, which is more than the 20 percent in the listed test. The offeror asks the Contracting Officer for a determination before submitting its offer. When the Government evaluates an offer based on the foreign pipe, it adds the appropriate percentage of the cost of the foreign pipe to the offered price.

## Never confuse

The plain 20 percent test is limited to domestic construction material that is not a critical item or does not contain critical components [@CL-SUP-1257; @CL-SUP-1258]. For a critical item or material that contains critical components, the test adds the additional preference factor [@CL-SUP-1287]. The notice provision asks for the request before offers [@CL-SUP-1260], and its Alternate I asks for the request with the offer [@CL-SUP-1292].

The supplies clause defines domestic end product [@CL-SUP-1230], while this clause defines domestic construction material [@CL-SUP-1291]; see [Buy American clause for supplies](sup-buy-american-supplies-clause.md).
