## Purpose

Shows what may not be acquired, and from where, for large medium-speed diesel engines, listed vessel components and fuel for overseas contingency operations.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-SUP-1732] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 252.225-7062 [@CL-SUP-1732], DFARS 252.225-7063 [@CL-SUP-1734] and DFARS 252.225-7065 [@CL-SUP-1737] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-1732] |
| Applies to | Contracts delivering large medium-speed diesel engines where DFARS 225.7004-7(b) prescribes the engine clause [@CL-SUP-1769; @CL-SUP-1732], contracts delivering listed vessel components [@CL-SUP-1734], and solicitations for fuel for an overseas contingency operation [@CL-SUP-1737; @CL-SUP-1738] |

Large medium-speed diesel engines are diesel engines whose revolutions per minute fall between 300 and 1500 RPM with a displacement greater than 1500 cubic inches per cylinder [@CL-SUP-1731]. The Contractor shall deliver under the contract large medium-speed diesel engines manufactured in the United States, Australia, Canada, New Zealand, or the United Kingdom [@CL-SUP-1732].

The listed components of T-AO 205 and T-ARC class vessels must be manufactured in the United States, Australia, Canada, New Zealand, or the United Kingdom of Great Britain and Northern Ireland [@CL-SUP-1734]. They are auxiliary equipment, including pumps, for all shipboard services; propulsion system components, including engines, reduction gears, and propellers; shipboard cranes; and spreaders for shipboard cranes [@CL-SUP-1735].

For an overseas contingency operation, DoD may not procure fuel in whole or in part, or derivatives of such fuel, that is sourced from nations or regions prohibited from selling petroleum to the United States [@CL-SUP-1737]. When requested by the Contracting Officer, the prospective Contractor shall submit records necessary to demonstrate compliance with applicable laws and regulations regarding export-controlled items and anticorruption statutes and regulations [@CL-SUP-1760]. Prior to contract award, the offeror shall promptly report to the Contracting Officer any instance of unsupported denial of access to a facility or equipment by a host-nation government that may prevent it from complying with the terms and conditions of the solicitation [@CL-SUP-1761].

## How it works

The engine clause requires the Contractor to deliver large medium-speed diesel engines manufactured in the listed countries [@CL-SUP-1732], and the vessel clause requires the listed components to be manufactured in the listed countries [@CL-SUP-1734]. The Contractor shall include the substance of the engine clause, including paragraph (c), in subcontracts that exceed the simplified acquisition threshold, including subcontracts for commercial products and commercial services, that require large medium-speed diesel engines for new construction of auxiliary ships [@CL-SUP-1733]. The Contractor shall insert the substance of the vessel clause, including paragraph (b), in subcontracts for the listed components that exceed the simplified acquisition threshold, including subcontracts for commercial products and commercial services [@CL-SUP-1736]. Under the fuel text, the offeror marks whether it does or does not certify that the fuel, in whole or in part, or derivatives of such fuel, to be provided under any resulting contract is not sourced from a nation or region prohibited from selling petroleum to the United States [@CL-SUP-1738]. Only offerors who certify that the fuel to be provided is not sourced from a prohibited nation or region will be eligible for award [@CL-SUP-1739].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

Assume a contractor plans to deliver a large engine with 2000 cubic inches of displacement per cylinder that runs at 600 RPM, and it was built in Canada. The engine meets the definition, and Canada is a listed origin, so the delivery rule is met. An engine built in a country that is not one of the listed countries would not meet the rule. For a fuel solicitation, an offeror whose fuel is sourced from a prohibited nation marks that it does not certify, and so is not eligible for award.

## Never confuse

The engine clause covers large medium-speed diesel engines [@CL-SUP-1732], and the vessel clause covers the listed components, which include propulsion system components, including engines [@CL-SUP-1735]. The fuel text restricts procurement by DoD and works through a certification [@CL-SUP-1737; @CL-SUP-1738]. For other items such as hand tools and satellite components, see [Restrictions on hand tools, personal protective equipment and satellite components](sup-dfars-hand-tools-ppe-and-satellite-components-restrictions.md). For the DoD Buy American clause, see [DoD Buy American and Balance of Payments Program clause: definitions and delivery rule](sup-dfars-buy-american-definitions-and-delivery-rule.md). For anchor chain, forgings and steel plate, see [Anchor chain, forgings and steel plate restrictions](sup-dfars-anchor-chain-forgings-and-steel-plate-restrictions.md). Under the specialty metals clause, any specialty metal delivered under the contract shall be melted or produced in the United States or its outlying areas [@CL-SUP-1696]; see [Specialty metals melt clause and commercial derivative military article certificate](sup-dfars-specialty-metals-us-melt-clause-and-derivative-certificate.md). Under the Russian Federation energy rule, contracts for the acquisition of furnished energy for a covered military installation shall not use any energy sourced from inside the Russian Federation as a means of generating that energy, unless a waiver is approved [@CL-SUP-1706]; see [Prohibition on energy sourced from inside the Russian Federation](sup-dfars-russian-energy-prohibition-and-representation.md). For sanctions-based purchase restrictions, see [Restrictions on certain foreign purchases and duty-free entry](sup-ofac-restrictions-and-duty-free-entry.md). The United Kingdom is a listed origin [@CL-SUP-1732]; for its levies, see [Waiver of United Kingdom levies in evaluation and performance](sup-dfars-united-kingdom-levies-waiver-evaluation-and-subcontracts.md). For commercial flowdown generally, see [Subcontracts for commercial products and commercial services](../sourcing-evidence/sup-subcontracts-commercial-products-and-services.md).
