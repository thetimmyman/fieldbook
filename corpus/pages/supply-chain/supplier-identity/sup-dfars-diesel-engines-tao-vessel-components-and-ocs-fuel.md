## Purpose

Shows what may not be acquired, and from where, for large medium-speed diesel engines, listed vessel components and fuel for overseas contingency operations.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-SUP-1732] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 252.225-7062 [@CL-SUP-1732], DFARS 252.225-7063 [@CL-SUP-1734] and DFARS 252.225-7065 [@CL-SUP-1737] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-1732] |
| Applies to | Contracts delivering large medium-speed diesel engines where DFARS 225.7004-7(b) prescribes the engine clause [@CL-SUP-1769; @CL-SUP-1732], contracts delivering listed vessel components [@CL-SUP-1734], and solicitations for fuel for an overseas contingency operation [@CL-SUP-1737] |

Large medium-speed diesel engines are diesel engines whose revolutions per minute fall between 300 and 1500 RPM with a displacement greater than 1500 cubic inches per cylinder [@CL-SUP-1731]. The Contractor shall deliver under the contract large medium-speed diesel engines manufactured in the United States, Australia, Canada, New Zealand, or the United Kingdom [@CL-SUP-1732].

The listed components of T-AO 205 and T-ARC class vessels must be manufactured in the United States, Australia, Canada, New Zealand, or the United Kingdom of Great Britain and Northern Ireland [@CL-SUP-1734]. They are auxiliary equipment including pumps, propulsion system components, shipboard cranes, and spreaders for shipboard cranes [@CL-SUP-1735].

For an overseas contingency operation, DoD may not procure fuel in whole or in part, or derivatives of such fuel, that is sourced from nations or regions prohibited from selling petroleum to the United States [@CL-SUP-1737]. When requested by the Contracting Officer, the prospective Contractor shall submit records necessary to demonstrate compliance with applicable laws and regulations regarding export-controlled items and anticorruption statutes and regulations [@CL-SUP-1760]. Prior to contract award, the offeror shall promptly report to the Contracting Officer any instance of unsupported denial of access to a facility or equipment by a host-nation government that may prevent it from complying with the terms and conditions of the solicitation [@CL-SUP-1761].

## How it works

The engine and component rules are origin rules on what the Contractor delivers [@CL-SUP-1732; @CL-SUP-1734]. Their substance goes into subcontracts above the simplified acquisition threshold, including subcontracts for commercial products and commercial services, for large engines used in new construction of auxiliary ships [@CL-SUP-1733] and for the listed components [@CL-SUP-1736]. The fuel text is a solicitation provision in which the offeror marks whether it does or does not certify that the fuel is not sourced from a prohibited nation or region [@CL-SUP-1738]. Only offerors who certify that are eligible for award [@CL-SUP-1739]. These sections print no exception to the origin rules, so none is established here.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

Assume a contractor plans to deliver a large engine with 2000 cubic inches of displacement per cylinder that runs at 600 RPM, and it was built in Canada. The engine meets the definition, and Canada is a listed origin, so the delivery rule is met. An engine built in another country would not meet the rule. For a fuel solicitation, an offeror whose fuel is sourced from a prohibited nation marks that it does not certify, and so is not eligible for award.

## Never confuse

The engine rule covers engines, and the vessel rule covers the listed components, including engines in the propulsion group [@CL-SUP-1735]. The fuel text restricts procurement by DoD and works through a certification [@CL-SUP-1737; @CL-SUP-1738]. A different DoD rule covers other items such as hand tools and satellite components; see [Restrictions on hand tools, personal protective equipment and satellite components](sup-dfars-hand-tools-ppe-and-satellite-components-restrictions.md). The DoD Buy American clause is a component test rather than an origin list; see [DoD Buy American and Balance of Payments Program clause: definitions and delivery rule](sup-dfars-buy-american-definitions-and-delivery-rule.md). Anchor chain, forgings and steel plate have their own restrictions; see [Anchor chain, forgings and steel plate restrictions](sup-dfars-anchor-chain-forgings-and-steel-plate-restrictions.md). The specialty metals melt clause names one place rather than a list [@CL-SUP-1696]; see [Specialty metals melt clause and commercial derivative military article certificate](sup-dfars-specialty-metals-us-melt-clause-and-derivative-certificate.md). The Russian Federation energy rule concerns furnished energy at covered installations, not fuel for operations [@CL-SUP-1706]; see [Prohibition on energy sourced from inside the Russian Federation](sup-dfars-russian-energy-prohibition-and-representation.md). Sanctions-based purchase restrictions are covered separately in [Restrictions on certain foreign purchases and duty-free entry](sup-ofac-restrictions-and-duty-free-entry.md). The United Kingdom is a listed origin here, and its levies are a separate topic; see [Waiver of United Kingdom levies in evaluation and performance](sup-dfars-united-kingdom-levies-waiver-evaluation-and-subcontracts.md). For commercial flowdown generally, see [Subcontracts for commercial products and commercial services](../sourcing-evidence/sup-subcontracts-commercial-products-and-services.md).
