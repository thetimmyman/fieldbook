## Purpose

Shows what an offeror certifies in the Buy American Certificate and in the Buy American, Free Trade Agreements and Israeli Trade Act Certificate, how it lists foreign end products, and how offers are evaluated.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-SUP-1236; @CL-SUP-1240] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.225-2 [@CL-SUP-1236]; FAR 52.225-4 [@CL-SUP-1240] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.225-2 [@CL-SUP-3552; @CL-SUP-3553; @CL-SUP-3554; @CL-SUP-3555; @CL-SUP-3556; @CL-SUP-3557]; RFO FAR 52.225-4 [@CL-SUP-3558; @CL-SUP-3559; @CL-SUP-3560; @CL-SUP-3561; @CL-SUP-3562] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-1236; @CL-SUP-1240] |
| Applies to | Offers in solicitations that include either provision [@CL-SUP-1236; @CL-SUP-1240] |

In the basic certificate, the offeror certifies that each end product, except those it lists in paragraph (b), is a domestic end product [@CL-SUP-1236], and that each domestic end product listed in paragraph (c) contains a critical component [@CL-SUP-1281]. It lists as foreign end products those end products manufactured in the United States that do not qualify as domestic end products [@CL-SUP-1237]. For foreign end products that do not consist wholly or predominantly of iron or steel or a combination of both, it also indicates whether they exceed 55 percent domestic content, except for those that are COTS items [@CL-SUP-1238]. If the percentage of the domestic content is unknown, it selects no [@CL-SUP-1283]. It separately lists the line item numbers of domestic end products that contain a critical component [@CL-SUP-1282].

In the Free Trade Agreements and Israeli Trade Act certificate, the offeror certifies that each end product, except those it lists in paragraph (b) or paragraph (c)(1), is a domestic end product and that each domestic end product listed in paragraph (c)(2) contains a critical component [@CL-SUP-1240]. It certifies that the supplies it lists in paragraph (b) are Free Trade Agreement country end products (other than Bahraini, Moroccan, Omani, Panamanian, or Peruvian end products) or Israeli end products [@CL-SUP-1241]. It lists the supplies that are foreign end products, other than those listed in paragraph (b), as defined in the matching clause [@CL-SUP-1242].

## How it works

The basic certificate sorts the offeror's end products into domestic end products and listed foreign end products [@CL-SUP-1236; @CL-SUP-1237]. The second certificate adds a separate list for Free Trade Agreement country and Israeli end products, and a list of other foreign end products [@CL-SUP-1241; @CL-SUP-1242]. Each provision says the Government evaluates offers in accordance with the policies and procedures of part 25 of the Federal Acquisition Regulation [@CL-SUP-1239; @CL-SUP-1243]. The text printed in the source does not set out the evaluation steps themselves, so this card does not state them.

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text from February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. RFO FAR 52.225-2 keeps the certification that each end product, except those listed in paragraph (b), is a domestic end product and that each domestic end product listed in paragraph (c) contains a critical component [@CL-SUP-3552; @CL-SUP-1236; @CL-SUP-1281]. It keeps the listing as foreign end products of those end products manufactured in the United States that do not qualify as domestic end products [@CL-SUP-3553; @CL-SUP-1237], the indication of whether foreign end products that do not consist wholly or predominantly of iron or steel or a combination of both exceed 55 percent domestic content, except for those that are COTS items [@CL-SUP-3554; @CL-SUP-1238], the answer no when the percentage of the domestic content is unknown [@CL-SUP-3555; @CL-SUP-1283], and the separate listing of the line item numbers of domestic end products that contain a critical component [@CL-SUP-3556; @CL-SUP-1282]. It keeps the rule that the Government evaluates offers in accordance with the policies and procedures of part 25 of the Federal Acquisition Regulation [@CL-SUP-3557; @CL-SUP-1239].

RFO FAR 52.225-4 keeps the certification that each end product, except those listed in paragraph (b) or (c)(1), is a domestic end product and that each domestic end product listed in paragraph (c)(2) contains a critical component [@CL-SUP-3558; @CL-SUP-1240]. It keeps the certification that the listed supplies are Free Trade Agreement country end products (other than Bahraini, Moroccan, Omani, Panamanian, or Peruvian end products) or Israeli end products [@CL-SUP-3559; @CL-SUP-1241]. It keeps the instruction to list the supplies that are foreign end products, other than those listed in paragraph (b), as defined in the matching clause [@CL-SUP-3560; @CL-SUP-1242], and the evaluation in accordance with the policies and procedures of part 25 of the Federal Acquisition Regulation [@CL-SUP-3562; @CL-SUP-1243]. Paragraph (c)(1) differs after its first sentence: the RFO text goes straight to an Other Foreign End Products table with columns for the line item and the country of origin [@CL-SUP-3561], where the codified paragraph also directs the offeror to list as other foreign end products those end products manufactured in the United States that do not qualify as domestic end products [@CL-SUP-9361], to indicate whether foreign end products that do not consist wholly or predominantly of iron or steel or a combination of both exceed 55 percent domestic content, except for those that are COTS items [@CL-SUP-9362], and to select no if the percentage of the domestic content is unknown [@CL-SUP-9368].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An offeror quotes three line items under the basic certificate. Line 1 is made in the United States and qualifies as a domestic end product, so the offeror does not list it as foreign. Line 2 is made in the United States from mostly imported components and does not qualify, so the offeror lists it as a foreign end product and answers the domestic content question. Line 3 is made abroad and is listed too. The offeror lists lines 2 and 3 and certifies that the rest are domestic end products. Line 1 contains a critical component, so the offeror also lists it separately as a domestic end product that contains a critical component.

## Never confuse

Listing a foreign end product is not the same as answering the domestic content question. The offeror lists the product first, and the indicator is a further answer for products that are not wholly or predominantly iron or steel [@CL-SUP-1237; @CL-SUP-1238]. The certificates state what the offeror offers. The delivery duty sits in the matching clauses [@CL-SUP-1235; @CL-SUP-1247], covered on [Buy American clause for supplies](sup-buy-american-supplies-clause.md) and [Buy American, Free Trade Agreements and Israeli Trade Act clause](sup-buy-american-fta-israeli-clause.md).

The place of manufacture question in an offer is separate and is for statistical purposes only [@CL-SUP-1269]; see [Place of manufacture and Iran-related certifications](sup-place-of-manufacture-and-iran-certifications.md). For the annual representations an offeror keeps in SAM, see [Annual representations and certifications in SAM](../../compliance/trade-cyber/cmp-annual-representations-and-certifications-in-sam.md).

In the Free Trade Agreements and Israeli Trade Act certificate, the codified paragraph (c)(1) has sentences on listing end products manufactured in the United States that do not qualify as domestic end products [@CL-SUP-9361], on indicating whether foreign end products exceed 55 percent domestic content [@CL-SUP-9362] and on selecting no if the percentage is unknown [@CL-SUP-9368]; the RFO paragraph (c)(1) goes from its first sentence straight to a table [@CL-SUP-3561].
