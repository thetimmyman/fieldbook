## Purpose

Shows what an offeror represents and discloses about ownership or control by the government of a country that is a state sponsor of terrorism, and the consequence for award.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-SUP-1501] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 252.225-7050 [@CL-SUP-1501] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-1501] |
| Applies to | Offerors, under solicitations with the provision [@CL-SUP-1502] |

Unless a waiver is granted by the Secretary of Defense, no contract may be awarded to a firm if the government of a country that is a state sponsor of terrorism owns or controls a significant interest in the firms or subsidiaries that the provision goes on to list [@CL-SUP-1501]. The award prohibition names the firm, a subsidiary of the firm, and any other firm that owns or controls the firm as the positions in which such an interest matters [@CL-SUP-1528]. Unless the Offeror submits with its offer the required disclosure, the Offeror represents by submission of its offer that the government of a country that is a state sponsor of terrorism does not own or control a significant interest in the parties that the provision goes on to list [@CL-SUP-1502].

The Offeror shall disclose in an attachment to its offer if the government of a country that is a state sponsor of terrorism owns or controls a significant interest in the Offeror, a subsidiary of the Offeror, or any other firm that owns or controls the Offeror [@CL-SUP-1503]. The disclosure shall include identification of each government holding a significant interest and a description of the significant interest held by each government [@CL-SUP-1504].

The provision defines significant interest by listed tests. It includes ownership of or beneficial interest in 5 percent or more of the firm's or subsidiary's securities [@CL-SUP-1529]. Two further tests are holding a management position in the firm, such as a director or officer, and the ability to control or influence the election, appointment, or tenure of directors or officers in the firm [@CL-SUP-1530]. Two more are ownership of 10 percent or more of the assets of a firm and holding 50 percent or more of the indebtedness of a firm [@CL-SUP-1531]. The provision says that, as of its date, state sponsors of terrorism include Iran, North Korea, and Syria [@CL-SUP-1532].

## How it works

The Offeror either submits the disclosure with its offer, or it represents by submitting the offer that no such interest exists [@CL-SUP-1502]. The disclosure names each government and describes its interest [@CL-SUP-1504]. The consequence is that no contract may be awarded to a firm in the listed position unless the Secretary of Defense grants a waiver [@CL-SUP-1501]. Significant interest is a defined term in the provision, with the listed tests set out above [@CL-SUP-1529; @CL-SUP-1530; @CL-SUP-1531]. The source text printed here does not describe the waiver procedure.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A government of a country that is a state sponsor of terrorism holds 8 percent of an offeror's securities. That is above the 5 percent securities test in the definition of significant interest.

Answer: the offeror attaches the disclosure to its offer, naming that government and describing the holding. Without a waiver from the Secretary of Defense, no contract may be awarded to the offeror.

## Never confuse

This provision is about an interest that a state sponsor of terrorism holds in the Offeror, its subsidiary, or a firm that owns or controls the Offeror [@CL-SUP-1503]. Disclosure of foreign government control under a different provision, and the clause on subcontracting with firms owned by a state sponsor of terrorism, are covered on the card "Foreign government ownership disclosure and subcontracting with terrorist-state firms". The question of who the immediate owner and the highest-level owner are is covered on [Ownership or control of offeror](sup-ownership-or-control-of-offeror.md). The satellite services clause defines a covered foreign country to include any country that is a state sponsor of terrorism [@CL-SUP-1495]; it is covered on [Prohibition on acquisition of certain foreign commercial satellite services](sup-dfars-foreign-commercial-satellite-services-prohibition.md).
