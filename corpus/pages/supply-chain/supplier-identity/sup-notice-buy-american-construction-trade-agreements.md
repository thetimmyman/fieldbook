## Purpose

Explains the notice provision that accompanies the Buy American clause for construction materials under trade agreements. It covers when and how an offeror asks for a determination that the Buy American statute does not apply, how the Government evaluates such an offer, how an alternate offer works, and what Alternates I and II change.

## The rule

|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-SUP-2801] |

| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.225-12 [@CL-SUP-3698]; Alternate I [@CL-SUP-3712]; Alternate II [@CL-SUP-3713] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-2801] |
| Applies to | Offers in solicitations that include the provision, from offerors that request a determination or submit an alternate offer [@CL-SUP-2801; @CL-SUP-2834] |

Definitions, paragraph (a). The terms commercially available off-the-shelf item, construction material, designated country construction material, domestic construction material and foreign construction material, as used in the provision, are defined in the clause of the solicitation [@CL-SUP-3033]. That clause is the Buy American clause for construction materials under trade agreements, FAR clause 52.225-11 [@CL-SUP-3034]. Alternate II adds the definition of Bahraini, Mexican, or Omani construction material to paragraph (a) [@CL-SUP-3035].

Basic provision, paragraph (b). An offeror requesting a determination that the Buy American statute is inapplicable should submit the request to the Contracting Officer in time to allow a determination before submission of offers, and the offeror shall include the information and applicable supporting data required by paragraphs (c) and (d) of FAR clause 52.225-11 in the request [@CL-SUP-2801]. If the offeror has not requested a determination before submitting its offer, or has not received a response to a previous request, the offeror shall include the information and supporting data in the offer [@CL-SUP-2802].

Basic provision, paragraph (c). The Government evaluates an offer requesting exception to the requirements of the Buy American statute, based on claimed unreasonable cost of domestic construction materials, by adding to the offered price the appropriate percentage of the cost of such foreign construction material, as specified in paragraph (b)(4)(i) of FAR clause 52.225-11 [@CL-SUP-2803]. If evaluation results in a tie between an offeror that requested the substitution of foreign construction material based on unreasonable cost and an offeror that did not request an exception, the Contracting Officer awards to the offeror that did not request an exception based on unreasonable cost [@CL-SUP-2804; @CL-SUP-3142].

Basic provision, paragraph (d). When an offer includes foreign construction material, other than designated country construction material, that is not listed by the Government in paragraph (b)(3) of FAR clause 52.225-11, the offeror also may submit an alternate offer [@CL-SUP-2834]. The alternate offer is an offer based on use of equivalent domestic or designated country construction material [@CL-SUP-2833]. If an alternate offer is submitted, the offeror shall submit a separate Standard Form 1442 for the alternate offer [@CL-SUP-2835]. The offeror also submits a separate price comparison table prepared in accordance with paragraphs (c) and (d) of FAR clause 52.225-11, for the offer that is based on the use of any foreign construction material for which the Government has not yet determined an exception applies [@CL-SUP-2836].

If the Government determines that a particular exception requested in accordance with paragraph (c) of FAR clause 52.225-11 does not apply, it evaluates only those offers based on use of the equivalent domestic or designated country construction material [@CL-SUP-2837]. Under the basic provision the offeror shall be required to furnish such domestic or designated country construction material [@CL-SUP-2838]. An offer based on use of the foreign construction material for which an exception was requested will be rejected as nonresponsive if the acquisition is conducted by sealed bidding, or may be accepted if revised during negotiations [@CL-SUP-2806].

Alternate I. Under Alternate I, an offeror requesting a determination regarding the inapplicability of the Buy American statute shall submit the request with its offer, including the information and applicable supporting data required by paragraphs (c) and (d) of FAR clause 52.225-11 [@CL-SUP-2805].

Alternate II. Under Alternate II, the alternate-offer paragraph applies when an offer includes foreign construction material, except foreign construction material from a designated country other than Bahrain, Mexico, or Oman that is not listed by the Government in paragraph (b)(3) of FAR clause 52.225-11 [@CL-SUP-2839]. Under Alternate II, the offeror also may submit an alternate offer based on use of equivalent domestic or designated country construction material other than Bahraini, Mexican, or Omani construction material [@CL-SUP-2840]. Under Alternate II, if the Government determines that a particular exception requested in accordance with paragraph (c) of FAR clause 52.225-11 does not apply, it evaluates only those offers based on use of the equivalent domestic or designated country construction material other than Bahraini, Mexican, or Omani construction material [@CL-SUP-2841].

## How it works

An offeror that wants to use foreign construction material, or wants a ruling that the Buy American statute does not apply, follows the steps below.

Timing. Under the basic provision the offeror should ask early enough for a determination before offers are submitted [@CL-SUP-2801]. If it did not ask before submitting its offer, or has not had a response, it puts the information and supporting data in the offer [@CL-SUP-2802]. Under Alternate I the request goes with the offer [@CL-SUP-2805].

Content. Whichever timing applies, the request carries the information and supporting data required by paragraphs (c) and (d) of FAR clause 52.225-11 [@CL-SUP-2801; @CL-SUP-2805]. Those paragraphs are covered on [Buy American, construction materials under trade agreements](sup-buy-american-construction-trade-agreements.md).

Evaluation. For an offer that claims unreasonable cost of domestic material, the Government adds the appropriate percentage of the cost of the foreign construction material to the offered price [@CL-SUP-2803]. The percentage is the one specified in paragraph (b)(4)(i) of FAR clause 52.225-11 [@CL-SUP-2803]. That paragraph states 20 percent for domestic construction material that is not a critical item or does not contain critical components [@CL-SUP-2842; @CL-SUP-2843], and it calls that figure the evaluation factor [@CL-SUP-2846]. For a domestic construction material that is a critical item or contains critical components it states 20 percent plus the additional preference factor identified for the critical item [@CL-SUP-2848]. A tie goes to the offeror that did not request an exception based on unreasonable cost [@CL-SUP-2804; @CL-SUP-3142].

Alternate offer. An offeror whose offer includes foreign construction material, other than designated country construction material, that the Government has not listed in paragraph (b)(3) of FAR clause 52.225-11 may also submit an alternate offer [@CL-SUP-2834]. The alternate offer is an offer based on use of equivalent domestic or designated country construction material [@CL-SUP-2833]. It needs its own Standard Form 1442 [@CL-SUP-2835]. The separate price comparison table goes with the offer based on foreign construction material for which the Government has not yet determined an exception applies [@CL-SUP-2836].

The decision. If the Government determines that the requested exception does not apply, it evaluates only the offers based on equivalent domestic or designated country construction material [@CL-SUP-2837]. Under the basic provision the offeror shall then be required to furnish that material [@CL-SUP-2838]. The offer based on the foreign material is rejected as nonresponsive in sealed bidding, and may be accepted if revised during negotiations [@CL-SUP-2806]. Alternate II repeats the rejected-or-accepted sentences unchanged [@CL-SUP-2806].

Alternate II changes the alternate-offer paragraph in three ways and adds a definition. Material from Bahrain, Mexico or Oman does not fall within the designated-country exception to the alternate-offer condition [@CL-SUP-2839]. The alternate offer and the evaluation after a denied exception use equivalent domestic or designated country material other than Bahraini, Mexican or Omani material [@CL-SUP-2840; @CL-SUP-2841]. Alternate II's evaluation sentence, as quoted in the claim, ends without the clause requiring the offeror to furnish the material, which the basic provision has [@CL-SUP-2841; @CL-SUP-2838]. Alternate II also adds the definition of Bahraini, Mexican, or Omani construction material to paragraph (a) [@CL-SUP-3035]. The construction materials clause, covered on [Buy American, construction materials under trade agreements](sup-buy-american-construction-trade-agreements.md), defines that term in its Alternate I to include material that is wholly the growth, product or manufacture of Bahrain, Mexico or Oman [@CL-SUP-2862] and material that consists in whole or in part of materials from another country and has been substantially transformed in Bahrain, Mexico or Oman into a new and different construction material distinct from the materials from which it was transformed [@CL-SUP-3083].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text under Class Deviation 2026-O0038, effective February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. The RFO text of FAR 52.225-12 keeps the pointer in paragraph (a) saying the defined terms are defined in the Buy American clause for construction materials under trade agreements, FAR clause 52.225-11 [@CL-SUP-9392; @CL-SUP-9393; @CL-SUP-3033; @CL-SUP-3034]. It keeps the timing and content rules for the request in paragraph (b) [@CL-SUP-3698; @CL-SUP-3699; @CL-SUP-3700; @CL-SUP-2801; @CL-SUP-2802]. It keeps the evaluation and tie rules in paragraph (c) [@CL-SUP-3701; @CL-SUP-3702; @CL-SUP-3703; @CL-SUP-3704; @CL-SUP-2803; @CL-SUP-2804; @CL-SUP-3142]. It keeps the alternate-offer rules in paragraph (d), including the separate Standard Form 1442 and price comparison table [@CL-SUP-3705; @CL-SUP-3706; @CL-SUP-3707; @CL-SUP-3708; @CL-SUP-2834; @CL-SUP-2833; @CL-SUP-2835; @CL-SUP-2836]. It keeps the rule for a denied exception, including the sealed-bidding and negotiation outcomes [@CL-SUP-3709; @CL-SUP-3710; @CL-SUP-3711; @CL-SUP-2837; @CL-SUP-2838; @CL-SUP-2806]. Alternate I keeps the rule that the request goes in with the offer [@CL-SUP-3712; @CL-SUP-2805]. Alternate II keeps its alternate-offer condition, its evaluation sentence and its added definition [@CL-SUP-3713; @CL-SUP-3714; @CL-SUP-3715; @CL-SUP-9391; @CL-SUP-3716; @CL-SUP-2839; @CL-SUP-2840; @CL-SUP-2841; @CL-SUP-3035].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A renovation solicitation uses the basic provision, with no alternate, and is awarded by sealed bidding. The list in paragraph (b)(3) of FAR clause 52.225-11 says none. Offeror A offers $1,000,000 using domestic doors that cost $250,000. Offeror B offers $960,000 using foreign doors that cost $200,000. The doors are not designated country construction material, not critical items and contain no critical components.

B asked for a determination before offers were due and attached the information and a price comparison table. Its claim of unreasonable cost rests on the domestic doors costing $250,000 against $200,000, which is 25 percent more and so exceeds the 20 percent threshold.

The Government evaluates B's offer by adding 20 percent of the cost of the foreign doors to its price, which is 0.20 x $200,000 = $40,000. B's evaluated price is $960,000 + $40,000 = $1,000,000. That equals A's price, and A did not request an exception based on unreasonable cost, so the Contracting Officer awards to A.

B also submitted an alternate offer based on equivalent domestic doors, with a separate Standard Form 1442. Suppose instead the Government had determined that B's requested exception does not apply. It would evaluate only offers based on equivalent domestic or designated country doors and B would be required to furnish such doors. Because the acquisition is by sealed bidding, B's offer based on the foreign doors would be rejected as nonresponsive.

Under Alternate I, B's request would instead have gone in with its offer.

## Never confuse

This provision is the offeror's notice and request procedure. The contractor's use requirement and the content of the request are in the construction materials clause, covered on [Buy American, construction materials under trade agreements](sup-buy-american-construction-trade-agreements.md). The clause for contracts without trade agreements is covered on [Buy American, construction materials](sup-buy-american-construction-materials.md).

Alternate I and Alternate II change different paragraphs. Alternate I changes the timing of the request [@CL-SUP-2805]. Alternate II changes the alternate-offer paragraph [@CL-SUP-2839].

The tie rule applies when evaluation results in a tie between an offeror that requested foreign construction material based on unreasonable cost and one that did not [@CL-SUP-2804]. A denied exception is handled instead by evaluating only offers based on equivalent domestic or designated country construction material [@CL-SUP-2837].

## Company calibration

Whether the company files its request early enough for a decision before offers are due, or builds the data into the offer under the alternate. [@Q-004]
