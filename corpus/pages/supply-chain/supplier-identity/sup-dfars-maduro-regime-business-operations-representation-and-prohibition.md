## Purpose

Shows what DoD may not buy from a person with business operations tied to the government of Venezuela, the license exception, what the terms person and business operations cover, and what an offeror represents.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-SUP-1712] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 252.225-7055 [@CL-SUP-1714] and DFARS 252.225-7056 [@CL-SUP-1712] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-1712] |
| Applies to | Solicitations with the representation [@CL-SUP-1714], and contracts with the clause where DFARS 225.7020-5(b) prescribes it [@CL-SUP-1766], for products or services bought by DoD [@CL-SUP-1712] |

DoD is prohibited from entering into a contract for the procurement of products or services with any person that has business operations with an authority of the government of Venezuela that is not recognized as the legitimate government of Venezuela by the United States Government [@CL-SUP-1712]. The prohibition carries an exception for a person that has a valid license to operate in Venezuela issued by the Office of Foreign Assets Control of the Department of the Treasury [@CL-SUP-1713].

In the clause, person includes a natural person, corporation, company, business association, partnership, society, trust, or any other nongovernmental entity, organization, or group, and any governmental entity or instrumentality of a government [@CL-SUP-1749]. It also includes any successor, subunit, parent entity, or subsidiary of, or any entity under common ownership or control with, such an entity [@CL-SUP-1750]. Business operations means engaging in commerce in any form, including acquiring, developing, maintaining, owning, selling, possessing, leasing, or operating equipment, facilities, personnel, products, services, personal property, real property, or any other apparatus of business or commerce [@CL-SUP-1751].

By submitting its offer, the offeror represents that it either has no such business operations or has that license [@CL-SUP-1714; @CL-SUP-1715]. The clause puts the same either-or on the Contractor: it shall not have any such business operations [@CL-SUP-1752], or it shall have the license [@CL-SUP-1753].

## How it works

The solicitation provision asks the offeror for the representation [@CL-SUP-1714]. The offeror can meet it in one of two ways, by having no business operations with such an authority or by holding the valid license to operate in Venezuela [@CL-SUP-1714; @CL-SUP-1715]. The clause puts the same two ways on the Contractor during performance [@CL-SUP-1752; @CL-SUP-1753]. It also reaches subcontracts, because its substance goes into all subcontracts, including subcontracts for commercial products [@CL-SUP-1716]. Because person reaches parents, subsidiaries and entities under common control, the group structure matters when answering [@CL-SUP-1750].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

Assume an offeror owns a small sales office that deals with an authority that the United States Government does not recognize as the legitimate government of Venezuela, and that these dealings are business operations as the clause defines them. It holds no license. It cannot give the first form of the representation and cannot give the license form either. If it holds a valid license from the Office of Foreign Assets Control, it can give the license form. The same choice applies to the Contractor under the clause.

## Never confuse

The prohibition is a limit on what DoD may award, and the representation is the offeror's statement made with the offer [@CL-SUP-1712; @CL-SUP-1714]. The clause paragraph on the Contractor is a separate duty in performance [@CL-SUP-1752]. For other sanctions-related restrictions, see [Restrictions on certain foreign purchases and duty-free entry](sup-ofac-restrictions-and-duty-free-entry.md). The Russian Federation energy provision is a different representation made with the offer [@CL-SUP-1709]; see [Prohibition on energy sourced from inside the Russian Federation](sup-dfars-russian-energy-prohibition-and-representation.md). A foreign person offeror also makes a boycott certification with the offer [@CL-SUP-1730]; see [Foreign military sales contingent fees, exclusionary policies and the Arab boycott certification](sup-dfars-fms-contingent-fees-exclusionary-policies-and-arab-boycott.md). A workforce disclosure about the People's Republic of China is separate; see [Disclosure of employment of individuals who work in the People's Republic of China](sup-dfars-china-workforce-preaward-and-postaward-disclosure.md). For commercial flowdown generally, see [Subcontracts for commercial products and commercial services](../sourcing-evidence/sup-subcontracts-commercial-products-and-services.md).
