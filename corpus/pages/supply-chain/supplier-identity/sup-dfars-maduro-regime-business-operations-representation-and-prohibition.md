## Purpose

Shows what DoD may not buy from a person with business operations tied to the government of Venezuela, the license exception, what the terms person and business operations cover, and what an offeror represents.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-SUP-1712] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 252.225-7055 [@CL-SUP-1714] and DFARS 252.225-7056 [@CL-SUP-1712] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-1712] |
| Applies to | Solicitations with the representation [@CL-SUP-1714], and contracts with the clause where DFARS 225.7020-5(b) prescribes it [@CL-SUP-1766], for products or services bought by DoD [@CL-SUP-1712] |

DoD is prohibited from entering into a contract for the procurement of products or services with any person that has business operations with an authority of the government of Venezuela that is not recognized as the legitimate government of Venezuela by the United States Government [@CL-SUP-1712]. The prohibition carries an exception for a person that has a valid license to operate in Venezuela issued by the Office of Foreign Assets Control of the Department of the Treasury [@CL-SUP-1713].

In the clause, person means a natural person, corporation, company, business association, partnership, society, trust, or any other nongovernmental entity, organization, or group [@CL-SUP-1749]; any governmental entity or instrumentality of a government, including a multilateral development institution (as defined in section 1701(c)(3) of the International Financial Institutions Act, section 262r(c)(3) of title 22 of the United States Code) [@CL-SUP-3995]; and any successor, subunit, parent entity, or subsidiary of, or any entity under common ownership or control with, any entity described in paragraph (1) or (2) of the definition [@CL-SUP-1750]. Business operations means engaging in commerce in any form, including acquiring, developing, maintaining, owning, selling, possessing, leasing, or operating equipment, facilities, personnel, products, services, personal property, real property, or any other apparatus of business or commerce [@CL-SUP-1751].

By submitting its offer, the offeror represents that it either has no business operations with an authority of the Maduro regime or the government of Venezuela that the United States Government does not recognize as the legitimate government of Venezuela, or has that license [@CL-SUP-1714; @CL-SUP-1715]. The clause puts the same either-or on the Contractor: it shall not have any business operations with an authority of the Maduro regime or the government of Venezuela that the United States Government does not recognize as the legitimate government of Venezuela [@CL-SUP-1752], or it shall have the license [@CL-SUP-1753].

## How it works

The solicitation provision asks the offeror for the representation [@CL-SUP-1714]. The offeror can meet it in one of two ways, by having no business operations with that kind of authority or by holding the valid license to operate in Venezuela [@CL-SUP-1714; @CL-SUP-1715]. The clause puts the same two ways on the Contractor during performance [@CL-SUP-1752; @CL-SUP-1753]. The Contractor shall insert the substance of the clause, including paragraph (d), in all subcontracts, including subcontracts for the acquisition of commercial products [@CL-SUP-1716]. The definition of person covers any parent entity or subsidiary of, and any entity under common ownership or control with, the entities it describes [@CL-SUP-1750].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

Assume an offeror owns a small sales office that deals with an authority that the United States Government does not recognize as the legitimate government of Venezuela, and that these dealings are business operations as the clause defines them. It holds no license. It cannot give the first form of the representation and cannot give the license form either. If it holds a valid license from the Office of Foreign Assets Control, it can give the license form. The same choice applies to the Contractor under the clause.

## Never confuse

The prohibition bars DoD from entering into a contract with a person that has the described business operations, unless the person has the license [@CL-SUP-1712; @CL-SUP-1713], and the offeror makes its representation by submission of its offer [@CL-SUP-1714]. The clause also provides that the Contractor shall not have any business operations with such an authority, or shall have the license [@CL-SUP-1752; @CL-SUP-1753].
