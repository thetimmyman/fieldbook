## Purpose

Shows where welded shipboard anchor and mooring chain, listed forging items, and carbon, alloy and armor steel plate must be made, the waivers and limits stated, and which clauses flow down.

## The rule

| Codified citation | DFARS 252.225-7019 [@CL-SUP-1474]; DFARS 252.225-7025 [@CL-SUP-1479]; DFARS 252.225-7030 [@CL-SUP-1483] |

| Edition date | DFARS 252.225-7019: MAY 2024 [@CL-SUP-9902]; DFARS 252.225-7025: DEC 2009 [@CL-SUP-9903]; DFARS 252.225-7030: DEC 2006 [@CL-SUP-9904] |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-1474] |
| Applies to | Welded shipboard anchor and mooring chain, forging items, and steel plate delivered under contracts with the matching clause [@CL-SUP-1474; @CL-SUP-1479; @CL-SUP-1483] |

Welded shipboard anchor and mooring chain delivered under the contract must meet both of two listed requirements [@CL-SUP-1474; @CL-SUP-1475]. The first is that it shall be manufactured in the United States or its outlying areas, including cutting, heat treating, quality control, testing, and welding (both forging and shot blasting process) [@CL-SUP-1474]. The second is that the cost of the components manufactured in the United States or its outlying areas shall exceed 50 percent of the total cost of components [@CL-SUP-1475]. The Contractor may request a waiver if adequate domestic supplies meeting the listed requirements are not available to meet the contract delivery schedule [@CL-SUP-1476]. The Contractor shall insert the substance of the chain clause, including its flowdown paragraph, in all subcontracts for items containing welded shipboard anchor and mooring chain [@CL-SUP-1477].

End products and their components delivered under the contract shall contain forging items that are of domestic manufacture only [@CL-SUP-1479], and domestic manufacture means manufactured in the United States, its outlying areas, or Canada [@CL-SUP-1485]. Forging items are ship propulsion shafts excluding service and landing craft shafts, all periscope tubes, and ring forgings for bull gears greater than 120 inches in diameter [@CL-SUP-1478]. The restriction may be waived upon request from the Contractor in accordance with the subsection of the Defense Federal Acquisition Regulation Supplement that the clause names [@CL-SUP-1480]. The Contractor shall retain compliance records until 3 years after final payment and make them available upon request of the Contracting Officer [@CL-SUP-1481]. The Contractor shall insert the substance of the forgings clause, including its flowdown paragraph, in subcontracts for forging items or for other items that contain forging items [@CL-SUP-1482].

Carbon, alloy, and armor steel plate shall be melted and rolled in the United States or Canada if two listed conditions are met. The first is that the plate is in Federal Supply Class 9515 or is described by specifications of the American Society for Testing Materials or the American Iron and Steel Institute [@CL-SUP-1483]. The second is that the plate will be delivered to the Government for use in a Government-owned facility or a facility under the control of the Department of Defense, or will be purchased by the Contractor for use in such a facility [@CL-SUP-1484]. The restriction applies to the acquisition of such plate as a finished steel mill product that may be used as is or may be used as an intermediate material for the fabrication of an end product [@CL-SUP-1517]. It does not apply to the acquisition of an end product, to be used in the facility, that contains such plate as a component [@CL-SUP-1486].

## How it works

Chain must be manufactured in the United States or its outlying areas [@CL-SUP-1474], forging items must be of domestic manufacture [@CL-SUP-1479; @CL-SUP-1485], and plate must be melted and rolled in the United States or Canada [@CL-SUP-1483]. The chain clause adds a cost share test on components [@CL-SUP-1475], and the forgings clause adds a records duty [@CL-SUP-1481]. The chain and forgings clauses each have a waiver paragraph and a flowdown paragraph [@CL-SUP-1476; @CL-SUP-1477; @CL-SUP-1480; @CL-SUP-1482].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor delivers a welded shipboard anchor chain. Its components cost 200 in total, and components manufactured in the United States cost 130. The 130 is 65 percent of the 200, which is above the 50 percent test.

Answer: the cost share test is met. The chain must also have been manufactured in the United States or its outlying areas, including the listed processes.

## Never confuse

The steel plate restriction does not apply to the acquisition of an end product, to be used in the facility, that contains such plate as a component [@CL-SUP-1486], while under the forgings clause end products and their components shall contain forging items that are of domestic manufacture only [@CL-SUP-1479]. For restrictions on specialty metals and on bearings, see [Restriction on acquisition of articles containing specialty metals](sup-dfars-specialty-metals-restriction.md) and [Ball and roller bearings and items from Communist Chinese military companies](sup-dfars-bearings-and-communist-chinese-military-companies.md). For flowdown into commercial subcontracts generally, see [Subcontracts for commercial products and commercial services](../sourcing-evidence/sup-subcontracts-commercial-products-and-services.md). For the clause on hand or measuring tools, see [Restrictions on hand tools, personal protective equipment and satellite components](sup-dfars-hand-tools-ppe-and-satellite-components-restrictions.md).
