## Purpose

Shows how the Trade Agreements clause treats designated country end products, which terms it defines, and its delivery rule with the exception that the offer can create.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-SUP-1252] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.225-5 [@CL-SUP-1252] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.225-5 [@CL-SUP-3790] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-1252] |
| Applies to | Acquisitions where the Contracting Officer has determined that the WTO GPA and FTAs apply [@CL-SUP-1252] |

The contractor shall deliver only U.S.-made or designated country end products, except to the extent that, in its offer, it specified delivery of other end products in the provision entitled Trade Agreements Certificate [@CL-SUP-1253]. The clause states that the Contracting Officer has determined that the WTO GPA and FTAs apply to the acquisition and that, unless otherwise specified, these trade agreements apply to all items in the Schedule [@CL-SUP-1252].

## How it works

A designated country end product is a WTO GPA country end product, an FTA country end product, a least developed country end product, or a Caribbean Basin country end product [@CL-SUP-1250]. A U.S.-made end product is an article that is mined, produced, or manufactured in the United States or that is substantially transformed in the United States into a new and different article of commerce with a name, character, or use distinct from that of the article or articles from which it was transformed [@CL-SUP-1251]. The delivery rule therefore admits two kinds of end product, and an offer can specify others [@CL-SUP-1253].

In RFO FAR part 52, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text from February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760; @CL-CMP-9762]. RFO FAR 52.225-5 keeps the definition of a designated country end product [@CL-SUP-3787; @CL-SUP-1250]. It keeps the definition of a U.S.-made end product [@CL-SUP-3788; @CL-SUP-1251]. It keeps the statement that the Contracting Officer has determined that the WTO GPA and FTAs apply and that, unless otherwise specified, they apply to all items in the Schedule [@CL-SUP-3789; @CL-SUP-1252]. It keeps the delivery rule and its exception for other end products specified in the offer [@CL-SUP-3790; @CL-SUP-1253].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contract applies the trade agreements. The contractor delivers pumps made wholly in Japan, which is on the clause's list of WTO GPA countries, so they are designated country end products. It also wants to deliver controllers made in a country that is neither designated nor the United States. It may do so only if its offer specified delivery of other end products in the Trade Agreements Certificate.

## Never confuse

A U.S.-made end product under this clause turns on where the article is mined, produced, manufactured or substantially transformed [@CL-SUP-1251]. The plain supplies clause uses its own domestic end product definition [@CL-SUP-1230], covered on [Buy American clause for supplies](sup-buy-american-supplies-clause.md). The Free Trade Agreements and Israeli Trade Act clause has its own delivery rule [@CL-SUP-1249], covered on [Buy American, Free Trade Agreements and Israeli Trade Act clause](sup-buy-american-fta-israeli-clause.md). A designated country end product is also a separate category from a U.S.-made end product, and the delivery rule names both and refers to the provision entitled Trade Agreements Certificate [@CL-SUP-1253] (see also [Buy American certificates in offers](sup-buy-american-certificates.md)).
