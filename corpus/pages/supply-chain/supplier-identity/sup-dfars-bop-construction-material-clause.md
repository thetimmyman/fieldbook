## Purpose

Shows the domestic construction material requirement of the Balance of Payments Program clause for construction material, the exceptions, and what the clause says about how an exception is set. It names the alternates in one sentence each.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-SUP-1877] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 252.225-7044 [@CL-SUP-1877] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | FEB 2024 [@CL-SUP-9907] |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-1877] |
| Applies to | Contracts with the Balance of Payments Program construction material clause, which requires only domestic construction material except for the listed exceptions [@CL-SUP-1877] |

The Contractor shall use only domestic construction material in performing this contract, except for the listed exceptions, which include construction material valued at or below the simplified acquisition threshold in part 2 of the Federal Acquisition Regulation and information technology that is a commercial product [@CL-SUP-1877]. A further exception covers the construction material or components listed by the Government in the clause, where the Contracting Officer lists the excepted materials or indicates none [@CL-SUP-1878].

Construction material means an article, material, or supply brought to the construction site by the Contractor or a subcontractor for incorporation into the building or work [@CL-SUP-1882]. The term also includes an item brought to the site preassembled from articles, materials, or supplies [@CL-SUP-1947]. Emergency life safety systems that are discrete systems incorporated into a public building or work and produced as complete systems are evaluated as a single and distinct construction material [@CL-SUP-1948], regardless of when or how the individual parts or components of those systems are delivered to the construction site [@CL-SUP-3978]. Materials purchased directly by the Government are supplies, not construction material [@CL-SUP-1949].

## How it works

### What counts as domestic

For construction material that does not consist wholly or predominantly of iron or steel or a combination of both, domestic construction material means either an unmanufactured construction material mined or produced in the United States [@CL-SUP-1914] or, as the second alternative, a construction material manufactured in the United States if one of two conditions is met [@CL-SUP-3979]. The first condition is that the cost of its components mined, produced, or manufactured in the United States exceeds 60 percent of the cost of all its components, except that the percentage will be 65 percent for items delivered in calendar years 2024 through 2028 and 75 percent for items delivered starting in calendar year 2029 [@CL-SUP-1879], unless an alternate percentage is established for a contract in accordance with FAR 25.201(c) [@CL-SUP-1880]. Under that condition, components of foreign origin of the same class or kind for which nonavailability determinations have been made are treated as domestic and components of unknown origin are treated as foreign [@CL-SUP-1915]. The second condition is that the construction material is a COTS item [@CL-SUP-1915].

For components the Contractor purchased, the cost of components is the acquisition cost, including transportation costs to the place of incorporation into the end product (whether or not such costs are paid to a domestic firm), and any applicable duty (whether or not a duty-free entry certificate is issued) [@CL-SUP-1940]. For components the Contractor manufactured, it is all costs associated with the manufacture of the component, including transportation costs, plus allocable overhead costs but excluding profit [@CL-SUP-1941]. The cost of components does not include any costs associated with the manufacture of the construction material [@CL-SUP-1942].

For construction material that consists wholly or predominantly of iron or steel or a combination of both [@CL-SUP-3985], domestic construction material means a construction material manufactured in the United States if the cost of iron and steel not produced in the United States (excluding fasteners), as estimated in good faith by the contractor, constitutes less than 5 percent of the cost of all the components used in such construction material [@CL-SUP-1881]. Iron or steel is produced in the United States only if all its manufacturing processes take place in the United States, except metallurgical processes involving refinement of steel additives [@CL-SUP-1943]. The cost of iron and steel not produced in the United States includes, but is not limited to, the cost of iron or steel mill products, castings or forgings not produced in the United States that are used in the manufacture of the end product [@CL-SUP-1944]. It also includes a good faith estimate of the cost of all iron or steel components not produced in the United States, excluding COTS fasteners, and iron or steel components of unknown origin are treated as foreign [@CL-SUP-1945]. Predominantly of iron or steel means that the cost of the iron and steel content exceeds 50 percent of the total cost of all its components [@CL-SUP-1960], and for that definition the cost of iron and steel is the cost of the iron or steel mill products, castings or forgings used in the manufacture of the product plus a good faith estimate of the cost of iron or steel components, excluding COTS fasteners [@CL-SUP-1946].

### Exceptions

One exception covers the construction material or components listed by the Government in the clause, where the Contracting Officer lists the excepted materials or indicates none [@CL-SUP-1878]. For the request step in the FAR clause, see [Buy American for construction materials](sup-buy-american-construction-materials.md).

### Alternates

Alternate I adds definitions for South Caucasus/Central and South Asian (SC/CASA) state and SC/CASA state construction material to paragraph (a), and uses "domestic construction material or SC/CASA state construction material" instead of "domestic construction material" in the second sentence of paragraph (b) [@CL-SUP-1883]. Alternate II includes, in the definition of domestic construction material at paragraph (1)(ii)(A), the domestic content threshold that will apply to the entire contract period of performance [@CL-SUP-1884]. Alternate III includes the domestic content threshold that will apply to the entire period of performance and adds definitions for SC/CASA state and SC/CASA state construction material to paragraph (a) [@CL-SUP-1916].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor plans to bring three items to a building site. A valve valued at 20,000 is below the simplified acquisition threshold, so the small-purchase exception applies and the valve need not be domestic. A steel beam is made in the United States and is mostly iron or steel. Counting mill products, castings, forgings and other iron or steel components, treating any of unknown origin as foreign and leaving out fasteners, the contractor estimates in good faith that iron and steel not produced in the United States cost 2 percent of the cost of all its components, which is under 5 percent, so the beam is domestic construction material. Wall lighting is made abroad. Assume it is not information technology, not a COTS item, and the Contracting Officer listed none, so the contractor may not use it.

## Never confuse

The trade agreements version of this clause is a separate clause, covered on [the basic card for the trade agreements clause](sup-dfars-bop-construction-material-trade-agreements-clause.md) and [its alternates card](sup-dfars-bop-construction-material-trade-agreements-alternates.md): in the trade agreements version, the Contracting Officer has determined that the WTO GPA and Free Trade Agreements apply to the acquisition, and the Balance of Payments Program restrictions are therefore waived for designated country construction materials [@CL-SUP-1885], unlike this clause, which requires only domestic construction material except for the listed exceptions [@CL-SUP-1877]. The FAR clause for construction materials is also separate, covered on [Buy American for construction materials](sup-buy-american-construction-materials.md).
