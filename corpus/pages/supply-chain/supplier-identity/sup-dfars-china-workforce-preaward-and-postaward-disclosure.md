## Purpose

Shows which contracts are covered by the China workforce disclosure rules, what a covered entity discloses before and after award, and where the rules do not reach.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-SUP-1719] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 252.225-7057 [@CL-SUP-1719] and DFARS 252.225-7058 [@CL-SUP-1754] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-1717] |
| Applies to | Covered contracts, meaning DoD contracts or subcontracts over $5 million that are not contracts for commercial products and commercial services [@CL-SUP-1717]; offers for covered contracts [@CL-SUP-1720]; and contracts with the clause where DFARS 225.7021-4(b) prescribes it [@CL-SUP-1767] |

A covered contract is any DoD contract or subcontract with a value in excess of $5 million, not including contracts for commercial products and commercial services [@CL-SUP-1717]. A covered entity is any corporation, company, limited liability company, limited partnership, business trust, business association, or other similar entity, including any subsidiary thereof, performing work on a covered contract in the People's Republic of China [@CL-SUP-1757]. Performing work there includes doing so by leasing or owning real property used in the performance of the covered contract [@CL-SUP-1718].

Before award, DoD may not award a contract to an offeror that is a covered entity and proposes to employ one or more individuals who will perform work in the People's Republic of China on a covered contract, unless the offeror has disclosed its use of workforce and facilities in the People's Republic of China [@CL-SUP-1719]. After award, DoD may not award, extend, or exercise an option on a covered contract with a covered entity unless the entity submits each required disclosure of its use of workforce and facilities in the People's Republic of China [@CL-SUP-1754]. That bar applies if the covered entity employs one or more individuals who perform work in the People's Republic of China on a covered contract [@CL-SUP-1755].

## How it works

At the time of submission of an offer for a covered contract, an offeror that is a covered entity shall provide disclosures that include the total number of individuals who will perform work in the People's Republic of China and a description of the physical presence there, with street addresses [@CL-SUP-1720; @CL-SUP-1721]. After award, a Contractor that is a covered entity shall disclose, for the Government's fiscal years 2023 and 2024, its employment of individuals who perform work in the People's Republic of China on any covered contract [@CL-SUP-1722]. The postaward items are the total number of such individuals who perform work there on the covered contracts funded by DoD, and a description of the physical presence, including street addresses, where the work is performed [@CL-SUP-1756]. The Contractor shall insert the clause, including paragraph (c), in all subcontracts that meet the definition of a covered contract, without alteration other than to identify the appropriate parties [@CL-SUP-1723].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

Assume a company offers on a DoD contract worth 8 million that is not for commercial products or services. It has 20 employees who would work in the People's Republic of China from one leased office. The contract is covered, and the company is a covered entity, so its offer must disclose the 20 employees and the office address. Later, when DoD wants to exercise an option on the contract, the company must have submitted each required disclosure first. Had the contract been worth 4 million, it would not be a covered contract.

## Never confuse

The preaward provision is about the offer, and it bars award unless the disclosure was made [@CL-SUP-1719]. The postaward clause requires a Contractor that is a covered entity to disclose, for the Government's fiscal years 2023 and 2024, its employment of one or more individuals who perform work in the People's Republic of China on any covered contract [@CL-SUP-1722]. Contracts for commercial products and commercial services are outside the covered contract definition [@CL-SUP-1717]. For the prohibition on certain procurements from one region, see [Prohibition on certain procurements from the Xinjiang Uyghur Autonomous Region](sup-dfars-xinjiang-uyghur-region-prohibition.md). For restrictions tied to Communist Chinese military companies, see [Ball and roller bearings and items from Communist Chinese military companies](sup-dfars-bearings-and-communist-chinese-military-companies.md). For the representation about business operations with the Maduro regime [@CL-SUP-1714], see [Business operations with the Maduro regime](sup-dfars-maduro-regime-business-operations-representation-and-prohibition.md). For commercial flowdown generally, see [Subcontracts for commercial products and commercial services](../sourcing-evidence/sup-subcontracts-commercial-products-and-services.md).
