## Purpose

Shows which supplies qualify for duty-free entry under the DFARS clause, what the shipping documents, markings, customs forms and notice must carry, and what flows down to subcontracts.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-SUP-1784] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 252.225-7013 [@CL-SUP-1784; @CL-SUP-1790; @CL-SUP-1839] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-1784] |
| Applies to | Contracts with the duty-free entry clause, which its text says is prescribed in a DFARS section it names [@CL-SUP-1839]; the price paragraph covers the supplies it lists [@CL-SUP-1784; @CL-SUP-1785; @CL-SUP-1786] |

Unless the exemption paragraph applies or the supplies were imported before the date of the contract or the applicable subcontract, the contract price shall not include any amount for duty on the supplies the price paragraph lists [@CL-SUP-1784]. The list includes end items that are eligible products or qualifying country end products, and components produced or made in qualifying countries that are to be incorporated in United States-made end products to be delivered under the contract [@CL-SUP-1785]. It also lists other supplies for which the contractor estimates that duty will exceed $300 per shipment into the customs territory of the United States [@CL-SUP-1786]. One kind of eligible product is a designated country end product, as defined in the Trade Agreements clause of the contract [@CL-SUP-1783].

The contractor shall claim duty-free entry only for supplies that it intends to deliver to the Government under the contract, either as end items or components of end items [@CL-SUP-1822]. It shall pay duty on supplies, or any portion of them, that are diverted to nongovernmental use, other than scrap or salvage or a competitive sale made, directed, or authorized by the Contracting Officer [@CL-SUP-1787].

## How it works

### Shipping documents and markings

For foreign supplies for which the Government will issue duty-free entry certificates, shipping documents submitted to Customs consign the shipments to the appropriate recipient identified in the clause [@CL-SUP-1788]. They also include, among other listed information, the prime contract number, any delivery order number, the subcontract number for foreign supplies if applicable, and the identification of the carrier [@CL-SUP-1789]. The contractor marks the exterior of all packages with the words United States Government, Department of Defense, and the activity address number of the contract administration office administering the prime contract [@CL-SUP-1825].

### Customs forms

Except for shipments consigned to a military installation, the contractor prepares any customs forms required for the entry of foreign supplies into the customs territory of the United States in connection with the contract [@CL-SUP-1823]. When a shipment holds both supplies to be accorded duty-free entry and supplies that are not, the customs forms identify the eligible items [@CL-SUP-1824]. In the FAR's general definitions, the customs territory of the United States means the 50 States, the District of Columbia, and Puerto Rico [@CL-SUP-9542]. The RFO model text of FAR 2.101 defines the customs territory of the United States in the same words as the codified definition [@CL-SUP-9598; @CL-SUP-9542].

### Notice to the Administrative Contracting Officer

The contractor notifies the Administrative Contracting Officer (ACO) in writing of any purchase of eligible products or qualifying country supplies to be accorded duty-free entry that are to be imported into the customs territory of the United States for delivery to the Government or for incorporation in end items to be delivered to the Government [@CL-SUP-1790; @CL-SUP-4065], and gives the notice immediately upon award to the supplier [@CL-SUP-1791]. The notice carries the contractor's name, address, and Commercial and Government Entity (CAGE) code, the prime contract number and any delivery order number, and the total dollar value of the prime contract or delivery order [@CL-SUP-1826]. It also carries the last scheduled delivery date under the prime contract, the foreign supplier's name and address, and the number and total dollar value of the subcontract for foreign supplies [@CL-SUP-1827]. It adds the last scheduled delivery date under the subcontract, a list of items purchased, and an agreement to pay duty on supplies diverted to nongovernmental use [@CL-SUP-1828]. The cases excepted from that agreement are scrap or salvage and a competitive sale made, directed, or authorized by the Contracting Officer, and the notice also gives the country of origin and the scheduled delivery dates [@CL-SUP-1829].

### When the clause does not apply

The clause does not apply to purchases of eligible products or qualifying country supplies in connection with the contract when the supplies are identical in nature to supplies the contractor or a subcontractor buys in connection with its commercial business [@CL-SUP-1792], and it is also not economical or feasible to account for them so that duty-free claims stay within the amount bought for the contract [@CL-SUP-1793].

### Flowdown

The contractor inserts the substance of the clause in all subcontracts for qualifying country components, or for nonqualifying country components for which it estimates that duty will exceed $200 per unit [@CL-SUP-1794]. It requires subcontractors to put the contract number on shipping documents submitted to Customs for supplies for which duty-free entry is claimed [@CL-SUP-1795]. Applicable subcontracts also carry the ACO and contract administration office details and the information required by the first three items of the notice [@CL-SUP-1796].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

Assume the contract has this clause. A prime contractor buys a component made in a qualifying country for an end product made in the United States that it will deliver to the Government, and the component was not imported before the contract date.

The contract price leaves out duty on that component. Right after awarding the supplier order, the contractor sends the ACO a written notice. The shipping documents carry the prime contract number, the packages are marked, and the subcontract carries the clause because the component is a qualifying country component.

Separately, the contractor estimates duty of 400 on one shipment of other supplies. That is above 300 per shipment, so the price leaves out that duty as well. For a nonqualifying country component with estimated duty of 150 per unit, the flowdown threshold of 200 per unit is not reached, so the clause does not have to flow down on that ground.

## Never confuse

The per shipment amount of $300 in the price paragraph is not the per unit amount of $200 that triggers flowdown for nonqualifying country components [@CL-SUP-1786; @CL-SUP-1794]. A related card covers restrictions on certain foreign purchases and duty-free entry; see [Restrictions on certain foreign purchases and duty-free entry](sup-ofac-restrictions-and-duty-free-entry.md). For the trade agreement definitions the clause points to, see [DoD trade agreements clause and certificate](sup-dfars-trade-agreements-clause-and-certificate.md) [@CL-SUP-1783]. The reporting clause that splits payment requests by expenditures in the United States is a different duty; see [Identification of expenditures in the United States](sup-dfars-identification-of-expenditures-in-the-united-states.md) [@CL-SUP-1774].
