## Purpose

Shows which contingent fees are unallowable on foreign military sales, the duty not to act on exclusionary policies of foreign governments in staffing, and the boycott certification by a foreign person offeror.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-SUP-1724] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 252.225-7027 [@CL-SUP-1724], DFARS 252.225-7028 [@CL-SUP-1728] and DFARS 252.225-7031 [@CL-SUP-1730] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-1724] |
| Applies to | Foreign military sale contracts with the contingent fees clause where DFARS 225.7307(a) prescribes it [@CL-SUP-1768; @CL-SUP-1726], contracts with the exclusionary policies clause [@CL-SUP-1728], and solicitations with the boycott provision for foreign person offerors [@CL-SUP-1730] |

Except as provided in the paragraph on foreign military sales, contingent fees are generally an allowable cost, provided the fees are paid to a bona fide employee of the Contractor or to a bona fide established commercial or selling agency maintained by the Contractor for the purpose of securing business [@CL-SUP-1724; @CL-SUP-1725]. For foreign military sales, unless the contingent fees have been identified and payment approved in writing by the foreign customer before contract award, the contingent fees listed in that paragraph are unallowable [@CL-SUP-1726]. One listed category is contingent fees in any amount for sales to the governments named in the blank that the contract fills in [@CL-SUP-1758]. Another is contingent fees exceeding $50,000 per foreign military sale case, for sales to governments not listed in the paragraph before it [@CL-SUP-1727].

The Contractor and its subcontractors shall not take into account the exclusionary policies or practices of any foreign government in employing or assigning personnel, if the personnel will perform functions required by the contract in the United States or abroad [@CL-SUP-1728] and the policies or practices are based on race, religion, national origin, or sex [@CL-SUP-1729].

If the offeror is a foreign person, it certifies by submitting an offer that it does not comply with the Secondary Arab Boycott of Israel [@CL-SUP-1730]. It also certifies that it is not taking or knowingly agreeing to take any action, with respect to the Secondary Boycott of Israel by Arab countries, which the cited statute prohibits a United States person from taking [@CL-SUP-1759].

## How it works

The fee clause starts from general allowability and then removes the listed fees on foreign military sales unless the foreign customer approved them in writing before award [@CL-SUP-1724; @CL-SUP-1726]. The listed fees are those in any amount for the governments named in the blank, and those over the dollar figure per case for other governments [@CL-SUP-1758; @CL-SUP-1727]. The staffing clause binds the Contractor and its subcontractors when both of its conditions are met [@CL-SUP-1728; @CL-SUP-1729]. In the boycott provision, a foreign person offeror makes two certifications by submitting an offer [@CL-SUP-1730; @CL-SUP-1759]. These three sections print no contractor reporting duty, so none is established here; the nearest item is that the fees must have been identified and approved in writing by the foreign customer before award [@CL-SUP-1726].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

Assume a foreign military sale to a government not named in the first category carries a contingent fee of 70,000, and the foreign customer did not approve it in writing before award. The fee exceeds 50,000 per case, so it is among the listed unallowable fees. A fee of 30,000 under the same terms is below that category, and it is not unallowable on that ground. If the first category had named the buyer's government, a fee of any amount would be among the listed fees.

## Never confuse

Contingent fees are not unallowable in general, because the clause starts from general allowability for fees paid to the listed kinds of recipient [@CL-SUP-1724; @CL-SUP-1725]. The definition of contingent fees sits in a different clause; see [Covenant against contingent fees](../../compliance/regulatory-framework/cmp-contingent-fees.md). Whether any cost is allowable also depends on the wider tests in [Factors determining cost allowability](../../compliance/cas-allowability/cmp-allowability-factors.md). The staffing clause concerns the foreign government's policies [@CL-SUP-1728], and the boycott provision is a certification by a foreign person offeror [@CL-SUP-1730]. Sanctions-based purchase restrictions are a different topic; see [Restrictions on certain foreign purchases and duty-free entry](sup-ofac-restrictions-and-duty-free-entry.md). The Maduro regime provision is another representation made with the offer [@CL-SUP-1714]; see [Business operations with the Maduro regime](sup-dfars-maduro-regime-business-operations-representation-and-prohibition.md). A levy in a United Kingdom firm's price is a separate price item [@CL-SUP-1740]; see [Waiver of United Kingdom levies in evaluation and performance](sup-dfars-united-kingdom-levies-waiver-evaluation-and-subcontracts.md).
