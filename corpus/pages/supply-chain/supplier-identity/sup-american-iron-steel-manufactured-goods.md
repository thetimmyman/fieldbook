## Purpose

Explains the required use of American iron, steel and manufactured goods in construction under the Recovery Act and the Buy American statute. It covers the domestic preference, the listed and added exceptions, how a request to use foreign material is made and decided, and how the notice provision evaluates offers and alternate offers.

The clause. The contractor shall use only domestic construction material in performing the contract, except as provided in paragraphs (b)(3) and (b)(4) of the clause [@CL-SUP-2811]. The clause requires, unless an exception applies, that all manufactured construction material in the project is manufactured in the United States and, if the construction material consists wholly or predominantly of iron or steel, that the iron or steel was produced in the United States [@CL-SUP-2812]. Produced in the United States means that all manufacturing processes of the iron or steel must take place in the United States, except metallurgical processes involving refinement of steel additives [@CL-SUP-2878].

The domestic-material requirement does not apply to the construction material or components that the Government lists in the clause [@CL-SUP-2813]. The list is a bracketed fill-in. The Contracting Officer lists the excepted materials or indicates none, so the list is contract-specific and this card cannot say what any contract's list holds [@CL-SUP-2813].

The Contracting Officer may add other foreign construction material to that list if the Government determines that the cost of domestic construction material would be unreasonable [@CL-SUP-2879]. The cost of domestic manufactured construction material, when compared to the cost of comparable foreign manufactured construction material, is unreasonable when the cumulative cost of such material will increase the cost of the contract by more than 25 percent [@CL-SUP-2814]. The cost of domestic unmanufactured construction material is unreasonable when the cost of such material exceeds the cost of comparable foreign unmanufactured construction material by more than 20 percent [@CL-SUP-2815]. Another ground is that the construction material is not mined, produced or manufactured in the United States in sufficient and reasonably available quantities and of a satisfactory quality [@CL-SUP-2880]. A further ground is that applying the restriction of section 1605 of the Recovery Act to a particular manufactured construction material would be inconsistent with the public interest [@CL-SUP-2881]. The same ground also covers applying the Buy American statute to a particular unmanufactured construction material where that would be impracticable or inconsistent with the public interest [@CL-SUP-2882].

A contractor request to use foreign construction material under paragraph (b)(4) shall include adequate information for Government evaluation, including a description of the foreign and domestic construction materials and the unit of measure [@CL-SUP-2816]. The request shall also include the quantity, the cost, the time of delivery or availability, the location of the construction project, the name and address of the proposed supplier, and a detailed justification of the reason for use of foreign construction materials cited in accordance with paragraph (b)(4) of the clause [@CL-SUP-2883]. A request based on unreasonable cost shall include a reasonable survey of the market and a completed cost comparison table in the format in paragraph (d) of the clause [@CL-SUP-2884]. The cost of construction material shall include all delivery costs to the construction site and any applicable duty [@CL-SUP-2885].

Any contractor request for a determination submitted after contract award shall explain why the contractor could not reasonably foresee the need for the determination and could not have requested it before contract award [@CL-SUP-2886]. If the contractor does not submit a satisfactory explanation, the Contracting Officer need not make a determination [@CL-SUP-2887]. If the Government determines after contract award that an exception to section 1605 of the Recovery Act or the Buy American statute applies and the Contracting Officer and the Contractor negotiate adequate consideration, the Contracting Officer will modify the contract [@CL-SUP-2888]. When the basis for the exception is the unreasonable cost of a domestic construction material, adequate consideration is not less than the differential established in paragraph (b)(4)(i) of the clause [@CL-SUP-2889]. Unless the Government determines that an exception to section 1605 of the Recovery Act or the Buy American statute applies, use of foreign construction material is noncompliant with that section or the Buy American statute [@CL-SUP-2890].

The notice provision. An offeror requesting a determination of inapplicability of section 1605 of the Recovery Act or the Buy American statute should submit the request to the Contracting Officer in time to allow a determination before submission of offers, and shall include the information and applicable supporting data required by paragraphs (c) and (d) of the clause at FAR 52.225-21 [@CL-SUP-2820]. If the offeror has not requested a determination before submitting its offer, or has not received a response to a previous request, the offeror shall include the information and supporting data in the offer [@CL-SUP-2891].

If the Government determines in accordance with FAR 25.604 that an exception based on unreasonable cost of domestic construction material applies, the Government will evaluate an offer requesting exception to the requirements of section 1605 of the Recovery Act or the Buy American statute [@CL-SUP-2892]. The Government evaluates such an offer by adding to the offered price of the contract 25 percent of the offered price, if foreign manufactured construction material is incorporated in the offer based on an exception for unreasonable cost of comparable manufactured domestic construction material [@CL-SUP-2817]. The evaluation also adds 20 percent of the cost of foreign unmanufactured construction material included in the offer based on an exception for the unreasonable cost of comparable domestic unmanufactured construction material [@CL-SUP-2818]. If the solicitation specifies award on the basis of factors in addition to cost or price, the Contracting Officer will apply the evaluation factors as specified in paragraph (c)(1) and use the evaluated price in determining the offer that represents the best value to the Government [@CL-SUP-2893]. Unless paragraph (c)(2) applies, if two or more offers are equal in price, the Contracting Officer will give preference to an offer that does not include foreign construction material excepted at the request of the offeror on the basis of unreasonable cost [@CL-SUP-2819].

When an offer includes foreign construction material not listed by the Government in paragraph (b)(3) of the clause at FAR 52.225-21, the offeror also may submit an alternate offer based on use of equivalent domestic construction material [@CL-SUP-2894]. If an alternate offer is submitted, the offeror shall submit a separate Standard Form 1442 for the alternate offer [@CL-SUP-2895]. The offeror also submits a separate cost comparison table prepared in accordance with paragraphs (c) and (d) of the clause at FAR 52.225-21, for the offer that is based on the use of any foreign construction material for which the Government has not yet determined an exception applies [@CL-SUP-2896]. If the Government determines that a particular exception requested in accordance with paragraph (c) of the clause at FAR 52.225-21 does not apply, it evaluates only those offers based on use of the equivalent domestic construction material [@CL-SUP-2897]. The offeror shall be required to furnish such domestic construction material [@CL-SUP-2898]. An offer based on use of the foreign construction material for which an exception was requested will be rejected as nonresponsive if the acquisition is conducted by sealed bidding, or may be accepted if revised during negotiations [@CL-SUP-2899].

Under Alternate I, an offeror requesting a determination regarding the inapplicability of section 1605 of the Recovery Act or the Buy American statute shall submit the request with its offer, including the information and applicable supporting data required by paragraphs (c) and (d) of the clause at FAR 52.225-21 [@CL-SUP-2900].

## How it works

The clause binds the contractor during performance, and the notice provision governs requests and the evaluation of offers before award [@CL-SUP-2811; @CL-SUP-2820].

Domestic preference. The contractor uses only domestic construction material unless the material is on the Government's list or is added under paragraph (b)(4) [@CL-SUP-2811; @CL-SUP-2813; @CL-SUP-2879]. For manufactured construction material the clause requires manufacture in the United States, and for material consisting wholly or predominantly of iron or steel it requires that the iron or steel was produced in the United States [@CL-SUP-2812]. Classify a material before choosing between the 25 percent and 20 percent grounds [@CL-SUP-2814; @CL-SUP-2815]. Manufactured construction material means any construction material that is not unmanufactured construction material [@CL-SUP-3038]. Unmanufactured construction material is raw material brought to the construction site for incorporation into the building or work that has not been processed into a specific form and shape, or combined with other raw material to create a material that has different properties than the properties of the individual raw materials [@CL-SUP-3039]. Domestic construction material includes an unmanufactured construction material mined or produced in the United States, to which the Buy American statute applies [@CL-SUP-3040]. It also includes a manufactured construction material that is manufactured in the United States and, if it consists wholly or predominantly of iron or steel, whose iron or steel was produced in the United States, to which section 1605 of the Recovery Act applies [@CL-SUP-3041]. The clause also implements the Buy American statute by providing a preference for unmanufactured construction material mined or produced in the United States over unmanufactured construction material mined or produced in a foreign country [@CL-SUP-3042].

Terms. Construction material is an article, material or supply brought to the construction site by the contractor or a subcontractor for incorporation into the building or work, and includes an item brought to the site preassembled from articles, materials or supplies [@CL-SUP-3043]. Emergency life safety systems, such as emergency lighting, fire alarm and audio evacuation systems, that are discrete systems incorporated into a public building or work and are produced as complete systems [@CL-SUP-3044] are evaluated as a single and distinct construction material regardless of when or how their individual parts or components are delivered to the construction site [@CL-SUP-3045]. A component is an article, material or supply incorporated directly into a construction material [@CL-SUP-3046]. In the notice provision the terms construction material, domestic construction material, foreign construction material, manufactured construction material, steel and unmanufactured construction material are defined in the clause [@CL-SUP-3036], FAR clause 52.225-21 [@CL-SUP-3037].

Iron and steel. A manufacturing process of the iron or steel carried out abroad means the iron or steel was not produced in the United States, with one stated exception for metallurgical processes involving refinement of steel additives [@CL-SUP-2878].

Cost grounds. For domestic manufactured construction material the test is whether its cumulative cost will increase the cost of the contract by more than 25 percent when compared with comparable foreign manufactured construction material [@CL-SUP-2814]. For domestic unmanufactured construction material the test is whether its cost exceeds that of comparable foreign unmanufactured construction material by more than 20 percent [@CL-SUP-2815]. Two further grounds use no percentage. One is insufficient and not reasonably available quantities of a satisfactory quality [@CL-SUP-2880]. The other is inconsistency with the public interest for manufactured material, and impracticability or inconsistency with the public interest for unmanufactured material [@CL-SUP-2881; @CL-SUP-2882].

The request. A request carries the information and description listed in the clause [@CL-SUP-2816; @CL-SUP-2883]. A request based on unreasonable cost also carries a market survey and a cost comparison table [@CL-SUP-2884]. The cost it states includes delivery to the construction site and any applicable duty [@CL-SUP-2885]. To permit evaluation of a request based on unreasonable cost, the contractor shall include the information in the data format and any applicable supporting data based on the survey of suppliers [@CL-SUP-3047]. The data format is a cost comparison table with columns for construction material description, unit of measure, quantity and cost in dollars, and rows for foreign and domestic construction material for each item [@CL-SUP-3048]. It also calls for the name, address, telephone number and contact of each supplier surveyed, with a copy or summary of its response, other applicable supporting information, and all delivery costs to the construction site [@CL-SUP-3049]. Those bracketed entries are filled in by the contractor for each request and are contract-specific.

After award. A request made after award must explain why the need could not reasonably be foreseen and could not have been raised before award [@CL-SUP-2886]. Without a satisfactory explanation the Contracting Officer need not make a determination [@CL-SUP-2887]. If the Government finds an exception applies and adequate consideration is negotiated, the Contracting Officer modifies the contract [@CL-SUP-2888]. For an unreasonable-cost exception the consideration is not less than the differential established in paragraph (b)(4)(i) [@CL-SUP-2889]. Foreign construction material used without an exception is noncompliant [@CL-SUP-2890].

Evaluating offers. The evaluation adjustments apply once the Government determines, in accordance with FAR 25.604, that an unreasonable-cost exception applies [@CL-SUP-2892]. The Government then adds 25 percent of the offered price of the contract where foreign manufactured construction material is incorporated on that ground [@CL-SUP-2817]. It adds 20 percent of the cost of foreign unmanufactured construction material included on that ground [@CL-SUP-2818]. Where the solicitation specifies factors in addition to cost or price, the Contracting Officer applies those factors and uses the evaluated price to find the best value [@CL-SUP-2893]. Equal-priced offers go to the one that includes no foreign material excepted at the offeror's request on the ground of unreasonable cost, unless paragraph (c)(2) applies [@CL-SUP-2819].

Alternate offers. An offeror whose offer includes foreign construction material not on the Government's list may also submit an alternate offer based on equivalent domestic construction material [@CL-SUP-2894]. The alternate offer needs its own Standard Form 1442 [@CL-SUP-2895]. The separate cost comparison table goes with the offer based on foreign material for which no exception has yet been determined to apply [@CL-SUP-2896]. If a requested exception is determined not to apply, the Government evaluates only offers based on equivalent domestic construction material, and the offeror shall be required to furnish it [@CL-SUP-2897; @CL-SUP-2898]. The offer based on the foreign material is rejected as nonresponsive in sealed bidding and may be accepted if revised during negotiations [@CL-SUP-2899].

Alternate I. Under Alternate I the request goes with the offer [@CL-SUP-2900].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A negotiated construction solicitation includes the notice provision, with no alternate, and the contract will include the clause. The Government's list in paragraph (b)(3) of the clause says none. Award is on price alone, so paragraph (c)(2) of the provision does not apply. Each offer states whether it uses foreign material, and each request for an exception is submitted with the offer and carries the market survey, the cost comparison table and delivered costs.

Offeror A offers $2,000,000 using only domestic construction material. Offeror D offers $1,990,000 using only domestic construction material.

Offeror B offers $1,800,000 and includes foreign pre-cast panels. The panels are not raw material, so they are manufactured construction material and not unmanufactured construction material. B's request says that using domestic panels would increase its cost by $540,000, which is 30 percent of $1,800,000. That is more than 25 percent, so the unreasonable-cost ground is supported.

Offeror C offers $1,980,000 and includes foreign gravel costing $50,000. The gravel is raw material brought to the site that has not been processed into a specific form and shape or combined with other raw material, so it is unmanufactured construction material. Comparable domestic gravel costs $62,000, which is $12,000 or 24 percent more. That exceeds 20 percent, so the unreasonable-cost ground is supported.

The Government determines, in accordance with FAR 25.604, that both exceptions apply, and it evaluates the offers. For B it adds 25 percent of the offered price of the contract: 0.25 x $1,800,000 = $450,000, which gives $2,250,000. For C it adds 20 percent of the cost of the foreign gravel: 0.20 x $50,000 = $10,000, which gives $1,990,000. The evaluated prices are therefore A at $2,000,000, B at $2,250,000, C at $1,990,000 and D at $1,990,000.

C and D are equal in price. D includes no foreign construction material excepted at its request, so the Contracting Officer gives preference to D.

B also submitted an alternate offer based on equivalent domestic panels at $2,340,000, with a separate Standard Form 1442 and a separate cost comparison table for the offer based on foreign panels. The table has one item, pre-cast panels, with a foreign cost row and a domestic cost row, and it attaches the name, address, telephone number and contact of each supplier surveyed with a summary of each response. Suppose the Government had instead determined that B's exception does not apply. It would evaluate only offers based on equivalent domestic panels, B's alternate at $2,340,000 would be evaluated, and B would be required to furnish domestic panels. The offer based on foreign panels could be accepted only if revised during negotiations.

Separately, the contractor buys steel beams that consist wholly of steel. The steel was melted abroad, and the beams were only cut and drilled in the United States. Not all manufacturing processes of the steel took place in the United States, so the steel was not produced in the United States and the beams are not domestic construction material unless an exception applies. Had the only work done abroad been refinement of a steel additive, that metallurgical process falls within the stated exception and would not by itself prevent the steel from counting as produced in the United States.

## Never confuse

The two 25 percent figures work differently [@CL-SUP-2814; @CL-SUP-2817]. In the clause, 25 percent is the test of whether the cumulative cost of domestic manufactured construction material increases the contract cost too much [@CL-SUP-2814]. In the provision, 25 percent is added to the offered price of the contract when evaluating an offer [@CL-SUP-2817]. It is a share of the offered price and not of the cost of the foreign material [@CL-SUP-2817].

The two 20 percent figures also differ [@CL-SUP-2815; @CL-SUP-2818]. In the clause, 20 percent tests the cost of domestic unmanufactured construction material against comparable foreign material [@CL-SUP-2815]. In the provision, 20 percent of the cost of the foreign unmanufactured construction material is added to the offered price [@CL-SUP-2818].

The clause is for performance and the provision is for offers. The clause on construction materials under trade agreements uses different tests and is covered on [Buy American, construction materials under trade agreements](sup-buy-american-construction-trade-agreements.md). Its notice provision is covered on [Notice of Buy American requirement, construction materials under trade agreements](sup-notice-buy-american-construction-trade-agreements.md). The plain construction materials clause is covered on [Buy American, construction materials](sup-buy-american-construction-materials.md).
