## Purpose

Shows what the satellite services clause limits, which countries count as covered foreign countries, the listed exceptions, and what the offeror represents in the matching provision.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-SUP-1496; @CL-SUP-1499] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 252.225-7051 [@CL-SUP-1496] and DFARS 252.225-7049 [@CL-SUP-1499] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-1496] |
| Applies to | Satellite services provided under contracts with the clause, and offers with the representations provision [@CL-SUP-1496; @CL-SUP-1499] |

Unless specified in its offer, the Contractor shall not provide satellite services under the contract that are from a covered foreign country or, except as provided in the exception paragraph, use satellites of the kinds the clause goes on to list [@CL-SUP-1496]. A covered foreign country is China, North Korea, the Russian Federation, or any country that is a state sponsor of terrorism [@CL-SUP-1495]. The second item of the limitation covers satellite services that, except as provided in the exception paragraph, use satellites that will be designed or manufactured in a covered foreign country or by an entity controlled in whole or in part by, or acting on behalf of, the government of a covered foreign country [@CL-SUP-1522]. The limitation's other listed group of satellites is one launched outside the United States using a launch vehicle that is designed or manufactured in a covered foreign country, among other listed launch vehicle tests [@CL-SUP-1523]. A further listed launch vehicle test is a launch vehicle provided by the government of a covered foreign country or by an entity controlled in whole or in part by, or acting on behalf of, the government of a covered foreign country [@CL-SUP-1524]. One listed exception to the satellite-use limitation is a launch that occurs prior to December 31, 2022 [@CL-SUP-1497]. A second listed exception covers a satellite service provider with a contract or other agreement relating to launch services that, prior to June 10, 2018, was either fully paid for by the provider or covered by a legally binding commitment of the provider to pay for the services [@CL-SUP-1498].

In the representations provision, unless an exception is determined to apply in accordance with DFARS 225.772-4, no contract for commercial satellite services may be awarded to a foreign entity if the Under Secretary of Defense for Acquisition and Sustainment or the Under Secretary of Defense for Policy reasonably believes a condition that the provision goes on to list [@CL-SUP-1525]. The first listed condition is that the foreign entity is an entity in which the government of a covered foreign country has an ownership interest that enables the government to affect satellite operations [@CL-SUP-1526]. Two further listed conditions are that the foreign entity plans to, or is expected to, provide satellite services under the contract from a covered foreign country, and that entering into such contract would create an unacceptable cybersecurity risk for DoD [@CL-SUP-1527].

One listed representation in the provision is that the Offeror states whether it is a foreign entity in which the government of a covered foreign country has an ownership interest that enables the government to affect satellite operations [@CL-SUP-1499]. The provision lists further representations that this card does not set out [@CL-SUP-1499]. If it is later determined that the Offeror knowingly rendered an erroneous representation, the Contracting Officer may terminate the resulting contract for default in addition to other remedies available to the Government [@CL-SUP-1500].

## How it works

The provision sits in the solicitation, and the clause limits what the Contractor may provide under the contract. The Offeror's representations ask, among other things, whether the Offeror is a foreign entity in which the government of a covered foreign country has an ownership interest that enables the government to affect satellite operations [@CL-SUP-1499]. The clause then limits the satellite services, subject to what the offer specified and to the listed exceptions [@CL-SUP-1496; @CL-SUP-1497; @CL-SUP-1498]. A knowingly erroneous representation can lead to termination for default and other remedies [@CL-SUP-1500]. The provision's award prohibition turns on the reasonable belief of an Under Secretary of Defense about the listed conditions [@CL-SUP-1525].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A satellite was launched in March 2021 and was manufactured in a covered foreign country. The contractor's offer does not specify it.

Answer: the launch exception covers the satellite-use limitation for that launch. It does not cover the separate limitation on satellite services that are from a covered foreign country.

## Never confuse

The representations provision asks the offeror to state facts, and the clause sets the limit on what is provided; the representation does not itself set the limit [@CL-SUP-1499; @CL-SUP-1496]. State sponsors of terrorism are one part of the covered foreign country definition [@CL-SUP-1495]. The disclosure provision lists, as of its date, Iran, North Korea, and Syria as state sponsors of terrorism [@CL-SUP-1532]. That disclosure of ownership or control by the government of a country that is a state sponsor of terrorism is a different provision, covered on [Disclosure of ownership or control by a state sponsor of terrorism](sup-dfars-state-sponsor-of-terrorism-ownership-disclosure.md). The clause on star trackers restricts a satellite component and is a different clause, covered on [Restrictions on hand tools, personal protective equipment and satellite components](sup-dfars-hand-tools-ppe-and-satellite-components-restrictions.md).
