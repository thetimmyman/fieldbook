## Purpose

Shows the convictions and civil judgments, the serious violations of contract performance, and the other causes that support debarment by the suspending and debarring official.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-SUP-0766] |
| Codified citation | FAR 9.406-2 [@CL-SUP-0766; @CL-SUP-0775] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-0766] |
| Applies to | Contractors and subcontractors considered for debarment by the suspending and debarring official [@CL-SUP-0766; @CL-SUP-0775] |

The suspending and debarring official may debar a contractor for a conviction of or civil judgment for the offenses listed in the FAR [@CL-SUP-0766]. One such offense is commission of fraud or a criminal offense in connection with obtaining, attempting to obtain, or performing a public contract or subcontract [@CL-SUP-0767].

The official may also debar a contractor or subcontractor based on any other cause of so serious or compelling a nature that it affects the present responsibility of the contractor or subcontractor [@CL-SUP-0766; @CL-SUP-0775].

The FAR's general definitions say conviction means a judgment or conviction of a criminal offense by any court of competent jurisdiction, whether entered upon a verdict or a plea, and includes a conviction entered upon a plea of nolo contendere [@CL-SUP-9537]. The first sentence of the RFO model text definition of conviction in FAR 2.101 reads the same as the codified first sentence [@CL-SUP-9594; @CL-SUP-9537]. For use in subpart 9.4, that definition points to the definition at 9.403 [@CL-SUP-9538].

## How it works

The first group of causes rests on a conviction or civil judgment [@CL-SUP-0766]. It includes violation of Federal or State antitrust statutes relating to the submission of offers, and embezzlement, theft, forgery, bribery, falsification or destruction of records, false statements, tax evasion, violating Federal criminal tax laws, or receiving stolen property [@CL-SUP-0766; @CL-SUP-0768], and any other offense indicating a lack of business integrity or business honesty that seriously and directly affects present responsibility [@CL-SUP-0766; @CL-SUP-0769]. A second group rests on a preponderance of the evidence [@CL-SUP-0770], and includes a violation of the terms of a Government contract or subcontract so serious as to justify debarment, such as willful failure to perform or a history of failure or unsatisfactory performance [@CL-SUP-0770; @CL-SUP-0771]. It also includes delinquent Federal taxes in an amount that exceeds the threshold at the cross-referenced section on responsibility representations [@CL-SUP-0770; @CL-SUP-0772]. That section sets the amount in its notification duty, which is an amount that exceeds $15,000 [@CL-SUP-0791]. Federal taxes are considered delinquent if both listed criteria apply: the tax liability is finally determined [@CL-SUP-0773], and the taxpayer is delinquent in making payment [@CL-SUP-0774]. A tax liability is finally determined if it has been assessed, and is not finally determined if there is a pending administrative or judicial challenge [@CL-SUP-0805]. A taxpayer is not delinquent where enforced collection action is precluded [@CL-SUP-0774].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A former officer of a contractor is convicted of bribery connected with a subcontract award. A different contractor has repeated late deliveries across several contracts, with no conviction or judgment.

Answer: the first matches a cause that rests on a conviction. The second can be debarred on a preponderance of the evidence only if the performance violation is so serious as to justify debarment.

## Never confuse

A cause for debarment is not a cause for suspension. A debarment on a listed offense rests on a conviction or civil judgment [@CL-SUP-0766], while suspension rests on adequate evidence of a suspected cause [@CL-SUP-0776] and is covered on the [Causes for suspension](sup-causes-for-suspension.md) card. The effect of a listing is on the [SAM exclusion records and the effect of listing](sup-sam-exclusion-records-and-effect-of-listing.md) card, and the length of a debarment is on the [Period and scope of debarment](sup-debarment-period-and-scope.md) card.
