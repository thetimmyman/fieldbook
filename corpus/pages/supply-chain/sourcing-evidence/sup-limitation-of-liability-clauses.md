## Purpose

Shows what loss of Government property the contractor is not liable for after acceptance under each of the three limitation of liability clauses, the exceptions, and how the high-value items clause differs.

## The rule

|---|---|

| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-1180] |
| Applies to | Loss of or damage to Government property after acceptance that results from defects or deficiencies, under a contract that includes the matching clause [@CL-SUP-1180; @CL-SUP-1183; @CL-SUP-1185] |

The limit in paragraph (a) of the basic clause applies except as provided in paragraphs (b) and (c) of the clause and except for remedies expressly provided elsewhere in the contract [@CL-SUP-1201]. Its operative words are that the contractor shall not be liable for loss of or damage to Government property, excluding the supplies delivered, that occurs after Government acceptance of the supplies and results from defects or deficiencies in them [@CL-SUP-1180]. The exceptions in the later paragraphs follow [@CL-SUP-1181; @CL-SUP-1182]. The basic limitation does not apply when a defect or deficiency in, or the Government's acceptance of, the supplies results from willful misconduct or lack of good faith on the part of any of the contractor's managerial personnel [@CL-SUP-1181]. If the contractor carries insurance, or has established a reserve for self-insurance, covering liability for loss or damage suffered by the Government through purchase or use of the supplies, the contractor shall be liable to the Government, to the extent of that insurance or reserve, for loss of or damage to Government property occurring after Government acceptance of, and resulting from any defects or deficiencies in, the supplies delivered [@CL-SUP-9781; @CL-SUP-1182; @CL-SUP-9782].

The limit in paragraph (a) of the high-value items clause applies except as provided in paragraphs (b) through (e) of the clause, and notwithstanding any other provision of the contract [@CL-SUP-1202]. Under paragraph (b), the limit does not apply when a defect or deficiency in, or the Government's acceptance of, the supplies results from willful misconduct or lack of good faith on the part of any of the contractor's managerial personnel [@CL-SUP-9677]. Under paragraph (c), if the contractor carries insurance, or has established a reserve for self-insurance, covering liability for loss or damage suffered by the Government through purchase or use of the supplies required to be delivered under the contract, the contractor shall be liable to the Government, to the extent of the insurance or reserve, for loss of or damage to Government property occurring after Government acceptance of, and resulting from any defects or deficiencies in, the supplies delivered [@CL-SUP-9678; @CL-SUP-9679]. Paragraphs (d) and (e) are covered below. Its operative words are that the contractor shall not be liable for loss of or damage to Government property, including the supplies delivered, that occurs after Government acceptance of the supplies and results from defects or deficiencies in them [@CL-SUP-1183]. The high-value items clause does not diminish the contractor's obligations, to the extent that they arise otherwise under the contract, relating to correction, repair, replacement, or other relief for any defect or deficiency in supplies delivered [@CL-SUP-1184]. Unless the contract is a cost-reimbursement contract, if loss or damage occurs and correction, repair, or replacement is not feasible or desired by the Government, the contractor shall, as determined by the contracting officer, give the relief that the clause lists [@CL-SUP-1203]. The listed relief is to pay the Government the amount it would have cost the contractor to make correction, repair, or replacement before the loss or damage occurred, or to provide other equitable relief [@CL-SUP-1204]. The high-value items clause does not limit or otherwise affect the Government's rights under clauses, if included in the contract, that cover warranty of technical data, ground and flight risks or aircraft flight risks, or Government property [@CL-SUP-1205].

The limit in paragraph (a) of the services clause applies except as provided in its paragraphs (b) and (c) and except to the extent that the contractor is expressly responsible under the contract for deficiencies in the services required to be performed under it, including any materials furnished in conjunction with those services [@CL-SUP-1186; @CL-SUP-9783]. Under paragraph (b), the limit does not apply when a defect or deficiency in, or the Government's acceptance of, services performed or materials furnished results from willful misconduct or lack of good faith on the part of any of the contractor's managerial personnel [@CL-SUP-9683]. Under paragraph (c), if the contractor carries insurance, or has established a reserve for self-insurance, covering liability for loss or damage suffered by the Government through the contractor's performance of services or furnishing of materials under the contract, the contractor shall be liable to the Government, to the extent of the insurance or reserve, for loss of or damage to Government property occurring after Government acceptance of, and resulting from any defects and deficiencies in, services performed or materials furnished [@CL-SUP-9684; @CL-SUP-9685]. Its operative words are that the contractor shall not be liable for loss of or damage to Government property that occurs after Government acceptance of services and results from defects or deficiencies in the services performed or materials furnished [@CL-SUP-1185].

## How it works

Each clause's first paragraph states a limit on the contractor's liability for loss of or damage to Government property that occurs after acceptance and results from defects or deficiencies [@CL-SUP-1180; @CL-SUP-1183; @CL-SUP-1185]. The basic clause leaves out the supplies delivered, while the high-value items clause includes them [@CL-SUP-1180; @CL-SUP-1183]. The exceptions for managerial misconduct and for insurance or a self-insurance reserve are stated in the basic clause [@CL-SUP-1181; @CL-SUP-1182]. The high-value items and services clauses print paragraphs on the same two subjects, as set out above [@CL-SUP-9677; @CL-SUP-9678; @CL-SUP-9683; @CL-SUP-9684]. The high-value items clause adds a rule for payment or other equitable relief when correction, repair or replacement is not feasible or desired, and that rule does not apply to a cost-reimbursement contract [@CL-SUP-1203; @CL-SUP-1204].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text from February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. In the three clauses, paragraphs (a) and (b) say "of this clause" where the codified text says "below" or "above" [@CL-SUP-3446; @CL-SUP-3448; @CL-SUP-3451; @CL-SUP-3457]. The RFO text of the basic clause states the limit on the contractor's liability in paragraph (a), with its exceptions [@CL-SUP-3446; @CL-SUP-3447; @CL-SUP-1201; @CL-SUP-1180], the managerial misconduct exception in paragraph (b) [@CL-SUP-3448; @CL-SUP-1181] and the insurance or self-insurance reserve rule in paragraph (c) [@CL-SUP-3449; @CL-SUP-3450; @CL-SUP-1182]. The RFO text of the high-value items clause states the limit in paragraph (a), with its exceptions [@CL-SUP-3451; @CL-SUP-3452; @CL-SUP-1202; @CL-SUP-1183], the rule that the clause does not diminish the contractor's correction obligations [@CL-SUP-3453; @CL-SUP-1184], the relief rule that does not apply to a cost-reimbursement contract [@CL-SUP-3454; @CL-SUP-1203] and the saving for clauses on warranty of technical data, ground and flight risks or aircraft flight risks, and Government property [@CL-SUP-3456; @CL-SUP-1205]. The RFO text of the services clause states the limit in paragraph (a), with its exceptions [@CL-SUP-3457; @CL-SUP-3458; @CL-SUP-1186; @CL-SUP-1185]. The RFO high-value items clause also prints the managerial misconduct exception in paragraph (b), under which the limit does not apply when a defect, deficiency or Government acceptance of the supplies results from willful misconduct or lack of good faith on the part of any of the contractor's managerial personnel [@CL-SUP-9680], and the insurance or self-insurance reserve rule in paragraph (c), under which a contractor that carries insurance or has such a reserve covering the loss is liable to the Government, to the extent of the insurance or reserve, for loss of or damage to Government property occurring after acceptance and resulting from defects or deficiencies in the supplies [@CL-SUP-9681; @CL-SUP-9682]. The RFO services clause prints the same two subjects in its paragraphs (b) and (c) [@CL-SUP-9686; @CL-SUP-9687; @CL-SUP-9688; @CL-SUP-9689]. In paragraph (d)(2) of the high-value items clause, the RFO text says the two items of relief are printed as (i) and (ii) with no "or" between them [@CL-SUP-3455], where the codified text joins them with "or" [@CL-SUP-1204].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor delivers 10 control units, and a defect in one unit, found after acceptance, damages a Government test bench worth 80,000. Under the basic clause, with no managerial misconduct, no insurance and no remedy expressly provided elsewhere in the contract, the contractor is not liable for the bench. The unit itself is not within that limit, so other contract terms govern it. If the contractor carries insurance of 50,000 that covers this liability, it is liable to that extent. The answer: no liability for the bench without insurance, and up to 50,000 with it.

## Never confuse

The limit concerns loss of or damage to Government property after acceptance, and it is not a bar on correction: the high-value items clause does not diminish the contractor's correction obligations, to the extent that they arise otherwise under the contract [@CL-SUP-1184]. The basic clause leaves out the supplies delivered, while the high-value items clause includes them [@CL-SUP-1180; @CL-SUP-1183]. The warranty clauses are separate, for example [Warranty of supplies of a noncomplex nature](sup-warranty-noncomplex-supplies.md), [Warranty of supplies of a complex nature](sup-warranty-complex-supplies.md), [Warranty of systems and equipment](sup-warranty-systems-and-equipment.md) and [Warranty of services and warranty of construction](sup-warranty-services-and-construction.md). The general policy and prescription for these clauses are on [Contractor liability for Government property loss after acceptance](sup-contractor-liability-after-acceptance.md), and warranty remedies are on [Warranties: criteria, terms and remedies](sup-warranties.md). In the high-value items clause, the codified text joins the two forms of relief with "or" [@CL-SUP-1204], while the RFO text prints them as items (i) and (ii) with no "or" between them [@CL-SUP-3455].
