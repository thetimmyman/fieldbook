## Purpose

Shows when war-hazard benefit costs are allowable, what the Government reimburses for detention benefits paid to a captured employee, and when it does not.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-SUP-1411] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 252.228-7000 and 252.228-7003 [@CL-SUP-1411; @CL-SUP-1414] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-1411] |
| Applies to | Employee war-hazard benefits under the Workers' Compensation and War-Hazard Insurance clause, and detention benefits paid to a captured person [@CL-SUP-1411; @CL-SUP-1414] |

Costs of providing employee war-hazard benefits in accordance with paragraph (b) of the Workers' Compensation and War-Hazard Insurance clause are allowable if the contractor meets all three listed conditions [@CL-SUP-1411; @CL-SUP-4163]. The first is submitting proof of loss files to support payment or denial of each claim [@CL-SUP-1411]. The second is, subject to contracting officer approval, making lump sum final settlement of any open claims and obtaining necessary release documents within one year of the expiration or termination of the contract, unless the contracting officer extends that period [@CL-SUP-1412]. The third is providing the contracting officer, at the time of final settlement of the contract, an investigation report and evaluation of any potential claim and an estimate of the dollar amount involved should the potential claim mature [@CL-SUP-4163]. The cost of insurance for liabilities reimbursable under the Workers' Compensation and War-Hazard Insurance clause is not allowable [@CL-SUP-1413].

## How it works

If the contractor, under an agreement entered into before capture, is obligated to pay and has paid detention benefits to a captured person or the person's dependents, the Government will reimburse the contractor up to the lesser of two listed amounts [@CL-SUP-1414]. The listed amounts are the total wage or salary being paid at the time of capture due from the contractor to the captured person for the period of detention, and the amount that would have been payable if the detention had occurred under circumstances covered by the War Hazards Compensation Act [@CL-SUP-1415]. The Government's obligation applies to the entire period of detention, except that it is subject to the availability of funds from which payment can be made [@CL-SUP-4164]. The contractor shall not be reimbursed under the capture and detention clause for payments made if the employees were entitled to compensation for capture and detention under that Act [@CL-SUP-1416].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

Before capture, a contractor agrees to pay a captured employee's salary during detention and pays 40,000. The salary due for the period was 40,000, and the Act would have paid 30,000. The Government reimburses up to the lesser, 30,000, subject to the availability of funds [@CL-SUP-4164], unless the employee was entitled to compensation under the Act, in which case there is no reimbursement under the clause.

## Never confuse

War-hazard benefit costs are addressed in DFARS 252.228-7000 [@CL-SUP-1411] and detention benefits in DFARS 252.228-7003 [@CL-SUP-1414]. The first makes benefit costs allowable on conditions [@CL-SUP-1411] and states that the cost of insurance for liabilities reimbursable under it is not allowable [@CL-SUP-1413]. The second reimburses detention benefits up to the lesser of two amounts [@CL-SUP-1414]. For the Government's assumption of aircraft risk, see [Ground and flight risk](sup-dfars-ground-and-flight-risk.md), and for indemnity of unusually hazardous risks, see [Indemnification for unusually hazardous risks](sup-dfars-indemnification-unusually-hazardous-risks.md). For how insurance costs are treated generally, see [Insurance and indemnification](../../compliance/cas-allowability/cmp-insurance-and-indemnification.md).
