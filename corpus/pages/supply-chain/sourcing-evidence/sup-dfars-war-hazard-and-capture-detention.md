## Purpose

Shows when war-hazard benefit costs are allowable, what the Government reimburses for detention benefits paid to a captured employee, and when it does not.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-SUP-1411] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 252.228-7000 and 252.228-7003 [@CL-SUP-1411; @CL-SUP-1414] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-1411] |
| Applies to | Employee war-hazard benefits under the Workers' Compensation and War-Hazard Insurance clause, and detention benefits paid to a captured person [@CL-SUP-1411; @CL-SUP-1414] |

Costs of providing employee war-hazard benefits under the Workers' Compensation and War-Hazard Insurance clause are allowable if the contractor meets the listed conditions, one of which is submitting proof of loss files to support payment or denial of each claim [@CL-SUP-1411]. Another is settling open claims by lump sum, subject to contracting officer approval, and obtaining releases within one year of expiration or termination, unless the contracting officer extends the period [@CL-SUP-1412]. The cost of insurance for liabilities reimbursable under this clause is not allowable [@CL-SUP-1413].

## How it works

If the contractor, under an agreement entered into before capture, is obligated to pay and has paid detention benefits to a captured person or the person's dependents, the Government will reimburse the contractor up to the lesser of two listed amounts [@CL-SUP-1414]. The listed amounts are the total wage or salary paid at the time of capture for the period of detention, and the amount that would have been payable had the detention been covered by the War Hazards Compensation Act [@CL-SUP-1415]. The contractor is not reimbursed under the capture and detention clause if the employees were entitled to compensation for capture and detention under that Act [@CL-SUP-1416].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

Before capture, a contractor agrees to pay a captured employee's salary during detention and pays 40,000. The salary due for the period was 40,000, and the Act would have paid 30,000. The Government reimburses up to the lesser, 30,000, unless the employee was entitled to compensation under the Act, in which case there is no reimbursement under the clause.

## Never confuse

War-hazard benefit costs and detention benefits sit in two clauses with different tests. The first makes benefit costs allowable on conditions and bars the cost of insurance [@CL-SUP-1411; @CL-SUP-1413]. The second reimburses detention benefits up to a lesser-of cap [@CL-SUP-1414]. For the Government's assumption of aircraft risk, see [Ground and flight risk](sup-dfars-ground-and-flight-risk.md), and for indemnity of unusually hazardous risks, see [Indemnification for unusually hazardous risks](sup-dfars-indemnification-unusually-hazardous-risks.md). For how insurance costs are treated generally, see [Insurance and indemnification](../../compliance/cas-allowability/cmp-insurance-and-indemnification.md).
