## Purpose

Gives the meaning of the terms that the quality assurance part of the FAR defines. They decide whether supplies or services conform, who accepts them and how serious a nonconformance is. The duties that use these terms are in [contract quality requirements](sup-contract-quality-requirements.md) and [Government contract quality assurance](sup-government-contract-quality-assurance.md).

The section defines the terms as they are used in part 46 of the FAR [@CL-SUP-3980]. It defines each term below. The section does not say who grades a nonconformance or who decides whether nonconforming supplies are accepted; other sections do, and the cards linked below cover them. Two rows below, inspection and latent defect, come from the definitions in FAR 2.101 instead [@CL-SUP-9554; @CL-SUP-9555]. The RFO model text of FAR 2.101 defines a latent defect in the same words as the codified definition [@CL-SUP-9596; @CL-SUP-9555]. The RFO model text of FAR 2.101 defines inspection in the same words as the codified definition [@CL-SUP-9595; @CL-SUP-9554].

### Definitions at a glance

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Acceptance | The act of an authorized representative of the Government by which the Government, for itself or as agent of another, assumes ownership of existing identified supplies tendered or approves specific services rendered as partial or complete performance of the contract [@CL-SUP-3880] |
| Conditional acceptance | Acceptance of supplies or services that do not conform to contract quality requirements, or are otherwise incomplete, that the contractor is required to correct or otherwise complete by a specified date [@CL-SUP-3881] |
| Contract quality requirements | The technical requirements in the contract relating to the quality of the product or service, and those contract clauses prescribing inspection, and other quality controls incumbent on the contractor, to assure that the product or service conforms to the contractual requirements [@CL-SUP-3882; @CL-SUP-3883] |
| Counterfeit item | An unlawful or unauthorized reproduction, substitution or alteration that has been knowingly mismarked, misidentified or otherwise misrepresented to be an authentic, unmodified item from the original manufacturer, or a source with the express written authority of the original manufacturer or current design activity, including an authorized aftermarket manufacturer [@CL-SUP-3884; @CL-SUP-3885] |
| Unlawful or unauthorized substitution | Includes used items represented as new, or the false identification of grade, serial number, lot number, date code or performance characteristics [@CL-SUP-3886] |
| Critical item | An item, the failure of which is likely to result in hazardous or unsafe conditions for individuals using, maintaining or depending upon the item, or is likely to prevent performance of a vital agency mission [@CL-SUP-3887] |
| Critical nonconformance | A nonconformance that is likely to result in hazardous or unsafe conditions for individuals using, maintaining or depending upon the supplies or services, or is likely to prevent performance of a vital agency mission [@CL-SUP-3888] |
| Design activity | An organization, Government or contractor, that has responsibility for the design and configuration of an item, including the preparation or maintenance of design documents; it could be the original organization, or an organization to which design responsibility has been transferred [@CL-SUP-3889; @CL-SUP-3890] |
| Government contract quality assurance | The various functions, including inspection, performed by the Government to determine whether a contractor has fulfilled the contract obligations pertaining to quality and quantity [@CL-SUP-3891] |
| Inspection (defined in FAR 2.101) | Examining and testing supplies or services (including, when appropriate, raw materials, components, and intermediate assemblies) to determine whether they conform to contract requirements [@CL-SUP-9554] |
| Latent defect (defined in FAR 2.101) | A defect that exists at the time of acceptance but cannot be discovered by a reasonable inspection [@CL-SUP-9555] |
| Major nonconformance | A nonconformance, other than critical, that is likely to result in failure of the supplies or services, or to materially reduce the usability of the supplies or services for their intended purpose [@CL-SUP-3892] |
| Minor nonconformance | A nonconformance that is not likely to materially reduce the usability of the supplies or services for their intended purpose, or is a departure from established standards having little bearing on the effective use or operation of the supplies or services [@CL-SUP-3893] |
| Off-the-shelf item | An item produced and placed in stock by a contractor, or stocked by a distributor, before receiving orders or contracts for its sale; the item may be commercial or produced to military or Federal specifications or description [@CL-SUP-3894] |
| Patent defect | Any defect which exists at the time of acceptance and is not a latent defect [@CL-SUP-3895] |
| Subcontractor | The entry is a cross-reference to 44.101 [@CL-SUP-3981] |
| Suspect counterfeit item | An item for which credible evidence (including but not limited to, visual inspection or testing) provides reasonable doubt that the item is authentic [@CL-SUP-3896] |
| Testing | That element of inspection that determines the properties or elements, including functional operation of supplies or their components, by the application of established scientific principles and procedures [@CL-SUP-3897] |

## How it works

Acceptance is an act of an authorized representative of the Government [@CL-SUP-3880]. Conditional acceptance covers supplies or services that do not conform or are otherwise incomplete, where the contractor is required to correct or complete them by a specified date [@CL-SUP-3881]. Whether the Government may accept nonconforming supplies, and who decides, is not in this section. It is in the section on rejection, covered in [rejection of nonconforming supplies](../../operating-model/corrective-action/opm-rejection-of-nonconforming-supplies.md).

Three terms grade a nonconformance by its likely effect. A critical nonconformance is likely to cause hazardous or unsafe conditions or to prevent performance of a vital agency mission [@CL-SUP-3888]. A major nonconformance is one other than critical that is likely to cause failure or to materially reduce usability for the intended purpose [@CL-SUP-3892]. A minor nonconformance is one that is not likely to materially reduce usability, or a departure from established standards having little bearing on effective use or operation [@CL-SUP-3893]. The contracting office establishes the significance of a nonconformance when it considers acceptability; that duty is covered in [quality assurance policy and responsibilities](sup-quality-assurance-policy-and-responsibilities.md) [@CL-SUP-3906; @CL-SUP-3911].

Government contract quality assurance means the functions, including inspection, that the Government performs to determine whether the contractor met its quality and quantity obligations [@CL-SUP-3891]. Testing is the element of inspection that determines properties or elements, including functional operation, by established scientific principles and procedures [@CL-SUP-3897].

A counterfeit item must have been knowingly mismarked, misidentified or otherwise misrepresented [@CL-SUP-3884]. The definition covers an item presented as coming from the original manufacturer or from a source with the original manufacturer's or current design activity's express written authority, including an authorized aftermarket manufacturer [@CL-SUP-3884; @CL-SUP-3885]. Unlawful or unauthorized substitution includes used items represented as new and false identification of grade, serial number, lot number, date code or performance characteristics [@CL-SUP-3886]. A suspect counterfeit item needs only credible evidence that gives reasonable doubt that the item is authentic; the evidence can include visual inspection or testing [@CL-SUP-3896].

The remaining terms fix scope. A design activity can be Government or contractor, and can be the original organization or one to which design responsibility has been transferred [@CL-SUP-3889; @CL-SUP-3890]. An off-the-shelf item can be commercial or produced to military or Federal specifications or description [@CL-SUP-3894]. A patent defect is a defect that exists at the time of acceptance and is not a latent defect [@CL-SUP-3895]. The entry for subcontractor points to 44.101 [@CL-SUP-3981].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A supplier tenders 100 pumps. Inspection finds 3 pumps with a defect likely to cause hazardous conditions for the people using them; each is a critical nonconformance [@CL-SUP-3888]. It finds 5 pumps with a nameplate that departs from an established standard but has little bearing on effective use; each is a minor nonconformance [@CL-SUP-3893]. The other 92 pumps conform. These definitions only grade the findings; they do not accept or reject anything. For the 3 critical units, the contracting officer ordinarily must give the contractor a chance to correct or replace them if that fits the delivery schedule [@CL-OPM-0063]. Where that paragraph does not apply, the contracting officer ordinarily must reject critical or major nonconformances [@CL-OPM-0065], though the contracting officer may determine, in circumstances such as economy or urgency, that acceptance or conditional acceptance is in the Government's best interest [@CL-OPM-0066]. If the 5 minor units were accepted conditionally, the contractor would be required to correct them by a specified date [@CL-SUP-3881].

## Never confuse

A critical item is not a critical nonconformance. The item term describes an item whose failure is likely to cause the listed harm, while the nonconformance term describes a nonconformance likely to cause it [@CL-SUP-3887; @CL-SUP-3888]. A counterfeit item requires a knowing misrepresentation, while a suspect counterfeit item requires only credible evidence that gives reasonable doubt [@CL-SUP-3884; @CL-SUP-3896]. Acceptance is not conditional acceptance: the second applies to nonconforming or incomplete supplies or services that the contractor is required to correct or complete by a specified date [@CL-SUP-3880; @CL-SUP-3881]. Related: [Government contract quality assurance](sup-government-contract-quality-assurance.md).
