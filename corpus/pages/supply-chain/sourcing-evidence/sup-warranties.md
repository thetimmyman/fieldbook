## Purpose

Shows that a warranty is optional, what the contracting officer considers in deciding whether one is appropriate, what the warranty terms must cover, and what the remedy guidelines say. It also covers how a warranty enters fixed-price incentive pricing and the use of commercial warranties.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-SUP-0872] |
| Codified citation | FAR 46.703, 46.704, 46.706, 46.707 and 46.709 [@CL-SUP-0872; @CL-SUP-0963; @CL-SUP-0873; @CL-SUP-0964; @CL-SUP-0965; @CL-SUP-0966; @CL-SUP-0960; @CL-SUP-0874; @CL-SUP-0875; @CL-SUP-0967; @CL-SUP-0876; @CL-SUP-0877; @CL-SUP-0878; @CL-SUP-0296; @CL-SUP-0879; @CL-SUP-4144; @CL-SUP-4145; @CL-SUP-4146; @CL-SUP-4148]; FAR 2.101 [@CL-SUP-9568] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 2.101 [@CL-SUP-9608] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-0872] |
| Applies to | Acquisitions where the contracting officer decides a warranty is appropriate, and where use of a warranty is approved under agency procedures [@CL-SUP-0872; @CL-SUP-0960] |

The use of warranties is not mandatory, and in determining whether a warranty is appropriate for a specific acquisition the contracting officer shall consider the listed factors [@CL-SUP-0872]. The use of a warranty in an acquisition shall be approved in accordance with agency procedures [@CL-SUP-0960]. The factors include the nature and use of the supplies or services, such as complexity and function, degree of development, state of the art, end use, difficulty in detecting defects before acceptance, and potential harm to the Government if the item is defective [@CL-SUP-0963]. They also include cost, because warranty costs arise from the contractor's charge for accepting the deferred liability created by the warranty and from Government administration and enforcement of the warranty [@CL-SUP-0873]. The Government's ability to enforce a warranty is essential to its effectiveness, and there must be some assurance that an adequate administrative system for reporting defects exists or can be established [@CL-SUP-0964]. Where an item is customarily warranted in the trade and, as a result of that practice, its cost to the Government will be the same whether or not a warranty is included, it would be in the Government's interest to include such a warranty [@CL-SUP-0965]. The contractor's charge for assumption of added liability may be partially or completely offset by reducing the Government's contract quality assurance requirements where the warranty provides adequate assurance of a satisfactory product [@CL-SUP-0966].

Under FAR 2.101, warranty means a promise or affirmation given by a contractor to the Government regarding the nature, usefulness, or condition of the supplies or performance of services furnished under the contract [@CL-SUP-9568]. Under RFO FAR 2.101, warranty means a promise or affirmation given by a contractor to the Government regarding the nature, usefulness, or condition of the supplies or performance of services furnished under the contract [@CL-SUP-9608].

To facilitate the pricing and enforcement of warranties, the contracting officer shall ensure that warranties clearly state the (1) exact nature of the item and its components and characteristics that the contractor warrants, (2) extent of the contractor's warranty including all of the contractor's obligations to the Government for breach of warranty, (3) specific remedies available to the Government, and (4) scope and duration of the warranty [@CL-SUP-0874; @CL-SUP-0875].

## How it works

The contracting officer shall consider the following guidelines when preparing warranty terms and conditions [@CL-SUP-0967]. Generally, the contractor's obligations under warranties extend to all defects discovered during the warranty period, but do not include damage caused by the Government [@CL-SUP-0967; @CL-SUP-0876]. Normally, a warranty shall provide as a minimum that the Government may (A) obtain an equitable adjustment of the contract, or (B) direct the contractor to repair or replace the defective items at the contractor's expense [@CL-SUP-0967; @CL-SUP-0877]. The time period or duration of the warranty must be clearly specified and shall be established after consideration of such factors as (i) the estimated useful life of the item, (ii) the nature of the item including storage or shelf-life, and (iii) trade practice, and the period specified shall not extend the contractor's liability for patent defects beyond a reasonable time after acceptance by the Government [@CL-SUP-0967; @CL-SUP-0878; @CL-SUP-0296]. The warranty shall specify a reasonable time for furnishing notice to the contractor regarding the discovery of defects [@CL-SUP-0967; @CL-SUP-0879]. See [Warranty contract clauses and their triggers](sup-warranty-contract-clauses.md).

Pricing. If a fixed-price incentive contract contains a warranty (see FAR 46.708), the estimated cost of the warranty to the contractor should be considered in establishing the incentive target price and the ceiling price of the contract [@CL-SUP-4144]. All costs incurred, or estimated to be incurred, by the contractor in complying with the warranty shall be considered when establishing the total final price [@CL-SUP-4145]. Contractor compliance with the warranty after the establishment of the total final price shall be at no additional cost to the Government [@CL-SUP-4146].

Commercial warranties. The contracting officer should take advantage of commercial warranties, including extended warranties, where appropriate and in the Government's best interests, offered by the contractor for the repair and replacement of commercial products and commercial services (see part 12) [@CL-SUP-4148].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor prices supplies at 400,000 and adds 10,000 as its charge for accepting a one-year warranty on them. In month 8 the Government finds a defect and notifies the contractor within the notice period. The warranty gives the usual minimum remedy, so the Government directs repair at the contractor's expense. The answer: the 10,000 is the priced charge for the deferred liability, and the repair work falls on the contractor under the warranty terms.

## Never confuse

Warranty costs arise from the contractor's charge for accepting the deferred liability created by the warranty and from Government administration and enforcement of the warranty [@CL-SUP-0873], while under the warranty guidelines, generally, the contractor's obligations under warranties extend to all defects discovered during the warranty period, but do not include damage caused by the Government, and normally a warranty shall provide as a minimum that the Government may obtain an equitable adjustment of the contract or direct the contractor to repair or replace the defective items at the contractor's expense [@CL-SUP-0967; @CL-SUP-0876; @CL-SUP-0877]. See [Contractor liability for Government property loss after acceptance](sup-contractor-liability-after-acceptance.md) and [Service and warranty costs](../../compliance/cas-allowability/cmp-service-and-warranty-costs.md).
