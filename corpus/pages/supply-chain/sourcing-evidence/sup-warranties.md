## Purpose

Shows that a warranty is optional, what the contracting officer considers in deciding whether one is appropriate, what the warranty terms must cover, and what the remedy guidelines say.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-SUP-0872] |
| Codified citation | FAR 46.703, 46.704 and 46.706 [@CL-SUP-0872; @CL-SUP-0960; @CL-SUP-0874] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-0872] |
| Applies to | Acquisitions where the contracting officer decides a warranty is appropriate, and where use of a warranty is approved under agency procedures [@CL-SUP-0872; @CL-SUP-0960] |

The use of warranties is not mandatory, and in determining whether a warranty is appropriate for a specific acquisition the contracting officer shall consider the listed factors [@CL-SUP-0872]. The use of a warranty in an acquisition shall be approved in accordance with agency procedures [@CL-SUP-0960]. The factors include the nature and use of the supplies or services, such as complexity and function, degree of development, state of the art, end use, difficulty in detecting defects before acceptance, and potential harm to the Government if the item is defective [@CL-SUP-0963]. They also include cost, because warranty costs arise from the contractor's charge for accepting the deferred liability created by the warranty and from Government administration and enforcement of the warranty [@CL-SUP-0873]. The Government's ability to enforce a warranty is essential to its effectiveness, and there must be some assurance that an adequate administrative system for reporting defects exists or can be established [@CL-SUP-0964]. Where an item is customarily warranted in the trade and its cost to the Government is the same whether or not a warranty is included, it would be in the Government's interest to include such a warranty [@CL-SUP-0965]. The contractor's charge for assumption of added liability may be partially or completely offset by reducing the Government's contract quality assurance requirements where the warranty provides adequate assurance of a satisfactory product [@CL-SUP-0966].

To facilitate the pricing and enforcement of warranties, the contracting officer shall ensure that warranties clearly state, among listed items, the exact nature of the item and its components and characteristics that the contractor warrants [@CL-SUP-0874]. Three further listed items are the extent of the contractor's warranty including all of the contractor's obligations to the Government for breach of warranty, the specific remedies available to the Government, and the scope and duration of the warranty [@CL-SUP-0874; @CL-SUP-0875].

## How it works

The contracting officer shall consider listed guidelines when preparing warranty terms and conditions [@CL-SUP-0967]. Under those guidelines, generally, the contractor's obligations under warranties extend to all defects discovered during the warranty period, but do not include damage caused by the Government [@CL-SUP-0967; @CL-SUP-0876]. The same guidelines say that normally, a warranty shall provide as a minimum that the Government may obtain an equitable adjustment of the contract or direct the contractor to repair or replace the defective items at the contractor's expense [@CL-SUP-0967; @CL-SUP-0877]. They also say that the duration must be clearly specified and shall be established after consideration of the estimated useful life of the item, the nature of the item including storage or shelf-life, and trade practice [@CL-SUP-0967; @CL-SUP-0878]. They add that the warranty shall specify a reasonable time for furnishing notice to the contractor regarding the discovery of defects [@CL-SUP-0967; @CL-SUP-0879]. On remedies, the contracting officer shall ensure that the warranty clearly states the specific remedies available to the Government [@CL-SUP-0874; @CL-SUP-0875], and under the usual minimum remedy it is the Government that may obtain an equitable adjustment or direct repair or replacement [@CL-SUP-0877]. The clauses that may be inserted are on [Warranty contract clauses and their triggers](sup-warranty-contract-clauses.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor prices supplies at 400,000 and adds 10,000 as its charge for accepting a one-year warranty on them. In month 8 the Government finds a defect and notifies the contractor within the notice period. The warranty gives the usual minimum remedy, so the Government directs repair at the contractor's expense. The answer: the 10,000 is the priced charge for the deferred liability, and the repair work falls on the contractor under the warranty terms.

## Never confuse

A warranty charge is a pricing element, not an accounting reserve rule. The sections cited here set no reserve method, amount or accounting treatment, so what to reserve is a company judgment from the obligations they do set, which are liability for defects discovered during the warranty period other than damage caused by the Government [@CL-SUP-0876] and the repair or replacement remedy at the contractor's expense [@CL-SUP-0877]. Liability for loss of Government property after acceptance is a separate subject, on [Contractor liability for Government property loss after acceptance](sup-contractor-liability-after-acceptance.md). The allowability of warranty costs is on [Service and warranty costs](../../compliance/cas-allowability/cmp-service-and-warranty-costs.md).
