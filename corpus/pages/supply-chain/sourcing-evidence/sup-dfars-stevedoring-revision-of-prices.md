## Purpose

Shows when the contracting officer or the contractor may demand a price revision, what data the contractor submits, and how the revised price is reached.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-SUP-1596] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 252.247-7002 [@CL-SUP-1671] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-1596] |
| Applies to | Contracts where the DFARS prescription calls for the Revision of Prices clause [@CL-SUP-1671] |

Either the contracting officer or the contractor may deliver to the other a written demand that the parties negotiate to revise the prices, at any time subject to the limitations in the clause [@CL-SUP-1596]. No demand may be made before 90 days after the date of the contract, and neither party may then make a demand with an effective date within 90 days of the effective date of any prior demand, but that limit does not apply to a wage adjustment during the 90 day period [@CL-SUP-1597]. A demand by the contractor must briefly state its basis and include the statements and data the clause calls for [@CL-SUP-1598]. If the contracting officer makes the demand, the contractor shall furnish the statements and data within 30 days of the delivery of the demand [@CL-SUP-1599].

In the event of a retroactive wage adjustment, either party may request an equitable adjustment in the prices, and the contractor shall request a price adjustment within 30 days of the retroactive wage adjustment [@CL-SUP-1604]. The contractor shall also notify the contracting officer in writing of any request by or on behalf of its employees which may result in a retroactive wage adjustment, within 20 days after the request or, if the request occurs before contract execution, at the time of execution [@CL-SUP-1676].

## How it works

The contractor's data starts with a new estimate and breakdown of the unit cost and the proposed prices for the services it will perform after the effective date of the price revision, itemized to be consistent with the original negotiations of the contract [@CL-SUP-1600]. It also includes an explanation of the difference from the original or last preceding estimate and such relevant operating data, cost records, overhead absorption reports, and accounting statements as may help test the new estimate [@CL-SUP-1674], and the actual costs of performance to the extent available and any other relevant data usually furnished in price negotiations under a new contract [@CL-SUP-1675]. After the data is filed, the contractor and the contracting officer negotiate promptly in good faith to agree on prices for services on and after the effective date, and the agreement is incorporated into the contract by supplemental agreement [@CL-SUP-1601; @CL-SUP-1672]. If the prices were established by competitive negotiation, they shall not be revised upward unless justified by changes in conditions occurring after the contract was awarded [@CL-SUP-1602]. If the parties fail to agree within 30 days after the date the statements and data are required, the failure to agree is resolved under the Disputes clause, and the prices fixed by the contracting officer remain in effect for the balance of the contract while the contractor continues performance [@CL-SUP-1603; @CL-SUP-1673].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

Assume a contract whose prices were set by competitive negotiation, and a local wage settlement after award. On day 100 the contractor sends a written demand that states its basis and carries the required statements and data, with a new unit cost estimate of 12 against an original 10. The demand is allowed because more than 90 days have passed since the date of the contract. Because the prices were set by competitive negotiation, the increase can stand only if changes in conditions after award justify it, and the wage settlement is the assumed justification. If the parties agree, the new price starts on the effective date named in the demand and is added by supplemental agreement. If they do not agree within 30 days after the data was due, the disagreement goes to the Disputes clause, and the prices fixed by the contracting officer stay in effect while the contractor keeps performing. A second demand whose effective date is 60 days after the first demand's effective date would fall inside the 90 day spacing limit, so it could not be made. The clause excepts a wage adjustment during the 90 day period without saying which 90 day period, so that exception must be read in the executed contract.

## Never confuse

A price revision demand is a request to negotiate, not an automatic change in price, and the clause sets the revised price by negotiation, not by a formula. An economic price adjustment clause is a separate mechanism; see [Economic price adjustments in proposals and proposal audits](../../estimating/escalation/est-economic-price-adjustments.md). A retroactive price redetermination clause is also separate; see [Fixed-ceiling-price contracts with retroactive price redetermination](../../close-revrec/revenue/cls-fixed-ceiling-price-retroactive-price-redetermination.md). A failure to agree goes to the Disputes clause; see [Disputes clause, claims and certification](../../close-revrec/revenue/cls-disputes-clause.md). Relief for unusual loading or unloading conditions is a different request, paid at the man-hour rates already in the contract schedule [@CL-SUP-1636]; see [Hardship conditions and motor carrier fuel surcharge pass-through (DFARS)](sup-dfars-hardship-conditions-and-fuel-surcharge.md). The revision of prices clause and the liability and insurance clause are prescribed in the same DFARS section [@CL-SUP-1671; @CL-SUP-1677]; the second is covered in [Liability and insurance in stevedoring contracts (DFARS)](sup-dfars-stevedoring-liability-and-insurance.md).
