## Purpose

Shows what the contractor is liable for, the insurance it must carry, and the Government's rights, including recovery, when a suit names the United States.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-SUP-1605] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 252.247-7007 [@CL-SUP-1677] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-1605] |
| Applies to | Contracts where the DFARS prescription calls for the Liability and Insurance clause [@CL-SUP-1677] |

The contractor shall be liable to the Government for loss or damage to property, real and personal, owned by the Government or for which the Government is liable [@CL-SUP-1605]. For the purpose of the clause, all cargo loaded or unloaded under the contract is agreed to be property owned by the Government or property for which the Government is liable [@CL-SUP-1678], and the amount of the loss or damage as determined by the contracting officer will be withheld from payments otherwise due the contractor [@CL-SUP-1679]. The contractor shall also be responsible for, and hold the Government harmless from, loss of or damage to property not included in the first of those liabilities [@CL-SUP-1606], and bodily injury and death of persons resulting in whole or in part from the negligence or fault of the contractor, its officers, agents, or employees in the performance of the work [@CL-SUP-1607].

The contractor's general liability and responsibility under the clause are subject only to the specific limitations the clause lists [@CL-SUP-1608]. Under the limitations, the contractor is not responsible to the Government for, and does not agree to hold the Government harmless from, loss or damage to property or bodily injury to or death of persons if the conditions listed after that lead-in apply [@CL-SUP-9768]. One listed limitation applies where the unseaworthiness of the vessel, or failure or defect of the gear or equipment furnished by the Government, contributed jointly with the fault or negligence of the contractor in causing the damage, injury, or death, and a further due diligence condition is also met [@CL-SUP-1680]. That further condition is either that the contractor, its officers, agents, and employees, by due diligence, could not have discovered the unseaworthiness or the defect, or that through due diligence they could not otherwise have avoided the damage, injury, or death [@CL-SUP-1681]. Another listed limitation applies where the damage, injury, or death resulted solely from an act or omission of the Government or its employees, or resulted solely from proper compliance by officers, agents, or employees of the contractor with specific directions of the contracting officer [@CL-SUP-4009].

## How it works

The contractor shall at its own expense acquire and maintain insurance during the term of the contract [@CL-SUP-1609]: standard workmen's compensation and employer's liability insurance and longshoremen's and harbor workers' compensation insurance, or such of these as may be proper under applicable state or Federal statutes [@CL-SUP-1610], bodily injury liability insurance of not less than $300,000 on account of any one occurrence [@CL-SUP-1611], and property damage liability insurance of not less than $300,000 for any one occurrence, which shall include any and all property whether or not in the care, custody, or control of the contractor [@CL-SUP-1612]. The contractor may, with the prior approval of the contracting officer, be a self-insurer against the risk of the workmen's compensation requirement, and that approval will be given upon receipt of satisfactory evidence that the contractor has qualified as a self-insurer under applicable provision of law [@CL-SUP-4158]. Each policy shall provide, by appropriate endorsement or otherwise, that cancellation or material change shall not be effective until after a 30 day written notice is furnished the contracting officer [@CL-SUP-1682], and the contractor shall furnish the contracting officer with satisfactory evidence of the required insurance before performance of any work under the contract [@CL-SUP-1683]. The contractor shall, at its own cost and expense, defend any suits, demands, claims, or actions in which the United States might be named as a co-defendant and which result from its performance of the work [@CL-SUP-1613], without regard to whether the suit resulted from the contractor's negligence, and the Government has the right to appear, participate in the defense, and take the actions necessary to protect the interest of the United States [@CL-SUP-1614]. The contractor shall equitably reimburse the Government if the contractor is indemnified, reimbursed, or relieved of any loss or damage to Government property, and shall do nothing to prevent the Government's right to recover against third parties for any such loss or damage [@CL-SUP-1684]. On the contracting officer's request and at the Government's expense it shall furnish all reasonable assistance and cooperation in obtaining recovery, including the prosecution of suit and the execution of instruments of assignment in favor of the Government [@CL-SUP-1685].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor's crane damages a Government-owned pier through its own fault, and the repair costs 50,000. The contractor is liable to the Government for the 50,000. Separately, cargo is lost during unloading and the contracting officer determines the loss at 8,000; the cargo counts as Government property for this clause, so 8,000 is withheld from a payment of 100,000 otherwise due. A worker is also injured and the claim is 400,000. The contractor must carry bodily injury liability insurance of at least 300,000 for any one occurrence, so a policy of 200,000 would fall short of the clause. Its insurer's policy must also give the contracting officer 30 days' written notice before cancellation takes effect, and the contractor must show the contracting officer evidence of the insurance before starting work.

## Never confuse

This is the Liability and Insurance clause prescribed at DFARS 247.270-4(c) [@CL-SUP-1677]. The vessel repair clause is a different clause with its own deductible and indemnity limit; see [Liability and insurance for vessel repair (DFARS)](sup-dfars-ship-repair-liability-and-insurance.md). The clause on loss or damage to shipped property under the transportation schedules is also separate; see [Contractor liability for loss or damage, and demurrage (DFARS)](sup-dfars-contractor-liability-and-demurrage.md). Revision of Prices is a different clause, prescribed at DFARS 247.270-4(b) [@CL-SUP-1671]; see [Revision of prices in transportation and stevedoring contracts (DFARS)](sup-dfars-stevedoring-revision-of-prices.md). Responsibility for property furnished under the Government property clause is separate; see [Responsibility and liability for Government property](../../close-revrec/fixed-assets/cls-responsibility-liability-government-property.md). Whether insurance premiums are allowable costs is a separate question; see [Insurance and indemnification](../../compliance/cas-allowability/cmp-insurance-and-indemnification.md). Liability after acceptance of supplies is also separate; see [Limitation of liability clauses: basic, high-value items and services](sup-limitation-of-liability-clauses.md).
