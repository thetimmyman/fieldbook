## Purpose

Shows what the contracting officer must do to find and resolve organizational conflicts of interest, who approves the recommended course, and what the contractor gets before award is withheld.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-SUP-0939] |
| Codified citation | FAR 9.504 and FAR 9.506 [@CL-SUP-0939; @CL-SUP-0942] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-0939] |
| Applies to | Contracting officers analyzing planned acquisitions, with the approval steps tied to a significant potential conflict [@CL-SUP-0939; @CL-SUP-0942] |

Contracting officers shall analyze planned acquisitions to identify and evaluate potential organizational conflicts of interest as early in the acquisition process as possible and to avoid, neutralize or mitigate significant potential conflicts before contract award [@CL-SUP-0939]. Before determining to withhold award based on conflict of interest considerations, the contracting officer shall notify the contractor, provide the reasons and allow the contractor a reasonable opportunity to respond [@CL-SUP-0940]. What counts as a conflict is on the [Organizational conflicts of interest: general rules](../../mna/diligence/mna-organizational-conflicts-of-interest-general-rules.md) card and is not restated here.

FAR 2.101 defines an organizational conflict of interest as existing where, because of other activities or relationships with other persons, a person is unable or potentially unable to render impartial assistance or advice to the Government [@CL-SUP-9556], or the person's objectivity in performing the contract work is or might be otherwise impaired, or a person has an unfair competitive advantage [@CL-SUP-9557]. The RFO text of FAR 2.101 defines an organizational conflict of interest in a way that is the same in substance as the codified definition, including the case where a person has an unfair competitive advantage [@CL-SUP-9602; @CL-SUP-9603; @CL-SUP-9556; @CL-SUP-9557].

## How it works

If information concerning prospective contractors is necessary to identify and evaluate potential organizational conflicts of interest or to develop recommended actions, contracting officers should first seek it from within the Government or from other readily available sources [@CL-SUP-0941]. When the contracting officer decides that a particular acquisition involves a significant potential organizational conflict of interest, the contracting officer shall, before issuing the solicitation, submit for approval to the chief of the contracting office, unless a higher level official is designated by the agency, the items listed in the paragraph [@CL-SUP-0942]. Among those items are a written analysis, including a recommended course of action for avoiding, neutralizing or mitigating the conflict, and a draft solicitation provision [@CL-SUP-0998].

The approving official shall review the contracting officer's analysis and recommended course of action, including the draft provision and any proposed clause, among the listed steps [@CL-SUP-0943]. Other listed steps are to consider the benefits and detriments to the Government and prospective contractors and to approve, modify or reject the recommendations in writing [@CL-SUP-0999]. The contractor-facing step shown on this card is the notice, the reasons and the reasonable opportunity to respond before award is withheld [@CL-SUP-0940].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

Before issuing a solicitation for 3,000,000 of engineering support, a contracting officer decides the acquisition involves a significant potential conflict. The contracting officer first checks agency files and then submits a written analysis and draft provision to the chief of the contracting office.

Answer: the approving official may approve, modify or reject the recommendations in writing. If the contracting officer later considers withholding award, the contractor is told why and may respond first.

## Never confuse

Analyzing an acquisition for conflicts is not the same as withholding award. Withholding on conflict grounds requires notice, reasons and a reasonable opportunity to respond [@CL-SUP-0940]. The general rules on what counts as a conflict are on the [Organizational conflicts of interest: general rules](../../mna/diligence/mna-organizational-conflicts-of-interest-general-rules.md) card. The restrictions that follow from specifications, work statements and proprietary information are on the [Organizational conflicts of interest: specifications, proprietary information and examples](sup-oci-specifications-proprietary-information-and-examples.md) card.
