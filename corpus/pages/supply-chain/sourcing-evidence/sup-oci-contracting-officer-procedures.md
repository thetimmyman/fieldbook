## Purpose

Shows what the contracting officer must do to find and resolve organizational conflicts of interest, who approves the recommended course, and what the contractor gets before award is withheld.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-SUP-0939] |
| Codified citation | FAR 9.504 and FAR 9.506 [@CL-SUP-4171; @CL-SUP-0939; @CL-SUP-0942]; FAR 2.101 [@CL-SUP-9556; @CL-SUP-9557] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 2.101 [@CL-SUP-9602; @CL-SUP-9603] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-0939] |
| Applies to | Contracting officers analyzing planned acquisitions, with the approval steps applying when a particular acquisition involves a significant potential organizational conflict of interest [@CL-SUP-4171; @CL-SUP-0939; @CL-SUP-0942] |

Using the general rules, procedures, and examples in FAR subpart 9.5, contracting officers shall analyze planned acquisitions in order to identify and evaluate potential organizational conflicts of interest as early in the acquisition process as possible, and to avoid, neutralize, or mitigate significant potential conflicts before contract award [@CL-SUP-4171; @CL-SUP-0939]. Before determining to withhold award based on conflict of interest considerations, the contracting officer shall notify the contractor, provide the reasons therefor, and allow the contractor a reasonable opportunity to respond [@CL-SUP-0940]. See [Organizational conflicts of interest: general rules](../../mna/diligence/mna-organizational-conflicts-of-interest-general-rules.md).

FAR 2.101 defines an organizational conflict of interest as existing where, because of other activities or relationships with other persons, a person is unable or potentially unable to render impartial assistance or advice to the Government [@CL-SUP-9556], or the person's objectivity in performing the contract work is or might be otherwise impaired, or a person has an unfair competitive advantage [@CL-SUP-9557]. RFO FAR 2.101 defines an organizational conflict of interest as existing where, because of other activities or relationships with other persons, a person is unable or potentially unable to render impartial assistance or advice to the Government [@CL-SUP-9602], or the person's objectivity in performing the contract work is or might be otherwise impaired, or a person has an unfair competitive advantage [@CL-SUP-9603].

## How it works

If information concerning prospective contractors is necessary to identify and evaluate potential organizational conflicts of interest or to develop recommended actions, contracting officers should first seek the information from within the Government or from other readily available sources [@CL-SUP-0941]. If the contracting officer decides that a particular acquisition involves a significant potential organizational conflict of interest, the contracting officer shall, before issuing the solicitation, submit for approval to the chief of the contracting office (unless a higher level official is designated by the agency) a written analysis, including a recommended course of action for avoiding, neutralizing, or mitigating the conflict, based on the general rules in FAR 9.505 or on another basis not expressly stated in that section, a draft solicitation provision (see FAR 9.507-1), and, if appropriate, a proposed contract clause (see FAR 9.507-2) [@CL-SUP-0942; @CL-SUP-0998; @CL-SUP-4172].

The approving official shall review the contracting officer's analysis and recommended course of action, including the draft provision and any proposed clause, consider the benefits and detriments to the Government and prospective contractors, and approve, modify, or reject the recommendations in writing [@CL-SUP-0943; @CL-SUP-0999]. Before determining to withhold award based on conflict of interest considerations, the contracting officer shall notify the contractor, provide the reasons therefor, and allow the contractor a reasonable opportunity to respond [@CL-SUP-0940].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

Before issuing a solicitation for 3,000,000 of engineering support, a contracting officer decides the acquisition involves a significant potential conflict. The contracting officer first checks agency files and then submits a written analysis and draft provision to the chief of the contracting office.

Answer: the approving official may approve, modify or reject the recommendations in writing. If the contracting officer later considers withholding award, the contractor is told why and may respond first.
