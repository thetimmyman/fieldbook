## Purpose

Shows what the offeror represents in the covered defense telecommunications provision and in the employee-owned business provision, and what the domestic source restriction limits.

In the covered defense telecommunications provision, the offeror states whether it does or does not provide covered defense telecommunications equipment or services as part of its offered products or services to the Government in the performance of any contract, subcontract, or other contractual instrument [@CL-SUP-1586]. The provision directs the offeror to review the list of excluded parties in the System for Award Management for entities excluded from receiving federal awards for covered defense telecommunications equipment or services [@CL-SUP-1585]. The provision gives the covered term the meaning provided in the clause on the prohibition on the acquisition of such equipment or services [@CL-SUP-1584].

The domestic source restriction provision, as printed, contains no representation. A solicitation with it is restricted to domestic sources under the authority of section 3204(a)(3) of title 10 of the United States Code, and foreign sources, except Canadian sources, are not eligible for award [@CL-SUP-1587].

In the employee-owned business provision, the offeror represents that it is a qualified business [@CL-SUP-1589]. The provision gives qualified business the meaning given in the clause on the pilot program to incentivize contracting with employee-owned businesses [@CL-SUP-1588]. That clause defines a qualified business as an S corporation, as defined in section 1361(a)(1) of title 26 of the United States Code, for which 100 percent of the outstanding stock is held through an employee stock ownership plan, as defined in section 4975(e)(7) of that title [@CL-SUP-1665].

## How it works

The telecommunications representation is a choice between does and does not provide, made after the offeror reviews the excluded parties list [@CL-SUP-1585; @CL-SUP-1586]. The restriction works at the solicitation level and bars foreign sources other than Canadian sources from award [@CL-SUP-1587]. The employee-owned representation is a single statement that the offeror is a qualified business, with its meaning set in another clause [@CL-SUP-1588; @CL-SUP-1665; @CL-SUP-1589].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An offeror reviews the excluded parties list, finds nothing, and marks that it does not provide covered defense telecommunications equipment or services in performing any contract. On another solicitation, marked as restricted to domestic sources, a foreign firm based in a non-Canadian country is not eligible for award, while a Canadian firm is not barred by that restriction.

## Never confuse

The two representations are different statements: one covers whether covered telecommunications equipment or services are provided, the other covers whether the offeror is a qualified business [@CL-SUP-1586; @CL-SUP-1589]. The restriction is a limit on who may receive award, not a statement the offeror makes [@CL-SUP-1587]. For the prohibition clause and its reporting duties, see [Prohibition on covered defense telecommunications equipment or services](../../compliance/trade-cyber/cmp-covered-defense-telecommunications-equipment-prohibition.md). For how annual representations in the System for Award Management apply, see [Annual representations and certifications provision](../../compliance/regulatory-framework/cmp-annual-representations-provision-paragraphs.md).
