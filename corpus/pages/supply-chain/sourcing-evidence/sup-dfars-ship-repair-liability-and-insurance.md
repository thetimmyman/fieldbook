## Purpose

Shows who bears loss of or damage to the vessel, what the Government does not assume, the contractor's deductible and indemnity limit, and the insurance the contractor must carry.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-SUP-1425] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 252.217-7012 [@CL-SUP-1425; @CL-SUP-1427; @CL-SUP-1429] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-1425] |
| Applies to | Loss or damage to the vessel and to materials or equipment the Government owns or furnished for installation [@CL-SUP-1425] |

The Government assumes the risks of loss of and damage to the vessel and to the materials or equipment to which the Government has title or which it furnished for installation by the contractor [@CL-SUP-1425]. That assumption has limits. The Government does not assume risk of, and will not pay for, repair of defects in the vessel or its material and equipment due to defective workmanship performed by the contractor or its subcontractors [@CL-SUP-1426]. The contractor still bears the first $50,000 of loss or damage from each occurrence or incident that the Government would otherwise have assumed [@CL-SUP-1427].

## How it works

The contractor indemnifies the Government and the vessel and its owners against all claims, demands or causes of action to which they might be subject as a result of the damage or injury described in the indemnification paragraph [@CL-SUP-1452]. That damage or injury is to the property or person, including death, of anyone other than the Government, its employees, the vessel or its owner, arising in whole or in part from the negligence or other wrongful act of the contractor or its agents or employees, or of any subcontractor [@CL-SUP-1428]. The contractor's obligation shall not exceed $300,000 as a consequence of any single occurrence with respect to any one vessel [@CL-SUP-1429].

The contractor shall, at its own expense, obtain and maintain insurance. One kind is casualty, accident and liability insurance, as approved by the contracting officer, insuring the performance of that indemnity [@CL-SUP-1430]. Another is workers compensation insurance, or its equivalent, covering the employees engaged on the work [@CL-SUP-1430; @CL-SUP-1453]. The contractor shall ensure that all subcontractors engaged on the work obtain and maintain the insurance required in paragraph (d)(1) of the clause [@CL-SUP-1454]. After a loss or damage for which the Government has assumed the risk, the contractor shall give the contracting officer written notice as soon as practicable, and the notice shall contain full details of the loss or damage [@CL-SUP-1455]. This card quotes only some listed exclusions and duties; the clause holds more.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A fire damages a Government-furnished engine during repair and the loss is 80,000, with no workmanship defect and no insurance that applies. The Government has assumed the risk, but the contractor bears the first 50,000, and the Government bears the remaining 30,000. If the cause were defective workmanship by the contractor, the Government would not assume the risk.

## Never confuse

The contractor's deductible and its indemnity limit are different figures for different exposures. The deductible applies to loss the Government has assumed on the vessel and its materials [@CL-SUP-1427]. The indemnity limit applies to the contractor's indemnity for third-party claims, per occurrence and per vessel [@CL-SUP-1429]. For aircraft in a contractor's hands, see [Ground and flight risk](sup-dfars-ground-and-flight-risk.md), and for Government indemnity of unusually hazardous risks, see [Indemnification for unusually hazardous risks](sup-dfars-indemnification-unusually-hazardous-risks.md). For insurance costs in general, see [Insurance and indemnification](../../compliance/cas-allowability/cmp-insurance-and-indemnification.md).
