## Purpose

Shows who bears loss of or damage to the vessel, what the Government does not assume, the contractor's deductible and indemnity limit, and the insurance the contractor must carry.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-SUP-1425] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 252.217-7012 [@CL-SUP-1425; @CL-SUP-1427; @CL-SUP-1429] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-1425] |
| Applies to | Loss or damage to the vessel and to materials or equipment the Government owns or furnished for installation [@CL-SUP-1425] |

The contractor shall exercise its best efforts to prevent accidents, injury or damage to all employees, persons and property in and about the work, and to the vessel or part of the vessel upon which work is done [@CL-CTF-2393]. The Government assumes the risks of loss of and damage to the vessel and to the materials or equipment to which the Government has title or which it furnished for installation by the contractor [@CL-SUP-1425]. Unless the Contracting Officer otherwise directs or approves in writing, the contractor shall not carry insurance against any form of loss or damage to the vessel [@CL-CTF-2394], or to that Government-owned or Government-furnished property [@CL-SUP-1425]. That assumption has limits. The Government does not assume any risk for loss or damage compensated for by insurance or otherwise, or resulting from risks for which the contractor failed to maintain insurance, if available, as required or approved by the Contracting Officer [@CL-CTF-2395]. The Government does not assume risk of, and will not pay for any costs of, inspection, repair, replacement or renewal of any defects in the vessel or its material and equipment due to defective workmanship performed by the contractor or its subcontractors [@CL-SUP-1426]. It also does not assume risk of, or pay for those costs for, defects due to defective materials or equipment that the contractor or its subcontracts furnished, or to workmanship, materials or equipment that do not conform to the contract, whether or not the defect is latent or the nonconformance results from negligence [@CL-SUP-1426; @CL-CTF-2396]. The same list excludes the costs of loss, damage, liability or expense caused by, resulting from, or incurred as a consequence of any delay or disruption, willful misconduct or lack of good faith by the contractor or any of its representatives that have supervision or direction of all or substantially all of the contractor's business, or of all or substantially all of its operation at any one plant [@CL-SUP-1426; @CL-CTF-2397; @CL-CTF-2414]. The contractor still bears the first $50,000 of loss or damage from each occurrence or incident that the Government would otherwise have assumed [@CL-SUP-1427].

## How it works

The contractor indemnifies the Government and the vessel and its owners against all claims, demands or causes of action to which they might be subject as a result of the damage or injury described in the indemnification paragraph [@CL-SUP-1452]. That damage or injury is to the property or person, including death, of anyone other than the Government, its employees, the vessel or its owner, arising in whole or in part from the negligence or other wrongful act of the contractor or its agents or employees, or of any subcontractor, or its agents or employees [@CL-SUP-1428; @CL-SUP-4027]. The contractor's obligation shall not exceed $300,000 as a consequence of any single occurrence with respect to any one vessel [@CL-SUP-1429]. The indemnity includes suits, actions, claims, costs or demands of any kind resulting from death, personal injury or property damage occurring during the period of performance of work on the vessel or within 90 days after redelivery of the vessel [@CL-CTF-2400]. For a claim made after 90 days, the rights of the parties are determined by other provisions of the agreement and by law, but the indemnity does apply to death occurring after 90 days where the injury was received during the period the indemnity covers [@CL-CTF-2401].

The contractor shall, at its own expense, obtain and maintain insurance. One kind is casualty, accident and liability insurance, as approved by the contracting officer, insuring the performance of that indemnity [@CL-SUP-1430]. Another is workers compensation insurance, or its equivalent, covering the employees engaged on the work [@CL-SUP-1430; @CL-SUP-1453]. The contractor shall ensure that all subcontractors engaged on the work obtain and maintain the insurance required in paragraph (d)(1) of the clause [@CL-SUP-1454]. Upon request of the Contracting Officer, the contractor shall provide evidence of the insurance required [@CL-CTF-2402]. It shall make no allowance in the job order price for any premium expense or reserve for self-insurance against a risk that the Government assumes [@CL-CTF-2403]. After a loss or damage for which the Government has assumed the risk, the contractor shall give the contracting officer written notice as soon as practicable, and the notice shall contain full details of the loss or damage [@CL-SUP-1455]. If a claim or suit is later filed against the contractor as a result of the event, the contractor shall immediately deliver to the Government every demand, notice, summons or other process it or its employees or representatives received [@CL-CTF-2404]. The contractor shall cooperate with the Government and, upon request, assist in effecting settlements, securing and giving evidence, obtaining the attendance of witnesses and conducting suits [@CL-CTF-2405], and the Government reimburses its expenses in doing so, other than the cost of maintaining its usual organization [@CL-CTF-2413]. Except at its own expense, the contractor shall not voluntarily make any payment, assume any obligation or incur any expense other than what is imperative for the protection of the vessel at the time of the event [@CL-CTF-2406].

For a risk the Government assumes, the Government is subrogated to the contractor's claims against third parties [@CL-CTF-2398], and the contractor shall execute a formal assignment or transfer of the claim if the Contracting Officer requires it [@CL-CTF-2415]. No party other than the contractor may proceed directly against the Government or join it as a co-defendant [@CL-CTF-2399]. In the event of loss of or damage to a vessel, material or equipment that may result in a claim against the Government under the insurance provisions, the contractor shall promptly notify the Contracting Officer [@CL-CTF-2407]. The Contracting Officer may then, without prejudice to other Government rights, order the contractor to proceed with replacement or repair, and the contractor shall effect it [@CL-CTF-2408]. The contractor then submits a request for reimbursement with the supporting documentation the Contracting Officer may reasonably require [@CL-CTF-2409], identified as submitted under the Insurance clause [@CL-CTF-2416]. If the Government determines that the risk is within those it assumed, it reimburses the reasonable, allowable cost of the replacement or repair [@CL-CTF-2410], plus a reasonable profit if the contractor performed it, less the deductible [@CL-CTF-2417]. If the Contracting Officer decides that the loss or damage shall not be replaced or repaired, the Contracting Officer shall modify the contract appropriately, or terminate the repair of any part or all of the vessel under the Termination for Convenience of the Government clause [@CL-CTF-2411; @CL-CTF-2412].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A fire damages a Government-furnished engine during repair and the loss is 80,000, with no workmanship defect and no insurance that applies. The contractor notifies the Contracting Officer promptly, and the Contracting Officer orders the repair; the contractor does not buy insurance on the engine, because the Government has assumed that risk. The Government has assumed the risk, but the contractor bears the first 50,000, and the Government bears the remaining 30,000. If the cause were defective workmanship by the contractor, the Government would not assume the risk.

## Never confuse

The contractor's deductible and its indemnity limit are different figures for different exposures. The deductible applies to loss the Government has assumed on the vessel and its materials [@CL-SUP-1427]. The indemnity limit applies to the contractor's indemnity for third-party claims, per occurrence and per vessel [@CL-SUP-1429]. For aircraft in a contractor's hands, see [Ground and flight risk](sup-dfars-ground-and-flight-risk.md), and for Government indemnity of unusually hazardous risks, see [Indemnification for unusually hazardous risks](sup-dfars-indemnification-unusually-hazardous-risks.md). For insurance costs in general, see [Insurance and indemnification](../../compliance/cas-allowability/cmp-insurance-and-indemnification.md).
