## Purpose

Shows who is responsible for acceptance, what acceptance acknowledges, when and where it may occur, how it is ordinarily evidenced, and when title to supplies and risk of loss pass to the Government.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-SUP-3938] |
| Codified citation | FAR 46.501, 46.502, 46.503 and 46.505 [@CL-SUP-3938; @CL-SUP-4138; @CL-SUP-4139; @CL-SUP-4140; @CL-SUP-4141; @CL-SUP-4142; @CL-SUP-4143; @CL-SUP-3942] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-3938; @CL-SUP-3942] |
| Applies to | Acceptance of supplies or services under a Government contract [@CL-SUP-3938]; title and risk of loss of supplies [@CL-SUP-3942; @CL-SUP-3943] |

Acceptance constitutes acknowledgment that the supplies or services conform with applicable contract quality and quantity requirements, except as provided in this subpart and subject to other terms and conditions of the contract [@CL-SUP-3938]. Acceptance may take place before delivery, at the time of delivery, or after delivery, depending on the terms and conditions of the contract [@CL-SUP-3939].

Supplies or services shall ordinarily not be accepted before completion of Government contract quality assurance actions, and the sentence adds a cross-reference to 46.504 [@CL-SUP-3940]. Acceptance shall ordinarily be evidenced by execution of an acceptance certificate on an inspection or receiving report form or commercial shipping document or packing list [@CL-SUP-3941]. That sentence does not name who executes the certificate [@CL-SUP-3941].

Acceptance of supplies or services is the responsibility of the contracting officer [@CL-SUP-4138]. When that responsibility is assigned to a cognizant contract administration office or to another agency (see 42.202(g)), acceptance by that office or agency is binding on the Government [@CL-SUP-4139].

Each contract shall specify the place of acceptance [@CL-SUP-4140]. Contracts that provide for Government contract quality assurance at source shall ordinarily provide for acceptance at source [@CL-SUP-4141], and contracts that provide for it at destination shall ordinarily provide for acceptance at destination [@CL-SUP-4142]. Supplies accepted at a place other than destination shall not be reinspected at destination for acceptance purposes, but should be examined at destination for quantity, damage in transit, and possible substitution or fraud [@CL-SUP-4143].

Title to supplies shall pass to the Government upon formal acceptance, regardless of when or where the Government takes physical possession, unless the contract specifically provides for earlier passage of title [@CL-SUP-3942].

Unless the contract specifically provides otherwise, risk of loss of or damage to supplies remains with the contractor until it passes to the Government. Where transportation is f.o.b. origin, it passes on delivery of the supplies to a carrier [@CL-SUP-3943]. Where transportation is f.o.b. destination, it passes at acceptance by the Government or at delivery to the Government at the destination specified in the contract, whichever is later [@CL-SUP-3944].

That risk rule does not apply to supplies that so fail to conform to contract requirements as to give a right of rejection. Their risk of loss or damage remains with the contractor until cure or acceptance [@CL-SUP-3945]. After cure or acceptance, the risk rule applies [@CL-SUP-3946].

Under the risk rule, the contractor is not liable for loss of or damage to supplies caused by the negligence of officers, agents, or employees of the Government acting within the scope of their employment [@CL-SUP-3947]. The policy in these paragraphs is specified in the clause at 52.246-16, Responsibility for Supplies, which is prescribed in 46.316 [@CL-SUP-3948].

## How it works

Three questions have separate answers.

- When was the work accepted? The contract's terms decide: before, at or after delivery [@CL-SUP-3939]. Acceptance ordinarily follows completion of Government contract quality assurance actions [@CL-SUP-3940].
- When did title pass? On formal acceptance, wherever the supplies physically are, unless the contract specifically provides for earlier passage [@CL-SUP-3942].
- Who bears loss or damage? The contractor, until the delivery terms move the risk to the Government, unless the contract specifically provides otherwise [@CL-SUP-3943; @CL-SUP-3944].

Supplies that give a right of rejection stay at the contractor's risk until cure or acceptance [@CL-SUP-3945]. The section does not say who decides whether supplies give a right of rejection. Government negligence within the scope of employment is not the contractor's liability under the risk rule [@CL-SUP-3947].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

Assume the contract has no special title or risk terms and the supplies conform. Shipment one is 10,000 units f.o.b. origin. The contractor delivers them to a carrier on 3 March, and the Government formally accepts them on 20 March, evidenced by an executed acceptance certificate [@CL-SUP-3941]. Risk passed to the Government on 3 March [@CL-SUP-3943]. Title passed on formal acceptance, on 20 March [@CL-SUP-3942].

Shipment two is 5,000 units f.o.b. destination. They reach the specified destination on 5 April, and the Government accepts them on 12 April. Damage on 8 April, with no Government negligence, falls on the contractor, because risk passes on the later of acceptance and delivery, which is 12 April [@CL-SUP-3944]. If Government negligence within the scope of employment caused the damage, the contractor is not liable [@CL-SUP-3947].

Shipment three fails to conform so badly that the Government has a right of rejection. Even if it ships f.o.b. origin, risk stays with the contractor until cure or acceptance [@CL-SUP-3945]. After cure or acceptance, the ordinary risk rule applies again [@CL-SUP-3946].

## Never confuse

Title is not risk of loss. Unless the contract specifically provides earlier passage of title, title follows formal acceptance [@CL-SUP-3942]. Unless the contract specifically provides otherwise, risk follows the delivery terms [@CL-SUP-3943; @CL-SUP-3944].

Acceptance is not delivery. It may come before, at or after delivery [@CL-SUP-3939]. Acceptance acknowledges conformity, but only as the subpart and the contract's other terms allow [@CL-SUP-3938].

The risk rule does not cover supplies that give a right of rejection [@CL-SUP-3945].
