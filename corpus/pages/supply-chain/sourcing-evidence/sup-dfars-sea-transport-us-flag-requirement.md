## Purpose

Shows which supplies the sea transport clause covers, what notice the contractor gives when sea shipment is anticipated, when the contractor and its subcontractors must use U.S.-flag vessels, and how a waiver request works.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-SUP-1389] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 252.247-7023(a), (b), (c), (d), (e) and (i) [@CL-SUP-1388; @CL-SUP-1437; @CL-SUP-1389; @CL-SUP-1393; @CL-SUP-1395; @CL-SUP-1440] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | OCT 2024 [@CL-SUP-9862] |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-1389] |
| Applies to | Supplies as the clause defines them, transported by sea under the contract [@CL-SUP-1388; @CL-SUP-1389] |

In the clause, supplies are supplies clearly identifiable for eventual use by or owned by DoD at the time of transportation by sea, or otherwise transported by DoD, regardless of ownership or use by DoD [@CL-SUP-1388]. The contractor shall use U.S.-flag vessels when transporting any supplies by sea under the contract [@CL-SUP-1389].

In the basic clause, a subcontractor must also use U.S.-flag vessels if the contract is a construction contract, if the supplies are other than commercial products, or if they are commercial products of the kinds listed [@CL-SUP-1390]. One listed kind is a commercial product the contractor is reselling or distributing to the Government without adding value [@CL-SUP-1391]. Two more of the listed alternative kinds of commercial product are products shipped in direct support of U.S. military contingency operations, exercises, or forces deployed in humanitarian or peacekeeping operations, or commissary or exchange cargoes transported outside the Defense Transportation System in accordance with section 2643 of title 10 of the United States Code [@CL-SUP-1390; @CL-SUP-1392].

In Alternates I and II, a subcontractor transporting supplies by sea shall use U.S.-flag vessels if the supplies are other than commercial products or are commercial products of the listed kinds [@CL-SUP-1444]. The construction contract condition appears in the basic clause [@CL-SUP-1390].

## How it works

If transportation of supplies by sea is anticipated under the contract, the contractor shall notify the contracting officer and MARAD [@CL-SUP-1437]. The notice is due within 3 business days after contract award, or immediately before the shipment departure date needed to meet delivery schedules, whichever is earlier [@CL-SUP-1438]. It must state the contractor's intent to transport supplies by sea and give the contract number and, when applicable, the task-order or delivery-order number [@CL-SUP-1439].

A contractor that did not anticipate sea transportation at award, and so gave no such notice, may learn before shipment that supplies will go by sea [@CL-SUP-1440]. It then gives the notification as soon as that is known and complies with all the terms and conditions of the clause [@CL-SUP-1441].

The contractor and its subcontractors may ask the contracting officer for a waiver of the U.S.-flag requirement, or for identification of available U.S.-flag vessels, if they sufficiently explain the reasons the clause lists [@CL-SUP-1393]. The listed reasons are alternatives: U.S.-flag vessels are not available at a fair and reasonable rate for commercial vessels of the United States, or U.S.-flag vessels are otherwise not available [@CL-SUP-1393; @CL-SUP-1394]. The contractor must submit any request for use of foreign-flag vessels in writing to the contracting officer at least 45 days before the sailing date necessary to meet its delivery schedules [@CL-SUP-1395]. The request shall contain at a minimum the type, weight and cube of cargo, the required shipping and delivery dates, special handling and discharge requirements, loading and discharge points, the names of shipper and consignee, the prime contract number, and a documented description of current, diligent efforts made to secure U.S.-flag vessels, including points of contact (with names and telephone numbers) with at least two U.S.-flag carriers contacted [@CL-SUP-1443; @CL-SUP-4008].

The bill of lading, the final-invoice representation, the price adjustment and the flowdown are on the companion card, [Transportation of supplies by sea: representation, reporting and flowdown](sup-dfars-sea-transport-representation-and-flowdown.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor ships spare parts from a depot to a military base overseas and the contract papers name a DoD contract number. The parts are supplies as the clause defines them. The contractor books a U.S.-flag carrier. If no U.S.-flag carrier is available at a fair rate, the contractor asks for a waiver, sending the request at least 45 days before the sailing date it needs.

## Never confuse

The contractor and its subcontractors may request a waiver of the U.S.-flag requirement via the contracting officer [@CL-SUP-1393]. The prime and subcontractor rules are worded differently: the prime shall use U.S.-flag vessels for any supplies by sea [@CL-SUP-1389], unlike a subcontractor, which shall do so if one of the listed conditions is met [@CL-SUP-1390]. For the wider rules on commercial subcontracts, see [Subcontracts for commercial products and commercial services](sup-subcontracts-commercial-products-and-services.md).
