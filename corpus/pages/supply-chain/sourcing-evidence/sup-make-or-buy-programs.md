## Purpose

Shows when a make-or-buy program can be required, what it must contain, and how it is agreed and then carried into the contract.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-SUP-0151] |
| Codified citation | FAR 15.407-2 [@CL-SUP-0151] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 15.105-5 [@CL-SUP-3421]; RFO FAR 15.405-2 [@CL-SUP-3429]; RFO FAR 2.101 [@CL-SUP-9601] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-0151] |
| Applies to | Negotiated acquisitions where the contracting officer requires a make-or-buy program [@CL-SUP-0155] |

The prime contractor is responsible for managing contract performance, including planning, placing, and administering subcontracts as necessary to ensure the lowest overall cost and technical risk to the Government [@CL-SUP-0151]. Where make-or-buy programs are required, the Government may reserve the right to review and agree on the program when necessary to ensure negotiation of reasonable contract prices, satisfactory performance, or implementation of socioeconomic policies [@CL-SUP-0152]. Contracting officers may require program plans for negotiated acquisitions requiring certified cost or pricing data whose estimated value is $20 million or more [@CL-SUP-0155], except where the proposed contract is for research or development and, if prototypes or hardware are involved, no significant follow-on production is anticipated [@CL-SUP-0156].

FAR 2.101 defines a make-or-buy program as that part of a contractor's written plan for a contract identifying those major items to be produced or work efforts to be performed in the prime contractor's facilities and those to be subcontracted [@CL-SUP-9553]. The RFO text of FAR 2.101 defines a make-or-buy program as that part of a contractor's written plan for a contract identifying those major items to be produced or work efforts to be performed in the prime contractor's facilities and those to be subcontracted, the same in substance as the codified definition [@CL-SUP-9601; @CL-SUP-9553].

## How it works

Below $20 million a program may still be required, but only if the contracting officer determines the information is necessary and documents the reasons in the contract file [@CL-SUP-0157]. The solicitation then must state that the program and required supporting information accompany the offer [@CL-SUP-0158]. Program content is confined to major items or work efforts that would normally need company management review of the make-or-buy decision because they are complex, costly, needed in large quantities, or require additional equipment or real property to produce [@CL-SUP-0159], and raw materials, commercial products, commercial services, and off-the-shelf items are excluded unless their impact on contract cost or schedule is critical [@CL-SUP-0160]. Programs normally should not include items or work efforts estimated to cost less than 1 percent of the total estimated contract price or any agency minimum dollar amount [@CL-SUP-0161]. The offeror's program should include or be supported by a list of information [@CL-SUP-0240], including identification of proposed subcontractors, if known, and their location and size status [@CL-SUP-0162]. Contracting officers evaluate and negotiate the program as soon as practicable after receipt and before award [@CL-SUP-0163]. The contracting officer may incorporate the make-or-buy program in negotiated contracts for major systems or their subsystems or components, regardless of contract type [@CL-SUP-0165]. For other supplies and services, the program may be incorporated if the contract is cost-reimbursable, or cost-sharing with the contractor's share of the cost under 25 percent [@CL-SUP-0166], and the contracting officer determines that technical or cost risks justify Government review and approval of changes or additions to the program [@CL-SUP-0167].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 15 text in place of the codified text under Class Deviation 2026-O0048, whose Revision 1 took effect immediately and supersedes the March 5, 2026 version [@CL-EST-3176] [@CL-EST-3177]. RFO FAR 15.105-5(a) says the Government may choose to review and agree on the offeror's make-or-buy program when necessary to ensure negotiation of reasonable contract prices, satisfactory performance, or implementation of socioeconomic policies, where the codified section says that when make-or-buy programs are required the Government may reserve the right to do so [@CL-SUP-3421; @CL-SUP-0152]. RFO paragraph (a) is one sentence, and paragraph (b), headed Acquisitions requiring make-or-buy programs, follows it [@CL-SUP-3421]; the codified paragraph (a) continues with sentences on the prime contractor's responsibility for managing contract performance and on consent to subcontracts and review of purchasing systems being separate actions covered in part 44, and the codified paragraph (b) defines a make item [@CL-SUP-0151; @CL-SUP-0153; @CL-SUP-0154]. RFO FAR 15.105-5(b)(1) says contracting officers may require offerors to submit make-or-buy program plans for acquisitions requiring certified cost or pricing data whose estimated value is $20 million or more [@CL-SUP-3422; @CL-SUP-0155], with an exception when the proposed contract is for research or development and, if prototypes or hardware are involved, no significant follow-on production is anticipated [@CL-SUP-3423; @CL-SUP-0156]. RFO FAR 15.105-5(b)(2) says contracting officers may only require offerors to submit make-or-buy programs for acquisitions whose estimated value is under $20 million if the contracting officer determines that the information is necessary and documents the reasons in the contract file [@CL-SUP-3424; @CL-SUP-0157]. RFO FAR 15.105-5(c) says that when offerors must submit proposed make-or-buy programs, the RFP must include a statement that the program and required supporting information must accompany the offer and a description of factors to be used in evaluating the proposed program [@CL-SUP-3433; @CL-SUP-0158]. Under RFO FAR 15.105-5(d)(1), the information required from an offeror about a make-or-buy program must be confined to those major items or work efforts that normally would require company management review of the make-or-buy decision because they are complex, costly, needed in large quantities, or require additional equipment or real property to produce [@CL-SUP-3425; @CL-SUP-0159]; it must not include raw materials, products, commercial services, and off-the-shelf items, unless their potential impact on contract cost or schedule is critical, where the codified text says "commercial products" [@CL-SUP-3425; @CL-SUP-3426; @CL-SUP-0160]; and it must not include items or work efforts estimated to cost less than 1 percent of the total estimated contract price or any minimum dollar amount set by the agency, where the codified text says that make-or-buy programs normally should not include such items [@CL-SUP-3425; @CL-SUP-3427; @CL-SUP-0161]. RFO FAR 15.105-5(d)(2) says the following information must be provided by an offeror in its proposal, where the codified section says the offeror's program should include or be supported by the listed information [@CL-SUP-3428; @CL-SUP-0240], and its item (vi) is identification of proposed subcontractors, if known, and their location and size status [@CL-SUP-3438; @CL-SUP-0162]. RFO FAR 15.405-2(a) says contracting officers must evaluate and negotiate proposed make-or-buy programs as soon as practicable after their receipt and before contract award [@CL-SUP-3437; @CL-SUP-0163]. RFO FAR 15.405-2(a)(1) says that when the program is to be incorporated in the contract and the design status of the product being acquired does not permit accurate precontract identification of major items or work efforts, contracting officers must notify the prospective contractor in writing [@CL-SUP-3429; @CL-SUP-0168] that these items or efforts, when identifiable, must be added under the clause at 52.215-9, Changes or Additions to Make-or-Buy Program [@CL-SUP-3430; @CL-SUP-0243]. RFO FAR 15.405-2(a)(2) says contracting officers normally must not agree to proposed make items when the products or services are not regularly manufactured or provided by the contractor and are available from another firm at equal or lower prices, quality, quantity, delivery, and other essential factors considered [@CL-SUP-3434; @CL-SUP-0242], and also when they are regularly manufactured or provided by the contractor but are available from another firm at lower prices [@CL-SUP-3435; @CL-SUP-0242]; it says contracting officers may agree to these as make items if an overall lower Governmentwide cost would result or it is otherwise in the best interest of the Government [@CL-SUP-3436; @CL-SUP-0242]. RFO FAR 15.405-2(a)(2) also says that if this situation occurs in any fixed-price incentive or cost-plus-incentive-fee contract, contracting officers must specify these items in the contract and state that they are subject to paragraph (d) of the clause at 52.215-9, Changes or Additions to Make-or-Buy Program [@CL-SUP-3431; @CL-SUP-0241], and that if the contractor proposes to reverse the categorization of such items during contract performance, the contract price must be subject to equitable reduction [@CL-SUP-3432; @CL-SUP-0164].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A negotiated acquisition with an estimated value of 30 million requires certified cost or pricing data, and the contracting officer requires a make-or-buy program. The offeror lists a component worth 0.2 percent of the estimated contract price; that component is left out of the program information under the RFO text, as the next sentence explains. RFO FAR 15.105-5(d)(1)(iii), which the class deviation directs DoD to use [@CL-EST-3176] [@CL-EST-3177], says the information must not include items or work efforts estimated to cost less than 1 percent of the total estimated contract price or any minimum dollar amount set by the agency, and the word "normally" does not appear in that item [@CL-SUP-3425; @CL-SUP-3427]. A second component at 12 percent of the price is complex and costly enough to need company management review, so it is a major item and needs a categorization as must make, must buy, or can either make or buy, with a proposal to make or to buy only if it is categorized as can either make or buy. The answer is that the program covers the second component and omits the first.

## Never confuse

The codified section says a make-or-buy program is not subcontract consent and not a review of the purchasing system; those are separate actions covered in part 44 [@CL-SUP-0153]. The codified section defines a make item as an item or work effort produced or performed by the prime contractor or its affiliates, subsidiaries, or divisions, so a bought item and a make item turn on who performs the work [@CL-SUP-0154]. RFO FAR 15.105-5 has paragraph (a) as one sentence and goes directly to paragraph (b), headed Acquisitions requiring make-or-buy programs [@CL-SUP-3421]. Where the program is to be incorporated in the contract and design status does not permit accurate precontract identification of major items or work efforts, the contracting officer shall notify the prospective contractor in writing [@CL-SUP-0168] that these items or efforts, when identifiable, shall be added under the clause at 52.215-9, Changes or Additions to Make-or-Buy Program [@CL-SUP-0243]. A contracting officer who would normally not agree to proposed make items because another firm offers them at lower prices may nonetheless agree if an overall lower Governmentwide cost would result or it is otherwise in the best interest of the Government [@CL-SUP-0242]. In a fixed-price incentive or cost-plus-incentive-fee contract the contracting officer then specifies those items in the contract and states that they are subject to paragraph (d) of the clause at 52.215-9 [@CL-SUP-0241], and if the contractor proposes to reverse their categorization during performance, the contract price is subject to equitable reduction [@CL-SUP-0164].

The codified section says make-or-buy programs normally should not include items or work efforts estimated to cost less than 1 percent of the total estimated contract price or any minimum dollar amount set by the agency [@CL-SUP-0161], while RFO FAR 15.105-5(d)(1)(iii) says the information must not include such items, in an item that contains neither "normally" nor "should" [@CL-SUP-3425; @CL-SUP-3427].
