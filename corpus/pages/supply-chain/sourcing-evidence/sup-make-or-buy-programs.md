## Purpose

Shows when a make-or-buy program can be required, what it must contain, and how it is agreed and then carried into the contract.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-SUP-0151] |
| Codified citation | FAR 15.407-2 [@CL-SUP-0151] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-0151] |
| Applies to | Negotiated acquisitions where the contracting officer requires a make-or-buy program [@CL-SUP-0155] |

The prime contractor is responsible for managing contract performance, including planning, placing, and administering subcontracts to ensure the lowest overall cost and technical risk to the Government [@CL-SUP-0151]. Where make-or-buy programs are required, the Government may reserve the right to review and agree on the program when necessary to ensure reasonable contract prices, satisfactory performance, or implementation of socioeconomic policies [@CL-SUP-0152]. Contracting officers may require program plans for negotiated acquisitions requiring certified cost or pricing data whose estimated value is $20 million or more [@CL-SUP-0155], except where the proposed contract is for research or development and, if prototypes or hardware are involved, no significant follow-on production is anticipated [@CL-SUP-0156].

## How it works

Below $20 million a program may still be required, but only if the contracting officer determines the information is necessary and documents the reasons in the contract file [@CL-SUP-0157]. The solicitation then must state that the program and required supporting information accompany the offer [@CL-SUP-0158]. Program content is confined to major items or work efforts that would normally need company management review of the make-or-buy decision because they are complex, costly, needed in large quantities, or require additional equipment or real property to produce [@CL-SUP-0159], and raw materials, commercial products, commercial services, and off-the-shelf items are excluded unless their impact on contract cost or schedule is critical [@CL-SUP-0160]. Programs normally should not include items or work efforts estimated to cost less than 1 percent of the total estimated contract price or any agency minimum dollar amount [@CL-SUP-0161]. The offeror's program should include or be supported by a list of information [@CL-SUP-0240], including identification of proposed subcontractors, if known, and their location and size status [@CL-SUP-0162]. Contracting officers evaluate and negotiate the program as soon as practicable after receipt and before award [@CL-SUP-0163].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A negotiated acquisition with an estimated value of 30 million requires certified cost or pricing data, and the contracting officer requires a make-or-buy program. The offeror lists a component worth 0.2 percent of the estimated contract price; that component is normally excluded from the program. A second component at 12 percent of the price is complex and costly enough to need company management review, so it is a major item and needs a categorization as must make, must buy, or can either make or buy, with a proposal to make or to buy only if it is categorized as can either make or buy. The answer is that the program covers the second component and normally omits the first.

## Never confuse

A make-or-buy program is not subcontract consent and not a review of the purchasing system; those are separate actions covered in part 44 [@CL-SUP-0153]. A make item is an item or work effort produced or performed by the prime contractor or its affiliates, subsidiaries, or divisions, so a bought item and a make item turn on who performs the work [@CL-SUP-0154]. Where the program is to be incorporated in the contract and design status does not permit accurate precontract identification of major items or work efforts, the contracting officer shall notify the prospective contractor in writing [@CL-SUP-0168] that these items or efforts, when identifiable, shall be added under the clause at 52.215-9, Changes or Additions to Make-or-Buy Program [@CL-SUP-0243]. A contracting officer who would normally not agree to proposed make items because another firm offers them at lower prices may nonetheless agree if an overall lower Governmentwide cost would result or it is otherwise in the best interest of the Government [@CL-SUP-0242]. In a fixed-price incentive or cost-plus-incentive-fee contract the contracting officer then specifies those items in the contract and states that they are subject to paragraph (d) of the clause at 52.215-9 [@CL-SUP-0241], and if the contractor proposes to reverse their categorization during performance, the contract price is subject to equitable reduction [@CL-SUP-0164].
