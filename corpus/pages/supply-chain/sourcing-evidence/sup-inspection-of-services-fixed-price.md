## Purpose

Shows how the services inspection clause for fixed-price contracts requires an inspection system, lets the Government require reperformance at no increase in price, and sets the remedies when defects cannot be corrected.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-SUP-1083] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.246-4 [@CL-SUP-1083] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.246-4 [@CL-SUP-3237] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-1083] |
| Applies to | Contracts that include the Inspection of Services—Fixed-Price clause [@CL-SUP-1083]; the clause is directed for solicitations and contracts for services, or supplies that involve the furnishing of services, when a fixed-price contract is contemplated and the contract amount is expected to exceed the simplified acquisition threshold [@CL-SUP-1132], and the contracting officer may insert it when the contract amount is expected to be at or below that threshold and inclusion is in the Government's interest [@CL-SUP-9777] |

The contractor shall provide and maintain an inspection system acceptable to the Government covering the services under the contract [@CL-SUP-1083], and complete records of all inspection work performed by the contractor shall be maintained and made available to the Government during contract performance and for as long afterwards as the contract requires [@CL-SUP-1084]. The Government has the right to inspect and test all services called for by the contract, to the extent practicable at all times and places during the term of the contract [@CL-SUP-1085].

If any of the services do not conform with contract requirements, the Government may require the contractor to perform the services again in conformity with contract requirements, at no increase in contract amount [@CL-SUP-1086]. When the defects in services cannot be corrected by reperformance, the Government may require the contractor to take necessary action to ensure that future performance conforms to contract requirements and reduce the contract price to reflect the reduced value of the services performed [@CL-SUP-1087].

## How it works

The contractor shall provide and maintain an inspection system acceptable to the Government covering the services under the contract and maintain complete records of all inspection work performed by the contractor and make them available to the Government during contract performance and for as long afterwards as the contract requires, and the Government has the right to inspect and test all services called for by the contract, to the extent practicable at all times and places during the term of the contract [@CL-SUP-1083; @CL-SUP-1084; @CL-SUP-1085]. If any of the services do not conform with contract requirements, the Government may require the contractor to perform the services again in conformity with contract requirements, at no increase in contract amount [@CL-SUP-1086]. When the defects in services cannot be corrected by reperformance, the Government may require the contractor to take necessary action to ensure that future performance conforms to contract requirements and reduce the contract price to reflect the reduced value of the services performed [@CL-SUP-1087]. If the contractor fails to promptly perform the services again or to take the necessary action to ensure future performance in conformity with contract requirements, the Government may, by contract or otherwise, perform the services and charge to the contractor any cost incurred by the Government that is directly related to the performance of such service, or terminate the contract for default [@CL-SUP-1088; @CL-SUP-1089].

Class Deviation 2026-O0038 directs DoD contracting officers to use the revised FAR part 52 on the Revolutionary FAR Overhaul web page in place of the text codified at 48 CFR chapter 1, effective February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760; @CL-CMP-9762]. Both the codified and revised FAR 52.246-4 require the contractor to provide and maintain an inspection system acceptable to the Government covering the services under the contract [@CL-SUP-1083; @CL-SUP-3237], require complete records of all inspection work performed by the contractor to be maintained and made available to the Government during contract performance and for as long afterwards as the contract requires [@CL-SUP-1084; @CL-SUP-3238], and give the Government the right to inspect and test all services called for by the contract, to the extent practicable at all times and places during the term of the contract [@CL-SUP-1085; @CL-SUP-3239]. Both provide that if any of the services do not conform with contract requirements, the Government may require the contractor to perform the services again in conformity with contract requirements, at no increase in contract amount [@CL-SUP-1086; @CL-SUP-3240], and that when the defects in services cannot be corrected by reperformance, the Government may require the contractor to take necessary action to ensure that future performance conforms to contract requirements and reduce the contract price to reflect the reduced value of the services performed [@CL-SUP-1087; @CL-SUP-3241]. Both provide that if the contractor fails to promptly perform the services again or to take the necessary action to ensure future performance in conformity with contract requirements, the Government may, by contract or otherwise, perform the services and charge to the contractor any cost incurred by the Government that is directly related to the performance of such service, or terminate the contract for default [@CL-SUP-1088; @CL-SUP-1089; @CL-SUP-3242; @CL-SUP-3243].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A fixed-price contract for cleaning services is worth 100,000. Inspection finds that one month of service fell below the contract standard. The Government requires the contractor to perform that month again at no increase in price, so the price stays 100,000. A missed safety check cannot be redone, so reperformance cannot correct it. The Government may then reduce the price to reflect the reduced value, for example by 2,000, and require action to keep future service conforming.

## Never confuse

If any of the services do not conform with contract requirements, the Government may require the contractor to perform the services again in conformity with contract requirements, at no increase in contract amount, and when the defects in services cannot be corrected by reperformance, the Government may require the contractor to take necessary action to ensure that future performance conforms to contract requirements and reduce the contract price to reflect the reduced value of the services performed [@CL-SUP-1086; @CL-SUP-1087]. Under the cost-reimbursement version of the clause on [Inspection of services, cost-reimbursement](sup-inspection-of-services-cost-reimbursement.md), when the defects in services cannot be corrected by reperformance, the Government may require the contractor to take necessary action to ensure that future performance conforms to contract requirements and reduce any fee payable under the contract to reflect the reduced value of the services performed [@CL-SUP-1093; @CL-SUP-4167]. The general policy on rejecting nonconforming supplies and services is on [Rejection of nonconforming supplies](../../operating-model/corrective-action/opm-rejection-of-nonconforming-supplies.md), and the place and manner of inspection are on [Government contract quality assurance](sup-government-contract-quality-assurance.md).
