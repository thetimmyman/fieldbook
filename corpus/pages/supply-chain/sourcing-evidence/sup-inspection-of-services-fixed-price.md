## Purpose

Shows how the services inspection clause for fixed-price contracts requires an inspection system, lets the Government require reperformance at no increase in price, and sets the remedies when defects cannot be corrected.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-SUP-1083] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.246-4 [@CL-SUP-1083] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.246-4 [@CL-SUP-3237] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-1083] |
| Applies to | Contracts that include the Inspection of Services—Fixed-Price clause [@CL-SUP-1083]; the clause is directed for solicitations and contracts for services, or supplies that involve the furnishing of services, when a fixed-price contract is contemplated and the contract amount is expected to exceed the simplified acquisition threshold [@CL-SUP-1132], and the contracting officer may insert it when the contract amount is expected to be at or below that threshold and inclusion is in the Government's interest [@CL-SUP-9777] |

The contractor must provide and maintain an inspection system acceptable to the Government that covers the services under the contract [@CL-SUP-1083], and must keep complete records of its inspection work available to the Government during performance and afterwards for as long as the contract requires [@CL-SUP-1084]. The Government has the right to inspect and test all services called for by the contract, to the extent practicable, at all times and places during the term of the contract [@CL-SUP-1085].

If any services do not conform with contract requirements, the Government may require the contractor to perform them again in conformity with contract requirements at no increase in contract amount [@CL-SUP-1086]. When the defects cannot be corrected by reperformance, the Government may require the necessary action to ensure future performance conforms and may reduce the contract price to reflect the reduced value of the services performed [@CL-SUP-1087].

## How it works

The contractor keeps the inspection system and records, and the Government may inspect and test all services during the term of the contract [@CL-SUP-1083; @CL-SUP-1084; @CL-SUP-1085]. Nonconforming services are first reperformed at no increase in contract amount [@CL-SUP-1086]. Where reperformance cannot correct the defects, the Government may require action to ensure future conformity and may reduce the contract price [@CL-SUP-1087]. The clause then lists remedies for a contractor that fails to promptly reperform or to take the necessary action [@CL-SUP-1088]. One is that the Government performs the services, by contract or otherwise, and charges the contractor any cost it incurs that is directly related to performing the services; another is termination for default [@CL-SUP-1089].

Under the RFO text, the DoD class deviation directs DoD contracting officers to use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text, effective February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. The RFO clause keeps the contractor's duty to provide and maintain an inspection system acceptable to the Government [@CL-SUP-1083] [@CL-SUP-3237], the duty to keep complete inspection records available to the Government [@CL-SUP-1084] [@CL-SUP-3238] and the Government's right to inspect and test all services during the term of the contract [@CL-SUP-1085] [@CL-SUP-3239]. It keeps reperformance of nonconforming services at no increase in contract amount [@CL-SUP-1086] [@CL-SUP-3240], and, when defects cannot be corrected by reperformance, the option to require action to ensure future conformity and to reduce the contract price [@CL-SUP-1087] [@CL-SUP-3241]. It keeps the listed remedies if the contractor fails to promptly perform again or take the necessary action [@CL-SUP-1088] [@CL-SUP-1089] [@CL-SUP-3242] [@CL-SUP-3243].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A fixed-price contract for cleaning services is worth 100,000. Inspection finds that one month of service fell below the contract standard. The Government requires the contractor to perform that month again at no increase in price, so the price stays 100,000. A missed safety check cannot be redone, so reperformance cannot correct it. The Government may then reduce the price to reflect the reduced value, for example by 2,000, and require action to keep future service conforming.

## Never confuse

Reperformance and price reduction are different remedies. Reperformance comes first, and the price reduction applies when defects cannot be corrected by reperformance [@CL-SUP-1086; @CL-SUP-1087]. The cost-reimbursement version of the clause, on [Inspection of services, cost-reimbursement](sup-inspection-of-services-cost-reimbursement.md), reduces any fee payable instead of the contract price [@CL-SUP-1093]. The general policy on rejecting nonconforming supplies and services is on [Rejection of nonconforming supplies](../../operating-model/corrective-action/opm-rejection-of-nonconforming-supplies.md), and the place and manner of inspection are on [Government contract quality assurance](sup-government-contract-quality-assurance.md).
