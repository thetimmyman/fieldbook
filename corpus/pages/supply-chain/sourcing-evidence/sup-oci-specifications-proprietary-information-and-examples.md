## Purpose

Shows which situations bar a contractor from a follow-on award, where specifications or work statements were prepared, and what a contractor must agree to when it gains access to other companies' proprietary information.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-SUP-0944] |
| Codified citation | FAR 9.505-2, FAR 9.505-4 and FAR 9.508 [@CL-SUP-0944; @CL-SUP-0948; @CL-SUP-0949] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-0944] |
| Applies to | Contractors that prepare specifications or work statements, or gain access to other companies' proprietary information, for Government acquisitions [@CL-SUP-0944; @CL-SUP-0946; @CL-SUP-0948] |

If a contractor prepares and furnishes complete specifications covering nondevelopmental items to be used in a competitive acquisition, that contractor shall not be allowed to furnish these items, as a prime contractor or as a subcontractor, for a reasonable period of time [@CL-SUP-0944]. That period includes, at least, the duration of the initial production contract [@CL-SUP-0978]. The restriction does not apply to contractors that furnish at Government request specifications or data regarding a product they provide, which is one of the listed exceptions [@CL-SUP-0945].

The work-statement restriction applies where a contractor prepares, or assists in preparing, a work statement to be used in competitively acquiring a system or services, or provides material leading directly, predictably and without delay to such a work statement [@CL-SUP-0946]. That contractor may not supply the system, major components of the system or the services unless it is the sole source, has participated in the development and design work, or more than one contractor has been involved in preparing the work statement [@CL-SUP-0947]. General rules and principles are on the [Organizational conflicts of interest: general rules](../../mna/diligence/mna-organizational-conflicts-of-interest-general-rules.md) card.

## How it works

The first bar covers the nondevelopmental items whose complete specifications the contractor prepared and furnished, and the second covers the system, major components or services whose work statement it prepared or helped prepare [@CL-SUP-0944; @CL-SUP-0946]. Each has its listed exceptions [@CL-SUP-0945; @CL-SUP-0947]. In one listed example, because the specifications are the basis for selection of commercial hardware, a potential conflict of interest exists and the company named in the example should be excluded from the initial follow-on information technology hardware acquisition [@CL-SUP-0949].

Proprietary information works differently: a contractor that gains access to other companies' proprietary information in performing advisory and assistance services for the Government must agree with the other companies to protect their information from unauthorized use or disclosure for as long as it remains proprietary and to refrain from using the information for any purpose other than that for which it was furnished [@CL-SUP-0948; @CL-SUP-4048]. This card does not summarise the rest of the printed section on access to proprietary information.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A firm is paid 500,000 to write complete specifications for a nondevelopmental item that the agency will buy competitively. Later the firm wants to supply the item in the production buy.

Answer: the firm shall not be allowed to furnish the item for a reasonable period of time, unless an exception applies, such as having supplied the specifications at Government request for a product it already provides.

## Never confuse

A bar on supplying is not the same as a duty to protect information. The follow-on bar stops the contractor from furnishing items or services, while the proprietary information rule requires an agreement with the other companies to protect their information and to use it only for the purpose for which it was furnished [@CL-SUP-0944; @CL-SUP-0948; @CL-SUP-4048]. A listed example illustrates one situation and does not replace the general rules on the [Organizational conflicts of interest: general rules](../../mna/diligence/mna-organizational-conflicts-of-interest-general-rules.md) card. How the contracting officer analyzes a planned acquisition and approves a course of action is on the [Organizational conflicts of interest: contracting officer procedures](sup-oci-contracting-officer-procedures.md) card.

## Company calibration

Confirm whether the company prepared specifications or work statements, or received other companies' proprietary information, on any acquisition it may later bid, and whether protective agreements are in place. [@Q-028]
