## Purpose

Shows which situations bar a contractor from a follow-on award, where specifications or work statements were prepared, and what a contractor must agree to when it gains access to other companies' proprietary information.

## The rule

|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-SUP-0944] |
| Codified citation | FAR 9.505-2, FAR 9.505-3, FAR 9.505-4 and FAR 9.508 [@CL-SUP-0944; @CL-SUP-4153; @CL-SUP-0948; @CL-SUP-0949] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-0944] |
| Applies to | Contractors that prepare specifications or work statements, or gain access to other companies' proprietary information, for Government acquisitions [@CL-SUP-0944; @CL-SUP-0946; @CL-SUP-0948] |

If a contractor prepares and furnishes complete specifications covering nondevelopmental items, to be used in a competitive acquisition, that contractor shall not be allowed to furnish these items, either as a prime contractor or as a subcontractor, for a reasonable period of time including, at least, the duration of the initial production contract [@CL-SUP-0944; @CL-SUP-0978]. The restriction in FAR 9.505-2(a)(1) shall not apply to either of two alternatives: contractors that furnish at Government request specifications or data regarding a product they provide, even though the specifications or data may have been paid for separately or in the price of the product [@CL-SUP-0945]; or situations in which contractors, acting as industry representatives, help Government agencies prepare, refine, or coordinate specifications, regardless of source, provided this assistance is supervised and controlled by Government representatives [@CL-SUP-4173].

If a contractor prepares, or assists in preparing, a work statement to be used in competitively acquiring a system or services—or provides material leading directly, predictably, and without delay to such a work statement—that contractor may not supply the system, major components of the system, or the services unless one of three alternatives applies: it is the sole source, it has participated in the development and design work, or more than one contractor has been involved in preparing the work statement [@CL-SUP-0946; @CL-SUP-0947]. See [Organizational conflicts of interest: general rules](../../mna/diligence/mna-organizational-conflicts-of-interest-general-rules.md).

Contracts for the evaluation of offers for products or services shall not be awarded to a contractor that will evaluate its own offers for products or services, or those of a competitor, without proper safeguards to ensure objectivity to protect the Government's interests [@CL-SUP-4153].

## How it works

Under FAR 9.505-2(a)(1), a contractor that prepares and furnishes complete specifications covering nondevelopmental items to be used in a competitive acquisition shall not be allowed to furnish these items, either as a prime contractor or as a subcontractor, for a reasonable period of time including, at least, the duration of the initial production contract, unless one of the two alternatives in FAR 9.505-2(a)(1)(i) or (ii) applies [@CL-SUP-0944; @CL-SUP-0978; @CL-SUP-0945; @CL-SUP-4173]. Under FAR 9.505-2(b)(1), a contractor that prepares, or assists in preparing, a work statement to be used in competitively acquiring a system or services—or provides material leading directly, predictably, and without delay to such a work statement—may not supply the system, major components of the system, or the services unless one of the three alternatives in FAR 9.505-2(b)(1)(i)-(iii) applies [@CL-SUP-0946; @CL-SUP-0947]. Under FAR 9.508(e), since the specifications are the basis for selection of commercial hardware, a potential conflict of interest exists, and Company A should be excluded from the initial follow-on information technology hardware acquisition [@CL-SUP-0949].

A contractor that gains access to proprietary information of other companies in performing advisory and assistance services for the Government must agree with the other companies to protect their information from unauthorized use or disclosure for as long as it remains proprietary and refrain from using the information for any purpose other than that for which it was furnished [@CL-SUP-0948; @CL-SUP-4048].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A firm is paid 500,000 to write complete specifications for a nondevelopmental item that the agency will buy competitively. Later the firm wants to supply the item in the production buy.

Answer: the firm shall not be allowed to furnish the item for a reasonable period of time, unless an exception applies, such as having supplied the specifications at Government request for a product it already provides.

## Never confuse

Under FAR 9.505-2(a)(1), a contractor that prepares and furnishes complete specifications covering nondevelopmental items to be used in a competitive acquisition shall not be allowed to furnish these items, either as a prime contractor or as a subcontractor, for a reasonable period of time [@CL-SUP-0944], whereas under FAR 9.505-4(b), a contractor that gains access to proprietary information of other companies in performing advisory and assistance services for the Government must agree with the other companies to protect their information from unauthorized use or disclosure for as long as it remains proprietary and refrain from using the information for any purpose other than that for which it was furnished [@CL-SUP-0948; @CL-SUP-4048]. See [Organizational conflicts of interest: general rules](../../mna/diligence/mna-organizational-conflicts-of-interest-general-rules.md) and [Organizational conflicts of interest: contracting officer procedures](sup-oci-contracting-officer-procedures.md).

## Company calibration

Confirm whether the company prepared specifications or work statements, or received other companies' proprietary information, on any acquisition it may later bid, and whether protective agreements are in place. [@Q-028]
