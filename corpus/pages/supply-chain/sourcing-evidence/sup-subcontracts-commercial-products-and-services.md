## Purpose

Shows which clauses a prime contractor must put in subcontracts for commercial products and commercial services, and the defense duty to decide whether a subcontract item is commercial.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-SUP-0550] |
| Codified citation | FAR 44.402 and FAR 52.244-6 [@CL-SUP-0550; @CL-SUP-0555]; FAR 44.400 [@CL-SUP-9466]; FAR 44.401 [@CL-SUP-9467; @CL-SUP-9468] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.244-6 [@CL-SUP-3806; @CL-SUP-3807; @CL-SUP-3808; @CL-SUP-9421; @CL-SUP-9422]; RFO FAR 2.101 [@CL-SUP-9619; @CL-SUP-9620; @CL-SUP-9630; @CL-SUP-9636; @CL-SUP-9638; @CL-SUP-9640] |
| Edition date | OCT 2025 [@CL-SUP-9878] |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-0550] |
| Applies to | Subcontracts for commercial products or commercial services, under the clause inserted as prescribed in FAR 44.403 [@CL-SUP-0685; @CL-SUP-0550] |

Notwithstanding any other clause in the prime contract, only those clauses identified in the clause at 52.244-6 are required to be in subcontracts for commercial products or commercial services [@CL-SUP-0550]. Agencies may supplement the clause only as necessary to reflect agency unique statutes applicable to the acquisition of commercial products and commercial services [@CL-SUP-0686].

Contractors and subcontractors at all tiers are required, to the maximum extent practicable, to incorporate commercial products, commercial services, or nondevelopmental items as components of items delivered to the Government [@CL-SUP-0551]. That is the first of two listed requirements in the paragraph; the second, on clauses that subcontractors furnishing commercial products or commercial services may not be required to apply, is not summarized here.

The FAR's general definitions say a product is a commercial product under paragraph (1) of that definition when it is a product, other than real property, of a type customarily used by the general public or by nongovernmental entities for purposes other than governmental purposes, and it has been sold, leased, or licensed to the general public or has been offered for sale, lease, or license to the general public [@CL-SUP-9540; @CL-SUP-9546]. The RFO text of FAR 2.101 states paragraph (1) of the commercial product definition with the added words (i.e., purposes that are not unique to a government) after purposes other than governmental purposes [@CL-SUP-9619; @CL-SUP-9620]; its alternative that the product has been offered for sale, lease, or license to the general public is the same [@CL-SUP-9620]. The RFO text goes on to include, as paragraph (2), a product that evolved from a product described in paragraph (1) through advances in technology or performance and that is not yet available in the commercial marketplace, but will be available in the commercial marketplace in time to satisfy the delivery requirements under a Government solicitation [@CL-SUP-9620; @CL-SUP-9644; @CL-SUP-9645]. Paragraph (3) includes a product that would satisfy a criterion expressed in paragraph (1) or (2) except for (i) modifications of a type customarily available in the commercial marketplace or (ii) minor modifications of a type not customarily available in the commercial marketplace made to meet Federal Government requirements [@CL-SUP-9645; @CL-SUP-9646], and a minor modification does not significantly alter the function or essential physical characteristics of an item or component or change the purpose of a process [@CL-SUP-9646]. Paragraph (4) includes any combination of products meeting the requirements of paragraph (1), (2), or (3) that are of a type customarily combined and sold in combination to the general public [@CL-SUP-9647]. Paragraph (5) includes a product, or combination of products, referred to in paragraphs (1) through (4), even though it is transferred between or among separate divisions, subsidiaries, or affiliates of a contractor [@CL-SUP-9648]. Paragraph (6) includes a nondevelopmental item developed exclusively at private expense and sold in substantial quantities, on a competitive basis to multiple State and local governments or to multiple foreign governments [@CL-SUP-9649]. Under paragraph (1) of the commercial service definition, installation services, maintenance services, repair services, training services, and other services are a commercial service if two conditions are met [@CL-SUP-9635]. The first is that the services are procured for support of a commercial product as defined in that section, regardless of whether they are provided by the same source or at the same time as the commercial product [@CL-SUP-9637]. The second is that the source of the services provides similar services contemporaneously to the general public under terms and conditions similar to those offered to the Federal Government [@CL-SUP-9639]. The RFO text of FAR 2.101 states paragraph (1) with a comma moved in the first condition, and with the words at the same time and the Government in place of contemporaneously and the Federal Government in the second [@CL-SUP-9636; @CL-SUP-9638; @CL-SUP-9640]. Under paragraph (2) of the commercial service definition, services of a type offered and sold competitively in substantial quantities in the commercial marketplace based on established catalog or market prices for specific tasks performed or specific outcomes to be achieved and under standard commercial terms and conditions are a commercial service [@CL-SUP-9541]. The RFO text of FAR 2.101 states paragraph (2) of the commercial service definition with the added words including construction after services, and the rest of the paragraph is the same [@CL-SUP-9630; @CL-SUP-9541]. The codified text of the other paragraphs of the commercial product definition is not summarized here.

Subpart 44.4 prescribes the policies limiting the contract clauses a contractor may be required to apply to any subcontractors that are furnishing commercial products, including commercial components, or commercial services in accordance with section 3307 of title 41 of the United States Code [@CL-SUP-9466]. The subpart applies to all contracts and subcontracts, and for its purposes the term subcontract has the same meaning as defined in part 12 [@CL-SUP-9467; @CL-SUP-9468].

## How it works

While not required, the contractor may flow down to subcontracts for commercial products or commercial services a minimal number of additional clauses necessary to satisfy its contractual obligations [@CL-SUP-0554]. The contractor shall include the terms of the clause, including its paragraph (d), in subcontracts awarded under the contract [@CL-SUP-0555]. Under the defense supplement, contractors are required to determine whether a particular subcontract item meets the definition of a commercial product or commercial service [@CL-SUP-0552]. This requirement does not affect the contracting officer's responsibilities or determinations made under FAR 15.403-1(c)(3) [@CL-SUP-9788]. They are expected to exercise reasonable business judgment, consistent with the guidelines for conducting market research in FAR part 10 [@CL-SUP-0687]. Items valued at less than $10,000 per item that a contractor purchases for use in the performance of multiple contracts with the Department of Defense and other parties, and that are not identifiable to any particular contract when purchased, are treated as commercial products [@CL-SUP-0553].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text from February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. RFO FAR 52.244-6 is inserted as prescribed in FAR 44.403, as the codified clause is [@CL-SUP-0685; @CL-SUP-3806]. Its paragraph (b)(1) says that, to the maximum extent practicable, the contractor shall incorporate, and require its subcontractors at all tiers to incorporate, commercial products, commercial services, or non-developmental items as components of items to be supplied under the contract [@CL-SUP-3807]. Its paragraph (b)(2) says that if a clause in the following table is included in the contract, the contractor shall insert the clause in subcontracts for commercial products or commercial services and must flow down the requirements of the clause to subcontracts as indicated in the specific clause [@CL-SUP-3808]. Its paragraph (c) says the contractor shall include the terms of the clause, including that paragraph (c), in subcontracts awarded under the contract, where the codified clause puts the same duty in its paragraph (d) [@CL-SUP-0555; @CL-SUP-9421]. After the table and its note, the RFO clause prints only that paragraph (c) before it ends, so the RFO text prints no counterpart to the codified sentence that lets the contractor flow down a minimal number of additional clauses necessary to satisfy its contractual obligations [@CL-SUP-0554; @CL-SUP-9422].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor buys commercial power assemblies for a defense contract whose prime contract carries many other clauses. It wants to add several of those clauses to the supplier's order.

Answer: if a clause in the clause's table is included in the contract, the contractor shall insert it in the order and flow down its requirements as the specific clause indicates [@CL-SUP-3808]. Under the codified clause the contractor may also flow down a minimal number of further clauses necessary to satisfy its contractual obligations, though it is not required to [@CL-SUP-0554]. The RFO text prints no counterpart to that sentence [@CL-SUP-9422], so this example does not establish that extra clauses may be added under the RFO text.

## Never confuse

Relief from flowdowns is not relief from deciding what is commercial. Under the defense supplement the contractor is required to determine whether a particular subcontract item meets the definition of a commercial product or commercial service, and that requirement does not affect the contracting officer's responsibilities or determinations made under FAR 15.403-1(c)(3) [@CL-SUP-0552; @CL-SUP-9788]. Purchasing system and consent topics are on the [Contractor purchasing system](../procure-to-pay/sup-contractor-purchasing-system.md) card, and make-or-buy on the [Make-or-buy programs](sup-make-or-buy-programs.md) card.
