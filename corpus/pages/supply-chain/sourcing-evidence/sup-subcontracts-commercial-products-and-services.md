## Purpose

Shows which clauses a prime contractor must put in subcontracts for commercial products and commercial services, and the defense duty to decide whether a subcontract item is commercial.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-SUP-0550] |
| Codified citation | FAR 44.402 and FAR 52.244-6 [@CL-SUP-0550; @CL-SUP-0555]; FAR 44.400 [@CL-SUP-9466]; FAR 44.401 [@CL-SUP-9467; @CL-SUP-9468] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.244-6 [@CL-SUP-3806; @CL-SUP-3807; @CL-SUP-3808; @CL-SUP-9421; @CL-SUP-9422] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-0550] |
| Applies to | Subcontracts for commercial products or commercial services, under the clause inserted as prescribed in FAR 44.403 [@CL-SUP-0685; @CL-SUP-0550] |

Notwithstanding any other clause in the prime contract, only those clauses identified in the clause at 52.244-6 are required to be in subcontracts for commercial products or commercial services [@CL-SUP-0550]. Agencies may supplement the clause only as necessary to reflect agency unique statutes applicable to the acquisition of commercial products and commercial services [@CL-SUP-0686].

Contractors and subcontractors at all tiers are required, to the maximum extent practicable, to incorporate commercial products, commercial services, or nondevelopmental items as components of items delivered to the Government [@CL-SUP-0551].

The FAR's general definitions say a product is a commercial product under paragraph (1) of that definition when it is a product, other than real property, of a type customarily used by the general public or by nongovernmental entities for purposes other than governmental purposes, and it has been sold, leased, or licensed to the general public or has been offered for sale, lease, or license to the general public [@CL-SUP-9540]. Under paragraph (2) of the commercial service definition, services of a type offered and sold competitively in substantial quantities in the commercial marketplace based on established catalog or market prices for specific tasks performed or specific outcomes to be achieved and under standard commercial terms and conditions are a commercial service [@CL-SUP-9541]. The other paragraphs of the two definitions are not summarized here.

Subpart 44.4 prescribes the policies limiting the contract clauses a contractor may be required to apply to any subcontractors that are furnishing commercial products, including commercial components, or commercial services in accordance with section 3307 of title 41 of the United States Code [@CL-SUP-9466]. The subpart applies to all contracts and subcontracts, and for its purposes the term subcontract has the same meaning as defined in part 12 [@CL-SUP-9467; @CL-SUP-9468].

## How it works

While not required, the contractor may flow down to subcontracts for commercial products or commercial services a minimal number of additional clauses necessary to satisfy its contractual obligations [@CL-SUP-0554]. The contractor shall include the terms of the clause, including its paragraph (d), in subcontracts awarded under the contract [@CL-SUP-0555]. Under the defense supplement, contractors are required to determine whether a particular subcontract item meets the definition of a commercial product or commercial service [@CL-SUP-0552]. They are expected to exercise reasonable business judgment, consistent with the guidelines for conducting market research in FAR part 10 [@CL-SUP-0687]. Items valued at less than $10,000 per item that a contractor purchases for use in the performance of multiple contracts with the Department of Defense and other parties, and that are not identifiable to any particular contract when purchased, are treated as commercial products [@CL-SUP-0553].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text from February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. RFO FAR 52.244-6 is inserted as prescribed in FAR 44.403, as the codified clause is [@CL-SUP-0685; @CL-SUP-3806]. Its paragraph (b)(1) says that, to the maximum extent practicable, the contractor shall incorporate, and require its subcontractors at all tiers to incorporate, commercial products, commercial services, or non-developmental items as components of items to be supplied under the contract [@CL-SUP-3807]. Its paragraph (b)(2) says that if a clause in the following table is included in the contract, the contractor shall insert the clause in subcontracts for commercial products or commercial services and must flow down the requirements of the clause to subcontracts as indicated in the specific clause [@CL-SUP-3808]. Its paragraph (c) says the contractor shall include the terms of the clause, including that paragraph (c), in subcontracts awarded under the contract, where the codified clause puts the same duty in its paragraph (d) [@CL-SUP-0555; @CL-SUP-9421]. After the table and its note, the RFO clause prints only that paragraph (c) before it ends, so the RFO text prints no counterpart to the codified sentence that lets the contractor flow down a minimal number of additional clauses necessary to satisfy its contractual obligations [@CL-SUP-0554; @CL-SUP-9422].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor buys commercial power assemblies for a defense contract whose prime contract carries many other clauses. It wants to add several of those clauses to the supplier's order.

Answer: the order is required to carry only the clauses identified in the commercial subcontracts clause. The contractor may add a minimal number of further clauses needed to meet its own contractual obligations, but it is not required to.

## Never confuse

Relief from flowdowns is not relief from deciding what is commercial. Under the defense supplement the contractor is required to determine whether a particular subcontract item meets the definition of a commercial product or commercial service [@CL-SUP-0552]. Purchasing system and consent topics are on the [Contractor purchasing system](../procure-to-pay/sup-contractor-purchasing-system.md) card, and make-or-buy on the [Make-or-buy programs](sup-make-or-buy-programs.md) card.
