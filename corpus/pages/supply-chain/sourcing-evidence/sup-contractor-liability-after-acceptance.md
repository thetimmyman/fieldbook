## Purpose

Shows which contracts the subpart covers, what loss the Government generally relieves the contractor of after acceptance, what the contractor still bears, when the relief does not apply, and which limitation of liability clauses are prescribed.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-SUP-0888] |
| Codified citation | FAR 46.801, 46.802, 46.803 and 46.805 [@CL-SUP-0968; @CL-SUP-0959; @CL-SUP-0888; @CL-SUP-0895] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-0888] |
| Applies to | Contracts other than those for commercial products and commercial services, information technology including telecommunications, construction, architect-engineer services, and maintenance and rehabilitation of real property [@CL-SUP-0968]; loss of or damage to Government property after acceptance that results from defects or deficiencies in the supplies or services [@CL-SUP-0888; @CL-SUP-4023] |

The subpart does not apply to commercial products and commercial services, and it applies to contracts other than those for information technology including telecommunications, construction, architect-engineer services, and maintenance and rehabilitation of real property [@CL-SUP-0968]. Within that scope, the Government will generally act as a self-insurer by relieving contractors, as specified in the subpart, of liability for loss of or damage to property of the Government that occurs after acceptance of supplies delivered or services performed under a contract and results from defects or deficiencies in the supplies or services [@CL-SUP-0888; @CL-SUP-4023]. The Government will not relieve the contractor of liability for loss of or damage to the contract end item itself, except for high-value items [@CL-SUP-0889]. In contracts requiring delivery of high-value items, the Government will relieve contractors of contractual liability for loss of or damage to those items [@CL-SUP-0890]. A high-value item is a contract end item that has a high unit cost, normally exceeding 100000 dollars per unit, and is designated by the contracting officer as a high-value item [@CL-SUP-0959]. That relief does not limit the Government's rights arising under the contract to have any defective item or its components corrected, repaired, or replaced when the defect or deficiency is discovered before the loss of or damage to a high-value item occurs [@CL-SUP-0970].

The Government will not provide contractual relief under the general and high-value paragraphs when contractor liability can be preserved without increasing the contract price [@CL-SUP-0891]. Subject to the specific terms of the limitation of liability clause in the contract, the relief does not apply, among other listed cases, to the extent that contractor liability is expressly provided under a contract clause authorized by this regulation [@CL-SUP-0892]. Where the relief applies at all, the loss the contractor keeps bearing includes the contract end item itself, except for high-value items [@CL-SUP-0889], and loss in the listed cases where the relief does not apply, subject to the clause terms [@CL-SUP-0892; @CL-SUP-0893; @CL-SUP-0894].

## How it works

Two further listed cases are a defect or deficiency in, or Government acceptance of, the supplies or services that results from willful misconduct or lack of good faith on the part of the contractor's managerial personnel [@CL-SUP-0892; @CL-SUP-0893], and liability to the extent that contractor insurance or a self-insurance reserve covers loss or damage suffered by the Government through purchase or use of the supplies or services [@CL-SUP-0892; @CL-SUP-0894]. For contracts expected to exceed the simplified acquisition threshold and subject to the subpart as indicated in FAR 46.801, FAR 46.805(a) directs insertion of the clause at FAR 52.246-23, Limitation of Liability, in contracts requiring delivery of end items that are not high-value items [@CL-SUP-0895], and the clause at FAR 52.246-24, Limitation of Liability—High-Value Items, in contracts requiring delivery of high-value items [@CL-SUP-0895; @CL-SUP-0896]. The same paragraph also lists the clause at FAR 52.246-25, Limitation of Liability—Services, for contracts requiring the performance of services [@CL-SUP-0895; @CL-SUP-0897]. The clauses prescribed for contracts exceeding the simplified acquisition threshold are not required for contracts at or below it [@CL-SUP-0898], but in response to a contractor's specific request the contracting officer may insert the clauses prescribed in FAR 46.805(a)(1) or (a)(4) in such a contract and may obtain any price reduction that is appropriate [@CL-SUP-0899]. The policy on contractor liability caused by nonconforming technical data is in the subpart on warranties, which is on [Warranties: criteria, terms and remedies](sup-warranties.md) [@CL-SUP-0969].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor delivers 20 end items that are not high-value items under a contract with a limitation of liability clause, and the Government accepts them. After acceptance a defect in one unit, worth 5,000, starts a fire that destroys the unit and a Government test stand worth 60,000. Assume no misconduct, no insurance cover, no other clause that makes the contractor liable, and no withholding of relief. The answer: the relief covers the loss of the test stand, but the contractor still bears the loss of the end item itself.

## Never confuse

This relief covers loss of or damage to Government property after acceptance that results from defects. It is not the rule on liability for Government property in the contractor's possession, which is on [Responsibility and liability for Government property](../../close-revrec/fixed-assets/cls-responsibility-liability-government-property.md), and it does not change the effect of acceptance, which is on [Inspection of Supplies—Fixed-Price clause](../../close-revrec/revenue/cls-inspection-of-supplies-fixed-price.md).
