## Purpose

Shows when a Certificate of Conformance may be shipped in place of source inspection, what the certificate says, and the Government's inspection rights for dismantling, demolition or removal work.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-SUP-1121; @CL-SUP-1125] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.246-15 and 52.246-13 [@CL-SUP-1121; @CL-SUP-1125] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.246-15 and 52.246-13 [@CL-SUP-2903; @CL-SUP-2909] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-1121; @CL-SUP-1125] |
| Applies to | Supplies for which the contract would otherwise require inspection at source, once the cognizant office authorizes a certificate in writing [@CL-SUP-1121]; the certificate clause is directed for solicitations and contracts for supplies or services when the conditions in FAR 46.504 apply [@CL-SUP-1143], which allow use of a certificate at the contracting officer's discretion [@CL-SUP-1148]; workmanship on dismantling or demolition work [@CL-SUP-1125]; the dismantling clause is directed for solicitations and contracts for dismantling, demolition, or removal of improvements [@CL-SUP-1146] |

When the cognizant Contract Administration Office authorizes it in writing, the contractor shall ship with a Certificate of Conformance any supplies for which the contract would otherwise require inspection at source [@CL-SUP-1121]. Under the Certificate of Conformance clause, in no case shall the Government's right to inspect supplies under the inspection provisions of the contract be prejudiced [@CL-SUP-1122].

A certificate of conformance may be used in certain instances instead of source inspection (whether the contract calls for acceptance at source or destination), at the discretion of the contracting officer, if the following conditions apply [@CL-SUP-1148]. One condition is that acceptance on the basis of the certificate is in the Government's interest [@CL-SUP-1149]. A second condition lists two alternatives: small losses would be incurred in the event of a defect, or, because of the contractor's reputation or past performance, it is likely that the supplies or services furnished will be acceptable and any defective work would be replaced, corrected or repaired without contest [@CL-SUP-1150].

The Government has the right to reject defective supplies or services within a reasonable time after delivery by written notification to the contractor, and the contractor shall then promptly replace, correct or repair the rejected supplies or services at its own expense [@CL-SUP-1144].

The clause requires the certificate to state the date, the contractor's name, the contract number, the carrier and the shipping document, and that the supplies or services were furnished in accordance with all applicable requirements [@CL-SUP-1123]. The certificate further states that the supplies or services are of the quality specified and conform in all respects with the contract requirements, including specifications, drawings, preservation, packaging, packing, marking requirements and physical item identification [@CL-SUP-1124]. It also states that the supplies or services are in the quantity shown on the document that carries the certificate or on the attached acceptance document [@CL-SUP-1145].

For dismantling, demolition or removal of improvements, unless the specifications designate otherwise, all workmanship performed under the contract is subject to Government inspection at all times and places where dismantling or demolition work is being performed [@CL-SUP-1125]. The contractor is responsible for damage to property caused by defective workmanship, and must promptly segregate and remove from the premises any unsatisfactory facilities, materials and equipment used in contract performance and promptly replace them with satisfactory items [@CL-SUP-1147].

## How it works

A certificate is not automatic. It may be used at the contracting officer's discretion if the listed conditions apply [@CL-SUP-1148]. When the cognizant office authorizes it in writing, the contractor ships with the certificate any supplies for which the contract would otherwise require inspection at source [@CL-SUP-1121]. The contractor ships the supplies with the certificate, whose text is set out in the clause [@CL-SUP-1123; @CL-SUP-1124]. The Government keeps its right to inspect the supplies under the contract's inspection provisions [@CL-SUP-1122], and may reject defective supplies or services within a reasonable time after delivery [@CL-SUP-1144]. For dismantling work, the separate clause makes the workmanship subject to Government inspection at all times and places where the dismantling or demolition work is being performed, unless the specifications designate otherwise [@CL-SUP-1125].

Class Deviation 2026-O0038 directs DoD contracting officers to use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text, effective February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. In RFO FAR 52.246-15 and 52.246-13, which keep these rules, the contractor ships with a Certificate of Conformance, when the cognizant Contract Administration Office authorizes it in writing, any supplies for which the contract would otherwise require inspection at source [@CL-SUP-2903] [@CL-SUP-1121], and in no case is the Government's right to inspect supplies under the inspection provisions of the contract prejudiced [@CL-SUP-2904] [@CL-SUP-1122]. The Government has the right to reject defective supplies or services within a reasonable time after delivery by written notification to the contractor, and the contractor then promptly replaces, corrects or repairs them at its own expense [@CL-SUP-2905] [@CL-SUP-1144]. The certificate states the date, the contractor's name, the contract number, the carrier and the shipping document, and that the supplies or services were furnished in accordance with all applicable requirements [@CL-SUP-2906] [@CL-SUP-1123]. It further states that they are of the quality specified and conform in all respects with the contract requirements, including specifications, drawings, preservation, packaging, packing, marking requirements and physical item identification [@CL-SUP-2907] [@CL-SUP-1124], and that they are in the quantity shown on the document that carries the certificate or on the attached acceptance document [@CL-SUP-2908] [@CL-SUP-1145]. Unless the specifications designate otherwise, all workmanship performed under the contract is subject to Government inspection at all times and places where dismantling or demolition work is being performed [@CL-SUP-2909] [@CL-SUP-1125]. The contractor is responsible for damage to property caused by defective workmanship and promptly segregates and removes from the premises any unsatisfactory facilities, materials and equipment used in contract performance and promptly replaces them with satisfactory items [@CL-SUP-2910] [@CL-SUP-1147].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contract requires source inspection for 1,000 fittings. The cognizant office authorizes a certificate in writing. The contractor ships the fittings with a certificate that states the date, the carrier and the shipping document. The Government can still inspect the fittings under the contract's inspection provisions, so the certificate does not remove that right.

## Never confuse

A Certificate of Conformance is shipped in place of source inspection when the cognizant office authorizes it in writing, and it does not prejudice the Government's right to inspect supplies [@CL-SUP-1121; @CL-SUP-1122]. It is a different thing from the inspection of dismantling work, which is a right to inspect workmanship where the work is being performed [@CL-SUP-1125]. The general policy on where inspection happens is on [Government contract quality assurance](sup-government-contract-quality-assurance.md), and the policy on rejecting nonconforming supplies is on [Rejection of nonconforming supplies](../../operating-model/corrective-action/opm-rejection-of-nonconforming-supplies.md). The inspection clauses that a certificate can stand in for at source are on [Inspection of supplies, cost-reimbursement](sup-inspection-of-supplies-cost-reimbursement.md) and [Inspection of supplies, fixed-price](../../close-revrec/revenue/cls-inspection-of-supplies-fixed-price.md).
