## Purpose

Shows how defective supplies and services are corrected under the inspection clause for time-and-materials and labor-hour contracts, what the Government pays for, and when acceptance is presumed.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-SUP-1096] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.246-6 [@CL-SUP-1096] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.246-6 [@CL-SUP-3258] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-1096] |
| Applies to | Contracts that include the Inspection—Time-and-Material and Labor-Hour clause [@CL-SUP-1096]; the clause is directed for solicitations and contracts when a time-and-material contract or a labor-hour contract is contemplated [@CL-SUP-1134] |

The contractor shall provide and maintain an inspection system acceptable to the Government covering the material, fabricating methods, work, and services under the contract [@CL-SUP-1096]. The Government has the right to inspect and test all materials furnished and services performed under the contract, to the extent practicable at all places and times, including the period of performance, and in any event before acceptance [@CL-SUP-1097].

Unless otherwise specified in the contract, the Government shall accept or reject services and materials at the place of delivery as promptly as practicable after delivery, and they shall be presumed accepted 60 days after the date of delivery, unless accepted earlier [@CL-SUP-1098].

At any time during contract performance, but not later than 6 months (or such other time as may be specified in the contract) after acceptance of the services or materials last delivered under the contract, the Government may require the contractor to replace or correct services or materials that at time of delivery failed to meet contract requirements [@CL-SUP-1099; @CL-SUP-1100]. Except as otherwise specified in paragraph (h) of the clause, the cost of replacement or correction shall be determined under the Payments Under Time-and-Materials and Labor-Hour Contracts clause, but the hourly rate for labor hours incurred in the replacement or correction shall be reduced to exclude that portion of the rate attributable to profit [@CL-SUP-1101; @CL-SUP-1102].

Under paragraph (h), notwithstanding paragraphs (f) and (g), the Government may at any time require the contractor to remedy by correction or replacement, without cost to the Government, any failure by the contractor to comply with the requirements of the contract, if the failure is due to one of two alternative causes: fraud, lack of good faith, or willful misconduct on the part of the contractor's managerial personnel, or the conduct of one or more of the contractor's employees selected or retained by the contractor after any of the contractor's managerial personnel has reasonable grounds to believe that the employee is habitually careless or unqualified [@CL-SUP-9779; @CL-SUP-1103; @CL-SUP-1104; @CL-SUP-4170; @CL-SUP-9780].

## How it works

The contractor shall provide and maintain an inspection system acceptable to the Government covering the material, fabricating methods, work, and services under the contract, and the Government has the right to inspect and test all materials furnished and services performed under the contract, to the extent practicable at all places and times, including the period of performance, and in any event before acceptance [@CL-SUP-1096; @CL-SUP-1097]. Unless otherwise specified in the contract, the Government shall accept or reject services and materials at the place of delivery as promptly as practicable after delivery, and they shall be presumed accepted 60 days after the date of delivery, unless accepted earlier [@CL-SUP-1098]. At any time during contract performance, but not later than 6 months (or such other time as may be specified in the contract) after acceptance of the services or materials last delivered under the contract, the Government may require the contractor to replace or correct services or materials that at time of delivery failed to meet contract requirements [@CL-SUP-1099; @CL-SUP-1100]. Except as otherwise specified in paragraph (h), the cost of replacement or correction shall be determined under the Payments Under Time-and-Materials and Labor-Hour Contracts clause, but the hourly rate for labor hours incurred in the replacement or correction shall be reduced to exclude that portion of the rate attributable to profit [@CL-SUP-1101; @CL-SUP-1102]. If Government inspection and acceptance are to be performed at the contractor's plant, the contracting officer shall use the clause with its Alternate I [@CL-SUP-1135]. Payment mechanics are on [Time-and-materials and labor-hour payments](../../contract-finance/billing-payment/ctf-tm-and-lh-payments.md).

Class Deviation 2026-O0038 directs DoD contracting officers to use the revised FAR part 52 on the Revolutionary FAR Overhaul web page in place of the text codified at 48 CFR chapter 1, effective February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760; @CL-CMP-9762]. Both the codified and revised FAR 52.246-6 require the contractor to provide and maintain an inspection system acceptable to the Government covering the material, fabricating methods, work, and services under the contract [@CL-SUP-1096; @CL-SUP-3258] and give the Government the right to inspect and test all materials furnished and services performed under the contract, to the extent practicable at all places and times, including the period of performance, and in any event before acceptance [@CL-SUP-1097; @CL-SUP-3259]. Both provide that unless otherwise specified in the contract, the Government shall accept or reject services and materials at the place of delivery as promptly as practicable after delivery, and they shall be presumed accepted 60 days after the date of delivery, unless accepted earlier [@CL-SUP-1098; @CL-SUP-3260]. Both provide that at any time during contract performance, but not later than 6 months (or such other time as may be specified in the contract) after acceptance of the services or materials last delivered under the contract, the Government may require the contractor to replace or correct services or materials that at time of delivery failed to meet contract requirements [@CL-SUP-1099; @CL-SUP-3261; @CL-SUP-1100; @CL-SUP-3262], and that except as otherwise specified in paragraph (h), the cost of replacement or correction shall be determined under the Payments Under Time-and-Materials and Labor-Hour Contracts clause, but the hourly rate for labor hours incurred in the replacement or correction shall be reduced to exclude that portion of the rate attributable to profit [@CL-SUP-1101; @CL-SUP-3263; @CL-SUP-1102; @CL-SUP-3264]. Under paragraph (h) of both, notwithstanding paragraphs (f) and (g), the Government may at any time require the contractor to remedy by correction or replacement, without cost to the Government, any failure by the contractor to comply with the requirements of the contract, if the failure is due to fraud, lack of good faith, or willful misconduct on the part of the contractor's managerial personnel, or to the conduct of one or more of the contractor's employees selected or retained by the contractor after any of the contractor's managerial personnel has reasonable grounds to believe that the employee is habitually careless or unqualified [@CL-SUP-9779; @CL-SUP-1103; @CL-SUP-3265; @CL-SUP-1104; @CL-SUP-4170; @CL-SUP-3266; @CL-SUP-9780; @CL-SUP-9272].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A labor-hour contract has an hourly rate of 100, of which 15 is profit. A technician spends 20 hours correcting a defective deliverable, and none of the listed no-cost causes applies. The correction labor is billed at 85 per hour, so the correction labor comes to 1,700, determined under the payments clause. If the defect had come from willful misconduct by managerial personnel, the Government could require the correction without cost to it.

## Never confuse

Unless otherwise specified in the contract, the Government shall accept or reject services and materials at the place of delivery as promptly as practicable after delivery, and they shall be presumed accepted 60 days after the date of delivery, unless accepted earlier [@CL-SUP-1098]. At any time during contract performance, but not later than 6 months (or such other time as may be specified in the contract) after acceptance of the services or materials last delivered under the contract, the Government may require the contractor to replace or correct services or materials that at time of delivery failed to meet contract requirements [@CL-SUP-1099; @CL-SUP-1100]. This clause points to the payments clause for the cost of correction, and the payments clause itself is on [Time-and-materials and labor-hour payments](../../contract-finance/billing-payment/ctf-tm-and-lh-payments.md). The general policy on rejecting nonconforming supplies and services is on [Rejection of nonconforming supplies](../../operating-model/corrective-action/opm-rejection-of-nonconforming-supplies.md). The services-only inspection clauses are on [Inspection of services, fixed-price](sup-inspection-of-services-fixed-price.md) and [Inspection of services, cost-reimbursement](sup-inspection-of-services-cost-reimbursement.md).
