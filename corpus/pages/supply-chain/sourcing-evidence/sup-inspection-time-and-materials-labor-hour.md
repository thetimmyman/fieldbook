## Purpose

Shows how defective supplies and services are corrected under the inspection clause for time-and-materials and labor-hour contracts, what the Government pays for, and when acceptance is presumed.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-SUP-1096] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.246-6 [@CL-SUP-1096] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.246-6 [@CL-SUP-3258] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-1096] |
| Applies to | Contracts that include the Inspection—Time-and-Material and Labor-Hour clause [@CL-SUP-1096]; the clause is directed for solicitations and contracts when a time-and-material contract or a labor-hour contract is contemplated [@CL-SUP-1134] |

The contractor must provide and maintain an inspection system acceptable to the Government that covers the material, fabricating methods, work and services under the contract [@CL-SUP-1096]. The Government has the right to inspect and test all materials furnished and services performed under the contract, to the extent practicable, at all places and times, including the period of performance, and in any event before acceptance [@CL-SUP-1097].

Unless the contract specifies otherwise, the Government shall accept or reject services and materials at the place of delivery as promptly as practicable after delivery, and they are presumed accepted 60 days after the date of delivery unless accepted earlier [@CL-SUP-1098].

The clause sets the period for replacement or correction as at any time during contract performance but not later than six months after acceptance of the services or materials last delivered, or such other time as the contract specifies [@CL-SUP-1099]. Within that period, the Government may require the contractor to replace or correct services or materials that failed to meet contract requirements at the time of delivery [@CL-SUP-1100]. Except as the listed no-cost cases provide, the cost of replacement or correction is determined under the Payments Under Time-and-Materials and Labor-Hour Contracts clause [@CL-SUP-1101]. Where that cost rule applies, the hourly rate for labor hours incurred in replacement or correction is reduced to exclude the portion of the rate attributable to profit [@CL-SUP-1102].

The Government may at any time require the contractor to remedy by correction or replacement, without cost to the Government, any failure to comply with contract requirements if the failure is due to a listed cause [@CL-SUP-1103]. One listed cause is fraud, lack of good faith or willful misconduct on the part of the contractor's managerial personnel [@CL-SUP-1104].

## How it works

Inspection comes first, under the contractor's system and the Government's right to inspect and test [@CL-SUP-1096; @CL-SUP-1097]. Acceptance is presumed 60 days after delivery unless it happens earlier or the contract says otherwise [@CL-SUP-1098]. If services or materials failed to meet requirements at delivery, the Government may require replacement or correction within the stated period [@CL-SUP-1099; @CL-SUP-1100]. Except in the listed no-cost cases, the cost of that work is determined under the payments clause, and where that cost rule applies the labor hours in the correction carry a rate that excludes profit [@CL-SUP-1101; @CL-SUP-1102]. Where Government inspection and acceptance are to be performed at the contractor's plant, the contracting officer uses the clause with its Alternate I; this card covers the basic clause and does not restate the text of Alternate I [@CL-SUP-1135]. Payment mechanics are on [Time-and-materials and labor-hour payments](../../contract-finance/billing-payment/ctf-tm-and-lh-payments.md).

Under the RFO text, the DoD class deviation directs DoD contracting officers to use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text, effective February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. The RFO clause keeps the contractor's duty to provide and maintain an inspection system acceptable to the Government [@CL-SUP-1096] [@CL-SUP-3258] and the Government's right to inspect and test all materials and services before acceptance [@CL-SUP-1097] [@CL-SUP-3259]. It keeps acceptance or rejection as promptly as practicable after delivery, with presumed acceptance 60 days after delivery unless accepted earlier or the contract specifies otherwise [@CL-SUP-1098] [@CL-SUP-3260]. It keeps the correction period [@CL-SUP-1099] [@CL-SUP-3261] and the right to require replacement or correction of services or materials that failed to meet contract requirements at delivery [@CL-SUP-1100] [@CL-SUP-3262]. It keeps the rule that the hourly rate for labor hours in replacement or correction is reduced to exclude profit [@CL-SUP-1102] [@CL-SUP-3264]. It keeps the first listed cause for no-cost remedy, fraud, lack of good faith or willful misconduct by managerial personnel [@CL-SUP-1104] [@CL-SUP-3266]. The RFO text also lists, as the second cause, the conduct of employees the contractor selected or retained after managerial personnel had reasonable grounds to believe they were habitually careless or unqualified [@CL-SUP-9272]. The RFO text determines the cost of replacement or correction under the Payments Under Time-and-Materials and Labor-Hour Contracts clause, except as specified in paragraph (h) of this clause [@CL-SUP-3263], and lets the Government require correction or replacement without cost to it notwithstanding paragraphs (f) and (g) of this clause where a listed cause applies [@CL-SUP-3265]. The substance of both is the same as in the codified text [@CL-SUP-1101] [@CL-SUP-1103], and the cross-reference wording differs.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A labor-hour contract has an hourly rate of 100, of which 15 is profit. A technician spends 20 hours correcting a defective deliverable, and none of the listed no-cost causes applies. The correction labor is billed at 85 per hour, so the correction labor comes to 1,700, determined under the payments clause. If the defect had come from willful misconduct by managerial personnel, the Government could require the correction without cost to it.

## Never confuse

The presumed acceptance date is a default that applies unless the contract specifies otherwise [@CL-SUP-1098]. It is not the same as the correction window, which is measured from acceptance of the services or materials last delivered [@CL-SUP-1099]. This clause points to the payments clause for the cost of correction, and the payments clause itself is on [Time-and-materials and labor-hour payments](../../contract-finance/billing-payment/ctf-tm-and-lh-payments.md). The general policy on rejecting nonconforming supplies and services is on [Rejection of nonconforming supplies](../../operating-model/corrective-action/opm-rejection-of-nonconforming-supplies.md). The services-only inspection clauses are on [Inspection of services, fixed-price](sup-inspection-of-services-fixed-price.md) and [Inspection of services, cost-reimbursement](sup-inspection-of-services-cost-reimbursement.md).
