## Purpose

Shows when an agency requirement may name one manufacturer's product or feature, what an agency may or must require offerors to identify about used, reconditioned or remanufactured material, and when an agency may require virgin material.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-SUP-3286] |
| Codified citation | FAR 11.001, 11.105, 11.301 and 11.302 [@CL-SUP-4155; @CL-SUP-4156; @CL-SUP-3286; @CL-SUP-3290; @CL-SUP-4154]; FAR 2.101 [@CL-SUP-9617] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 2.101 [@CL-SUP-9618] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-3286] |
| Applies to | Agency requirements peculiar to one manufacturer, and agency requests for information on used, reconditioned or remanufactured material, and agency requirements for virgin material [@CL-SUP-3286; @CL-SUP-3290] |

Agency requirements may not be written to require a particular brand name, product or product feature peculiar to one manufacturer, which would preclude consideration of a product made by another company, unless the conditions that follow in the section are met [@CL-SUP-3286]. One listed condition is that the brand name, product or feature is essential to the Government's requirements and market research indicates that other companies' similar products, or products lacking the feature, do not meet or cannot be modified to meet the agency's minimum needs [@CL-SUP-3287]. A second listed condition has two alternatives: the authority to contract without providing for full and open competition is supported by the required justifications and approvals [@CL-SUP-3288], or the basis for not providing for maximum practicable competition is documented in the file or justified when the acquisition is awarded using simplified acquisition procedures [@CL-SUP-3336]. A third listed condition is that the documentation or justification is posted for acquisitions over $25,000 [@CL-SUP-3289].

The section refers multiple award schedule orders to FAR 8.405-6 and orders under indefinite-quantity contracts to FAR 16.505(a)(4) [@CL-SUP-3337].

Agencies must not require virgin material, or supplies composed of or manufactured using virgin material, unless compelled by law or regulation or unless virgin material is vital for safety or for meeting the contract's performance requirements [@CL-SUP-3290]. The FAR definitions section defines virgin material as previously unused raw material, including previously unused copper, aluminum, lead, zinc, iron, other metal or metal ore, or any undeveloped resource that is, or with new technology will become, a source of raw materials [@CL-SUP-9617]. The RFO text of FAR 2.101 has the same definition word for word [@CL-SUP-9618; @CL-SUP-9617].

When acquiring products other than commercial products, agencies must require offerors to identify used, reconditioned or remanufactured supplies, or unused former Government surplus property, proposed for use under the contract [@CL-SUP-3291]. These supplies and that property may not be used in contract performance unless authorized by the contracting officer [@CL-SUP-3338].

As used in FAR part 11, reconditioned means restored to the original normal operating condition by readjustments and material replacement [@CL-SUP-4155], and remanufactured means factory rebuilt to original specifications [@CL-SUP-4156]. The Material Requirements clause is to be inserted in solicitations and contracts for supplies that are not commercial products; the text names no official who inserts it [@CL-SUP-4154].

When acquiring commercial products, the contracting officer must consider the customary practices in the industry for the product being acquired [@CL-SUP-3339]. The contracting officer may require offerors to provide information on used, reconditioned or remanufactured supplies, or unused former Government surplus property, proposed for use under the contract [@CL-SUP-3340]. The request must be included in the solicitation and, to the maximum extent practicable, limited to information or standards consistent with normal commercial practices [@CL-SUP-3292].

When the contracting officer needs additional information to determine whether supplies meet minimum recovered material or biobased standards stated in the solicitation, the contracting officer may require offerors to submit additional information on the recycled or biobased content or related standards [@CL-SUP-3293]. That request must be included in the solicitation, and when acquiring commercial products the information is to be limited to the maximum extent practicable to that available under normal commercial practices [@CL-SUP-3341]. For biobased products, agencies may not require, as a condition of purchase, that the vendor or manufacturer provide more data than would typically be provided by other business entities offering products for sale to the agency, other than data confirming the biobased content [@CL-SUP-3342].

## How it works

The brand-name bar is a rule about how the requirement is written, and the listed conditions rest on evidence in the file: market research, the justification or file documentation, and posting [@CL-SUP-3286; @CL-SUP-3287; @CL-SUP-3288; @CL-SUP-3336; @CL-SUP-3289].

For material status the solicitation does the work. For products other than commercial products the agency must require the offeror to identify used, reconditioned or remanufactured supplies and unused former Government surplus property, and the contracting officer decides whether they may be used [@CL-SUP-3291; @CL-SUP-3338]. For commercial products the contracting officer decides whether to ask for that information, and any request must be in the solicitation and kept close to normal commercial practices [@CL-SUP-3340; @CL-SUP-3292].

The detailed clause and provision wording sits in [Material requirements and brand name or equal](../sourcing-evidence/sup-material-requirements-and-brand-name-or-equal.md), and this card states only the policy that drives it. The order of precedence for requirements documents is on [Requirements documents, standardization and commercial market acceptance](../sourcing-evidence/sup-requirements-documents-and-market-acceptance.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A buy of 500 pumps, awarded using simplified acquisition procedures and valued at 60,000, needs a seal that only one maker produces. The file records that the seal is essential and that market research shows other makers' seals do not meet, or cannot be modified to meet, the minimum need [@CL-SUP-3287]. The basis for not providing for maximum practicable competition is documented in the file [@CL-SUP-3336]. Because the buy is above 25,000, the documentation is posted [@CL-SUP-3289]. For the pumps themselves, which are not commercial products, the solicitation requires offerors to identify reconditioned units [@CL-SUP-3291]. The contracting officer authorizes none, so no reconditioned unit may be used in performance [@CL-SUP-3338].

## Never confuse

Virgin material is not the same as used, reconditioned or remanufactured material: the first is a limit on what an agency may require, while for the others, when acquiring products other than commercial products, offerors identify what they propose and its use in performance needs the contracting officer's authorization [@CL-SUP-3290; @CL-SUP-3291; @CL-SUP-3338]. A brand-name requirement is also not a brand name or equal provision: the requirement names a product or feature that must meet the conditions above [@CL-SUP-3286], while an offer of an equal product under the provision must, as one listed requirement, meet the salient physical, functional, or performance characteristic specified in the solicitation [@CL-SUP-1197].
