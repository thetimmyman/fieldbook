## Purpose

Shows the inspection system duty, the Government's right to inspect and test, the duty to correct or remove rejected work, and when acceptance of construction is final.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-SUP-1114] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.246-12 [@CL-SUP-1114] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-1114] |
| Applies to | Contracts that include the Inspection of Construction clause [@CL-SUP-1114]; the clause is directed for solicitations and contracts for construction when a fixed-price contract is contemplated and the contract amount is expected to exceed the simplified acquisition threshold [@CL-SUP-1141] |

The contractor shall maintain an adequate inspection system and perform such inspections as will ensure that the work performed under the contract conforms to contract requirements [@CL-SUP-1114]. All work is conducted under the general direction of the Contracting Officer and is subject to Government inspection and test at all places and at all reasonable times before acceptance to ensure strict compliance with the terms of the contract [@CL-SUP-1115].

The contractor shall, without charge, replace or correct work found by the Government not to conform to contract requirements, unless in the public interest the Government consents to accept the work with an appropriate adjustment in contract price [@CL-SUP-1117]. The contractor shall also promptly segregate and remove rejected material from the premises [@CL-SUP-1118]. If the contractor does not promptly replace or correct rejected work, the Government may replace or correct it by contract or otherwise and charge the cost to the contractor, or terminate for default the contractor's right to proceed [@CL-SUP-1119].

Acceptance is final and conclusive except for latent defects, fraud, gross mistakes amounting to fraud, or the Government's rights under any warranty or guarantee [@CL-SUP-1120].

## How it works

The contractor inspects its own work, and the Government inspects and tests it before acceptance [@CL-SUP-1114; @CL-SUP-1115]. Rejected work is replaced or corrected without charge, unless the Government consents to accept it with a price adjustment in the public interest [@CL-SUP-1117]. Rejected material is segregated and removed from the premises [@CL-SUP-1118]. If the contractor does not act promptly, the Government may do the work and charge the cost, or terminate for default [@CL-SUP-1119]. Once the work is accepted, acceptance is final subject to the listed exceptions [@CL-SUP-1120].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A construction contract is worth 500,000. The Government rejects a concrete pour that does not meet the specification, which is 20,000 of the work. The contractor must replace it without charge and promptly remove the rejected material from the premises. If the contractor does nothing, the Government may have the pour redone by another party and charge the 20,000 cost to the contractor. After acceptance, acceptance is final and conclusive except for the listed exceptions.

## Never confuse

Government inspection and test are for the sole benefit of the Government, and they do not relieve the contractor of quality control responsibility or of responsibility for damage or loss before acceptance [@CL-SUP-1116]. They also do not constitute or imply acceptance [@CL-SUP-1142]. Acceptance is a separate step, and it is final and conclusive only subject to the listed exceptions [@CL-SUP-1120]. Contractor liability after acceptance under the limitation of liability clauses is on [Contractor liability after acceptance](sup-contractor-liability-after-acceptance.md), and the general inspection policy is on [Government contract quality assurance](sup-government-contract-quality-assurance.md).
