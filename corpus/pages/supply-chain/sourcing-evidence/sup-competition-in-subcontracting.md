## Purpose

Shows the contractor's duty to select subcontractors and suppliers competitively and the exception for approved mentors and their protégés.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-SUP-0556] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.244-5 [@CL-SUP-0556] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-0556] |
| Applies to | Contracts with the Competition in Subcontracting clause, inserted as prescribed in FAR 44.204(c) [@CL-SUP-0688] |

The contractor shall select subcontractors (including suppliers) on a competitive basis to the maximum practical extent consistent with the objectives and requirements of the contract [@CL-SUP-0556].

If the contractor is an approved mentor under the DoD Mentor-Protégé Program, the contractor may award subcontracts under the contract on a noncompetitive basis to its protégés [@CL-SUP-0557].

## How it works

The clause sets a competition duty for selecting subcontractors and suppliers, qualified by the maximum practical extent consistent with the objectives and requirements of the contract [@CL-SUP-0556]. The mentor exception covers subcontracts awarded to the mentor's protégés by a contractor that is an approved mentor under the DoD Mentor-Protégé Program [@CL-SUP-0557]. When the contracting officer evaluates a request for consent, one question is whether adequate price competition was obtained or its absence properly justified [@CL-SUP-0655]. A purchasing system review gives special attention to the degree of price competition obtained [@CL-SUP-0661].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor asks three suppliers to quote machined castings and awards to the best-value bidder. For a separate engineering task it awards a subcontract directly to a protégé. The contractor is an approved mentor under the DoD Mentor-Protégé Program.

Answer: the castings award follows the competition duty. The direct award to the protégé is allowed under the mentor exception.

## Never confuse

The mentor exception is not a general exception to the competition duty. It lets an approved mentor award subcontracts noncompetitively to its protégés [@CL-SUP-0557], while the duty to select subcontractors and suppliers competitively to the maximum practical extent otherwise stands [@CL-SUP-0556]. Consent questions are on the [Subcontract consent evaluation](../procure-to-pay/sup-subcontract-consent-evaluation.md) card and review topics on the [Contractor purchasing system review](../procure-to-pay/sup-contractor-purchasing-system-review.md) card.

## Company calibration

Confirm whether purchasing procedures require competitive solicitation and a recorded reason for any award without competition. [@Q-040]
