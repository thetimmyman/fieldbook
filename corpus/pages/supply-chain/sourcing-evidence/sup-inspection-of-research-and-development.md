## Purpose

Shows the Government's inspection rights, the correction duty and how acceptance is made under the research and development inspection clauses for fixed-price and cost-reimbursement contracts.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-SUP-1105; @CL-SUP-1109] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.246-7 and 52.246-8 [@CL-SUP-1105; @CL-SUP-1109] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.246-7 [@CL-SUP-3176] and RFO FAR 52.246-8 [@CL-SUP-3180] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-1105; @CL-SUP-1109] |
| Applies to | Research and development contracts that include the fixed-price or the cost-reimbursement inspection clause [@CL-SUP-1105; @CL-SUP-1109]; the fixed-price clause is prescribed when the primary objective is delivery of end items other than designs, drawings or reports, a fixed-price contract is contemplated and the contract amount is expected to exceed the simplified acquisition threshold [@CL-SUP-1136], subject to an exception for impracticality [@CL-SUP-1137]; the cost-reimbursement clause is prescribed when the primary objective is the same and a cost-reimbursement contract is contemplated [@CL-SUP-1138], subject to the same kind of exception [@CL-SUP-1139] |

Under the fixed-price clause, the Government has the right to inspect and test all work called for by the contract, to the extent practicable, at all places and times, including the period of performance, and in any event before acceptance [@CL-SUP-1105]. Unless the contract specifies otherwise, the Government shall accept or reject the work as promptly as practicable after delivery [@CL-SUP-1106]. If the contractor fails or is unable to correct or replace nonconforming work within the delivery schedule or a later time the Contracting Officer authorizes, the Contracting Officer may accept the work and make an equitable price reduction [@CL-SUP-1107]. Acceptance of the work is conclusive except for latent defects, fraud, gross mistakes amounting to fraud, or as otherwise specified in the contract [@CL-SUP-1108].

Under the cost-reimbursement clause, the Government has the same right to inspect and test all work called for by the contract before acceptance [@CL-SUP-1109]. Unless the contract provides otherwise, the Government shall accept work as promptly as practicable after delivery, and work is deemed accepted 90 days after delivery unless accepted earlier [@CL-SUP-1110]. The clause sets the period for correction or replacement as at any time during contract performance but no later than six months after acceptance of all of the end items other than designs, drawings or reports, or such other time as the contract specifies [@CL-SUP-1111]. Within that period, the Government may require the contractor to replace or correct work not meeting contract requirements [@CL-SUP-1112]. Except as another paragraph of the clause provides otherwise, the cost of replacement or correction is determined as specified in the Allowable Cost and Payment clause, but no additional fee is paid [@CL-SUP-1113]. Paragraph (h) is that other paragraph: notwithstanding paragraphs (f) and (g), the Government may at any time require the contractor to remedy by correction or replacement, without cost to the Government, any failure by the contractor to comply with the requirements of the contract, if the failure is due to fraud, lack of good faith or willful misconduct on the part of the contractor's managerial personnel, or to the conduct of one or more of the contractor's employees selected or retained by the contractor after any of its managerial personnel has reasonable grounds to believe that the employee is habitually careless or unqualified [@CL-SUP-9671; @CL-SUP-9672; @CL-SUP-9673]. If it is contemplated that the contract will be on a no-fee basis, the contracting officer uses the cost-reimbursement clause with its Alternate I, and this card covers the basic clause only [@CL-SUP-1140].

## How it works

Both clauses give the Government the right to inspect and test work before acceptance [@CL-SUP-1105; @CL-SUP-1109]. The fixed-price clause deals with uncorrected nonconforming work by price reduction [@CL-SUP-1107], and makes acceptance conclusive subject to listed exceptions [@CL-SUP-1108]. The cost-reimbursement clause fixes a deemed-acceptance date of 90 days after delivery unless work is accepted earlier or the contract provides otherwise [@CL-SUP-1110]. It then lets the Government require replacement or correction within the stated period [@CL-SUP-1111; @CL-SUP-1112], with no additional fee, subject to its exceptions, including paragraph (h) described above [@CL-SUP-1113; @CL-SUP-9671].

Under the RFO text, the DoD class deviation directs DoD contracting officers to use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text, effective February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. The RFO fixed-price clause keeps the Government's right to inspect and test all work before acceptance [@CL-SUP-1105] [@CL-SUP-3176], the duty to accept or reject the work as promptly as practicable after delivery unless the contract specifies otherwise [@CL-SUP-1106] [@CL-SUP-3177], the equitable price reduction when the contractor fails or is unable to correct or replace nonconforming work in time [@CL-SUP-1107] [@CL-SUP-3178], and conclusive acceptance with the listed exceptions [@CL-SUP-1108] [@CL-SUP-3179]. The RFO cost-reimbursement clause keeps the same right to inspect and test before acceptance [@CL-SUP-1109] [@CL-SUP-3180], the deemed acceptance 90 days after delivery unless accepted earlier or the contract provides otherwise [@CL-SUP-1110] [@CL-SUP-3181], the correction period [@CL-SUP-1111] [@CL-SUP-3182], and the right to require replacement or correction of work not meeting contract requirements [@CL-SUP-1112] [@CL-SUP-3183]. It keeps the rule that time devoted to replacement or correction of such work is not included in computing that period [@CL-SUP-9776] [@CL-SUP-9273]. It states the cost rule, that the cost is determined under the Allowable Cost and Payment clause with no additional fee, except as provided in paragraph (h) of this clause [@CL-SUP-3184]; the substance is the same as in the codified text [@CL-SUP-1113], and the cross-reference wording differs. Under RFO paragraph (h), the same exception applies: notwithstanding paragraphs (f) and (g), the Government may at any time require the contractor to remedy by correction or replacement, without cost to the Government, any failure by the contractor to comply with the requirements of the contract, if the failure is due to fraud, lack of good faith or willful misconduct on the part of the contractor's managerial personnel, or to the conduct of one or more of the contractor's employees selected or retained by the contractor after any of its managerial personnel has reasonable grounds to believe that the employee is habitually careless or unqualified [@CL-SUP-9674; @CL-SUP-9675; @CL-SUP-9676].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A fixed-price research contract is worth 300,000. The contractor delivers a prototype that does not conform and cannot correct it within the delivery schedule. The Contracting Officer accepts the prototype and makes an equitable price reduction of 30,000. Under a cost-reimbursement research contract, the same prototype would be deemed accepted on day 90 after delivery unless accepted earlier, and any correction would carry no additional fee.

## Never confuse

Deemed acceptance after a set number of days is not the same as conclusive acceptance. The fixed-price text makes acceptance conclusive with listed exceptions [@CL-SUP-1108], while the cost-reimbursement text printed in the source states a deemed-acceptance date [@CL-SUP-1110] and has no conclusiveness paragraph. The two clauses also treat nonconforming work differently: the fixed-price clause lets the Contracting Officer accept uncorrected work with an equitable price reduction [@CL-SUP-1107], and the cost-reimbursement clause says the cost of replacement or correction is determined under the Allowable Cost and Payment clause with no additional fee [@CL-SUP-1113]. The supplies clause on [Inspection of supplies, cost-reimbursement](sup-inspection-of-supplies-cost-reimbursement.md) uses similar no-additional-fee wording [@CL-SUP-1080]. That clause makes an exception in its paragraph (h): the Government may at any time require the contractor to correct or replace nonconforming supplies, without cost to the Government, if the nonconformances are due to fraud, lack of good faith or willful misconduct on the part of the contractor's managerial personnel, or to the conduct of one or more of the contractor's employees selected or retained by the contractor after any of its managerial personnel has reasonable grounds to believe that the employee is habitually careless or unqualified [@CL-SUP-1081; @CL-SUP-1131]. The general policy on rejecting nonconforming supplies is on [Rejection of nonconforming supplies](../../operating-model/corrective-action/opm-rejection-of-nonconforming-supplies.md), and inspection places are on [Government contract quality assurance](sup-government-contract-quality-assurance.md).
