## Purpose

Shows what the fixed-price indemnification clause covers, the conditions and exclusions, the contractor's notice duty, and one difference that the cost-reimbursement clause adds.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-SUP-1417] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 252.235-7000 and 252.235-7001 [@CL-SUP-1417; @CL-SUP-1424] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-1417] |
| Applies to | Contractors that meet all the terms and conditions of the clause [@CL-SUP-1417] |

The fixed-price clause provides indemnification under section 3861 of title 10 of the United States Code if the contractor meets all the terms and conditions of the clause [@CL-SUP-1417]. One covered category is claims by third persons, including the contractor's employees, for death, bodily injury, sickness, disease, or loss, damage or lost use of property, with reasonable litigation or settlement expenses [@CL-SUP-1418]. Two more are loss, damage and lost use of the contractor's property, excluding lost profit, and of the Government's property [@CL-SUP-1419].

## How it works

The claim, loss or damage must arise from direct performance, must not be compensated by insurance or other means or fall within deductibles, and must result from an unusually hazardous risk the contract specifically defines [@CL-SUP-1420]. Other listed conditions include no willful misconduct or lack of good faith by the contractor's directors, officers, managers, superintendents or other equivalent representatives who have supervision or direction of all or substantially all of the business, of all or substantially all of the operations at any one plant or separate location where the contract is being performed, or of a separate and complete major industrial operation connected with performing the contract [@CL-SUP-1421; @CL-SUP-9765; @CL-SUP-9766], and no liability assumed under a contract or agreement, except for covered subcontracts, unless the contracting officer (or, in contracts with the Department of the Navy, the Department) specifically approved the assumption of liability [@CL-SUP-1422]. The contractor shall promptly notify the contracting officer of any occurrence, action or claim that might trigger the Government's liability [@CL-SUP-1423]. The cost-reimbursement clause adds that the Limitation of Cost clause does not apply to the Government's obligations, and that those obligations are excepted from the release required by the Allowable Cost, Fee, and Payment clause [@CL-SUP-1424]. This card claims only the points listed; the clause holds further conditions and duties on certification, insurance, settlement and subcontract flowdown.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor on a fixed-price contract faces a third-party injury claim from a risk the contract defines as unusually hazardous. Insurance pays 200,000 of 500,000 of the loss. The part that insurance compensates is outside the indemnity, so only the uninsured 300,000 can qualify, and only if every listed condition is met and the claim was noticed promptly.

## Never confuse

The indemnity depends on the contractor meeting all the terms and conditions of the clause [@CL-SUP-1417], and a claim, loss or damage that is compensated by insurance or other means, or is within the deductible amounts of the contractor's insurance, does not meet the clause's conditions [@CL-SUP-1420]. The cost-reimbursement clause sets aside the Limitation of Cost clause for these obligations [@CL-SUP-1424]; see [Limitation of Cost notice](../../contract-finance/funding/ctf-limitation-of-cost.md), [Liability and insurance for vessel repair](sup-dfars-ship-repair-liability-and-insurance.md), [Ground and flight risk](sup-dfars-ground-and-flight-risk.md) and [Insurance and indemnification](../../compliance/cas-allowability/cmp-insurance-and-indemnification.md).

The FAR clause on Government indemnification against unusually hazardous or nuclear risks is covered in [Indemnification against unusually hazardous or nuclear risks](../../compliance/regulatory-framework/cmp-pub-law-85-804-indemnification.md).

## Company calibration

Whether the contract defines any unusually hazardous risk, which of the two clauses it carries, and what insurance the company holds against that risk. [@Q-004]
