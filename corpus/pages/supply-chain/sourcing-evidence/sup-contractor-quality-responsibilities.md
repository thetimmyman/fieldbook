## Purpose

Shows what a contractor is responsible for under a contract's quality requirements, and which records a supplier-quality or finance team can tie to those duties.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-SUP-0850] |
| Codified citation | FAR 46.105 [@CL-SUP-0850; @CL-SUP-0851; @CL-SUP-0852; @CL-SUP-0853; @CL-SUP-0854; @CL-SUP-0953] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-0850] |
| Applies to | The contractor's obligations under the contract [@CL-SUP-0850] |

The contractor is responsible for carrying out its obligations under the contract by controlling the quality of supplies or services and by tendering to the Government for acceptance only supplies or services that conform to contract requirements [@CL-SUP-0850].

Under the same lead-in, the contractor is also responsible for ensuring that vendors or suppliers of raw materials, parts, components, subassemblies and similar items have an acceptable quality control system [@CL-SUP-0850; @CL-SUP-0851], and for maintaining substantiating evidence, when required by the contract, that the supplies or services conform to contract quality requirements, and furnishing such information to the Government as required [@CL-SUP-0850; @CL-SUP-4003; @CL-SUP-0852].

## How it works

The contractor may be required to provide and maintain an inspection system or program for the control of quality that is acceptable to the Government [@CL-SUP-0853]. The contractor is responsible for performing all inspections and tests required by the contract except those specifically reserved for performance by the Government [@CL-SUP-0854]. The duty to maintain substantiating evidence that supplies or services conform to contract quality requirements applies when required by the contract, and the contractor furnishes such information to the Government as required [@CL-SUP-0852]. The control of quality may relate to, but is not limited to, listed areas such as manufacturing processes [@CL-SUP-0953]. Another duty is ensuring that vendors or suppliers have an acceptable quality control system [@CL-SUP-0851].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contract requires the contractor to keep test results showing that each shipment conforms. The contractor ships 3 lots over a year and keeps the results for each lot. The Government asks for the results for lot 2. The answer: the contract required the evidence, so the contractor furnishes it as required. For a contract with no such requirement, this duty to keep evidence does not apply, although the other duties still do.

## Never confuse

The contractor is responsible for controlling the quality of supplies or services [@CL-SUP-0850] and for performing all inspections and tests required by the contract except those specifically reserved for performance by the Government [@CL-SUP-0854]. Although contracts generally make contractors responsible for performing inspection before tendering supplies, some contracts provide for specialized inspections to be performed solely by the Government [@CL-SUP-0974]. The Government side is on [Government contract quality assurance](sup-government-contract-quality-assurance.md), the level of requirements the contract sets is on [Contract quality requirements](sup-contract-quality-requirements.md), and assurance at a subcontractor is on [Quality assurance on subcontracts](sup-subcontract-quality-assurance.md). The effect of Government inspection on the contractor's responsibility for defects is on [Inspection of Supplies—Fixed-Price clause](../../close-revrec/revenue/cls-inspection-of-supplies-fixed-price.md), and the handling of nonconforming supplies is on [Rejection of nonconforming supplies](../../operating-model/corrective-action/opm-rejection-of-nonconforming-supplies.md).
