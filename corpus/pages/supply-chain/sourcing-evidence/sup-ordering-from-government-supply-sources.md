## Purpose

Shows what authorization a contractor needs to order from Government supply sources, how it orders and pays, what happens if it pays late, and what it may and may not do.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-SUP-1651] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 252.251-7000 [@CL-SUP-1651] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | AUG 2012 [@CL-SUP-9875] |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-1651] |
| Applies to | Contracts that use the clause as prescribed in DFARS 251.107 [@CL-SUP-1651], for orders under Federal Supply Schedules, Personal Property Rehabilitation Price Schedules, or Enterprise Software Agreements [@CL-SUP-1567], orders under GSA Office of Information Resources Management nonmandatory schedule contracts and requirements contracts [@CL-SUP-1659], and orders for Government stock [@CL-SUP-1570; @CL-SUP-1660] |

When placing orders under Federal Supply Schedules, Personal Property Rehabilitation Price Schedules, or Enterprise Software Agreements, the contractor shall follow the terms of the applicable schedule or agreement and authorization [@CL-SUP-1567]. The contractor shall include in each order: (1) a copy of the authorization (unless a copy was previously furnished to the Federal Supply Schedule, Personal Property Rehabilitation Price Schedule, or Enterprise Software Agreement contractor) [@CL-SUP-1568]; (2) the statement that any price reductions negotiated as part of an Enterprise Software Agreement issued under a Federal Supply Schedule contract shall control, and that in the event of any other inconsistencies between an Enterprise Software Agreement, established as a Federal Supply Schedule blanket purchase agreement, and the Federal Supply Schedule contract, the latter shall govern [@CL-SUP-9785; @CL-SUP-1569]; and (3) the completed address(es) to which the contractor's mail, freight, and billing documents are to be directed [@CL-SUP-4174].

When placing orders under nonmandatory schedule contracts and requirements contracts, issued by the General Services Administration (GSA) Office of Information Resources Management, for automated data processing equipment, software and maintenance, communications equipment and supplies, and teleprocessing services, the contractor shall follow the terms of the applicable contract and the procedures in paragraph (a) of the clause [@CL-SUP-1659; @CL-SUP-4175; @CL-SUP-1664].

When placing orders for Government stock on a reimbursable basis, the contractor shall: (1) comply with the requirements of the Contracting Officer's authorization, using FEDSTRIP or MILSTRIP procedures, as appropriate [@CL-SUP-1570]; (2) use only the GSA Form 1948-A, Retail Services Shopping Plate, when ordering from GSA Self-Service Stores [@CL-SUP-1570; @CL-SUP-1661]; (3) order only those items required in the performance of Government contracts [@CL-SUP-1570; @CL-SUP-1571]; and (4) pay invoices from Government supply sources promptly, which for purchases made from DoD supply sources means within 30 days of the date of a proper invoice [@CL-SUP-1570; @CL-SUP-1572]. The contractor shall annotate each invoice with the date of receipt [@CL-SUP-1662]. For purposes of computing interest for late contractor payments, the Government's invoice is deemed to be a demand for payment in accordance with the Interest clause of the contract [@CL-SUP-1663].

The contractor's failure to pay may also result in the DoD supply source refusing to honor the requisition (see DFARS 251.102(f)) or in the Contracting Officer terminating the contractor's authorization to use DoD supply sources [@CL-SUP-1573]. In the event the Contracting Officer decides to terminate the authorization due to the contractor's failure to pay in a timely manner, the Contracting Officer shall provide the contractor with prompt written notice of the intent to terminate the authorization and the basis for such action [@CL-SUP-1574]. The contractor shall have 10 days after receipt of the Government's notice in which to provide additional information as to why the authorization should not be terminated [@CL-SUP-1575]. The termination shall not provide the contractor with an excusable delay for failure to perform or complete the contract in accordance with the terms of the contract, and the contractor shall be solely responsible for any increased costs [@CL-SUP-1576].

When placing orders for Government stock on a non-reimbursable basis, the contractor shall comply with the requirements of the Contracting Officer's authorization [@CL-SUP-1660].

Only the contractor may request authorization for subcontractor use of Government supply sources, and the Contracting Officer will not grant authorizations for subcontractor use without approval of the contractor [@CL-SUP-1577].

## How it works

Under paragraph (a), when placing orders under Federal Supply Schedules, Personal Property Rehabilitation Price Schedules, or Enterprise Software Agreements, the contractor shall follow the terms of the applicable schedule or agreement and authorization, and include in each order a copy of the authorization (unless previously furnished to the schedule or agreement contractor), the required statement on price reductions and other inconsistencies, and the completed address(es) to which the contractor's mail, freight, and billing documents are to be directed [@CL-SUP-1567; @CL-SUP-1568; @CL-SUP-9785; @CL-SUP-1569; @CL-SUP-4174]. When placing orders for Government stock on a reimbursable basis, the contractor shall comply with the requirements of the Contracting Officer's authorization using FEDSTRIP or MILSTRIP procedures as appropriate, use only the GSA Form 1948-A when ordering from GSA Self-Service Stores, order only those items required in the performance of Government contracts, and pay invoices from Government supply sources promptly (within 30 days of the date of a proper invoice for purchases made from DoD supply sources) [@CL-SUP-1570; @CL-SUP-1661; @CL-SUP-1571; @CL-SUP-1572]. In the event the Contracting Officer decides to terminate the authorization due to the contractor's failure to pay in a timely manner, the Contracting Officer shall provide prompt written notice of the intent to terminate the authorization and the basis for such action, and the contractor shall have 10 days after receipt of the Government's notice in which to provide additional information as to why the authorization should not be terminated [@CL-SUP-1574; @CL-SUP-1575].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor orders stock on a reimbursable basis from a DoD supply source and receives a proper invoice. It must pay within 30 days of the date of the proper invoice. It pays late, and the Contracting Officer decides to terminate the authorization due to the failure to pay in a timely manner. The Contracting Officer sends prompt written notice of the intent and basis, the contractor has 10 days after receipt of the notice to provide additional information as to why the authorization should not be terminated, and if the authorization is terminated, the termination gives the contractor no excusable delay and the contractor is solely responsible for any increased costs.

## Never confuse

When placing orders for Government stock on a reimbursable basis, the contractor shall pay invoices from Government supply sources promptly—which for purchases made from DoD supply sources means within 30 days of the date of a proper invoice [@CL-SUP-1570; @CL-SUP-1572]—unlike orders placed under Federal Supply Schedules, Personal Property Rehabilitation Price Schedules, or Enterprise Software Agreements, which follow the terms of the applicable schedule or agreement and authorization [@CL-SUP-1567]. See [Prompt payment for invoices](../../contract-finance/billing-payment/ctf-prompt-payment.md) and [Interagency Fleet Management System vehicles and related services](sup-ifms-vehicles-and-related-services.md).
