## Purpose

Shows what a potential offeror is entitled to when an agency sets a qualification requirement, who pays for qualification testing, and how a missing listing affects an offer.

## The rule

|---|---|

| Codified citation | FAR 9.200, FAR 9.202, FAR 9.203, FAR 9.204 and FAR 9.205 [@CL-SUP-4150; @CL-SUP-0933; @CL-SUP-0997; @CL-SUP-0934; @CL-SUP-0938] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-0933] |

The subpart implements section 3243 of title 10 and section 3311 of title 41 of the United States Code and prescribes policies and procedures regarding qualification requirements and the acquisitions that are subject to such requirements [@CL-SUP-4150].

Upon request to the contracting activity, potential offerors shall be provided all requirements to become qualified and, at their expense but subject to a cross-referenced rule for small businesses, a prompt opportunity to demonstrate their abilities [@CL-SUP-0933].

If active competition on anticipated future qualification requirements is likely to be fewer than two manufacturers or the products of two manufacturers, the activity responsible for establishing the qualification requirements must take the steps listed next [@CL-SUP-0993]. One listed step is to bear the cost of conducting the specified testing and evaluation, excluding the costs of producing the item or establishing the system to be tested, for a small business concern [@CL-SUP-0934]. That cost duty applies to a small business concern or a product manufactured by a small business concern which has met the standards specified for qualification and which could reasonably be expected to compete for a contract for that requirement [@CL-SUP-0994]. Those costs may be borne by the agency only if it is determined in accordance with agency procedures that additional qualified sources or products are likely to result in cost savings from increased competition [@CL-SUP-0935]. The cost savings must be for future requirements and sufficient to amortize the costs incurred by the agency within a reasonable period of time, considering the duration and dollar value of anticipated future requirements [@CL-SUP-0995]. Further conditions in the same paragraph are not set out on this card.

## How it works

The protection for offers applies if a potential offeror can demonstrate to the satisfaction of the contracting officer that it or its product meets the standards established for qualification, or can meet them before the date specified for award of the contract [@CL-SUP-0936]. In that case a potential offeror may not be denied the opportunity to submit and have considered an offer solely because it is not on a QPL, QML or QBL maintained by the Department of Defense or the National Aeronautics and Space Administration, which is one listed basis [@CL-SUP-0937; @CL-SUP-0936]. The contracting officer need not delay a proposed award in order to provide a potential offeror with an opportunity to demonstrate its ability to meet the standards specified for qualification [@CL-SUP-0996].

Before establishing any qualification requirement, the responsible agency activity must furnish notice through the Governmentwide point of entry [@CL-SUP-0938]. Generally, qualification is performed in advance and independently of any specific acquisition action [@CL-SUP-0997]. On who pays and when, the potential offeror generally pays for its own demonstration, the small business rule above being the cross-referenced exception, and the opportunity is to be prompt once requested [@CL-SUP-0933; @CL-SUP-0934]. The printed sections also cover listing, withdrawing and reexamining qualified products and manufacturers, which this card does not summarise.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A potential offeror that is not a small business is not on the qualified list. It asks to demonstrate its product, and the testing would cost 40,000.

Answer: the offeror pays the 40,000 for the demonstration. If it can show before the award date that its product meets the standards, it may not be denied the chance to submit an offer solely because it is not listed, although the contracting officer need not delay the award to let it demonstrate. Had it been a small business concern, the agency could have borne the testing cost only if the conditions in the rule were met, including the determination that cost savings from increased competition would be sufficient.

## Never confuse

A qualification requirement is not a responsibility finding. Responsibility standards are on the [Contractor responsibility standards](../../mna/screening/mna-contractor-responsibility-standards.md) card. Where the offeror demonstrates to the satisfaction of the contracting officer that it or its product meets the standards, or can meet them before award, absence from a QPL, QML or QBL maintained by the Department of Defense or the National Aeronautics and Space Administration is not by itself a ground to deny it the opportunity to submit and have considered an offer [@CL-SUP-0937; @CL-SUP-0936].
