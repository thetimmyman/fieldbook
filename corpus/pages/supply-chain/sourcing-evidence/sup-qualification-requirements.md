## Purpose

Shows what a potential offeror is entitled to when an agency sets a qualification requirement, who pays for qualification testing, and how a missing listing affects an offer.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-SUP-0933] |
| Codified citation | FAR 9.200, FAR 9.202, FAR 9.203, FAR 9.204 and FAR 9.205 [@CL-SUP-4150; @CL-SUP-0933; @CL-SUP-0997; @CL-SUP-0934; @CL-SUP-0938]; FAR 2.101 [@CL-SUP-9566] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 2.101 [@CL-SUP-9604] |
| Edition date | Not established. |

| Applies to | Qualification requirements and the acquisitions that are subject to such requirements [@CL-SUP-4150] |

FAR subpart 9.2 implements section 3243 of title 10 and section 3311 of title 41 of the United States Code and prescribes policies and procedures regarding qualification requirements and the acquisitions that are subject to such requirements [@CL-SUP-4150]. FAR 2.101 defines a qualification requirement as a Government requirement for testing or other quality assurance demonstration that must be completed before award of a contract [@CL-SUP-9566]. RFO FAR 2.101 defines a qualification requirement as a Government requirement for testing or other quality assurance demonstration that must be completed before award of a contract [@CL-SUP-9604].

Upon request to the contracting activity, potential offerors shall be provided: (i) all requirements that they or their products must satisfy to become qualified, and (ii) at their expense (but see FAR 9.204(a)(2) with regard to small businesses), a prompt opportunity to demonstrate their abilities to meet the standards specified for qualification using qualified personnel and facilities of the agency concerned, or of another agency obtained through interagency agreements, or under contract, or other methods approved by the agency (including use of approved testing and evaluation services not provided under contract to the agency) [@CL-SUP-0933; @CL-SUP-4176; @CL-SUP-4177].

If active competition on anticipated future qualification requirements is likely to be fewer than two manufacturers or the products of two manufacturers, the activity responsible for establishment of the qualification requirements must, among other duties, bear the cost of conducting the specified testing and evaluation (excluding the costs associated with producing the item or establishing the production, quality control, or other system to be tested and evaluated) for a small business concern or a product manufactured by a small business concern which has met the standards specified for qualification and which could reasonably be expected to compete for a contract for that requirement [@CL-SUP-0993; @CL-SUP-0934; @CL-SUP-0994]. However, such costs may be borne only if it is determined in accordance with agency procedures that such additional qualified sources or products are likely to result in cost savings from increased competition for future requirements sufficient to amortize the costs incurred by the agency within a reasonable period of time, considering the duration and dollar value of anticipated future requirements [@CL-SUP-0935; @CL-SUP-0995].

## How it works

If a potential offeror can demonstrate to the satisfaction of the contracting officer that the potential offeror (or its product) meets the standards established for qualification or can meet them before the date specified for award of the contract, a potential offeror may not be denied the opportunity to submit and have considered an offer for a contract solely because the potential offeror: (1) is not on a QPL, QML, or QBL maintained by the Department of Defense (DoD) or the National Aeronautics and Space Administration (NASA), (2) has not been identified as meeting a qualification requirement established after October 19, 1984, by DoD or NASA, or (3) has not been identified as meeting a qualification requirement established by a civilian agency (not including NASA) [@CL-SUP-0936; @CL-SUP-0937; @CL-SUP-4178]. The contracting officer need not delay a proposed award in order to provide a potential offeror with an opportunity to demonstrate its ability to meet the standards specified for qualification [@CL-SUP-0996].

The responsible agency activity must, before establishing any qualification requirement, furnish notice through the GPE [@CL-SUP-0938]. Generally, qualification is performed in advance and independently of any specific acquisition action [@CL-SUP-0997]. Upon request to the contracting activity, potential offerors shall be provided all requirements that they or their products must satisfy to become qualified and, at their expense (but see FAR 9.204(a)(2) with regard to small businesses), a prompt opportunity to demonstrate their abilities to meet the standards specified for qualification using qualified personnel and facilities of the agency concerned, or of another agency obtained through interagency agreements, or under contract, or other methods approved by the agency (including use of approved testing and evaluation services not provided under contract to the agency) [@CL-SUP-0933; @CL-SUP-4176; @CL-SUP-4177; @CL-SUP-0934].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A potential offeror that is not a small business is not on the qualified list. It asks to demonstrate its product, and the testing would cost 40,000.

Answer: the offeror pays the 40,000 for the demonstration. If it can show before the award date that its product meets the standards, it may not be denied the chance to submit an offer solely because it is not listed, although the contracting officer need not delay the award to let it demonstrate. Had it been a small business concern, the agency could have borne the testing cost only if the conditions in the rule were met, including the determination that cost savings from increased competition would be sufficient.

## Never confuse

See [Contractor responsibility standards](../../mna/screening/mna-contractor-responsibility-standards.md). If a potential offeror can demonstrate to the satisfaction of the contracting officer that the potential offeror (or its product) meets the standards established for qualification or can meet them before the date specified for award of the contract, a potential offeror may not be denied the opportunity to submit and have considered an offer for a contract solely because of one of the three alternatives in FAR 9.202(c)(1) through (c)(3) [@CL-SUP-0936; @CL-SUP-0937; @CL-SUP-4178].
