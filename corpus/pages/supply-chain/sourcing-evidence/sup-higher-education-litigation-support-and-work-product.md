## Purpose

Shows what the offeror represents about institutions of higher education that host a Confucius Institute, when the Government may disclose information to litigation support contractors, and what control Government personnel keep over their work product.

By submission of an offer, the offeror represents either that it is not an institution of higher education that hosts a Confucius Institute or that it has obtained a waiver approved by the Office of the Under Secretary of Defense for Research and Engineering [@CL-SUP-1592]. The restriction paragraph states that, as required by section 1062 of the National Defense Authorization Act for Fiscal Year 2021, DoD may not award a contract with any institution of higher education that hosts a Confucius Institute [@CL-SUP-1666]. The next sentence pairs that with a waiver route: section 1062 prohibits DoD from providing funding to any United States institution of higher education hosting a Confucius Institute unless that institution receives a waiver from that office [@CL-SUP-1590]. The waiver authority terminates on October 1, 2026, and any waiver issued will not apply on or after that date [@CL-SUP-1591]. An offeror that relies on a waiver should check that date against the current text [@CL-SUP-1591].

The provision defines a Confucius Institute to include any program that receives funding or support from the Chinese International Education Foundation or from the Center for Language Exchange Cooperation of the Ministry of Education of the People's Republic of China [@CL-SUP-1667]. It also includes any cultural institute directly or indirectly funded by the government of the People's Republic of China [@CL-SUP-1668].

The litigation support notice clause defines a litigation support contractor as a contractor, including its experts, technical consultants, subcontractors, and suppliers, providing litigation support under a contract that contains the clause on limitations on the use or disclosure of information by litigation support contractors [@CL-SUP-1670]. Under the notice, the Government may disclose to a litigation support contractor, for the sole purpose of litigation support activities, any information, including sensitive information, received in connection with a quotation or offer or in the performance of or in connection with a contract [@CL-SUP-1593]. The contractor shall include the substance of the clause, including its subcontracts paragraph, in all subcontracts, including subcontracts for commercial products or commercial services [@CL-SUP-1594].

Under the work product clause, the contractor's procedures for protecting against unauthorized disclosure of information shall not require Department of Defense employees or members of the Armed Forces to relinquish control of their work products, whether classified or not, to the contractor [@CL-SUP-1595].

## How it works

The higher education representation is made by submitting an offer, and it is satisfied by one of two statements, the second of which depends on a waiver [@CL-SUP-1592]. The waiver route is limited by the provision's own end date for waiver authority [@CL-SUP-1591], and the no-award sentence sits beside the waiver sentence in the same restriction paragraph [@CL-SUP-1666; @CL-SUP-1590]. The litigation support notice tells the contractor that the Government may share offer and contract information with litigation support contractors for litigation support only, and it flows to every subcontract [@CL-SUP-1593; @CL-SUP-1594]. The work product clause keeps Government personnel in control of their own work products [@CL-SUP-1595].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A university submits an offer and represents that it does not host a Confucius Institute, so the first statement applies. A prime contractor places a subcontract for commercial services, and it includes the substance of the litigation support notice in that subcontract. The Government later shares the prime contractor's proposal with a litigation support contractor for a case, which the notice allows when it is solely for litigation support.

## Never confuse

The notice here tells the contractor the Government may disclose information; it does not set what a litigation support contractor may do with it [@CL-SUP-1593]. The clause defines that contractor by reference to a contract that contains a separate clause on limits of use and disclosure [@CL-SUP-1670], covered in [Limits on use of cyber incident and litigation support information](../../compliance/trade-cyber/cmp-cyber-incident-information-and-litigation-support-limits.md). The higher education restriction paragraph states a no-award sentence and a waiver sentence together, so read them together and do not read it as a rule with no waiver route [@CL-SUP-1666; @CL-SUP-1590]. The notice flows to subcontracts for commercial products or commercial services as well [@CL-SUP-1594]; for commercial subcontracts in general, see [Subcontracts for commercial products and commercial services](sup-subcontracts-commercial-products-and-services.md). The work product clause limits the contractor's procedures; it is not a rule about disclosure to litigation support contractors [@CL-SUP-1595].
