## Purpose

Sets out the quality assurance policy that agencies must follow and the quality duties of the contracting office and of the contract administration office. It also names the activity responsible for technical requirements, which prescribes the quality requirements.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-SUP-3898] |
| Codified citation | FAR 46.000, 46.102, 46.103 and 46.104 [@CL-SUP-4123; @CL-SUP-3898; @CL-SUP-3906; @CL-SUP-3912] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-3898] |
| Applies to | Agencies (policy); contracting offices; and the contract administration office cognizant of the contractor's plant when a contract is assigned to it for administration, unless specified otherwise [@CL-SUP-3898; @CL-SUP-3906; @CL-SUP-3912] |

Part 46 prescribes policies and procedures to ensure that supplies and services acquired under Government contract conform to the contract's quality and quantity requirements, and includes inspection, acceptance, warranty, and other measures associated with quality requirements [@CL-SUP-4123].

Policy. Agencies shall ensure the following [@CL-SUP-3898].

- Contracts include inspection and other quality requirements, including warranty clauses when appropriate, that are determined necessary to protect the Government's interest [@CL-SUP-3898].
- Supplies or services (including commercial services) tendered by contractors meet contract requirements [@CL-SUP-3899].
- Government contract quality assurance is conducted before acceptance, except as otherwise provided in this part, by or under the direction of Government personnel [@CL-SUP-3900].
- No contract precludes the Government from performing inspection [@CL-SUP-3901].
- Nonconforming supplies or services are rejected, except as otherwise provided in FAR 46.407 [@CL-SUP-3902].
- Contracts for commercial products rely on a contractor's existing quality assurance system as a substitute for compliance with Government inspection and testing before tender for acceptance, unless customary market practices for the commercial product being acquired permit in-process inspection (41 USC 3307), and any in-process inspection by the Government shall be conducted in a manner consistent with commercial practice [@CL-SUP-3903; @CL-SUP-3904].
- The quality assurance and acceptance services of other agencies are used when this will be effective, economical, or otherwise in the Government's interest (see FAR 42.002 and subpart 42.2) [@CL-SUP-3905].

Contracting office responsibilities. Contracting offices are responsible for the following [@CL-SUP-3906].

- Receiving from the activity responsible for technical requirements any specifications for inspection, testing, and other contract quality requirements essential to ensure the integrity of the supplies or services [@CL-SUP-3906]. The activity responsible for technical requirements is responsible for prescribing contract quality requirements, such as inspection and testing requirements or, for service contracts, a quality assurance surveillance plan [@CL-SUP-3907].
- Including in solicitations and contracts the appropriate requirements for the contractor's control of quality for the supplies or services to be acquired [@CL-SUP-3908].
- Issuing any necessary instructions to the cognizant contract administration office and acting on recommendations submitted by that office (see FAR 42.301 and 46.104(f)) [@CL-SUP-3909].
- When contract administration is retained (see FAR 42.201), verifying that the contractor fulfills the contract quality requirements [@CL-SUP-3910].
- Ensuring that nonconformances are identified, and establishing the significance of a nonconformance when considering the acceptability of supplies or services which do not meet contract requirements [@CL-SUP-3911].

Contract administration office responsibilities. When a contract is assigned for administration to the contract administration office cognizant of the contractor's plant, that office, unless specified otherwise, shall do the following [@CL-SUP-3912].

- Develop and apply efficient procedures for performing Government contract quality assurance actions under the contract in accordance with the written direction of the contracting office [@CL-SUP-3913].
- Perform all actions necessary to verify whether the supplies or services conform to contract quality requirements [@CL-SUP-3914].
- Maintain, as part of the performance records of the contract, suitable records reflecting the nature of Government contract quality assurance actions, including, when appropriate, the number of observations made and the number and type of defects [@CL-SUP-3915]. The records also reflect decisions regarding the acceptability of the products, the processes, and the requirements, as well as action to correct defects [@CL-SUP-3916].
- Implement any specific written instructions from the contracting office [@CL-SUP-3917].
- Report to the contracting office any defects observed in design or technical requirements, including contract quality requirements [@CL-SUP-3918].
- Recommend any changes necessary to the contract, specifications, instructions, or other requirements that will provide more effective operations or eliminate unnecessary costs (see FAR 46.103(c)) [@CL-SUP-3919].

## How it works

The contractor's quality responsibilities are covered on [contractor responsibilities for quality](sup-contractor-quality-responsibilities.md).

Who decides what. The activity responsible for technical requirements is responsible for prescribing contract quality requirements, such as inspection and testing requirements or, for service contracts, a quality assurance surveillance plan, and the contracting office is responsible for receiving from that activity any specifications for inspection, testing, and other contract quality requirements essential to ensure the integrity of the supplies or services and for including in solicitations and contracts the appropriate requirements for the contractor's control of quality for the supplies or services to be acquired [@CL-SUP-3906; @CL-SUP-3907; @CL-SUP-3908]. The contracting office is responsible for ensuring that nonconformances are identified and establishing the significance of a nonconformance when considering the acceptability of supplies or services which do not meet contract requirements [@CL-SUP-3906; @CL-SUP-3911]. Agencies shall ensure that nonconforming supplies or services are rejected, except as otherwise provided in FAR 46.407 [@CL-SUP-3898; @CL-SUP-3902]. See [rejection of nonconforming supplies](../../operating-model/corrective-action/opm-rejection-of-nonconforming-supplies.md).

Who verifies. When contract administration is retained (see FAR 42.201), the contracting office is responsible for verifying that the contractor fulfills the contract quality requirements [@CL-SUP-3906; @CL-SUP-3910]. When a contract is assigned for administration to the contract administration office cognizant of the contractor's plant, that office, unless specified otherwise, shall perform all actions necessary to verify whether the supplies or services conform to contract quality requirements [@CL-SUP-3912; @CL-SUP-3914].

How the offices work together. When a contract is assigned for administration to the contract administration office cognizant of the contractor's plant, that office, unless specified otherwise, shall develop and apply efficient procedures for performing Government contract quality assurance actions under the contract in accordance with the written direction of the contracting office and implement any specific written instructions from the contracting office [@CL-SUP-3912; @CL-SUP-3913; @CL-SUP-3917]. Contracting offices are responsible for issuing any necessary instructions to the cognizant contract administration office and acting on recommendations submitted by that office (see FAR 42.301 and 46.104(f)) [@CL-SUP-3906; @CL-SUP-3909]. The cognizant contract administration office, unless specified otherwise, shall report to the contracting office any defects observed in design or technical requirements, including contract quality requirements, and recommend any changes necessary to the contract, specifications, instructions, or other requirements that will provide more effective operations or eliminate unnecessary costs (see FAR 46.103(c)) [@CL-SUP-3912; @CL-SUP-3918; @CL-SUP-3919].

Commercial products. Agencies shall ensure that contracts for commercial products rely on a contractor's existing quality assurance system as a substitute for compliance with Government inspection and testing before tender for acceptance unless customary market practices for the commercial product being acquired permit in-process inspection (41 USC 3307), and any in-process inspection by the Government shall be conducted in a manner consistent with commercial practice [@CL-SUP-3898; @CL-SUP-3903; @CL-SUP-3904]. Under FAR 46.202-1, when acquiring commercial products (see part 12), the Government shall rely on contractors' existing quality assurance systems as a substitute for Government inspection and testing before tender for acceptance unless customary market practices for the commercial product being acquired include in-process inspection, and any in-process inspection by the Government shall be conducted in a manner consistent with commercial practice [@CL-SUP-3983; @CL-SUP-3920; @CL-SUP-3921]. See [types of contract quality requirements](sup-contract-quality-requirement-types.md) and [quality assurance definitions](sup-quality-assurance-definitions.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contract for 400 pumps is assigned for administration to the contract administration office cognizant of the contractor's plant, and the assignment says nothing different. The office makes 40 observations and finds 2 defects. Where appropriate, it records the number of observations and the number and type of defects in the performance records of the contract. The records also reflect decisions regarding the acceptability of the products, the processes and the requirements, as well as action to correct the defects. The office also notices that a drawing tolerance cannot be met by any pump built to the drawing. That is a defect in technical requirements, so the office reports it to the contracting office. When the contracting office considers acceptability, it establishes the significance of the 2 defects. These sections do not settle whether the 2 pumps are accepted. Under the policy, nonconforming supplies are rejected, except as otherwise provided in FAR 46.407.

## Never confuse

When contract administration is retained (see FAR 42.201), the contracting office is responsible for verifying that the contractor fulfills the contract quality requirements [@CL-SUP-3906; @CL-SUP-3910]. When a contract is assigned for administration to the contract administration office cognizant of the contractor's plant, that office, unless specified otherwise, shall perform all actions necessary to verify whether the supplies or services conform to contract quality requirements and maintain, as part of the performance records of the contract, suitable records reflecting the nature of Government contract quality assurance actions (including, when appropriate, the number of observations made and the number and type of defects) and decisions regarding the acceptability of the products, the processes, and the requirements, as well as action to correct defects [@CL-SUP-3912; @CL-SUP-3914; @CL-SUP-3915; @CL-SUP-3916]. The activity responsible for technical requirements is responsible for prescribing contract quality requirements, such as inspection and testing requirements or, for service contracts, a quality assurance surveillance plan, while the contracting office is responsible for receiving from that activity any specifications for inspection, testing, and other contract quality requirements essential to ensure the integrity of the supplies or services and for including in solicitations and contracts the appropriate requirements for the contractor's control of quality for the supplies or services to be acquired [@CL-SUP-3906; @CL-SUP-3907; @CL-SUP-3908].
