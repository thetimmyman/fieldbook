## Purpose

Sets out the quality assurance policy that agencies must follow and the quality duties of the contracting office and of the contract administration office. It also names the activity responsible for technical requirements, which prescribes the quality requirements.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-SUP-3898] |
| Codified citation | FAR 46.102, 46.103 and 46.104 [@CL-SUP-3898; @CL-SUP-3906; @CL-SUP-3912] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-3898] |
| Applies to | Agencies (policy); contracting offices; and the contract administration office cognizant of the contractor's plant when a contract is assigned to it for administration, unless specified otherwise [@CL-SUP-3898; @CL-SUP-3906; @CL-SUP-3912] |

Policy. Agencies must ensure the following [@CL-SUP-3898].

- Contracts include inspection and other quality requirements, including warranty clauses when appropriate, that are determined necessary to protect the Government's interest [@CL-SUP-3898]. The paragraph does not say who makes that determination.
- Supplies or services (including commercial services) tendered by contractors meet contract requirements [@CL-SUP-3899].
- Government contract quality assurance is conducted before acceptance, except as otherwise provided in this part, by or under the direction of Government personnel [@CL-SUP-3900].
- No contract precludes the Government from performing inspection [@CL-SUP-3901].
- Nonconforming supplies or services are rejected, except as otherwise provided in 46.407 [@CL-SUP-3902].
- Contracts for commercial products rely on a contractor's existing quality assurance system as a substitute for compliance with Government inspection and testing before tender for acceptance, unless customary market practices for the commercial product being acquired permit in-process inspection [@CL-SUP-3903]. The paragraph cites the statute at section 3307 of title 41 of the United States Code [@CL-SUP-3904]. Any in-process inspection by the Government shall be conducted in a manner consistent with commercial practice [@CL-SUP-3904].
- The quality assurance and acceptance services of other agencies are used when this will be effective, economical, or otherwise in the Government's interest; the paragraph cross-refers to 42.002 and subpart 42.2 [@CL-SUP-3905].

Contracting office responsibilities. Contracting offices are responsible for the following [@CL-SUP-3906].

- Receiving from the activity responsible for technical requirements any specifications for inspection, testing, and other contract quality requirements essential to ensure the integrity of the supplies or services [@CL-SUP-3906]. The activity responsible for technical requirements is responsible for prescribing contract quality requirements, such as inspection and testing requirements or, for service contracts, a quality assurance surveillance plan [@CL-SUP-3907].
- Including in solicitations and contracts the appropriate requirements for the contractor's control of quality for the supplies or services to be acquired [@CL-SUP-3908].
- Issuing any necessary instructions to the cognizant contract administration office and acting on recommendations submitted by that office; the paragraph cross-refers to 42.301 and 46.104(f) [@CL-SUP-3909].
- When contract administration is retained, verifying that the contractor fulfills the contract quality requirements; the paragraph cross-refers to 42.201 [@CL-SUP-3910].
- Ensuring that nonconformances are identified, and establishing the significance of a nonconformance when considering the acceptability of supplies or services which do not meet contract requirements [@CL-SUP-3911].

Contract administration office responsibilities. When a contract is assigned for administration to the contract administration office cognizant of the contractor's plant, that office, unless specified otherwise, shall do the following [@CL-SUP-3912].

- Develop and apply efficient procedures for performing Government contract quality assurance actions under the contract in accordance with the written direction of the contracting office [@CL-SUP-3913].
- Perform all actions necessary to verify whether the supplies or services conform to contract quality requirements [@CL-SUP-3914].
- Maintain, as part of the performance records of the contract, suitable records reflecting the nature of Government contract quality assurance actions, including, when appropriate, the number of observations made and the number and type of defects [@CL-SUP-3915]. The records also reflect decisions regarding the acceptability of the products, the processes, and the requirements, as well as action to correct defects [@CL-SUP-3916].
- Implement any specific written instructions from the contracting office [@CL-SUP-3917].
- Report to the contracting office any defects observed in design or technical requirements, including contract quality requirements [@CL-SUP-3918].
- Recommend any changes necessary to the contract, specifications, instructions, or other requirements that will provide more effective operations or eliminate unnecessary costs; the paragraph cross-refers to 46.103(c) [@CL-SUP-3919].

## How it works

These sections put their duties on agencies, contracting offices and the contract administration office. The contractor's own quality duties are in [contractor responsibilities for quality](sup-contractor-quality-responsibilities.md).

Who decides what. The activity responsible for technical requirements prescribes the quality requirements, and the contracting office receives them and includes the appropriate requirements for the contractor's control of quality in the solicitation and contract [@CL-SUP-3906; @CL-SUP-3907; @CL-SUP-3908]. The contracting office establishes the significance of a nonconformance when it considers acceptability [@CL-SUP-3911]. When and by whom nonconforming supplies may be accepted or rejected is in 46.407, which the policy names as the exception to rejection [@CL-SUP-3902]. That section is covered in [rejection of nonconforming supplies](../../operating-model/corrective-action/opm-rejection-of-nonconforming-supplies.md).

Who verifies. If the contracting office retains contract administration, it verifies that the contractor fulfills the contract quality requirements [@CL-SUP-3910]. If the contract is assigned to the cognizant contract administration office, that office performs the actions needed to verify conformance, unless the assignment specifies otherwise [@CL-SUP-3912; @CL-SUP-3914].

How the offices work together. The contract administration office applies its procedures in accordance with the contracting office's written direction and implements the contracting office's specific written instructions [@CL-SUP-3913; @CL-SUP-3917]. The contracting office issues any necessary instructions to that office and acts on its recommendations [@CL-SUP-3909]. The contract administration office reports defects it observes in design or technical requirements, and recommends changes to the contract, specifications, instructions or other requirements [@CL-SUP-3918; @CL-SUP-3919].

Commercial products. The policy and the later section on contracts for commercial products both state reliance on the contractor's existing quality assurance system. The policy words its exception as customary market practices that permit in-process inspection [@CL-SUP-3903]. The later section words it as practices that include in-process inspection [@CL-SUP-3920]. The later section is covered in [types of contract quality requirements](sup-contract-quality-requirement-types.md). The terms used here are defined in [quality assurance definitions](sup-quality-assurance-definitions.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contract for 400 pumps is assigned for administration to the contract administration office cognizant of the contractor's plant, and the assignment says nothing different [@CL-SUP-3912]. The office makes 40 observations and finds 2 defects. Where appropriate, it records the number of observations and the number and type of defects in the performance records of the contract [@CL-SUP-3915]. The records also reflect decisions regarding the acceptability of the products, the processes and the requirements, as well as action to correct the defects [@CL-SUP-3916]. The office also notices that a drawing tolerance cannot be met by any pump built to the drawing. That is a defect in technical requirements, so the office reports it to the contracting office [@CL-SUP-3918]. When the contracting office considers acceptability, it establishes the significance of the 2 defects [@CL-SUP-3911]. These sections do not settle whether the 2 pumps are accepted. The policy says that nonconforming supplies are rejected, except as otherwise provided in 46.407 [@CL-SUP-3902].

## Never confuse

Retaining contract administration is not assigning it. When the contracting office retains administration it verifies that the contractor fulfills the contract quality requirements [@CL-SUP-3910]. When the contract is assigned, the contract administration office does the verification and keeps the records, unless specified otherwise [@CL-SUP-3912; @CL-SUP-3914; @CL-SUP-3915]. The paragraph puts the prescribing of contract quality requirements on the activity responsible for technical requirements; the contracting office's duty is to receive the specifications and to include the appropriate requirements for the contractor's control of quality in the solicitation and contract [@CL-SUP-3906; @CL-SUP-3907; @CL-SUP-3908].
