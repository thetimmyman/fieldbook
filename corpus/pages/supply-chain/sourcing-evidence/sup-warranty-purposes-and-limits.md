## Purpose

Shows why a Government contract carries a warranty, what a warranty should contain, and where warranty clauses may not be used or may not cut down other rights.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-SUP-3949] |
| Codified citation | FAR 46.702, 46.705 and 46.708 [@CL-SUP-3949; @CL-SUP-3953; @CL-SUP-4147] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-3949; @CL-SUP-3953] |
| Applies to | Warranties in Government contracts [@CL-SUP-3949]; the limits apply to warranty clauses and to the contracting officer's use of warranties in cost-reimbursement contracts [@CL-SUP-3953; @CL-SUP-3955; @CL-SUP-3956] |

The principal purposes of a warranty in a Government contract are to delineate the rights and obligations of the contractor and the Government for defective items and services and to foster quality performance [@CL-SUP-3949].

Generally, a warranty should provide a contractual right for the correction of defects notwithstanding any other requirement of the contract pertaining to acceptance of the supplies or services by the Government [@CL-SUP-3950]. It should also provide a stated period of time or use, or the occurrence of a specified event, after acceptance by the Government to assert that right [@CL-SUP-3951]. The benefits to be derived from a warranty must be commensurate with the cost of the warranty to the Government [@CL-SUP-3952].

Except for the warranties in the clauses at 52.246-3, Inspection of Supplies—Cost-Reimbursement, and 52.246-8, Inspection of Research and Development—Cost-Reimbursement, the contracting officer shall not include warranties in cost-reimbursement contracts [@CL-SUP-3953]. The sentence adds a second exception for warranties authorized in accordance with agency regulations, and points to 46.708 [@CL-SUP-3954]. Section 46.708 says that warranties of data shall be developed and used in accordance with agency regulations [@CL-SUP-4147].

Warranty clauses shall not limit the Government's rights under an inspection clause in relation to latent defects, fraud, or gross mistakes that amount to fraud [@CL-SUP-3955]. Except for warranty clauses in construction contracts, warranty clauses shall provide that the warranty applies notwithstanding inspection and acceptance or other clauses or terms of the contract [@CL-SUP-3956].

## How it works

Section 46.702 states purposes and general expectations [@CL-SUP-3949; @CL-SUP-3950]. Its correction-right and stated-period sentence says "should" and "generally" [@CL-SUP-3950; @CL-SUP-3951]. Its cost-benefit sentence says "must" [@CL-SUP-3952]. The section does not say who judges whether the benefits are commensurate with the cost.

Section 46.705 states limits in "shall" terms [@CL-SUP-3953; @CL-SUP-3955; @CL-SUP-3956]. The bar on warranties in cost-reimbursement contracts is a duty of the contracting officer [@CL-SUP-3953]. The other two limits are written as requirements on warranty clauses, and the sentences do not name who drafts the clause [@CL-SUP-3955; @CL-SUP-3956].

The warranty therefore sits beside acceptance. Where required, it applies notwithstanding inspection and acceptance [@CL-SUP-3956], and it cannot limit the Government's inspection-clause rights for latent defects, fraud or gross mistakes that amount to fraud [@CL-SUP-3955].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A fixed-price equipment contract is not a construction contract. Its warranty clause states a period of 12 months after acceptance. The Government accepts the units on 1 March, and a defect appears about 7 months later. The warranty clause shall provide that it applies notwithstanding inspection and acceptance [@CL-SUP-3956]. Assume the clause gives the correction right that a warranty generally should provide [@CL-SUP-3950]. The defect appears inside the stated 12 months, so the correction right is still open under the clause as written [@CL-SUP-3951].

A separate cost-reimbursement contract carries no warranty beyond the two named inspection-clause warranties. The contracting officer shall not add one unless authorized in accordance with agency regulations [@CL-SUP-3953; @CL-SUP-3954].

## Never confuse

A warranty is not a substitute for inspection and acceptance. The warranty clause must not limit inspection-clause rights for latent defects, fraud or gross mistakes that amount to fraud [@CL-SUP-3955].

"Should" in section 46.702 is not "shall" in section 46.705 [@CL-SUP-3950; @CL-SUP-3956]. In section 46.705, the construction exception appears only in the sentence on warranties applying notwithstanding acceptance [@CL-SUP-3956]. The bar on cost-reimbursement warranties has two named clause exceptions and an agency-regulation route [@CL-SUP-3953; @CL-SUP-3954].
