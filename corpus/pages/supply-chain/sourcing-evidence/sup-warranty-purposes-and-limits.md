## Purpose

Shows why a Government contract carries a warranty, what a warranty should contain, and where warranty clauses may not be used or may not cut down other rights.

The principal purposes of a warranty in a Government contract are (1) to delineate the rights and obligations of the contractor and the Government for defective items and services and (2) to foster quality performance [@CL-SUP-3949].

Generally, a warranty should provide: (1) a contractual right for the correction of defects notwithstanding any other requirement of the contract pertaining to acceptance of the supplies or services by the Government, and (2) a stated period of time or use, or the occurrence of a specified event, after acceptance by the Government to assert a contractual right for the correction of defects [@CL-SUP-3950; @CL-SUP-3951]. The benefits to be derived from a warranty must be commensurate with the cost of the warranty to the Government [@CL-SUP-3952].

Except for the warranties in the clauses at 52.246-3, Inspection of Supplies—Cost-Reimbursement, and 52.246-8, Inspection of Research and Development—Cost-Reimbursement, the contracting officer shall not include warranties in cost-reimbursement contracts unless authorized in accordance with agency regulations (see 46.708) [@CL-SUP-3953; @CL-SUP-3954]. Warranties of data shall be developed and used in accordance with agency regulations [@CL-SUP-4147].

Warranty clauses shall not limit the Government's rights under an inspection clause (see subpart 46.3) in relation to latent defects, fraud, or gross mistakes that amount to fraud [@CL-SUP-3955]. Except for warranty clauses in construction contracts, warranty clauses shall provide that the warranty applies notwithstanding inspection and acceptance or other clauses or terms of the contract [@CL-SUP-3956].

## How it works

Under FAR 46.702, a warranty generally should provide a contractual right for the correction of defects notwithstanding any other requirement of the contract pertaining to acceptance of the supplies or services by the Government, and a stated period of time or use, or the occurrence of a specified event, after acceptance by the Government to assert a contractual right for the correction of defects [@CL-SUP-3950; @CL-SUP-3951]. The benefits to be derived from a warranty must be commensurate with the cost of the warranty to the Government [@CL-SUP-3952].

Under FAR 46.705, except for the warranties in the clauses at 52.246-3, Inspection of Supplies—Cost-Reimbursement, and 52.246-8, Inspection of Research and Development—Cost-Reimbursement, the contracting officer shall not include warranties in cost-reimbursement contracts unless authorized in accordance with agency regulations (see 46.708), and under FAR 46.708 warranties of data shall be developed and used in accordance with agency regulations [@CL-SUP-3953; @CL-SUP-3954; @CL-SUP-4147]. Warranty clauses shall not limit the Government's rights under an inspection clause (see subpart 46.3) in relation to latent defects, fraud, or gross mistakes that amount to fraud, and except for warranty clauses in construction contracts, warranty clauses shall provide that the warranty applies notwithstanding inspection and acceptance or other clauses or terms of the contract [@CL-SUP-3955; @CL-SUP-3956].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A fixed-price equipment contract is not a construction contract. Its warranty clause states a period of 12 months after acceptance. The Government accepts the units on 1 March, and a defect appears about 7 months later. The warranty clause provides that it applies notwithstanding inspection and acceptance. Assume the clause gives the correction right that a warranty generally should provide. The defect appears inside the stated 12 months, so the correction right is still open under the clause as written.

A separate cost-reimbursement contract carries no warranty beyond the two named inspection-clause warranties. The contracting officer shall not add one unless authorized in accordance with agency regulations.

## Never confuse

Warranty clauses shall not limit the Government's rights under an inspection clause (see subpart 46.3) in relation to latent defects, fraud, or gross mistakes that amount to fraud, and except for warranty clauses in construction contracts, warranty clauses shall provide that the warranty applies notwithstanding inspection and acceptance or other clauses or terms of the contract [@CL-SUP-3955; @CL-SUP-3956].
