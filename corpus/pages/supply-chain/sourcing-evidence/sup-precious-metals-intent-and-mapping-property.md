## Purpose

Shows what an offeror must state about precious metals that the Government may furnish, and the handling limits on Government-furnished mapping, charting, and geodesy property.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-SUP-1649; @CL-SUP-1650] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 252.208-7000 [@CL-SUP-1649]; DFARS 252.245-7000 [@CL-SUP-1650] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-1649; @CL-SUP-1650] |
| Applies to | Contracts that use DFARS 252.208-7000 as prescribed in DFARS 208.7305(a) [@CL-SUP-1649]; contracts that use DFARS 252.245-7000 as prescribed in DFARS 245.107(2) [@CL-SUP-1650] |

Under DFARS 252.208-7000(a), the Government intends to furnish precious metals required in the manufacture of items to be delivered under the contract if the Contracting Officer determines it to be in the Government's best interest [@CL-SUP-1558]. The use of Government-furnished silver is mandatory when the quantity required is one hundred troy ounces or more [@CL-SUP-1559].

The offeror shall cite the type (silver, gold, platinum, palladium, iridium, rhodium, and ruthenium) and quantity in whole troy ounces of precious metals required in the performance of the contract (including precious metals required for any first article or production sample), and shall specify the national stock number (NSN) and nomenclature, if known, of the deliverable item requiring precious metals [@CL-SUP-1560; @CL-SUP-1561]. Offerors shall submit two prices for each deliverable item which contains precious metals—one based on the Government furnishing precious metals, and one based on the contractor furnishing precious metals—and award will be made on the basis which is in the best interest of the Government [@CL-SUP-1562]. The contractor agrees to insert the clause, including paragraph (d), in solicitations for subcontracts and purchase orders issued in performance of the contract, unless the contractor knows that the item being purchased contains no precious metals [@CL-SUP-1563].

Under DFARS 252.245-7000(a), mapping, charting, and geodesy (MC&G) property means geodetic, geomagnetic, gravimetric, aeronautical, topographic, hydrographic, cultural, and toponymic data presented in the form of topographic, planimetric, relief, or thematic maps and graphics, nautical and aeronautical charts and publications, and in simulated, photographic, digital, or computerized formats [@CL-SUP-1564; @CL-SUP-1669]. The contractor shall not duplicate, copy, or otherwise reproduce MC&G property for purposes other than those necessary for performance of the contract [@CL-SUP-1565]. At the completion of performance of the contract, the contractor, as directed by the Contracting Officer, shall either destroy or return to the Government all Government-furnished MC&G property not consumed in the performance of the contract [@CL-SUP-1566].

## How it works

Under DFARS 252.208-7000, the offeror shall cite the type (silver, gold, platinum, palladium, iridium, rhodium, and ruthenium) and quantity in whole troy ounces of precious metals required in the performance of the contract (including precious metals required for any first article or production sample), specify the NSN and nomenclature, if known, of the deliverable item requiring precious metals, and submit two prices for each deliverable item which contains precious metals—one based on the Government furnishing precious metals, and one based on the contractor furnishing precious metals [@CL-SUP-1560; @CL-SUP-1561; @CL-SUP-1562]. The Government intends to furnish precious metals required in the manufacture of items to be delivered under the contract if the Contracting Officer determines it to be in the Government's best interest, the use of Government-furnished silver is mandatory when the quantity required is one hundred troy ounces or more, and award will be made on the basis which is in the best interest of the Government [@CL-SUP-1558; @CL-SUP-1559; @CL-SUP-1562]. The contractor agrees to insert the clause, including paragraph (d), in solicitations for subcontracts and purchase orders issued in performance of the contract, unless the contractor knows that the item being purchased contains no precious metals [@CL-SUP-1563]. Under DFARS 252.245-7000, the contractor shall not duplicate, copy, or otherwise reproduce MC&G property for purposes other than those necessary for performance of the contract, and at the completion of performance of the contract, as directed by the Contracting Officer, shall either destroy or return to the Government all Government-furnished MC&G property not consumed in the performance of the contract [@CL-SUP-1565; @CL-SUP-1566].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An offeror needs 150 troy ounces of silver for a deliverable item. It cites the metal and the quantity, gives the national stock number if it knows it, and submits one price assuming Government-furnished silver and one assuming its own. Because the quantity is above one hundred troy ounces, the Government-furnished route is mandatory for silver. Separately, a contractor given a set of Government charts makes no spare copies and, at the end, returns the charts as the Contracting Officer directs.

## Never confuse
