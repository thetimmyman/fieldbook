## Purpose

Shows what an offeror must state about precious metals that the Government may furnish, and the handling limits on Government-furnished mapping, charting, and geodesy property.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-SUP-1649; @CL-SUP-1650] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 252.208-7000 [@CL-SUP-1649]; DFARS 252.245-7000 [@CL-SUP-1650] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-1649; @CL-SUP-1650] |
| Applies to | Contracts that use the precious metals clause as prescribed in section 208.7305(a) of the Defense Federal Acquisition Regulation Supplement [@CL-SUP-1649]; contracts that use the mapping clause as prescribed in section 245.107(2) [@CL-SUP-1650], for Government-furnished mapping, charting, and geodesy property [@CL-SUP-1566] |

The precious metals clause states that the Government intends to furnish precious metals required in the manufacture of items to be delivered under the contract if the Contracting Officer determines it to be in the Government's best interest [@CL-SUP-1558]. It makes the use of Government-furnished silver mandatory when the quantity required is one hundred troy ounces or more [@CL-SUP-1559].

The offeror shall cite the type of precious metal (silver, gold, platinum, palladium, iridium, rhodium, and ruthenium) and the quantity in whole troy ounces required in performance of the contract, including metals required for any first article or production sample [@CL-SUP-1560]. It shall also specify the national stock number and nomenclature, if known, of the deliverable item requiring precious metals [@CL-SUP-1561]. Offerors shall submit two prices for each deliverable item that contains precious metals, one based on the Government furnishing the metals and one based on the contractor furnishing them, and award will be made on the basis which is in the best interest of the Government [@CL-SUP-1562]. The contractor agrees to insert the clause in solicitations for subcontracts and purchase orders issued in performance of the contract, unless it knows that the item being purchased contains no precious metals [@CL-SUP-1563].

The mapping clause defines mapping, charting, and geodesy property to include geodetic, geomagnetic, gravimetric, aeronautical, topographic, hydrographic, cultural, and toponymic data presented in the form of topographic, planimetric, relief, or thematic maps and graphics [@CL-SUP-1564]. The definition also covers nautical and aeronautical charts and publications, and property in simulated, photographic, digital, or computerized formats [@CL-SUP-1669]. The contractor shall not duplicate, copy, or otherwise reproduce that property for purposes other than those necessary for performance of the contract [@CL-SUP-1565]. At the completion of performance, as directed by the Contracting Officer, it shall either destroy or return to the Government all Government-furnished property of this kind not consumed in performance [@CL-SUP-1566].

## How it works

In an offer, the offeror lists each precious metal by type and whole troy ounces, adds the national stock number and nomenclature of the deliverable item if known, and prices each such item two ways [@CL-SUP-1560; @CL-SUP-1561; @CL-SUP-1562]. The clause says the Government intends to furnish the metals if the Contracting Officer determines it to be in the Government's best interest, and it makes Government-furnished silver mandatory at one hundred troy ounces or more [@CL-SUP-1558; @CL-SUP-1559]. Award is made on the basis, Government-furnished or contractor-furnished, that is in the Government's best interest [@CL-SUP-1562]. The metals clause flows to subcontract solicitations and purchase orders, unless the contractor knows the item contains no precious metals [@CL-SUP-1563]. Mapping property may be copied only as performance requires, and what is not consumed is destroyed or returned as the Contracting Officer directs [@CL-SUP-1565; @CL-SUP-1566].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An offeror needs 150 troy ounces of silver for a deliverable item. It cites the metal and the quantity, gives the national stock number if it knows it, and submits one price assuming Government-furnished silver and one assuming its own. Because the quantity is above one hundred troy ounces, the Government-furnished route is mandatory for silver. Separately, a contractor given a set of Government charts makes no spare copies and, at the end, returns the charts as the Contracting Officer directs.

## Never confuse

Furnishing precious metals and furnishing mapping property are separate clauses with separate duties. The first is about what the offeror states and prices in the offer [@CL-SUP-1560; @CL-SUP-1562]; the second limits copying and sets the end-of-contract disposal [@CL-SUP-1565; @CL-SUP-1566]. For the general duties on Government property, see [Government Property clause](../../close-revrec/fixed-assets/cls-government-property-clause.md).
