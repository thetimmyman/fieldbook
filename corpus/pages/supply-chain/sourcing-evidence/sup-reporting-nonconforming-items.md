## Purpose

Shows what a contractor must screen and report under the reporting nonconforming items clause, by when, to whom, and which subcontracts the clause flows to.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-SUP-1187] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.246-26 [@CL-SUP-1187; @CL-SUP-1188; @CL-SUP-1189; @CL-SUP-1190; @CL-SUP-1191; @CL-SUP-1192; @CL-SUP-1193; @CL-SUP-1194; @CL-SUP-1206; @CL-SUP-1207; @CL-SUP-1208; @CL-SUP-1209; @CL-SUP-1210; @CL-SUP-1211] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-1187] |
| Applies to | Contracts that include the clause [@CL-SUP-1187] |

The clause states that the contractor shall screen Government-Industry Data Exchange Program (GIDEP) reports as part of its inspection system or quality-control program, to avoid the use and delivery of counterfeit or suspect counterfeit items [@CL-SUP-1187]. As a part of that inspection system or program, the clause also aims to avoid delivery of items that contain a major or critical nonconformance [@CL-SUP-1206]. The screening requirement does not apply if the contractor is a foreign corporation or partnership that has no office, place of business, or fiscal paying agent in the United States [@CL-SUP-1188].

One listed duty is to provide written notification to the contracting officer within 60 days of becoming aware or having reason to suspect, such as through inspection, testing, record review, or notification from another source [@CL-SUP-1189]. The notification duty concerns any end item, component, subassembly, part, or material contained in supplies purchased by the contractor for delivery to, or for, the Government that is counterfeit or suspect counterfeit [@CL-SUP-1190]. Except as provided in the clause's paragraph on cases where no report is submitted, the contractor shall submit a report to GIDEP within 60 days of becoming aware or having reason to suspect, as to the items the clause lists [@CL-SUP-1191]. The items that can trigger the GIDEP report duty are an item purchased by the contractor for delivery to, or for, the Government that is a counterfeit or suspect counterfeit item or a common item that has a major or critical nonconformance [@CL-SUP-1207]. The contractor shall not submit the GIDEP report required by the clause if it is a foreign corporation or partnership that does not have an office, place of business, or fiscal paying agent in the United States, which is the first of the cases listed [@CL-SUP-1208]. Another listed case in which the contractor shall not submit the GIDEP report is that the contractor is aware that the counterfeit, suspect counterfeit, or nonconforming item is the subject of an on-going criminal investigation, unless the report is approved by the cognizant law-enforcement agency [@CL-SUP-1209]. The contractor shall retain counterfeit or suspect counterfeit items in its possession at the time of discovery until the contracting officer has provided disposition instructions [@CL-SUP-1192].

Except as provided in the exclusions paragraph, the contractor shall insert the clause in subcontracts for items subject to higher-level quality standards, as one of the listed subcontract types [@CL-SUP-1193]. Another listed subcontract type is items that the contractor determines to be critical items for which use of the clause is appropriate [@CL-SUP-1210]. The clause is also to be inserted in subcontracts for materials containing electronic parts, whether or not covered in the earlier listed types, if the subcontract exceeds the simplified acquisition threshold as defined in FAR 2.101 on the date of subcontract award and the contract is by, or for, the Department of Defense [@CL-SUP-1211]. The contractor shall not insert the clause in subcontracts for commercial products and commercial services or for medical devices subject to the Food and Drug Administration reporting requirements [@CL-SUP-1194]. The printed clause lists a further subcontract type for services and bars altering the clause, and this card does not restate those terms.

## How it works

Screening is part of the contractor's inspection system or quality-control program [@CL-SUP-1187]. The notice goes to the contracting officer and the report goes to GIDEP, each on a 60-day clock that runs from becoming aware or having reason to suspect [@CL-SUP-1189; @CL-SUP-1191]. The printed clause also has definitions, a further case in which no report is submitted, a bar on trade secrets in reports and a liability protection for certain Department of Defense reports, and this card does not restate those terms. When a nonconforming item is found in supplies offered for acceptance, the rejection rules are on [Rejection of nonconforming supplies and who may accept them](../../operating-model/corrective-action/opm-rejection-of-nonconforming-supplies.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor buys 1,000 electronic parts for delivery to the Government. A record review shows that 50 parts in one lot are suspect counterfeit. Within 60 days of becoming aware, the contractor notifies the contracting officer in writing and, unless an exception applies, reports to GIDEP. It keeps the 50 parts until the contracting officer gives disposition instructions. The answer: two filings inside the 60 days, and the parts are held and not scrapped or returned.

## Never confuse

Screening GIDEP reports is a duty that sits inside the inspection program [@CL-SUP-1187], while the report to GIDEP is triggered by becoming aware or having reason to suspect [@CL-SUP-1191]. A report to GIDEP is not a rejection decision: the Government's rejection and acceptance rules are separate and are on [Rejection of nonconforming supplies and who may accept them](../../operating-model/corrective-action/opm-rejection-of-nonconforming-supplies.md). Cost allowability for counterfeit parts is a different subject, covered on [Costs of counterfeit and suspect counterfeit electronic parts](../../compliance/cas-allowability/cmp-counterfeit-electronic-part-costs.md).
