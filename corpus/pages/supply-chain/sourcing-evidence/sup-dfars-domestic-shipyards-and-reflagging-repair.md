## Purpose

Shows what shipyard information an offeror provides, how it is used in evaluation, and where reflagging or repair work must be done.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-SUP-1622] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 252.247-7026 and 252.247-7025 [@CL-SUP-1622; @CL-SUP-1625] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-1622] |
| Applies to | Solicitations that include the domestic shipyard evaluation criterion, and contracts that include the reflagging or repair work clause [@CL-SUP-1622; @CL-SUP-1625] |

The provision states that the solicitation includes an evaluation criterion that considers the extent to which the offeror has had overhaul, repair, and maintenance work for covered vessels performed in United States shipyards [@CL-SUP-1622]. The offeror shall provide with its offer the listed information on all covered vessels for which that work has been performed from the start of the current calendar year to proposal submission and in the preceding four calendar years [@CL-SUP-1623]. Offerors are responsible for submitting accurate information, which the contracting officer will use to evaluate offers in accordance with the criteria specified in the solicitation [@CL-SUP-1624]. The contracting officer also reserves the right to request supporting documentation if determined necessary in the proposal evaluation process [@CL-SUP-9764].

Under the reflagging clause, unless DoD waives the requirement, the contractor shall ensure that reflagging or repair work is performed in the United States or its outlying areas if the work meets the conditions listed in the clause [@CL-SUP-1625]. The listed conditions are that the work is performed on a vessel for which the contractor submitted an offer in response to the solicitation, and that it is performed before the Government accepts the vessel [@CL-SUP-1626]. The second item of the same requirement paragraph is corrective and preventive maintenance or repair work for the duration of the contract, to the greatest extent practicable [@CL-SUP-1688]; the paragraph's lead-in applies to it as well, so the contractor shall ensure that work is performed in the United States or its outlying areas unless DoD waives the requirement [@CL-SUP-1625].

## How it works

The preference works through evaluation: the criterion looks at where the offeror has had work done on its covered vessels [@CL-SUP-1622]. The offer carries the listed information for the current calendar year and the four preceding calendar years, and the contracting officer uses it to evaluate offers by the criteria in the solicitation [@CL-SUP-1623; @CL-SUP-1624]. The reflagging clause requires, unless DoD waives the requirement, that the contractor ensure performance of reflagging or repair work in the United States or its outlying areas [@CL-SUP-1625].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

Assume an offeror has two covered vessels and that the work below meets the provision's definition of overhaul, repair, and maintenance work. In the period it had 3 million of overhaul work done in United States shipyards on one vessel and 1 million of emergency repair done in a foreign shipyard on the other. The offer lists both, with vessel names, costs, shipyards and dates, and says the foreign work was emergency repair. The contracting officer weighs the extent of United States shipyard work under the criteria in the solicitation. Under the reflagging clause, the contractor could not have reflagging work on a vessel it offered done abroad before Government acceptance unless DoD waived the requirement [@CL-SUP-1625; @CL-SUP-1626]; the same lead-in applies to corrective and preventive maintenance, which must be done in the United States or its outlying areas, to the greatest extent practicable, for the duration of the contract [@CL-SUP-1625; @CL-SUP-1688].

## Never confuse

The provision includes an evaluation criterion on work performed in United States shipyards and requires information with the offer [@CL-SUP-1622; @CL-SUP-1623]. The reflagging clause requires performance in the United States or its outlying areas unless DoD waives the requirement [@CL-SUP-1625]. For the rule on use of United States-flag vessels for sea transport, see [Transportation of supplies by sea: definitions and the United States-flag requirement](sup-dfars-sea-transport-us-flag-requirement.md), and for the sea transport representation and flowdown, see [Transportation of supplies by sea: representation, reporting and flowdown](sup-dfars-sea-transport-representation-and-flowdown.md). Vessel crews are covered separately; see [Riding gang member requirements and shipping documentation applications (DFARS)](sup-dfars-riding-gang-members-and-shipping-documentation.md). Liability when a contractor repairs vessels is a different clause; see [Liability and insurance for vessel repair (DFARS)](sup-dfars-ship-repair-liability-and-insurance.md). For how price and past performance are weighed in general, see [Evaluation factors: price or cost, past performance and the DoD additions](../../estimating/pricing/est-evaluation-factors-price-and-past-performance.md).
