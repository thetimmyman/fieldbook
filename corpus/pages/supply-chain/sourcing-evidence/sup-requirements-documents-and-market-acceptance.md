## Purpose

Shows how an agency selects the requirements document for a buy, the order of precedence it follows, the duty to prefer voluntary consensus standards, and the limits on requiring commercial market acceptance.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-SUP-3276] |
| Codified citation | FAR 11.101, 11.102 and 11.103 [@CL-SUP-3276; @CL-SUP-3279; @CL-SUP-3280]; FAR 2.101 [@CL-SUP-9621; @CL-SUP-9623; @CL-SUP-9625; @CL-SUP-9627; @CL-SUP-9633] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-3276] |
| Applies to | Agency selection of requirements documents, and offerors who may be asked to show market acceptance [@CL-SUP-3276; @CL-SUP-3280] |

Agencies may select from existing requirements documents, modify or combine existing documents, or create new ones to meet agency needs, consistent with an order of precedence [@CL-SUP-3276]. The order is documents mandated for use by law, then performance-oriented documents, then detailed design-oriented documents, and last standards, specifications and related publications issued by the Government outside the Defense or Federal series for the non-repetitive acquisition of items [@CL-SUP-3276; @CL-SUP-3277]. A performance work statement (PWS) or a statement of objectives (SOO) is an example of a performance-oriented document [@CL-SUP-3326]. The FAR's general definitions say a PWS is a statement of work for performance-based acquisitions that describes the required results in clear, specific and objective terms with measurable outcomes [@CL-SUP-9625], and a SOO is a Government-prepared document incorporated into the solicitation that states the overall performance objectives [@CL-SUP-9627]. The RFO model text of FAR 2.101 defines both in the same words [@CL-SUP-9626; @CL-SUP-9628]. The FAR's general definitions add that a SOO is used in solicitations when the Government intends to provide the maximum flexibility to each offeror to propose an innovative approach [@CL-SUP-9633], and the RFO model text of FAR 2.101 says the same [@CL-SUP-9634].

Agencies must use voluntary consensus standards, when they exist, in place of Government-unique standards, except where that is inconsistent with law or otherwise impractical [@CL-SUP-3278]. The private sector manages and administers voluntary consensus standards, and they are not mandated by law; ISO 9000 and IEEE 1680 are examples [@CL-SUP-3327]. The FAR's general definitions say voluntary consensus standards means common and repeated use of rules, conditions, guidelines or characteristics for products, or related processes and production methods and related management systems [@CL-SUP-9621], and that they are developed or adopted by domestic and international voluntary consensus standard making bodies, for example the International Organization for Standardization (ISO) and ASTM-International [@CL-SUP-9623]. The RFO model text of FAR 2.101 has both sentences in the same words [@CL-SUP-9622; @CL-SUP-9624]. The definition's last sentence, a pointer to another document, is left out here.

Agencies must select existing requirements documents or develop new ones that meet agency needs in accordance with the guidance in the Federal Standardization Manual, FSPM-0001 [@CL-SUP-3279]. For DoD components the guidance is DoD Manual 4120.24, Defense Standardization Program (DSP) Procedures, and for IT standards and guidance it is the Federal Information Processing Standards Publications (FIPS PUBS) [@CL-SUP-3328]. The Federal Standardization Manual may be obtained from the General Services Administration, DoD Manual 4120.24 from DoD, and FIPS PUBS from the Government Publishing Office or the Department of Commerce's National Technical Information Service [@CL-SUP-3329; @CL-SUP-3330; @CL-SUP-3331].

## How it works

In accordance with agency procedures and under appropriate circumstances, the head of an agency may require offerors to demonstrate that the items offered meet the listed conditions [@CL-SUP-3280]. Under such a requirement the items must have either achieved commercial market acceptance or been satisfactorily supplied to an agency under current or recent contracts for the same or similar requirements [@CL-SUP-3332], and they must otherwise meet the item description, specifications or other criteria prescribed in the public notice and solicitation [@CL-SUP-3333]. Appropriate circumstances may, for example, include a minimum need for an item with a demonstrated reliability, performance or product support record in a specified environment [@CL-SUP-3334]. Market acceptance is inappropriate when new or evolving items may meet the agency's needs [@CL-SUP-3281].

In developing criteria for demonstrating that an item has achieved commercial market acceptance, the contracting officer must ensure that the criteria in the solicitation meet five conditions [@CL-SUP-3335]. They must reflect the agency's minimum need and be reasonably related to showing an item's acceptability to meet that need, and they must relate to an item's performance and intended use, not an offeror's capability [@CL-SUP-3282]. They must also be supported by market research, include consideration of items supplied satisfactorily under recent or current Government contracts for the same or similar items, and consider the entire relevant commercial market, including small business concerns [@CL-SUP-3283].

Commercial market acceptance may not be used as the sole criterion for evaluating whether an item meets the Government's requirements [@CL-SUP-3284]. When it is used, the contracting officer must document the file to describe the circumstances that justify the criteria and to support the specific criteria used [@CL-SUP-3285].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An agency needs 200 pumps with a field reliability record. Under agency procedures, the head of the agency requires offerors to show that their pumps have achieved commercial market acceptance or have been satisfactorily supplied to an agency under recent contracts for the same or similar requirements [@CL-SUP-3280; @CL-SUP-3332]. The contracting officer writes criteria that reflect the minimum need, relate to the pump's performance and intended use rather than the maker's capability, rest on market research, and consider the whole commercial market including small business concerns [@CL-SUP-3282; @CL-SUP-3283]. The solicitation also evaluates the pumps against a voluntary consensus standard, so market acceptance is not the sole criterion [@CL-SUP-3278; @CL-SUP-3284]. The contracting officer documents in the file why market acceptance is justified and what supports the criteria [@CL-SUP-3285].

## Never confuse

Market acceptance is not a brand-name justification. Acceptance asks whether an item has proved itself in the market or in recent Government supply, and the head of an agency may require it under appropriate circumstances [@CL-SUP-3280; @CL-SUP-3332]. A requirement written around a brand name, product or feature peculiar to one manufacturer is a different matter, with its own conditions, justification or file documentation, and posting for acquisitions over $25,000 [@CL-SUP-3286; @CL-SUP-3288; @CL-SUP-3336; @CL-SUP-3289]; see [Brand name requirements and used, reconditioned or virgin material](../sourcing-evidence/sup-brand-name-and-material-status.md).
