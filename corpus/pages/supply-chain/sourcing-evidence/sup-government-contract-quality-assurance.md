## Purpose

Shows when and where the Government performs contract quality assurance, including inspection at destination and the policy for acquisitions at or below the simplified acquisition threshold, and why the place of inspection and acceptance matters to billing.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-SUP-0862] |
| Codified citation | FAR 46.401, 46.402, 46.403 and 46.404 [@CL-SUP-0862; @CL-SUP-0958; @CL-SUP-0866; @CL-SUP-0867] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-0862] |
| Applies to | Government contract quality assurance, with a separate policy for contracts at or below the simplified acquisition threshold [@CL-SUP-0862; @CL-SUP-0867] |

Government contract quality assurance shall be performed at such times, including any stage of manufacture or performance of services, and places, including subcontractors' plants, as may be necessary to determine that the supplies or services conform to contract requirements [@CL-SUP-0862]. Each contract shall designate the place or places where the Government reserves the right to perform quality assurance [@CL-SUP-0863]. If the contract provides for performance of Government quality assurance at source, the place or places of performance may not be changed without the authorization of the contracting officer [@CL-SUP-0954]. Agencies shall perform contract quality assurance, including inspection, at source if a listed circumstance applies, such as that performance at any other place would require uneconomical disassembly or destructive testing [@CL-SUP-0958].

Inspection shall be performed at destination under the listed circumstances, which include supplies purchased off-the-shelf that require no technical inspection and cases where necessary testing equipment is located only at destination [@CL-SUP-0866]. For contracts at or below the simplified acquisition threshold, the contracting officer shall consider, among other listed factors, the criticality of application of the supplies or services and the amount of possible losses [@CL-SUP-0867]. When the conditions in the cross-referenced paragraph apply, the listed policies govern [@CL-SUP-0868]. Under the first of them, unless a special situation exists, the Government shall inspect those contracts at destination and only for type and kind, quantity, damage, operability if readily determinable, and preservation, packaging, packing, and marking if applicable [@CL-SUP-0868; @CL-SUP-0973]. Special situations may require more detailed quality assurance and the use of a standard inspection or higher-level contract quality requirement, and those situations include those listed in the source-inspection section and contracts for items having critical applications [@CL-SUP-0956]. Detailed Government inspection may be limited to those characteristics that are special or likely to cause harm to personnel or property [@CL-SUP-0957].

## How it works

If a contract provides for delivery and acceptance at destination and the Government inspects the supplies at a place other than destination, the supplies shall not ordinarily be reinspected at destination, but should be examined for quantity, damage in transit, and possible substitution or fraud [@CL-SUP-0864]. Government inspection shall be performed by or under the direction or supervision of Government personnel [@CL-SUP-0955], and it shall be documented on an inspection or receiving report form or commercial shipping document/packing list, under agency procedures [@CL-SUP-0865]. These quality assurance sections give no billing rule, but the link between acceptance and payment is on [Payments and responsibility for supplies](../../close-revrec/revenue/cls-payments-and-responsibility-for-supplies.md), which is why the place of acceptance and the inspection report matter to billing. The conditions that trigger the small-purchase inspection policy are those of the cross-referenced paragraph, which says the Government shall not rely on inspection by the contractor if the contracting officer determines that the Government has a need to test in advance of tender or to pass judgment upon the adequacy of the contractor's internal work processes [@CL-SUP-0951]; the factors the contracting officer considers are on [Contract quality requirements](sup-contract-quality-requirements.md) [@CL-SUP-0952]. The threshold amount is on [Simplified acquisition and micro-purchase thresholds](../../compliance/regulatory-framework/cmp-acquisition-thresholds.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contract for 200 units provides for delivery and acceptance at destination, and the Government inspects the units at the supplier's plant. At the destination, the receiving office counts the units and checks for transit damage. The answer: the units are not ordinarily reinspected in full at destination, and the destination check covers quantity, damage in transit and possible substitution or fraud. The supplier keeps the inspection or receiving report with its invoice file.

## Never confuse

Government inspection is not acceptance, and it is not the contractor's own quality control, which is on [Contractor responsibilities for quality](sup-contractor-quality-responsibilities.md). Quality assurance at a subcontractor's plant is on [Quality assurance on subcontracts](sup-subcontract-quality-assurance.md). The effect of inspection and acceptance is on [Inspection of Supplies—Fixed-Price clause](../../close-revrec/revenue/cls-inspection-of-supplies-fixed-price.md), and rejection of nonconforming supplies is on [Rejection of nonconforming supplies](../../operating-model/corrective-action/opm-rejection-of-nonconforming-supplies.md).
