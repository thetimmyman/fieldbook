## Purpose

Shows what the services and construction warranties cover, when the Government must give notice, what the contractor must correct and what the Government may do if the contractor does not.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-SUP-1171] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.246-20 and 52.246-21 [@CL-SUP-1171; @CL-SUP-1172; @CL-SUP-1173; @CL-SUP-1174; @CL-SUP-1175; @CL-SUP-1176; @CL-SUP-1177; @CL-SUP-1178; @CL-SUP-1179; @CL-SUP-1221; @CL-SUP-1222; @CL-SUP-1223] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-1171] |
| Applies to | Services performed, and construction work performed, under a contract that includes the matching clause [@CL-SUP-1171; @CL-SUP-1175] |

Notwithstanding Government inspection and acceptance, the contractor warrants that all services performed under the contract will, at the time of acceptance, be free from defects in workmanship and conform to the contract requirements [@CL-SUP-1171]. The contracting officer shall give the contractor written notice of any defect or nonconformance within a period of time that the contracting officer inserts in the clause [@CL-SUP-1172]. If the contractor is required to correct or reperform, it is at no cost to the Government, and the corrected or reperformed services are subject to the clause to the same extent as work initially performed [@CL-SUP-1173]. If the contractor fails or refuses to correct or reperform, the contracting officer may, by contract or otherwise, correct or replace with similar services and charge the contractor the cost to the Government, or make an equitable adjustment in the contract price [@CL-SUP-1174]. If the Government does not require correction or reperformance, the contracting officer shall make an equitable adjustment in the contract price [@CL-SUP-1221].

For construction, the contractor warrants, except as provided in paragraph (i) of the clause, that work performed under the contract conforms to the contract requirements and is free of any defect in equipment, material, or design furnished, or workmanship performed, by the contractor or any subcontractor or supplier at any tier [@CL-SUP-1175]. That exception is that, unless a defect is caused by the negligence of the contractor or subcontractor or supplier at any tier, the contractor shall not be liable for the repair of any defects of material or design furnished by the Government [@CL-SUP-1222]. The construction warranty continues for 1 year from the date of final acceptance of the work, or for 1 year from the date the Government takes possession if it takes possession of any part of the work before final acceptance [@CL-SUP-1176]. The contractor shall remedy at its own expense any failure to conform, or any defect [@CL-SUP-1177]. The contracting officer shall notify the contractor in writing within a reasonable time after the discovery of any failure, defect, or damage [@CL-SUP-1178].

## How it works

The services clause leaves the notice period as a blank that the contracting officer fills in, while the construction clause sets it as a reasonable time after discovery [@CL-SUP-1172; @CL-SUP-1178]. If the contractor fails to remedy any failure, defect, or damage within a reasonable time after receipt of notice, the Government has the right to replace, repair, or otherwise remedy it at the contractor's expense [@CL-SUP-1179]. The construction warranty does not limit the Government's rights under the inspection and acceptance clause of the contract with respect to latent defects, gross mistakes, or fraud [@CL-SUP-1223]. The source text does not establish a standard notice period for services, because the clause leaves it for the contracting officer to fill in.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor services 200 hours of equipment, and the clause lets the contracting officer give notice within 30 days of acceptance. A defect is found on day 20, and the contracting officer gives notice and requires reperformance at no cost to the Government. For a separate building contract, a roof leak appears 8 months after final acceptance, which is inside the 1-year warranty, so the contractor remedies it at its own expense. A leak first found at 14 months is outside that warranty. The answer: the first two are covered and the last is not covered by this warranty, though the Government's inspection-clause rights for latent defects, gross mistakes or fraud are separate.

## Never confuse

The services clause warrants conformance at the time of acceptance [@CL-SUP-1171], while the construction clause warrants work for a period that runs from final acceptance or possession [@CL-SUP-1176]. The allowability of warranty costs is a separate question, covered on [Service and warranty costs](../../compliance/cas-allowability/cmp-service-and-warranty-costs.md). Inspection and acceptance are covered by separate clauses, for example [Inspection of Services—Fixed-Price clause](sup-inspection-of-services-fixed-price.md) and [Inspection of Construction clause](sup-inspection-of-construction.md). The supplies warranty is a different clause, covered on [Warranty of supplies of a noncomplex nature](sup-warranty-noncomplex-supplies.md). The rule that lets the contracting officer insert each clause is on [Warranty contract clauses and their triggers](sup-warranty-contract-clauses.md), and the criteria for choosing a warranty are on [Warranties: criteria, terms and remedies](sup-warranties.md).
