## Purpose

Shows when the Government assumes the risk of loss to aircraft in a contractor's hands, what share the contractor still bears, which losses fall outside the Government's assumption, how the Government's assumption is revoked, and which duties and flowdown rules the contractor must meet.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-SUP-1405] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 252.228-7001 [@CL-SUP-1405; @CL-SUP-1407; @CL-SUP-1442] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-1405] |
| Applies to | Covered aircraft as the clause defines them [@CL-SUP-1404] |

In the clause, covered aircraft means an aircraft owned by or to be delivered to the Government, and, when the contracting officer determines it and the contract Schedule specifically identifies it, may include contractor-furnished aircraft that are not intended for induction into the DoD inventory [@CL-SUP-1404]. The Government self-insures and assumes the risk of damage to, or loss or destruction of, covered aircraft subject to listed conditions [@CL-SUP-1405]. One listed condition for flight is that the Government flight representative has approved the crewmembers and the flight in writing [@CL-SUP-1406].

## How it works

Except for loss or damage caused by negligence of Government personnel, the contractor's share of loss or damage to covered aircraft is the least of the amounts the paragraph lists [@CL-SUP-1442]. Those are a dollar figure, a percentage of the price or estimated acquisition cost of the affected aircraft, and a percentage of the price or estimated cost of the contract, task order or delivery order [@CL-SUP-1407]. The Government's assumption does not extend to loss from willful misconduct or lack of good faith by the contractor's managerial personnel, including its oversight of subcontractors [@CL-SUP-1408]. Other listed exclusions include loss covered by insurance, loss that occurs after the contracting officer has revoked the assumption of risk in writing, and loss sustained due to workmanship errors [@CL-SUP-1408; @CL-SUP-1409]. The contractor must comply with the combined regulation/instruction named in the clause from contract award throughout performance, whether or not the Government has assumed the risk [@CL-SUP-1410].

The assumption can be revoked. When the contracting officer finds that the contractor's managerial personnel failed to comply with paragraph (b) of the clause, the paragraph that requires compliance with the combined regulation/instruction [@CL-SUP-1410], the contracting officer will issue a preliminary notice of revocation requiring the contractor to comply with contract requirements within a timeframe specified by the contracting officer [@CL-SUP-1446]. If the contracting officer finds that the contractor failed to correct the cited noncompliance or conditions within that timeframe, the contracting officer will issue a notice of revocation of the assumption of risk for any covered aircraft [@CL-SUP-1447]. After a notice of revocation, the contractor shall thereafter assume the entire risk for the previously covered aircraft [@CL-SUP-1448].

Unless the flight and crewmembers have been approved in writing by the Government flight representative, the contractor shall not be reimbursed for liability to third parties for property loss or damage, death, or bodily injury caused by covered aircraft during flight, even if the Government has accepted such liability under any other provisions of the contract [@CL-SUP-1449; @CL-SUP-4028]. For flowdown, the contractor shall incorporate the requirements of the clause in subcontracts, including subcontracts for commercial products and commercial services, subject to the exceptions that follow [@CL-SUP-1450]. Both exceptions apply: the contractor shall not include paragraph (f) of the clause in subcontracts for commercial products or commercial services [@CL-SUP-1451], and the contractor shall not incorporate the requirements of the clause in subcontracts with Federal Aviation Administration part 145 repair stations performing work pursuant to their FAA license [@CL-SUP-4006].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor holds a Government-furnished aircraft and the contract is worth 3,000,000. Twenty percent of the contract is 600,000 and twenty percent of the aircraft price is 400,000. The least of 200,000, 400,000 and 600,000 is 200,000, so the contractor's share of a covered loss is 200,000 [@CL-SUP-1442; @CL-SUP-1407]. If the loss came from a workmanship error, the Government's assumption would not apply at all [@CL-SUP-1409].

## Never confuse

The contractor's share is the least of the listed amounts [@CL-SUP-1442; @CL-SUP-1407]. The Government's assumption of risk does not extend to the excluded losses [@CL-SUP-1408; @CL-SUP-1409]. A preliminary notice of revocation requires compliance within a timeframe specified by the contracting officer [@CL-SUP-1446]; the notice of revocation is issued if the contracting officer finds the contractor failed to correct the cited noncompliance or conditions within that timeframe [@CL-SUP-1447]. For the general property clause, see [Responsibility and liability for Government property](../../close-revrec/fixed-assets/cls-responsibility-liability-government-property.md). For the Government's assumption of vessel risk, see [Liability and insurance for vessel repair](sup-dfars-ship-repair-liability-and-insurance.md). For insurance costs generally, see [Insurance and indemnification](../../compliance/cas-allowability/cmp-insurance-and-indemnification.md).
