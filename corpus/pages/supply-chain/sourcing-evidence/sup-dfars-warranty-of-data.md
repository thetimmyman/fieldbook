## Purpose

Shows what a contractor warrants about delivered technical data, how long the warranty runs, what the Government may do about a breach, and how the two alternates add contractor liability for damages.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-SUP-1381] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 252.246-7001 [@CL-SUP-1381; @CL-SUP-1382] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-1381] |
| Applies to | Technical data delivered under a contract that includes the clause [@CL-SUP-1381] |

In the basic clause, the contractor warrants that all technical data delivered under the contract will conform with the specifications and all other requirements of the contract at the time of delivery [@CL-SUP-1381]. The warranty period is three years after delivery of the data line item is complete, or any longer period the contract specifies [@CL-SUP-1382].

## How it works

The contractor must notify the contracting officer in writing immediately of any breach it discovers within the warranty period [@CL-SUP-1383]. The listed remedies apply to all breaches, whether the contractor gives that notice or the Government notifies the contractor of the breach in writing within the warranty period [@CL-SUP-1431]. Within a reasonable time after notification, the contracting officer may direct the contractor by written notice to correct or replace the nonconforming data promptly at the contractor's expense [@CL-SUP-1384]. As a listed alternative, the contracting officer may inform the contractor that the Government elects a price or fee adjustment, when the Government no longer needs correction or replacement or can more reasonably correct the data itself [@CL-SUP-1384; @CL-SUP-1385].

If the contractor refuses or fails to comply with a direction to correct or replace, the contracting officer may act within a reasonable time of the refusal or failure [@CL-SUP-1432]. The two listed actions are to correct or replace the data by contract or otherwise and charge the cost to the contractor, or to elect a price or fee adjustment instead [@CL-SUP-1433]. In the basic clause, these remedies represent the only way to enforce the Government's rights under the clause [@CL-SUP-1434].

The two alternates add contractor liability for damages [@CL-SUP-1386; @CL-SUP-1387]. Alternate I limits the additional liability to 75 percent of the target profit [@CL-SUP-1386]. Alternate II limits it to ten percent of the total contract price [@CL-SUP-1387]. Alternate I lists cases in which the additional liability does not apply [@CL-SUP-1435]. In Alternates I and II, one listed exception covers defects that the contractor discovers and gives written notice of to the Government before the Government discovers the error [@CL-SUP-1435; @CL-SUP-1436]. The alternates list further exceptions that this card does not restate.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor finishes a data line item on a set date and the contract sets no longer period. The warranty then runs three years from that date. In year two the contractor finds a drawing error and tells the contracting officer in writing at once. The contracting officer directs the contractor to correct the drawing at the contractor's expense. If the contractor refused, the contracting officer could correct the drawing by contract or otherwise and charge the cost to the contractor. Under Alternate I, if damages also arose, the additional liability would be capped at a share of target profit. If target profit were 100,000, the cap would be 75,000.

## Never confuse

This clause warrants technical data only, and the FAR warranty clauses are covered on separate cards; see [Warranties: criteria, terms and remedies](sup-warranties.md), [Warranty contract clauses and their triggers](sup-warranty-contract-clauses.md) and [Warranty of systems and equipment](sup-warranty-systems-and-equipment.md). The basic clause and the alternates differ on damages: in the basic clause the listed remedies are the only way to enforce the Government's rights [@CL-SUP-1434], while Alternate I and Alternate II add liability for all damages with different limits [@CL-SUP-1386; @CL-SUP-1387].
