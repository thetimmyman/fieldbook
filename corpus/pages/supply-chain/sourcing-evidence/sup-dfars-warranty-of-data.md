## Purpose

Shows what a contractor warrants about delivered technical data, how long the warranty runs, what the Government may do about a breach, and how the two alternates add contractor liability for damages.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-SUP-1381] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 252.246-7001 [@CL-SUP-1381; @CL-SUP-1382] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-1381] |
| Applies to | Technical data delivered under a contract that includes the clause [@CL-SUP-1381] |

In the basic clause, notwithstanding inspection and acceptance by the Government of technical data furnished under the contract, and notwithstanding any provision of the contract concerning the conclusiveness of acceptance [@CL-SUP-9772], the contractor warrants that all technical data delivered under the contract will conform with the specifications and all other requirements of the contract at the time of delivery [@CL-SUP-1381]. The warranty period extends for three years after completion of the delivery of the line item of data of which the data forms a part, or for any longer period specified in the contract [@CL-SUP-1382].

## How it works

The contractor agrees to notify the contracting officer in writing immediately of any breach of the warranty that it discovers within the warranty period [@CL-SUP-1383]. The listed remedies apply to all breaches, whether the contractor gives that notice or the Government notifies the contractor of the breach in writing within the warranty period [@CL-SUP-1431]. Within a reasonable time after notification, the contracting officer may direct the contractor by written notice to correct or replace the nonconforming data promptly at the contractor's expense [@CL-SUP-1384]. As the other listed alternative, within that time the contracting officer may, if the contracting officer determines that the Government no longer has a requirement for correction or replacement of the data, or that the data can be more reasonably corrected by the Government, inform the contractor by written notice that the Government elects a price or fee adjustment instead of correction or replacement [@CL-SUP-1384; @CL-SUP-1385; @CL-SUP-4165].

If the contractor refuses or fails to comply with a direction to correct or replace, the contracting officer may act within a reasonable time of the refusal or failure [@CL-SUP-1432]. The contracting officer may do one of two listed things: correct or replace the nonconforming data by contract or otherwise and charge the cost to the contractor, or elect a price or fee adjustment instead of correction or replacement [@CL-SUP-1433]. In the basic clause, the remedies in the clause represent the only way to enforce the Government's rights under the clause [@CL-SUP-1434].

The two alternates add contractor liability for all damages to the Government as a result of the breach of warranty, in addition to the remedies under paragraphs (d)(1) and (2) of the clause [@CL-SUP-4166; @CL-SUP-1386; @CL-SUP-1387]. Under Alternate I, the additional liability shall not exceed 75 percent of the target profit [@CL-SUP-1386]. Under Alternate II, it shall not exceed ten percent of the total contract price [@CL-SUP-1387]. In both alternates, if the breach of the warranty is with respect to data supplied by an equipment subcontractor, the limit of the contractor's liability is the one the clause lists for that case [@CL-SUP-9771]. Alternate I lists cases in which the additional liability does not apply, and one of those alternatives covers defects that the contractor discovers and gives written notice of to the Government before the Government discovers the error [@CL-SUP-1435; @CL-SUP-1436]. Alternate II also lists cases in which it does not apply [@CL-SUP-9770], and one of those alternatives is the same exception for defects the contractor discovers and gives written notice of before the Government discovers the error [@CL-SUP-9769].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor finishes a data line item on a set date and the contract sets no longer period. The warranty then runs three years from that date. In year two the contractor finds a drawing error and tells the contracting officer in writing at once, before the Government has found it. The contracting officer directs the contractor to correct the drawing at the contractor's expense. If the contractor refused, the contracting officer could correct the drawing by contract or otherwise and charge the cost to the contractor. Because the contractor found and reported this error first, the Alternate I additional damages liability does not apply to it. Later in year two the Government finds a different error in a test procedure and notifies the contractor in writing, and the contract uses Alternate I. For that Government-found error, the contractor is also liable for damages, capped at 75 percent of target profit: if target profit were 100,000, the cap would be 75,000.

## Never confuse

This clause applies to technical data delivered under a contract that includes it [@CL-SUP-1381], and the FAR warranty clauses are covered on separate cards; see [Warranties: criteria, terms and remedies](sup-warranties.md), [Warranty contract clauses and their triggers](sup-warranty-contract-clauses.md) and [Warranty of systems and equipment](sup-warranty-systems-and-equipment.md). The basic clause and the alternates differ on damages: in the basic clause the listed remedies are the only way to enforce the Government's rights [@CL-SUP-1434], while Alternate I and Alternate II add liability for all damages with different limits [@CL-SUP-1386; @CL-SUP-1387].
