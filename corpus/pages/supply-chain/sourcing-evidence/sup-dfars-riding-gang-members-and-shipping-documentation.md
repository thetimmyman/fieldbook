## Purpose

Shows who may serve as a riding gang member, the vetting of exempt individuals, and how the contractor applies for shipping documentation.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-SUP-1627] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 252.247-7027 and 252.247-7028 [@CL-SUP-1627; @CL-SUP-1632] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | MAY 2018 [@CL-SUP-9860] |
| Text status | Final [@CL-SUP-1627] |
| Applies to | Contracts that include the riding gang member clause, and contracts that include the shipping documentation application clause [@CL-SUP-1627; @CL-SUP-1632] |

Notwithstanding section 8106 of title 46, United States Code, the contractor shall ensure that each riding gang member holds a valid United States Merchant Mariner's Document issued under chapter 73 of that title, or a transportation security card issued under section 70105 of that title [@CL-SUP-1627]. An individual is exempt from the credential requirement, and is not treated as a riding gang member for the statutory purpose the clause names, if the individual is on a vessel for purposes other than operating or maintaining it and falls within a listed category [@CL-SUP-1628]. The categories are alternatives: supercargo personnel, one of the force protection personnel of the vessel, a specialized repair technician, or an individual otherwise required by the Secretary of Defense or designee to be aboard the vessel [@CL-SUP-1629].

Any individual who is exempt must pass a DoD background check before going aboard the vessel [@CL-SUP-1630]. The contractor shall render all necessary assistance to United States Armed Forces personnel with respect to the identification and screening of exempted individuals [@CL-SUP-4007; @CL-SUP-1689]. That assistance will require, at a minimum, that the contractor submit the name and other biographical information necessary to the Government official specified in the contract for the purposes of conducting a background check [@CL-SUP-1695]. The contractor shall deny access or immediately remove from the vessel any individual deemed unsuitable for any reason by the Government agency conducting the background checks, and shall promptly replace that individual with a replacement who fully complies with all screening requirements [@CL-SUP-1690]. The head of the contracting activity may waive the background check requirement for an exempt individual if the individual possesses a valid United States Merchant Mariner's Document issued under chapter 73 of title 46, United States Code, or a transportation security card issued under section 70105 of that title [@CL-SUP-1631].

## How it works

Each riding gang member must hold the credential [@CL-SUP-1627]; an individual is exempt from that requirement if on a vessel for purposes other than engaging in the operation or maintenance of the vessel and within a listed category [@CL-SUP-1628]. Vetting applies to the exempt individuals, who must pass a DoD background check before going aboard, with the contractor submitting their details and removing and replacing anyone the screening agency finds unsuitable [@CL-SUP-1630; @CL-SUP-1695; @CL-SUP-1690]. Separately, except as the automated-system paragraph provides, the contractor requests bills of lading by submitting a DD Form 1659 to the transportation officer, if named in the contract schedule, or to the contract administration office [@CL-SUP-1632; @CL-SUP-1691]. If an automated system is available for shipment requests, use service or agency systems in lieu of DD Form 1659 [@CL-SUP-1633; @CL-SUP-9767].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A charter carries two specialized repair technicians who, assume, are aboard only to repair mission equipment, and four other individuals who, assume, are riding gang members. Those four must hold the mariner's document or the security card [@CL-SUP-1627]. The technicians fall in a listed exempt category, so they need no such credential, but each must pass the DoD background check before boarding, and the contractor sends their names and biographical details to the Government official named in the contract. If the screening agency finds one technician unsuitable, the contractor keeps that person off the vessel and promptly replaces the technician with someone who meets all the screening requirements. For the cargo, the contractor submits a DD Form 1659 to one of the two listed offices, unless an automated service or agency system is available for the shipment request.

## Never confuse

An exempt individual is not treated as a riding gang member for the statutory purpose the clause names [@CL-SUP-1628]. The credential requirement applies to each riding gang member [@CL-SUP-1627], and the background check applies to any individual who is exempt [@CL-SUP-1630]. The shipping documentation application concerns bills of lading [@CL-SUP-1632]; for the ocean bill of lading copy sent after a shipment, see [Transportation of supplies by sea: representation, reporting and flowdown](sup-dfars-sea-transport-representation-and-flowdown.md). For the reflagging and domestic shipyard clauses on vessel contracts, see [Domestic shipyard evaluation preference and reflagging or repair work (DFARS)](sup-dfars-domestic-shipyards-and-reflagging-repair.md). A motor carrier fuel surcharge is another shipping-side duty handled elsewhere; see [Hardship conditions and motor carrier fuel surcharge pass-through (DFARS)](sup-dfars-hardship-conditions-and-fuel-surcharge.md).
