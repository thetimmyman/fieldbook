## Purpose

Shows, under 13 CFR 121.404 [@CL-MKT-0383], the date on which a concern's size status is measured for a contract, a multiple award contract, an order, a subcontract and a follow-on contract.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Not established. |
| Codified citation | Not established. |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Not established. |
| Applies to | Concerns that self-certify as small on a contract under its assigned NAICS code [@CL-MKT-0383] |

A concern, including its affiliates, must qualify as small under the NAICS code assigned to a contract as of the date it submits a written self-certification that it is small to the procuring activity as part of its initial offer or response that includes price [@CL-MKT-0383].

Once awarded a contract as a small business, a firm is generally considered to be a small business throughout the life of that contract [@CL-MKT-0525].

A follow-on or renewal contract is a new contracting action, so size is determined as of the date the concern submits a written self-certification that it is small as part of its initial offer, including price, for that contract [@CL-MKT-0388].

## How it works

For a multiple award contract with a single NAICS code, SBA determines size status for the underlying contract as of the date of the initial offer, which includes price [@CL-MKT-0384]. Where an order is set aside for small business under an unrestricted multiple award contract, SBA determines size for each order as of the date of the initial offer or other formal response, which includes price, for each order [@CL-MKT-0385; @CL-MKT-9403]. The paragraph on set-aside or reserved contracts addresses an order issued under a multiple award contract that itself was set aside or reserved for small business [@CL-MKT-0543]. For such an order, size is determined as of the initial offer, including price, for the set-aside or reserved contract, unless a contracting officer requests size recertification for a specific order [@CL-MKT-0386]. Where a contracting officer requests size recertification for a specific order, size is determined as of the date of the initial offer or other formal response, including price, for the order [@CL-MKT-0548]. For a subcontract, a concern must qualify as small as of the date it certifies that it is small for the subcontract [@CL-MKT-0387].

The same section lists exceptions that can displace these order rules. For orders or BPAs to be placed against the GSA Federal Supply Schedule Multiple Award Schedule contract, size is determined as of the date of the initial offer, which includes price, for that contract [@CL-MKT-0544]. Size is determined on the date of recertification when a recertification is required under the cited provisions, or on the date of initial offer, which includes price, if a contracting officer requests it under the cited provision [@CL-MKT-0545]. That exception applies to all provisions of paragraphs (a), (b), (c) and (d) of the section [@CL-MKT-9404]. The exceptions also cover sole source orders in the Business Development program, which this card does not detail.

A concern must self-certify that it is small under the size standard specified in the solicitation, or as clarified, completed or supplied by SBA pursuant to section 121.402(d) [@CL-MKT-2359]. A contracting officer may accept the concern's self-certification as true for the particular procurement involved in the absence of a written protest by other offerors or other credible information which causes the contracting officer or SBA to question the size of the concern [@CL-MKT-2360].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A concern has 400 employees when it submits its initial offer with price, and its size standard is 500 employees. Later the concern grows to 900 employees. For this contract, size is measured at the offer date, so the concern qualified as small, and the later growth does not change that measure.

## Never confuse

The date on which size is measured is a different question from how an acquisition changes the receipts or employees counted. The section text printed for this card does not address mergers or the purchase of another firm, so this card does not establish their effect. See [SBA size after an acquisition](../../mna/screening/mna-sba-size-after-acquisition.md) for the receipts and employees rules. The size standard itself comes from the NAICS code named in the solicitation, which [Which size standard applies to a federal procurement](../taxonomy/mkt-sba-size-standard-designation.md) covers. How subcontract size is certified, and the penalties for misrepresenting it, are on [Size misrepresentation penalties and subcontract size](../taxonomy/mkt-sba-size-misrepresentation-penalties-and-subcontract-size.md).
