## Purpose

Lists, under 13 CFR 121.1001 and 121.1004 [@CL-MKT-0405; @CL-MKT-0409], who may file a size protest and how long each kind of protest has.

| Applies to | Size protests about a particular procurement or order [@CL-MKT-0405] |

For SBA's Small Business Set-Aside Program, including the Property Sales Program [@CL-MKT-9405], or any instance in which a procurement or order has been restricted to or reserved for small businesses or a particular group of small businesses, including a partial set-aside, the entities listed in the paragraph may file a size protest in connection with a particular procurement, sale or order [@CL-MKT-0405]. The list includes any offeror that the contracting officer has not eliminated from consideration for any procurement-related reason, such as non-responsiveness, technical unacceptability or outside of the competitive range, and the contracting officer [@CL-MKT-0405; @CL-MKT-0406]. It also includes the SBA Government Contracting Area Director having responsibility for the area in which the headquarters of the protested offeror is located, regardless of the location of a parent company or affiliates, the Director, Office of Government Contracting, or the Associate General Counsel for Procurement Law; and other interested parties [@CL-MKT-0405; @CL-MKT-0407; @CL-MKT-1262]. Other interested parties include large businesses where only one concern submitted an offer for the specific procurement in question [@CL-MKT-0533]. A concern found to be other than small in connection with the procurement is not an interested party unless there is only one remaining offeror after the concern is found to be other than small [@CL-MKT-0534]. For any unrestricted Government procurement in which a business concern has represented itself as a small business concern, any offeror, the contracting officer, and the responsible SBA Government Contracting Area Director, the Director, Office of Government Contracting, the Associate Administrator for Business Development, or the Associate General Counsel for Procurement Law may protest in connection with a particular procurement [@CL-MKT-0408; @CL-MKT-1263].

A protest concerning the contract that is keyed to bid opening must be received by the contracting officer before the close of business on the fifth day, exclusive of Saturdays, Sundays and legal holidays, after bid opening [@CL-MKT-0409]. Where the contracting officer notifies the protestor of the identity of the prospective awardee, a protest must be received by the contracting officer before the close of business on the fifth day, exclusive of Saturdays, Sundays and legal holidays, after that notice, for a protest concerning the contract [@CL-MKT-0410].

## How it works

The deadline is counted in days that exclude Saturdays, Sundays and legal holidays, and it is the date of receipt by the contracting officer that matters [@CL-MKT-0409; @CL-MKT-0410]. The time limits do not apply to contracting officers, funding agreement officers or SBA, who may file before or after awards, except to the extent set forth in the rule on premature protests, except that for the SBIR and STTR programs the funding agreement officer or SBA may file a protest in anticipation of an award [@CL-MKT-0411; @CL-MKT-1840; @CL-MKT-0535]. A protest filed by any party, including the contracting officer, before bid opening or notification to offerors of the selection of the apparent successful offer will be dismissed as premature [@CL-MKT-0535]. A protest received after the allotted time limits must still be forwarded to SBA, and SBA will dismiss untimely protests [@CL-MKT-0536]. The section also covers orders against multiple award contracts, long-term contracts, option periods, recertifications, electronic notice and bid protest corrective action, which this card does not detail.

A protest must be delivered to the contracting officer by hand, telegram, mail, facsimile, Federal Express or other overnight delivery service, e-mail, or telephone [@CL-MKT-2392]. If a protest is made by telephone, the contracting officer must later receive a confirming letter either within the 5-day period in section 121.1004(a)(1) or postmarked no later than one day after the date of the telephone protest [@CL-MKT-2393].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A negotiated procurement notifies a protestor of the prospective awardee on a Monday. The Wednesday of that week is a legal holiday. Counting Tuesday, Thursday, Friday, the next Monday and the next Tuesday, the fifth day is that second Tuesday. The protest must be received by the contracting officer by the close of business that day.

## Never confuse

A protest is not the same as a request for a formal size determination, although, when no bid protest is pending, SBA's time frame for issuing a formal size determination runs from receipt of either one [@CL-MKT-0416]. This card lists protesters and the time limits from the SBA rule. The FAR side of protests is in [Small business size protests](../taxonomy/mkt-small-business-size-protests.md). What SBA does after it receives a protest is in [How SBA handles a size protest and decides size](../taxonomy/mkt-sba-size-determination-procedure.md). The protest of a subcontractor's self-certification has its own list of parties, which [Size misrepresentation penalties and subcontract size](../taxonomy/mkt-sba-size-misrepresentation-penalties-and-subcontract-size.md) covers.
