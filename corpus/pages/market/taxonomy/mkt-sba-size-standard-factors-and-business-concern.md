## Purpose

Shows the factors SBA weighs when it establishes size standards and each element of the definition of a business concern, under 13 CFR 121.102 and 121.105 [@CL-MKT-0655; @CL-MKT-0658].

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Not established. |
| Codified citation | Not established. |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |

| Applies to | Business concerns that want SBA assistance as a small business, with a separate definition for small agricultural cooperatives [@CL-MKT-0658; @CL-MKT-0660] |

In establishing size standards, SBA considers the economic characteristics that make up the structure of an industry: degree of competition, average firm size, start-up costs and entry barriers, and the distribution of firms by size [@CL-MKT-0655]. The text also names technological changes [@CL-MKT-0655]. It lists competition from other industries, growth trends, historical activity within the industry, unique factors that may distinguish small firms from other firms, and the objectives of SBA's programs and the impact on those programs of different size standard levels [@CL-MKT-0656]. When SBA reviews a size standard, it investigates whether any concern at or below the standard would be dominant in the industry [@CL-MKT-0657].

Except for small agricultural cooperatives, a business concern eligible for assistance from SBA as a small business is a business entity organized for profit [@CL-MKT-0658]. In the same definition, the entity must have a place of business located in the United States and either operate primarily within the United States or make a significant contribution to the U.S. economy through payment of taxes or use of American products, materials or labor [@CL-MKT-0659]. A business concern may be in the legal form of an individual proprietorship, partnership, limited liability company, corporation, joint venture, association, trust or cooperative [@CL-MKT-0661]. Where the form is a joint venture, no more than 49 percent participation by foreign business entities is allowed [@CL-MKT-0662].

## How it works

The definition has several elements. Unless it is a small agricultural cooperative, the concern must be an entity organized for profit, with a United States place of business, that operates primarily in the United States or contributes significantly to its economy [@CL-MKT-0658; @CL-MKT-0659]. A small agricultural cooperative is instead an association acting pursuant to the Agricultural Marketing Act whose size does not exceed the size standard SBA established for other similar agricultural small business concerns [@CL-MKT-0660]. A firm is not treated as a separate business concern if a substantial portion of its assets or liabilities are the same as those of a predecessor entity, and the predecessor's annual receipts and employees are then taken into account in determining size [@CL-MKT-0663]. The printed text gives no industry size standard figures, so this card gives none.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A firm formed this year takes over most of the assets and liabilities of an older firm that had 300 employees. The new firm has 40 employees of its own. Because a substantial portion of its assets and liabilities are the same as the predecessor's, the 300 employees of the predecessor are counted in deciding the new firm's size, so it cannot rely on its own 40.

## Never confuse

The factors SBA weighs when it sets a standard are not the size test applied to one concern, and the definition of a business concern says who can be measured, not what the limit is [@CL-MKT-0658]. How size standards, NAICS codes and appeals of the contracting officer's code work is on the [Size standards, NAICS codes and appeals of the contracting officer's code](mkt-size-standards-and-naics-codes.md) card, which this card does not restate. A Federal agency that finds SBA's standard unsuitable for its programs may, in limited circumstances, set its own definition; that is on the [SBIR and STTR size timing, self-certification and agency size standards](mkt-sbir-sttr-size-timing-and-agency-size-standards.md) card.
