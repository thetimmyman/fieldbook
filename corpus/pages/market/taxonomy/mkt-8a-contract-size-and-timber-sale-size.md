## Purpose

Shows the size certification and SBA review for a Participant in the 8(a) Business Development program on a particular subcontract, and the size standard for buying Government-owned timber, under 13 CFR 121.603 and 121.507 [@CL-MKT-0680; @CL-MKT-0684].

## The rule

| Applies to | 8(a) Participants offering on a particular 8(a) subcontract, and concerns buying Government-owned timber other than Special Salvage Timber [@CL-MKT-0680; @CL-MKT-0684] |

A Participant must certify that it qualifies as a small business under the NAICS code assigned to a particular 8(a) subcontract as part of its initial offer including price to the procuring agency [@CL-MKT-0680]. Within 30 days of receiving the self-certification, the SBA district office serving the area of the Participant's principal office reviews it and determines whether the Participant is small [@CL-MKT-0681]. A Participant may request a formal size determination from the SBA Government Contracting Area Office serving the geographic area in which its principal office is located within 5 working days of receiving notice from the SBA district office that it is not small for the particular 8(a) subcontract [@CL-MKT-0886; @CL-MKT-0887]. If a Participant found other than small does not timely request a formal size determination, SBA may accept the procurement in support of another Participant, or may rescind its acceptance of the offer for the 8(a) Business Development program, as appropriate [@CL-MKT-0713].

To be small for the sale of Government-owned timber other than Special Salvage Timber, a concern must, among other requirements, be primarily engaged in the logging or forest products industry and not exceed 500 employees, taking into account its affiliates [@CL-MKT-0684]. One listed condition for a concern that intends at the time of offer to resell the timber is to agree not to sell more than 30 percent of the timber, or 50 percent if it is an Alaskan business, to a business that does not meet the stated industry and employee requirements [@CL-MKT-0685]. A concern that buys timber under a small business set-aside, other than through the Special Salvage Timber Sale program, must keep a record for three years [@CL-MKT-0686]. The record lists the name, address and size status of every concern to which it sells the timber or sawlogs, and the species, grades and volumes of sawlogs sold [@CL-MKT-0715].

## How it works

Once SBA verifies the selected Participant as small, later changes in size up to the date of award do not affect its status for that procurement, except changes due to merger with or acquisition by another business concern [@CL-MKT-0682]. After such a merger or acquisition between self-certification and award, the concern must recertify its size status, and SBA must verify the new certification before award can occur [@CL-MKT-0683]. Recertification is not required when the ownership of a concern that is at least 51 percent owned by a tribe, an Alaska Native Corporation or a Community Development Corporation changes to or from a wholly-owned business concern of the same entity, as long as the ultimate owner remains that entity [@CL-MKT-0888]. The measurement date for a sale or lease of Government property, which includes the Timber Sales Program, is in the paragraph below, and the date for other contracts is on the [When a concern's size status is measured](mkt-sba-size-measurement-date.md) card.

Sections 121.501 through 121.512 apply to small business size determinations for the purpose of the sale or lease of Government property, including the Timber Sales Program, the Special Salvage Timber Sales Program, and the sale of Government petroleum, coal and uranium [@CL-MKT-2366]. Formal size determinations based upon a specific Government sale or lease, or made in response to a request from another Government agency under section 121.901, are binding upon the parties [@CL-MKT-2367]. Other SBA opinions provided to contracting officers or others are only advisory, and are not binding or appealable [@CL-MKT-2368]. For a sale or lease, SBA determines the size status of a concern, including its affiliates, as of the date the concern submits a written self-certification that it is small to the Government as part of its initial offer including price where there is a specific sale or lease at issue, or as set forth in section 121.903 if the determination is made in response to a request of another Government agency [@CL-MKT-2369; @CL-MKT-2370]. A contracting officer may accept the concern's self-certification as true for the particular sale or lease involved, in the absence of a written protest by other offerors or other credible information which would cause the contracting officer or SBA to question the size of the concern [@CL-MKT-2371].

For sales or leases of Government-owned timber, forest product industry means logging, wood preserving, and the manufacture of lumber and wood related products such as veneer, plywood, hardboard, particle board, or wood pulp, and of products of which lumber or wood related products are the principal raw materials [@CL-MKT-2372]. To purchase Government-owned Special Salvage Timber from the United States Forest Service or the Bureau of Land Management as a small business, a concern must meet the conditions listed in the paragraph, which include being primarily engaged in the logging or forest product industry and having, together with its affiliates, no more than twenty-five employees during any pay period for the last twelve months [@CL-MKT-2373; @CL-MKT-2374]. The paragraph lists further conditions that this card does not state.

An applicant must not exceed the size standard corresponding to its primary industry classification in order to qualify for admission to SBA's 8(a) Business Development Program [@CL-MKT-2375]. It must be small for its primary industry at the time SBA certifies it for admission into the program [@CL-MKT-2376]. A concern which SBA determines to be a small business for the award of an 8(a) Business Development subcontract will be considered to have met applicable size eligibility requirements of other SBA programs where that assistance directly and primarily relates to the performance of that subcontract [@CL-MKT-2377].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A Participant certifies that it is small, with its initial offer, on day 0. SBA verifies on day 20. The Participant grows from 80 to 150 employees by day 60, without any merger or acquisition, and receives the award on day 90. Its size status for that procurement is unchanged. If it had instead been acquired by another firm on day 60, it would have to recertify, and SBA would have to verify the new certification before award, unless the transfer was to or from a wholly-owned business concern of the same tribe, Alaska Native Corporation or Community Development Corporation that already owned at least 51 percent of it and remained its ultimate owner.

## Never confuse

The Government timber-sale standard measures a buyer purchasing timber sold by the Government [@CL-MKT-0684], rather than a contractor selling supplies or services to the Government under procurement NAICS codes on [Size standards, NAICS codes and appeals of the contracting officer's code](mkt-size-standards-and-naics-codes.md) [@CL-MKT-0200], while an 8(a) participant's contract-specific size certification triggers a 30-day SBA district office review [@CL-MKT-0680; @CL-MKT-0681].

## Company calibration

Whether the company is or plans to be a Participant in the Business Development program, and whether it would ever buy Government timber. [@Q-019]
