## Purpose

Shows the factors an agency should weigh before it offers a requirement for 8(a) support, and the rules for orders under multiple-award contracts held by 8(a) contractors, including when separate offers and acceptances are not required for individual orders and when the contracting officer may place an order on a sole source basis or directly with one 8(a) contractor [@CL-MKT-1645; @CL-MKT-1652; @CL-MKT-1656; @CL-MKT-1658].

Agency evaluation. In deciding how far a requirement should be offered in support of the 8(a) program, the agency should evaluate the listed factors [@CL-MKT-1645]. The direction is stated as should and falls on the agency [@CL-MKT-1645]. The first factor is the agency's current and future plans to acquire the specific items or work that 8(a) participants are seeking to provide [@CL-MKT-1646]. Those plans are identified by the estimated quantities of the supplies or services required or the estimated number of construction projects planned, and by the length of the contract including option periods [@CL-MKT-1647]. They are also identified by performance or delivery requirements, which include required monthly production rates when applicable and, for construction, the geographical location where the work is to be performed [@CL-MKT-1648]. The other factors are the impact of any delay in delivery, whether the items or work were previously acquired using small business set-asides and the date of that acquisition, and problems encountered in previous acquisitions of the items or work from the 8(a) participants or other contractors [@CL-MKT-1649]. The last factor is any other pertinent information about known 8(a) participants, the items or the work, including information about the participants' products or capabilities [@CL-MKT-1650].

When necessary, the contracting agency shall make an independent review of the factors in 19.803(a) and other aspects of the participants' capabilities that would ensure the satisfactory performance of the requirement being considered for commitment to the 8(a) program [@CL-MKT-1651]. The duty falls on the contracting agency [@CL-MKT-1651]. Section 19.803(a) is the paragraph on the SBA's search letter [@CL-MKT-0496]; the [Selecting acquisitions for the 8(a) program](../programs/mkt-8a-selecting-acquisitions.md) card names that path [@CL-MKT-0496].

Orders under multiple-award contracts. Separate offers and acceptances are not required for individual orders under multiple-award contracts [@CL-MKT-1652]. The section lists these contracts as including the Federal Supply Schedules managed by GSA, multi-agency contracts or Governmentwide acquisition contracts, and indefinite-delivery, indefinite-quantity (IDIQ) contracts [@CL-MKT-1681]. That relief applies where the contract has been set aside for exclusive competition among 8(a) contractors, and the individual order is then to be competed among all 8(a) contract holders [@CL-MKT-1653]. In that case SBA's acceptance of the original contract is valid for the term of the contract [@CL-MKT-1654]. Offers and acceptances are required for individual orders under multiple-award contracts that have not been set aside for exclusive competition among 8(a) contractors [@CL-MKT-1655].

The contracting officer may issue an order on a sole source basis when the multiple-award contract was set aside for exclusive competition among 8(a) participants [@CL-MKT-1656]. Two more conditions apply: the order has an estimated value less than or equal to the dollar thresholds set forth at 19.805-1(a)(2), and the offering and acceptance procedures at 19.804-2 and 19.804-3 are followed [@CL-MKT-1657]. Section 19.805-1(a)(2) states those amounts as $8.5 million for acquisitions assigned manufacturing NAICS codes and $5.5 million for all other acquisitions [@CL-MKT-1682]. There, except as provided in paragraph (b), an acquisition offered to the SBA under the 8(a) program shall be awarded on the basis of competition limited to eligible 8(a) participants when there is a reasonable expectation that at least two eligible and responsible 8(a) participants will submit offers and that award can be made at a fair market price, and the anticipated total value of the contract, including options, will exceed those thresholds [@CL-MKT-1687; @CL-MKT-0508; @CL-MKT-1682]. Paragraph (b) of that section is the exception: where an acquisition exceeds the competitive threshold, the SBA may accept the requirement for a sole source 8(a) award if there is not a reasonable expectation that at least two eligible and responsible 8(a) participants will submit offers at a fair market price, or if SBA accepts the requirement on behalf of a concern owned by an Indian tribe or an Alaska Native Corporation [@CL-MKT-0510; @CL-MKT-0511; @CL-MKT-0512]. Section 19.804-6(b)(2) uses the same dollar thresholds as a ceiling on the estimated value of the order [@CL-MKT-1657]. The [Competitive 8(a) awards](../programs/mkt-8a-competitive-awards.md) card covers that section [@CL-MKT-1682].

The contracting officer may issue an order directly to one 8(a) contractor in accordance with 19.504(c)(1)(ii) when the multiple-award contract was reserved for 8(a) participants [@CL-MKT-1658]. Two more conditions apply: the order has an estimated value less than or equal to $8.5 million for acquisitions assigned manufacturing NAICS codes and $5.5 million for all other acquisitions, and the offering and acceptance procedures at 19.804-2 and 19.804-3 are followed [@CL-MKT-1659].

An 8(a) contractor may continue to accept new orders under the contract even if it exits the 8(a) program or becomes other than small for the NAICS codes assigned to the contract [@CL-MKT-1660]. Agencies may continue to take credit toward their prime contracting small disadvantaged business or small business goals for orders awarded to 8(a) contractors, even after the contractor's 8(a) program term expires [@CL-MKT-1661]. The same permission applies where the contractor otherwise exits the 8(a) program or becomes other than small for the NAICS codes assigned under the 8(a) contract [@CL-MKT-1662]. The agency may not credit any subsequent orders awarded to the contractor toward those goals if the contractor rerepresents that it is other than small for the assigned NAICS codes in accordance with 19.301-2, or where ownership or control of the contractor has changed and SBA has granted a waiver to allow the contractor to continue performance (see 13 CFR 124.515) [@CL-MKT-1663; @CL-MKT-1664].

## How it works

The agency evaluates a requirement before it offers it. The section directs the evaluation with should [@CL-MKT-1645]. The independent review by the contracting agency when necessary is stated as shall [@CL-MKT-1651].

For orders, the first question is how the multiple-award contract was awarded. If it was set aside for exclusive competition among 8(a) contractors and the individual order is to be competed among all 8(a) contract holders, individual orders need no separate offer and acceptance [@CL-MKT-1652; @CL-MKT-1653]. If it was not, each order needs its own offer and acceptance [@CL-MKT-1655]. The contracting officer decides whether to use either single-contractor route, because both are stated as may [@CL-MKT-1656; @CL-MKT-1658]. The sole source route is for a set-aside contract, and the direct route is for a contract reserved for 8(a) participants [@CL-MKT-1656; @CL-MKT-1658]. Each route needs the offering and acceptance procedures at 19.804-2 and 19.804-3 and an estimated order value within its limit [@CL-MKT-1657; @CL-MKT-1659].

Two points fall on others. The contractor may accept new orders after it exits or becomes other than small [@CL-MKT-1660]. Rerepresenting as other than small is the contractor's act, and an SBA waiver after a change in ownership or control is SBA's act; either ends the agency's goal credit for subsequent orders [@CL-MKT-1663; @CL-MKT-1664].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An agency weighs a requirement. It records plans for 12 units a month and a 3 year contract with two 1 year priced options [@CL-MKT-1646; @CL-MKT-1647; @CL-MKT-1648]. The work is supplies, not construction, so no geographical location is recorded [@CL-MKT-1648]. It notes that the same work was set aside for small business 4 years earlier and that a prior award to a participant had delivery problems [@CL-MKT-1649]. Assume the review is necessary (the section does not say who decides), so the contracting agency makes an independent review of the factors in 19.803(a) and of the participants' capabilities [@CL-MKT-1651].

A multiple-award contract was set aside for exclusive competition among 8(a) contractors. A 2 million dollar order under it needs no separate offer and acceptance and is competed among all 8(a) contract holders [@CL-MKT-1652; @CL-MKT-1653]. The contracting officer may instead issue that order on a sole source basis when the offering and acceptance procedures at 19.804-2 and 19.804-3 are followed and the order value is at or below the 19.805-1(a)(2) thresholds [@CL-MKT-1656; @CL-MKT-1657]. The 2 million dollar value is below both threshold amounts, $5.5 million and $8.5 million [@CL-MKT-1682].

A second multiple-award contract was reserved for 8(a) participants. An order estimated at 4 million for an acquisition without a manufacturing NAICS code is at or below 5.5 million, so the contracting officer may issue it directly to one 8(a) contractor if the procedures at 19.804-2 and 19.804-3 are followed [@CL-MKT-1658; @CL-MKT-1659]. An order estimated at 9 million for an acquisition assigned a manufacturing NAICS code is above 8.5 million, so the direct route in 19.804-6(c) is not open for it [@CL-MKT-1659]. This card does not say which other route then applies.

A contractor on the first contract exits the 8(a) program. It may still accept new orders, and the agency may still take credit for them [@CL-MKT-1660; @CL-MKT-1662]. Later the contractor rerepresents that it is other than small under 19.301-2. The agency may not credit any subsequent orders toward its goals [@CL-MKT-1663; @CL-MKT-1664].

## Never confuse

The evaluation before an offering is a separate step from the offering and SBA acceptance, which the [Agency offering and SBA acceptance of 8(a) requirements](../programs/mkt-8a-offering-and-acceptance.md) card covers [@CL-MKT-1645]. A multiple-award contract set aside for exclusive competition among 8(a) contractors, where the individual order is to be competed among all 8(a) contract holders, needs no separate offer and acceptance for each order, while one that was not set aside that way does [@CL-MKT-1652; @CL-MKT-1655]. The sole source route needs a set-aside contract, an order within the thresholds at 19.805-1(a)(2), and the offering and acceptance procedures [@CL-MKT-1656; @CL-MKT-1657]. The direct route needs a reserved contract, an order within the $8.5 million or $5.5 million limit, and the same procedures [@CL-MKT-1658; @CL-MKT-1659]. A contractor's freedom to accept new orders is not the agency's goal credit: the first holds after exit [@CL-MKT-1660], while credit for subsequent orders ends on rerepresentation or on an SBA waiver after a change in ownership or control [@CL-MKT-1663; @CL-MKT-1664]. The [Notice of set-aside of orders](../programs/mkt-set-aside-of-orders.md) card covers a different clause that sets aside orders for small business concerns.
