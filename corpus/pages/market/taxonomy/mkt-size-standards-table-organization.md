## Purpose

Shows how the SBA size standards table is laid out: a standard for each industry code, stated either in employees or in annual receipts in millions of dollars, with exceptions printed as their own rows that carry their own standards. It also states what the sector notes say and what the footnotes that this card covers say. The related card on [the size standards and industry codes](mkt-size-standards-and-naics-codes.md) covers where the standards come from and how the contracting officer assigns the code.

| Applies to | All SBA programs, unless otherwise specified in that part [@CL-MKT-2159] |

Section 121.201 of the size regulations applies its standards to all SBA programs unless that part specifies otherwise [@CL-MKT-2159].

Each standard is stated either as a number of employees or as annual receipts in millions of dollars, unless otherwise specified [@CL-MKT-2160].

The number of employees or the annual receipts shown is the maximum allowed for a concern and its affiliates to be considered small [@CL-MKT-2161].

The columns are the industry code, the industry title, the size standard in millions of dollars and the size standard in number of employees [@CL-MKT-2162].

## How it works

A row in the dollar column prints a dollar figure and a row in the employee column prints a bare number, as the forestry rows show side by side [@CL-MKT-2162; @CL-MKT-2163].

One industry code may carry more than one row, because exceptions are printed as separate rows with their own standards [@CL-MKT-2164].

The wholesale trade and retail trade sector notes say that those codes shall not be used to classify Government acquisitions for supplies, and that the applicable manufacturing code shall be used instead [@CL-MKT-2165; @CL-MKT-2341; @CL-MKT-2166]. Under FAR 19.102, unless paragraph (b)(2)(ii)(B) of that section requires otherwise, contracting officers must assign one NAICS code and its size standard to all solicitations, contracts, and task and delivery orders [@CL-MKT-0203].

Federal Government contractors also shall not use those trade codes when subcontracting for the acquisition for supplies [@CL-MKT-2168].

A wholesale or retail trade concern that submits an offer or a quote on a supply acquisition is categorized as a nonmanufacturer and deemed small if it has 500 or fewer employees and meets the requirements of section 121.406 [@CL-MKT-2167]. The conditions for a nonmanufacturer are on [the nonmanufacturer card](mkt-sba-nonmanufacturer-qualification.md).

The footnotes add rules for particular codes. This card states these:

- For dredging, to be considered small for purposes of Government procurement, a firm or its similarly situated subcontractors must perform at least 40 percent of the volume dredged with their own equipment or equipment owned by another small dredging concern [@CL-MKT-2198].
- For food canning and preserving, for Government procurement, the standard of 1,000 employees excludes agricultural labor as defined in section 3306(k) of the Internal Revenue Code [@CL-MKT-2199].
- For the leasing of building space to the Federal Government, for Government procurement, a size standard of $47 million in gross receipts applies to the owners of the space and does not apply to an agent [@CL-MKT-2200].
- For the codes that one footnote lists, size is measured by total revenues, excluding funds received in trust for an unaffiliated third party such as bookings or sales subject to commissions, and the commissions received are included as revenue [@CL-MKT-2201].
- For Government procurement, the purchase of software subject to potential waiver of the nonmanufacturer rule under section 121.1203(d) should be classified under NAICS Code 513210 [@CL-MKT-2202].
- Concerns performing operational services for the administration of a government program are classified under the private sector industry based on the activities performed [@CL-MKT-2203]. Procurements for services of that type are classified under the private sector industry that best describes the activities to be performed [@CL-MKT-2208].
- For rebuilding machinery or equipment on a factory basis, or equivalent, the footnote on subsectors 333, 334, 335 and 336 says to use the code for a newly manufactured product [@CL-MKT-2169]. It adds that concerns performing major rebuilding or overhaul do not necessarily have to meet the criteria for being a manufacturer even though the activities may be classified under a manufacturing code, and that ordinary repair services or preservation are not considered rebuilding [@CL-MKT-2192]. [The aerospace card](mkt-aerospace-defense-size-standards.md) applies this to aircraft codes and research and development.

The information technology value added reseller exception has its own footnote rules. These bind the procurement and the offeror:

- For Government procurement, a procurement classified under the exception and its 150-employee standard must consist of at least 15 percent and not more than 50 percent of value added services, measured by the total contract price [@CL-MKT-2204]. The exception sits under NAICS code 541519 [@CL-MKT-0392].
- The offeror must comply with the manufacturing performance requirements, or comply with the nonmanufacturer rule by supplying the products of small business concerns, unless SBA has issued a class or contract specific waiver of the nonmanufacturer rule [@CL-MKT-2205].
- A contract with less than 15 percent value added services must be classified under a NAICS manufacturing industry [@CL-MKT-2206].
- A contract with more than 50 percent value added services must be classified under the NAICS industry that best describes the predominate service of the procurement [@CL-MKT-2207].

This card does not state the other footnotes, such as those on petroleum refiners, tires, financial institution assets, facilities support services, building and property specialty trade services and Job Corps centers. [The aerospace card](mkt-aerospace-defense-size-standards.md) states the environmental remediation footnote and the research definition for the small business innovation programs, where they bear on engineering services and research and development. Read the rest in the section itself.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A reseller submits an offer on a procurement that a buyer proposes to classify under the information technology value added reseller exception. The total contract price is $2,000,000, of which $240,000 is value added services. That is 12 percent, below the 15 percent floor, so the contract must be classified under a manufacturing industry and not under the exception [@CL-MKT-2204; @CL-MKT-2206]. If the value added services were $600,000, that is 30 percent, within the 15 to 50 percent band, so the exception can apply [@CL-MKT-2204]. The reseller would also have to meet the manufacturing performance requirements or the nonmanufacturer rule, unless SBA has issued a class or contract specific waiver of the nonmanufacturer rule [@CL-MKT-2205].

## Never confuse

Each standard is stated in one measure or the other, employees or annual receipts, unless the text specifies otherwise [@CL-MKT-2160]. A footnote can specify otherwise, as the revenue measure for listed codes does [@CL-MKT-2201].

The size standard is not an allowability or accounting rule. It fixes the maximum for a concern and its affiliates to be considered small [@CL-MKT-2161].

An exception row is not a separate industry code, because it sits under the same code with its own standard [@CL-MKT-2164].

The trade-sector bar is about classifying acquisitions for supplies and subcontracting for them [@CL-MKT-2165; @CL-MKT-2341; @CL-MKT-2168]. The status question that a standard answers is on [the identification card](mkt-size-standards-and-identification-as-small.md), and the code choice for a procurement is on [the designation card](mkt-sba-size-standard-designation.md).
