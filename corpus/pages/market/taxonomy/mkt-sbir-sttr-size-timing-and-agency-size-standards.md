## Purpose

Shows when SBA determines SBIR and STTR size, what a concern self-certifies, and what an agency must do to use its own size standard, under 13 CFR 121.704, 121.705 and 121.903 [@CL-MKT-0693; @CL-MKT-0697; @CL-MKT-0698].

The size and eligibility status of a concern for an SBIR or STTR funding agreement award is determined at the time of award for both Phase I and Phase II awards, or on the date of the request for a size determination if an award is pending [@CL-MKT-0693]. A business concern must self-certify that it meets the eligibility requirements for a Phase I or Phase II SBIR or STTR funding agreement [@CL-MKT-0697]. A concern that qualified as a small business when it received an SBIR or STTR funding agreement is considered a small business throughout the life of that specific funding agreement, subject to listed exceptions [@CL-MKT-0694; @CL-MKT-0695].

A Federal agency that decides the SBA size standard is not suitable for its programs may, in limited circumstances, have its agency head establish a different small business definition for exclusive use in those programs, but only when the listed conditions are met [@CL-MKT-0698].

## How it works

The life-of-agreement rule has listed exceptions [@CL-MKT-0694; @CL-MKT-0695]. In the case of a merger or acquisition, the awardee must, within 30 days of the transaction becoming final or of the approved funding agreement novation if one is required, recertify its small business size status to the funding agreement agency [@CL-MKT-0695]. The awardee may instead of recertifying inform the agency that it is other than small [@CL-MKT-0696]. If the awardee is other than small, the agency can no longer fund the options or issue a continuation pursuant to the funding agreement, from that point forward, with SBIR or STTR funds [@CL-MKT-0722]. For funding agreements with durations of more than five years, a funding agreement officer must request that a business concern re-certify its small business size status no more than 120 days prior to the end of the fifth year of the funding agreement [@CL-MKT-0723].

To use its own standard, one listed condition is that the agency consulted in writing with the Division Chief of SBA's Office of Size Standards at least 14 calendar days before publishing the proposed rule [@CL-MKT-0699]. That written consultation includes what size standard the agency contemplates using, to what agency program it will apply, how the agency arrived at that standard for the program, and why SBA's existing size standards do not satisfy the program requirements [@CL-MKT-0725]. Two further listed conditions are that the agency proposes the size standard for public comment under the Administrative Procedure Act and provides the Division Chief a copy of the proposed rule when it publishes it for public comment [@CL-MKT-0724]. Another is that SBA's Administrator approves the size standard before the agency adopts a final rule or otherwise prescribes the standard for its use [@CL-MKT-0700]. The section also lists a condition on what the standard will measure and what the request for the Administrator's approval must include, which this card does not state.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A firm wins a Phase I award as a small business on day 0. By day 300 it has grown past the size standard, yet it is still treated as small under that award. On day 400 it is bought by another company, and the sale becomes final that day. It must recertify its size to the funding agency, or tell the agency it is other than small, within 30 days of that day.

## Never confuse

Self-certification at award is a different step from the recertification that follows a merger or acquisition [@CL-MKT-0697; @CL-MKT-0695]. The ownership, control and employee tests an awardee certifies against are on the [SBIR and STTR ownership, control and employee limit](mkt-sbir-sttr-size-and-eligibility-standards.md) card. An agency definition set up through the listed conditions is for exclusive use in that agency's programs, so it does not replace SBA's standards elsewhere [@CL-MKT-0698]. The factors SBA weighs in setting its own standards are on the [What SBA weighs in setting size standards and what a business concern is](mkt-sba-size-standard-factors-and-business-concern.md) card. Rerepresentation on ordinary federal contracts is on the [Rerepresentation of size and status after award](mkt-size-rerepresentation-after-award.md) card, and the measurement date for contracts is on the [When a concern's size status is measured](mkt-sba-size-measurement-date.md) card.
