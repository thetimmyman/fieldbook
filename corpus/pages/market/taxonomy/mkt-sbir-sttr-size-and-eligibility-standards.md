## Purpose

Shows which programs the SBA size and eligibility sections cover, and the ownership, control and employee rules an SBIR or STTR awardee must meet, under 13 CFR 121.701 and 121.702 [@CL-MKT-0687; @CL-MKT-0688].

## The rule

| Applies to | Business concerns seeking SBIR or STTR Phase I or Phase II funding agreements [@CL-MKT-0688] |

The size and eligibility sections apply to SBA's Small Business Innovation Research and Small Business Technology Transfer programs [@CL-MKT-0687]. To be eligible for award of funding agreements in SBA's SBIR and STTR programs, a business concern must meet the stated requirements at the time of award of a Phase I or Phase II funding agreement [@CL-MKT-0688]. An SBIR or STTR awardee, together with its affiliates, must not have more than 500 employees [@CL-MKT-0692].

For SBIR, one listed way to meet the ownership and control test is to be a concern more than 50 percent directly owned and controlled by one or more individuals who are citizens or permanent resident aliens of the United States [@CL-MKT-0689]. The same list lets the owners be other small business concerns each more than 50 percent directly owned and controlled by such individuals, an Indian tribe, ANC or NHO or a wholly owned business entity of such tribe, ANC or NHO, or any combination of these [@CL-MKT-0716]. Another listed way is to be a concern more than 50 percent owned by multiple venture capital operating companies, hedge funds, private equity firms, or any combination of these, for agencies electing to use the authority in the statute cited in the text [@CL-MKT-0690]. No single venture capital operating company, hedge fund, or private equity firm may own more than 50 percent of an SBIR awardee unless that single firm qualifies as a small business concern [@CL-MKT-0721], one that is more than 50 percent directly owned and controlled by individuals who are citizens or permanent resident aliens of the United States [@CL-MKT-0714]. For STTR, one listed way is to be a concern more than 50 percent directly owned and controlled by one or more individuals who are citizens or permanent resident aliens of the United States [@CL-MKT-0691].

For the SBIR program, if an Employee Stock Ownership Plan owns all or part of the concern each stock trustee and plan member is considered an owner, and if a trust owns all or part of the concern each trustee and trust beneficiary is considered an owner [@CL-MKT-0717]. The STTR program has the same two rules [@CL-MKT-0718].
## How it works

The ownership paths above are items in lists of ways a concern can qualify; this card does not state the joint venture paths. The employee limit counts the awardee together with its affiliates [@CL-MKT-0692]. Concerns and entities are affiliates of each other when one controls or has the power to control the other, or a third party or parties controls or has the power to control both, and it does not matter whether control is exercised so long as the power to control exists [@CL-MKT-0719]. For determining affiliation based on equity ownership, a concern is an affiliate of an individual, concern, or entity that owns or has the power to control more than 50 percent of the concern's voting equity [@CL-MKT-0720]. SBA may also find a concern an affiliate of an individual, concern, or entity that owns or has the power to control 40% or more of the voting equity based upon the totality of circumstances [@CL-MKT-9318]. The requirements are checked at the time of award [@CL-MKT-0688]. The source section also sets other bases of affiliation, including common management, identity of interest, the newly organized concern rule, ostensible subcontractors and license agreements, and a fully diluted method of calculating ownership; this card does not state them.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An SBIR applicant is more than half owned and controlled by two founders who are United States citizens. It has 380 employees counting its affiliates. At the time of award it meets the ownership test and is under the employee limit, so it can qualify. If its affiliates brought the count to 520, it would be over the limit.

## Never confuse

The employee limit is a headcount test that includes affiliates, and the ownership and control test is a separate requirement; a concern must meet the stated requirements at award [@CL-MKT-0688; @CL-MKT-0692]. When SBA measures size for a funding agreement award, and what the awardee must do after a merger or acquisition, are on the [SBIR and STTR size timing, self-certification and agency size standards](mkt-sbir-sttr-size-timing-and-agency-size-standards.md) card. The general SBA affiliation principles are on the [SBA affiliation: general principles, negative control and stock ownership](../../mna/screening/mna-sba-affiliation-and-stock-ownership.md) card; this card does not compare them with the bases stated for these programs.

## Company calibration

Whether the company or any affiliate would apply for SBIR or STTR awards, who owns and controls it, and how many employees it has together with its affiliates. [@Q-019]
