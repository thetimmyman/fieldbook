## Purpose

Shows who may issue a small business status advisory opinion, what it covers and what weight SBA gives it, under 13 CFR 121.109 [@CL-MKT-0664].

## The rule

| Applies to | Firms that want a written opinion that they may represent themselves as small for federal procurement opportunities [@CL-MKT-0665] |

A small business status advisory opinion is a written opinion issued by either a Small Business Development Center operating under part 130 of the chapter or a Procurement Technical Assistance Center operating under chapter 142 of title 10 of the United States Code [@CL-MKT-0664]. It concludes that a firm is entitled to represent itself as a small business concern for purposes of federal government procurement opportunities [@CL-MKT-0665]. To be a small business status advisory opinion, the written opinion must: (1) provide a written analysis explaining why the center concluded that the covered concern, along with its affiliates, either does or does not exceed the size standard or standards considered, and this analysis must be dated and signed by an SBDC or PTAC business counselor or similarly qualified individual [@CL-MKT-0666; @CL-MKT-1613]; (2) include, as an attachment, a completed copy of an SBA Form 355 for the covered concern and its affiliates [@CL-MKT-1613]; and (3) include, as an attachment, copies of the evidence provided by the covered concern to the center clearly documenting its annual receipts and/or number of employees as those terms are defined by §§ 121.104 and 121.106 [@CL-MKT-1614; @CL-MKT-1702].

Unless a referral is made under the paragraph on referral for size determination, SBA decides within 10 business days of receiving the opinion whether to accept or reject it, based on its consistency with part 121 [@CL-MKT-0667]. Nothing in the section precludes the Associate General Counsel, Office of Procurement Law, from requesting a formal size determination for a concern that is the subject of an opinion [@CL-MKT-0705].

## How it works

A concern that receives an opinion holding that it does not exceed the applicable size standard may rely on that determination in responding to Federal procurement opportunities from the date it is issued [@CL-MKT-0668]. That reliance ends when SBA rejects the opinion under the review paragraph or when the concern undergoes a significant change in its ownership, management or other factors bearing on its status as a small business concern [@CL-MKT-0669]. The firm's size may still be protested by interested parties in connection with a specific procurement [@CL-MKT-0670]. The opinion therefore gives a concern a basis for reliance, but SBA review, a significant change or a protest can still affect its small business status [@CL-MKT-0667; @CL-MKT-0669; @CL-MKT-0670].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A center issues an opinion that a firm with 40 employees is under a 100 employee standard. SBA accepts the opinion on business day 6. The firm relies on it in offers. Months later a much larger company buys the firm, which is a significant change in ownership, so the firm can no longer rely on the opinion. A competitor may protest the firm's size in connection with a specific procurement.

## Never confuse

An advisory opinion is written by a center, not by SBA, and SBA reviews it afterwards [@CL-MKT-0664; @CL-MKT-0667]. It is not the representation an offeror makes in an offer, which is on the [Offeror size and status representation at initial offer](mkt-offeror-size-and-status-representation.md) card. It is also not a protest decision; who may file a size protest and the time limits are on the [Who may file a size protest and the time limits](mkt-sba-size-protest-parties-and-deadlines.md) card, and the rule that a protest must relate to a particular procurement is on the [Size protests tied to a procurement and appeals of the NAICS code](mkt-size-protest-procurement-and-naics-appeal.md) card. Penalties for misrepresenting size are on the [Size misrepresentation penalties and subcontract size under SBA's subcontracting program](mkt-sba-size-misrepresentation-penalties-and-subcontract-size.md) card.

## Company calibration

Whether the company holds or relies on any status advisory opinion, and on which procurements it would offer as a small business. [@Q-019]
