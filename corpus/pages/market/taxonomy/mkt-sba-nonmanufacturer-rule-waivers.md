## Purpose

Shows, under 13 CFR 121.1202, 121.1203 and 121.1204 [@CL-MKT-0397; @CL-MKT-0399; @CL-MKT-0401], when SBA grants a class waiver or an individual waiver of the Nonmanufacturer Rule and how each is requested.

| Applies to | Requests for a waiver of the Nonmanufacturer Rule, for a class of products or for a solicitation [@CL-MKT-0397; @CL-MKT-0399] |

A waiver for a class of products will be granted when there are no small business manufacturers or processors available to participate in the Federal market for that class of products [@CL-MKT-0397]. Federal market means acquisitions by the Federal Government from offerors located in the United States, or such smaller area as SBA designates if it concludes that the class of products is not supplied on a national basis [@CL-MKT-0530]. Contractors that have been awarded or have performed a contract to supply the class to the Federal Government within 24 months of the waiver request count as available to participate [@CL-MKT-0398]. A contract for the class may have been performed either directly or through a dealer, or the contractor may instead have submitted an offer on a solicitation for that class of products within the same time frame [@CL-MKT-0529].

An individual waiver for an item in a solicitation will be approved when the SBA Director, Office of Government Contracting, reviews and accepts a contracting officer's determination that no small business manufacturer or processor can reasonably be expected to offer a product [@CL-MKT-0399]. That determination concerns a product meeting the specifications of a solicitation, including the period of performance [@CL-MKT-0549]. An individual waiver applies only to the contract for which it is granted, and not to modifications outside the scope of the contract or to other procurement actions such as follow-on or bridge contracts [@CL-MKT-0400].

## How it works

Any interested person, business, association or Federal agency may submit a request for a class waiver [@CL-MKT-0401]. The request need not take a particular form, but should state the class of products, the applicable NAICS code and detailed information on the efforts made to identify small business manufacturers or processors for the class [@CL-MKT-0402]. A contracting officer's request for an individual waiver for specific solicitations also need not take a particular form, but must, at a minimum, include: a definitive statement of each specific item sought to be waived and justification as to why the specific item is required [@CL-MKT-0403]; the proposed solicitation number, NAICS code, dollar amount of the procurement, dollar amount of the item(s) for which a waiver is sought, and a brief statement of the procurement history [@CL-MKT-1185]; a determination by the contracting officer that no small business manufacturer or processor reasonably can be expected to offer a product or products meeting the specifications (including period of performance) required by a particular solicitation—and for a multiple item procurement, that no small business manufacturer or processor reasonably can be expected to offer each item for which a waiver is sought—together with a narrative describing market research and supporting documentation [@CL-MKT-1186; @CL-MKT-1258]; and, for contracts or orders expected to exceed $500,000, a copy of the Statement of Work [@CL-MKT-1259]. If SBA's research verifies that no small business manufacturers or processors exist for the item, the Director, Office of Government Contracting, will grant an individual, one-time waiver [@CL-MKT-0404]. A nonmanufacturer rule waiver for a specific solicitation expires one year after SBA's determination to grant the waiver, meaning that contract award must occur within one year of the date SBA granted the waiver, and where a contract is not awarded within one year, the procuring agency must come back to SBA with revised market research requesting that the waiver (or waivers in the case of a multiple item procurement) be extended [@CL-MKT-0531; @CL-MKT-1705; @CL-MKT-1706]. A waiver of the Nonmanufacturer Rule for classes of products has no specific time limitation [@CL-MKT-0532]. SBA will, however, periodically review existing class waivers to determine whether small business manufacturers or processors have become available to participate in the Federal market for the waived classes of products and the waiver should be terminated [@CL-MKT-9399].

A list of classes of products for which waivers of the Nonmanufacturer Rule have been granted is maintained on an SBA web site [@CL-MKT-2398].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

SBA finds one small business manufacturer that supplied a class of products to the Federal Government 10 months before a waiver request. That manufacturer has performed a contract within 24 months of the request, so it counts as available to participate. A class waiver is not granted on this basis.

## Never confuse

A class waiver turns on whether small business manufacturers are available for a whole class of products [@CL-MKT-0397]. An individual waiver turns on one solicitation and applies only to the contract for which it is granted [@CL-MKT-0400]. Whether a firm must satisfy the nonmanufacturer rule at all is a separate question; see [How a nonmanufacturer qualifies as a small business on a supply contract](../taxonomy/mkt-sba-nonmanufacturer-qualification.md) and [Limitations on subcontracting and the nonmanufacturer rule](../programs/mkt-limitations-on-subcontracting-and-nonmanufacturer-rule.md).
