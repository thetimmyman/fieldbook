## Purpose

Shows what the Women-Owned Small Business Program is for, when its subpart does not apply, and which notice clauses the contracting officer must insert.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-MKT-1967] |
| Codified citation | FAR 19.1500, 19.1504 and 19.1508 [@CL-MKT-1967; @CL-MKT-1983; @CL-MKT-1974] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-MKT-1967] |
| Applies to | Solicitations and contracts for acquisitions set aside or awarded on a sole-source basis to EDWOSB concerns or to women-owned small business concerns eligible under the WOSB Program [@CL-MKT-1975; @CL-MKT-1978] |

Section 8(m) of the Small Business Act, cited in the paragraph to title 15 of the United States Code, section 637(m), created the Women-Owned Small Business (WOSB) Program [@CL-MKT-1967]. The stated purpose is to ensure that women-owned small business concerns have an equal opportunity to participate in Federal contracting and to assist agencies in achieving their women-owned small business participation goals, and the paragraph ends with a cross-reference that the text prints as "13 part CFR 127" [@CL-MKT-1968]. An economically disadvantaged women-owned small business (EDWOSB) concern and a WOSB concern eligible under the WOSB Program are subcategories of "women-owned small business concern" as defined in section 2.101 [@CL-MKT-1969]. The section does not state that definition, and this card does not either.

The section lists what this subpart does not apply to [@CL-MKT-1983]. Paragraph (a) covers requirements that an 8(a) contractor is currently performing under the 8(a) program or that SBA has accepted for performance under the authority of the 8(a) program, unless SBA has consented to release them from the 8(a) program [@CL-MKT-1970]. SBA is the party whose consent ends that exclusion [@CL-MKT-1970]. Paragraph (b) covers requirements that can be satisfied through award to mandatory Government sources, with a cross-reference to section 8.002 [@CL-MKT-1971]. Paragraph (c) covers orders under indefinite-delivery contracts, and paragraph (d) covers orders against Federal Supply Schedules [@CL-MKT-1972; @CL-MKT-1973]. Each of those two paragraphs adds a "But see" cross-reference to discretionary set-asides of orders, at 16.505(b)(2)(i)(F) for indefinite-delivery orders and at 8.405-5 for schedule orders [@CL-MKT-1972; @CL-MKT-1973].

The contracting officer shall insert the clause at 52.219-29, Notice of Set-Aside for, or Sole-Source Award to, Economically Disadvantaged Women-owned Small Business Concerns [@CL-MKT-1974]. The insertion rule covers solicitations and contracts for acquisitions that are set aside, or awarded on a sole-source basis, to EDWOSB concerns under 19.1505(b) or 19.1506(a) [@CL-MKT-1975]. The paragraph says this includes multiple-award contracts when orders may be set aside for EDWOSB concerns as described in 8.405-5 and 16.505(b)(2)(i)(F), or when orders may be issued directly to one EDWOSB contractor in accordance with 19.504(c)(1)(ii) [@CL-MKT-1976]. The contracting officer shall insert the clause at 52.219-30, Notice of Set-Aside for, or Sole-Source Award to, Women-Owned Small Business Concerns Eligible Under the Women-Owned Small Business Program [@CL-MKT-1977]. That rule covers solicitations and contracts for acquisitions that are set aside, or awarded on a sole-source basis, to WOSB concerns under 19.1505(c) or 19.1506(b) [@CL-MKT-1978]. The paragraph says this includes multiple-award contracts when orders may be set aside for WOSB concerns eligible under the WOSB Program as described in 8.405-5 and 16.505(b)(2)(i)(F), or when orders may be issued directly to one WOSB contractor in accordance with 19.504(c)(1)(ii) [@CL-MKT-1979]. Paragraphs (c) and (d) place no insertion duty themselves. They refer to the prescription at 19.507(e) for clause 52.219-14, Limitations on Subcontracting, and to the prescription at 19.507(h) for clause 52.219-33, Nonmanufacturer Rule [@CL-MKT-1980].

## How it works

The program rests on section 8(m) of the Small Business Act, and its purpose is stated in terms of equal opportunity and agency participation goals [@CL-MKT-1967; @CL-MKT-1968]. The contracting officer checks the requirement against the four paragraphs of 19.1504 [@CL-MKT-1983]. Two of them carry a cross-reference to discretionary set-asides of orders. Orders under indefinite-delivery contracts and orders against Federal Supply Schedules are outside the subpart, but the cross-references point to discretionary set-asides of orders under 16.505(b)(2)(i)(F) and 8.405-5 [@CL-MKT-1972; @CL-MKT-1973]. The 8(a) paragraph carries a condition: the exclusion applies unless SBA has consented to release the requirements from the 8(a) program [@CL-MKT-1970]. The clause rules turn on which concern the acquisition is set aside or awarded to, and each duty falls on the contracting officer [@CL-MKT-1974; @CL-MKT-1977]. An EDWOSB set-aside or sole-source award under 19.1505(b) or 19.1506(a) calls for 52.219-29, and a set-aside or sole-source award to a WOSB concern eligible under the program under 19.1505(c) or 19.1506(b) calls for 52.219-30 [@CL-MKT-1974; @CL-MKT-1975; @CL-MKT-1977; @CL-MKT-1978]. For multiple-award contracts where orders may be set aside or issued directly to one such concern, each rule includes the contract [@CL-MKT-1976; @CL-MKT-1979]. This card does not restate how a set-aside or sole-source award is made. The [Women-Owned Small Business Program set-asides and sole-source awards](../programs/mkt-wosb-set-asides-and-sole-source.md) card covers that. The [Set-asides of orders under multiple-award contracts and the set-aside clauses](../programs/mkt-set-aside-orders-and-clauses.md) card covers the clauses prescribed at 19.507 [@CL-MKT-1980], and the [8(a) contract administration and release of requirements](../programs/mkt-8a-administration-and-release.md) card covers release of 8(a) requirements.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A requirement is being performed by an 8(a) contractor, and SBA has not consented to release it. The contracting officer considers it for a WOSB set-aside. Answer: paragraph (a) of 19.1504 applies, so the subpart does not apply to that requirement [@CL-MKT-1970]. For a separate acquisition set aside for EDWOSB concerns under 19.1505(b), the contracting officer shall insert the clause at 52.219-29 [@CL-MKT-1974; @CL-MKT-1975]. If that acquisition were instead set aside for WOSB concerns eligible under the program under 19.1505(c), the clause would be 52.219-30 [@CL-MKT-1977; @CL-MKT-1978].

## Never confuse

An EDWOSB concern and a WOSB concern eligible under the program are each a subcategory of the same parent term, and each has its own notice clause [@CL-MKT-1969; @CL-MKT-1974; @CL-MKT-1977]. The 8(a) exclusion turns on work that an 8(a) contractor is currently performing or that SBA has accepted for the 8(a) program, and it does not apply once SBA consents to release the requirements [@CL-MKT-1970]. Excluding indefinite-delivery and schedule orders from the subpart does not by itself say those orders cannot be set aside, because each exclusion points to discretionary set-asides of orders [@CL-MKT-1972; @CL-MKT-1973]. Section 19.1508 does not itself prescribe the Limitations on Subcontracting or Nonmanufacturer Rule clauses [@CL-MKT-1980].
