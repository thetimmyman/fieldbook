## Purpose

Shows when a contracting officer must tell potential offerors that a waiver applies to an acquisition and what follows when the notice is missing, under 13 CFR 121.1206 [@CL-MKT-2066; @CL-MKT-2069]. The waivers in question are waivers of the Nonmanufacturer Rule, which section 121.1204 provides for as class waivers and as requests by a contracting officer for specific solicitations [@CL-MKT-2073; @CL-MKT-2074].

| Applies to | Solicitations to which a class or contract specific waiver applies, here a waiver of the Nonmanufacturer Rule [@CL-MKT-2066; @CL-MKT-2073; @CL-MKT-2074] |

Contracting officers must provide written notification to potential offerors of any waivers being applied to a specific acquisition, whether it is a class waiver or a contract specific waiver [@CL-MKT-2066]. This notification must be provided at the time a solicitation is issued [@CL-MKT-2067]. If the notification is provided after a solicitation is issued, the contracting officer must provide potential offerors a reasonable amount of additional time to respond to the solicitation [@CL-MKT-2068]. If a contracting officer does not provide notice, and additional reasonable time for responses when required, then the waiver cannot be applied to the solicitation; this applies to both class waivers and individual waivers [@CL-MKT-2069].

## How it works

The duty is the contracting officer's, and it runs to potential offerors [@CL-MKT-2066]. It covers any waiver being applied to a specific acquisition, whether a class waiver or a contract specific waiver, and the notification must be written [@CL-MKT-2066]. The notification is due at the time the solicitation is issued [@CL-MKT-2067]. A notification given after issuance brings a second duty: the contracting officer must give potential offerors a reasonable amount of additional time to respond [@CL-MKT-2068]. If the contracting officer does not provide notice, and additional reasonable time for responses when required, the waiver cannot be applied to the solicitation, for class waivers and individual waivers alike [@CL-MKT-2069].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A solicitation is issued and applies a class waiver. The contracting officer first gives written notification of the waiver to potential offerors five days after the solicitation was issued, not at the time it was issued [@CL-MKT-2066; @CL-MKT-2067]. Because the notification came after issuance, the officer must give potential offerors a reasonable amount of additional time to respond [@CL-MKT-2068]. Had the officer not provided notice, and additional reasonable time for responses when required, the waiver could not have been applied to the solicitation [@CL-MKT-2069].

## Never confuse

The notification is a duty of the contracting officer: if the officer does not provide notice, and additional reasonable time for responses when required, the waiver cannot be applied to that solicitation [@CL-MKT-2066; @CL-MKT-2069]. The notice duty covers a class waiver or a contract specific waiver, and the bar when notice and required additional time are not provided applies to both class waivers and individual waivers [@CL-MKT-2066; @CL-MKT-2069]. The waiver itself is covered in [Nonmanufacturer rule waivers](../taxonomy/mkt-sba-nonmanufacturer-rule-waivers.md).
