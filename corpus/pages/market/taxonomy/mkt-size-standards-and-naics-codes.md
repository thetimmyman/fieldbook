## Purpose

Shows where small business size standards and NAICS codes come from, when SBA measures a concern's size, how the contracting officer assigns the code, and how that code can be appealed.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-MKT-0200] |
| Codified citation | FAR 19.102 and FAR 19.103 [@CL-MKT-0200; @CL-MKT-0204] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-MKT-0200] |
| Applies to | Solicitations, contracts, and task and delivery orders, each assigned one NAICS code unless a later rule for some multiple-award solicitations requires otherwise [@CL-MKT-0203; @CL-MKT-0271] |

SBA establishes small business size standards on an industry-by-industry basis, and small business size standards and corresponding NAICS codes are provided at 13 CFR 121.201 [@CL-MKT-0200]. NAICS codes are updated by the Office of Management and Budget through its Economic Classification Policy Committee every five years [@CL-MKT-0201]. SBA determines the size status of a concern, including its affiliates, as of the date the concern represents that it is small to the contracting officer as part of its initial offer, which includes price [@CL-MKT-0202]. When an agency uses a solicitation for a multiple-award contract that does not require offers for the contract to include price, SBA determines size as of the date of initial offer for the multiple-award contract, whether or not the offer includes price or the price is evaluated [@CL-MKT-0270].

Unless required to do otherwise by paragraph (b)(2)(ii)(B) of the section, contracting officers shall assign one NAICS code and corresponding size standard to all solicitations, contracts, and task and delivery orders [@CL-MKT-0203]. For solicitations issued after October 1, 2028, that will result in multiple-award contracts, the contracting officer shall either assign a single NAICS code and corresponding size standard that best describes the principal purpose of both the acquisition and each subsequent order, or divide the acquisition into distinct portions or categories and assign each portion or category a single NAICS code and size standard that best describes the principal purpose of the supplies or services to be acquired under that distinct portion or category [@CL-MKT-0271; @CL-MKT-0272; @CL-MKT-9408].

## How it works

The contracting officer shall assign the code and size standard [@CL-MKT-0203], and an appeal from a contracting officer's NAICS code designation and the applicable size standard shall be served and filed within 10 calendar days after the issuance of the initial solicitation or any amendment affecting the NAICS code or size standard [@CL-MKT-0204]. SBA itself may file a NAICS code appeal at any time before offers are due [@CL-MKT-0205]. Appeals may be filed with SBA's Office of Hearings and Appeals (OHA) by one of two alternatives listed [@CL-MKT-0273]. One alternative is any person adversely affected by a NAICS code designation or applicable size standard, except that for a particular sole source 8(a) contract only the SBA Associate Administrator for Business Development may appeal the designation [@CL-MKT-0206]. The other alternative is the Associate or Assistant Director for the SBA program involved, through SBA's Office of General Counsel [@CL-MKT-9407]. OHA issues its decision after the close of record: a decision received by the contracting officer before offers are due is final and the solicitation will be amended if appropriate [@CL-MKT-0207]. A decision received after the due date of the initial offers shall not apply to the pending solicitation but will apply to future solicitations of the same products or services [@CL-MKT-0208].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A solicitation is issued on day 0 with one NAICS code. An amendment issued on day 20 changes the code. A firm that is hurt by the new code has until day 30 to serve and file its appeal, which is 10 calendar days after the amendment. SBA could file its own appeal at any time until offers are due on day 45. If OHA's decision reaches the contracting officer on day 40, it is final and the solicitation is amended if appropriate. If it reaches the officer on day 50, it applies only to later solicitations for the same products or services.

## Never confuse

A size standard is set by SBA for an industry [@CL-MKT-0200], and the size of a concern is measured as of its initial offer [@CL-MKT-0202]. The dollar thresholds that decide which acquisition procedures apply are separate and are on the [Simplified acquisition and micro-purchase thresholds](../../compliance/regulatory-framework/cmp-acquisition-thresholds.md) card. What an offeror represents at that offer is on the [Offeror size and status representation](mkt-offeror-size-and-status-representation.md) card, and a later merger, acquisition or novation can call for a new representation, which is on the [Rerepresentation after award](mkt-size-rerepresentation-after-award.md) card. Who counts as an affiliate is on the [SBA affiliation](../../mna/screening/mna-sba-affiliation-and-stock-ownership.md) card, and how an acquisition changes the receipts and employees counted is on the [SBA size after an acquisition](../../mna/screening/mna-sba-size-after-acquisition.md) card.
