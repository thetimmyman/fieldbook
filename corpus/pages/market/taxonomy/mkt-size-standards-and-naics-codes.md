## Purpose

Shows where small business size standards and NAICS codes come from, when SBA measures a concern's size, how the contracting officer assigns the code, and how that code can be appealed.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-MKT-0200] |
| Codified citation | FAR 19.102 and FAR 19.103 [@CL-MKT-0200; @CL-MKT-0204] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-MKT-0200] |
| Applies to | Solicitations, contracts, and task and delivery orders, each assigned one NAICS code unless a later rule for some multiple-award solicitations requires otherwise [@CL-MKT-0203; @CL-MKT-0271] |

SBA establishes size standards industry by industry, and the standards and their NAICS codes are provided in an SBA regulation that the FAR names [@CL-MKT-0200]. The Office of Management and Budget updates NAICS codes every five years [@CL-MKT-0201]. SBA determines a concern's size, including its affiliates, as of the date the concern represents to the contracting officer that it is small as part of its initial offer, which includes price [@CL-MKT-0202]. For a multiple-award contract that does not require offers to include price, SBA determines size as of the date of initial offer for the multiple-award contract, whether or not the offer includes price or the price is evaluated [@CL-MKT-0270].

Unless a later paragraph requires otherwise, the contracting officer assigns one NAICS code and its size standard to each solicitation, contract, and task and delivery order [@CL-MKT-0203]. For solicitations issued after October 1, 2028, that will result in multiple-award contracts, the contracting officer shall either assign a single NAICS code and size standard that best describes the principal purpose of both the acquisition and each subsequent order, or divide the acquisition into distinct portions or categories and assign each portion or category a single NAICS code and size standard that best describes the principal purpose of the supplies or services to be acquired under that distinct portion or category [@CL-MKT-0271; @CL-MKT-0272; @CL-MKT-9408]. The printed text points to the SBA table but does not print the industry size standard figures or the code list, so this card gives none.

## How it works

The contracting officer assigns the code and size standard, and an appeal of that designation must be served and filed within 10 calendar days after the initial solicitation or any amendment that affects the code or size standard [@CL-MKT-0204]. SBA itself may file a NAICS code appeal at any time before offers are due [@CL-MKT-0205]. Appeals may be filed with SBA's Office of Hearings and Appeals (OHA) by one of two alternatives listed [@CL-MKT-0273]. One alternative is any person adversely affected by a NAICS code designation or applicable size standard, except that for a particular sole source 8(a) contract only the SBA Associate Administrator for Business Development may appeal the designation [@CL-MKT-0206]. The other alternative is the Associate or Assistant Director for the SBA program involved, through SBA's Office of General Counsel [@CL-MKT-9407]. OHA issues its decision after the close of record: a decision received by the contracting officer before offers are due is final and the solicitation is amended if appropriate [@CL-MKT-0207]. A decision received after the due date of the initial offers does not apply to the pending solicitation but applies to future solicitations of the same products or services [@CL-MKT-0208].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A solicitation is issued on day 0 with one NAICS code. An amendment issued on day 20 changes the code. A firm that is hurt by the new code has until day 30 to serve and file its appeal, which is 10 calendar days after the amendment. SBA could file its own appeal at any time until offers are due on day 45. If OHA's decision reaches the contracting officer on day 40, it is final and the solicitation is amended if appropriate. If it reaches the officer on day 50, it applies only to later solicitations for the same products or services.

## Never confuse

A size standard is set by SBA for an industry [@CL-MKT-0200], and the size of a concern is measured as of its initial offer [@CL-MKT-0202]. The dollar thresholds that decide which acquisition procedures apply are separate and are on the [Simplified acquisition and micro-purchase thresholds](../../compliance/regulatory-framework/cmp-acquisition-thresholds.md) card. What an offeror represents at that offer is on the [Offeror size and status representation](mkt-offeror-size-and-status-representation.md) card, and a later merger, acquisition or novation can call for a new representation, which is on the [Rerepresentation after award](mkt-size-rerepresentation-after-award.md) card. Who counts as an affiliate is on the [SBA affiliation](../../mna/screening/mna-sba-affiliation-and-stock-ownership.md) card, and how an acquisition changes the receipts and employees counted is on the [SBA size after an acquisition](../../mna/screening/mna-sba-size-after-acquisition.md) card.
