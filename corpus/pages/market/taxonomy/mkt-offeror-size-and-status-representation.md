## Purpose

Shows what an offeror represents about its size and status, when it must do so, and how the representation reaches joint ventures, multiple-award orders and offers for ordering agreements and blanket purchase agreements.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-MKT-0209] |
| Codified citation | FAR 19.301-1 [@CL-MKT-0209; @CL-MKT-0211] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-MKT-0209] |
| Applies to | Offerors seeking award as a small business concern, with the representation made at initial offer [@CL-MKT-0209; @CL-MKT-0211] |

To be eligible for award as a small business concern of the kinds the FAR identifies for this purpose, an offeror is required to represent in good faith, among other listed representations, either that it meets the size standard for the NAICS code identified in the solicitation [@CL-MKT-0211] or, for a multiple-award contract where more than one NAICS code is assigned, that it meets the size standard for each distinct portion or category for which it submits an offer [@CL-MKT-0274]. A concern that submits an offer for the entire multiple-award contract must meet the size standard for each distinct portion or category [@CL-MKT-9397]. The offeror is required to represent its size and socioeconomic status in writing to the contracting officer at the time of initial offer, whether or not the offer includes price or the price is evaluated [@CL-MKT-0209].

The representation at initial offer includes offers for basic ordering agreements and for blanket purchase agreements issued under part 13 [@CL-MKT-0209; @CL-MKT-0210].

## How it works

Under the FAR's multiple-award contract representations, a business that represents as a small business concern at the time of its initial offer for a distinct portion or category as set forth in paragraph (a)(1)(ii) is considered a small business concern for each order issued under that distinct portion or category, but see the rerepresentation rules [@CL-MKT-0213; @CL-MKT-9398]. One listed way a joint venture may qualify as a small business concern is to comply with the SBA joint venture rules and have each party qualify as small under the solicitation's size standard [@CL-MKT-0212]. The FAR text points to those SBA rules, but the printed FAR text does not set out what they require for a joint venture to qualify, so this card does not establish it [@CL-MKT-0212]. The section also covers orders under ordering agreements and blanket purchase agreements, other joint venture routes, and the case of a multiple-award contract with a single NAICS code, none of which this card restates.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A firm offers on a multiple-award contract that has two NAICS codes, one for each of two distinct line-item groups. It offers only on group A and represents that it is small for group A. Answer: it is considered small for each order issued under group A. Its representation does not reach group B, which it did not offer on. A later rerepresentation can still change that status.

## Never confuse

The representation at initial offer is not SAM registration, which carries the annual representations and certifications and is on the [System for Award Management registration](../feeds/mkt-sam-registration-and-awards.md) card. It is also not the rerepresentation after award, which is on the [Rerepresentation after award](mkt-size-rerepresentation-after-award.md) card, and the size standards behind the representation are on the [Size standards and NAICS codes](mkt-size-standards-and-naics-codes.md) card. Whether a concern is small depends on its affiliates [@CL-MKT-0202], covered on the [SBA affiliation](../../mna/screening/mna-sba-affiliation-and-stock-ownership.md) card.
