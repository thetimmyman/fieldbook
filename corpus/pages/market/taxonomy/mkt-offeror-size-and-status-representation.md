## Purpose

Shows what an offeror represents about its size and status, when it must do so, and how the representation reaches joint ventures, multiple-award orders and offers for ordering agreements and blanket purchase agreements.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-MKT-0209] |
| Codified citation | FAR 19.301-1 [@CL-MKT-0209; @CL-MKT-0211] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-MKT-0209] |
| Applies to | Offerors seeking award as a small business concern, with the representation made at initial offer [@CL-MKT-0209; @CL-MKT-0211] |

To be eligible for award as a small business concern identified in 19.000(a)(3), an offeror is required to represent in good faith both that it meets the size standard—either that it meets the small business size standard corresponding to the NAICS code identified in the solicitation [@CL-MKT-0211], or, for a multiple-award contract where there is more than one NAICS code assigned, that it meets the small business size standard for each distinct portion or category for which it submits an offer, and if it submits an offer for the entire multiple-award contract, that it meets the size standard for each distinct portion or category [@CL-MKT-0274; @CL-MKT-9397]—and that SBA has not issued a written determination stating otherwise pursuant to 13 CFR 121.1009 [@CL-MKT-0188]. The offeror is required to represent its size and socioeconomic status in writing to the contracting officer at the time of initial offer, whether or not the offer includes price or the price is evaluated [@CL-MKT-0209].

The representation at initial offer includes offers for basic ordering agreements (see 16.703) and for blanket purchase agreements issued pursuant to part 13 [@CL-MKT-0209; @CL-MKT-0210].

## How it works

Under the FAR's multiple-award contract representations, a business that represents as a small business concern at the time of its initial offer for a distinct portion or category as set forth in paragraph (a)(1)(ii) is considered a small business concern for each order issued under that distinct portion or category, but see 19.301-2 for rerepresentations [@CL-MKT-0213; @CL-MKT-9398]. A joint venture may qualify as a small business concern if it complies with the requirements of 13 CFR 121.103(h) and 13 CFR 125.8(a) and (b) and if either each party to the joint venture qualifies as small under the size standard for the solicitation [@CL-MKT-0212], or the protégé is small under the size standard for the solicitation in a joint venture comprised of a mentor and protégé with an approved mentor-protégé agreement under an SBA mentor-protégé program [@CL-MKT-0189].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A firm offers on a multiple-award contract that has two NAICS codes, one for each of two distinct line-item groups. It offers only on group A and represents that it is small for group A. Answer: it is considered small for each order issued under group A. Its representation does not reach group B, which it did not offer on. A later rerepresentation can still change that status.

## Never confuse

The representation at initial offer is not SAM registration, which carries the annual representations and certifications and is on the [System for Award Management registration](../feeds/mkt-sam-registration-and-awards.md) card. It is also not the rerepresentation after award, which is on the [Rerepresentation after award](mkt-size-rerepresentation-after-award.md) card, and the size standards behind the representation are on the [Size standards and NAICS codes](mkt-size-standards-and-naics-codes.md) card. Whether a concern is small depends on its affiliates [@CL-MKT-0202], covered on the [SBA affiliation](../../mna/screening/mna-sba-affiliation-and-stock-ownership.md) card.
