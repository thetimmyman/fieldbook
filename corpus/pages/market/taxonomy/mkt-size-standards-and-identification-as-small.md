## Purpose

Shows what SBA size standards are and what a concern must do to be identified as small in the System for Award Management database, under 13 CFR 121.101 and 121.110 [@CL-MKT-2039; @CL-MKT-2043].

SBA's size standards define whether a business entity is small and, thus, whether it is eligible for Government programs and preferences reserved for small business concerns [@CL-MKT-2039]. Size standards have been established for types of economic activity, or industry, generally under the North American Industry Classification System (NAICS) [@CL-MKT-2040]. NAICS is described in the North American Industry Classification Manual-United States [@CL-MKT-2072]. The manual includes definitions for each industry, tables showing relationships between 1997 NAICS and 1987 SICs, and a comprehensive index, and NAICS assigns codes to all economic activity within twenty broad sectors [@CL-MKT-2070].

Section 121.201 provides a full table of small business size standards matched to the NAICS industry codes of the United States [@CL-MKT-2041]. SBA also publishes annually in the Federal Register a full table matching a size standard with each NAICS Industry or United States Industry code [@CL-MKT-2042].

To be identified as a small business concern in the System for Award Management database, or any successor, a concern must certify its size in connection with specific size standards at least annually [@CL-MKT-2043]. If a firm identified as a small business concern in that database fails to certify its size within one year of a size certification, the firm will not be listed as a small business concern there, unless and until it recertifies its size [@CL-MKT-2044].

## How it works

The duty to certify falls on the concern, and the certification is made in connection with specific size standards [@CL-MKT-2043]. It must be made at least annually [@CL-MKT-2043]. A firm that does not certify within one year of a size certification is not listed as small, unless and until it recertifies its size [@CL-MKT-2044].

Size standards are established for types of economic activity, or industry, generally under NAICS, and the full tables that match a standard to an industry code are in section 121.201 and in SBA's annual Federal Register table [@CL-MKT-2040; @CL-MKT-2041; @CL-MKT-2042].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A concern certifies its size in the System for Award Management database in connection with the size standard for one industry code, and it is listed as small [@CL-MKT-2043]. Fourteen months later it has not certified again. That is more than one year after its size certification, so the firm will not be listed as small unless and until it recertifies its size [@CL-MKT-2044]. It cannot be listed as small again until it recertifies [@CL-MKT-2044].

## Never confuse

A size standard is not the same as a size certification. The standard defines whether a business entity is small and, thus, eligible for programs and preferences reserved for small business concerns, and it is established by industry, while the certification is the concern's own submission in the database, made in connection with specific size standards at least annually [@CL-MKT-2039; @CL-MKT-2040; @CL-MKT-2043]. A concern that does not certify within one year of a size certification is not listed as small unless and until it recertifies [@CL-MKT-2044]. The industry code side of the question is covered in [Size standards and NAICS codes](../taxonomy/mkt-size-standards-and-naics-codes.md).
