## Purpose

Shows that a size protest must pertain to a particular procurement, and who may appeal a NAICS code designation, to whom and by when, under 13 CFR 121.1007 and 121.1103 [@CL-MKT-0701; @CL-MKT-0702].

| Applies to | Size protests about a particular procurement or sale, and appeals of a contracting officer's NAICS code or size standard designation [@CL-MKT-0701; @CL-MKT-0704] |

A protest challenging the size of a concern that does not pertain to a particular procurement or sale will not be acted on by SBA [@CL-MKT-0701]. A size protest must include specific facts, be sufficiently specific to give reasonable notice of the grounds on which the protested concern's size is questioned, and give some basis for the belief or allegation stated [@CL-MKT-0726]. Protests that do not contain sufficient specificity will be dismissed by SBA [@CL-MKT-0727].

Any interested party adversely affected by a NAICS code designation may appeal the designation to the Office of Hearings and Appeals (OHA) [@CL-MKT-0702]. An interested party includes a business concern seeking to change the NAICS code designation in order to be considered a small business for the challenged procurement, regardless of whether the procurement is reserved for small businesses or unrestricted [@CL-MKT-2088]. The only exception is for a sole source contract reserved under SBA's 8(a) Business Development program (see part 124 of 13 CFR chapter I), in which case only SBA's Associate Administrator for Business Development may appeal the NAICS code designation [@CL-MKT-0703]. The contracting officer's determination of the applicable NAICS code is final unless appealed as follows: an appeal from a contracting officer's NAICS code or size standard designation must be served and filed within 10 calendar days after the issuance of the solicitation or amendment affecting the NAICS code or size standard, except that SBA may file a NAICS code appeal at any time before offers are due [@CL-MKT-2144; @CL-MKT-0704]. OHA will summarily dismiss an untimely NAICS code appeal [@CL-MKT-0728]. Upon receipt of the service copy of a NAICS code appeal, the contracting officer shall (i) stay the date for the closing of receipt of offers; (ii) advise the public, by amendment to the solicitation or other method, of the existence of the NAICS code appeal and the procedures and deadline for interested parties to file and serve arguments concerning the appeal; (iii) send a copy of (or an electronic link to) the entire solicitation, including amendments, to OHA; (iv) file and serve any response to the appeal prior to the close of the record; and (v) inform OHA of any amendments, actions or developments concerning the procurement in question [@CL-MKT-0729; @CL-MKT-2145; @CL-MKT-2146].

## How it works

A protest challenging the size of a concern that does not pertain to a particular procurement or sale will not be acted on by SBA, and protests that do not contain sufficient specificity will be dismissed by SBA [@CL-MKT-0701; @CL-MKT-0726; @CL-MKT-0727]. A NAICS code appeal challenges a NAICS code or size standard designation made by a contracting officer and is filed with OHA within 10 calendar days after issuance of the solicitation or amendment affecting the NAICS code or size standard, except that SBA may file at any time before offers are due [@CL-MKT-0702; @CL-MKT-0704]. Who may file a size protest and the protest time limits are covered on [Who may file a size protest and the time limits](mkt-sba-size-protest-parties-and-deadlines.md).

Unless an appeal is made to the Office of Hearings and Appeals, the size determination made by an SBA Government Contracting Area Office or Disaster Area Office is the final decision of the agency [@CL-MKT-2394]. An appeal to OHA is an administrative remedy that must be exhausted before judicial review of a formal size determination may be sought in a court [@CL-MKT-2395]. A NAICS code designation made by a procuring activity contracting officer may also be appealed to OHA [@CL-MKT-2396]. The procedures governing OHA appeals are set forth in 13 CFR part 134, and the OHA appeal is an administrative remedy that must be exhausted before judicial review of a NAICS code designation may be sought in a court [@CL-MKT-2147].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A solicitation is issued on day 0 and an amendment on day 10 changes its NAICS code. A small firm hurt by the new code has until day 20 to serve and file an appeal. SBA could file its own appeal at any time until offers are due on day 45. An appeal filed on day 25 would be summarily dismissed as untimely, and when a timely appeal is served the contracting officer must stay the closing date. A letter saying a rival is large, with no procurement attached, would not be acted on by SBA.

## Never confuse

A size protest challenges the size of a concern and must pertain to a particular procurement or sale [@CL-MKT-0701], whereas a NAICS code appeal challenges a contracting officer's NAICS code or size standard designation before OHA [@CL-MKT-0702; @CL-MKT-0704]. Both a formal size determination appeal and a NAICS code appeal to OHA are administrative remedies that must be exhausted before judicial review may be sought in a court [@CL-MKT-2394; @CL-MKT-2395; @CL-MKT-2147]. See [Size standards, NAICS codes and appeals of the contracting officer's code](mkt-size-standards-and-naics-codes.md), [Protesting a small business representation or rerepresentation](mkt-small-business-size-protests.md), and [Small business status advisory opinions](mkt-small-business-status-advisory-opinions.md).
