## Purpose

Shows what size form a firm must file to apply for a Certificate of Competency, what SBA does with it, and which size standard governs a section 8(d) subcontract, under 13 CFR 121.408 and 121.410 [@CL-MKT-2045; @CL-MKT-2071].

| Applies to | Firms applying for a Certificate of Competency, and subcontracts under the section 8(d) subcontracting program [@CL-MKT-2045; @CL-MKT-2071] |

A firm which applies for a Certificate of Competency must file an Application for Small Business Size Determination, SBA Form 355 [@CL-MKT-2045]. If the initial review of that form indicates the applicant, including its affiliates, is small for purposes of the Certificate of Competency program, SBA will process the application [@CL-MKT-2046]. If the review indicates the applicant, including its affiliates, is other than small, SBA will initiate a formal size determination as set forth in section 121.1001(b)(3)(ii) [@CL-MKT-2047]. In that case SBA will not further process the application until a formal size determination is made [@CL-MKT-2048]. A concern is ineligible for a Certificate of Competency if a formal SBA size determination finds the concern other than small [@CL-MKT-2049].

For subcontracting purposes pursuant to section 8(d) of the Small Business Act, a concern is small for subcontracts which relate to Government procurements if it does not exceed the size standard for the NAICS code that the prime contractor believes best describes the product or service being acquired by the subcontract [@CL-MKT-2071; @CL-MKT-2050]. However, subcontracts for engineering services awarded under the National Energy Policy Act of 1992 have the same size standard as Military and Aerospace Equipment and Military Weapons under NAICS code 541330 [@CL-MKT-2051].

## How it works

For a Certificate of Competency, the applicant files SBA Form 355, and SBA reviews it for the applicant including its affiliates [@CL-MKT-2045; @CL-MKT-2046; @CL-MKT-2047]. If the initial review indicates the applicant is small for purposes of the Certificate of Competency program, SBA will process the application [@CL-MKT-2046]. If the review indicates the applicant, including its affiliates, is other than small, SBA will initiate a formal size determination as set forth in section 121.1001(b)(3)(ii), and will not further process the application until a formal size determination is made [@CL-MKT-2047; @CL-MKT-2048]. If a formal SBA size determination finds the concern other than small, the concern is ineligible for a Certificate of Competency [@CL-MKT-2049].

For subcontracting purposes pursuant to section 8(d) of the Small Business Act, a concern is small for subcontracts which relate to Government procurements if it does not exceed the size standard for the NAICS code that the prime contractor believes best describes the product or service being acquired by the subcontract [@CL-MKT-2071; @CL-MKT-2050]. However, subcontracts for engineering services awarded under the National Energy Policy Act of 1992 have the same size standard as Military and Aerospace Equipment and Military Weapons under NAICS code 541330 [@CL-MKT-2051]. The codes and standards are covered in [Size standards and NAICS codes](../taxonomy/mkt-size-standards-and-naics-codes.md) and the formal determination process is covered in [SBA size determination procedure](mkt-sba-size-determination-procedure.md).

For the purpose of receiving a Certificate of Competency in an unrestricted procurement, the applicable size standard is that corresponding to the NAICS code set forth in the solicitation [@CL-MKT-2364]. For that purpose, the offeror need not be the manufacturer of any of the items acquired [@CL-MKT-2365].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A firm applying for a Certificate of Competency files SBA Form 355. The form shows 38,000,000 dollars in average annual receipts for the firm and its affiliates, against a standard of 45,000,000 dollars. The initial review indicates the group is small, so SBA will process the application. Had the initial review indicated that the group was other than small, SBA would have initiated a formal size determination and would not have processed the application further until that determination was made. If that determination had found the concern other than small, the concern would have been ineligible.

Separately, a prime contractor believes that one NAICS code best describes a subcontracted product. A concern is small for that subcontract if it does not exceed the size standard for that code. For an engineering services subcontract awarded under the named energy statute, the standard is instead the same as for Military and Aerospace Equipment and Military Weapons under NAICS code 541330.

## Never confuse

An initial review of SBA Form 355 indicating that the applicant, including its affiliates, is small leads SBA to process the Certificate of Competency application, while a review indicating that the applicant is other than small leads SBA to initiate a formal size determination and withhold further processing until a formal size determination is made [@CL-MKT-2046; @CL-MKT-2047; @CL-MKT-2048]. For a section 8(d) subcontract, the size standard is the one for the NAICS code that the prime contractor believes best describes the product or service being acquired by the subcontract, except that subcontracts for engineering services awarded under the National Energy Policy Act of 1992 have the same size standard as Military and Aerospace Equipment and Military Weapons under NAICS code 541330 [@CL-MKT-2071; @CL-MKT-2050; @CL-MKT-2051]. Background on the certificate process is in [Certificate of Competency general rules](../programs/mkt-coc-general-and-referral.md).
