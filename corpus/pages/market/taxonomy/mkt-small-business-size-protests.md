## Purpose

Shows who may protest a small business representation or rerepresentation, the deadlines, what a protest must contain and who decides.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-MKT-0312] |
| Codified citation | FAR 19.302 [@CL-MKT-0312; @CL-MKT-0313; @CL-MKT-0314; @CL-MKT-0315; @CL-MKT-0316; @CL-MKT-0317; @CL-MKT-0318; @CL-MKT-0319; @CL-MKT-0350; @CL-MKT-0351; @CL-MKT-0352; @CL-MKT-0353; @CL-MKT-0354; @CL-MKT-0355] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-MKT-0312] |
| Applies to | Protests of an offeror's small business representation in a specific offer, and of a contractor's rerepresentation [@CL-MKT-0312; @CL-MKT-0314] |

An offeror, the contracting officer, SBA, or another interested party may protest the small business representation of an offeror in a specific offer for a contract [@CL-MKT-0312]. For competitive 8(a) contracts, the filing of a protest is limited to an offeror, the contracting officer, or SBA [@CL-MKT-0312]. The protest, or its confirmation if it was initiated orally, shall be in writing and shall contain the basis for the protest with specific, detailed evidence to support the allegation that the offeror is not small [@CL-MKT-0313]. A contracting officer who receives a protest (other than from the contracting officer) whether timely or not, or when the contracting officer protests the small business representation of an offeror, or rerepresentation of a contractor, shall promptly forward the protest to the SBA Government Contracting Area Director at the Government Contracting Area Office serving the area in which the headquarters of the offeror is located [@CL-MKT-0314; @CL-MKT-1701]. SBA decides size status through its Area Office [@CL-MKT-0318].

To be timely, a protest by any concern or other interested party must be received by the contracting officer by the close of business of the fifth business day after one of several listed events [@CL-MKT-0315]. For sealed bid acquisitions, the first listed event is bid opening [@CL-MKT-0315]. For negotiated acquisitions, one listed event is receipt of the special notification that identifies the apparently successful offeror, including, among other cases, partial set-asides and reserves of multiple-award contracts [@CL-MKT-0316]. A protest filed by the contracting officer or SBA is always considered timely whether filed before or after award, except as provided in paragraph (d)(6) of that section [@CL-MKT-0317]. Within 15 business days after receipt of a protest or request for a formal size determination, or within any extension of time granted by the contracting officer, the SBA Area Office will determine the size status of the challenged concern [@CL-MKT-0318].

After receiving a protest involving an offeror being considered for award, the contracting officer shall not award the contract until SBA has made a size determination or 15 business days have expired since SBA's receipt of a protest, whichever occurs first, subject to an exception [@CL-MKT-0350]. The exception is that award shall not be withheld when the contracting officer determines in writing that an award must be made to protect the public interest [@CL-MKT-0351]. If SBA has not made a determination within 15 business days, or within any extension of time granted by the contracting officer, the contracting officer may award the contract after determining in writing that there is an immediate need to award the contract and that waiting until SBA makes its determination will be disadvantageous to the Government [@CL-MKT-9409; @CL-MKT-0352]. If a protest challenges the small business status of an offeror not being considered for award, the contracting officer is not required to suspend contract action [@CL-MKT-0353].

When a concern is found to be other than small under a protest concerning a size status rerepresentation made in accordance with the postaward rerepresentation clause, a contracting officer may permit contract performance to continue [@CL-MKT-0354]. The contracting officer may also issue orders or exercise options in that case, because the contract remains a valid contract [@CL-MKT-0355].

## How it works

A protest is raised with the contracting officer, who forwards it to SBA, and SBA decides [@CL-MKT-0314; @CL-MKT-0318]. Timeliness depends on who files and on the event that starts the clock [@CL-MKT-0315; @CL-MKT-0317]. Whether award waits depends on whether the offeror is being considered for award [@CL-MKT-0350; @CL-MKT-0353]. A concern or other interested party whose protest has been denied by an SBA Government Contracting Area Director, an adversely affected concern, or the SBA Associate Administrator for Government Contracting may appeal a government contracting area office's size determination [@CL-MKT-0319; @CL-MKT-1611].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

Sealed bids are opened on day 0. A competing bidder believes the apparent low bidder is not small. Answer: the competitor's protest must reach the contracting officer by close of business on the fifth business day after bid opening, in writing, with specific, detailed evidence. The contracting officer forwards it to the SBA Government Contracting Area Director. The SBA Area Office determines size status within 15 business days after receipt unless the contracting officer extends the time. A protest by the contracting officer would be timely whenever filed, except as the premature-protest paragraph provides. While the protest is pending and the bidder is being considered for award, the contracting officer holds award until SBA decides or 15 business days pass, unless the contracting officer determines in writing that award must be made to protect the public interest.

## Never confuse

A size protest asks whether an offeror is small. It is not the offeror's own representation, which is on the [Offeror size and status representation](mkt-offeror-size-and-status-representation.md) card. It is not a rerepresentation after award, which is on the [Rerepresentation after award](mkt-size-rerepresentation-after-award.md) card. The size standard behind the protest is on the [Size standards and NAICS codes](mkt-size-standards-and-naics-codes.md) card. A HUBZone status protest is a different protest with its own decider and deadlines, and it is on the [Protesting a firm's HUBZone status](mkt-hubzone-status-protests.md) card.
