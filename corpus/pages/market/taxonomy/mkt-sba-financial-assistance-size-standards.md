## Purpose

Shows the alternative size standards for SBA business loans, disaster loans, surety bond guarantees and investment company financing, the date SBA measures an applicant's size, and the size certification an applicant is deemed to make, under 13 CFR 121.301, 121.302 and 121.303 [@CL-MKT-0671; @CL-MKT-0678; @CL-MKT-0711].

| Applies to | Applicants for SBA financial assistance, with separate standards for Business Loans other than 7(a) loans, Disaster Loans other than physical disaster loans, 7(a) and Development Company programs, the SBIC program, and Surety Bond Guarantee assistance [@CL-MKT-0671; @CL-MKT-0674; @CL-MKT-0676; @CL-MKT-0706] |

For Business Loans other than 7(a) Business Loans, and for Disaster Loans other than physical disaster loans, an applicant business concern must satisfy two criteria [@CL-MKT-0671]. One listed criterion is that the size of the applicant alone, without affiliates, must not exceed the size standard designated for the industry in which the applicant is primarily engaged [@CL-MKT-0672]. The other is that the size of the applicant combined with its affiliates must not exceed the size standard designated for either the primary industry of the applicant alone or the primary industry of the applicant and its affiliates, whichever is higher [@CL-MKT-0673].

For 7(a) Business Loans and Development Company programs, an applicant must meet one of the listed standards [@CL-MKT-0674]. One is the same standards as above [@CL-MKT-0674]. The other is tangible net worth, including affiliates, not in excess of $20 million, together with average net income after Federal income taxes, excluding any carry over losses, for the preceding two completed fiscal years not in excess of $6.5 million [@CL-MKT-0675]. Under that standard, first, if the applicant is not required by law to pay State (and local, if any) income taxes at the enterprise level, its net income is multiplied by the marginal State income tax rate (or the combined State and local income tax rates, as applicable) that would have applied if it were a taxable corporation [@CL-MKT-9400]; second, its net income, less any deduction for State and local income taxes calculated under the first step, is multiplied by the marginal Federal income tax rate that would have applied if the applicant were a taxable corporation [@CL-MKT-0190]; and third, the results obtained in those two steps are summed [@CL-MKT-0191]. For the Small Business Investment Company program, an applicant must meet one of the listed standards, one being the same standards as above [@CL-MKT-0676]. The other is tangible net worth, including affiliates, not in excess of $24 million, together with average net income on the same basis, excluding any carry-over losses, for the preceding two completed fiscal years not in excess of $8 million, subject to the rule that, for an applicant that is not required by law to pay Federal income taxes at the enterprise level but is required to pass income through to its shareholders, partners, beneficiaries or other equitable owners, net income after Federal income taxes is its net income reduced by an amount computed in three steps: multiplying net income by the marginal State (or combined State and local) income tax rate that would have applied if it were a taxable corporation when the applicant is not required by law to pay State (and local, if any) income taxes at the enterprise level, multiplying net income less that State and local tax deduction by the marginal Federal income tax rate that would have applied if it were a taxable corporation, and adding the two results [@CL-MKT-0677; @CL-MKT-9329; @CL-MKT-9330; @CL-MKT-1703; @CL-MKT-1704].

For Surety Bond Guarantee assistance, a business concern, combined with its affiliates, must meet the size standard for the primary industry in which the concern, combined with its affiliates, is engaged [@CL-MKT-0706]. The applicable size standards for SBA's financial assistance programs, excluding the Surety Bond Guarantee assistance program, are increased by 25 percent whenever the applicant agrees to use all of the financial assistance within a labor surplus area [@CL-MKT-0707].

## How it works

The size status of an applicant for SBA financial assistance is determined as of the date SBA accepts the application for processing, except for applications under the Preferred Lenders Program (PLP), the Disaster Loan program, the SBIC program, and the New Markets Venture Capital (NMCV) program [@CL-MKT-0678; @CL-MKT-0192]. For the Preferred Lenders Program, size is determined as of the date of approval of the loan by the Preferred Lender [@CL-MKT-0708]. For disaster loan assistance other than physical disaster loans, size status is determined as of the date the disaster commenced, as set forth in the Disaster Declaration [@CL-MKT-0679]. For financial assistance from a Small Business Investment Company licensee or a New Markets Venture Capital company, size is determined as of the date a concern's application is accepted for processing by that licensee or company [@CL-MKT-0709]. Changes in size after the applicable date when size is determined will not disqualify an applicant for assistance [@CL-MKT-0710].

A concern that submits an application for financial assistance is deemed to have certified that it is small under the applicable size standard, and SBA may question the concern's status based on information supplied in the application or from any other source [@CL-MKT-0711]. A small business investment company, a development company, a surety bond company, or a preferred lender may accept as true the size information provided by an applicant, unless credible evidence to the contrary is apparent [@CL-MKT-0712].

A concern that applies to refinance an existing SBA loan or guarantee will be considered small for the refinancing even though its size has increased since the date of the original financing to exceed its applicable size standard, provided that the increase in size is due to natural growth (as distinguished from merger, acquisition or similar management action) and SBA determines that refinancing is necessary to protect the Government's financial interest [@CL-MKT-2351; @CL-MKT-2352]. If the concern's size has increased other than by natural growth, the concern and its affiliates must be small at the time SBA accepts the application for refinancing for processing [@CL-MKT-2353].

A concern qualified as small for a particular procurement, including an 8(a) subcontract, is small for financial assistance directly and primarily relating to the performance of the particular procurement [@CL-MKT-2354].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An applicant for a 7(a) loan has tangible net worth of 15 million including affiliates, and average net income of 4 million over the preceding two completed fiscal years. It does not need to pass the two-criteria test, because the net worth and net income standard is an alternative. It is under 20 million of net worth and 6.5 million of net income, so it meets that standard. The loan is not under the Preferred Lenders Program, so its size is measured on the day SBA accepts the application for processing, and growth after that day does not disqualify it.

## Never confuse

These standards apply to SBA's own financial assistance, not to a federal procurement. Procurement size standards and NAICS codes are on the [Size standards, NAICS codes and appeals of the contracting officer's code](mkt-size-standards-and-naics-codes.md) card, and the procurement measurement date is on the [When a concern's size status is measured](mkt-sba-size-measurement-date.md) card, which uses a different date.
