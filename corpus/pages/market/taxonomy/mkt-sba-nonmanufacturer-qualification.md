## Purpose

Shows, under 13 CFR 121.406 [@CL-MKT-0389], what an offeror must meet to qualify as a small business on a supply contract when it did not make the item itself.

To qualify as a small business concern for a small business set-aside, service-disabled veteran-owned small business set-aside or sole source contract, HUBZone set-aside or sole source contract, WOSB or EDWOSB set-aside or sole source contract, 8(a) set-aside or sole source contract, partial set-aside, or set-aside of an order against a multiple award contract to provide manufactured products or other supply items [@CL-MKT-0389; @CL-MKT-0193; @CL-MKT-0194], an offeror must either be the manufacturer or producer of the end item being procured, which must be manufactured or produced in the United States, or comply with the requirements of paragraph (b), (c) or (d) of the section as a nonmanufacturer, a kit assembler or a supplier under Simplified Acquisition Procedures [@CL-MKT-0390; @CL-MKT-0527].

A firm may qualify as a small business concern for a requirement to provide manufactured products or other supply items as a nonmanufacturer if it meets the conditions listed in paragraph (b)(1) [@CL-MKT-0391]. One condition is that the firm does not exceed 500 employees, or 150 employees for the Information Technology Value Added Reseller exception to NAICS Code 541519, found at section 121.201, footnote 18 [@CL-MKT-0392]. Two more are that the firm is primarily engaged in the retail or wholesale trade and normally sells the type of item being supplied, and that it takes ownership or possession of the items with its own personnel, equipment or facilities in a manner consistent with industry practice [@CL-MKT-0393]. The last listed condition is that the firm will supply the end item of a small business manufacturer, processor or producer made in the United States, or obtains a waiver of that requirement pursuant to paragraph (b)(5) of the section [@CL-MKT-0394].

The nonmanufacturer rule, the limitations on subcontracting requirements and the ostensible subcontracting rule do not apply to small business set-aside acquisitions with an estimated value between the micro-purchase threshold and the simplified acquisition threshold [@CL-MKT-0528].

## How it works

The nonmanufacturer rule applies only to procurements that have been assigned a manufacturing or supply NAICS code, or the Information Technology Value Added Resellers (ITVAR) exception to NAICS code 541519 [@CL-MKT-0395]. The nonmanufacturer rule does not apply to contracts that have been assigned a service (except for the ITVAR exception to NAICS code 541519), construction, or specialty trade construction NAICS code [@CL-MKT-1184]. In a multiple item acquisition, a waiver of the nonmanufacturer rule is not required if at least 50 percent of the estimated contract value is composed of items that are manufactured by small business concerns [@CL-MKT-0396]. Acquisitions for supplies must be classified under the appropriate manufacturing or supply NAICS code, not under a Wholesale Trade or Retail Trade NAICS code [@CL-MKT-0546]. Waivers are covered in [Waivers of the Nonmanufacturer Rule](../taxonomy/mkt-sba-nonmanufacturer-rule-waivers.md), and the NAICS code choice is covered in [Which size standard applies to a federal procurement](../taxonomy/mkt-sba-size-standard-designation.md).

The section that asks what the Nonmanufacturer Rule is says that the rule is set forth in section 121.406(b) [@CL-MKT-2397].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A reseller with 300 employees offers on a small business set-aside under a supply NAICS code, in an acquisition above the simplified acquisition threshold. It is primarily engaged in wholesale trade, normally sells this type of item, takes delivery in its own warehouse, and supplies the end item of a small business manufacturer made in the United States. It meets each listed condition, so it qualifies as a nonmanufacturer.

## Never confuse

Qualifying as a small business nonmanufacturer on a set-aside or sole-source supply contract under 13 CFR 121.406(a) [@CL-MKT-0193; @CL-MKT-0194; @CL-MKT-0389] differs from obtaining an SBA class or individual waiver when no small business manufacturer exists on [Waivers of the Nonmanufacturer Rule](../taxonomy/mkt-sba-nonmanufacturer-rule-waivers.md) [@CL-MKT-0397], and is distinct from the subcontracting percentage caps in [Limitations on subcontracting and the nonmanufacturer rule](../programs/mkt-limitations-on-subcontracting-and-nonmanufacturer-rule.md) [@CL-MKT-0226].

Whether the company makes its end items itself or resells other firms' products. [@Q-019]
