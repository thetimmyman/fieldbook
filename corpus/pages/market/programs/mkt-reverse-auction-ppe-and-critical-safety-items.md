## Purpose

Explains the Defense Department rule that bars contracting officers from using a reverse auction for designated personal protective equipment and aviation critical safety items when the requiring activity advises of combat casualty risk.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-MKT-2116] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 217.7801 [@CL-MKT-2116] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-MKT-2116] |
| Applies to | Procurements of items the requiring activity designates as personal protective equipment or an aviation critical safety item, when it gives the combat casualty advice [@CL-MKT-2116; @CL-MKT-2117] |

Contracting officers shall not use reverse auctions when procuring items designated by the requiring activity as personal protective equipment or an aviation critical safety item [@CL-MKT-2116]. That prohibition applies when the requiring activity advises the contracting officer that the level of quality or failure of the equipment or item could result in combat casualties [@CL-MKT-2117]. Both elements are needed: the designation by the requiring activity and its advice about combat casualties [@CL-MKT-2116; @CL-MKT-2117].

The section refers to 252.209-7010 for the definition and identification of critical safety items [@CL-MKT-2118]. It states that the rule follows section 814 of the National Defense Authorization Act for Fiscal Year 2017, as amended by section 882 of the National Defense Authorization Act for Fiscal Year 2018 [@CL-MKT-2119].

## How it works

The requiring activity designates the item and gives the advice; the contracting officer then may not use a reverse auction for that procurement [@CL-MKT-2116; @CL-MKT-2117]. The prohibition binds the contracting officer, not the contractor, and this section states no exception, waiver or approval route. The reference to 252.209-7010 points to where critical safety items are defined and identified [@CL-MKT-2118].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A requiring activity designates a batch of protective masks as personal protective equipment and advises the contracting officer that a failure of quality could result in combat casualties. The contracting officer may not use a reverse auction for that buy and chooses another method [@CL-MKT-2116; @CL-MKT-2117]. If the requiring activity gave no such advice, this section would not bar the auction, but the general reverse auction rules separately bar reverse auctions for the acquisition of personal protective equipment, so masks would still be barred [@CL-MKT-2117; @CL-MKT-1605].

## Never confuse

This section is the Defense supplement's prohibition for designated items; it sets no dollar threshold, and it does not displace the separate bar on reverse auctions for personal protective equipment in the general rules [@CL-MKT-1605]. The general limits on when a reverse auction is suitable and how it is awarded are on [Reverse auctions: suitability and award](mkt-reverse-auctions-suitability-and-award.md). The prohibition here turns on the requiring activity's designation and its advice, not on how the contracting officer would otherwise run the auction [@CL-MKT-2116; @CL-MKT-2117].
