## Purpose

Shows what master and commercial plans are, how a master plan is incorporated and how a commercial plan is used, the reporting duties through the Individual Subcontract Report and the Summary Subcontract Report in the Electronic Subcontracting Reporting System, the flowdown assurances that reach subcontractors, and the material-breach rule.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-MKT-0826] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.219-9 [@CL-MKT-0826; @CL-MKT-0829] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-MKT-0826] |
| Applies to | Contractors, other than small business concerns, with a master plan, a commercial plan, or the listed reporting and flowdown assurances in their plans [@CL-MKT-0821; @CL-MKT-0826; @CL-MKT-0827; @CL-MKT-0828] |

A master subcontracting plan contains all the required elements of an individual plan except goals, and may be incorporated into individual plans if it has been approved [@CL-MKT-0826]. A master plan on a plant or division-wide basis that contains all the elements the clause requires, except goals, may be incorporated by reference as part of the offeror's plan, provided the conditions that follow are met [@CL-MKT-0856]. One set of conditions is that the master plan has been approved, and that the offeror ensures it is updated as necessary and provides copies of the approved plan, including evidence of its approval, to the contracting officer [@CL-MKT-0856; @CL-MKT-0857]. A further condition is that goals, and any deviations from the master plan that the contracting officer considers necessary to satisfy the contract's requirements, are set forth in the individual subcontracting plan [@CL-MKT-0856; @CL-MKT-0858].

A commercial plan is a subcontracting plan, including goals, that covers the offeror's fiscal year and applies to the entire production of commercial products and commercial services sold by the entire company or a portion of it [@CL-MKT-0827]. A commercial plan is the preferred type of plan for contractors furnishing commercial products and commercial services, and it shall relate to the offeror's planned subcontracting generally, for both commercial and Government business, rather than solely to the Government contract [@CL-MKT-0859]. Once a contractor's commercial plan has been approved, the Government will not require another subcontracting plan from the same contractor while the plan remains in effect [@CL-MKT-0860], and that holds as long as the product or service being provided continues to meet the definition of a commercial product or commercial service [@CL-MKT-0860; @CL-MKT-0861].

## How it works

Reporting begins with the plan itself. One listed plan element is assurances that the offeror will cooperate in any studies or surveys as may be required and submit periodic reports so that the Government can determine the extent of compliance with the plan [@CL-MKT-0828]. One listed assurance is that the offeror will submit the Individual Subcontract Report and/or the Summary Subcontract Report, in accordance with a later paragraph of the clause, using the Electronic Subcontracting Reporting System [@CL-MKT-0829].

The Individual Subcontract Report is not required for commercial plans, is required for each contract containing an individual subcontracting plan, and is submitted semi-annually during contract performance for the periods ending March 31 and September 30 [@CL-MKT-0864]. It is also required for each contract within 30 days of contract completion, and it is due 30 days after the close of each reporting period unless the contracting officer directs otherwise [@CL-MKT-0864; @CL-MKT-0865].

A Summary Subcontract Report submitted under an individual plan encompasses all subcontracting under prime contracts and subcontracts with an executive agency, regardless of the dollar value of the subcontracts [@CL-MKT-0866]. It shall be submitted annually by October 30 for the twelve month period ending September 30 [@CL-MKT-0866; @CL-MKT-0867]. A contractor with a commercial plan complies with the reporting requirements by submitting one Summary Subcontract Report in the system for all contracts covered by its commercial plan [@CL-MKT-0862]. That report shall include all subcontract awards under the plan in effect during the Government's fiscal year and all indirect costs, and shall be submitted annually within thirty days after the end of the Government's fiscal year [@CL-MKT-0863].

For flowdown, one listed plan element is assurances that the offeror will include the clause entitled Utilization of Small Business Concerns in all subcontracts that offer further subcontracting opportunities [@CL-MKT-0830]. A further assurance is that the offeror will require all subcontractors, except small business concerns and entities treated as small by statute for certain purposes, to meet the conditions the paragraph states [@CL-MKT-0831]. Those conditions cover subcontractors that receive subcontracts above the applicable threshold on the date of subcontract award, with further subcontracting possibilities, and require them to adopt a plan that complies with the clause [@CL-MKT-0832]. The clause names the threshold only by reference to section 19.702(a) [@CL-MKT-0832]. For a contract in a negotiated acquisition, the amount that section states as the level a contract is expected to exceed is $900,000, or $2 million for construction, as shown on the [When a subcontracting plan is required](mkt-subcontracting-plan-when-required.md) card [@CL-MKT-0235].

Another listed assurance is that the offeror will ensure that its subcontractors with subcontracting plans agree to submit the Individual Subcontract Report and/or the Summary Subcontract Report using the system [@CL-MKT-0868]. Subcontracting plans are not required from subcontractors when the prime contract contains the clause at FAR 52.212-5 [@CL-MKT-0869]. A plan is also not required from a subcontractor that provides a commercial product or commercial service subject to the clause at FAR 52.244-6 under a prime contract [@CL-MKT-0869; @CL-MKT-0870].

Failure of the contractor or a subcontractor to comply in good faith with the Utilization of Small Business Concerns clause, or with an approved plan required by the plan clause, shall be a material breach of the contract [@CL-MKT-0871]. The clause itself is on the [Utilization of small business concerns](mkt-utilization-of-small-business-concerns-clause.md) card.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A prime with a plan awards a subcontract of 5,000,000 to a large supplier that has further subcontracting possibilities. Suppose the amount is above the applicable threshold on the award date, and the prime contract does not contain the clause at 52.212-5. Answer: the prime's plan assurance means it requires that supplier to adopt a compliant plan. If the supplier were a small business concern, the exception would apply and no plan would be required.

A contractor with an approved commercial plan covers three Government contracts. Answer: it submits one summary report in the system for all three, within 30 days after the end of the Government's fiscal year, and it does not submit an individual report for them.

## Never confuse

A master plan has all the elements of an individual plan except goals [@CL-MKT-0826], while an individual plan has goals based on planned subcontracting for the specific contract [@CL-MKT-0823]. A commercial plan has goals and covers the offeror's fiscal year [@CL-MKT-0827], so it is not a master plan. The two also report differently: a commercial plan files one Summary Subcontract Report for all its contracts [@CL-MKT-0862], and the Individual Subcontract Report is not required for it [@CL-MKT-0864]. The scope and the individual plan are on the [scope and required elements](mkt-subcontracting-plan-clause-scope-and-required-elements.md) card for this clause. The rest of the plan content and the liquidated damages for a failed good faith effort are on the [Subcontracting plan content, good faith effort and liquidated damages](mkt-subcontracting-plan-content-and-compliance.md) card and the [Postaward rerepresentation and subcontracting liquidated damages](mkt-postaward-rerepresentation-and-subcontracting-liquidated-damages.md) card. When a plan is required is on the [When a subcontracting plan is required](mkt-subcontracting-plan-when-required.md) card.
