## Purpose

Shows who approves an undefinitized contract action (UCA), how much of the not-to-exceed price may be obligated before definitization, and which actions fall outside those limits.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-MKT-1545] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 217.7404-1, DFARS 217.7404-4 and DFARS 217.7404-5 [@CL-MKT-1545; @CL-MKT-1548; @CL-MKT-1551] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-MKT-1545] |
| Applies to | Approval of undefinitized contract actions and the funds obligated before definitization [@CL-MKT-1545; @CL-MKT-1548] |

The contracting officer shall obtain approval from the head of the contracting activity before entering into a UCA [@CL-MKT-1545]. The request must fully explain the need to begin performance before definitization, including the adverse impact on agency requirements from delay [@CL-MKT-1615]. The same approval route covers two further steps: including requirements for non-urgent spare parts and support equipment, and modifying the scope of a UCA when performance has already begun [@CL-MKT-1545; @CL-MKT-1546; @CL-MKT-1547]. The request for either of those two steps should show that the step is consistent with good business practices and in the best interest of the United States [@CL-MKT-1546; @CL-MKT-1547].

The FAR's general definitions say contracting activity means an element of an agency designated by the agency head and delegated broad authority regarding acquisition functions [@CL-MKT-9196].

The Government shall not obligate more than 50 percent of the not-to-exceed price before definitization [@CL-MKT-1548]. If a contractor submits a qualifying proposal before 50 percent of the not-to-exceed price has been obligated, the limit may be increased to no more than 75 percent [@CL-MKT-1549]. The section points to DFARS 232.102-70 for provisional delivery payments [@CL-MKT-1549]. When deciding how much to obligate, the contracting officer shall assess the contractor's proposal for the undefinitized period and obligate funds only in an amount consistent with the contractor's requirements for that period [@CL-MKT-1550].

The limitations in DFARS 217.7404-2, 217.7404-3 and 217.7404-4, which include the obligation limit above, do not apply to UCAs for the purchase of initial spares [@CL-MKT-1551; @CL-MKT-1548]. The head of an agency may waive the limitations in DFARS 217.7404(a), 217.7404-2, 217.7404-3 and 217.7404-4 for UCAs if the head of the agency determines that the waiver is necessary to support a contingency operation or a humanitarian or peacekeeping operation [@CL-MKT-1552]. Under that paragraph the head of the agency makes the determination [@CL-MKT-1552].

The limitation in DFARS 217.7404(a) that the waiver names concerns foreign military sales contracts [@CL-MKT-1552; @CL-MKT-1695]. A contracting officer may not enter into a UCA for a foreign military sale unless, among other conditions, the UCA provides for agreement upon contractual terms, specifications, and price by the end of the 180-day period beginning on the date on which the contractor submits a qualifying proposal [@CL-MKT-1695]. A further condition is that the contracting officer obtains approval from the head of the contracting activity in accordance with DFARS 217.7404-1 [@CL-MKT-1696]. For those requirements, the head of the contracting activity may also waive them if a waiver is necessary to support a contingency operation or a humanitarian or peacekeeping operation [@CL-MKT-1697].

## How it works

The approval and obligation duties bind Government officials, not the contractor. The contractor's part is its proposal: a qualifying proposal submitted before half of the not-to-exceed price is obligated is what makes the higher limit possible [@CL-MKT-1549]. A qualifying proposal is a proposal that contains sufficient information to enable DoD to conduct meaningful analyses and audits of the information contained in the proposal [@CL-MKT-1698].

Approval comes first, on a request that explains the need to start early and the harm of delay [@CL-MKT-1545; @CL-MKT-1615]. After that, the contracting officer sizes each obligation to the contractor's requirements for the undefinitized period, within the limit [@CL-MKT-1548; @CL-MKT-1550]. Initial spares and an agency-head waiver sit outside the limitations [@CL-MKT-1551; @CL-MKT-1552]. Provisional delivery payments are on [provisional delivery payments](../../contract-finance/cash/ctf-dod-provisional-delivery-payments.md), and the wider topic is on [undefinitized contract actions](../../estimating/pricing/est-dod-undefinitized-contract-actions.md).

Every UCA shall include a not-to-exceed price [@CL-MKT-2347]. The obligation limit above is stated as a percentage of that price [@CL-MKT-1548].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A UCA has a not-to-exceed price of 10,000,000. The head of the contracting activity approved entering into the UCA before it was awarded [@CL-MKT-1545]. Before definitization the Government may obligate no more than 5,000,000 [@CL-MKT-1548]. The contractor submits a qualifying proposal when 4,000,000 has been obligated, which is before 50 percent of the not-to-exceed price has been obligated, so the limit may be raised to no more than 7,500,000 [@CL-MKT-1549]. The contracting officer assesses the proposal and finds that the contractor needs 6,000,000 for the undefinitized period, so only 6,000,000 is obligated rather than the full 7,500,000 [@CL-MKT-1550]. Had the proposal arrived after 5,000,000 was already obligated, it would not have come before 50 percent was obligated and the higher limit would not be available under that paragraph [@CL-MKT-1549]. If the same UCA were for the purchase of initial spares, the obligation limitation would not apply [@CL-MKT-1551].

## Never confuse

Approval to enter a UCA is not approval of a definitized price, and the obligation limit governs funds obligated before definitization, not the price negotiated later [@CL-MKT-1548]. The obligation limit is a separate rule from the definitization schedule on the linked page on [undefinitized contract actions](../../estimating/pricing/est-dod-undefinitized-contract-actions.md). The initial-spares exclusion and the agency-head waiver name the limitations in DFARS 217.7404-2, 217.7404-3 and 217.7404-4, and the waiver also names DFARS 217.7404(a), so neither lists the approval step in DFARS 217.7404-1 [@CL-MKT-1551; @CL-MKT-1552; @CL-MKT-1545].
