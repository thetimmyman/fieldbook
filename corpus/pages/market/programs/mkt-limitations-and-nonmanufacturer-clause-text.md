## Purpose

Shows the percentage limits in the limitations on subcontracting clause by type of contract, what counts toward them, and what the nonmanufacturer rule clause requires. The companion card covers the acquisition-rule text.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-MKT-0834] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.219-14 and FAR 52.219-33 [@CL-MKT-0834; @CL-MKT-0839] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.219-14 [@CL-MKT-0197]; RFO FAR 52.219-33 [@CL-MKT-0260] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-MKT-0834] |
| Applies to | The listed cases in the limitations clause, which include contracts set aside for small business concerns [@CL-MKT-0833] |

The limitations clause applies only to the six cases in FAR 52.219-14(c)(1)-(6): contracts set aside for any of the small business concerns identified in FAR 19.000(a)(3), a part or parts of a multiple-award contract set aside for any of those concerns, contracts awarded on a sole-source basis in accordance with subparts 19.8, 19.13, 19.14, and 19.15, orders expected to exceed the simplified acquisition threshold that are set aside for small business concerns under multiple-award contracts as described in 8.405-5 and 16.505(b)(2)(i)(F) or issued directly to small business concerns under multiple-award contracts as described in 19.504(c)(1)(ii), orders, regardless of dollar value, that are set aside or issued directly to qualifying concerns in accordance with subparts 19.8, 19.13, 19.14, or 19.15 under multiple-award contracts as the cited provisions describe, and contracts using the HUBZone price evaluation preference to award to a HUBZone small business concern unless the concern waived the evaluation preference [@CL-MKT-0833; @CL-MKT-1824; @CL-MKT-9068; @CL-MKT-9069; @CL-MKT-9070; @CL-MKT-9071; @CL-MKT-1825]. By submission of an offer and execution of a contract, the contractor agrees to limits that depend on the NAICS code assigned to the contract [@CL-MKT-0874]. For a contract assigned a NAICS code for services (except construction), the contractor will not pay more than 50 percent of the amount paid by the Government for contract performance to subcontractors that are not similarly situated entities [@CL-MKT-0874; @CL-MKT-0834]. For supplies other than procurement from a nonmanufacturer, the limit is 50 percent with the cost of materials excluded [@CL-MKT-0874; @CL-MKT-0835]. For general construction it is 85 percent with the cost of materials excluded [@CL-MKT-0874; @CL-MKT-0836], and for construction by special trade contractors it is 75 percent with the cost of materials excluded [@CL-MKT-0874; @CL-MKT-0837].

## How it works

Each limit caps the share of the amount paid by the Government that may go to subcontractors that are not similarly situated entities [@CL-MKT-0834; @CL-MKT-0835; @CL-MKT-0836; @CL-MKT-0837]. For supplies, general construction and construction by special trade contractors, the amount excludes the cost of materials [@CL-MKT-0835; @CL-MKT-0836; @CL-MKT-0837]. A similarly situated entity is a first-tier subcontractor, including an independent contractor, that has the same small business program status as that which qualified the prime contractor for the award—for example, for a small business set-aside contract, any small business concern, without regard to its socioeconomic status—and that is also considered small for the size standard under the NAICS code the prime contractor assigned to the subcontract [@CL-MKT-0876; @CL-MKT-1708; @CL-MKT-0877]. An independent contractor shall be considered a subcontractor [@CL-MKT-0875]. In every category, work that a similarly situated entity further subcontracts counts toward the prime contractor's limit: the 50 percent amount for services [@CL-MKT-0838], the 50 percent amount for supplies [@CL-MKT-0971], the 85 percent amount for general construction [@CL-MKT-0972], and the 75 percent amount for construction by special trade contractors [@CL-MKT-0973]. On a contract with both services and supplies, the 50 percent limitation applies only to the service portion under the services paragraph [@CL-MKT-0838] and only to the supply portion under the supplies paragraph [@CL-MKT-0971]. For the nonmanufacturer rule, the contractor is to provide an end item that a small business has manufactured, processed, or produced in the United States or its outlying areas, and kit assemblers who are nonmanufacturers are directed to a separate paragraph [@CL-MKT-0839]. Two further requirements listed for the contractor are that it be primarily engaged in the retail or wholesale trade and normally sell the type of item being supplied, and that it take ownership or possession of the items with its personnel, equipment, or facilities in a manner consistent with industry practice, for example providing storage, transportation, or delivery [@CL-MKT-0839; @CL-MKT-0879; @CL-MKT-1709]. For a kit of supplies, at least 50 percent of the total cost of the components shall be manufactured, processed, or produced in the United States or its outlying areas by small business concerns [@CL-MKT-0840]. The nonmanufacturer rule clause does not apply to contracts awarded pursuant to the unrestricted portion of a partial set-aside or to a contractor that is the manufacturer of the product or end item [@CL-MKT-0878].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text because Class Deviation 2026-O0038 directs it, effective February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. RFO FAR 52.219-14 states the definition of a similarly situated entity as a first-tier subcontractor with the same small business program status [@CL-MKT-0876; @CL-MKT-1708; @CL-MKT-0195] that is considered small for the size standard under the NAICS code assigned to the subcontract [@CL-MKT-0877; @CL-MKT-0196]. It states that an independent contractor shall be considered a subcontractor [@CL-MKT-0875; @CL-MKT-0198]. In RFO FAR 52.219-14(c)(1)-(6), the clause applies to contracts and parts of multiple-award contracts set aside for the identified small business concerns [@CL-MKT-0833; @CL-MKT-0197], contracts awarded on a sole-source basis in accordance with sections 19.105, 19.106, 19.107, and 19.108 [@CL-MKT-1826], orders expected to exceed the simplified acquisition threshold that are set aside for small business concerns under multiple-award contracts as described in 8.4 and 16.5 [@CL-MKT-9066], orders regardless of dollar value that are set aside in accordance with sections 19.105, 19.106, 19.107, and 19.108 under multiple-award contracts as described in 8.4 and 16.5 [@CL-MKT-9067], and contracts using the HUBZone price evaluation preference to award to a HUBZone small business concern unless the concern waived the evaluation preference [@CL-MKT-1827], whereas codified items (c)(4)(ii) and (c)(5)(ii) also include orders issued directly under 19.504(c)(1)(ii) [@CL-MKT-9068; @CL-MKT-9069; @CL-MKT-9070; @CL-MKT-9071]. The RFO text keeps the agreement made by submission of an offer and execution of a contract [@CL-MKT-0874; @CL-MKT-0199]. It keeps the four limits: 50 percent for services (except construction) [@CL-MKT-0834; @CL-MKT-0251], 50 percent with the cost of materials excluded for supplies other than procurement from a nonmanufacturer [@CL-MKT-0835; @CL-MKT-0253], 85 percent with the cost of materials excluded for general construction [@CL-MKT-0836; @CL-MKT-0255], and 75 percent with the cost of materials excluded for construction by special trade contractors [@CL-MKT-0837; @CL-MKT-0257]. It keeps the rule that work a similarly situated entity further subcontracts counts toward the prime contractor's limit, for services with the mixed services and supplies rule [@CL-MKT-0838; @CL-MKT-0252], for supplies with the mixed supplies and services rule [@CL-MKT-0971; @CL-MKT-0254], for general construction [@CL-MKT-0972; @CL-MKT-0256], and for special trade construction [@CL-MKT-0973; @CL-MKT-0258]. RFO FAR 52.219-33 states the exclusion for the unrestricted portion of a partial set-aside and for a contractor that is the manufacturer [@CL-MKT-0878; @CL-MKT-0259], the end item and kit assembler direction [@CL-MKT-0839; @CL-MKT-0260], the retail or wholesale trade and ownership or possession requirements [@CL-MKT-0879; @CL-MKT-0261], and the 50 percent kit component rule [@CL-MKT-0840; @CL-MKT-0262].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

The Government pays 3,000,000 under a general construction contract, and all of its subcontractors are not similarly situated entities. Materials cost 1,000,000, so the base is 2,000,000. The limit is 85 percent of the base, so at most 1,700,000 may go to subcontractors that are not similarly situated entities. Paying 1,800,000 would break the limit.

## Never confuse

Under FAR 52.219-14(e), the limitations on subcontracting cap the percentage of the amount paid by the Government that the contractor may pay to subcontractors that are not similarly situated entities [@CL-MKT-0834; @CL-MKT-0835; @CL-MKT-0836; @CL-MKT-0837]. Under FAR 52.219-33(c), the nonmanufacturer rule requires the contractor to provide an end item that a small business has manufactured, processed, or produced in the United States or its outlying areas [@CL-MKT-0839]. The acquisition-rule text, the compliance timing and the size conditions are on the [Limitations on subcontracting and the nonmanufacturer rule](mkt-limitations-on-subcontracting-and-nonmanufacturer-rule.md) card. Which clause goes in which solicitation is on the [Set-aside orders and clauses](mkt-set-aside-orders-and-clauses.md) card, and the notice clauses that reject offers from concerns that are not small on set-aside solicitations are on the [Small business set-aside notice clauses](mkt-small-business-set-aside-notice-clauses.md) card.
