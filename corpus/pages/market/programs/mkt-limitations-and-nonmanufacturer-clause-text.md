## Purpose

Shows the percentage limits in the limitations on subcontracting clause by type of contract, what counts toward them, and what the nonmanufacturer rule clause requires. The companion card covers the acquisition-rule text.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-MKT-0834] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.219-14 and FAR 52.219-33 [@CL-MKT-0834; @CL-MKT-0839] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-MKT-0834] |
| Applies to | The listed cases in the limitations clause, which include contracts set aside for small business concerns [@CL-MKT-0833] |

The limitations clause applies only to listed cases, which include contracts, and parts of multiple-award contracts, set aside for the small business concerns the cross-referenced section identifies [@CL-MKT-0833]. By submission of an offer and execution of a contract, the contractor agrees to limits that depend on the NAICS code assigned to the contract [@CL-MKT-0874]. For a contract assigned a NAICS code for services (except construction), the contractor will not pay more than 50 percent of the amount paid by the Government for contract performance to subcontractors that are not similarly situated entities [@CL-MKT-0874; @CL-MKT-0834]. For supplies other than procurement from a nonmanufacturer, the limit is 50 percent with the cost of materials excluded [@CL-MKT-0874; @CL-MKT-0835]. For general construction it is 85 percent with the cost of materials excluded [@CL-MKT-0874; @CL-MKT-0836], and for construction by special trade contractors it is 75 percent with the cost of materials excluded [@CL-MKT-0874; @CL-MKT-0837].

## How it works

Each limit caps the share of the amount paid by the Government that may go to subcontractors that are not similarly situated entities [@CL-MKT-0834; @CL-MKT-0835; @CL-MKT-0836; @CL-MKT-0837]. A similarly situated entity is a first-tier subcontractor, including an independent contractor, that has the same small business program status as that which qualified the prime contractor for the award [@CL-MKT-0876], and that is also considered small for the size standard under the NAICS code the prime contractor assigned to the subcontract [@CL-MKT-0877]. An independent contractor shall be considered a subcontractor [@CL-MKT-0875]. Under the services paragraph, work that a similarly situated entity further subcontracts counts toward the 50 percent amount, and on a contract with both services and supplies the 50 percent limitation applies only to the service portion [@CL-MKT-0838]. For the nonmanufacturer rule, the contractor is to provide an end item that a small business has manufactured, processed, or produced in the United States or its outlying areas, and kit assemblers who are nonmanufacturers are directed to a separate paragraph [@CL-MKT-0839]. Two further requirements listed for the contractor are that it be primarily engaged in the retail or wholesale trade and normally sell the type of item being supplied, and that it take ownership or possession of the items with its personnel, equipment, or facilities in a manner consistent with industry practice [@CL-MKT-0839; @CL-MKT-0879]. For a kit of supplies, at least 50 percent of the total cost of the components shall be manufactured, processed, or produced in the United States or its outlying areas by small business concerns [@CL-MKT-0840]. The nonmanufacturer rule clause does not apply to contracts awarded pursuant to the unrestricted portion of a partial set-aside or to a contractor that is the manufacturer of the product or end item [@CL-MKT-0878]. The limitations clause has its own exclusion and its own compliance timing, which this card does not restate.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

The Government pays 3,000,000 under a general construction contract, and all of its subcontractors are not similarly situated entities. Materials cost 1,000,000, so the base is 2,000,000. The limit is 85 percent of the base, so at most 1,700,000 may go to subcontractors that are not similarly situated entities. Paying 1,800,000 would break the limit.

## Never confuse

The limitations clause caps how much of the work a set-aside contractor passes to subcontractors that are not similarly situated entities. The nonmanufacturer rule asks who made the end item. The acquisition-rule text, the compliance timing and the size conditions are on the [Limitations on subcontracting and the nonmanufacturer rule](mkt-limitations-on-subcontracting-and-nonmanufacturer-rule.md) card. Which clause goes in which solicitation is on the [Set-aside orders and clauses](mkt-set-aside-orders-and-clauses.md) card, and the notice clauses that reject offers from concerns that are not small on set-aside solicitations are on the [Small business set-aside notice clauses](mkt-small-business-set-aside-notice-clauses.md) card.
