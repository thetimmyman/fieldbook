## Purpose

Shows the conditions for a Women-Owned Small Business Program set-aside and a sole-source award, including the industry and dollar limits.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-MKT-0624] |
| Codified citation | FAR 19.1505 and FAR 19.1506 [@CL-MKT-0623; @CL-MKT-0626] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-MKT-0624] |
| Applies to | Acquisitions under the Women-Owned Small Business Program [@CL-MKT-0623; @CL-MKT-0626] |

The contracting officer shall comply with 19.203 before deciding to set aside an acquisition under the Women-Owned Small Business Program, and the paragraph then lists what the contracting officer may set aside [@CL-MKT-0623]. The listed options are alternatives. One of the alternatives is to set aside acquisitions exceeding the micro-purchase threshold for competition restricted to EDWOSB concerns when the acquisition is assigned a NAICS code in which SBA has determined that WOSB concerns are underrepresented in Federal procurement [@CL-MKT-0624; @CL-MKT-9357]. The other alternative is to set aside acquisitions exceeding the micro-purchase threshold for competition restricted to WOSB concerns eligible under the program when the acquisition is assigned a NAICS code in which SBA has determined that WOSB concerns are substantially underrepresented in Federal procurement, as specified on SBA's website [@CL-MKT-0625; @CL-MKT-9358]. For requirements in NAICS codes designated by SBA as underrepresented, a contracting officer may restrict competition to EDWOSB concerns if, among other listed conditions, the contracting officer has a reasonable expectation based on market research that two or more EDWOSB concerns will submit offers for the contract [@CL-MKT-0652]. A contracting officer may restrict competition to WOSB concerns eligible under the WOSB Program, including EDWOSB concerns, for requirements in NAICS codes designated by SBA as substantially underrepresented if there is a reasonable expectation based on market research that the conditions that follow are met [@CL-MKT-0653]. The printed section does not list those NAICS codes, and this card does not establish them.

A contracting officer shall consider a contract award to an EDWOSB concern on a sole-source basis before considering small business set-asides, provided none of the listed exclusions apply and the conditions that follow exist [@CL-MKT-0626]. A contracting officer shall consider a contract award to a WOSB concern, including an EDWOSB concern, eligible under the WOSB Program on a sole-source basis before considering small business set-asides, provided none of the listed exclusions apply and the conditions that follow exist [@CL-MKT-0650]. For the EDWOSB sole-source award, two of the listed conditions are that the acquisition is assigned a NAICS code in which SBA has determined that WOSB concerns are underrepresented in Federal procurement and that the contracting officer does not have a reasonable expectation that offers would be received from two or more EDWOSB concerns [@CL-MKT-0627]. A further listed condition there is that the conditions in paragraph (c) of that section exist [@CL-MKT-9359]. One of the paragraph (c) conditions is that the anticipated award price, including options, will not exceed $8.5 million for a requirement within the NAICS codes for manufacturing or $5.5 million for a requirement within any other NAICS codes [@CL-MKT-0628]. A contracting officer shall only award a sole-source contract to a concern that has been certified pursuant to 13 CFR 127.300 as an EDWOSB or WOSB eligible under the program [@CL-MKT-0629]. Contracting officers shall not request a status determination from SBA on pending applications for certification for EDWOSB or WOSB sole-source awards [@CL-MKT-0651].

## How it works

Both the set-aside options and the sole-source conditions depend in part on whether SBA has designated the industry code as underrepresented or substantially underrepresented [@CL-MKT-0624; @CL-MKT-0625; @CL-MKT-0627; @CL-MKT-0650]. The dollar limit applies to sole-source awards and depends on whether the requirement is in a manufacturing industry code [@CL-MKT-0628]. The section also sets the remaining market research conditions for set-asides, offeror eligibility checks, awards on one offer, SBA procurement center representative recommendations and appeals, and it lists the remaining sole-source conditions; this card does not restate them.

The procedures in FAR subpart 19.15 apply to all Federal agencies that employ one or more contracting officers [@CL-MKT-2212].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A requirement is in a manufacturing NAICS code that SBA has designated as one where WOSB concerns are underrepresented. The anticipated award price, including options, is 7 million, under the 8.5 million ceiling. The contracting officer does not expect offers from two or more EDWOSB concerns. The price condition is met, the other listed conditions must still be met, and the awardee must be a certified EDWOSB concern.

## Never confuse

Underrepresented and substantially underrepresented are different SBA designations, tied to EDWOSB concerns and to WOSB concerns respectively [@CL-MKT-0624; @CL-MKT-0625]. A sole-source award goes only to a concern certified under the cited regulation [@CL-MKT-0629]. For a sole-source award the contracting officer does not request a status determination from SBA on pending applications [@CL-MKT-0651]. Status protests are a different procedure: see the [Protesting a firm's economically disadvantaged or women-owned small business status](mkt-edwosb-wosb-status-protests.md) card, and the [Protesting a small business representation](../taxonomy/mkt-small-business-size-protests.md) card for the size route.
