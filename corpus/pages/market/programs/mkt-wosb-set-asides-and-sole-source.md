## Purpose

Shows the conditions for a Women-Owned Small Business Program set-aside and a sole-source award, including the industry and dollar limits.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-MKT-0624] |
| Codified citation | FAR 19.1505 and FAR 19.1506 [@CL-MKT-0623; @CL-MKT-0626] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-MKT-0624] |
| Applies to | Acquisitions under the Women-Owned Small Business Program [@CL-MKT-0623; @CL-MKT-0626] |

The contracting officer shall comply with FAR 19.203 before deciding to set aside an acquisition under the WOSB Program, and may set aside acquisitions exceeding the micro-purchase threshold for competition restricted to EDWOSB concerns when the acquisition is assigned a NAICS code in which SBA has determined that WOSB concerns are underrepresented in Federal procurement, or may set aside acquisitions exceeding the micro-purchase threshold for competition restricted to WOSB concerns eligible under the WOSB Program when the acquisition is assigned a NAICS code in which SBA has determined that WOSB concerns are substantially underrepresented in Federal procurement, as specified on SBA's website [@CL-MKT-0623; @CL-MKT-0624; @CL-MKT-9357; @CL-MKT-0625; @CL-MKT-9358]. For requirements in NAICS codes designated by SBA as underrepresented, a contracting officer may restrict competition to EDWOSB concerns if the contracting officer has a reasonable expectation based on market research that two or more EDWOSB concerns will submit offers for the contract and contract award will be made at a fair and reasonable price [@CL-MKT-0652; @CL-MKT-2016]. A contracting officer may restrict competition to WOSB concerns eligible under the WOSB Program (including EDWOSB concerns), for requirements in NAICS codes designated by SBA as substantially underrepresented, if there is a reasonable expectation based on market research that two or more WOSB concerns eligible under the WOSB Program (including EDWOSB concerns) will submit offers and contract award may be made at a fair and reasonable price [@CL-MKT-0653; @CL-MKT-2017].

A contracting officer shall consider a contract award to an EDWOSB concern on a sole-source basis (see FAR 6.302-5(b)(7)) before considering small business set-asides (see FAR 19.203 and subpart 19.5) provided none of the exclusions at FAR 19.1504 apply, the acquisition is assigned a NAICS code in which SBA has determined that WOSB concerns are underrepresented in Federal procurement, the contracting officer does not have a reasonable expectation that offers would be received from two or more EDWOSB concerns, and the conditions in FAR 19.1506(c) exist [@CL-MKT-0626; @CL-MKT-0627; @CL-MKT-9359]. A contracting officer shall consider a contract award to a WOSB concern (including EDWOSB concerns) eligible under the WOSB Program on a sole-source basis (see FAR 6.302-5(b)(7)) before considering small business set-asides (see FAR 19.203 and subpart 19.5) provided none of the exclusions at FAR 19.1504 apply, the acquisition is assigned a NAICS code in which SBA has determined that WOSB concerns are substantially underrepresented in Federal procurement, the contracting officer does not have a reasonable expectation that offers would be received from two or more WOSB concerns (including EDWOSB concerns), and the conditions in FAR 19.1506(c) exist [@CL-MKT-0650; @CL-MKT-2018; @CL-MKT-2019]. The conditions in FAR 19.1506(c) are that the anticipated award price of the contract, including options, will not exceed $8.5 million for a requirement within the NAICS codes for manufacturing or $5.5 million for a requirement within any other NAICS codes, the EDWOSB concern or WOSB concern has been determined to be a responsible contractor with respect to performance, and the award can be made at a fair and reasonable price [@CL-MKT-0628; @CL-MKT-2020]. A contracting officer shall only award a sole-source contract to a concern that has been certified pursuant to 13 CFR 127.300 as an EDWOSB or WOSB eligible under the WOSB program [@CL-MKT-0629]. Contracting officers shall not request a status determination from SBA on pending applications for certification for EDWOSB or WOSB sole-source awards [@CL-MKT-0651].

## How it works

Both the set-aside options in FAR 19.1505 and the sole-source conditions in FAR 19.1506 depend on whether the acquisition is assigned a NAICS code in which SBA has determined that WOSB concerns are underrepresented or substantially underrepresented in Federal procurement [@CL-MKT-0624; @CL-MKT-0625; @CL-MKT-0627; @CL-MKT-2018]. For set-asides, the contracting officer must have a reasonable expectation based on market research that two or more eligible concerns will submit offers and that award will or may be made at a fair and reasonable price [@CL-MKT-0652; @CL-MKT-2016; @CL-MKT-0653; @CL-MKT-2017]. For sole-source awards, the contracting officer must not have a reasonable expectation that offers would be received from two or more eligible concerns, the anticipated award price including options must not exceed $8.5 million for manufacturing NAICS codes or $5.5 million for any other NAICS codes, the concern must be determined to be a responsible contractor with respect to performance, the award must be able to be made at a fair and reasonable price, and the concern must be certified pursuant to 13 CFR 127.300 [@CL-MKT-0627; @CL-MKT-2019; @CL-MKT-0628; @CL-MKT-2020; @CL-MKT-0629].

The procedures in FAR subpart 19.15 apply to all Federal agencies that employ one or more contracting officers [@CL-MKT-2212].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A requirement is in a manufacturing NAICS code that SBA has designated as one where WOSB concerns are underrepresented. The anticipated award price, including options, is 7 million, under the 8.5 million ceiling. The contracting officer does not expect offers from two or more EDWOSB concerns. The price condition is met, the other listed conditions must still be met, and the awardee must be a certified EDWOSB concern.

## Never confuse

Underrepresented and substantially underrepresented are different SBA designations, tied to EDWOSB concerns and to WOSB concerns respectively [@CL-MKT-0624; @CL-MKT-0625]. A sole-source award goes only to a concern certified under the cited regulation [@CL-MKT-0629]. For a sole-source award the contracting officer does not request a status determination from SBA on pending applications [@CL-MKT-0651]. Status protests are a different procedure: see the [Protesting a firm's economically disadvantaged or women-owned small business status](mkt-edwosb-wosb-status-protests.md) card, and the [Protesting a small business representation](../taxonomy/mkt-small-business-size-protests.md) card for the size route.
