## Purpose

Maps the scope of the FAR part on special contracting methods, the contracts its options subpart does not cover, and the scope of its subpart on acquisitions for the Defense Department by nondefense agencies.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-MKT-2136] |
| Codified citation | FAR 17.000, 17.200 and 17.700 [@CL-MKT-2136; @CL-MKT-2137; @CL-MKT-2141] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-MKT-2136] |
| Applies to | Special contracting methods generally, the options subpart, and acquisitions for the Defense Department by nondefense agencies [@CL-MKT-2136; @CL-MKT-2137; @CL-MKT-2142] |

FAR part 17 prescribes policies and procedures for acquiring supplies and services through special contracting methods, including multi-year contracting, options, leader company contracting, and the use of reverse auctions to obtain competitive pricing [@CL-MKT-2136]. The word "including" means the list names methods without claiming to be complete [@CL-MKT-2136].

The options subpart prescribes policies and procedures for the use of option solicitation provisions and contract clauses [@CL-MKT-2137]. Except as provided in agency regulations, it does not apply to the contracts the section lists [@CL-MKT-2158]. Those are contracts for services involving the construction, alteration, or repair (including dredging, excavating, and painting) of buildings, bridges, roads, or other kinds of real property [@CL-MKT-2138], and contracts for architect-engineer services and for research and development services [@CL-MKT-2139]. The subpart does not preclude the use of options in those contracts [@CL-MKT-2140].

Compliance with the subpart on acquisitions for the Defense Department by nondefense agencies is in addition to the interagency acquisition policies and procedures in subpart 17.5 [@CL-MKT-2141]. That subpart prescribes policies and procedures specific to acquisitions of supplies and services by nondefense agencies on behalf of the Department of Defense [@CL-MKT-2142], and it implements Public Law 110-181, section 801, as amended [@CL-MKT-2143].

## How it works

The part is organized by method, and each subpart holds the operative rules for its method. The scope sections name no decision maker and state coverage, not duties on a contractor. For the options subpart, agency regulations can change which listed contracts it reaches, so the agency's own rules decide that question [@CL-MKT-2158]. The nondefense-agency subpart adds requirements on top of the general interagency rules and does not replace them [@CL-MKT-2141].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An agency plans a research and development services contract with a priced option. The options subpart does not apply to that contract unless the agency's regulations provide otherwise, but the subpart does not preclude the option [@CL-MKT-2158; @CL-MKT-2139; @CL-MKT-2140]. A nondefense agency buying supplies for the Defense Department follows the general interagency acquisition rules and also the subpart specific to such acquisitions [@CL-MKT-2141; @CL-MKT-2142].

## Never confuse

Exclusion from the options subpart is not a ban on options in the excluded contracts [@CL-MKT-2140]. The list of methods in the scope of the part is not exhaustive [@CL-MKT-2136]. The Defense subpart sits on top of the general interagency rules and does not displace them [@CL-MKT-2141]. For the rules themselves see [Use of options](../../contract-finance/funding/ctf-use-of-options.md), [Multiyear contracting](mkt-multiyear-contracting-method-and-clauses.md), [Leader company contracting](mkt-leader-company-contracting.md), [Reverse auctions](mkt-reverse-auctions-suitability-and-award.md), [Interagency acquisitions](mkt-interagency-acquisitions-scope-and-reporting.md) and [Acquisitions by nondefense agencies for DoD](mkt-nondefense-agency-acquisitions-for-dod.md).
