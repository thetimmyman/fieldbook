## Purpose

Shows the general rules for subcontracting plans: plans on indefinite-delivery contracts, goals on orders, incentive provisions and a reminder of the three plan types.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-MKT-0440] |
| Codified citation | FAR 19.705-1 [@CL-MKT-0439; @CL-MKT-0440] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-MKT-0440] |
| Applies to | Contracts that use any contractual incentive provision based upon rewarding the contractor monetarily for exceeding goals in the subcontracting plan, and indefinite-delivery, indefinite-quantity contracts without a commercial plan [@CL-MKT-1996; @CL-MKT-0439; @CL-MKT-0440] |

Except where a contractor has a commercial plan, the contracting officer shall require a subcontracting plan for each indefinite-delivery, indefinite-quantity contract (including task or delivery order contracts, FSS, GWACs, and MACs), when the estimated value of the contract meets the subcontracting plan thresholds at FAR 19.702(a) and small business subcontracting opportunities exist [@CL-MKT-0440; @CL-MKT-0441]. The thresholds themselves are on the [When a small business subcontracting plan is required](mkt-subcontracting-plan-when-required.md) card.

When using any contractual incentive provision based upon rewarding the contractor monetarily for exceeding goals in the subcontracting plan, the contracting officer must ensure that the goals are realistic and that any rewards for exceeding the goals are commensurate with the efforts the contractor would not have otherwise expended [@CL-MKT-1996; @CL-MKT-0439]. Contracting officers placing orders may establish small business subcontracting goals for each order, and establishing goals shall not be in the form of a new subcontracting plan as a contract may not have more than one plan [@CL-MKT-0442].

## How it works

An individual subcontracting plan covers the entire contract period (including option periods), applies to a specific contract, and has goals based on the offeror's planned subcontracting in support of the specific contract, except that indirect costs incurred for common or joint purposes may be allocated on a prorated basis to the contract [@CL-MKT-0555; @CL-MKT-0430]. A master subcontracting plan contains all the required elements of an individual subcontracting plan, except goals, and may be incorporated into individual subcontracting plans, provided the master subcontracting plan has been approved [@CL-MKT-0431]. A commercial plan is a subcontracting plan (including goals) that covers the offeror's fiscal year and applies to the entire production of commercial products and performance of commercial services sold by either the entire company or a portion of it (such as a division, plant, or product line) [@CL-MKT-0426; @CL-MKT-1997]. Except where a contractor has a commercial plan, the contracting officer requires a subcontracting plan for each indefinite-delivery, indefinite-quantity contract that meets the thresholds at FAR 19.702(a) when small business subcontracting opportunities exist [@CL-MKT-0440; @CL-MKT-0441]. The definitions of the three plan types are on the [Subcontracting program definitions and eligibility to participate](mkt-subcontracting-program-definitions-and-eligibility.md) card, and the review of a plan is on the [Reviewing the subcontracting plan](mkt-subcontracting-plan-review.md) card. The incentive clause itself is on the [Subcontracting contract clauses](mkt-subcontracting-contract-clauses.md) card.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An indefinite-delivery contract is expected to be worth 5,000,000, meets the plan thresholds and has small business subcontracting opportunities. The contractor has no commercial plan, so the contracting officer requires one plan. A later order worth 400,000 may carry its own small business goals, but those goals are not a second plan.

## Never confuse

Contracting officers placing orders may establish small business subcontracting goals for each order, and establishing goals shall not be in the form of a new subcontracting plan because a contract may not have more than one plan [@CL-MKT-0442]. A commercial plan covers the offeror's fiscal year and applies to the entire production of commercial products and performance of commercial services sold by either the entire company or a portion of it [@CL-MKT-0426; @CL-MKT-1997], while an individual subcontracting plan covers the entire contract period (including option periods) and applies to a specific contract [@CL-MKT-0555; @CL-MKT-0430]. When using any contractual incentive provision based upon rewarding the contractor monetarily for exceeding goals in the subcontracting plan, the contracting officer must ensure that the goals are realistic and that any rewards for exceeding the goals are commensurate with the efforts the contractor would not have otherwise expended [@CL-MKT-1996; @CL-MKT-0439].
