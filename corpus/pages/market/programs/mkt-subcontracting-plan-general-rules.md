## Purpose

Shows the general rules for subcontracting plans: plans on indefinite-delivery contracts, goals on orders, incentive provisions and a reminder of the three plan types.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-MKT-0440] |
| Codified citation | FAR 19.705-1 [@CL-MKT-0439; @CL-MKT-0440] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-MKT-0440] |
| Applies to | Contracts that use an incentive for exceeding plan goals, and indefinite-delivery, indefinite-quantity contracts without a commercial plan [@CL-MKT-0439; @CL-MKT-0440] |

Except where a contractor has a commercial plan, the contracting officer shall require a subcontracting plan for each indefinite-delivery, indefinite-quantity contract, including task or delivery order contracts, FSS, GWACs and MACs [@CL-MKT-0440]. That applies when the estimated value of the contract meets the subcontracting plan thresholds and small business subcontracting opportunities exist [@CL-MKT-0441]. The thresholds themselves are on the [When a small business subcontracting plan is required](mkt-subcontracting-plan-when-required.md) card.

When the contracting officer uses a contractual incentive provision that rewards the contractor monetarily for exceeding the goals in the plan, the contracting officer must ensure that the goals are realistic and that any rewards for exceeding the goals are commensurate with the efforts the contractor would not have otherwise expended [@CL-MKT-0439]. Contracting officers placing orders may establish small business subcontracting goals for each order, but the goals shall not take the form of a new plan because a contract may not have more than one plan [@CL-MKT-0442].

## How it works

An individual plan covers the entire contract period, including option periods, applies to a specific contract and has goals based on the offeror's planned subcontracting in support of it, except that indirect costs incurred for common or joint purposes may be allocated on a prorated basis to the contract [@CL-MKT-0555; @CL-MKT-0430]. A master plan holds the required elements except goals and may be incorporated into individual plans once approved [@CL-MKT-0431]. A commercial plan covers the offeror's fiscal year and the whole production of commercial products and services sold by the company or a portion of it [@CL-MKT-0426]. A contractor with a commercial plan is excepted from the plan requirement for an indefinite-delivery contract [@CL-MKT-0440]. The definitions of the three plan types are on the [Subcontracting program definitions and eligibility to participate](mkt-subcontracting-program-definitions-and-eligibility.md) card, and the review of a plan is on the [Reviewing the subcontracting plan](mkt-subcontracting-plan-review.md) card. The incentive clause itself is on the [Subcontracting contract clauses](mkt-subcontracting-contract-clauses.md) card.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An indefinite-delivery contract is expected to be worth 5,000,000, meets the plan thresholds and has small business subcontracting opportunities. The contractor has no commercial plan, so the contracting officer requires one plan. A later order worth 400,000 may carry its own small business goals, but those goals are not a second plan.

## Never confuse

Goals set for an order are not a new subcontracting plan [@CL-MKT-0442]. A commercial plan is not an individual plan, because the commercial plan covers a fiscal year of commercial production and the individual plan covers one contract [@CL-MKT-0426; @CL-MKT-0430]. An incentive for exceeding goals does not relax the realism check, because the contracting officer must still ensure that the goals are realistic [@CL-MKT-0439].
