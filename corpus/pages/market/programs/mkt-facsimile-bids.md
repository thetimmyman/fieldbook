## Purpose

Explains what a facsimile bid is, what the bidder must do for it to be considered, who carries the risk of transmission failures, and what the Government may still ask for afterwards.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-MKT-1129] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.214-31 [@CL-MKT-1129] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR part 52 lists 52.214-31 as "[Reserved]" [@CL-MKT-0125] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-MKT-1129] |
| Applies to | Bidders responding to a solicitation that includes the provision [@CL-MKT-1130] |

A facsimile bid is a bid, a modification of a bid, or a withdrawal of a bid that is transmitted to and received by the Government via electronic equipment that communicates and reproduces both printed and handwritten material [@CL-MKT-1129]. Bidders may submit facsimile bids as responses to the solicitation, and those responses must arrive at the place and by the time specified in the solicitation [@CL-MKT-1130]. Facsimile bids must contain the required signatures [@CL-MKT-1132]. Facsimile bids that fail to furnish required representations or information, or that reject any of the terms, conditions, and provisions of the solicitation, may be excluded from consideration [@CL-MKT-1131].

The Government reserves the right to make award solely on the facsimile bid, but if the contracting officer requests it, the apparently successful bidder agrees to promptly submit the complete original signed bid [@CL-MKT-1133]. Paragraph (f) lists the facsimile receiving data and compatibility characteristics, namely the telephone number of the receiving equipment and its compatibility characteristics [@CL-MKT-1178]. Those entries are contract-specific and are left blank in the provision text [@CL-MKT-1178].

If the bidder chooses to transmit a facsimile bid, the Government will not be responsible for any failure attributable to the transmission or receipt of the bid, including but not limited to the listed failures [@CL-MKT-1134]. The listed failures include receipt of a garbled or incomplete bid, availability or condition of the receiving equipment, and incompatibility between the sending and receiving equipment [@CL-MKT-1135]. They also include delay in transmission or receipt of the bid, failure of the bidder to properly identify the bid, illegibility of the bid, and security of bid data [@CL-MKT-1179].

## How it works

The bidder may use facsimile, and the Government is not responsible for failures in transmission or receipt once the bidder chooses to transmit [@CL-MKT-1130; @CL-MKT-1134]. The response must arrive at the place and by the time specified in the solicitation, and delay in transmission or receipt is one of the failures for which the Government is not responsible [@CL-MKT-1130; @CL-MKT-1179]. The Government may award on the facsimile alone, and the original signed bid is due promptly only if the contracting officer requests it from the apparently successful bidder [@CL-MKT-1133]. The receiving telephone number and the equipment's compatibility characteristics are entered for the solicitation [@CL-MKT-1178].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text from February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. RFO FAR part 52 lists FAR 52.214-31 as "[Reserved]" [@CL-MKT-0125].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A solicitation names a receiving number and sets bid opening at 14:00. Bidder A sends a facsimile bid at 13:15, but the receiving machine is occupied and the bid is received at 14:10. The bid did not arrive by the time specified, and delay in transmission or receipt is a failure for which the Government is not responsible [@CL-MKT-1130; @CL-MKT-1134; @CL-MKT-1179]. Bidder B's signed bid arrives complete at 13:50. The Government may award on B's facsimile alone [@CL-MKT-1133]. If the contracting officer requests it, B, as the apparently successful bidder, agrees to promptly submit the complete original signed bid [@CL-MKT-1133]. Bidder C's signed facsimile bid arrives in time but omits a required representation, so it may be excluded from consideration [@CL-MKT-1131]. The example follows the codified provision [@CL-MKT-1129]; RFO FAR part 52 lists FAR 52.214-31 as "[Reserved]" [@CL-MKT-0125].

## Never confuse

A facsimile bid is not an informal or unsigned submission: it must carry the required signatures, and it may be excluded for missing representations or information or for rejecting terms [@CL-MKT-1131; @CL-MKT-1132]. A facsimile bid is also not only an original offer: the definition covers a modification of a bid and a withdrawal of a bid [@CL-MKT-1129]. Choosing to transmit one does not shift the risk of a failed transmission to the Government [@CL-MKT-1134]. The codified provision sets the facsimile bid rules [@CL-MKT-1129], but RFO FAR part 52 lists the same section number as "[Reserved]" [@CL-MKT-0125].

The facsimile proposal provision for negotiated procurements is covered in [Request for information, facsimile proposals and order of precedence](../../estimating/pricing/est-planning-solicitations-facsimile-and-precedence.md), and late bids, modifications and withdrawals are covered in [Late submissions, modifications and withdrawals of bids](../../estimating/pricing/est-late-bid-submissions-and-withdrawals.md).
