## Purpose

Explains what a facsimile bid is, what the bidder must do for it to be considered, who carries the risk of transmission failures, and what the Government may still ask for afterwards.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-MKT-1129] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.214-31 [@CL-MKT-1129] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR part 52 lists 52.214-31 as "[Reserved]" [@CL-MKT-0125] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-MKT-1129] |
| Applies to | Bidders responding to a solicitation that includes the provision [@CL-MKT-1130] |

A facsimile bid is a bid, a modification of a bid, or a withdrawal of a bid that is transmitted to and received by the Government via electronic equipment that communicates and reproduces both printed and handwritten material [@CL-MKT-1129]. Bidders may submit facsimile bids as responses to the solicitation, and those responses must arrive at the place and by the time specified in the solicitation [@CL-MKT-1130]. Facsimile bids must contain the required signatures [@CL-MKT-1132]. Facsimile bids that fail to furnish required representations or information, or that reject any of the terms, conditions, and provisions of the solicitation, may be excluded from consideration [@CL-MKT-1131].

The Government reserves the right to make award solely on the facsimile bid, and if requested by the contracting officer, the apparently successful bidder agrees to promptly submit the complete original signed bid [@CL-MKT-1133]. Paragraph (f) lists the facsimile receiving data and compatibility characteristics: the telephone number of the receiving facsimile equipment and the compatibility characteristics of the receiving facsimile equipment, such as make and model number, receiving speed, and communications protocol [@CL-MKT-1178].

If the bidder chooses to transmit a facsimile bid, the Government will not be responsible for any failure attributable to the transmission or receipt of the facsimile bid, including, but not limited to, the listed failures [@CL-MKT-1134]. The listed failures include receipt of a garbled or incomplete bid, availability or condition of the receiving facsimile equipment, and incompatibility between the sending and receiving equipment [@CL-MKT-1134; @CL-MKT-1135]. They also include delay in transmission or receipt of the bid, failure of the bidder to properly identify the bid, illegibility of the bid, and security of bid data [@CL-MKT-1134; @CL-MKT-1179].

## How it works

The bidder may submit facsimile bids as responses to the solicitation, and the Government will not be responsible for any failure attributable to the transmission or receipt of the facsimile bid if the bidder chooses to transmit one [@CL-MKT-1130; @CL-MKT-1134]. Facsimile bids must arrive at the place and by the time specified in the solicitation, and delay in transmission or receipt of the bid is one of the failures for which the Government will not be responsible [@CL-MKT-1130; @CL-MKT-1134; @CL-MKT-1179]. The Government reserves the right to make award solely on the facsimile bid, and if requested by the contracting officer, the apparently successful bidder agrees to promptly submit the complete original signed bid [@CL-MKT-1133]. Paragraph (f) lists the telephone number of the receiving facsimile equipment and the compatibility characteristics of the receiving facsimile equipment, such as make and model number, receiving speed, and communications protocol [@CL-MKT-1178].

Effective February 1, 2026, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text [@CL-CMP-9760; @CL-CMP-9762]. RFO FAR part 52 lists FAR 52.214-31 as "[Reserved]" [@CL-MKT-0125].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A solicitation names a receiving number and sets bid opening at 14:00. Bidder A sends a facsimile bid at 13:15, but the receiving machine is occupied and the bid is received at 14:10. The bid did not arrive by the time specified, and delay in transmission or receipt is a failure for which the Government is not responsible [@CL-MKT-1130; @CL-MKT-1134; @CL-MKT-1179]. Bidder B's signed bid arrives complete at 13:50. The Government may award on B's facsimile alone [@CL-MKT-1133]. If the contracting officer requests it, B, as the apparently successful bidder, agrees to promptly submit the complete original signed bid [@CL-MKT-1133]. Bidder C's signed facsimile bid arrives in time but omits a required representation, so it may be excluded from consideration [@CL-MKT-1131]. The example follows the codified provision [@CL-MKT-1129]. RFO FAR part 52 lists FAR 52.214-31 as "[Reserved]" [@CL-MKT-0125].

## Never confuse

Under codified FAR 52.214-31, facsimile bids must contain the required signatures, and facsimile bids that fail to furnish required representations or information or that reject any of the terms, conditions, and provisions of the solicitation may be excluded from consideration [@CL-MKT-1131; @CL-MKT-1132]. The definition of a facsimile bid covers a bid, a modification of a bid, or a withdrawal of a bid transmitted to and received by the Government via electronic equipment that communicates and reproduces both printed and handwritten material [@CL-MKT-1129]. If the bidder chooses to transmit a facsimile bid, the Government will not be responsible for any failure attributable to the transmission or receipt of the facsimile bid [@CL-MKT-1134]. RFO FAR part 52 lists FAR 52.214-31 as "[Reserved]" [@CL-MKT-0125].

The facsimile proposal provision for negotiated procurements is covered in [Request for information, facsimile proposals and order of precedence](../../estimating/pricing/est-planning-solicitations-facsimile-and-precedence.md), and late bids, modifications and withdrawals are covered in [Late submissions, modifications and withdrawals of bids](../../estimating/pricing/est-late-bid-submissions-and-withdrawals.md).
