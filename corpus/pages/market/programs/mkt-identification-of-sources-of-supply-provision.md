## Purpose

Explains when a solicitation for supplies carries the defense supplement's provision on identification of sources of supply, who decides, and which exceptions apply.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-MKT-2105] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 217.7303 [@CL-MKT-2105] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-MKT-2105] |
| Applies to | Solicitations for supplies conducted under other than full and open competition, apart from the listed exceptions, and service contracts requiring the delivery of supplies where appropriate [@CL-MKT-2105; @CL-MKT-2111] |

The provision at 252.217-7026, Identification of Sources of Supply, or one substantially the same, is to be used in all solicitations for supplies when the acquisition is being conducted under other than full and open competition, except in the situations the section lists [@CL-MKT-2105]. If appropriate, the same provision is to be used in service contracts requiring the delivery of supplies [@CL-MKT-2111]. The section does not name who judges whether use in a service contract is appropriate.

The first exception is an acquisition that uses FAR 6.302-5 [@CL-MKT-2106]. The second is a case where the contracting officer already has the information the provision requires, for example from other acquisitions [@CL-MKT-2107]. The third is a contract for subsistence, clothing or textiles, fuels, or supplies purchased and used outside the United States [@CL-MKT-2108]. The fourth is a case where the contracting officer determines that it would not be practicable to require offerors or contractors to provide the information, such as nonrepetitive local purchases [@CL-MKT-2109]. The fifth is a case where the contracting officer determines that the exception in DFARS 217.7302(b) applies to all items under the solicitation [@CL-MKT-2110]. That exception covers contracts for commercial products and contracts valued at or below the simplified acquisition threshold [@CL-MKT-2155].

## How it works

The contracting officer makes the determination in the two exceptions that call for one: impracticability and the all-items exception [@CL-MKT-2109; @CL-MKT-2110]. The exception for information already held rests on what the contracting officer already has, and the first and third exceptions turn on facts of the acquisition itself [@CL-MKT-2107; @CL-MKT-2106; @CL-MKT-2108]. The all-items exception needs the commercial-product or simplified-threshold exception to cover every item, so one item outside it means that exception does not apply, although another listed exception might still apply [@CL-MKT-2110; @CL-MKT-2155; @CL-MKT-2105]. This section does not say what an offeror must provide; that is in the text of the provision, and the contract-level source identification requirement is on the linked card.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A solicitation for supplies is run under other than full and open competition. The contracting officer already holds the source information from an earlier award for the same items, so the exception for information already held applies and the provision is left out [@CL-MKT-2107]. In a second solicitation run the same way, none of the exceptions applies, so the provision goes in [@CL-MKT-2105]. In a third, the contracting officer determines that every item is a commercial product, so the exception in DFARS 217.7302(b) applies to all items and the provision is not required [@CL-MKT-2110; @CL-MKT-2155]. A service contract that requires the delivery of supplies uses the provision if appropriate [@CL-MKT-2111].

## Never confuse

The rule here is a solicitation provision. The contract-level requirement that a contractor identify its sources of supply, and the exception for commercial products and low-value contracts, are on [Order review, source identification and replenishment parts](mkt-non-dod-order-review-source-identification-and-replenishment-parts.md). The supply-solicitation rule does not authorize other than full and open competition; it applies where the acquisition is already conducted that way [@CL-MKT-2105].
