## Purpose

Explains how an award is made and signed, what a partial award notice must say, and what offerors learn when a protest sends an award back to a new solicitation or a new request for revised proposals.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-MKT-1340] |
| Codified citation | FAR 15.504 and FAR 15.507 [@CL-MKT-1340] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-MKT-1340] |
| Applies to | Awards in negotiated acquisitions, including awards reopened after a protest [@CL-MKT-1340; @CL-MKT-1344] |

The contracting officer awards a contract to the successful offeror by furnishing the executed contract or other notice of the award to that offeror [@CL-MKT-1340]. If the award document includes information different from the latest signed proposal, as amended by the offeror's written correspondence, both the offeror and the contracting officer must sign the contract award [@CL-MKT-1341]. When an award covers less than all the items that may be awarded and items are withheld for later award, each notice must state that the Government may make subsequent awards on those items within the proposal acceptance period [@CL-MKT-1342].

If Optional Form 307, Standard Form 26 or Standard Form 33 is not used to award the contract, the first page of the award document must contain the Government's acceptance statement from Block 15 of that form [@CL-MKT-1343]. That statement excludes the Item 3 reference language, and the first page must also contain the contracting officer's name, signature and date [@CL-MKT-1495]. If the award document also differs from the signed proposal as amended by the offeror's written correspondence, the first page must include the contractor's agreement statement from Block 14 of Optional Form 307 and the signature of the contractor's authorized representative [@CL-MKT-1496].

## How it works

Protests against award in negotiated acquisitions must be handled in accordance with FAR part 33, and agency protest procedures that incorporate the alternative dispute resolution provisions of Executive Order 12979 are encouraged for both preaward and postaward protests [@CL-MKT-1344].

If a protest causes the agency, within 1 year of contract award, to issue a new solicitation on the protested award, the contracting officer must give the paragraph (c) information to all prospective offerors for the new solicitation [@CL-MKT-1345]. The other route under the same one-year paragraph is a new request for revised proposals on the protested award, where the contracting officer must give that information to offerors that were in the competitive range and are asked to submit revised proposals [@CL-MKT-1497]. The information is what unsuccessful offerors received in any debriefings on the original award about the successful offeror's proposal, plus other nonproprietary information that would have gone to the original offerors [@CL-MKT-1346]. See [Postaward debriefing of offerors](../../estimating/pricing/est-postaward-debriefing.md) for the debriefing itself.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An agency awards a contract in March by furnishing the executed contract to the successful offeror [@CL-MKT-1340]. The award document gives a delivery schedule that differs from the offeror's latest signed proposal as amended by its written correspondence, so the offeror and the contracting officer both sign [@CL-MKT-1341]. Two items are withheld for later award, so the notice states that the Government may make later awards on them within the proposal acceptance period [@CL-MKT-1342].

A protest follows in April. In September, within 1 year of the March award, the agency issues a new solicitation on the protested award [@CL-MKT-1345]. The contracting officer gives every prospective offeror for the new solicitation the debriefing information on the former winner's proposal and the other nonproprietary information that the original offerors would have had [@CL-MKT-1346]. The list covers only those two groups, so proprietary information that was not given out in the debriefings, such as the winner's price build-up, is not on it [@CL-MKT-1346]. Had the agency instead asked for revised proposals, only the competitive range offerors asked to revise would receive the information [@CL-MKT-1497].

## Never confuse

A notice of award is not necessarily a signed contract: the award is made by furnishing the executed contract or other notice, and the offeror must also sign where the award document differs from its latest signed proposal as amended [@CL-MKT-1340; @CL-MKT-1341]. A protest is handled under the protest part of the FAR, while the disclosure duty runs to offerors for the new solicitation or to the competitive range offerors asked to revise their proposals [@CL-MKT-1344; @CL-MKT-1345; @CL-MKT-1497]. For the protest clause and stop-work order see [Protest after award clause and stop-work order](../../close-revrec/revenue/cls-protest-after-award.md).
