## Purpose

Shows how long the ordering period of a DoD task or delivery order contract may run, how an option or modification extends it, who decides on a longer period, which contracts are outside the rule, and when a sealed bid solicitation may provide for evaluating options.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-MKT-1903] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 217.204(e) and 217.208 [@CL-MKT-1903; @CL-MKT-1910] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-MKT-1903] |
| Applies to | Task order and delivery order contracts awarded by DoD under the statute the paragraph cites, and sealed bid solicitations that provide for evaluating options [@CL-MKT-1903; @CL-MKT-1910] |

Notwithstanding FAR 17.204(e), the ordering period of a task order or delivery order contract, including a contract for information technology, awarded by DoD pursuant to section 3403 of title 10, United States Code, may be for any period up to 5 years [@CL-MKT-1903]. That ordering period may be subsequently extended for one or more successive periods in accordance with an option provided in the contract or a modification of the contract [@CL-MKT-1904]. It shall not exceed 10 years unless the head of the agency determines in writing that exceptional circumstances require a longer ordering period [@CL-MKT-1905].

The ordering-period paragraph does not apply to contracts, including task or delivery order contracts, awarded under other statutory authority [@CL-MKT-1906]. It does not apply to advisory and assistance service task order contracts authorized by section 3405 of title 10, United States Code, that are limited by statute to 5 years, with the authority to extend an additional 6 months [@CL-MKT-1906; @CL-MKT-1907]. It also does not apply to definite-quantity contracts, GSA schedule contracts, or multi-agency contracts awarded by agencies other than NASA, DoD or the Coast Guard [@CL-MKT-1906; @CL-MKT-1908].

Approval from the senior procurement executive must be obtained before an order is issued against a task or delivery order contract subject to the ordering-period paragraph, if performance under the order is expected to extend more than 1 year beyond the 10-year limit or the extended limit described in the paragraph on exceptional circumstances (see FAR 37.106 for funding and term of service contracts) [@CL-MKT-1909; @CL-MKT-2029].

Sealed bid solicitations shall not include provisions for evaluations of options unless the contracting officer determines that there is a reasonable likelihood that the options will be exercised [@CL-MKT-1910]. The limitation also applies to sealed bid solicitations for the contracts excluded by FAR 17.200 [@CL-MKT-1911].

## How it works

Three officials decide: the head of the agency determines in writing whether exceptional circumstances require an ordering period longer than 10 years [@CL-MKT-1905], the senior procurement executive approves an order whose performance is expected to extend more than 1 year beyond the 10-year limit or the extended limit [@CL-MKT-1909; @CL-MKT-2029], and the contracting officer determines whether there is a reasonable likelihood that options will be exercised before a sealed bid solicitation may include provisions for evaluations of options [@CL-MKT-1910]. The ordering period may be subsequently extended for one or more successive periods in accordance with an option provided in the contract or a modification of the contract, and the ordering period shall not exceed 10 years unless the head of the agency determines in writing that exceptional circumstances require a longer ordering period [@CL-MKT-1904; @CL-MKT-1905].

The FAR rules on options in solicitations and contracts are on [Options in solicitations and contracts: evaluation and limits](../../contract-finance/funding/ctf-options-in-solicitations-and-contracts.md), and the provisions that evaluate options are on [Evaluation of options and options exercised at award provisions](../../contract-finance/funding/ctf-option-evaluation-provisions.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A DoD delivery order contract is awarded under the statute the paragraph cites and has a 5-year ordering period [@CL-MKT-1903]. The contract provides for two 2-year extensions by option. If both are exercised the ordering period is 9 years, which is under the 10-year cap, so no written determination of the head of the agency is needed [@CL-MKT-1904; @CL-MKT-1905]. If the agency wanted a 12-year ordering period, the head of the agency would first have to determine in writing that exceptional circumstances require it [@CL-MKT-1905]. On a contract whose ordering period runs the full 10 years, an order expected to be performed until 18 months after the end of the ordering period is more than 1 year beyond the 10-year limit, so the senior procurement executive's approval must be obtained before the order is issued [@CL-MKT-1909; @CL-MKT-2029]. An order expected to end 6 months after the limit does not trigger that approval [@CL-MKT-1909; @CL-MKT-2029]. A GSA schedule contract is outside the ordering-period paragraph altogether [@CL-MKT-1906; @CL-MKT-1908].

## Never confuse

The ordering period may be subsequently extended in accordance with an option provided in the contract or a modification of the contract [@CL-MKT-1904], and the ordering period shall not exceed 10 years unless the head of the agency determines in writing that exceptional circumstances require a longer ordering period [@CL-MKT-1905]. Sealed bid solicitations shall not include provisions for evaluations of options unless the contracting officer determines that there is a reasonable likelihood that the options will be exercised [@CL-MKT-1910], and that limitation also applies to sealed bid solicitations for the contracts excluded by FAR 17.200 [@CL-MKT-1911].
