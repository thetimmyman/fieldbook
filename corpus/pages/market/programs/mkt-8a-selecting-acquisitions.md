## Purpose

Shows how an agency requirement reaches the SBA's business development program, and who can start the match between a requirement and a participant.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-MKT-0493] |
| Codified citation | FAR 19.800 and FAR 19.803 [@CL-MKT-0493; @CL-MKT-0495] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-MKT-0493] |
| Applies to | Agency requirements that may be awarded to the SBA for performance by eligible 8(a) participants [@CL-MKT-0493] |

Contracts may be awarded to the SBA for performance by eligible 8(a) participants on either a sole source or competitive basis [@CL-MKT-0493]. For acquisitions above the simplified acquisition threshold, the contracting officer shall consider 8(a) set-asides or sole source awards before considering small business set-asides [@CL-MKT-0494].

The SBA and an agency match the agency's requirements with the capabilities of 8(a) participants to establish a basis for the agency to contract with the SBA, and selection is initiated in one of three ways [@CL-MKT-0495].

## How it works

Of the three listed ways, one is that the SBA advises the contracting activity of a participant's capabilities through a search letter and requests the contracting activity to identify acquisitions to support the participant's business plans [@CL-MKT-0495; @CL-MKT-0496]. A second listed way is that the SBA identifies a specific requirement for one or more participants and sends a requirements letter to the agency's Office of Small and Disadvantaged Business Utilization or, for the Department of Defense, to the Office of Small Business Programs [@CL-MKT-0495; @CL-MKT-0497]. A third listed way is that agencies review other proposed acquisitions to identify requirements that may be offered to the SBA [@CL-MKT-0495; @CL-MKT-0498]. This card does not restate the information the SBA must supply with a search letter or a requirements letter, the contracting officer's duty to comply with another section before offering an acquisition under the program, or what an agency may offer when it identifies a requirement itself.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An agency plans a supply requirement worth 600,000, which is above its simplified acquisition threshold. The SBA sends the agency's small business office a requirements letter that names the requirement and a participant. Before it considers a small business set-aside, the contracting officer considers an 8(a) set-aside or sole source award. The agency then prepares its offering to the SBA.

## Never confuse

Selection is the matching step, started by a search letter, a requirements letter or the agency's own review. The agency's offering letter and the SBA's acceptance come after it and are on the [Agency offering and SBA acceptance of requirements for the program](mkt-8a-offering-and-acceptance.md) card. Considering an 8(a) award before a small business set-aside applies above the simplified acquisition threshold [@CL-MKT-0494]; the general small business set-aside rules are on the [Small business set-asides](mkt-small-business-set-asides.md) card, and how a NAICS code is assigned is on the [Size standards, NAICS codes and appeals of the contracting officer's code](../taxonomy/mkt-size-standards-and-naics-codes.md) card.
