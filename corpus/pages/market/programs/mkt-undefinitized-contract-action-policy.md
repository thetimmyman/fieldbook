## Purpose

Explains the Defense Department policy that limits when an undefinitized contract action may be used and requires it to be as complete and definite as practicable.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-MKT-2112] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 217.7403 [@CL-MKT-2112] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-MKT-2112] |
| Applies to | Undefinitized contract actions in the Defense Department [@CL-MKT-2112] |

Defense Department policy is that undefinitized contract actions shall be used only when the listed conditions are met [@CL-MKT-2112]. The first condition is that negotiation of a definitive contract action is not possible in sufficient time to meet the Government's requirements [@CL-MKT-2113]. The second is that the Government's interest demands that the contractor be given a binding commitment so that contract performance can begin immediately [@CL-MKT-2114]. The policy joins the two with "and", so both must be present [@CL-MKT-2113; @CL-MKT-2114].

The same policy says an undefinitized contract action shall be as complete and definite as practicable under the particular circumstances [@CL-MKT-2112; @CL-MKT-2115]. That standard applies to every such action within the subpart, in addition to the two conditions. The subpart excludes purchases at or below the simplified acquisition threshold, special access programs and congressionally mandated long-lead procurement contracts, and for those the contracting officer shall apply the policy and procedures to the maximum extent practicable [@CL-MKT-1553; @CL-MKT-1554].

## How it works

The section states Defense Department policy. It names no approving official, sets no dollar limit and gives no deadline. The two conditions gate whether the device is used, and the completeness standard governs how the action is written. The approval route and the limits on obligations before definitization are on [Approval and obligation limits](mkt-uca-approval-and-obligation-limits.md). The exceptions, profit and reporting are on [Exceptions, profit and reporting](mkt-uca-exceptions-profit-and-reporting.md), and the wider topic is on [Undefinitized contract actions](../../estimating/pricing/est-dod-undefinitized-contract-actions.md). This section states no duty on the contractor.

The DFARS subpart on undefinitized contract actions prescribes policies and procedures implementing title 10 of the United States Code, section 3371 and the sections that follow it [@CL-MKT-2346].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A unit needs repairs to a critical vehicle within weeks. A definitive contract cannot be negotiated in time to meet that need, and the Government needs the contractor bound now so that work can start at once, so both conditions are met [@CL-MKT-2113; @CL-MKT-2114]. The action is written with its scope, deliverables and schedule defined as completely as the circumstances allow [@CL-MKT-2115]. If a definitive contract could be negotiated in time to meet the need, the first condition would fail and the policy would not support an undefinitized action [@CL-MKT-2112; @CL-MKT-2113].

## Never confuse

This section states when an undefinitized action may be used and how definite it must be. It is not the approval rule, the limit on obligations before definitization, or the profit and reporting rules, which are on the linked cards. The standard of as complete and definite as practicable does not excuse the two conditions; for an action within the subpart, both conditions and the standard apply [@CL-MKT-2112; @CL-MKT-2115].
