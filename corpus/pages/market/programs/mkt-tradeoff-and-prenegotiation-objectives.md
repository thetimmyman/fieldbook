## Purpose

Shows when an agency may weigh price against other factors, what the solicitation has to say about that weighing, and how the contracting officer fixes the objectives that open a negotiation.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-MKT-1325] |
| Codified citation | FAR 15.101-1 and FAR 15.406-1 [@CL-MKT-1325] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-MKT-1325] |
| Applies to | Source selections that use a tradeoff process, and pricing actions for which the contracting officer sets prenegotiation objectives [@CL-MKT-1325; @CL-MKT-1330] |

A tradeoff process is appropriate when it may be in the best interest of the Government to consider award to other than the lowest priced offeror or other than the highest technically rated offeror [@CL-MKT-1325]. The process permits tradeoffs among cost or price and non-cost factors, and allows the Government to accept other than the lowest priced proposal [@CL-MKT-1478].

When a tradeoff process is used, the solicitation must clearly state all evaluation factors and significant subfactors that will affect contract award and their relative importance [@CL-MKT-1326]. It must also state whether all evaluation factors other than cost or price, when combined, are significantly more important than, approximately equal to, or significantly less important than cost or price [@CL-MKT-1327]. The perceived benefits of the higher priced proposal must merit the additional cost, and the rationale for tradeoffs must be documented in the file in accordance with FAR 15.406 [@CL-MKT-1328].

## How it works

The prenegotiation objectives establish the Government's initial negotiation position and assist the contracting officer's determination of a fair and reasonable price [@CL-MKT-1479]. They should be based on the results of the contracting officer's analysis of the offeror's proposal, taking into consideration all pertinent information including field pricing assistance, audit reports and technical analysis, fact-finding results, independent Government cost estimates and price histories [@CL-MKT-1329].

The contracting officer must establish the objectives before the negotiation of any pricing action [@CL-MKT-1330]. The scope and depth of the analysis supporting the objectives should be directly related to the dollar value, importance and complexity of the pricing action [@CL-MKT-1330]. When cost analysis is required, the contracting officer must document the pertinent issues to be negotiated, the cost objectives and a profit or fee objective [@CL-MKT-1331]. The factor-by-factor evaluation is on [Lowest price technically acceptable source selection](../../estimating/pricing/est-lpta-source-selection.md), and the fee build-up is on [Profit analysis in cost-based price negotiations](../../estimating/pricing/est-profit-analysis.md); this card does not restate them.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

Two offerors answer a solicitation that states that the non-price factors, when combined, are significantly more important than price [@CL-MKT-1327]. The lower priced proposal scores 70 on the non-price factors and asks 2,000,000. The higher priced proposal scores 88 and asks 2,400,000, which is 400,000 more.

The agency may consider award to the higher priced offeror if that may be in the Government's best interest [@CL-MKT-1325]. The perceived benefits of that proposal must merit the additional 400,000, and the rationale is documented in the file [@CL-MKT-1328]. The contracting officer establishes prenegotiation objectives before negotiating the pricing action [@CL-MKT-1330]. If cost analysis is required for that action, the contracting officer also documents the pertinent issues to be negotiated, the cost objectives and a profit or fee objective [@CL-MKT-1331].

## Never confuse

A tradeoff is not a lowest-price award: its point is that the Government may accept other than the lowest priced proposal, but only where the perceived benefits merit the added cost [@CL-MKT-1478; @CL-MKT-1328]. Prenegotiation objectives are not the offeror's estimate: they are the Government's initial negotiation position, based on the contracting officer's analysis [@CL-MKT-1479; @CL-MKT-1329].
