## Purpose

Shows when an agency may weigh price against other factors, what the solicitation has to say about that weighing, and how the contracting officer fixes the objectives that open a negotiation.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-MKT-1325] |
| Codified citation | FAR 15.101-1 and FAR 15.406-1 [@CL-MKT-1325] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 15.103-1 [@CL-MKT-2262; @CL-MKT-2263], RFO FAR 15.104(a), (b)(1)(ii)(A) and (d) [@CL-MKT-2264; @CL-MKT-2308; @CL-MKT-2309; @CL-MKT-2011; @CL-MKT-2265; @CL-MKT-2266], RFO FAR 15.205 [@CL-MKT-2267] and RFO FAR 15.408-1 [@CL-MKT-2268; @CL-MKT-2269; @CL-MKT-2270]; RFO FAR 15.103-4 [@CL-MKT-2092; @CL-MKT-2093] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-MKT-1325] |
| Applies to | Source selections that use a tradeoff process, and pricing actions for which the contracting officer sets prenegotiation objectives [@CL-MKT-1325; @CL-MKT-1330] |

A tradeoff process is appropriate when it may be in the best interest of the Government to consider award to other than the lowest priced offeror or other than the highest technically rated offeror [@CL-MKT-1325]. The process permits tradeoffs among cost or price and non-cost factors, and allows the Government to accept other than the lowest priced proposal [@CL-MKT-1478].

When a tradeoff process is used, the solicitation must clearly state all evaluation factors and significant subfactors that will affect contract award and their relative importance [@CL-MKT-9356; @CL-MKT-1326]. It must also state whether all evaluation factors other than cost or price, when combined, are significantly more important than, approximately equal to, or significantly less important than cost or price [@CL-MKT-1327]. The perceived benefits of the higher priced proposal must merit the additional cost, and the rationale for tradeoffs must be documented in the file in accordance with FAR 15.406 [@CL-MKT-1328].

## How it works

The prenegotiation objectives establish the Government's initial negotiation position and assist the contracting officer's determination of a fair and reasonable price [@CL-MKT-1479]. They should be based on the results of the contracting officer's analysis of the offeror's proposal, taking into consideration all pertinent information including field pricing assistance, audit reports and technical analysis, fact-finding results, independent Government cost estimates and price histories [@CL-MKT-1329].

The contracting officer must establish the objectives before the negotiation of any pricing action [@CL-MKT-1330]. The scope and depth of the analysis supporting the objectives should be directly related to the dollar value, importance and complexity of the pricing action [@CL-MKT-1330]. When cost analysis is required, the contracting officer must document the pertinent issues to be negotiated, the cost objectives and a profit or fee objective [@CL-MKT-1331]. The factor-by-factor evaluation is on [Lowest price technically acceptable source selection](../../estimating/pricing/est-lpta-source-selection.md), and the fee build-up is on [Profit analysis in cost-based price negotiations](../../estimating/pricing/est-profit-analysis.md).

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 15 text in place of the codified text under Class Deviation 2026-O0048, whose Revision 1 took effect immediately and supersedes the March 5, 2026 version [@CL-EST-3176; @CL-EST-3177]. RFO FAR 15.103-1 says the tradeoff approach to source selection is appropriate when it may be in the Government's best interest to consider award to other than the lowest priced offeror or other than the highest technically rated offeror [@CL-MKT-2262]. It also says that this process permits tradeoffs among cost or price and non-cost or non-price factors, and that the anticipated benefits of a higher priced proposal must merit the additional cost [@CL-MKT-2263]. RFO FAR 15.104(a) says all factors and significant subfactors that will affect contract award and their relative importance must be stated clearly in the RFP [@CL-MKT-2264]. RFO FAR 15.104(d) says that in addition to stating the relative importance of each evaluation factor, and unless the exception at paragraph (b)(1)(ii) of that section applies, the RFP must also state, at a minimum, whether all evaluation factors other than cost or price, when combined, are significantly more important than cost or price, approximately equal to cost or price, or significantly less important than cost or price [@CL-MKT-2011; @CL-MKT-2265; @CL-MKT-2266]. In accordance with title 10 of the United States Code, section 3206(c), the exception at RFO FAR 15.104(b)(1)(ii)(A) says that for DoD, NASA, and the Coast Guard, contracting officers may choose not to include price or cost as an evaluation factor for award when an RFP has an estimated value above the simplified acquisition threshold, will result in multiple-award contracts that are for the same or similar services, and states that the Government intends to make an award to each and all qualifying offerors [@CL-MKT-2308; @CL-MKT-2309]. RFO FAR 15.205 says the source selection decision must be documented and include the rationale for any business judgments and tradeoffs made or relied on by the SSA, including benefits associated with additional costs, and that the documentation does not need to quantify the tradeoffs that led to the decision [@CL-MKT-2267]. RFO FAR 15.408-1(a) says the prenegotiation objectives establish the Government's initial negotiation position and are based on the results of the contracting officer's analysis of the offeror's proposal [@CL-MKT-2268]. RFO FAR 15.408-1(b) says contracting officers must establish prenegotiation objectives before negotiating a pricing action, that the scope and depth of the analysis supporting the objectives should be directly related to the dollar value, importance, and complexity of the pricing action [@CL-MKT-2269], and that when cost analysis is required, contracting officers must document the pertinent issues to be negotiated, the cost objectives, and a profit or fee objective [@CL-MKT-2270]. RFO FAR 15.103-4(a) says that phased acquisitions break down complex or high-risk acquisitions into distinct parts for contract award and, instead of a single and comprehensive proposal and evaluation process, are structured to make separate awards at distinct phases of the acquisition [@CL-MKT-2092]. RFO FAR 15.103-4(b) says that when using a phased approach, the RFP must establish the phases for the acquisition and describe the process that will be used to evaluate proposals and any criteria offerors must meet to progress to the next phase in the RFP [@CL-MKT-2093].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

Two offerors answer a solicitation that states that the non-price factors, when combined, are significantly more important than price [@CL-MKT-1327]. The lower priced proposal scores 70 on the non-price factors and asks 2,000,000. The higher priced proposal scores 88 and asks 2,400,000, which is 400,000 more.

The agency may consider award to the higher priced offeror if that may be in the Government's best interest [@CL-MKT-1325]. The perceived benefits of that proposal must merit the additional 400,000, and the rationale is documented in the file [@CL-MKT-1328]. For DoD, under the RFO text, the benefits are described as anticipated benefits and the rationale goes in the documentation of the source selection decision [@CL-EST-3176; @CL-MKT-2263; @CL-MKT-2267]. The contracting officer establishes prenegotiation objectives before negotiating the pricing action [@CL-MKT-1330]. If cost analysis is required for that action, the contracting officer also documents the pertinent issues to be negotiated, the cost objectives and a profit or fee objective [@CL-MKT-1331].

## Never confuse

The tradeoff process lets the Government accept a higher-priced proposal when non-cost benefits merit the additional cost [@CL-MKT-1325; @CL-MKT-2263], unlike [Lowest price technically acceptable source selection](../../estimating/pricing/est-lpta-source-selection.md), where award goes to the lowest evaluated price among technically acceptable proposals without paying a premium for technical superiority [@CL-EST-1350].
