## Purpose

Explains how long a vessel repair master agreement lasts, who can end it and with what notice, whom the Government solicits for a job order, and when the contracting officer may order emergency work without a solicitation.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-MKT-1928] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 217.7103-2, 217.7103-3 and 217.7103-4 [@CL-MKT-1928; @CL-MKT-1932; @CL-MKT-1934] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-MKT-1928] |
| Applies to | Master agreements for repair and alteration of vessels and the job orders placed under them [@CL-MKT-1928; @CL-MKT-1932] |

Master agreements remain in effect until canceled by either the contractor or the contracting officer [@CL-MKT-1928]. Either of them can cancel a master agreement by giving 30 days written notice to the other [@CL-MKT-1929]. Cancellation of a master agreement does not affect the rights and liabilities under any job order existing at the time of cancellation [@CL-MKT-1930]. The contractor must continue to perform all work covered by any job order issued before the effective date of cancellation of the master agreement [@CL-MKT-1931].

When a requirement arises within the United States or its outlying areas for the type of work covered by the master agreement, offers must be solicited from prospective contractors that previously executed a master agreement [@CL-MKT-1932]. Offers must also be solicited from prospective contractors that have not previously executed a master agreement but possess the necessary qualifications to perform the work and agree to execute a master agreement before award of a job order [@CL-MKT-1933]. The procedures at PGI 217.7103-3 are to be followed when preparing solicitations for job orders [@CL-MKT-2033]. The section states no solicitation rule for a requirement that arises outside the United States and its outlying areas.

The contracting officer, without soliciting offers, may issue a written job order to a contractor that has previously executed a master agreement when delay in the performance of necessary repair work would endanger a vessel, its cargo or stores [@CL-MKT-1934]. The same may be done when military necessity requires immediate work on a vessel [@CL-MKT-1935]. For emergency work, the procedures at PGI 217.7103-4 are to be followed when processing this type of undefinitized contract action [@CL-MKT-2034].

## How it works

The master agreement is a standing arrangement that runs until the contractor or the contracting officer cancels it on written notice, and cancellation works only forward, because job orders already issued keep their effect and the contractor must finish the work they cover [@CL-MKT-1928; @CL-MKT-1929; @CL-MKT-1930; @CL-MKT-1931]. For a requirement in the covered area, the Government solicits both groups: existing agreement holders, and qualified contractors that agree to execute an agreement before the job order is awarded [@CL-MKT-1932; @CL-MKT-1933]. The solicitation duty falls on the Government, and a contractor without an agreement must agree to execute one before award to be solicited [@CL-MKT-1933]. The emergency route lets the contracting officer drop the solicitation but not the requirement that the contractor already holds an agreement [@CL-MKT-1934]. The section calls the emergency job order an undefinitized contract action, and the approval and obligation limits for those actions are on [Approval and obligation limits for undefinitized contract actions](mkt-uca-approval-and-obligation-limits.md) [@CL-MKT-2034].

How master agreements and job orders are defined, and which contractors the Government enters into agreements with, are on [Master agreements for vessel repair and the job orders placed under them](mkt-vessel-repair-master-agreements-and-job-orders.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor gives the contracting officer 30 days written notice cancelling its vessel repair master agreement [@CL-MKT-1929]. A job order for 400,000 that was issued before the cancellation takes effect still binds both sides, and the contractor must finish all of its work [@CL-MKT-1930; @CL-MKT-1931]. Later, a requirement arises in the United States for work the master agreements cover. The Government solicits offers from three contractors that hold master agreements and from one qualified contractor that has none but agrees to execute one before award [@CL-MKT-1932; @CL-MKT-1933]. In a separate case, a delay in necessary repair work would endanger a vessel, so the contracting officer issues a written job order, without soliciting offers, to a contractor that already holds a master agreement [@CL-MKT-1934]. A contractor without a master agreement could not receive an order under that emergency authority [@CL-MKT-1934].

## Never confuse

Cancellation ends the master agreement but not the job orders already issued [@CL-MKT-1930]. The emergency exception removes the solicitation step, not the need for an existing master agreement [@CL-MKT-1934]. A contractor that has not executed a master agreement can be solicited for a job order if it is qualified and agrees to execute one before award, but it cannot receive an order under the emergency authority, which is for contractors that previously executed a master agreement [@CL-MKT-1933; @CL-MKT-1934].
