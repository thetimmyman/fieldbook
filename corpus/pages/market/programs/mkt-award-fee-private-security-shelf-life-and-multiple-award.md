## Purpose

Connects award fee to compliance with private security requirements, shows the order design and reporting data for short shelf-life items, and sets out who makes the DoD single-source determination for task or delivery order contracts.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-MKT-1588] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 216.405-2-71, DFARS 216.501-2-70 and DFARS 216.504 [@CL-MKT-1588; @CL-MKT-1591; @CL-MKT-1593] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-MKT-1588] |
| Applies to | Award-fee plans, indefinite-delivery contracts for short shelf-life items, and the single-source determination for task or delivery order contracts [@CL-MKT-1588; @CL-MKT-1591; @CL-MKT-1593] |

The contracting officer shall include in any award-fee plan a requirement to review contractor compliance with, or violation of, applicable requirements of the contract with regard to the performance of private security functions [@CL-MKT-1588]. The functions covered are those performed in an area of contingency operations, complex contingency operations, or other military operations or exercises that are designated by the combatant commander [@CL-MKT-1639]. The fee reduction and recovery consideration below applies in evaluating performance under a contract that includes the clause at DFARS 252.225-7039, Defense Contractors Performing Private Security Functions Outside the United States [@CL-MKT-1640]. The contracting officer shall consider reducing or denying award fees for a period if the contractor fails to comply with the requirements of the clause during such period [@CL-MKT-1589]. The evaluation also shall consider recovering all or part of award fees previously paid for such period [@CL-MKT-1590].

For items with a shelf-life of less than 6 months, the use of indefinite-delivery type contracts is to be considered, with orders placed either directly by the users or by central purchasing offices with deliveries direct to users [@CL-MKT-1591]. Whenever an indefinite-delivery contract is issued, the issuing office must furnish all ordering offices sufficient information for the ordering office to complete its contract reporting responsibilities under DFARS 204.670-2 [@CL-MKT-1641]. That data must be furnished by the issuing office to the ordering activity in sufficient time for the activity to prepare its report for the action within 3 working days of the order [@CL-MKT-1592]. DFARS 216.501-2-70 refers to DFARS 217.204(e)(i) for limitations on the period for task order or delivery order contracts awarded by DoD pursuant to section 3403 of title 10, United States Code [@CL-MKT-1642].

The senior procurement executive has the authority to make the determination authorized in FAR 16.504(c)(1)(ii)(D)(1) [@CL-MKT-1643]. When making the determination at FAR 16.504(c)(1)(ii)(D)(1)(i), the senior procurement executive shall determine that the task or delivery orders expected under the contract are so integrally related that only a single source can efficiently perform the work, instead of reasonably perform the work as required by the FAR [@CL-MKT-1699; @CL-MKT-1593]. The congressional notification requirement at FAR 16.504(c)(1)(ii)(D)(2) does not apply to DoD [@CL-MKT-1594]. The determination at FAR 16.504(c)(1)(ii)(D) is not required if a justification has been executed in accordance with FAR subpart 6.3 and DFARS subpart 206.3 [@CL-MKT-1644].

## How it works

In evaluating the contractor's performance under a contract that includes the clause at DFARS 252.225-7039, the contracting officer shall consider reducing or denying award fees for a period if the contractor fails to comply with the requirements of the clause during that period, and the evaluation also shall consider recovering all or part of the award fees previously paid for that period [@CL-MKT-1640; @CL-MKT-1589; @CL-MKT-1590]. The contracting officer shall also include in any award-fee plan a requirement to review contractor compliance with, or violation of, applicable contract requirements on private security functions performed in an area of contingency operations, complex contingency operations, or other military operations or exercises designated by the combatant commander [@CL-MKT-1588; @CL-MKT-1639].

For items with a shelf-life of less than 6 months, the use of indefinite-delivery type contracts is to be considered, with orders placed either directly by the users or by central purchasing offices with deliveries direct to users [@CL-MKT-1591]. Whenever an indefinite-delivery contract is issued, the issuing office must furnish all ordering offices sufficient information for them to complete their contract reporting responsibilities, in sufficient time for the activity to prepare its report for the action within 3 working days of the order [@CL-MKT-1641; @CL-MKT-1592]. The single-source determination sits with the senior procurement executive and, for the integrally-related ground, uses the efficiently-perform standard, and a justification executed in accordance with FAR subpart 6.3 and DFARS subpart 206.3 removes the need for the determination [@CL-MKT-1643; @CL-MKT-1593; @CL-MKT-1644].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An award-fee contract includes the clause at DFARS 252.225-7039, and its award-fee plan requires the private security compliance review [@CL-MKT-1588; @CL-MKT-1640]. The contractor fails to comply with the clause during one evaluation period. The contracting officer must consider reducing or denying the award fee for that period [@CL-MKT-1589] and must also consider recovering award fee already paid for it [@CL-MKT-1590]. After considering both, the contracting officer could decide to reduce the fee by 40,000 and recover 15,000, but the section requires that these steps be considered [@CL-MKT-1589; @CL-MKT-1590].

An item has a shelf-life of 4 months, which is less than 6 months, so the contracting officer considers an indefinite-delivery contract with orders placed by users or by central offices delivering direct to users [@CL-MKT-1591]. For an order placed on Day 10, the issuing office must have furnished the data in time for the ordering activity to prepare its report within 3 working days of the order [@CL-MKT-1641; @CL-MKT-1592].

When the determination at FAR 16.504(c)(1)(ii)(D)(1)(i) is that the expected orders are so integrally related that only a single source can perform the work, the senior procurement executive makes it and applies the efficiently-perform wording [@CL-MKT-1643; @CL-MKT-1699; @CL-MKT-1593]. If a justification has already been executed in accordance with FAR subpart 6.3 and DFARS subpart 206.3, the determination is not required [@CL-MKT-1644].

## Never confuse

The contracting officer's consideration of reducing or denying award fees for a period of noncompliance is part of evaluating the contractor's performance under a contract that includes the clause [@CL-MKT-1640; @CL-MKT-1589]. The single-source determination on the integrally-related ground concerns whether the expected orders are so integrally related that only a single source can efficiently perform the work [@CL-MKT-1593]. The ordering side is on [ordering multiple-award contracts](../../estimating/pricing/est-dod-ordering-multiple-award-contracts.md). The contractor's private security obligations are on [private security functions outside the United States](../../compliance/regulatory-framework/cmp-private-security-functions-outside-the-united-states.md), and indefinite-delivery contract mechanics on [indefinite-delivery contracts](../../contract-finance/funding/ctf-indefinite-delivery-and-indefinite-quantity-contracts.md).
