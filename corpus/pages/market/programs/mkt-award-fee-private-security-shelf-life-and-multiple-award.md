## Purpose

Connects award fee to compliance with private security requirements, shows the order design and reporting data for short shelf-life items, and sets out who makes the DoD single-source determination for task or delivery order contracts.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-MKT-1588] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 216.405-2-71, DFARS 216.501-2-70 and DFARS 216.504 [@CL-MKT-1588; @CL-MKT-1591; @CL-MKT-1593] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-MKT-1588] |
| Applies to | Award-fee plans, indefinite-delivery contracts for short shelf-life items, and the single-source determination for task or delivery order contracts [@CL-MKT-1588; @CL-MKT-1591; @CL-MKT-1593] |

The contracting officer shall include in any award-fee plan a requirement to review contractor compliance with, or violation of, applicable requirements of the contract with regard to the performance of private security functions [@CL-MKT-1588]. The functions covered are those performed in an area of contingency operations, complex contingency operations, or other military operations or exercises that are designated by the combatant commander [@CL-MKT-1639]. The fee reduction and recovery consideration below applies in evaluating performance under a contract that includes the clause at DFARS 252.225-7039, Defense Contractors Performing Private Security Functions Outside the United States [@CL-MKT-1640]. The contracting officer shall consider reducing or denying award fees for a period if the contractor fails to comply with the requirements of the clause during such period [@CL-MKT-1589]. The evaluation also shall consider recovering all or part of award fees previously paid for such period [@CL-MKT-1590].

For items with a shelf-life of less than 6 months, the use of indefinite-delivery type contracts is to be considered, with orders placed either directly by the users or by central purchasing offices with deliveries direct to users [@CL-MKT-1591]. Whenever an indefinite-delivery contract is issued, the issuing office must furnish all ordering offices sufficient information for the ordering office to complete its contract reporting responsibilities under DFARS 204.670-2 [@CL-MKT-1641]. That data must be furnished by the issuing office to the ordering activity in sufficient time for the activity to prepare its report for the action within 3 working days of the order [@CL-MKT-1592]. The section refers to DFARS 217.204(e)(i) for limitations on the period for task order or delivery order contracts awarded by DoD pursuant to a statute [@CL-MKT-1642].

The senior procurement executive has the authority to make the determination authorized in FAR 16.504(c)(1)(ii)(D)(1) [@CL-MKT-1643]. When making the determination at FAR 16.504(c)(1)(ii)(D)(1)(i), the senior procurement executive shall determine that the task or delivery orders expected under the contract are so integrally related that only a single source can efficiently perform the work, instead of reasonably perform the work as required by the FAR [@CL-MKT-1699; @CL-MKT-1593]. The congressional notification requirement at FAR 16.504(c)(1)(ii)(D)(2) does not apply to DoD [@CL-MKT-1594]. The determination at FAR 16.504(c)(1)(ii)(D) is not required if a justification has been executed in accordance with FAR subpart 6.3 and DFARS subpart 206.3 [@CL-MKT-1644].

## How it works

The review, reporting and determination duties bind Government officials. The private security clause is the part that reaches the contractor's fee: a period of noncompliance requires the contracting officer to consider reducing or denying award fee for that period and recovering fee already paid for it, and the award-fee plan must require the review that finds it [@CL-MKT-1588; @CL-MKT-1589; @CL-MKT-1590].

For short shelf-life items the contracting officer considers an indefinite-delivery contract with direct ordering or direct delivery, and the issuing office supplies the data so that each ordering activity can report within the time allowed [@CL-MKT-1591; @CL-MKT-1641; @CL-MKT-1592]. The single-source determination sits with the senior procurement executive and, for the integrally-related ground, uses the efficiently-perform standard, and a justification executed in accordance with FAR subpart 6.3 and DFARS subpart 206.3 removes the need for the determination [@CL-MKT-1643; @CL-MKT-1593; @CL-MKT-1644].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An award-fee contract includes the clause at DFARS 252.225-7039, and its award-fee plan requires the private security compliance review [@CL-MKT-1588; @CL-MKT-1640]. The contractor fails to comply with the clause during one evaluation period. The contracting officer must consider reducing or denying the award fee for that period [@CL-MKT-1589] and must also consider recovering award fee already paid for it [@CL-MKT-1590]. After considering both, the contracting officer could decide to reduce the fee by 40,000 and recover 15,000, but the section requires only that these steps be considered [@CL-MKT-1589; @CL-MKT-1590].

An item has a shelf-life of 4 months, which is less than 6 months, so the contracting officer considers an indefinite-delivery contract with orders placed by users or by central offices delivering direct to users [@CL-MKT-1591]. For an order placed on Day 10, the issuing office must have furnished the data in time for the ordering activity to prepare its report within 3 working days of the order [@CL-MKT-1641; @CL-MKT-1592].

When the determination at FAR 16.504(c)(1)(ii)(D)(1)(i) is that the expected orders are so integrally related that only a single source can perform the work, the senior procurement executive makes it and applies the efficiently-perform wording [@CL-MKT-1643; @CL-MKT-1699; @CL-MKT-1593]. If a justification under FAR subpart 6.3 has already been executed, the determination is not required [@CL-MKT-1644].

## Never confuse

Reducing or denying fee for a noncompliant period is a matter for the award-fee evaluation under the clause [@CL-MKT-1589]. The single-source determination on the integrally-related ground concerns whether a single source can efficiently perform the expected orders, and it is not the placement of an individual order [@CL-MKT-1593]. The ordering side is on [ordering multiple-award contracts](../../estimating/pricing/est-dod-ordering-multiple-award-contracts.md). The contractor's private security obligations are on [private security functions outside the United States](../../compliance/regulatory-framework/cmp-private-security-functions-outside-the-united-states.md), and indefinite-delivery contract mechanics on [indefinite-delivery contracts](../../contract-finance/funding/ctf-indefinite-delivery-and-indefinite-quantity-contracts.md).
