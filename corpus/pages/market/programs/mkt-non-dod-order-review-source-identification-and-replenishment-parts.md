## Purpose

Covers the review that precedes a DoD order placed under a non-DoD contract, the contract requirement for a contractor to identify its sources of supply, and the rules for buying replenishment parts.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-MKT-1575] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 217.770, DFARS 217.7302 and DFARS 217.7502 [@CL-MKT-1575; @CL-MKT-1578; @CL-MKT-1580] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-MKT-1575] |
| Applies to | Orders above the simplified acquisition threshold under non-DoD contracts, contracts for the delivery of supplies, and replenishment parts [@CL-MKT-1575; @CL-MKT-1578; @CL-MKT-1580] |

Departments and agencies shall establish and maintain procedures for reviewing and approving orders placed for supplies and services under non-DoD contracts, whether through direct acquisition or assisted acquisition, when the amount of the order exceeds the simplified acquisition threshold [@CL-MKT-1575]. The procedures shall include evaluating whether using a non-DoD contract is in the best interest of DoD, considering satisfying customer requirements, schedule, and cost effectiveness taking into account discounts and fees [@CL-MKT-1576]. To ensure awareness of the total cost of fees, the procedures at PGI 217.770(a)(3) are followed [@CL-MKT-1624]. The procedures shall also address contract administration including oversight, determining that the tasks or supplies are within the scope of the contract to be used, and reviewing funding to ensure that it is used in accordance with appropriation limitations [@CL-MKT-1577]. They shall also include collecting and reporting data on the use of assisted acquisition for analysis, following the reporting requirements in subpart 204.6 [@CL-MKT-1625].

Whenever practicable, a requirement for contractor identification of sources of supply is to be included in all contracts for the delivery of supplies [@CL-MKT-1578]. The identification shall include the item's actual manufacturer or producer, or all the contractor's sources for the item, and the item's national stock number if there is one [@CL-MKT-1626]. It shall also include the item identification number used by the actual manufacturer or producer or by each of the contractor's sources, and the source of any technical data delivered under the contract [@CL-MKT-1627]. The requirement does not apply to contracts that are for commercial products or valued at or below the simplified acquisition threshold [@CL-MKT-1579].

Departments and agencies may acquire replenishment parts concurrently with production of the end item, and shall provide for full and open competition when fully adequate drawings and any other needed data are available with the right to use for acquisition purposes [@CL-MKT-1580]. When data is not available for a competitive acquisition, one of the procedures in PGI 217.7504 is used [@CL-MKT-1628]. Replenishment parts must be acquired so as to ensure the safe, dependable, and effective operation of the equipment [@CL-MKT-1629]. Where that assurance is not possible with new sources, competition may be limited to the original manufacturer of the equipment or other sources that have previously manufactured or furnished the parts as long as the action is justified [@CL-MKT-1581]. The section refers to DFARS 209.270 for requirements applicable to replenishment parts for aviation or ship critical safety items [@CL-MKT-1630]. Departments and agencies shall follow the limitations on price increases in DFARS 217.7505 [@CL-MKT-1631].

## How it works

The order review and the replenishment rules bind departments and agencies. The source identification requirement is the one that reaches a contractor, as a term of a contract for the delivery of supplies [@CL-MKT-1578].

The order review is a gate before the order is placed, so the file shows the best-interest evaluation, the fee review, the scope check and the appropriation check [@CL-MKT-1576; @CL-MKT-1624; @CL-MKT-1577]. The threshold for the review is the amount of the order, while the threshold for the identification requirement is the value of the contract [@CL-MKT-1575; @CL-MKT-1579]. For replenishment parts, the default is competition on adequate data, and limiting competition to earlier sources needs both the assurance problem and a justification [@CL-MKT-1580; @CL-MKT-1581]. The price increase limits are on [multi-year services and spare parts price limits](../../estimating/pricing/est-dod-multiyear-services-and-spare-parts-price-limits.md).

The DFARS subpart on identifying sources of supply implements title 10 of the United States Code, section 4753, and contains policy and procedures for requiring contractors to identify the actual manufacturer of supplies furnished to DoD [@CL-MKT-2343]. Its policy is that contractors shall identify their sources of supply in contracts for supplies [@CL-MKT-2344]. That identification enables solicitation, in subsequent acquisitions, of actual manufacturers or other suppliers of items, which enhances competition and potentially avoids payment of additional costs for no significant added value [@CL-MKT-2345].

For spare parts breakout requirements, the DFARS section on the spare parts breakout program points to PGI 217.7506 and DoD Manual 4140.01, Volume 9, DoD Supply Chain Materiel Management Procedures: Materiel Programs [@CL-MKT-2348]. That section states no breakout requirement itself and names no official.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An activity plans an order of 900,000 for supplies under another agency's contract, either directly or as an assisted acquisition. The amount exceeds the simplified acquisition threshold, so the department's review procedures apply [@CL-MKT-1575]. The review weighs customer requirements, schedule, and cost effectiveness including a fee of 30,000 and any discounts [@CL-MKT-1576; @CL-MKT-1624]. It also covers contract administration, whether the supplies are within the scope of the contract used, and whether the funding fits the appropriation limitations [@CL-MKT-1577], and assisted acquisition data is reported [@CL-MKT-1625].

Separately, a supply contract valued at 600,000 for items that are not commercial products is awarded. Whenever practicable it includes the requirement that the contractor identify the actual manufacturer or all of its sources, the national stock number if there is one, the item identification number and the source of delivered technical data [@CL-MKT-1578; @CL-MKT-1626; @CL-MKT-1627]. A contract for commercial products would not carry the requirement [@CL-MKT-1579].

For a replenishment part, fully adequate drawings and other needed data are available with the right to use for acquisition purposes, and new sources can still ensure the safe, dependable, and effective operation of the equipment, so the activity provides for full and open competition [@CL-MKT-1580; @CL-MKT-1629]. Had safe and dependable operation not been assured with new sources, competition could be limited to the original manufacturer or earlier sources if the action is justified [@CL-MKT-1581].

## Never confuse

The order review is the ordering gate for the Government, and the source identification requirement is a contract term for the contractor [@CL-MKT-1575; @CL-MKT-1578]. An assisted acquisition is still an order under a non-DoD contract for the review [@CL-MKT-1575]. Limiting replenishment competition to earlier sources is not a general preference for the incumbent, because it needs the assurance problem with new sources and a justification [@CL-MKT-1581]. Interagency ordering mechanics are on [interagency acquisitions](../../contract-finance/funding/ctf-interagency-acquisitions.md).
