## Purpose

Shows how the contracting officer treats a small disadvantaged business representation, who determines HUBZone and women-owned status, and what the contracting officer verifies and does when status is lost.

## The rule

| Codified citation | FAR 19.304, FAR 19.1303 and FAR 19.1503 [@CL-MKT-1290; @CL-MKT-1293; @CL-MKT-1393] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |

The contracting officer may accept an offeror's representation that it is a small disadvantaged business concern [@CL-MKT-1290]. The provision at 52.219-1 or 52.212-3(c)(5) is used to collect small disadvantaged business data [@CL-MKT-1390]. A representation of that status on a Federal prime contract is deemed a misrepresentation if the firm does not meet the requirements of 13 CFR 124.1001 [@CL-MKT-1291].

Any person or entity that misrepresents a firm's status as a small disadvantaged business concern in order to obtain a contracting opportunity under section 8(d) of the Small Business Act is subject to the penalties imposed by section 16(d) of that Act [@CL-MKT-1292]. It is also subject to any other penalty authorized by law [@CL-MKT-1398].

## How it works

### HUBZone

The SBA determines HUBZone status in accordance with 13 CFR part 126 [@CL-MKT-1293]. If the SBA determines that a concern is a HUBZone small business, it designates the concern in the Dynamic Small Business Search, and the designation also appears in SAM [@CL-MKT-1391; @CL-MKT-1392]. Only firms designated in both as HUBZone small business concerns are eligible for HUBZone preferences, and those preferences are not contingent on the place of performance [@CL-MKT-1294].

A joint venture may be considered a HUBZone small business concern if it qualifies as small under 19.301-1(a)(2)(i), at least one party is a HUBZone small business concern, and it complies with 13 CFR 126.616(a) through (c) [@CL-MKT-1295]. To be eligible for a HUBZone contract under FAR 19.1303, a concern must be a HUBZone small business concern at the time of its initial offer [@CL-MKT-1296].

### Women-owned programs

The SBA determines status as an EDWOSB concern or WOSB concern eligible under the WOSB Program in accordance with 13 CFR part 127 [@CL-MKT-1393]. For a WOSB that seeks a WOSB or EDWOSB set-aside or sole-source contract, the contracting officer verifies that the offeror is registered in SAM and designated there as a certified EDWOSB or WOSB concern [@CL-MKT-1297]. Pending applications for certification appear only in the Dynamic Small Business Search, not in that SAM designation [@CL-MKT-1394; @CL-MKT-1297]. A joint venture may be considered an EDWOSB or WOSB concern eligible under the WOSB Program if the EDWOSB or WOSB participant is certified in SAM and the joint venture meets the requirements of 13 CFR 127.506 [@CL-MKT-1397].

If an SBA decision from a current eligibility examination finds that the concern did not qualify as an EDWOSB concern or WOSB concern eligible under the WOSB Program, the contracting officer may terminate the contract and shall not exercise any option or award further task or delivery orders [@CL-MKT-1298; @CL-MKT-1395]. In that same case, agencies shall not count or include the award toward the small business goals for the concern and must update FPDS from the date of award to reflect the final SBA decision [@CL-MKT-1395; @CL-MKT-1396].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A firm represents small disadvantaged business status on a Federal prime contract but does not meet the requirements of 13 CFR 124.1001, so the representation is deemed a misrepresentation [@CL-MKT-1291]. If it made the representation to obtain a contracting opportunity under section 8(d) of the Small Business Act, the person or entity is subject to the penalties imposed by section 16(d) of that Act [@CL-MKT-1292]. In a separate case, a firm bids a WOSB set-aside; the contracting officer verifies its SAM registration and certified designation [@CL-MKT-1297]. A later SBA decision from a current eligibility examination finds the firm did not qualify as a WOSB concern eligible under the WOSB Program. The contracting officer may terminate the contract, shall not exercise its options or place further orders, and the agency does not count the award toward its small business goals and updates FPDS from the date of award to reflect the final SBA decision [@CL-MKT-1298; @CL-MKT-1395; @CL-MKT-1396].

## Never confuse

Accepting a representation is not a determination: the contracting officer may accept a small disadvantaged business representation, while the SBA determines HUBZone and women-owned status [@CL-MKT-1290; @CL-MKT-1293; @CL-MKT-1393]. A HUBZone preference needs the designation in both the Dynamic Small Business Search and SAM, not the place of performance [@CL-MKT-1294]. The protest mechanics are on [the HUBZone status card](../taxonomy/mkt-hubzone-status-protests.md) and [the women-owned status card](mkt-edwosb-wosb-status-protests.md).
