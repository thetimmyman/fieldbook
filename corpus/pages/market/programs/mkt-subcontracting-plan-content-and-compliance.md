## Purpose

Shows what a subcontracting plan must contain, how the contracting officer tests good faith effort, and how a finding of failure leads to notice, a response period and liquidated damages.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-MKT-0242] |
| Codified citation | FAR 19.704 and FAR 19.705-7 [@CL-MKT-0242; @CL-MKT-0244] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-MKT-0242] |
| Applies to | Each subcontracting plan required under the listed provisions [@CL-MKT-0242] |

Each subcontracting plan required under the listed provisions shall include the items listed in the paragraph [@CL-MKT-0242]. One listed item is separate percentage goals for using small business (including ANCs and Indian tribes), veteran-owned small business, service-disabled veteran-owned small business, HUBZone small business, small disadvantaged business (including ANCs and Indian tribes) and women-owned small business concerns [@CL-MKT-0243]. The other listed items, including the statements of dollars and the assurances, are not restated here.

In deciding whether a contractor failed to make a good faith effort to comply with its plan, a contracting officer must look to the totality of the contractor's actions, consistent with the information and assurances provided in its plan [@CL-MKT-0244]. The fact that the contractor failed to meet its subcontracting goals does not, in and of itself, constitute a failure to make a good faith effort [@CL-MKT-0245].

## How it works

The section lists items that the contracting officer may consider, though not all inclusive, to be indicators of a failure to make a good faith effort, when they are considered in the context of the contractor's total effort in accordance with its plan [@CL-MKT-0246]. If the contracting officer decides in accordance with paragraph (b) of section 19.705-7 that the contractor failed to make a good faith effort to comply with its subcontracting plan, the contracting officer shall give the contractor written notice [@CL-MKT-0283]. The notice advises the contractor of the possibility of liquidated damages and provides a period of 15 working days (or longer period as necessary) within which to respond [@CL-MKT-0283; @CL-MKT-0247]. If, after consideration of all the pertinent data, the contracting officer finds that the contractor failed to make a good faith effort, the contracting officer shall issue a final decision [@CL-MKT-0293] and require the payment of liquidated damages in an amount stated [@CL-MKT-0249]. The damages equal the actual dollar amount by which the contractor failed to achieve each subcontracting goal, and the section refers commercial plan calculations to a separate paragraph [@CL-MKT-0248]. Liquidated damages are in addition to any other remedies that the Government may have [@CL-MKT-0284]. The listed indicators, commercial plans, master plans and reporting dates are not restated here.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A plan sets a small business goal of 200,000 of a contractor's subcontract dollars. The contractor reaches 150,000 and misses by 50,000. Missing the goal does not by itself show a failure of good faith. The contracting officer looks at all of the contractor's actions against its plan. If the officer finds a failure, the notice gives the contractor 15 working days to respond. If the finding stands, the final decision requires liquidated damages of 50,000 for that goal, and the officer repeats the calculation for each goal that was missed.

## Never confuse

Missing a subcontracting goal is not the same as failing to make a good faith effort [@CL-MKT-0245]. Liquidated damages follow the contracting officer's finding of failure and a final decision [@CL-MKT-0249; @CL-MKT-0293], not the miss alone. A plan's content and compliance are separate from the question of when a plan is required, which is on the [When a subcontracting plan is required](mkt-subcontracting-plan-when-required.md) card.
