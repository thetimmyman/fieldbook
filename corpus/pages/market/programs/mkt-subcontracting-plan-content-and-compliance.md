## Purpose

Shows what a subcontracting plan must contain, how the contracting officer tests good faith effort, and how a finding of failure leads to notice, a response period and liquidated damages.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-MKT-0242] |
| Codified citation | FAR 19.704 and FAR 19.705-7 [@CL-MKT-0242; @CL-MKT-0244] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-MKT-0242] |
| Applies to | Each subcontracting plan required under FAR 19.301-2(e) and FAR 19.702(a)(1)(i), (ii), and (iii) [@CL-MKT-0242] |

Each subcontracting plan required under FAR 19.301-2(e) and FAR 19.702(a)(1)(i), (ii), and (iii) shall include the items listed in FAR 19.704(a), including separate percentage goals for using small business (including ANCs and Indian tribes), veteran-owned small business, service-disabled veteran-owned small business, HUBZone small business, small disadvantaged business (including ANCs and Indian tribes), and women-owned small business concerns as subcontractors [@CL-MKT-0242; @CL-MKT-0243].

In deciding whether a contractor failed to make a good faith effort to comply with its plan, a contracting officer must look to the totality of the contractor's actions, consistent with the information and assurances provided in its plan [@CL-MKT-0244]. The fact that the contractor failed to meet its subcontracting goals does not, in and of itself, constitute a failure to make a good faith effort [@CL-MKT-0245].

## How it works

When considered in the context of the contractor's total effort in accordance with its plan, the contracting officer may consider the items listed in FAR 19.705-7(b)(2), though not all inclusive, to be indicators of a failure to make a good faith effort [@CL-MKT-0246]. If the contracting officer decides in accordance with FAR 19.705-7(b) that the contractor failed to make a good faith effort to comply with its subcontracting plan, the contracting officer shall give the contractor written notice in accordance with FAR 52.219-16, Liquidated Damages—Subcontracting Plan, specifying the material breach, which may be included in the contractor's past performance information, advising the contractor of the possibility that the contractor may have to pay to the Government liquidated damages, and providing a period of 15 working days (or longer period as necessary) within which to respond [@CL-MKT-0283; @CL-MKT-1993; @CL-MKT-0247]. The notice shall give the contractor an opportunity to demonstrate what good faith efforts have been made before the contracting officer issues the final decision, and shall further state that failure of the contractor to respond may be taken as an admission that no valid explanation exists [@CL-MKT-1994]. If, after consideration of all the pertinent data, the contracting officer finds that the contractor failed to make a good faith effort to comply with its subcontracting plan, the contracting officer shall issue a final decision to the contractor to that effect and require the payment of liquidated damages in an amount stated [@CL-MKT-0293; @CL-MKT-0249]. The contracting officer's final decision shall state that the contractor has the right to appeal under the clause in the contract entitled Disputes, and calculations and procedures shall be in accordance with FAR 52.219-16 [@CL-MKT-1995]. The amount of damages attributable to the contractor's failure to comply shall be an amount equal to the actual dollar amount by which the contractor failed to achieve each subcontracting goal, and for calculations for commercial plans see FAR 19.705-7(f) [@CL-MKT-0248]. Liquidated damages shall be in addition to any other remedies that the Government may have [@CL-MKT-0284].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A plan sets a small business goal of 200,000 of a contractor's subcontract dollars. The contractor reaches 150,000 and misses by 50,000. Missing the goal does not by itself show a failure of good faith. The contracting officer looks at all of the contractor's actions against its plan. If the officer finds a failure, the notice gives the contractor 15 working days to respond. If the finding stands, the final decision requires liquidated damages of 50,000 for that goal, and the officer repeats the calculation for each goal that was missed.

## Never confuse

The fact that the contractor failed to meet its subcontracting goals does not, in and of itself, constitute a failure to make a good faith effort [@CL-MKT-0245]. Requiring the payment of liquidated damages follows the contracting officer's written notice, consideration of all the pertinent data, and a final decision finding that the contractor failed to make a good faith effort to comply with its subcontracting plan [@CL-MKT-0283; @CL-MKT-0293; @CL-MKT-0249]. When a subcontracting plan is required is covered on the [When a subcontracting plan is required](mkt-subcontracting-plan-when-required.md) card.
