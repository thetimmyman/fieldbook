## Purpose

Covers what the contracting officer must do after a responsibility referral to the SBA, and what the SBA may and may not do under the subcontracting program.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-MKT-1310] |
| Codified citation | FAR 19.602-4 and FAR 19.707 [@CL-MKT-1310; @CL-MKT-1312; @CL-MKT-1313] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-MKT-1310] |
| Applies to | Responsibility referrals to the SBA, and SBA review under the small business subcontracting program [@CL-MKT-1310; @CL-MKT-1313] |

The contracting officer shall award the contract to the concern in question if the SBA issues a certificate of competency after receiving the referral, and an SBA-certified concern shall not be required to meet any other requirements of responsibility [@CL-MKT-1310]. SBA certificates are conclusive with respect to all elements of responsibility of prospective small business contractors [@CL-MKT-1311]. Where the SBA issues a certificate, the contracting officer may decide not to award to that offeror for reasons unrelated to responsibility [@CL-MKT-1409].

## How it works

### The referral outcome

If new information causes the contracting officer to determine that the referred concern is actually responsible, and award has not already been made to another offeror after the SBA's period has run, the contracting officer reverses the nonresponsibility determination [@CL-MKT-1410; @CL-MKT-1312]. The contracting officer then notifies the SBA, withdraws the referral, and proceeds to award the contract [@CL-MKT-1411].

If the SBA has not issued a certificate within 15 business days, or a longer period agreed with the SBA, after receiving the referral, the contracting officer proceeds with the acquisition and awards to another appropriately selected and responsible offeror [@CL-MKT-1312].

### The SBA's role in the subcontracting program

Under the program, the SBA may assist both Government agencies and contractors in carrying out their responsibilities with regard to subcontracting plans [@CL-MKT-1412]. It may review, within 5 working days, any solicitation that meets the dollar threshold in 19.702(a)(1)(i) or (ii) before the solicitation is issued [@CL-MKT-1414]. It may also review, within 5 working days before execution, any negotiated contractual document requiring a subcontracting plan, including the plan itself, and submit recommendations to the contracting officer, which are advisory in nature [@CL-MKT-1313]. It may evaluate compliance with subcontracting plans, either contract by contract or, for contractors having multiple contracts, on an aggregate basis [@CL-MKT-1413].

The SBA is not authorized to prescribe the extent to which any contractor or subcontractor shall subcontract, to specify the concerns to which subcontracts will be awarded, or to exercise any authority regarding the administration of individual prime contracts or subcontracts [@CL-MKT-1314].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contracting officer finds a small offeror nonresponsible on a capacity ground and refers the matter to the SBA. The SBA issues a certificate of competency, so the contracting officer awards to that offeror and does not require it to meet any other responsibility requirement [@CL-MKT-1310]. The contracting officer may still decline the award for a reason unrelated to responsibility [@CL-MKT-1409]. In a separate referral, the SBA has not issued a certificate 15 business days after receiving the referral and no longer period was agreed, so the contracting officer awards to another responsible offeror [@CL-MKT-1312]. For a subcontracting plan, the SBA's recommendations on the negotiated document are advisory, and the SBA cannot direct which subcontractors the contractor uses [@CL-MKT-1313; @CL-MKT-1314].

## Never confuse

Certificates of competency are conclusive with respect to all elements of responsibility of prospective small business contractors [@CL-MKT-1311]; where the SBA issues one, the contracting officer may decide not to award to that offeror for reasons unrelated to responsibility [@CL-MKT-1409]. The SBA's recommendations to the contracting officer on a negotiated contractual document requiring a subcontracting plan are advisory in nature [@CL-MKT-1313], and the SBA is not authorized to prescribe the extent to which any contractor or subcontractor shall subcontract [@CL-MKT-1314]. The certificate process is on [the certificate card](mkt-coc-issuance-denial-and-resolution.md) and plan review on [the plan review card](mkt-subcontracting-plan-review.md).
