## Purpose

Shows the DoD rule that contracting officers use objective criteria to measure performance, when a multiple-incentive contract is the most appropriate type, and where the incentive arrangement for a fixed-price incentive (firm target) contract starts.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-MKT-1848] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 216.401-71 and DFARS 216.403-1(b) [@CL-MKT-1848; @CL-MKT-1852; @CL-MKT-1853] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-MKT-1848] |
| Applies to | Cost-plus-incentive-fee and fixed-price-incentive contracts, including fixed-price incentive (firm target) contracts [@CL-MKT-1849; @CL-MKT-1852] |

Contracting officers shall use objective criteria to the maximum extent possible to measure contract performance [@CL-MKT-1848]. The section associates objective criteria with cost-plus-incentive-fee and fixed-price-incentive contracts [@CL-MKT-1849]. When objective criteria exist but the contracting officer determines that it is in the best interest of the Government also to incentivize subjective elements of performance, the most appropriate contract type is a multiple-incentive contract [@CL-MKT-1850]. A multiple-incentive contract contains both objective incentives and subjective award-fee criteria, that is, cost-plus-incentive-fee/award-fee or fixed-price-incentive/award-fee [@CL-MKT-1851]. The section refers to PGI 216.401(e) for guidance on the use of award-fee contracts [@CL-MKT-1945].

The contracting officer shall give particular consideration to the use of fixed-price incentive (firm target) contracts, especially for acquisitions moving from development to production [@CL-MKT-1852]. The contracting officer shall also pay particular attention to share lines and ceiling prices for those contracts [@CL-MKT-1853]. A 120 percent ceiling and a 50/50 share ratio are the point of departure for establishing the incentive arrangement [@CL-MKT-1854]. The section refers to PGI 216.403-1 for guidance on the use of these contracts [@CL-MKT-1946].

## How it works

The contracting officer measures performance with objective criteria as far as possible [@CL-MKT-1848]. If objective criteria exist and the contracting officer determines that the Government's best interest also calls for incentivizing subjective elements, the section names a multiple-incentive contract as the most appropriate type [@CL-MKT-1850; @CL-MKT-1851]. For a firm-target contract, the contracting officer gives particular consideration to the type, especially when work moves from development to production, and watches share lines and ceiling prices from the stated starting point [@CL-MKT-1852; @CL-MKT-1853; @CL-MKT-1854]. The section calls that starting point a point of departure for establishing the incentive arrangement [@CL-MKT-1854].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A program is moving from development to production. The contracting officer gives particular consideration to a fixed-price incentive (firm target) contract [@CL-MKT-1852]. The contracting officer begins from a 120 percent ceiling and a 50/50 share ratio and pays particular attention to the share lines and the ceiling price [@CL-MKT-1853; @CL-MKT-1854]. The program has measurable cost and delivery targets, so the contracting officer uses objective criteria to measure performance [@CL-MKT-1848]. The contracting officer also determines that it is in the best interest of the Government to incentivize a subjective element such as responsiveness. The most appropriate type is then a multiple-incentive contract, such as a fixed-price-incentive contract with award fee [@CL-MKT-1850; @CL-MKT-1851]. Without that determination, this section does not name a multiple-incentive contract as the most appropriate type [@CL-MKT-1850].

## Never confuse

A multiple-incentive contract contains both objective incentives and subjective award-fee criteria [@CL-MKT-1851]. For fixed-price incentive (firm target) contracts, a 120 percent ceiling and a 50/50 share ratio are the point of departure for establishing the incentive arrangement [@CL-MKT-1854]. The general rules for the contract type are on [Fixed-price incentive (firm target) contracts](../../estimating/pricing/est-fpi-firm-target.md), and the award-fee rules are on [Award-fee contracts: cost-plus-award-fee and fixed-price with award fee](../../estimating/pricing/est-award-fee-contracts.md).

## Company calibration

Which of the company's incentive contracts are cost-plus-incentive-fee, fixed-price-incentive or combined with an award fee, and where each share line and ceiling price is recorded. [@Q-004]
