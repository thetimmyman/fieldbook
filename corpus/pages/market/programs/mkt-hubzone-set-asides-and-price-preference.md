## Purpose

Shows the conditions for a HUBZone set-aside and a HUBZone sole-source award, and how the HUBZone price evaluation preference is applied.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-MKT-0612] |
| Codified citation | FAR 19.1305, FAR 19.1306 and FAR 19.1307 [@CL-MKT-0612; @CL-MKT-0613; @CL-MKT-0615]; FAR 2.101 [@CL-MKT-9246; @CL-MKT-9247; @CL-MKT-9248] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 2.101 [@CL-MKT-9280; @CL-MKT-9281; @CL-MKT-9282] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-MKT-0612] |
| Applies to | Acquisitions considered for a HUBZone set-aside, a HUBZone sole-source award or the price evaluation preference [@CL-MKT-0612; @CL-MKT-0613; @CL-MKT-0615] |

To set aside an acquisition for competition restricted to HUBZone small business concerns, the contracting officer must have a reasonable expectation that offers will be received from two or more HUBZone small business concerns and that award will be made at a fair market price [@CL-MKT-0612]. A contracting officer shall consider a contract award to a HUBZone small business concern on a sole-source basis (see FAR 6.302-5(b)(5)) before considering a small business set-aside (see FAR 19.203 and subpart 19.5), provided none of the exclusions at FAR 19.1304 apply and the further listed conditions are met [@CL-MKT-0613]. One listed condition is that the anticipated price of the contract, including options, will not exceed $8.5 million for a requirement within the North American Industry Classification System (NAICS) codes for manufacturing or $5.5 million for a requirement within all other NAICS codes [@CL-MKT-0613; @CL-MKT-0614].

In codified FAR 2.101, a HUBZone small business concern means a small business concern that meets the requirements described in 13 CFR 126.200, is certified by the Small Business Administration (SBA) and designated by SBA as a HUBZone small business concern in the Dynamic Small Business Search (DSBS) (13 CFR 126.103) [@CL-MKT-9246]. A HUBZone means a historically underutilized business zone that is an area located within one or more qualified census tracts, qualified nonmetropolitan counties, lands within the external boundaries of an Indian reservation, qualified base closure areas, redesignated areas, governor-designated covered areas, or qualified disaster areas, as defined in 13 CFR 126.103 [@CL-MKT-9247; @CL-MKT-9248]. In RFO FAR 2.101, a HUBZone small business concern means a small business concern that meets the requirements described in 13 CFR 126.200, is certified by the Small Business Administration (SBA) and designated by SBA as a HUBZone small business concern in the Dynamic Small Business Search (DSBS) (13 CFR 126.103) [@CL-MKT-9280], and a HUBZone means a historically underutilized business zone that is an area located within one or more qualified census tracts, qualified nonmetropolitan counties, lands within the external boundaries of an Indian reservation, qualified base closure areas, redesignated areas, governor-designated covered areas, or qualified disaster areas, as defined in 13 CFR 126.103 [@CL-MKT-9281; @CL-MKT-9282].

The price evaluation preference for HUBZone small business concerns shall be used in acquisitions conducted using full and open competition, and shall not be used where price is not a selection factor so that a price evaluation preference would not be considered (e.g., Architect/Engineer acquisitions), where all fair and reasonable offers are accepted (e.g., the award of multiple award schedule contracts), or for the reserved portion of a solicitation for a multiple-award contract (see FAR 19.503) [@CL-MKT-0615; @CL-MKT-1462; @CL-MKT-1463]. The contracting officer shall give offers from HUBZone small business concerns a price evaluation preference by adding a factor of 10 percent to all offers, except offers from HUBZone small business concerns that have not waived the evaluation preference, or otherwise successful offers from small business concerns [@CL-MKT-0616; @CL-MKT-0645].

## How it works

The factor of 10 percent shall be applied on a line item basis or to any group of items on which award may be made [@CL-MKT-0617]. Other evaluation factors, such as transportation costs or rent-free use of Government property, shall be added to the offer to establish the base offer before adding the factor of 10 percent [@CL-MKT-0644].

The purpose of the HUBZone Program is to provide Federal contracting assistance for qualified small business concerns located in historically underutilized business zones, in an effort to increase employment opportunities, investment, and economic development in those areas [@CL-MKT-2209].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An acquisition is competed under full and open competition. A HUBZone small business concern that has not waived the preference offers 108,000. A large business offers 100,000. The factor of 10 percent is added to the large business's base offer, giving 110,000. The HUBZone offer is lower after the preference and is the better evaluated offer.

## Never confuse

The price evaluation preference for HUBZone small business concerns is used in acquisitions conducted using full and open competition unless one of the exclusions in FAR 19.1307(a) applies [@CL-MKT-0615; @CL-MKT-1462; @CL-MKT-1463], while a HUBZone set-aside restricts competition to HUBZone small business concerns [@CL-MKT-0612]. HUBZone status protests are on the [Protesting a firm's HUBZone status](../taxonomy/mkt-hubzone-status-protests.md) card, and the general set-aside rules are on the [Small business set-asides](mkt-small-business-set-asides.md) card. The service-disabled veteran-owned and women-owned programs have their own cards: [Service-disabled veteran-owned small business status, set-asides and sole-source awards](mkt-sdvosb-status-set-asides-and-sole-source.md) and [Women-Owned Small Business Program set-asides and sole-source awards](mkt-wosb-set-asides-and-sole-source.md).
