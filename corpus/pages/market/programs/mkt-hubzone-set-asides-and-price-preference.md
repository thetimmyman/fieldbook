## Purpose

Shows the conditions for a HUBZone set-aside and a HUBZone sole-source award, and how the HUBZone price evaluation preference is applied.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-MKT-0612] |
| Codified citation | FAR 19.1305, FAR 19.1306 and FAR 19.1307 [@CL-MKT-0612; @CL-MKT-0613; @CL-MKT-0615]; FAR 2.101 [@CL-MKT-9246; @CL-MKT-9247; @CL-MKT-9248] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-MKT-0612] |
| Applies to | Acquisitions considered for a HUBZone set-aside, a HUBZone sole-source award or the price evaluation preference [@CL-MKT-0612; @CL-MKT-0613; @CL-MKT-0615] |

To set aside an acquisition for competition restricted to HUBZone small business concerns, the contracting officer must have a reasonable expectation that offers will be received from two or more HUBZone small business concerns and that award will be made at a fair market price [@CL-MKT-0612]. A contracting officer shall consider a contract award to a HUBZone small business concern on a sole-source basis before considering a small business set-aside, provided none of the listed exclusions apply and the further listed conditions are met [@CL-MKT-0613]. One listed condition is that the anticipated price of the contract, including options, will not exceed $8.5 million for a requirement within the manufacturing NAICS codes or $5.5 million for a requirement within all other NAICS codes [@CL-MKT-0614]. The section lists further sole-source conditions that this card does not restate.

The FAR definitions section defines a HUBZone small business concern as a small business concern that meets the requirements described in 13 CFR 126.200 and is certified by SBA and designated by SBA as a HUBZone small business concern in the Dynamic Small Business Search [@CL-MKT-9246]. A HUBZone is a historically underutilized business zone that is an area located within one or more qualified census tracts, qualified nonmetropolitan counties, or lands within the external boundaries of an Indian reservation [@CL-MKT-9247], qualified base closure areas, redesignated areas, governor-designated covered areas, or qualified disaster areas, as defined in 13 CFR 126.103 [@CL-MKT-9248].

The price evaluation preference for HUBZone small business concerns shall be used in acquisitions conducted using full and open competition, and the paragraph then lists cases where it shall not be used [@CL-MKT-0615]. The contracting officer shall give offers from HUBZone small business concerns a price evaluation preference by adding a factor of 10 percent to all offers, except the listed exceptions, the first of which is offers from HUBZone small business concerns that have not waived the evaluation preference [@CL-MKT-0616]. The second listed exception is otherwise successful offers from small business concerns [@CL-MKT-0645].

## How it works

The factor of 10 percent shall be applied on a line item basis or to any group of items on which award may be made [@CL-MKT-0617]. Other evaluation factors, such as transportation costs or rent-free use of Government property, shall be added to the offer to establish the base offer before adding the factor of 10 percent [@CL-MKT-0644]. The preference does not apply in the cases the paragraph lists, so a company should check those cases before counting on it [@CL-MKT-0615]. The section also covers a tie between a HUBZone small business concern and a large business after the preference, set-asides with one or no acceptable offer, and SBA appeals, and this card does not restate them.

The purpose of the HUBZone Program is to provide Federal contracting assistance for qualified small business concerns located in historically underutilized business zones, in an effort to increase employment opportunities, investment, and economic development in those areas [@CL-MKT-2209].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An acquisition is competed under full and open competition. A HUBZone small business concern that has not waived the preference offers 108,000. A large business offers 100,000. The factor of 10 percent is added to the large business's base offer, giving 110,000. The HUBZone offer is lower after the preference and is the better evaluated offer.

## Never confuse

The price evaluation preference is not a set-aside. The preference applies in full and open competition [@CL-MKT-0615], while a set-aside restricts competition to HUBZone small business concerns [@CL-MKT-0612]. HUBZone status protests are on the [Protesting a firm's HUBZone status](../taxonomy/mkt-hubzone-status-protests.md) card, and the general set-aside rules are on the [Small business set-asides](mkt-small-business-set-asides.md) card. The service-disabled veteran-owned and women-owned programs have their own cards: [Service-disabled veteran-owned small business status, set-asides and sole-source awards](mkt-sdvosb-status-set-asides-and-sole-source.md) and [Women-Owned Small Business Program set-asides and sole-source awards](mkt-wosb-set-asides-and-sole-source.md).
