## Purpose

Shows when a partial set-aside applies to a contract other than a multiple-award contract, when it may apply to a multiple-award contract, and when a class of acquisitions may be set aside.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-MKT-0339] |
| Codified citation | FAR 19.502-3, FAR 19.502-4 and FAR 19.502-6 [@CL-MKT-0339; @CL-MKT-0340; @CL-MKT-0341; @CL-MKT-0342; @CL-MKT-0343; @CL-MKT-0365; @CL-MKT-0366; @CL-MKT-0367; @CL-MKT-0368; @CL-MKT-0369] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-MKT-0339] |
| Applies to | Partial set-asides, except for construction, and classes of acquisitions that meet the listed criteria [@CL-MKT-0339; @CL-MKT-0342; @CL-MKT-0343] |

For a contract other than a multiple-award contract, the contracting officer shall set aside a portion or portions of an acquisition, except for construction, for exclusive small business participation when the listed conditions are met [@CL-MKT-0339; @CL-MKT-0341]. Those conditions include that market research indicates a total set-aside is not appropriate and that the requirement can be divided into distinct portions [@CL-MKT-0339]. They also include that the acquisition is not subject to simplified acquisition procedures and that two or more responsible small business concerns are reasonably expected to submit offers on the set-aside portion or portions that are competitive in terms of fair market prices, quality, and delivery [@CL-MKT-0340]. The last two are that the specific program eligibility requirements identified in the part apply and that the solicitation will result in a contract other than a multiple-award contract [@CL-MKT-0341]. For a contract other than a multiple-award contract, when the contracting officer determines that a requirement is to be partially set aside, the solicitation shall identify which portion or portions are set aside and not set aside [@CL-MKT-0368]. The contracting officer shall also specify in that solicitation how offers shall be submitted with regard to the set-aside and non-set-aside portions [@CL-MKT-0369].

For a multiple-award contract, contracting officers may, at their discretion, set aside a portion or portions, except for construction, for any of the small business concerns identified in the cross-reference when listed conditions are met, the first being that market research indicates a total set-aside is not appropriate [@CL-MKT-0342]. Two further listed conditions are that the requirement can be divided into distinct portions and that the acquisition is not subject to simplified acquisition procedures [@CL-MKT-0365]. Another is that two or more responsible small business concerns are reasonably expected to submit an offer on the set-aside portion or portions that are competitive in terms of fair market prices, quality, and delivery [@CL-MKT-0366]. The last is that the specific program eligibility requirements identified in the part apply [@CL-MKT-0367]. A class of acquisitions of selected products or services, or a portion of the acquisitions, may be set aside for exclusive participation by small business concerns if individual acquisitions in the class will meet the criteria in 19.502-1, 19.502-2, or 19.502-3(a) [@CL-MKT-0343].

## How it works

The two partial set-aside paragraphs differ in force and in scope: the first says shall and applies to a contract other than a multiple-award contract [@CL-MKT-0339; @CL-MKT-0341], and the second says may, at the contracting officer's discretion, and applies to a multiple-award contract [@CL-MKT-0342]. Both exclude construction [@CL-MKT-0339; @CL-MKT-0342]. A class set-aside is a separate tool for a class of acquisitions rather than one acquisition [@CL-MKT-0343]. The multiple-award section has its own paragraphs on the solicitation and on offers, and both sections deal with offers from concerns that do not qualify as small, and this card does not restate those paragraphs. Questions of size go through the [Protesting a small business representation or rerepresentation](../taxonomy/mkt-small-business-size-protests.md) card.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A requirement is not subject to simplified acquisition procedures and can be divided into two distinct portions, A and B. Market research shows a total set-aside is not appropriate, but two or more responsible small business concerns are expected to offer competitively on portion A. The program eligibility requirements apply. The buy is a single-award contract and is not construction. Answer: the contracting officer shall set aside a portion. If the same facts arose on a multiple-award contract, the contracting officer may do so at discretion. If the work were construction, neither partial set-aside paragraph would apply.

## Never confuse

A partial set-aside is not a total set-aside. The tests for total set-asides, above and below the simplified acquisition threshold, are on the [Small business set-asides](mkt-small-business-set-asides.md) card. A partial set-aside reserves a portion of one acquisition, and a class set-aside covers a class of acquisitions [@CL-MKT-0343].
