## Purpose

Shows the policy the clause states, the contractor's duty to carry it out when it awards subcontracts, and when the contractor may rely on a subcontractor's representation of its size and status.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-MKT-0817] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.219-8 [@CL-MKT-0815; @CL-MKT-0817] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.219-8 [@CL-MKT-1239; @CL-MKT-1243] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-MKT-0817] |
| Applies to | Contractors that agree to the clause in their contracts [@CL-MKT-0817] |

The clause states a policy of the United States that names six categories of concern: small business, veteran-owned small business, service-disabled veteran-owned small business, HUBZone small business, small disadvantaged business and women-owned small business [@CL-MKT-0814]. Those concerns shall have the maximum practicable opportunity to participate in performing contracts let by any Federal agency, including contracts and subcontracts for subsystems, assemblies, components, and related services for major systems [@CL-MKT-0815]. It is further the policy that prime contractors establish procedures to ensure the timely payment of amounts due under their subcontracts with small business, veteran-owned small business, service-disabled veteran-owned small business, HUBZone small business, small disadvantaged business, and women-owned small business concerns [@CL-MKT-0816; @CL-MKT-0975].

The contractor agrees to carry out the policy in the awarding of subcontracts to the fullest extent consistent with efficient contract performance [@CL-MKT-0817].

## How it works

The duty is the contractor's agreement, limited by efficient contract performance [@CL-MKT-0817]. The printed clause sets no dollar or percentage target, and the subcontracting plan clause, on the [scope and required elements](mkt-subcontracting-plan-clause-scope-and-required-elements.md) card, is where goals sit. On representations, unless the contractor has reason to question the representation, it may accept a subcontractor's written representations of its size and socioeconomic status as a small business, small disadvantaged business, veteran-owned small business, service-disabled veteran-owned small business, or women-owned small business [@CL-MKT-0818; @CL-MKT-0976]. That route applies if the subcontractor represents that the representations with its offer are current, accurate, and complete as of the date of the offer for the subcontract [@CL-MKT-0819]. A contractor acting in good faith is not liable for misrepresentations made by its subcontractors regarding size or socioeconomic status [@CL-MKT-0820]. The clause also has a separate route for representations made in the System for Award Management, a limit on requiring that system, and a HUBZone confirmation step, which this card does not restate.

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text under Class Deviation 2026-O0038, effective February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. The RFO text of the clause keeps the policy that the six categories of concern shall have the maximum practicable opportunity to participate in performing contracts let by any Federal agency, using the abbreviations VOSB, SDVOSB, SDB and WOSB for four of them [@CL-MKT-1239] [@CL-MKT-1240] [@CL-MKT-0814] [@CL-MKT-0815], the policy that prime contractors establish procedures to ensure timely payment of amounts due under their subcontracts with those concerns [@CL-MKT-1241] [@CL-MKT-1242] [@CL-MKT-0816] [@CL-MKT-0975], and the contractor's agreement to carry out the policy in the awarding of subcontracts to the fullest extent consistent with efficient contract performance [@CL-MKT-1243] [@CL-MKT-0817]. The RFO text says that, unless the Contractor has reason to question the representation, it may accept a subcontractor's written representations of size and socioeconomic status as a small business or small disadvantaged business (SDB) [@CL-MKT-1244], where the codified clause also lists veteran-owned, service-disabled veteran-owned and women-owned small business [@CL-MKT-0976] [@CL-MKT-0818]. It keeps the condition that the subcontractor represents that the representations with its offer are current, accurate, and complete as of the date of the offer for the subcontract [@CL-MKT-1247] [@CL-MKT-0819]. For the separate route through the System for Award Management, the RFO text again lists only small business or SDB [@CL-MKT-1248], where the codified paragraph lists the same five categories as the written-representation route [@CL-MKT-9106]. Both texts condition that route on the subcontractor being registered in SAM and representing that the representations made in SAM are current, accurate and complete as of the date of the offer for the subcontract [@CL-MKT-1249] [@CL-MKT-9107]. On the limit on requiring that system, the codified clause says the Contractor may not require the use of SAM for the purposes of representing size or socioeconomic status in connection with a subcontract [@CL-MKT-9108], while the RFO text says the Contractor may not require that the subcontractor register in SAM for those purposes [@CL-MKT-1250]. The codified paragraph on good-faith liability opens with the words In accordance with 13 CFR 121.411, 126.900, 127.700, and 128.600 [@CL-MKT-9109], and the RFO paragraph does not print those opening words [@CL-MKT-1251]. Both say a contractor acting in good faith is not liable for misrepresentations made by its subcontractors regarding the subcontractor's size or socioeconomic status [@CL-MKT-1251] [@CL-MKT-0820]. On confirmation, the codified clause requires the Contractor to confirm that a subcontractor representing itself as a HUBZone small business concern is certified by SBA as a HUBZone small business concern by accessing SAM or DSBS [@CL-MKT-9110], and it has a further sentence for a subcontractor that is a joint venture and one on confirming by accessing SAM [@CL-MKT-9111]. The RFO text requires the Contractor to confirm that a subcontractor representing itself as a HUBZone small business concern, VOSB, SDVOSB, or WOSB concern is certified by SBA by checking SAM or SBS [@CL-MKT-1252]. In paragraph (a), the RFO text defines a women-owned small business (WOSB) concern with a third condition that it is certified by SBA or an approved third-party certifier in accordance with 13 CFR 127.300 and is designated by SBA as a WOSB concern in SBS and SAM [@CL-MKT-9113], where the codified definition states ownership and control conditions [@CL-MKT-9112]. It defines a service-disabled veteran-owned small business (SDVOSB) concern as an SDVOSB concern that meets the requirements described in 13 CFR 128.300, is certified by SBA, and is designated by SBA as an SDVOSB concern in SBS and SAM [@CL-MKT-9114].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A prime contractor awards a subcontract to a supplier that gives a written representation, with its offer, that it is small and that the representation is current, accurate and complete. The prime has no reason to question it. Answer: the prime may accept the representation. If the supplier's statement later proves wrong and the prime acted in good faith, the prime is not liable for it.

## Never confuse

The clause states the policy and the contractor's duty, with no goals. Goals and plan content sit in the subcontracting plan clause; see [Subcontracting plan content, good faith effort and liquidated damages](mkt-subcontracting-plan-content-and-compliance.md). The subcontracting plan clause makes a failure to comply in good faith with this clause a material breach of the contract, as the [master and commercial plan](mkt-subcontracting-plan-clause-master-commercial-reporting-flowdown.md) card shows [@CL-MKT-0871]. The policy for Government acquisitions is on the [Small business general policy](mkt-small-business-general-policy.md) card. Under the codified clause the written representations a contractor may accept include veteran-owned, service-disabled veteran-owned and women-owned small business status [@CL-MKT-0976], while the RFO text of that route lists only small business or small disadvantaged business [@CL-MKT-1244]. Accepting a representation protects a contractor that acts in good faith; it does not decide whether the subcontractor is small. The penalties for misrepresenting size are on the [Size misrepresentation penalties](../taxonomy/mkt-sba-size-misrepresentation-penalties-and-subcontract-size.md) card.
