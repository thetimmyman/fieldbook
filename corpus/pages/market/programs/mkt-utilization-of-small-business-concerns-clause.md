## Purpose

Shows the policy the clause states, the contractor's duty to carry it out when it awards subcontracts, and when the contractor may rely on a subcontractor's representation of its size and status.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-MKT-0817] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.219-8 [@CL-MKT-0815; @CL-MKT-0817] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-MKT-0817] |
| Applies to | Contractors that agree to the clause in their contracts [@CL-MKT-0817] |

The clause states a policy of the United States that names six categories of concern: small business, veteran-owned small business, service-disabled veteran-owned small business, HUBZone small business, small disadvantaged business and women-owned small business [@CL-MKT-0814]. Those concerns shall have the maximum practicable opportunity to participate in performing contracts let by any Federal agency, including contracts and subcontracts for subsystems, assemblies, components, and related services for major systems [@CL-MKT-0815]. It is further the policy that prime contractors establish procedures to ensure the timely payment of amounts due under their subcontracts with small business concerns [@CL-MKT-0816].

The contractor agrees to carry out the policy in the awarding of subcontracts to the fullest extent consistent with efficient contract performance [@CL-MKT-0817].

## How it works

The duty is the contractor's agreement, limited by efficient contract performance [@CL-MKT-0817]. The printed clause sets no dollar or percentage target, and the subcontracting plan clause, on the [scope and required elements](mkt-subcontracting-plan-clause-scope-and-required-elements.md) card, is where goals sit. On representations, unless the contractor has reason to question the representation, it may accept a subcontractor's written representation of its size and socioeconomic status as a small business [@CL-MKT-0818]. That route applies if the subcontractor represents that the representations with its offer are current, accurate, and complete as of the date of the offer for the subcontract [@CL-MKT-0819]. A contractor acting in good faith is not liable for misrepresentations made by its subcontractors regarding size or socioeconomic status [@CL-MKT-0820]. The clause also has a separate route for representations made in the System for Award Management, a limit on requiring that system, and a HUBZone confirmation step, which this card does not restate.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A prime contractor awards a subcontract to a supplier that gives a written representation, with its offer, that it is small and that the representation is current, accurate and complete. The prime has no reason to question it. Answer: the prime may accept the representation. If the supplier's statement later proves wrong and the prime acted in good faith, the prime is not liable for it.

## Never confuse

The clause states the policy and the contractor's duty, with no goals. Goals and plan content sit in the subcontracting plan clause; see [Subcontracting plan content, good faith effort and liquidated damages](mkt-subcontracting-plan-content-and-compliance.md). The subcontracting plan clause makes a failure to comply in good faith with this clause a material breach of the contract, as the [master and commercial plan](mkt-subcontracting-plan-clause-master-commercial-reporting-flowdown.md) card shows [@CL-MKT-0871]. The policy for Government acquisitions is on the [Small business general policy](mkt-small-business-general-policy.md) card. Accepting a representation protects a contractor that acts in good faith; it does not decide whether the subcontractor is small. The penalties for misrepresenting size are on the [Size misrepresentation penalties](../taxonomy/mkt-sba-size-misrepresentation-penalties-and-subcontract-size.md) card.
