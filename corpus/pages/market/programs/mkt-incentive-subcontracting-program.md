## Purpose

Shows the incentive a contractor can receive for exceeding its subcontracting plan goals, and when the clause says the contractor does not receive it.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-MKT-1110] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.219-10 [@CL-MKT-1110] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-MKT-1110] |
| Applies to | Contracts with the clause, where the contractor has committed to goals in its subcontracting plan [@CL-MKT-1175] |

Of the total dollars it plans to spend under subcontracts, the contractor has committed itself in its subcontracting plan to try to award certain percentages [@CL-MKT-1175]. The percentages are for small business, veteran-owned small business, service-disabled veteran-owned small business, HUBZone small business, small disadvantaged business, and women-owned small business concerns [@CL-MKT-1110]. The incentive depends on the contractor exceeding its subcontracting goals for those categories in performing the contract [@CL-MKT-1176]. The clause text states that the contractor will receive a percent of the dollars in excess of each goal in the plan [@CL-MKT-1111]. The contracting officer inserts that percent as a number between 0 and 10, so it is a contract-specific fill-in [@CL-MKT-1111].

The contractor does not receive that percent if the contracting officer determines that the excess was not due to the contractor's efforts [@CL-MKT-1112]. The clause gives two examples of such an excess: a subcontractor cost overrun that caused the actual subcontract amount to exceed the estimate in the plan, and the award of subcontracts that had been planned but not disclosed in the plan during contract negotiations [@CL-MKT-1113]. Determinations under paragraph (b) are unilateral decisions made solely at the discretion of the Government [@CL-MKT-1114]. If the contract is cost-plus-fixed-fee, the sum of the fixed fee and the incentive fee earned under the contract may not exceed the limitations in FAR 15.404-4 [@CL-MKT-1115].

## How it works

The inserted percent applies to the dollars in excess of each goal in the plan [@CL-MKT-1111]. The text names the six categories in one condition and then speaks of each goal, and it does not say in terms whether each goal is tested alone or whether the condition needs all six goals to be exceeded, so confirm the reading with the contracting officer where only some goals are exceeded [@CL-MKT-1176; @CL-MKT-1111]. The contracting officer can deny the percent on an excess by determining that the excess was not due to the contractor's efforts, and that determination is the Government's alone to make [@CL-MKT-1112; @CL-MKT-1114]. The clause text does not itself provide a route to challenge that determination. A separate paragraph limits the fixed fee plus the incentive fee earned on a cost-plus-fixed-fee contract [@CL-MKT-1115]. The clause text does not say when or how the amount is paid.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor plans to spend 1,000,000 under subcontracts and its plan sets a small business goal of 10 percent, which is 100,000. The contract's fill-in is 5 percent. The contractor awards 120,000 to small business concerns and also exceeds each of its other five goals, so the condition is met however the text is read [@CL-MKT-1176]. The example follows only the small business goal. The dollars in excess of the small business goal are 20,000, and 5 percent of 20,000 is 1,000 [@CL-MKT-1111]. Now suppose the 20,000 above the goal arose because a small business subcontract cost more than the plan estimated. If the contracting officer determines that the excess was not due to the contractor's efforts, the contractor does not receive the 1,000 [@CL-MKT-1112; @CL-MKT-1113].

## Never confuse

The incentive is not a penalty for missing a goal: the clause text provides the percent only on dollars in excess of a goal [@CL-MKT-1111]. It is also not the subcontracting plan: the plan holds the commitments, and the clause supplies the incentive for exceeding the goals [@CL-MKT-1175; @CL-MKT-1111]. Plan content and the remedies for failing to meet a plan are on the [Subcontracting plan content, good faith effort and liquidated damages](mkt-subcontracting-plan-content-and-compliance.md) card.

## Company calibration

Whether the purchasing system has been reviewed, and how plan goals and the dollars above each goal are tracked against it. [@Q-040]
