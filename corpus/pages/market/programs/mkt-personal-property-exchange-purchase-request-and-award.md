## Purpose

Explains what must support a purchase request that offers property in exchange, how the solicitation asks for prices, and how the award handles a trade-in allowance.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-MKT-1560] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 217.7003 and DFARS 217.7004 [@CL-MKT-1560; @CL-MKT-1564] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-MKT-1560] |
| Applies to | Purchase requests, solicitations and awards that involve an exchange of personal property [@CL-MKT-1560; @CL-MKT-1564] |

In support of the purchase request, the requiring activity must provide all of the listed items [@CL-MKT-1560]. The first is a certification that the property is eligible for exchange and complies with all conditions and limitations of DoD Manual 4140.01, Volume 9, DoD Supply Chain Materiel Management Procedures: Materiel Programs [@CL-MKT-1560; @CL-MKT-1712]. The second is a written determination of economic advantage that indicates the anticipated economic advantage to the Government from use of the exchange authority [@CL-MKT-1561]. That determination must also indicate that exchange allowances shall be applied toward, or in partial payment of, the items to be acquired, and that, if required, the exchange property has been rendered safe or innocuous or demilitarized [@CL-MKT-1562]. The third and fourth items are all applicable approvals for the exchange and a description of the property available for exchange, such as its nomenclature, location, serial number and estimated travel value [@CL-MKT-1563].

Solicitations shall include a request for offerors to state prices for the new items without any exchange and for the new items with the exchange (trade-in allowance) for the exchange property listed [@CL-MKT-1564]. The contracting officer is not obligated to award on an exchange basis. If the lowest evaluated offer is for the new items without any exchange, the contracting officer may award on that basis and forgo the exchange [@CL-MKT-1565]. Exchanges may be made only with the successful offeror, and when the successful offer includes an exchange, one contract is awarded for both the acquisition of the new property and the trade-in of the exchange property [@CL-MKT-1566]. The only exception to awarding one contract is when the items must be acquired against a mandatory Federal supply schedule contract, in which case a separate contract is awarded for the exchange [@CL-MKT-1623].

## How it works

The requiring activity provides the four items supporting the purchase request [@CL-MKT-1560; @CL-MKT-1561; @CL-MKT-1562; @CL-MKT-1563], and the solicitation requests offerors to state prices for the new items both without any exchange and with the exchange (trade-in allowance) [@CL-MKT-1564].

If the lowest evaluated offer is for the new items without any exchange, the contracting officer may award on that basis and forgo the exchange [@CL-MKT-1565]. When the successful offer includes an exchange, one contract is awarded for both the acquisition of the new property and the trade-in of the exchange property, unless the items must be acquired against a mandatory Federal supply schedule contract, in which case a separate contract is awarded for the exchange [@CL-MKT-1566; @CL-MKT-1623].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A requiring activity offers 3 replaceable vehicles against 12 new ones. The purchase request carries the eligibility certification, the economic advantage determination, the applicable approvals and a description of each vehicle [@CL-MKT-1560; @CL-MKT-1561; @CL-MKT-1562; @CL-MKT-1563]. The solicitation asks offerors to price the 12 new vehicles without the exchange and with the trade-in allowance [@CL-MKT-1564]. If the lowest evaluated offer is for the new vehicles without any exchange, the contracting officer may award on that basis and forgo the exchange [@CL-MKT-1565]. If instead the successful offer includes the trade-in, one contract covers both the new vehicles and the trade-in [@CL-MKT-1566]. If the vehicles had to be bought against a mandatory Federal supply schedule contract, a separate contract would cover the exchange [@CL-MKT-1623].

## Never confuse

An exchange allowance is normally part of one award with the new items, but a mandatory Federal supply schedule buy needs a separate contract for the exchange [@CL-MKT-1566; @CL-MKT-1623]. Offering a trade-in does not oblige the Government to award on an exchange basis [@CL-MKT-1565]. The eligibility certification and the economic advantage determination are different items, and the purchase request needs all of the listed items [@CL-MKT-1560; @CL-MKT-1561].
