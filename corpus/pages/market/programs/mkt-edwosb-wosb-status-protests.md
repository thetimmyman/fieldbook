## Purpose

Shows who may protest a firm's status as an economically disadvantaged women-owned small business (EDWOSB) or a women-owned small business (WOSB) eligible under the Women-Owned Small Business Program, the time limits, and what SBA decides.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-MKT-0589] |
| Codified citation | FAR 19.308 [@CL-MKT-0589]; FAR 2.101 [@CL-MKT-9232; @CL-MKT-9233] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-MKT-0589] |
| Applies to | Protests of an offeror's EDWOSB or WOSB status [@CL-MKT-0589; @CL-MKT-0590] |

For sole source acquisitions, the contracting officer or SBA may protest the offeror's status as an economically disadvantaged women-owned small business concern or as a women-owned small business concern eligible under the Women-Owned Small Business Program [@CL-MKT-0589]. For all other acquisitions, an interested party may protest the apparent successful offeror's status [@CL-MKT-0590]. An interested party seeking to protest both the small business size and the WOSB or EDWOSB status of an apparent successful offeror shall file two separate protests [@CL-MKT-0654].

The FAR definitions section describes a women-owned small business concern eligible under the WOSB Program as a small business concern that is at least 51 percent directly and unconditionally owned by, and whose management and daily business operations are controlled by, one or more women who are citizens of the United States [@CL-MKT-9232], and that is certified by SBA or an approved third-party certifier in accordance with 13 CFR 127.300 [@CL-MKT-9233].

An offeror shall submit its protest to the contracting officer in accordance with one of three deadline alternatives: to be received by the close of business by the fifth business day after bid opening for sealed bid acquisitions [@CL-MKT-0591]; to be received by the close of business by the fifth business day after receipt of the special notification from the contracting officer (see FAR 15.503(a)(2)) that identifies the apparently successful offeror for negotiated acquisitions, including orders placed under multiple-award contracts where the contracting officer requested rerepresentation for the order (see 13 CFR 127.603(c)(1)) and orders set aside for EDWOSB or WOSB concerns under multiple-award contracts that are not partially or totally set aside or reserved for EDWOSB or WOSB concerns (see 13 CFR 127.603(c)(1)), except for orders and blanket purchase agreements placed under a Federal Supply Schedule contract (see FAR 8.405 and 19.302(d)(5)) [@CL-MKT-0591; @CL-MKT-0592; @CL-MKT-1453; @CL-MKT-1454; @CL-MKT-1455]; or to be received by the close of business on the fifth business day after receipt of notification using other communication means when written notification is not required [@CL-MKT-0591; @CL-MKT-1455; @CL-MKT-1456]. Any protest received after the designated time limit is untimely, unless it is from the contracting officer or SBA [@CL-MKT-0593].

## How it works

SBA determines the merits of the status protest within 15 business days after receipt of a protest, or within any extension of that time granted by the contracting officer [@CL-MKT-0594]. SBA notifies the contracting officer, the protester and the protested concern of its determination, and the determination is effective immediately and is final unless overturned on appeal by OHA pursuant to 13 CFR part 134 [@CL-MKT-0595]. OHA must receive the appeal no later than 10 business days after the date of receipt of the protest determination, and SBA will dismiss an untimely appeal [@CL-MKT-0596].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A women-owned set-aside draws a status protest, and the contracting officer withholds award. SBA sustains the protest and the protested concern receives the determination on a Monday. OHA receives the concern's appeal 9 business days later. The appeal is timely because it arrived inside the 10 business days. Without an appeal, the contracting officer may not award to the protested concern.

## Never confuse

Under FAR 19.308, SBA determines the merits of a WOSB or EDWOSB status protest [@CL-MKT-0594], and its determination is final unless overturned on appeal by OHA [@CL-MKT-0595], while under FAR 19.307 for protests of an offeror's status as a service-disabled veteran-owned small business concern, OHA will determine the merits of the status protest [@CL-MKT-0580; @CL-MKT-0587]. The service-disabled route is on the [Protesting a firm's service-disabled veteran-owned small business status](mkt-sdvosb-status-protests.md) card. An interested party seeking to protest both the small business size and the WOSB or EDWOSB status of an apparent successful offeror shall file two separate protests [@CL-MKT-0654]. The size route is on the [Protesting a small business representation](../taxonomy/mkt-small-business-size-protests.md) card. The conditions for a set-aside or sole-source award under the program are on the [Women-Owned Small Business Program set-asides and sole-source awards](mkt-wosb-set-asides-and-sole-source.md) card.
