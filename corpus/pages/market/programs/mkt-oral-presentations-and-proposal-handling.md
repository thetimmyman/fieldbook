## Purpose

Explains how oral presentations fit into a negotiated acquisition, and how the Government receives, marks, protects and repairs the written parts of a proposal.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-MKT-1315] |
| Codified citation | FAR 15.102 and FAR 15.207 [@CL-MKT-1315] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 15.105-1 [@CL-MKT-2249; @CL-MKT-2250; @CL-MKT-2251; @CL-MKT-2252], RFO FAR 15.202(a)(3) [@CL-MKT-2253; @CL-MKT-2254; @CL-MKT-2255; @CL-MKT-2256] and RFO FAR 15.108 [@CL-MKT-2257; @CL-MKT-2258; @CL-MKT-2259; @CL-MKT-2260; @CL-MKT-2261] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-MKT-1315] |
| Applies to | Solicitations that call for oral presentations, and proposals and request-for-information responses received under a solicitation [@CL-MKT-1315] |

Oral presentations by offerors, as requested by the Government, may replace written information or add to it [@CL-MKT-1315]. The solicitation may require each offeror to submit part of its proposal through oral presentations [@CL-MKT-1467]. Even then, representations and certifications must be submitted as required in the FAR provisions at 52.204-8(d) or 52.212-3(b), and a signed offer sheet, including any exceptions to the Government's terms and conditions, must be submitted in writing [@CL-MKT-1316]. An oral presentation may occur at any time in the acquisition process and is subject to the same restrictions as written information on timing and content [@CL-MKT-1465]. A pre-recorded videotaped presentation without real-time interactive dialogue is not an oral presentation under this section, although it may be included in an offeror's submission when appropriate [@CL-MKT-1466].

When oral presentations are required, the solicitation must give offerors enough information to prepare [@CL-MKT-1470]. Accordingly, the solicitation may describe the types of information to be presented orally with the evaluation factors attached to them, and the qualifications required of the presenting personnel [@CL-MKT-9340; @CL-MKT-1317]. It may also describe the requirements, limits and prohibitions on written material or other media used to supplement the presentations, and the location, date and time [@CL-MKT-1471]. It may describe the time allowed for each presentation [@CL-MKT-1472], and the scope and content of exchanges between Government participants and the offeror's representatives, including whether discussions will be permitted [@CL-MKT-1473].

Information that the parties intend to make a material term or condition of the contract must be put in writing, and oral statements cannot be incorporated by reference [@CL-MKT-1319]. If the Government conducts discussions during an oral presentation, it must comply with the discussion and proposal revision rules in FAR 15.306 and 15.307 [@CL-MKT-1320].

## How it works

In deciding what to take orally, the Government considers its ability to adequately evaluate the information and the need to incorporate it into the contract [@CL-MKT-1468], as well as the effect on the efficiency of the acquisition and on small businesses, including cost [@CL-MKT-1469]. The contracting officer keeps a record of oral presentations that documents what the Government relied on in the source selection decision [@CL-MKT-1318]. The source selection authority decides the method and level of detail of that record [@CL-MKT-1474]. A copy of the record placed in the file may be provided to the offeror [@CL-MKT-1475].

Proposals and information received in response to a request for information are marked with the date and time of receipt at the location the solicitation specifies, then passed to the designated officials [@CL-MKT-1321]. Proposals must be safeguarded from unauthorized disclosure throughout the source selection process [@CL-MKT-1322]. Information received in response to a request for information must also be safeguarded adequately [@CL-MKT-1476].

If any portion of a proposal received electronically or by fax is unreadable, the contracting officer must immediately notify the offeror and permit resubmission of that portion [@CL-MKT-1323]. The contracting officer sets the method and time for resubmission after consulting the offeror, and documents them in the file [@CL-MKT-1477]. The resubmitted portion counts as received at the date and time of the original submission for timeliness, provided the offeror meets the time and format requirements the contracting officer set [@CL-MKT-1324].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 15 text in place of the codified text under Class Deviation 2026-O0048, whose Revision 1 took effect immediately and supersedes the March 5, 2026 version [@CL-EST-3176] [@CL-EST-3177]. RFO FAR 15.105-1(a) says oral presentations may be used as a substitute for requesting portions of written proposal information from offerors, where the codified paragraph says they may substitute for, or augment, written information [@CL-MKT-2249; @CL-MKT-1315]. RFO FAR 15.105-1(b) says oral presentations may occur at any time during the source selection process, where the codified paragraph says in the acquisition process and adds the same restrictions as written information [@CL-MKT-2250; @CL-MKT-1465], and says that when requesting oral presentations the RFP must provide offerors with the necessary information to prepare and provide the presentation, where the codified paragraph says the solicitation shall provide sufficient information when oral presentations are required [@CL-MKT-2251; @CL-MKT-1470]. RFO FAR 15.105-1(a) also states that information on an offeror's capability, past performance, work plans or approaches, staffing resources, transition plans, or sample tasks can be suitable for oral presentations [@CL-MKT-2249; @CL-MKT-2252]. RFO FAR 15.202(a)(3) states that contracting officers must maintain a record of oral presentations to document what the Government relied upon in making the source selection decision [@CL-MKT-2253; @CL-MKT-1318], that the method and level of detail of the record is at the discretion of the SSA [@CL-MKT-2254; @CL-MKT-1474], and that when an oral presentation includes information that the parties intend to include in the contract as material terms or conditions, the information must be put in writing and incorporated in the proposal and any resultant contract [@CL-MKT-2255; @CL-MKT-2256; @CL-MKT-1319]. RFO FAR 15.108(a) states that when there is no electronic record, contracting officers must mark proposals with the date and time of receipt and provide them to the designated officials, whereas FAR 15.207(a) covers proposals and information received in response to a request for information, marked upon receipt at the location specified in the solicitation [@CL-MKT-2257; @CL-MKT-1321]. RFO FAR 15.108(b) states that proposals should be safeguarded from unauthorized disclosure throughout the source selection process in accordance with part 3, whereas FAR 15.207(b) states that proposals shall be safeguarded in accordance with 3.104 and that information received in response to a request for information shall also be safeguarded adequately from unauthorized disclosure [@CL-MKT-2258; @CL-MKT-1322; @CL-MKT-9341; @CL-MKT-1476]. RFO FAR 15.108(c) says that if any portion of a proposal submitted electronically is unreadable, contracting officers must immediately notify the offeror and permit the offeror to resubmit the unreadable portion [@CL-MKT-2259; @CL-MKT-1323], must establish the method and time for resubmission after consultation with the offeror and document the file [@CL-MKT-2260; @CL-MKT-1477], and treats the resubmission as received at the date and time of the original unreadable submission for timeliness under 15.107, where the codified paragraph refers to 15.208(a), provided the offeror complies with the time and format requirements established by the contracting officer [@CL-MKT-2261; @CL-MKT-1324].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A solicitation requires each offeror to present its staffing approach orally, and the offeror does so [@CL-MKT-1467]. Its offer sheet, with one exception to the Government's delivery terms, and its representations and certifications are submitted in writing as required [@CL-MKT-1316]. The source selection authority has chosen audio recording as the method for the record, and the contracting officer keeps that record [@CL-MKT-1318; @CL-MKT-1474].

The offeror faxes its cost volume, and the last three pages arrive blank. The contracting officer notifies the offeror at once and, after consulting the offeror, sets resubmission of those pages in the same format by the end of the next day, and records this in the file [@CL-MKT-1323; @CL-MKT-1477]. The pages arrive in that format before the set time, so they count as received at the date and time of the original fax [@CL-MKT-1324]. Pages sent after the set time would not get that treatment [@CL-MKT-1324].

## Never confuse

Under FAR 15.102(a), when an offeror is required to submit part of its proposal through oral presentations, representations and certifications must still be submitted as required in the FAR provisions at 52.204-8(d) or 52.212-3(b), and a signed offer sheet, including any exceptions to the Government's terms and conditions, must be submitted in writing [@CL-MKT-1316]. Under FAR 15.102(f), information intended to be included in the contract as material terms or conditions must be put in writing, and incorporation by reference of oral statements is not permitted [@CL-MKT-1319]. Under FAR 15.207(c), a resubmitted unreadable portion of a proposal that complies with the time and format requirements established by the contracting officer is considered received at the date and time of the original unreadable submission for timeliness [@CL-MKT-1324]. For the general timing rules on late proposals see [Submitting, modifying, withdrawing and late proposals](../../estimating/pricing/est-proposal-submission-and-late-proposals.md). The codified text says proposals shall be safeguarded and that oral presentations may augment written information [@CL-MKT-1322; @CL-MKT-1315], while the RFO text says proposals should be safeguarded and that oral presentations may be used as a substitute for requesting portions of written proposal information [@CL-MKT-2258; @CL-MKT-2249].
