## Purpose

Explains how oral presentations fit into a negotiated acquisition, and how the Government receives, marks, protects and repairs the written parts of a proposal.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-MKT-1315] |
| Codified citation | FAR 15.102 and FAR 15.207 [@CL-MKT-1315] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-MKT-1315] |
| Applies to | Solicitations that call for oral presentations, and proposals and request-for-information responses received under a solicitation [@CL-MKT-1315] |

Oral presentations by offerors, as requested by the Government, may replace written information or add to it [@CL-MKT-1315]. The solicitation may require each offeror to submit part of its proposal through oral presentations [@CL-MKT-1467]. Even then, representations and certifications must be submitted as the FAR provisions require, and a signed offer sheet, including any exceptions to the Government's terms and conditions, must be submitted in writing [@CL-MKT-1316]. An oral presentation may occur at any time in the acquisition process and is subject to the same restrictions as written information on timing and content [@CL-MKT-1465]. A pre-recorded videotaped presentation without real-time interactive dialogue is not an oral presentation under this section, although it may be included in an offeror's submission when appropriate [@CL-MKT-1466].

When oral presentations are required, the solicitation must give offerors enough information to prepare [@CL-MKT-1470]. It may describe the types of information to be presented orally with the evaluation factors attached to them, and the qualifications required of the presenting personnel [@CL-MKT-1317]. It may also describe the requirements, limits and prohibitions on written material or other media used to supplement the presentations, and the location, date and time [@CL-MKT-1471]. It may describe the time allowed for each presentation [@CL-MKT-1472], and the scope and content of exchanges between Government participants and the offeror's representatives, including whether discussions will be permitted [@CL-MKT-1473].

Information that the parties intend to make a material term or condition of the contract must be put in writing, and oral statements cannot be incorporated by reference [@CL-MKT-1319]. If the Government conducts discussions during an oral presentation, it must comply with the discussion and proposal revision rules in FAR 15.306 and 15.307 [@CL-MKT-1320].

## How it works

In deciding what to take orally, the Government considers its ability to evaluate the information and the need to incorporate it into the contract [@CL-MKT-1468], as well as the effect on the efficiency of the acquisition and on small businesses, including cost [@CL-MKT-1469]. The contracting officer keeps a record of oral presentations that documents what the Government relied on in the source selection decision [@CL-MKT-1318]. The source selection authority decides the method and level of detail of that record [@CL-MKT-1474]. A copy of the record placed in the file may be provided to the offeror [@CL-MKT-1475].

Proposals and information received in response to a request for information are marked with the date and time of receipt at the location the solicitation specifies, then passed to the designated officials [@CL-MKT-1321]. Proposals must be safeguarded from unauthorized disclosure throughout the source selection process [@CL-MKT-1322]. Information received in response to a request for information must also be safeguarded adequately [@CL-MKT-1476].

If any portion of a proposal received electronically or by fax is unreadable, the contracting officer must immediately notify the offeror and permit resubmission of that portion [@CL-MKT-1323]. The contracting officer sets the method and time for resubmission after consulting the offeror, and documents them in the file [@CL-MKT-1477]. The resubmitted portion counts as received at the date and time of the original submission for timeliness only if the offeror meets the time and format requirements the contracting officer set [@CL-MKT-1324].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A solicitation requires each offeror to present its staffing approach orally, and the offeror does so [@CL-MKT-1467]. Its offer sheet, with one exception to the Government's delivery terms, and its representations and certifications are submitted in writing as required [@CL-MKT-1316]. The source selection authority has chosen audio recording as the method for the record, and the contracting officer keeps that record [@CL-MKT-1318; @CL-MKT-1474].

The offeror faxes its cost volume, and the last three pages arrive blank. The contracting officer notifies the offeror at once and, after consulting the offeror, sets resubmission of those pages in the same format by the end of the next day, and records this in the file [@CL-MKT-1323; @CL-MKT-1477]. The pages arrive in that format before the set time, so they count as received at the date and time of the original fax [@CL-MKT-1324]. Pages sent after the set time would not get that treatment [@CL-MKT-1324].

## Never confuse

An oral presentation is not an oral proposal: representations and certifications must still be submitted as the FAR provisions require, and the signed offer sheet, with its exceptions, must be in writing [@CL-MKT-1316]. Oral statements are not contract terms: material terms must be put in writing, and incorporation by reference of oral statements is not permitted [@CL-MKT-1319]. An unreadable fax is not a late one: a resubmission that meets the time and format requirements keeps the original receipt time [@CL-MKT-1324]. For the general timing rules on late proposals see [Submitting, modifying, withdrawing and late proposals](../../estimating/pricing/est-proposal-submission-and-late-proposals.md).
