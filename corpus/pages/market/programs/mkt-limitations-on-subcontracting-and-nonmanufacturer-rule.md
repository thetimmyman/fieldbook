## Purpose

Shows how much of the Government's payment a small business may pass to subcontractors that are not similarly situated entities, when that is checked, and what a nonmanufacturer must meet.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-MKT-0226] |
| Codified citation | FAR 19.505 [@CL-MKT-0226; @CL-MKT-0227] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-MKT-0226] |
| Applies to | Small business concerns subject to the limitations on subcontracting, including those on small business set-asides above the simplified acquisition threshold [@CL-MKT-0226; @CL-MKT-0277] |

A small business concern subject to the limitations on subcontracting is required to comply with the limits listed in the paragraph [@CL-MKT-0226]. The limits depend on the NAICS category. One listed limit is 50 percent for a contract or order assigned a NAICS code for services (except construction) [@CL-MKT-0227]. A second limit applies to a contract or order assigned a NAICS code for supplies or products, other than a procurement from a nonmanufacturer [@CL-MKT-0228], and is 50 percent with the cost of materials excluded [@CL-MKT-0229]. A third is 85 percent for general construction, with the cost of materials excluded [@CL-MKT-0230]. A fourth is 75 percent for construction by special trade contractors, with the cost of materials excluded [@CL-MKT-0231]. The section applies to small business set-asides above the simplified acquisition threshold and to orders issued directly to a small business under a listed paragraph above that threshold [@CL-MKT-0277]. It applies regardless of dollar value to contracts that are set aside and contracts that are awarded on a sole-source basis under the listed subparts, among other listed awards [@CL-MKT-0278].

Each limit caps what the concern may pay to subcontractors that are not similarly situated entities as a share of the amount paid by the Government for contract performance, and every limit except the services limit excludes the cost of materials [@CL-MKT-0227; @CL-MKT-0229; @CL-MKT-0230; @CL-MKT-0231]. A similarly situated entity is a first-tier subcontractor, including an independent contractor, that has the same small business program status as that which qualified the prime contractor for the award [@CL-MKT-0285], and that is also considered small for the size standard under the NAICS code the prime contractor assigned to the subcontract [@CL-MKT-0286].

## How it works

A small business contractor subject to the limitations is required to comply within the compliance periods that follow [@CL-MKT-0294]. For a contract that has been set aside, compliance is due either by the end of the base term and then by the end of each later option period, or by the end of the performance period for each order issued under the contract, and the contracting officer chooses between those two at the contracting officer's discretion [@CL-MKT-0232]. A nonmanufacturer is a concern, including a supplier, that is awarded a contract or order subject to the nonmanufacturer rule, other than a construction or service acquisition, but proposes to furnish an end item that it did not itself manufacture, process, or produce [@CL-MKT-0280]. It is required to provide an end item that a small business has manufactured, processed, or produced in the United States or its outlying areas, among other listed conditions [@CL-MKT-0233]. One listed condition is that it not exceed 500 employees, or 150 employees for information technology value-added resellers under one NAICS code [@CL-MKT-0234]. SBA may grant an individual or a class waiver to the rule to allow a nonmanufacturer to provide an end item of an other than small business without regard to the place of manufacture, processing, or production [@CL-MKT-0279]. This card does not restate how work that a similarly situated entity further subcontracts is counted, the treatment of contracts that include both services and supplies, the compliance timing for set-aside orders, the other nonmanufacturer conditions, kit assemblers or the waiver procedures.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

The Government pays 1,000,000 under a services contract that has a services NAICS code and is not a construction contract. The concern pays 400,000 to a subcontractor that is not a similarly situated entity, which is 40 percent and within a 50 percent limit. If it paid 600,000, it would be at 60 percent and over the limit. Under a supplies contract the Government pays 2,000,000, of which materials cost 800,000. The 50 percent limit is measured on the 1,200,000 left after materials, so 600,000 is the most the concern may pay to such subcontractors.

## Never confuse

The limits on subcontracting cap how much performance a small business passes on to subcontractors that are not similarly situated entities. The duty to select subcontractors competitively is a different rule, on the [Competition in Subcontracting clause](../../supply-chain/sourcing-evidence/sup-competition-in-subcontracting.md) card. The nonmanufacturer rule asks who made the end item and how large the supplier is [@CL-MKT-0233; @CL-MKT-0234], while the supplies limit applies to a contract that is not a procurement from a nonmanufacturer [@CL-MKT-0228]. Which acquisitions are set aside for small business is on the [Small business set-asides](mkt-small-business-set-asides.md) card, and the size standards are on the [Size standards and NAICS codes](../taxonomy/mkt-size-standards-and-naics-codes.md) card.
