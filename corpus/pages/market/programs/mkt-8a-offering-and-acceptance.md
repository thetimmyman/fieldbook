## Purpose

Shows what an agency's offering letter must carry to the SBA and how long the SBA has to accept a requirement.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-MKT-0499] |
| Codified citation | FAR 19.804-2 and FAR 19.804-3 [@CL-MKT-0499; @CL-MKT-0503] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-MKT-0499] |
| Applies to | Requirements an agency offers to the SBA for the 8(a) program [@CL-MKT-0499] |

After completing its evaluation, the contracting office shall notify the SBA of the extent of its plans to place 8(a) contracts with the SBA for specific quantities of items or work [@CL-MKT-0499]. The notification, called the offering letter, shall identify the time frames within which resulting 8(a) awards must be completed in order for the agency to meet its responsibilities, and shall also contain the listed information applicable to each prospective contract [@CL-MKT-0500].

SBA's decision whether to accept a requirement will be transmitted to the contracting office in writing within 10 working days of receipt of the offer if the contract is likely to exceed the simplified acquisition threshold [@CL-MKT-0503]. For a contract at or below the simplified acquisition threshold, the time limit stated for SBA's written decision is two working days of receipt [@CL-MKT-0504].

## How it works

Among the listed contents of the offering letter are the estimated period of performance, the NAICS code that applies to the principal nature of the acquisition and the anticipated dollar value of the requirement including options [@CL-MKT-0500; @CL-MKT-0501]. Another listed item is a statement that prior to the offering no solicitation for the specific acquisition has been issued as a small business, HUBZone, service-disabled veteran-owned small business or Women-Owned Small Business Program set-aside, and that no other public communication, such as a notice through the Governmentwide point of entry, has been made showing the contracting agency's clear intention to set aside the acquisition for small business, HUBZone small business, service-disabled veteran-owned small business concerns, or a set-aside under the Women-Owned Small Business Program [@CL-MKT-0500; @CL-MKT-0502; @CL-MKT-2006; @CL-MKT-2007]. For acquisitions exceeding the simplified acquisition threshold, if SBA does not respond to an offering letter within ten working days, the contracting office may seek SBA's acceptance through the Associate Administrator for Business Development [@CL-MKT-0505]. In that case, the contracting office may assume that SBA has accepted the requirement if it receives no reply from the Associate Administrator within five calendar days of receipt of its request [@CL-MKT-0505; @CL-MKT-0506]. For an acquisition not exceeding the simplified acquisition threshold, when the contracting office makes an offer on behalf of a specific participant and receives no reply to its offering letter within two working days, it may assume the offer is accepted [@CL-MKT-0577]. The source lists more contents than this card restates, and also covers an extension of the decision periods, the review of the NAICS code and the steps for sole source awards.

In order for repetitive acquisitions to be awarded through the 8(a) program, there must be separate offers and acceptances [@CL-MKT-2100]. The section also lists four matters that this allows the SBA to determine, and this card does not restate them.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An agency offers a requirement worth 900,000 and expects it to exceed its simplified acquisition threshold. The offering letter gives the work description, the period of performance, the NAICS code, the value and the statement that no set-aside solicitation has been issued. SBA does not reply within ten working days, so the contracting office writes to the Associate Administrator. Five calendar days pass with no reply, so the contracting office may treat the requirement as accepted.

## Never confuse

The offering letter goes from the agency to the SBA; the search letter and requirements letter that start selection come from the SBA and are on the [Selecting acquisitions for the program](mkt-8a-selecting-acquisitions.md) card. The SBA decision period counts working days [@CL-MKT-0503], while the escalation period to the Associate Administrator counts calendar days [@CL-MKT-0506]. The two decision periods turn on the simplified acquisition threshold [@CL-MKT-0503; @CL-MKT-0504], which is on the [Simplified acquisition and micro-purchase thresholds](../../compliance/regulatory-framework/cmp-acquisition-thresholds.md) card, and the NAICS code is on the [Size standards, NAICS codes and appeals of the contracting officer's code](../taxonomy/mkt-size-standards-and-naics-codes.md) card.
