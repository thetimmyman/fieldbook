## Purpose

Gives the clause text for a service-disabled veteran-owned small business (SDVOSB) set-aside or sole-source procurement: the definition, who may offer, joint ventures and the joint venture work share. The policy rules are on the [Service-disabled veteran-owned small business status, set-asides and sole-source awards](mkt-sdvosb-status-set-asides-and-sole-source.md) card, and status protests are on the [SDVOSB status protests](mkt-sdvosb-status-protests.md) card.

## The rule

| Codified citation | FAR 52.219-27 [@CL-MKT-1005] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |

Under the first alternative in the definition, a small business concern must be not less than 51 percent owned and controlled by one or more service-disabled veterans or, for a publicly owned business, not less than 51 percent of its stock must be owned by them [@CL-MKT-1005]. Its management and daily business operations must also be controlled by one or more service-disabled veterans or, where a service-disabled veteran has a permanent and severe disability, by the spouse or permanent caregiver of the veteran [@CL-MKT-1057]. The second alternative is a small business concern eligible under the SDVOSB Program in accordance with 13 CFR part 128 [@CL-MKT-1058]. A service-disabled veteran is a veteran as defined in title 38 of the United States Code, section 101(2), with a service-connected disability as defined in section 101(16) of that title [@CL-MKT-1059]. The veteran must also be registered as a service-disabled veteran in the Beneficiary Identification and Records Locator Subsystem or its successor system maintained by the Veterans Benefits Administration of the Department of Veterans Affairs [@CL-MKT-1060].

An SDVOSB concern eligible under the SDVOSB Program is an SDVOSB concern that, effective January 1, 2024, is designated in the System for Award Management (SAM) as certified by the Small Business Administration (SBA) [@CL-MKT-1061]. The certification is in accordance with 13 CFR 128.300, and a concern is alternatively eligible if it has represented that it is an SDVOSB concern in SAM and submitted a complete application for certification to SBA on or before December 31, 2023 [@CL-MKT-1062]. The SDVOSB Program authorizes contracting officers to limit competition, including award on a sole-source basis, to SDVOSB concerns eligible under it [@CL-MKT-1063].

## How it works

The clause applies only to the listed cases, the first being contracts set aside for, or awarded on a sole-source basis to, SDVOSB concerns eligible under the SDVOSB Program and the second being parts of a multiple-award contract set aside for them [@CL-MKT-1055]. It also applies to orders set aside for those concerns and to orders issued directly to them under multiple-award contracts [@CL-MKT-1056].

Effective January 1, 2024, in SDVOSB set-aside or sole-source procurements, offers are solicited only from, and awards are made only to, the concerns the clause lists [@CL-MKT-1006]. Those are concerns designated in SAM as SDVOSB concerns certified by SBA, or that represented their SDVOSB status in SAM and submitted a complete application for certification to SBA on or before December 31, 2023 [@CL-MKT-1007]. Offers from concerns that meet neither test are not considered [@CL-MKT-1064].

A joint venture may be considered an SDVOSB concern if its managing partner complies with the criteria in the definition and with 13 CFR 128.402 [@CL-MKT-1008]. In a joint venture that complies with paragraph (d) of the clause, the SDVOSB party or parties shall perform at least 40 percent of the work performed by the joint venture [@CL-MKT-1009]. Their work must be more than administrative functions [@CL-MKT-1065]. The general limits on subcontracting are on the [Limitations on subcontracting and the nonmanufacturer rule](mkt-limitations-on-subcontracting-and-nonmanufacturer-rule.md) card.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A joint venture whose managing partner meets the definition criteria and 13 CFR 128.402 performs 500,000 of work. The SDVOSB partner performs 200,000 of it, which is exactly 40 percent and meets the work share if that work is more than administrative functions. At 150,000, or 30 percent, the share would fall short. Separately, a concern that represented its SDVOSB status in SAM and submitted a complete certification application on December 30, 2023 is within the clause's second test. A concern that submitted its application on January 5, 2024 is not within that test and can be considered only if it is designated in SAM as certified.

## Never confuse

The ownership test in the definition is not the same question as eligibility to offer, because the clause also turns on designation in SAM as certified or on the timely application [@CL-MKT-1005; @CL-MKT-1007]. The 51 percent figure is the ownership test [@CL-MKT-1005], and the 40 percent figure is the work share inside a joint venture [@CL-MKT-1009].
