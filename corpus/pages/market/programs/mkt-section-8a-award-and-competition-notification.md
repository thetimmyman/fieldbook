## Purpose

Shows, from the clause text, who may offer when competition is limited to eligible participants, what each offeror represents, and what the Small Business Administration (SBA) agrees to when the award is made to it and it subcontracts the work. The related contract conditions are on the [special contract and subcontract conditions](mkt-special-8a-contract-and-subcontract-conditions.md) card, the limits on subcontracting for program awards are on the [limitations on subcontracting and SBA appeals](mkt-8a-subcontracting-limits-and-appeals.md) card, and competition thresholds are on the [competitive awards](mkt-8a-competitive-awards.md) card.

Offers are solicited only from the listed categories [@CL-MKT-0998]. The first is small business concerns expressly certified by SBA for participation in its 8(a) program that meet the listed criteria at the time of submission of offer [@CL-MKT-0998]. The criteria are conformance with the 8(a) support limitation set forth in the offeror's approved business plan, and conformance with the Business Activity Targets set forth in that plan or in any remedial action directed by SBA [@CL-MKT-0999]. The clause does not set the support limitation itself: it refers to the one in the offeror's approved business plan [@CL-MKT-0999]. By submitting its offer, the offeror represents that it meets the applicable criteria in paragraph (a) of the clause [@CL-MKT-1000].

The second category is a joint venture in which at least one 8(a) participant that is a party complies with those criteria, and that complies with 13 CFR 124.513(c) [@CL-MKT-1048]. The third is a joint venture comprised of a mentor and an 8(a) protégé with an approved mentor-protégé agreement under the 8(a) program [@CL-MKT-1049]. In that joint venture at least one 8(a) participant that is a party complies with the same criteria, and the joint venture complies with 13 CFR 124.513(c) [@CL-MKT-1050].

## How it works

Any award is made to SBA, which subcontracts performance to the successful 8(a) offeror selected through the evaluation criteria in the solicitation [@CL-MKT-1001]. A contracting officer may consider a joint venture for contract award, and SBA does not approve joint ventures for competitive awards but refers to 13 CFR 124.501(g) for its determination of participant eligibility [@CL-MKT-1051].

By execution of a contract, SBA agrees to the commitments the award clause lists [@CL-MKT-1043]. It agrees to furnish the supplies or services by subcontracting with the offeror determined an eligible concern under section 8(a) of the Small Business Act [@CL-MKT-1044]. Except for novation agreements, it delegates to the contracting activity the responsibility for administering the contract, with complete authority to take any action on behalf of the Government under its terms and conditions [@CL-MKT-1002]. The contracting agency must give SBA advance notice before it issues a final notice terminating the subcontractor's right to proceed, in whole or in part [@CL-MKT-1045]. Payments are made directly to the subcontractor by the contracting activity [@CL-MKT-1003]. SBA agrees to notify the contracting agency's contracting officer immediately upon notification by the subcontractor that the owner or owners on whom 8(a) eligibility was based plan to relinquish ownership or control of the concern [@CL-MKT-1046]. The subcontractor has the right of appeal from decisions of the cognizant contracting officer under the Disputes clause of the subcontract [@CL-MKT-1047]. The offeror or subcontractor agrees and acknowledges that it will, for and on behalf of SBA, fulfill and perform all of the requirements of the contract [@CL-MKT-1004].

The notice clause also requires SBA's contractor to notify the contracting officer of the contracting agency in writing immediately upon entering an agreement, oral or written, to transfer all or part of its stock [@CL-MKT-1052]. Alternate I applies if the competition is to be limited to 8(a) participants within one or more specific SBA regions or districts, and it adds a paragraph (a)(1)(iii) to paragraph (a) of the clause [@CL-MKT-1053]. The added criterion is that the offeror's approved business plan is on file and serviced by the SBA District or Area Office or Offices that the contracting officer inserts [@CL-MKT-1054]. The blanks and bracketed fill-ins in both clauses are contract-specific.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A solicitation limited to 8(a) participants closes on day 30. On day 20, a certified participant submits an offer and is in conformance with the support limitation and the Business Activity Targets in its approved business plan, so it meets the criteria at the time of submission. A certified participant that submits an offer on day 20 while not in conformance with its support limitation does not meet them. A joint venture of two participants may offer if at least one of them meets the criteria and the venture complies with the joint venture regulation the clause cites. The award of 2,000,000 is made to SBA, which subcontracts performance to the selected 8(a) offeror, and the contracting activity pays the subcontractor directly.

## Never confuse

The notice clause and the award clause do different jobs. The notice clause limits who may offer and has each offeror represent that it meets the criteria [@CL-MKT-0998; @CL-MKT-1000], while the award clause records what SBA agrees to once it executes the contract [@CL-MKT-1043]. The holder of the award is SBA, not the participant, because the award is made to SBA and SBA subcontracts performance [@CL-MKT-1001]. The support limitation comes from the participant's own approved business plan, not from these clauses [@CL-MKT-0999].
