## Purpose

Shows what master and commercial plans are, how a master plan is incorporated and how a commercial plan is used, the reporting duties through the Individual Subcontract Report and the Summary Subcontract Report in the Electronic Subcontracting Reporting System, the flowdown assurances that reach subcontractors, and the material-breach rule.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-MKT-0826] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.219-9 [@CL-MKT-0826; @CL-MKT-0829] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.219-9 [@CL-MKT-1206; @CL-MKT-1217] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-MKT-0826] |
| Applies to | Contractors, other than small business concerns, with a master plan, a commercial plan, or the listed reporting and flowdown assurances in their plans [@CL-MKT-0821; @CL-MKT-0826; @CL-MKT-0827; @CL-MKT-0828] |

A master subcontracting plan contains all the required elements of an individual plan except goals, and may be incorporated into individual plans if it has been approved [@CL-MKT-0826]. A master plan on a plant or division-wide basis that contains all the elements the clause requires, except goals, may be incorporated by reference as part of the offeror's plan, provided the conditions that follow are met [@CL-MKT-0856]. One set of conditions is that the master plan has been approved, and that the offeror ensures it is updated as necessary and provides copies of the approved plan, including evidence of its approval, to the contracting officer [@CL-MKT-0856; @CL-MKT-0857]. A further condition is that goals, and any deviations from the master plan that the contracting officer considers necessary to satisfy the contract's requirements, are set forth in the individual subcontracting plan [@CL-MKT-0856; @CL-MKT-0858].

A commercial plan is a subcontracting plan, including goals, that covers the offeror's fiscal year and applies to the entire production of commercial products and commercial services sold by the entire company or a portion of it [@CL-MKT-0827]. A commercial plan is the preferred type of plan for contractors furnishing commercial products and commercial services, and it shall relate to the offeror's planned subcontracting generally, for both commercial and Government business, rather than solely to the Government contract [@CL-MKT-0859]. Once a contractor's commercial plan has been approved, the Government will not require another subcontracting plan from the same contractor while the plan remains in effect [@CL-MKT-0860], and that holds as long as the product or service being provided continues to meet the definition of a commercial product or commercial service [@CL-MKT-0860; @CL-MKT-0861].

## How it works

Reporting begins with the plan itself. One listed plan element is assurances that the offeror will cooperate in any studies or surveys as may be required and submit periodic reports so that the Government can determine the extent of compliance with the plan [@CL-MKT-0828]. One listed assurance is that the offeror will submit the Individual Subcontract Report and/or the Summary Subcontract Report, in accordance with paragraph (l) of the clause, using the Electronic Subcontracting Reporting System [@CL-MKT-0829].

The Individual Subcontract Report is not required for commercial plans, is required for each contract containing an individual subcontracting plan, and is submitted semi-annually during contract performance for the periods ending March 31 and September 30 [@CL-MKT-0864]. It is also required for each contract within 30 days of contract completion, and it is due 30 days after the close of each reporting period unless the contracting officer directs otherwise [@CL-MKT-0864; @CL-MKT-0865].

A Summary Subcontract Report submitted under an individual plan encompasses all subcontracting under prime contracts and subcontracts with an executive agency, regardless of the dollar value of the subcontracts [@CL-MKT-0866]. It shall be submitted annually by October 30 for the twelve month period ending September 30 [@CL-MKT-0866; @CL-MKT-0867]. A contractor with a commercial plan complies with the reporting requirements by submitting one Summary Subcontract Report in the system for all contracts covered by its commercial plan [@CL-MKT-0862]. A contractor authorized to use a commercial subcontracting plan shall include in its subcontracting goals and in its Summary Subcontract Report all indirect costs, with the exception of those such as employee salaries and benefits; payments for petty cash; depreciation; interest; income taxes; property taxes; lease payments; bank fees; fines, claims, and dues; original equipment manufacturer relationships during warranty periods (negotiated up front with the product); utilities and other services purchased from a municipality or solely authorized by the municipality to provide those services in a particular geographical region; and philanthropic contributions [@CL-MKT-0977; @CL-MKT-1987; @CL-MKT-1988]. A report submitted under a commercial plan shall include all subcontract awards under the commercial plan in effect during the Government's fiscal year and all indirect costs, and shall be submitted annually within thirty days after the end of the Government's fiscal year [@CL-MKT-0863].

For flowdown, one listed plan element is assurances that the offeror will include the clause entitled Utilization of Small Business Concerns in all subcontracts that offer further subcontracting opportunities [@CL-MKT-0830]. A further assurance is that the offeror will require all subcontractors, except small business concerns and entities treated as small by statute for certain purposes, to meet the conditions the paragraph states [@CL-MKT-0831]. Those conditions cover subcontractors that receive subcontracts in excess of the applicable threshold specified in FAR 19.702(a) on the date of subcontract award, with further subcontracting possibilities, and require them to adopt a subcontracting plan that complies with the requirements of the clause [@CL-MKT-0832]. For a solicitation in a negotiated acquisition for a contract that has subcontracting possibilities, the amount FAR 19.702(a)(1)(i) states as the level the contract is expected to exceed is $900,000, or $2 million for construction, as shown on the [When a subcontracting plan is required](mkt-subcontracting-plan-when-required.md) card [@CL-MKT-0235].

Another listed assurance is that the offeror will ensure that its subcontractors with subcontracting plans agree to submit the Individual Subcontract Report and/or the Summary Subcontract Report using the system [@CL-MKT-0868]. Subcontracting plans are not required from subcontractors in either of two alternative cases: when the prime contract contains the clause at FAR 52.212-5 [@CL-MKT-0869], or when the subcontractor provides a commercial product or commercial service subject to the clause at FAR 52.244-6 under a prime contract [@CL-MKT-0870].

Failure of the contractor or a subcontractor to comply in good faith with the Utilization of Small Business Concerns clause, or with an approved plan required by the plan clause, shall be a material breach of the contract [@CL-MKT-0871]. The clause itself is on the [Utilization of small business concerns](mkt-utilization-of-small-business-concerns-clause.md) card.

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text under Class Deviation 2026-O0038, effective February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760; @CL-CMP-9762]. RFO FAR 52.219-9 keeps the definitions of a master plan and a commercial plan [@CL-MKT-1206; @CL-MKT-1207], the conditions for incorporating a master plan by reference [@CL-MKT-1208; @CL-MKT-1209; @CL-MKT-1210], and the commercial plan rules that it is the preferred type, that an approved plan means no further plan while it remains in effect and as long as the product or service stays commercial, and that all indirect costs are included except those such as employee salaries and benefits; payments for petty cash; depreciation; interest; income taxes; property taxes; lease payments; bank fees; fines, claims, and dues; original equipment manufacturer relationships during warranty periods (negotiated up front with the product); utilities and other services purchased from a municipality or solely authorized by the municipality to provide those services in a particular geographical region; and philanthropic contributions [@CL-MKT-1211; @CL-MKT-1212; @CL-MKT-1213; @CL-MKT-1215; @CL-MKT-1989; @CL-MKT-1990]. RFO FAR 52.219-9 also keeps the assurances to cooperate in studies or surveys and submit periodic reports, to include the Utilization of Small Business Concerns clause in subcontracts that offer further subcontracting opportunities, and to require subcontractors other than small business concerns (including entities treated as small business concerns by statute for certain purposes) to meet the further conditions the paragraph states [@CL-MKT-1217; @CL-MKT-1221; @CL-MKT-1222]. RFO FAR 52.219-9 states that reports are submitted using Subcontracting Plan Reporting (SPR), defined as the electronic subcontracting reporting system at SAM.gov for small business subcontracting program reporting [@CL-MKT-1218; @CL-MKT-1219], that subcontractors with plans agree to submit the ISR and/or the SSR using SPR [@CL-MKT-1220], and that a contractor with a commercial plan submits one SSR in SPR for all contracts covered by its commercial plan [@CL-MKT-1214]. RFO FAR 52.219-9(d)(9) cites FAR 19.109(a) for the applicable threshold that subcontracts received by a subcontractor must exceed on the date of subcontract award [@CL-MKT-1223]. RFO FAR 52.219-9(j) states that subcontracting plans are not required from subcontractors when the prime contract is awarded under FAR part 12, Acquisition of Commercial Products and Commercial Services, or when the subcontractor provides a commercial product or commercial service subject to the clause at FAR 52.244-6 under a prime contract [@CL-MKT-1224; @CL-MKT-1225]. RFO FAR 52.219-9(k) states that the Contracting Officer will consider failure of the Contractor or subcontractor to comply in good faith with the Utilization Of Small Business Concerns clause or an approved plan required by the clause a material breach of the contract and may consider the failure in any past performance evaluation of the Contractor [@CL-MKT-1226; @CL-MKT-9115]. RFO FAR 52.219-9(l)(1) keeps the rules that the ISR is not required for commercial plans, is required for each contract containing an individual plan, and is submitted semi-annually during contract performance for the periods ending March 31 and September 30 [@CL-MKT-1227], and states that a report is also required for each contract within 45 days of contract completion and that reports are due 45 days after the close of each reporting period unless otherwise directed by the Contracting Officer [@CL-MKT-1228]. RFO FAR 52.219-9(l)(2)(i) keeps the scope of the SSR under an individual plan [@CL-MKT-1229] and states that the report shall be submitted annually by November 14 for the twelve month period ending September 30 [@CL-MKT-1230]. For a commercial plan report, RFO FAR 52.219-9(l)(2)(ii) states that the report shall include all subcontract awards under the commercial plan in effect during the Government's fiscal year and all indirect costs and shall be submitted annually within thirty days after the end of the Government's fiscal year [@CL-MKT-1231], while RFO FAR 52.219-9(g) states that the report shall be monitored in SPR by the agency that approved the plan and submitted within 45 days after the end of the Government's fiscal year [@CL-MKT-1216].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A prime with a plan awards a subcontract of 5,000,000 to a large supplier that has further subcontracting possibilities. Suppose the amount is above the applicable threshold on the award date, and the prime contract does not contain the clause at 52.212-5 [@CL-MKT-0869]. Under the RFO text this exemption turns instead on whether the prime contract is awarded under FAR part 12 [@CL-MKT-1224]. Answer: assuming the prime contract is not awarded under FAR part 12, the prime's plan assurance means it requires that supplier to adopt a compliant plan. If the supplier were a small business concern, the exception would apply and no plan would be required.

A contractor with an approved commercial plan covers three Government contracts. Answer: it submits one summary report in the system for all three, within 30 days after the end of the Government's fiscal year under the codified clause [@CL-MKT-0863], and it does not submit an individual report for them. Under the RFO text, paragraph (g) says 45 days [@CL-MKT-1216] and paragraph (l)(2)(ii) says thirty days [@CL-MKT-1231].

## Never confuse

A master plan has all the elements of an individual plan except goals [@CL-MKT-0826], while an individual subcontracting plan covers the entire contract period (including option periods), applies to a specific contract, and has goals based on the offeror's planned subcontracting in support of the specific contract, except that indirect costs incurred for common or joint purposes may be allocated on a prorated basis to the contract [@CL-MKT-1991; @CL-MKT-0823]. A commercial plan includes goals and covers the offeror's fiscal year [@CL-MKT-0827]. A commercial plan files one Summary Subcontract Report for all contracts covered by the plan [@CL-MKT-0862], and the Individual Subcontract Report is not required for it [@CL-MKT-0864]. The codified clause points reports to the Electronic Subcontracting Reporting System [@CL-MKT-0829], and RFO FAR 52.219-9 points them to SPR [@CL-MKT-1218]. The codified ISR is due 30 days after the close of each reporting period [@CL-MKT-0865], and RFO FAR 52.219-9(l)(1)(i) says 45 days [@CL-MKT-1228]. The codified SSR under an individual plan is due by October 30 [@CL-MKT-0867], and RFO FAR 52.219-9(l)(2)(i)(D) says November 14 [@CL-MKT-1230]. Codified FAR 52.219-9(k) says failure to comply in good faith shall be a material breach [@CL-MKT-0871], and RFO FAR 52.219-9(k) says the Contracting Officer will consider it a material breach and may consider it in any past performance evaluation [@CL-MKT-1226; @CL-MKT-9115]. The scope and the individual plan are on the [scope and required elements](mkt-subcontracting-plan-clause-scope-and-required-elements.md) card for this clause. The rest of the plan content and the liquidated damages for a failed good faith effort are on the [Subcontracting plan content, good faith effort and liquidated damages](mkt-subcontracting-plan-content-and-compliance.md) card and the [Postaward rerepresentation and subcontracting liquidated damages](mkt-postaward-rerepresentation-and-subcontracting-damages.md) card. When a plan is required is on the [When a subcontracting plan is required](mkt-subcontracting-plan-when-required.md) card.

## Company calibration

Whether the company has an approved master plan or commercial plan, which subcontractors above the threshold have further subcontracting possibilities, and how the company collects their plans and reports. [@Q-040]
