## Purpose

Shows the dollar thresholds that trigger a small business subcontracting plan, the listed exclusions, how multiple-award contracts and modifications are handled, and who decides whether subcontracting possibilities exist.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-MKT-0235] |
| Codified citation | FAR 19.702 and FAR 19.705-2 [@CL-MKT-0235; @CL-MKT-0241] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-MKT-0235] |
| Applies to | Negotiated acquisitions expected to exceed $900,000 ($2 million for construction) that have subcontracting possibilities, except as stated in the listed exclusions [@CL-MKT-0235; @CL-MKT-0237; @CL-MKT-0282] |

Except as stated in FAR 19.702(b) [@CL-MKT-0282], in negotiated acquisitions each solicitation of offers to perform a contract that is expected to exceed $900,000 ($2 million for construction) and that has subcontracting possibilities shall require the apparently successful offeror to submit an acceptable subcontracting plan [@CL-MKT-0235]. If the apparently successful offeror fails to negotiate a subcontracting plan acceptable to the contracting officer within the time limit prescribed by the contracting officer, the offeror will be ineligible for award [@CL-MKT-1999]. Except as stated in FAR 19.702(b) [@CL-MKT-0282], each contract modification that causes the value of a contract without a subcontracting plan to exceed $900,000 ($2 million for construction) shall require the contractor to submit a subcontracting plan for the contract, if the contracting officer determines that subcontracting opportunities exist [@CL-MKT-0236].

Subcontracting plans (see FAR 19.702(a)(1) and (2)) are not required from small business concerns, for personal services contracts, for contracts or contract modifications that will be performed entirely outside of the United States and its outlying areas, or for modifications that are within the scope of the contract and the contract does not contain the clause at FAR 52.219-8, Utilization of Small Business Concerns [@CL-MKT-0237; @CL-MKT-0238].

## How it works

For a multiple-award contract with more than one NAICS code, the solicitation referred to in FAR 19.702(a)(1)(i) and (ii) shall require the apparently successful offeror to submit an acceptable subcontracting plan [@CL-MKT-0239]. The plan required for such a solicitation may cover either the distinct portions or categories of the proposal for which the offeror is other than small or the entirety of the proposal, at the offeror's discretion [@CL-MKT-0240]. When determining the need for a plan in that case, the contracting officer shall consider the cumulative dollar value of the portions or categories for which the offeror is other than small [@CL-MKT-0281]. On a modification the contracting officer determines whether subcontracting opportunities exist [@CL-MKT-0236]. A determination that there are no subcontracting possibilities shall include a detailed rationale, be approved at a level above the contracting officer, and be placed in the contract file [@CL-MKT-0241]. A change in size status does not change the terms and conditions of the contract [@CL-MKT-0290]. However, the contracting officer may require a plan for a contract containing the subcontracting plan clause if a prime contractor's size status changes from small to other than small as a result of a size rerepresentation [@CL-MKT-0291], which is on the [Rerepresentation after award](../taxonomy/mkt-size-rerepresentation-after-award.md) card.

The contracting officer shall provide the procurement center representative of the Small Business Administration (or, if none is assigned, see FAR 19.402(a)) a reasonable period of time to review any solicitation requiring submission of a subcontracting plan [@CL-MKT-2023] and to submit advisory findings before the solicitation is issued [@CL-MKT-2024].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A negotiated supply contract is expected to be worth 1,500,000 and has subcontracting possibilities. The apparently successful offeror is not a small business, so it must submit an acceptable plan. A construction contract of the same expected value is below the construction figure, so it does not trigger the requirement on its own. A contract with no plan is worth 800,000 and a modification lifts it to 1,100,000. If the contracting officer finds subcontracting opportunities exist, the contractor must submit a plan.

## Never confuse

The dollar thresholds and exclusions in FAR 19.702 determine when a subcontracting plan is required [@CL-MKT-0235; @CL-MKT-0236; @CL-MKT-0237; @CL-MKT-0238], while what a plan must contain and how failure to comply is handled are on the [Subcontracting plan content, good faith effort and liquidated damages](mkt-subcontracting-plan-content-and-compliance.md) card. Subcontracting plans are not required from small business concerns [@CL-MKT-0237], and if it is determined that there are no subcontracting possibilities, the determination shall include a detailed rationale, be approved at a level above the contracting officer, and be placed in the contract file [@CL-MKT-0241].
