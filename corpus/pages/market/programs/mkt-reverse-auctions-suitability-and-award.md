## Purpose

Shows when an agency may run a reverse auction, how a provider's fee reaches the offeror's price, what the contracting officer must check and alone decides, and what happens when only one offeror takes part.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-MKT-1360] |
| Codified citation | FAR 17.802, FAR 17.803 and FAR 17.804 [@CL-MKT-1360; @CL-MKT-1604] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-MKT-1360] |
| Applies to | Acquisitions in which the agency considers a reverse auction, other than the excluded acquisition types [@CL-MKT-1360; @CL-MKT-1604; @CL-MKT-1605] |

Reverse auction processes may not be used for design-build construction contracts, for procurements of architect-engineer services subject to chapter 11 of title 40 of the United States Code, or for procurements using sealed bidding procedures [@CL-MKT-1604]. Nor may they be used for the acquisition of personal protective equipment covered by the defense authorization provisions FAR 17.803(d) lists [@CL-MKT-1605]. Outside those exclusions, a reverse auction may be appropriate when market research indicates three conditions. First, a competitive marketplace exists for the supplies or services being acquired, and second, multiple offerors can satisfy the agency's requirement [@CL-MKT-1360]. Third, the nature of the supplies or services, such as clearly defined specifications and less complex requirements, encourages an iterative bidding process in which multiple offerors participate and at least one offeror submits more than one offer during the reverse auction [@CL-MKT-1361].

The reverse auction process is used to obtain pricing for an acquisition [@CL-MKT-1514]. Contracting officers must still follow the acquisition policies and procedures, such as those prescribed in subpart 8.4 or 16.5 or part 13 or 15, as appropriate for the particular acquisition [@CL-MKT-1362].

## How it works

A service platform for conducting reverse auctions may be provided by a commercial or Government entity [@CL-MKT-1515]. Some providers are paid directly by the Government, while others may use a fee structure with an indirect payment method [@CL-MKT-1516]. Under an indirect payment method, the provider adds a fee to the price of the successful offer that is provided to the Government at the close of the auction [@CL-MKT-1363]. The Government then pays the successful offeror the total price of the offer, which includes the provider's fee, and the provider then collects its fee from the successful offeror [@CL-MKT-1517; @CL-MKT-1518]. The fee is therefore part of the total price the Government pays, and the provider collects it from the successful offeror [@CL-MKT-1517; @CL-MKT-1518].

When considering a provider, the contracting officer must conduct market research for available sources of reverse auction services [@CL-MKT-1536]. The contracting officer must evaluate the fee structure for each provider and document the contract file that the use of a provider is cost effective [@CL-MKT-1536; @CL-MKT-1537]. When using a provider's services, the contracting officer must include contact information, including the contracting officer's name and email address, in the synopsis and solicitation so that offerors can ask questions directly [@CL-MKT-1540]. On receiving a successful offer, the contracting officer must verify that any provider fees or charges in the price are in accordance with the provider's fee structure, as evaluated under the fee-structure evaluation above [@CL-MKT-1541]. The contracting officer must also include in the contract file the information and documentation the provider received from offerors [@CL-MKT-1542].

When acquiring reverse auction services from a commercial provider, agencies must use competitive procedures unless an exception applies and must detail the provider's fee structure in the contract or agreement [@CL-MKT-1519]. They must make those details, including the fee structure, available to contracting officers for consideration when determining whether to use a provider, in accordance with FAR 17.804(a) [@CL-MKT-1520]. When acquiring reverse auction services, the contracting officer must ensure that the solicitation and contract include descriptions of Government data and Government-related data [@CL-MKT-1521] and data ownership, licensing, delivery and disposition instructions specific to the relevant types of that data [@CL-MKT-1522]. The disposition instructions must provide for transition of data in commercially available, or open and non-proprietary, format and for permanent records, in accordance with National Archives and Records Administration guidance [@CL-MKT-1523].

Contracting officers may use only the services of a provider that meets each of these conditions. It does not assert or imply that it can or will obtain a Government contract for participants [@CL-MKT-1524]. It lets entities register at no cost as potential offerors for reverse auctions conducted on behalf of the Government on its platform [@CL-MKT-1525], and, as part of registration, gives each entity the opportunity to execute a proprietary data protection agreement with the provider, provided its terms do not affect the terms and conditions of a Government solicitation or contract [@CL-MKT-1526]. It protects from unauthorized use or disclosure, and does not release outside the Government, contractor bid or proposal information and source selection information associated with providing reverse auction services to the Government [@CL-MKT-1527], all information similarly generated to support the issuance of a task order, a delivery order or an order under a blanket purchase agreement [@CL-MKT-1528], and information an offeror identified as restricted from duplication, use or disclosure for any purpose other than to evaluate its price or proposal [@CL-MKT-1529]. It lets offerors see the successive lowest prices without revealing an offeror's identity [@CL-MKT-1530]. At the close of each auction it gives the Government the successful offer with the offeror's price and each provider fee or charge separately identified [@CL-MKT-1531], and all information and documentation received from offerors [@CL-MKT-1532]. It does not participate as an offeror in an auction it hosts for the Government [@CL-MKT-1533], including through an entity whose relationship with it raises an actual or potential conflict of interest [@CL-MKT-1600], and it asserts no rights or license in the data gathered or generated [@CL-MKT-1534]. The provider's own contract duties are on [Duties of a reverse auction service provider](../../estimating/pricing/est-reverse-auction-services.md), and the offeror's solicitation terms are on [Reverse auction under a solicitation](../../estimating/pricing/est-reverse-auction.md); this card does not restate them.

Only a contracting officer may exclude an offeror from participating in an auction, determine the awardee of any reverse auction, or determine that the offeror is a responsible prospective contractor [@CL-MKT-1535]. When conducting a reverse auction, the contracting officer must not disclose the identity of the offerors, except the awardee's identity after an award resulting from the auction [@CL-MKT-1538]. The contracting officer must allow offerors to continually revise their prices downward until the close of the auction, and must allow an offeror to withdraw an offer from further consideration before the close [@CL-MKT-1539]. If only one offeror participates, the contracting officer may cancel the auction and document the contract file with evidence of the single participation, or accept the offer only if the price is determined to be fair and reasonable [@CL-MKT-1364].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An agency considers a reverse auction for 5,000 standard brackets, bought without sealed bidding, which is not design-build construction, architect-engineer services or personal protective equipment, so no exclusion applies [@CL-MKT-1604; @CL-MKT-1605]. Market research shows a competitive marketplace and multiple offerors able to meet the requirement, and the clearly defined specifications encourage iterative bidding, so an auction may be appropriate [@CL-MKT-1360; @CL-MKT-1361]. The contracting officer evaluates the fee structure of the provider and documents that using the provider is cost effective [@CL-MKT-1537].

Five offerors register, and two lower their prices repeatedly. The successful offer is 117,600, and under the indirect method the provider adds its fee of 2,400, so the price provided to the Government at the close is 120,000 [@CL-MKT-1363]. The provider reports the offeror's price and its fee separately [@CL-MKT-1531]. The Government pays the awardee 120,000, and the provider collects the 2,400 from the awardee, which keeps 117,600 [@CL-MKT-1517; @CL-MKT-1518]. The contracting officer verifies that the 2,400 matches the provider's fee structure [@CL-MKT-1541]. The contracting officer, not the provider, determines the awardee and whether it is responsible [@CL-MKT-1535]. No offeror's identity is disclosed during the auction, and the awardee's identity is disclosed only after the award [@CL-MKT-1538].

A second auction draws a single offeror. The contracting officer may cancel it and document the single participation in the file, or accept the offer only if the price is determined to be fair and reasonable [@CL-MKT-1364].

## Never confuse

Meeting the three market-research conditions does not make a reverse auction permissible for an excluded acquisition type, such as a sealed bid procurement [@CL-MKT-1604; @CL-MKT-1605]. A reverse auction is not a competition-free purchase: it is a way to obtain pricing, and the ordinary acquisition policies and procedures still govern [@CL-MKT-1514; @CL-MKT-1362]. A provider's fee is not a separate Government charge under the indirect method: it sits inside the total price the Government pays the offeror, who then pays the provider [@CL-MKT-1517; @CL-MKT-1518]. A single offer does not settle the award: the contracting officer may cancel, or accept only at a fair and reasonable price [@CL-MKT-1364].
