## Purpose

Shows when an acquisition is set aside for small business, what happens when one or no offer arrives, and how the socioeconomic programs sit beside set-asides.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-MKT-0219] |
| Codified citation | FAR 19.502-2 and FAR 19.203 [@CL-MKT-0219; @CL-MKT-0223; @CL-MKT-0225] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-MKT-0219] |
| Applies to | Acquisitions above the micro-purchase threshold, with different tests at and below, and above, the simplified acquisition threshold [@CL-MKT-0219; @CL-MKT-0223] |

Before setting aside an acquisition under this paragraph, the contracting officer refers to FAR 19.203(b) [@CL-MKT-9352]. Each acquisition above the micro-purchase threshold but not over the simplified acquisition threshold shall be set aside for small business unless the contracting officer determines that the exception described next applies [@CL-MKT-0219]. The exception applies when the contracting officer determines there is not a reasonable expectation of obtaining offers from two or more responsible small business concerns that are competitive in terms of fair market prices, quality, and delivery [@CL-MKT-0219; @CL-MKT-0220].

Before setting aside an acquisition under this paragraph, the contracting officer refers to FAR 19.203(c) [@CL-MKT-9353]. Any acquisition over the simplified acquisition threshold shall be set aside for small business participation when there is a reasonable expectation that offers will be obtained from at least two responsible small business concerns and that award will be made at fair market prices [@CL-MKT-0223]. Total small business set-asides shall not be made unless that reasonable expectation exists [@CL-MKT-0275]. The dollar amounts of the thresholds are on the [Simplified acquisition and micro-purchase thresholds](../../compliance/regulatory-framework/cmp-acquisition-thresholds.md) card.

## How it works

In the paragraph for acquisitions not over the simplified acquisition threshold, if the contracting officer receives only one acceptable offer from a responsible small business concern in response to a set-aside, the contracting officer should make an award to that firm [@CL-MKT-0221]. If the contracting officer receives no acceptable offers from responsible small business concerns, the set-aside shall be withdrawn and the requirement, if still valid, shall be resolicited on an unrestricted basis [@CL-MKT-0222]. The same paragraph says the small business set-aside does not preclude the award of a contract as described in the section on the relationship among small business programs [@CL-MKT-0276]. For an acquisition exceeding the simplified acquisition threshold, the contracting officer shall first consider the 8(a), HUBZone, SDVOSB or WOSB programs before considering a small business set-aside [@CL-MKT-0224]. However, if a requirement has been accepted by the SBA under the 8(a) Program, it must remain in that program unless the SBA agrees to its release in accordance with 13 CFR parts 124, 126, 127, and 128 [@CL-MKT-9319]. There is no order of precedence among those four programs [@CL-MKT-0225].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An acquisition is above the micro-purchase threshold and not over the simplified acquisition threshold, and market research shows three capable small businesses. The contracting officer sets it aside. If only one acceptable offer arrives, the officer should award to that firm. If none arrives, the set-aside is withdrawn and the requirement is resolicited on an unrestricted basis. A second, larger acquisition exceeds the simplified acquisition threshold. The officer first considers the four socioeconomic programs and only then a small business set-aside.

## Never confuse

Above the simplified acquisition threshold the contracting officer considers those programs first [@CL-MKT-0224], although a requirement the SBA has accepted under the 8(a) Program stays there unless the SBA agrees to its release [@CL-MKT-9319], and none of them outranks another [@CL-MKT-0225]. A total small business set-aside above that threshold is not made unless offers are reasonably expected from at least two responsible small business concerns and award at fair market prices [@CL-MKT-0223; @CL-MKT-0275]. The limits on subcontracting, which apply to small business set-asides above that threshold and to orders issued directly to a small business under FAR 19.504(c)(1)(ii) above that threshold [@CL-MKT-0277], are on the [Limitations on subcontracting](mkt-limitations-on-subcontracting-and-nonmanufacturer-rule.md) card. Whether a concern is small is decided against a size standard, on the [Size standards and NAICS codes](../taxonomy/mkt-size-standards-and-naics-codes.md) card.
