## Purpose

Covers how an agency authorizes a management and operating contract, the functions that cannot be placed in one, and the criteria used to identify one.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-MKT-1735] |
| Codified citation | FAR 17.602, 17.603 and 17.604 [@CL-MKT-1735; @CL-MKT-1740; @CL-MKT-1744] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-MKT-1735] |
| Applies to | Management and operating contracts that an agency head authorizes in writing [@CL-MKT-1735] |

Heads of agencies, with requisite statutory authority, may determine in writing to authorize contracting officers to enter into or renew any management and operating contract in accordance with the agency's statutory authority, or chapter 33 of title 41 of the United States Code [@CL-MKT-1735], and the agency's regulations governing such contracts [@CL-MKT-1780]. This authority shall not be delegated, and every contract so authorized shall show its authorization upon its face [@CL-MKT-1736].

Agencies may authorize management and operating contracts only in a manner consistent with the guidance of the subpart and only if they are consistent with the situations described in the identification section [@CL-MKT-1737].

A management and operating contract shall not be authorized for functions involving the direction, supervision or control of Government personnel, except for supervision incidental to training [@CL-MKT-1740]. It shall also not be authorized for functions involving the exercise of police or regulatory powers in the name of the Government, other than guard or plant protection services, or for functions of determining basic Government policies [@CL-MKT-1741]. Nor shall it be authorized for day-to-day staff or management functions of the agency or of any of its elements, or for functions that can more properly be accomplished under the subpart on authorizing the use and rental of Government property [@CL-MKT-1742].

Because issuance of an authorization is deemed sufficient proof of compliance with the list of prohibited functions, nothing in that list shall affect the validity or legality of such an authorization [@CL-MKT-1743]. For use of project labor agreements, the limitations section refers to subpart 22.5 [@CL-MKT-1782].

The DFARS scope section for its subpart says that FAR subpart 17.6 does not apply to DoD [@CL-MKT-2232].

## How it works

Within 2 years of the effective date of the regulation, agencies shall review their current contractual arrangements in light of the subpart [@CL-MKT-1738]. The review is to identify, modify as necessary and authorize management and operating contracts, and to modify as necessary or terminate contracts not so identified and authorized [@CL-MKT-1781]. A contract with less than 4 years remaining as of that effective date need not be terminated, nor identified, modified or authorized, unless it is renewed or its terms are substantially renegotiated [@CL-MKT-1739].

A management and operating contract is characterized both by its purpose and by the special relationship it creates between Government and contractor, and the following criteria can generally be applied in identifying one [@CL-MKT-1744].

- First, Government-owned or controlled facilities must be utilized, for instance in the interest of national defense or mobilization readiness, to perform the agency's mission adequately, or because private enterprise is unable or unwilling to use its own facilities for the work [@CL-MKT-1745].
- Second, because of the nature of the work or its performance in Government facilities, the Government must maintain a special, close relationship with the contractor and its personnel in important areas such as safety, security, cost control and site conditions [@CL-MKT-1746].
- Third, the conduct of the work is wholly or at least substantially separate from the contractor's other business, if any [@CL-MKT-1783].
- Fourth, the work is closely related to the agency's mission and is of a long-term or continuing nature, and there is a need to ensure its continuity and for special protection covering the orderly transition of personnel and work in the event of a change in contractors [@CL-MKT-1784].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An agency wants a contractor to operate a Government-owned test range for a long-term mission, because private firms will not use their own facilities for the work. The work calls for a close relationship with the contractor in safety and security, is wholly separate from the contractor's other business, and is closely related to the agency's mission. The agency head, who has the requisite statutory authority, determines in writing to authorize the contracting officer to enter into the contract. The authorization is not delegated to a deputy, and the contract shows it on its face [@CL-MKT-1735; @CL-MKT-1736].

The arrangement fits the identification criteria: Government-owned facilities are needed, the work calls for a close relationship in safety and security, it is separate from the contractor's other business, and it is long-term work closely related to the agency's mission [@CL-MKT-1745; @CL-MKT-1746; @CL-MKT-1783; @CL-MKT-1784].

The contract cannot give the contractor direction, supervision or control of Government personnel, other than supervision incidental to training, and cannot make the contractor the one who determines basic Government policies [@CL-MKT-1740; @CL-MKT-1741].

## Never confuse

A management and operating contract is characterized both by its purpose and by the special relationship it creates between Government and contractor [@CL-MKT-1744], and it requires a written authorization by the agency head that shall not be delegated and must be shown on the face of the contract [@CL-MKT-1735; @CL-MKT-1736]. Functions that can more properly be accomplished under the Government property use and rental subpart shall not be authorized for a management and operating contract [@CL-MKT-1742].
