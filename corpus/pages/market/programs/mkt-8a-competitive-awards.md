## Purpose

Shows when an acquisition offered under the program must be competed among eligible participants, when a sole source award is allowed, and how SBA checks eligibility.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-MKT-0507] |
| Codified citation | FAR 19.805-1 and FAR 19.805-2 [@CL-MKT-0507; @CL-MKT-0513] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-MKT-0507] |
| Applies to | Acquisitions offered to the SBA under the 8(a) program [@CL-MKT-0507] |

Except as provided in the sole source paragraph, an acquisition offered to the SBA under the 8(a) program shall be awarded on the basis of competition limited to eligible 8(a) participants when listed conditions are met [@CL-MKT-0507]. The listed conditions are joined by "and", so both apply together [@CL-MKT-0508]. The first is a reasonable expectation that at least two eligible and responsible 8(a) participants will submit offers and that award can be made at a fair market price [@CL-MKT-0508]. The second is that the anticipated total value of the contract, including options, will exceed $8.5 million for acquisitions assigned manufacturing NAICS codes and $5.5 million for all other acquisitions [@CL-MKT-0509].

Where an acquisition exceeds the competitive threshold, the SBA may accept the requirement for a sole source 8(a) award on the listed grounds [@CL-MKT-0510]. One listed ground is that there is not a reasonable expectation that at least two eligible and responsible 8(a) participants will submit offers at a fair market price [@CL-MKT-0511]. The grounds are joined by "or", so either is enough [@CL-MKT-0510; @CL-MKT-0511]. The other listed ground is that SBA accepts the requirement on behalf of a concern owned by an Indian tribe or an Alaska Native Corporation [@CL-MKT-0512]. A proposed 8(a) requirement with an estimated value exceeding the applicable competitive threshold amount shall not be divided into several requirements for lesser amounts in order to use 8(a) sole source procedures for award to a single firm [@CL-MKT-0576].

## How it works

In competitive 8(a) acquisitions SBA will determine the eligibility of the apparent successful offeror and advise the contracting office within 5 working days after receipt of the contracting office's request for an eligibility determination [@CL-MKT-0513]. If the contracting officer believes that the apparent successful offeror, or the offeror SBA has determined eligible for award, is not responsible to perform the contract, the contracting officer must refer the matter to SBA for Certificate of Competency consideration, which is on the [Certificates of Competency: general rules and referral](mkt-coc-general-and-referral.md) card [@CL-MKT-0575]. This card does not restate what happens when the apparent successful offeror is ineligible, how joint ventures are treated, or approval of a competitive award below the thresholds.

In competitive 8(a) acquisitions, including follow-on 8(a) acquisitions, subject to FAR part 15, the contracting officer conducts negotiations directly with the competing 8(a) participants [@CL-MKT-2101]. Conducting competitive negotiations among eligible 8(a) participants before SBA's formal acceptance of the acquisition for the 8(a) program may be grounds for SBA's not accepting the acquisition for the program [@CL-MKT-2102].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An agency offers a services requirement, with a non-manufacturing NAICS code, worth 7,000,000 including options. Two eligible and responsible participants are expected to offer at a fair market price. The value is above the 5,500,000 test for all other acquisitions, so the requirement is competed among eligible participants. After the evaluation, the contracting office asks SBA to confirm that the apparent successful offeror is eligible, and SBA answers within 5 working days.

## Never confuse

Competitive 8(a) awards restrict competition to eligible 8(a) participants when at least two are expected to offer at a fair market price and SBA checks the apparent successful offeror's eligibility within 5 working days [@CL-MKT-0507; @CL-MKT-0508; @CL-MKT-0513], unlike SBA's initial acceptance of an offering letter on [Agency offering and SBA acceptance of requirements for the program](mkt-8a-offering-and-acceptance.md) [@CL-MKT-0499] or a general responsibility referral under [Certificates of Competency: general rules and referral](mkt-coc-general-and-referral.md) [@CL-MKT-0475].
