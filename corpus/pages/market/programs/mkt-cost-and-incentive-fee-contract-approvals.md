## Purpose

Shows the military construction bar on cost-plus-fixed-fee contracts and who approves an exception, who may sign the determination for an incentive-fee or award-fee contract, and the weapon system incentive requirement.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-MKT-1582] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 216.306, DFARS 216.401 and DFARS 216.402-2 [@CL-MKT-1582; @CL-MKT-1585; @CL-MKT-1587] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-MKT-1582] |
| Applies to | Military construction cost-plus-fixed-fee contracts, incentive-fee and award-fee contracts, and weapon system development or production solicitations [@CL-MKT-1582; @CL-MKT-1585; @CL-MKT-1587] |

Except as provided in paragraph (c)(ii) of DFARS 216.306, annual military construction appropriations acts prohibit the use of cost-plus-fixed-fee contracts that are funded by a military construction appropriations act, are estimated to exceed $25,000, and will be performed within the United States, except Alaska [@CL-MKT-1445; @CL-MKT-1582]. For contracts in connection with a military construction project or military family housing project, the section refers to the prohibition at DFARS 216.301-3 [@CL-MKT-1632]. The prohibition does not apply to contracts specifically approved in writing, setting forth the reasons, in accordance with paragraph (c)(ii) [@CL-MKT-1633]. The Secretaries of the military departments are authorized to approve such contracts that are for environmental work only, provided the environmental work is not classified as construction, as defined by section 2801 of title 10, United States Code [@CL-MKT-1583]. The Secretary of Defense or designee must approve such contracts that are not for environmental work only or are for environmental work classified as construction [@CL-MKT-1584].

The determination and findings justifying that the use of an incentive- or award-fee contract is in the best interest of the Government may be signed by the head of contracting activity or a designee [@CL-MKT-1634]. The designee is no lower than one level below the head of the contracting activity for award-fee contracts, or one level above the contracting officer for incentive-fee contracts [@CL-MKT-1585]. Award-fee plans required in FAR 16.401(e) shall be incorporated into all award-fee type contracts [@CL-MKT-1586], and the procedures at PGI 216.401(e) are followed when planning to award an award-fee contract [@CL-MKT-1635].

Contracting officers shall ensure that requirements about the payment of incentive fees or the imposition of penalties are included in the solicitation for a contract for the engineering and manufacturing development or production of a weapon system, including embedded software [@CL-MKT-1587]. That duty applies if the program manager or comparable requiring activity official exercising program manager responsibilities includes one of two alternatives [@CL-MKT-1636]. One alternative is provisions for the payment of incentive fees to the contractor, based on achievement of design specification requirements for reliability and maintainability of weapons systems under the contract [@CL-MKT-1637]. The other alternative is the imposition of penalties to be paid by the contractor to the Government for failure to achieve such design specification requirements [@CL-MKT-1638].

## How it works

The construction bar turns on three conditions together: the funding source, the estimate and the place of performance [@CL-MKT-1582]. For contracts in connection with a military construction project or a military family housing project, the prohibition at DFARS 216.301-3 says contracting officers shall not use cost-plus-fixed-fee, cost-plus-award-fee, or cost-plus-incentive-fee contract types [@CL-MKT-1632; @CL-MKT-1700]. The paragraph (c)(i) prohibition does not apply to contracts specifically approved in writing, setting forth the reasons [@CL-MKT-1633]; the Secretaries of the military departments are authorized to approve such contracts that are for environmental work only, provided the environmental work is not classified as construction [@CL-MKT-1583], and the Secretary of Defense or designee must approve such contracts that are not for environmental work only or are for environmental work classified as construction [@CL-MKT-1584].

The signing level for the determination and findings depends on the contract type [@CL-MKT-1634; @CL-MKT-1585]. The weapon system duty applies if the program manager or comparable requiring activity official exercising program manager responsibilities includes provisions for the payment of incentive fees to the contractor or the imposition of penalties to be paid by the contractor to the Government [@CL-MKT-1587; @CL-MKT-1636; @CL-MKT-1637; @CL-MKT-1638].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A cost-plus-fixed-fee contract for a military construction project is funded by a military construction appropriations act, is estimated at 900,000 and will be performed in the United States outside Alaska. Under paragraph (c)(i), the contract is prohibited [@CL-MKT-1582] unless it is specifically approved in writing with reasons [@CL-MKT-1633]. Because the contract is in connection with a military construction project, the separate prohibition at DFARS 216.301-3 also has to be checked, and this example does not resolve it [@CL-MKT-1632; @CL-MKT-1700]. The work is construction, so it is not for environmental work only, and the Secretary of Defense or designee must approve [@CL-MKT-1584]. Had the work been environmental work only and not classified as construction, the Secretary of the military department could approve [@CL-MKT-1583]. A contract estimated at 20,000 would not exceed the 25,000 estimate condition, so the paragraph (c)(i) prohibition would not apply to it [@CL-MKT-1582].

For an incentive-fee contract the head of the contracting activity or a designee one level above the contracting officer may sign the determination and findings [@CL-MKT-1634; @CL-MKT-1585]. For an award-fee contract, a designee two levels below the head of the contracting activity may not sign, because the designee must be no lower than one level below [@CL-MKT-1585].

## Never confuse

The paragraph (c)(i) prohibition applies to cost-plus-fixed-fee contracts that meet all three conditions [@CL-MKT-1582], and it does not apply to contracts specifically approved in writing, setting forth the reasons [@CL-MKT-1633]. The award-fee and incentive-fee signing levels differ [@CL-MKT-1585]. Fee mechanics are on [fixed fee](../../contract-finance/billing-payment/ctf-fixed-fee.md), award fee on [award fee contracts](../../estimating/pricing/est-award-fee-contracts.md) and incentive contracts on [incentive contracts](../../estimating/pricing/est-incentive-contracts-general.md).
