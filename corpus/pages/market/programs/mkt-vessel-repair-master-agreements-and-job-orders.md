## Purpose

Separates the master agreement for vessel repair, which is not a contract, from the job order that is the contract for a particular job, and shows who gets a master agreement and what a job order must fix.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-MKT-1567] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 217.7101 and DFARS 217.7102 [@CL-MKT-1567; @CL-MKT-1571] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-MKT-1567] |
| Applies to | Master agreements and job orders for repair and alteration of vessels [@CL-MKT-1567; @CL-MKT-1569] |

A master agreement for repair and alteration of vessels is a written instrument of understanding, negotiated between a contracting activity and a contractor, that contains contract clauses, terms and conditions applying to future contracts for repairs, alterations, and/or additions to vessels [@CL-MKT-1567]. It contemplates separate future contracts that will incorporate by reference or attachment the clauses agreed in it, and it is not a contract [@CL-MKT-1568]. A job order is a fixed price contract incorporating, by reference or attachment, a master agreement, and it may include clauses on subjects the master agreement does not cover but that are applicable to the job order being awarded [@CL-MKT-1569]. A job order applies to a specific acquisition and sets forth the scope of work, price, delivery date and other appropriate terms for that job order [@CL-MKT-1570].

Activities shall enter into master agreements with all prospective contractors located within the United States or its outlying areas that request ship repair work and possess the organization and facilities to perform the work satisfactorily [@CL-MKT-1571]. Issuance of a master agreement does not indicate approval of the contractor's facility for any particular acquisition and is not an affirmative determination of responsibility under FAR subpart 9.1 for any particular acquisition [@CL-MKT-1572]. Activities may use master agreements with prospective contractors located outside the United States and its outlying areas, and may issue job orders under master agreements for vessels belonging to foreign governments [@CL-MKT-1573].

For a vessel of a foreign government, contractors shall treat it as if it were a vessel of the United States Government whenever the contracting officer requests that. The vessel and the foreign government shall be identified in the solicitation and job order [@CL-MKT-1574].

## How it works

Activities enter into a master agreement with every prospective contractor in the covered area that requests ship repair work and has the organization and facilities to perform it [@CL-MKT-1571]. The agreement carries the standing terms, and each job order incorporates them and adds the terms of one job [@CL-MKT-1567; @CL-MKT-1569; @CL-MKT-1570]. The contractor's own duty on a foreign government vessel is triggered when the contracting officer requests that treatment [@CL-MKT-1574].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An activity holds a master agreement with a shipyard in the United States. A job order for 400,000 of repairs to a vessel incorporates the master agreement by reference, sets out the scope of work, price and delivery date, and adds a clause on a subject the master agreement does not cover [@CL-MKT-1569; @CL-MKT-1570]. The master agreement is not itself a contract [@CL-MKT-1568]. Issuing it did not approve the yard's facility for this job or amount to an affirmative determination of responsibility for it [@CL-MKT-1572]. If the vessel belonged to a foreign government, the activity could still issue the job order under the master agreement [@CL-MKT-1573]. The solicitation and job order name the vessel and the foreign government, and the yard treats the vessel as a vessel of the United States Government if the contracting officer asks [@CL-MKT-1574].

## Never confuse

The master agreement is the standing set of terms and is not a contract, while the job order is the fixed price contract for a particular job [@CL-MKT-1568; @CL-MKT-1569]. Holding a master agreement is neither a facility approval nor a responsibility determination for a particular acquisition [@CL-MKT-1572]. Exchange, changes and job order handling are on [ship repair changes and job orders](../../contract-finance/billing-payment/ctf-dfars-ship-repair-exchange-changes-and-job-orders.md), and bonds and default on [ship repair bonds and default](../../contract-finance/billing-payment/ctf-dfars-ship-repair-bonds-and-default.md).
