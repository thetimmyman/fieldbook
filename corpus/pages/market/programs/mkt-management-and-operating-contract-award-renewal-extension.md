## Purpose

Explains the steps before award of a management and operating contract, the recurring review duty, the factors for judging an incumbent, and the level at which a renewal or extension must be authorized.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-MKT-1747] |
| Codified citation | FAR 17.605 [@CL-MKT-1747] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-MKT-1747] |
| Applies to | Award, renewal and extension of management and operating contracts [@CL-MKT-1747; @CL-MKT-1748; @CL-MKT-1749] |

Because of program requirements and the unusual nature of the work, the Government is often limited in its ability to effect competition or to replace a contractor [@CL-MKT-1785]. Contracting officers therefore should take extraordinary steps before award to assure themselves that the prospective contractor's technical and managerial capacity are sufficient and that organizational conflicts of interest are adequately covered [@CL-MKT-1747], and that the contract will grant the Government broad and continuing rights to involve itself, if necessary, in technical and managerial decisionmaking concerning performance [@CL-MKT-1786].

The contracting officer shall review each management and operating contract, following agency procedures, at appropriate intervals and at least once every 5 years, and the review should determine whether meaningful improvement in performance or cost might reasonably be achieved [@CL-MKT-1748]. Any extension or renewal must be authorized at a level within the agency no lower than the level at which the original contract was authorized under the policy paragraph [@CL-MKT-1749].

Replacement of an incumbent contractor is usually based largely upon expectation of meaningful improvement in performance or cost [@CL-MKT-1787]. Therefore, when reviewing contractor performance, contracting officers should consider the incumbent contractor's overall performance, including specifically its technical, administrative and cost performance [@CL-MKT-1750]. They should also consider the potential impact of a change in contractors on program needs, including safety, national defense and mobilization considerations, and whether it is likely that qualified offerors will compete for the contract [@CL-MKT-1788].

## How it works

Duties fall on the contracting officer: the pre-award assurance, the periodic review and the performance factors. The pre-award and performance-factor duties are stated as "should", and the review duty is "shall" [@CL-MKT-1747; @CL-MKT-1750; @CL-MKT-1748].

The review is a recurring oversight duty and is separate from any renewal decision. An extension or renewal needs its own authorization [@CL-MKT-1748; @CL-MKT-1749].

The original authorization is made by the head of the agency and cannot be delegated [@CL-MKT-1735; @CL-MKT-1736]. The renewal rule refers back to the level of that original authorization, so a renewal approved lower in the agency does not satisfy it [@CL-MKT-1749].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A management and operating contract was first authorized by the agency head. The contracting officer reviews it in year 4 and again in year 8. Each review comes within 5 years of the last, so the 5-year minimum is met [@CL-MKT-1748].

At the second review the officer weighs the incumbent's technical, administrative and cost performance, the effect of a change of contractor on safety and mobilization needs, and whether qualified offerors are likely to compete [@CL-MKT-1750; @CL-MKT-1788]. The agency decides to renew. The renewal is authorized by the agency head, the level of the original authorization [@CL-MKT-1749]. A renewal approved by an official below that level would not satisfy the rule [@CL-MKT-1749].

## Never confuse

The periodic review is not a renewal trigger. Review is a recurring duty of the contracting officer, while renewal or extension is a separate decision that must be authorized at no lower than the original level [@CL-MKT-1748; @CL-MKT-1749]. Both differ from the first authorization, which is covered on the [policy and limitations card](mkt-management-and-operating-contracts-policy-and-limitations.md).
