## Purpose

Sets out which acquisitions count as interagency acquisitions, what is excluded, the general limits on using another agency, and who reports on the use.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-MKT-1727] |
| Codified citation | FAR 17.500, 17.501 and 17.504 [@CL-MKT-1727; @CL-MKT-1730; @CL-MKT-1733] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-MKT-1727] |
| Applies to | Interagency acquisitions under any authority, except the excluded reimbursable work, incidental-contracting activities and small Federal Supply Schedule orders [@CL-MKT-1727; @CL-MKT-1777; @CL-MKT-1729] |

The subpart prescribes policies and procedures applicable to all interagency acquisitions under any authority, except as provided in its exclusion paragraph [@CL-MKT-1727]. It applies when an agency needing supplies or services obtains them using another agency's contract [@CL-MKT-1775], and when an agency uses another agency to provide acquisition assistance, such as awarding and administering a contract, a task order or a delivery order [@CL-MKT-1776].

The subpart does not apply to interagency reimbursable work performed by Federal employees other than acquisition assistance, or to interagency activities where contracting is incidental to the purpose of the transaction [@CL-MKT-1777]. It also does not apply to orders of $750,000 or less issued against Federal Supply Schedules [@CL-MKT-1729].

In addition to complying with this subpart, nondefense agencies acquiring supplies and services on behalf of the Department of Defense shall also comply with the policy and procedures of the subpart on acquisitions by nondefense agencies for the Department of Defense [@CL-MKT-1728].

An agency shall not use an interagency acquisition to circumvent conditions and limitations imposed on the use of funds [@CL-MKT-1730]. An agency shall not use an interagency acquisition to make acquisitions conflicting with any other agency's authority or responsibility [@CL-MKT-1732]. An interagency acquisition is not exempt from the requirements of subpart 7.3 on contractor versus Government performance [@CL-MKT-1731].

FAR 2.101 defines an interagency acquisition as a procedure by which an agency needing supplies or services (the requesting agency) obtains them from another agency (the servicing agency), by an assisted acquisition or a direct acquisition [@CL-MKT-9211]. The term includes acquisitions under the Economy Act (section 1535 of title 31 of the United States Code) and non-Economy Act acquisitions completed under other statutory authorities, for example General Services Administration Federal Supply Schedules in subpart 8.4 and Governmentwide acquisition contracts [@CL-MKT-9212].

## How it works

Interagency acquisitions are commonly conducted through indefinite-delivery contracts, such as task- and delivery-order contracts [@CL-MKT-1778]. The ones used most frequently are Federal Supply Schedules, Governmentwide acquisition contracts and multi-agency contracts [@CL-MKT-1779].

Reporting splits by role. The senior procurement executive for each executive agency shall submit to the Director of OMB an annual report on interagency acquisitions, as directed by OMB [@CL-MKT-1733]. The contracting officer for the servicing agency shall ensure that service contractor reporting requirements are met in accordance with subpart 4.17 on the service contracts inventory [@CL-MKT-1734].

The subpart sets general policy. The agreements, determinations and order procedures for each kind of interagency acquisition are on the linked interagency acquisitions card.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A civilian agency needs services and obtains them by placing a 900,000 dollar task order under another agency's contract. The order is not placed against a Federal Supply Schedule. The action is an interagency acquisition because the agency obtains the services using another agency's contract, and neither exclusion applies [@CL-MKT-1775; @CL-MKT-1727; @CL-MKT-1777; @CL-MKT-1729].

The servicing agency's contracting officer ensures that service contractor reporting requirements are met under the service contracts inventory subpart [@CL-MKT-1734]. The agency could not use the order to get around a limit on the use of funds [@CL-MKT-1730].

The same agency places a 600,000 dollar order against a Federal Supply Schedule. That order falls outside the subpart because it is an order of $750,000 or less issued against a Federal Supply Schedule [@CL-MKT-1729].

## Never confuse

Using another agency's contract or its acquisition assistance is not the same as interagency reimbursable work performed by Federal employees. The subpart excludes that work unless it is acquisition assistance [@CL-MKT-1777]. Nor is every Federal Supply Schedule order excluded: only orders of $750,000 or less are [@CL-MKT-1729].
