## Purpose

Explains the tie-bid priority that a small business bidder can seek by identifying the labor surplus area in which more than half of the contract price is incurred in manufacturing or production costs.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-MKT-1105] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.219-2 [@CL-MKT-1105] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-MKT-1105] |
| Applies to | Bidders that are small business concerns [@CL-MKT-1105] |

The provision applies to small business concerns only [@CL-MKT-1105]. A bidder's status as a labor surplus area concern may affect entitlement to award in case of tie bids [@CL-MKT-1106]. A bidder that wishes to be considered for that priority must identify, in the space the provision supplies, the labor surplus area in which the costs to be incurred on account of manufacturing or production, by the bidder or its first-tier subcontractors, amount to more than 50 percent of the contract price [@CL-MKT-1107].

Failure to identify the labor surplus areas as specified precludes the bidder from receiving priority consideration [@CL-MKT-1108]. A bidder that is awarded a contract as a result of priority consideration, and would not otherwise have received award, must perform the contract, or cause it to be performed, in accordance with the obligations of a labor surplus area concern [@CL-MKT-1109].

## How it works

The provision names tie bids as the case in which labor surplus area status may affect entitlement to award [@CL-MKT-1106]. A bidder claims the priority by filling in the space the provision supplies with the labor surplus area that meets the cost test [@CL-MKT-1107]. A bidder that does not identify the areas as specified cannot receive priority consideration [@CL-MKT-1108]. A bidder that wins only because of the priority takes on the duty to perform, or cause performance, as a labor surplus area concern [@CL-MKT-1109]. The provision does not define those obligations.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

Three small business bidders submit the same low price. Bidder A expects manufacturing costs equal to 30 percent of the contract price at its own plant and 25 percent at a first-tier subcontractor, both in one labor surplus area, and names that area in the space provided. The total is 55 percent, which is more than 50 percent, so A meets the condition and may be considered for the priority in the tie. Bidder B names an area where its costs are exactly 50 percent. That is not more than 50 percent, so B has not identified an area as specified. Bidder C names no area. B and C are precluded from priority consideration. If A is awarded the contract only because of the priority, A must perform the contract, or cause it to be performed, in accordance with the obligations of a labor surplus area concern.

## Never confuse

Labor surplus area status is not small business status: the provision applies to small business concerns only, and the labor surplus area identification matters when bids are tied [@CL-MKT-1105; @CL-MKT-1106]. The identification states where the manufacturing or production costs will be incurred, and it is not a price [@CL-MKT-1107]. The small business set-aside rules are on the [Small business set-asides and the socioeconomic programs](mkt-small-business-set-asides.md) card.

## Company calibration

Whether each bidding entity and contract is inside the finance remit, so the labor surplus area identification is made and kept with the bid. [@Q-003]
