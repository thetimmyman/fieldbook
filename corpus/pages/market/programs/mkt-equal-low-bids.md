## Purpose

Explains the tie-bid priority that a small business bidder can seek by identifying the labor surplus area in which more than half of the contract price is incurred in manufacturing or production costs.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-MKT-1105] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.219-2 [@CL-MKT-1105]; FAR 2.101 [@CL-MKT-9243; @CL-MKT-9244; @CL-MKT-9245] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.219-2 [@CL-MKT-0121; @CL-MKT-0122; @CL-MKT-0123; @CL-MKT-0124] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-MKT-1105] |
| Applies to | Bidders that are small business concerns [@CL-MKT-1105] |

The provision applies to small business concerns only [@CL-MKT-1105]. A bidder's status as a labor surplus area concern may affect entitlement to award in case of tie bids [@CL-MKT-1106]. A bidder that wishes to be considered for that priority must identify, in the space the provision supplies, the labor surplus area in which the costs to be incurred on account of manufacturing or production, by the bidder or its first-tier subcontractors, amount to more than 50 percent of the contract price [@CL-MKT-1107].

Failure to identify the labor surplus areas as specified in paragraph (b) of the provision precludes the bidder from receiving priority consideration [@CL-MKT-1108]. If the bidder is awarded a contract as a result of priority consideration under the provision and would not otherwise have received award, the bidder shall perform the contract or cause the contract to be performed in accordance with the obligations of a labor surplus area concern [@CL-MKT-1109].

## How it works

The provision names tie bids as the case in which a bidder's status as a labor surplus area concern may affect entitlement to award [@CL-MKT-1106]. If the bidder wishes to be considered for priority under the provision, it must identify, in the space provided in paragraph (b) of the provision, the labor surplus area in which the costs to be incurred on account of manufacturing or production by the bidder or by first-tier subcontractors amount to more than 50 percent of the contract price [@CL-MKT-1107]. Failure to identify the labor surplus areas as specified in paragraph (b) of the provision precludes the bidder from receiving priority consideration [@CL-MKT-1108]. If the bidder is awarded a contract as a result of priority consideration under the provision and would not otherwise have received award, the bidder shall perform the contract or cause the contract to be performed in accordance with the obligations of a labor surplus area concern [@CL-MKT-1109].

In codified FAR 2.101, a labor surplus area means a geographical area identified by the Department of Labor in accordance with 20 CFR part 654, subpart A, as an area of concentrated unemployment or underemployment or an area of labor surplus [@CL-MKT-9243]. A labor surplus area concern means a concern that together with its first-tier subcontractors will perform substantially in labor surplus areas [@CL-MKT-9244]. Performance is substantially in labor surplus areas if the costs incurred under the contract on account of manufacturing, production, or performance of appropriate services in labor surplus areas exceed 50 percent of the contract price [@CL-MKT-9245]. In RFO FAR 2.101, a labor surplus area means a geographical area identified by the Department of Labor in accordance with 20 CFR part 654, subpart A, as an area of concentrated unemployment or underemployment or an area of labor surplus [@CL-MKT-9258], and a labor surplus area concern means a concern that together with its first-tier subcontractors will perform substantially in labor surplus areas, where performance is substantially in labor surplus areas if the costs incurred under the contract on account of manufacturing, production, or performance of appropriate services in labor surplus areas exceed 50 percent of the contract price [@CL-MKT-9259; @CL-MKT-9306].

Effective February 1, 2026, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text [@CL-CMP-9760; @CL-CMP-9762]. RFO FAR 52.219-2 applies to small business concerns only and states that the bidder's status as a labor surplus area (LSA) concern may affect entitlement to award in case of tie bids [@CL-MKT-0121]. Under RFO FAR 52.219-2(b), if the bidder wishes to be considered for this priority, the bidder must identify, in the following space, the LSA in which the costs to be incurred on account of manufacturing or production (by the bidder or the first-tier subcontractors) amount to more than 50 percent of the contract price [@CL-MKT-0122]. Under RFO FAR 52.219-2(c), failure to identify the labor surplus areas as specified in paragraph (b) of the provision precludes the bidder from receiving priority consideration, and if the bidder is awarded a contract as a result of priority consideration under the provision and would not otherwise have received award, the bidder shall perform the contract or cause the contract to be performed in accordance with the obligations of a labor surplus area concern [@CL-MKT-0123; @CL-MKT-0124].

Under FAR 19.202-3, in the event of equal low bids (see FAR 14.408-6), awards shall be made first to small business concerns that are also labor surplus area concerns, and second to small business concerns that are not also labor surplus area concerns [@CL-MKT-2002].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

Three small business bidders submit the same low price. Bidder A expects manufacturing costs equal to 30 percent of the contract price at its own plant and 25 percent at a first-tier subcontractor, both in one labor surplus area, and names that area in the space provided. The total is 55 percent, which is more than 50 percent, so A meets the condition and may be considered for the priority in the tie [@CL-MKT-1106; @CL-MKT-1107]. Bidder B names an area where its costs are exactly 50 percent. That is not more than 50 percent, so B has not identified an area as specified [@CL-MKT-1107]. Bidder C names no area. B and C are precluded from priority consideration [@CL-MKT-1108]. If A is awarded the contract as a result of priority consideration under the provision and would not otherwise have received award, A must perform the contract, or cause it to be performed, in accordance with the obligations of a labor surplus area concern [@CL-MKT-1109].

## Never confuse

The provision at FAR 52.219-2 applies to small business concerns only, and a bidder's status as a labor surplus area concern may affect entitlement to award in case of tie bids [@CL-MKT-1105; @CL-MKT-1106]. If the bidder wishes to be considered for priority, it must identify the labor surplus area in which the costs to be incurred on account of manufacturing or production by the bidder or first-tier subcontractors amount to more than 50 percent of the contract price [@CL-MKT-1107]. The small business set-aside rules are on the [Small business set-asides and the socioeconomic programs](mkt-small-business-set-asides.md) card.

## Company calibration

Whether each bidding entity and contract is inside the finance remit, so the labor surplus area identification is made and kept with the bid. [@Q-003]
