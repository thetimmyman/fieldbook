## Purpose

Covers what an offeror represents about institutional status and about presence in a disaster or emergency set-aside area, and the work-share limits that the subcontracting restriction clause sets for the contractor. The general limits on subcontracting are on the [Limitations on subcontracting and the nonmanufacturer rule](mkt-limitations-on-subcontracting-and-nonmanufacturer-rule.md) card.

The offeror represents that it is or is not a historically black college or university, and that it is or is not a minority institution [@CL-MKT-1024]. A historically black college or university is an institution determined by the Secretary of Education to meet the requirements of 34 CFR 608.2 [@CL-MKT-1090]. A minority institution is an institution of higher education meeting the requirements of section 365(3) of the Higher Education Act of 1965, including a Hispanic-serving institution of higher education as defined in section 502(a) of that Act [@CL-MKT-1091]. The FAR's general definitions say a minority institution means an institution of higher education meeting the requirements of section 365(3) of the Higher Education Act of 1965, including a Hispanic-serving institution of higher education as defined in section 502(a) of the Act [@CL-MKT-9298]. The RFO model text of FAR 2.101 states it in the same words [@CL-MKT-9299; @CL-MKT-9298].

In the disaster or emergency area provision, the set-aside area is filled in by the contracting officer with definite geographic boundaries [@CL-MKT-1092]. The offeror represents that it does or does not reside or primarily do business in that area [@CL-MKT-1093].

## How it works

An offeror is considered to be residing or primarily doing business in the set-aside area if, during the last twelve months, it had its main operating office in the area [@CL-MKT-1025]. That office must also have generated at least half of the offeror's gross revenues and employed at least half of the offeror's permanent employees [@CL-MKT-1026]. If the offeror does not meet those criteria, the factors to be considered in determining whether it resides or primarily does business in the set-aside area include those the provision lists [@CL-MKT-1094]. They include the physical locations of its permanent offices and the date any office in the area was established, its current state licenses, and its record of past work in the area [@CL-MKT-1095]. They also include its contractual history with subcontractors and suppliers in the area, the percentage of its gross revenues attributable to work performed in the area, and the number of permanent employees it employs in the area [@CL-MKT-1096]. The last listed factors are its membership in local and state organizations in the area and other evidence that it resides or primarily does business there, such as utility bills and bank statements from a sole proprietorship [@CL-MKT-1097].

If the offeror represents that it resides or primarily does business in the set-aside area, it shall furnish documentation to support the representation if the contracting officer requests it [@CL-MKT-1098]. The solicitation may require the offeror to submit that documentation with its offer [@CL-MKT-1099]. The blank for the set-aside area and the check boxes are contract-specific fill-ins.

The subcontracting restriction clause sets a work-share limit for each type of contract, and the contractor agrees that in performance of the contract the limit for its type applies [@CL-MKT-1155]. The terms it uses are defined in the Small Business Administration regulations at 13 CFR 125.6(e) [@CL-MKT-1100]. For services other than construction, at least 50 percent of the cost of contract performance incurred for personnel shall be expended for employees of the contractor or employees of other businesses residing or primarily doing business in the area under the notice clause at FAR 52.226-4 [@CL-MKT-1027]. For supplies other than procurement from a nonmanufacturer, the contractor or employees of other businesses residing or primarily doing business in the set-aside area shall perform work for at least 50 percent of the cost of manufacturing the supplies, not including the cost of materials [@CL-MKT-1028; @CL-MKT-1029]. For general construction the contractor will perform at least 15 percent of the cost of the contract, not including the cost of materials, with its own employees or employees of other businesses residing or primarily doing business in the set-aside area [@CL-MKT-1030]. For construction by special trade contractors the figure is at least 25 percent on the same basis [@CL-MKT-1031].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text from February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. The RFO provision keeps the definitions of a historically black college or university and a minority institution and the representation about them [@CL-MKT-0126; @CL-MKT-1090; @CL-MKT-0127; @CL-MKT-1091; @CL-MKT-0128; @CL-MKT-1024]. The RFO disaster or emergency area provision keeps the set-aside area filled in by the contracting officer with definite geographic boundaries and the representation about residing or primarily doing business there [@CL-MKT-0129; @CL-MKT-1092; @CL-MKT-0130; @CL-MKT-1093], the residence test on the main operating office over the last twelve months [@CL-MKT-0131; @CL-MKT-1025; @CL-MKT-1026], the further factors [@CL-MKT-0132; @CL-MKT-1094; @CL-MKT-0133; @CL-MKT-1095; @CL-MKT-0134; @CL-MKT-1096; @CL-MKT-0135; @CL-MKT-1097] and the documentation rules [@CL-MKT-0136; @CL-MKT-1098; @CL-MKT-0137; @CL-MKT-1099]. The RFO subcontracting restriction clause keeps the source of its definitions in 13 CFR 125.6(e) [@CL-MKT-0138; @CL-MKT-1100] and the limits for supplies, general construction and special trade construction [@CL-MKT-0140; @CL-MKT-1028; @CL-MKT-1029; @CL-MKT-0141; @CL-MKT-1030; @CL-MKT-0142; @CL-MKT-1031]. For services other than construction, the RFO text says at least 50 percent of the cost of contract performance incurred for personnel shall be expended for employees of the Contractor or of other businesses residing or primarily doing business in the area designated in the clause at FAR 52.226-4, where the codified text says "in the clause at FAR 52.226-4" [@CL-MKT-0139; @CL-MKT-1027].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An offeror's main operating office in the set-aside area generated 60 percent of its gross revenues in the last twelve months but employed only 40 percent of its permanent employees. It does not meet the residence criteria, so the further listed factors are considered. A general construction contract in the area has a cost of 2,000,000, of which 800,000 is the cost of materials. The cost of the contract not including materials is 1,200,000, so the contractor must perform at least 15 percent of it, or 180,000, with its own employees or employees of businesses in the area. On special trade construction the same base gives 25 percent, or 300,000. On a supply contract with manufacturing cost of 600,000 not including materials, work for at least 300,000 must be performed by the contractor or area businesses' employees. On a services contract with personnel cost of 1,000,000, at least 500,000 must be spent for employees of the contractor or of area businesses.

## Never confuse

Residing in the set-aside area is a test on the main operating office over the last twelve months, built on its revenues and permanent employees, and the further factors apply only when the offeror does not meet it [@CL-MKT-1025; @CL-MKT-1026; @CL-MKT-1094]. The limits are measured on different bases: personnel cost for services, manufacturing cost without materials for supplies, and contract cost without materials for construction [@CL-MKT-1027; @CL-MKT-1028; @CL-MKT-1030; @CL-MKT-1031].
