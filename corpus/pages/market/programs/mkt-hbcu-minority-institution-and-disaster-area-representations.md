## Purpose

Covers what an offeror represents about institutional status and about presence in a disaster or emergency set-aside area, and the work-share limits that the subcontracting restriction clause sets for the contractor. The general limits on subcontracting are on the [Limitations on subcontracting and the nonmanufacturer rule](mkt-limitations-on-subcontracting-and-nonmanufacturer-rule.md) card.

The offeror represents that it is or is not a historically black college or university, and that it is or is not a minority institution [@CL-MKT-1024]. A historically black college or university means an institution determined by the Secretary of Education to meet the requirements of 34 CFR 608.2 [@CL-MKT-1090]. A minority institution means an institution of higher education meeting the requirements of section 365(3) of the Higher Education Act of 1965 (title 20, United States Code, section 1067k), including a Hispanic-serving institution of higher education, as defined in section 502(a) of the Act (title 20, United States Code, section 1101a) [@CL-MKT-1091]. In codified FAR 2.101, a minority institution means an institution of higher education meeting the requirements of section 365(3) of the Higher Education Act of 1965 (title 20, United States Code, section 1067k), including a Hispanic-serving institution of higher education, as defined in section 502(a) of the Act (title 20, United States Code, section 1101a) [@CL-MKT-9298]. In RFO FAR 2.101, a minority institution means an institution of higher education meeting the requirements of section 365(3) of the Higher Education Act of 1965 (title 20, United States Code, section 1067k), including a Hispanic-serving institution of higher education, as defined in section 502(a) of the Act (title 20, United States Code, section 1101a) [@CL-MKT-9299].

In the disaster or emergency area provision, the set-aside area is filled in by the contracting officer with definite geographic boundaries [@CL-MKT-1092]. The offeror represents, as part of its offer, that it does or does not reside or primarily do business in the set-aside area [@CL-MKT-1093].

## How it works

An offeror is considered to be residing or primarily doing business in the set-aside area if, during the last twelve months, the offeror had its main operating office in the area and that office generated at least half of the offeror's gross revenues and employed at least half of the offeror's permanent employees [@CL-MKT-1025; @CL-MKT-1026]. If the offeror does not meet those criteria, factors to be considered in determining whether an offeror resides or primarily does business in the set-aside area include the listed items [@CL-MKT-1094]. They include the physical location(s) of the offeror's permanent office(s) and date any office in the set-aside area(s) was established, the offeror's current state licenses, and the offeror's record of past work in the set-aside area(s) (e.g., how much work was performed and how much recent work was performed in the set-aside area(s)) [@CL-MKT-1094; @CL-MKT-1095]. They also include the offeror's contractual history with subcontractors and suppliers in the set-aside area(s), the percentage of the offeror's gross revenues attributable to work performed in the set-aside area(s), and the number of permanent employees the offeror employs in the set-aside area(s) [@CL-MKT-1094; @CL-MKT-1096]. The remaining listed factors are membership in local and state organizations in the set-aside area(s) and other evidence that establishes the offeror resides or primarily does business in the set-aside area(s) (e.g., utility bills or bank statements from a sole proprietorship) [@CL-MKT-1094; @CL-MKT-1097].

If the offeror represents it resides or primarily does business in the set-aside area, the offeror shall furnish documentation to support its representation if requested by the contracting officer [@CL-MKT-1098]. The solicitation may require the offeror to submit with its offer documentation to support the representation [@CL-MKT-1099].

Under the subcontracting restriction clause, the contractor agrees that in performance of the contract the restriction for its contract type applies [@CL-MKT-1155]. The terms "cost of the contract," "cost of contract performance incurred for personnel," "cost of manufacturing," "cost of materials," "personnel," and "subcontracting," as used in the clause, are defined in the Small Business Administration regulations at 13 CFR 125.6(e) [@CL-MKT-1100]. For services (except construction), at least 50 percent of the cost of contract performance incurred for personnel shall be expended for employees of the contractor or employees of other businesses residing or primarily doing business in the clause at FAR 52.226-4, Notice of Disaster or Emergency Area Set-Aside [@CL-MKT-1155; @CL-MKT-1027; @CL-MKT-1457]. For supplies (other than procurement from a nonmanufacturer of such supplies), the contractor or employees of other businesses residing or primarily doing business in the set-aside area shall perform work for at least 50 percent of the cost of manufacturing the supplies, not including the cost of materials [@CL-MKT-1155; @CL-MKT-1028; @CL-MKT-1029]. For general construction, the contractor will perform at least 15 percent of the cost of the contract, not including the cost of materials, with its own employees or employees of other businesses residing or primarily doing business in the set-aside area [@CL-MKT-1155; @CL-MKT-1030; @CL-MKT-1458], or for construction by special trade contractors, the contractor will perform at least 25 percent of the cost of the contract, not including the cost of materials, with its own employees or employees of other businesses residing or primarily doing business in the set-aside area [@CL-MKT-1155; @CL-MKT-1458; @CL-MKT-1031].

Effective February 1, 2026, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text [@CL-CMP-9760; @CL-CMP-9762]. In RFO FAR 52.226-2, a historically black college or university means an institution determined by the Secretary of Education to meet the requirements of 34 CFR 608.2, a minority institution means an institution of higher education meeting the requirements of section 365(3) of the Higher Education Act of 1965 (title 20, United States Code, section 1067k), including a Hispanic-serving institution of higher education, as defined in section 502(a) of the Act (title 20, United States Code, section 1101a), and the offeror represents that it is or is not a historically black college or university and is or is not a minority institution [@CL-MKT-0126; @CL-MKT-0127; @CL-MKT-0128]. In RFO FAR 52.226-3, the set-aside area is filled in by the contracting officer with definite geographic boundaries, and the offeror represents, as part of its offer, that it does or does not reside or primarily do business in the set-aside area [@CL-MKT-0129; @CL-MKT-0130]. Under RFO FAR 52.226-3(c), an offeror is considered to be residing or primarily doing business in the set-aside area if, during the last twelve months, the offeror had its main operating office in the area and that office generated at least half of the offeror's gross revenues and employed at least half of the offeror's permanent employees [@CL-MKT-1461; @CL-MKT-0131]. Under RFO FAR 52.226-3(d), if the offeror does not meet the criteria in paragraph (c), factors to be considered in determining whether an offeror resides or primarily does business in the set-aside area include the physical location(s) of the offeror's permanent office(s) and date any office in the set-aside area(s) was established, current state licenses, record of past work in the set-aside area(s), contractual history with subcontractors and suppliers in the set-aside area(s), percentage of the offeror's gross revenues attributable to work performed in the set-aside area(s), number of permanent employees the offeror employs in the set-aside area(s), membership in local and state organizations in the set-aside area(s), and other evidence that establishes the offeror resides or primarily does business in the set-aside area(s) (e.g., utility bills or bank statements from a sole proprietorship) [@CL-MKT-0132; @CL-MKT-0133; @CL-MKT-0134; @CL-MKT-0135]. Under RFO FAR 52.226-3(e), if the offeror represents it resides or primarily does business in the set-aside area, the offeror shall furnish documentation to support its representation if requested by the contracting officer, and the solicitation may require the offeror to submit with its offer documentation to support the representation [@CL-MKT-0136; @CL-MKT-0137]. In RFO FAR 52.226-5, the terms "cost of the contract," "cost of contract performance incurred for personnel," "cost of manufacturing," "cost of materials," "personnel," and "subcontracting," as used in the clause, are defined in the Small Business Administration regulations at 13 CFR 125.6(e) [@CL-MKT-0138]. Under RFO FAR 52.226-5(b), the contractor agrees that in performance of the contract: for services (except construction), at least 50 percent of the cost of contract performance incurred for personnel shall be expended for employees of the contractor or employees of other businesses residing or primarily doing business in the area designated in the clause at FAR 52.226-4, Notice of Disaster or Emergency Area Set-Aside [@CL-MKT-1459; @CL-MKT-0139; @CL-MKT-1460]; for supplies (other than procurement from a nonmanufacturer of such supplies), the contractor or employees of other businesses residing or primarily doing business in the set-aside area shall perform work for at least 50 percent of the cost of manufacturing the supplies, not including the cost of materials [@CL-MKT-1459; @CL-MKT-1460; @CL-MKT-0140]; for general construction, the contractor will perform at least 15 percent of the cost of the contract, not including the cost of materials, with its own employees or employees of other businesses residing or primarily doing business in the set-aside area [@CL-MKT-1459; @CL-MKT-0141]; or for construction by special trade contractors, the contractor will perform at least 25 percent of the cost of the contract, not including the cost of materials, with its own employees or employees of other businesses residing or primarily doing business in the set-aside area [@CL-MKT-1459; @CL-MKT-0141; @CL-MKT-0142].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An offeror's main operating office in the set-aside area generated 60 percent of its gross revenues in the last twelve months but employed only 40 percent of its permanent employees. It does not meet the residence criteria, so the further listed factors are considered. A general construction contract in the area has a cost of 2,000,000, of which 800,000 is the cost of materials. The cost of the contract not including materials is 1,200,000, so the contractor must perform at least 15 percent of it, or 180,000, with its own employees or employees of businesses in the area. On special trade construction the same base gives 25 percent, or 300,000. On a supply contract with manufacturing cost of 600,000 not including materials, work for at least 300,000 must be performed by the contractor or area businesses' employees. On a services contract with personnel cost of 1,000,000, at least 500,000 must be spent for employees of the contractor or of area businesses.

## Never confuse

These institutional and disaster-area clauses require representing HBCU/minority-institution status or local disaster-area residence and spending at least 50 percent of non-construction service personnel costs on contractors inside the disaster or emergency area [@CL-MKT-1024; @CL-MKT-1027; @CL-MKT-1093], rather than measuring small-business status alone as general [Limitations on subcontracting and the nonmanufacturer rule](mkt-limitations-on-subcontracting-and-nonmanufacturer-rule.md) do [@CL-MKT-0226].
