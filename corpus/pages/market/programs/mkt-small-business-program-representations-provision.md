## Purpose

Shows what the small business program representations provision asks an offeror to state about its size and status, how the NAICS code and size standard enter, and what the notice in the provision says about misrepresentation.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-MKT-0806] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.219-1 [@CL-MKT-0806; @CL-MKT-0809] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.219-1 [@CL-MKT-1166; @CL-MKT-1171] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-MKT-0806] |
| Applies to | Offerors that make the representations as part of their offers [@CL-MKT-0808] |

The basic provision gives the NAICS code for the acquisition and the small business size standard as blanks to be inserted [@CL-MKT-0806]. In the provision, a small business concern is a concern, including its affiliates, that is independently owned and operated, not dominant in its field of operation, and qualified as a small business under the criteria in part 121 of title 13 of the Code of Federal Regulations and the size standard in paragraph (b) [@CL-MKT-0809]. For a concern that offers an end item it did not itself manufacture, process, or produce, on an acquisition other than construction or service, the size standard is 500 employees, or 150 employees for information technology value-added resellers under NAICS code 541519 [@CL-MKT-0807]. That standard applies if the acquisition meets any one of three alternatives. The first is that it is set aside for small business and has a value above the simplified acquisition threshold, and the second is that it uses the HUBZone price evaluation preference regardless of dollar value unless the offeror waives it [@CL-MKT-0807; @CL-MKT-0872; @CL-MKT-9350]. The third is that it is an 8(a), HUBZone, service-disabled veteran-owned, economically disadvantaged women-owned, or women-owned small business set-aside or sole-source award regardless of dollar value [@CL-MKT-0807; @CL-MKT-0873].

The offeror represents as part of its offer that it is, or is not, a small business concern [@CL-MKT-0808]. An alternative representation in the same paragraph is that the offeror is, or is not, a small business joint venture that complies with the requirements of sections 121.103(h) and 125.8(a) and (b) of title 13 of the Code of Federal Regulations [@CL-MKT-9351; @CL-MKT-0974]. Under Alternate II, the offeror shall represent its small business size status for each one of the NAICS codes assigned to the acquisition under which it is submitting an offer [@CL-MKT-0813].

## How it works

Once the blanks are filled, the offeror states its size status against the NAICS code and size standard as part of its offer [@CL-MKT-0806; @CL-MKT-0808]. In codified FAR 52.219-1(c)(3) and (c)(6), an offeror that represented itself as a small business concern in paragraph (c)(1) also represents whether it is, or is not, a women-owned small business concern [@CL-MKT-9075] and whether it is, or is not, a veteran-owned small business concern [@CL-MKT-9076]. The notice paragraph is aimed at any person who misrepresents a firm's status as small, HUBZone small, small disadvantaged, service-disabled veteran-owned small, economically disadvantaged women-owned small, or women-owned small eligible under the WOSB Program in order to obtain a contract [@CL-MKT-0810]. A person who does so to obtain a contract to be awarded under the preference programs established under listed sections of the Small Business Act, or other Federal law that references section 8(d) for program eligibility, shall be punished by fine, imprisonment, or both [@CL-MKT-0811]. Such a person shall also be subject to administrative remedies, including suspension and debarment, and be ineligible for participation in programs conducted under the authority of the Act [@CL-MKT-0812].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text because Class Deviation 2026-O0038 directs it, effective February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. RFO FAR 52.219-1 states the NAICS code and size standard as blanks to insert [@CL-MKT-0806; @CL-MKT-1166], the definition of a small business concern [@CL-MKT-0809; @CL-MKT-1165], and the 500-employee or 150-employee size standard for an offeror that furnishes an end item it did not manufacture [@CL-MKT-0807; @CL-MKT-1167; @CL-MKT-1168] under the conditions on a set-aside above the simplified acquisition threshold, on the HUBZone price evaluation preference [@CL-MKT-0872; @CL-MKT-1168; @CL-MKT-1169], and on an 8(a), HUBZone, service-disabled veteran-owned, economically disadvantaged women-owned, or women-owned small business set-aside or sole-source award [@CL-MKT-0873; @CL-MKT-1170]. It states the representation that the offeror is, or is not, a small business concern [@CL-MKT-0808; @CL-MKT-1171], the alternative small business joint venture representation [@CL-MKT-0974; @CL-MKT-1171], and, in Alternate II, the size status representation for each NAICS code [@CL-MKT-0813; @CL-MKT-1172]. It states the notice on misrepresenting a firm's status [@CL-MKT-0810; @CL-MKT-1173], including the reach to a person who misrepresents status in order to obtain a contract under the listed preference programs [@CL-MKT-0811; @CL-MKT-1174]. RFO FAR 52.219-1(d) states that such a person will be punished by fine, imprisonment, or both, will be subject to administrative remedies, including suspension and debarment, and will be ineligible for participation in programs conducted under the authority of the Act [@CL-MKT-1180], whereas codified FAR 52.219-1(d) states that such a person shall be punished by fine, imprisonment, or both [@CL-MKT-0811] and shall be subject to administrative remedies and ineligibility [@CL-MKT-0812]. In RFO FAR 52.219-1(c)(2)-(3), an offeror that represented itself as a small business concern in paragraph (c)(1) represents whether it is, or is not, a women-owned small disadvantage business concern and whether it is, or is not, a WOSB joint venture eligible under the WOSB Program [@CL-MKT-9074], whereas codified FAR 52.219-1 includes paragraphs (c)(3) and (c)(6) for representing status as a women-owned small business concern or a veteran-owned small business concern [@CL-MKT-9075; @CL-MKT-9076].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A solicitation names a size standard of 600 employees. An offeror has 450 employees, counting its affiliates, and meets the other criteria. It can represent that it is a small business concern. With 700 employees it would represent that it is not.

## Never confuse

Under FAR 52.219-1(c)(1), the offeror represents as part of its offer whether it is, or is not, a small business concern [@CL-MKT-0808]. The rule on when and how the offeror makes the representation is on the [Offeror size and status representation](../taxonomy/mkt-offeror-size-and-status-representation.md) card, and the SBA penalty rules for misrepresenting size are on the [Size misrepresentation penalties](../taxonomy/mkt-sba-size-misrepresentation-penalties-and-subcontract-size.md) card. The representation after award is on the [Postaward rerepresentation and subcontracting liquidated damages](mkt-postaward-rerepresentation-and-subcontracting-damages.md) card, and the notice clauses that reject offers from concerns that are not small on set-aside solicitations are on the [Small business set-aside notice clauses](mkt-small-business-set-aside-notice-clauses.md) card.
