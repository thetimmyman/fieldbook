## Purpose

Shows the screening an unsolicited proposal gets before any comprehensive evaluation, when it goes back to the offeror, when sole source negotiation may begin, and what the Government may not do with what was submitted.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-MKT-1347] |
| Codified citation | FAR 15.606-1, FAR 15.607 and FAR 15.608 [@CL-MKT-1347] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 15.504-1 [@CL-MKT-2292; @CL-MKT-2293; @CL-MKT-2294; @CL-MKT-2295; @CL-MKT-2296], RFO FAR 15.505(e) [@CL-MKT-2297; @CL-MKT-2298; @CL-MKT-2299], RFO FAR 15.506 [@CL-MKT-2300; @CL-MKT-2301] and RFO FAR 15.502(e) [@CL-MKT-2302; @CL-MKT-2303; @CL-MKT-2304; @CL-MKT-2305]; RFO FAR 15.500 [@CL-MKT-2098] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-MKT-1347] |
| Applies to | Unsolicited proposals submitted to an agency contact point [@CL-MKT-1347] |

Before initiating a comprehensive evaluation, the agency contact point must determine whether the proposal is a valid unsolicited proposal and whether it is suitable for submission in response to an existing agency requirement [@CL-MKT-1347]. It must also determine whether the proposal relates to the agency mission and contains sufficient technical information and cost-related or price-related information for evaluation [@CL-MKT-1348]. The remaining checks are whether the proposal has overall scientific, technical or socioeconomic merit, has been approved by a responsible official or other representative authorized to obligate the offeror contractually, and complies with the marking requirements [@CL-MKT-1349]. Together these are seven checks [@CL-MKT-1347; @CL-MKT-1348; @CL-MKT-1349].

If the proposal meets the requirements, the contact point must promptly acknowledge receipt and process it [@CL-MKT-1498]. If a proposal is rejected because it does not meet them, the contact point must promptly inform the offeror in writing of the reasons for rejection and of the proposed disposition [@CL-MKT-1350].

## How it works

The agency point of contact must return an unsolicited proposal to the offeror, citing reasons, when its substance is available to the Government without restriction from another source or closely resembles a pending competitive acquisition requirement [@CL-MKT-1499]. It must also return the proposal, citing reasons, when its substance does not relate to the activity's mission, does not demonstrate an innovative and unique method, approach or concept, or is otherwise not deemed a meritorious proposal [@CL-MKT-1500].

A favorable comprehensive evaluation does not, in itself, justify awarding a contract without full and open competition [@CL-MKT-1351]. The contracting officer may commence negotiations on a sole source basis only when, among other conditions, the proposal has received a favorable comprehensive evaluation and a justification and approval has been obtained [@CL-MKT-1352]. That approval is obtained as provided at FAR 6.302-1(a)(2)(i) for research proposals or other appropriate provisions of subpart 6.3, and at FAR 6.303-2(c) [@CL-MKT-1599]. The other two conditions are that the agency technical office sponsoring the contract furnishes the necessary funds [@CL-MKT-1501] and that the contracting officer has complied with the synopsis requirements of subpart 5.2 [@CL-MKT-1502].

Government personnel must not use any data, concept, idea or other part of an unsolicited proposal as the basis, or part of the basis, for a solicitation or in negotiations with any other firm unless the offeror is notified of and agrees to the intended use [@CL-MKT-1353]. This does not preclude using any data, concept or idea in the proposal that is also available from another source without restriction [@CL-MKT-1503]. Government personnel must not disclose restrictively marked information included in an unsolicited proposal [@CL-MKT-1504]. Disclosure of such information concerning trade secrets, processes, operations, style of work, apparatus and other matters, except as authorized by law, may result in criminal penalties under title 18 of the United States Code, section 1905 [@CL-MKT-1505]. The tests for a valid proposal, the content it should carry and the legend that marks protected data are on [Valid unsolicited proposals and agency points of contact](../../estimating/pricing/est-unsolicited-proposals-general.md), [Content of unsolicited proposals](../../estimating/pricing/est-unsolicited-proposal-content.md) and [Limited use of data in unsolicited proposals](../../estimating/pricing/est-unsolicited-proposal-data-legend.md); this card does not restate them.

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 15 text in place of the codified text under Class Deviation 2026-O0048, whose Revision 1 took effect immediately and supersedes the March 5, 2026 version [@CL-EST-3176] [@CL-EST-3177]. RFO FAR 15.504-1(a) says that before beginning a comprehensive evaluation, the agency contact point must determine if the proposal is a valid unsolicited proposal, as described at 15.503-1(b), and is suitable for submission in response to an existing agency requirement [@CL-MKT-2292; @CL-MKT-1347], is related to the agency mission and contains sufficient technical information and cost-related or price-related information for evaluation [@CL-MKT-2293; @CL-MKT-1348], and has overall scientific, technical, or socioeconomic merit, has been approved by a responsible official or other representative authorized to obligate the offeror contractually, and complies with the marking requirements of this subpart [@CL-MKT-2294; @CL-MKT-1349]. RFO FAR 15.504-1(b) says that if the proposal meets these requirements, the contact point must promptly acknowledge receipt and process it [@CL-MKT-2295; @CL-MKT-1498], and RFO FAR 15.504-1(c) says that if a proposal is rejected because it does not meet these requirements, the agency contact point must promptly inform the offeror in writing of the reasons for rejection and the proposed disposition [@CL-MKT-2296; @CL-MKT-1350]. RFO FAR 15.505(e) says a favorable evaluation of an unsolicited proposal does not, in itself, justify awarding a contract without providing for full and open competition, where the codified sentence says a favorable comprehensive evaluation [@CL-MKT-2297; @CL-MKT-1351]. It says the agency point of contact should return an unsolicited proposal to the offeror, citing reasons, when its substance is available to the Government without restriction from another source or closely resembles a pending competitive acquisition requirement, where the codified sentence says shall return [@CL-MKT-2298; @CL-MKT-1499], and lists as further circumstances that the substance does not relate to the activity's mission or does not demonstrate an innovative and unique method, approach, or concept, or is otherwise not deemed a meritorious proposal [@CL-MKT-2299; @CL-MKT-1500]. RFO FAR 15.506 says contracting officers may begin negotiations on a sole source basis only when, among other conditions, an unsolicited proposal receives a favorable comprehensive evaluation and a justification and approval is obtained in accordance with part 6, where the codified paragraph points to 6.302-1(a)(2)(i) for research proposals or other appropriate provisions of subpart 6.3, and 6.303-2(c) [@CL-MKT-2300; @CL-MKT-1352; @CL-MKT-1599]. It also lists as conditions that the agency technical office sponsoring the contract furnishes the necessary funds and that the requirements for synopsizing a proposed contract action are met [@CL-MKT-2301; @CL-MKT-1501; @CL-MKT-1502]. RFO FAR 15.502(e)(1) says Government personnel should not use any data, concept, idea, or other part of an unsolicited proposal as the basis, or part of the basis, for a RFP or in negotiations with any other firm unless the offeror is notified of and agrees to the intended use, where the codified paragraph says shall not [@CL-MKT-2302; @CL-MKT-1353], and that this prohibition does not preclude using any data, concept, or idea in the proposal that also is available from another source without restriction [@CL-MKT-2303; @CL-MKT-1503]. RFO FAR 15.502(e)(2) says Government personnel should not disclose restrictively marked information included in an unsolicited proposal [@CL-MKT-2304; @CL-MKT-1504], and that disclosure of such information concerning trade secrets, processes, operations, style of work, apparatus, and other matters, except as authorized by law, may result in criminal penalties under 18 U.S.C. 1905 [@CL-MKT-2305; @CL-MKT-1505]. RFO FAR 15.500 says that its subpart contains policies and procedures for the submission, receipt, evaluation, and acceptance or rejection of unsolicited proposals [@CL-MKT-2098].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A small firm sends a concept paper with a cost sketch for a test rig. Every page carries a proprietary legend, and an officer authorized to obligate the firm signed it. The agency contact point finds that the paper meets all seven checks before any comprehensive evaluation, so it acknowledges receipt and processes the proposal [@CL-MKT-1347; @CL-MKT-1348; @CL-MKT-1349; @CL-MKT-1498].

The evaluation is favorable, which does not by itself justify an award without full and open competition [@CL-MKT-1351]. The contracting officer begins sole source negotiation only after a justification and approval is obtained, the sponsoring technical office furnishes the funds and the synopsis requirements are met, in addition to the favorable evaluation [@CL-MKT-1352; @CL-MKT-1501; @CL-MKT-1502].

A competitor later asks for the concept. Government personnel may not use it as the basis, or part of the basis, for a solicitation or for negotiations with the competitor unless the offeror is notified of and agrees to the intended use, and may not disclose its restrictively marked content [@CL-MKT-1353; @CL-MKT-1504]. A different paper whose substance the Government already has without restriction from another source is returned to its sender with reasons [@CL-MKT-1499]. For DoD, under the RFO text, Government personnel should not use or disclose the concept, and the agency point of contact should return the other paper [@CL-EST-3176; @CL-MKT-2302; @CL-MKT-2304; @CL-MKT-2298].

## Never confuse

An unsolicited proposal is not a bid on an advertised requirement: it is screened for validity, mission fit and merit before any comprehensive evaluation [@CL-MKT-1347; @CL-MKT-1348; @CL-MKT-1349]. A favorable evaluation is not an award decision, because sole source negotiation still needs a justification and approval, funds from the sponsoring technical office and synopsis compliance [@CL-MKT-1351; @CL-MKT-1352; @CL-MKT-1501; @CL-MKT-1502]. The codified text says Government personnel shall not use proposal data as a basis for a solicitation or negotiations or disclose restrictively marked information, and that the agency point of contact shall return a proposal whose substance is available without restriction from another source [@CL-MKT-1353; @CL-MKT-1504; @CL-MKT-1499], while the RFO text says Government personnel should not use or disclose them and the agency point of contact should return it [@CL-MKT-2302; @CL-MKT-2304; @CL-MKT-2298].
