## Purpose

Explains what the multiyear method is for, how the agency must fund it, how cancellation differs from termination, and which clauses carry the cancellation and price adjustment terms.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-MKT-1354] |
| Codified citation | FAR 17.104 and FAR 17.109 [@CL-MKT-1354] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-MKT-1354] |
| Applies to | Multiyear contracts for known requirements, up to the year limit in the section unless a statute allows more [@CL-MKT-1354] |

Multiyear contracting is a special contracting method to acquire known requirements in quantities and total cost not over planned requirements for up to 5 years unless otherwise authorized by statute, even though the total funds ultimately to be obligated may not be available at the time of contract award [@CL-MKT-1354]. It may be used in sealed bidding or contracting by negotiation [@CL-MKT-1506]. The extent to which cancellation terms are used depends on the unique circumstances of each contract [@CL-MKT-1507]. For multiyear contracts, the agency head may authorize modification of the requirements of the subpart and of the cancellation clause [@CL-MKT-1355].

Agency funding of multiyear contracts must conform to the policies in OMB Circular A-11 and other applicable guidance [@CL-MKT-1508]. As provided by that guidance, the funds obligated must be sufficient to cover any potential cancellation and/or termination costs [@CL-MKT-9339], and multiyear contracts for the acquisition of fixed assets should be fully funded or funded in stages that are economically or programmatically viable [@CL-MKT-1356].

## How it works

The termination for convenience procedure may apply to any Government contract, including multiyear contracts [@CL-MKT-1509]. As contrasted with cancellation, termination can be effected at any time during the life of the contract and for the total or a partial quantity, while cancellation is effected between fiscal years and must be for all subsequent fiscal years' quantities [@CL-MKT-1357].

The contracting officer must insert the cancellation clause at FAR 52.217-2 in solicitations and contracts when a multiyear contract is contemplated [@CL-MKT-1358]. That is subject to the agency head's authority to authorize modification of the requirements of the subpart and of the clause [@CL-MKT-1355]. Under that clause, cancellation means that the Government is canceling its requirements for all supplies or services in program years subsequent to that in which notice of cancellation is provided [@CL-MKT-1595]. It occurs by the date or within the time period specified in the Schedule, unless a later date is agreed to, if the contracting officer notifies the contractor that funds are not available for contract performance for any subsequent program year [@CL-MKT-1596], or fails to notify the contractor that funds are available for performance of the succeeding program year requirement [@CL-MKT-1597]. Except for cancellation under that clause or termination under the Default clause, any reduction by the contracting officer in the requirements of the contract is considered a termination for convenience [@CL-MKT-1598].

Economic price adjustment clauses are adaptable to multiyear contracting needs [@CL-MKT-1510]. When the period of production is likely to warrant a labor and material costs contingency in the contract price, the contracting officer should normally use an economic price adjustment clause [@CL-MKT-1359]. When contracting for services, the contracting officer must add the clause at FAR 52.222-43 when the contract includes the clause at FAR 52.222-41, Service Contract Labor Standards [@CL-MKT-1511]. The contracting officer may modify the clause at FAR 52.222-43 in overseas contracts when laws, regulations or international agreements require contractors to pay higher wage rates [@CL-MKT-1512]. The contracting officer may also use an economic price adjustment clause authorized by FAR 16.203 when potential fluctuations require coverage and are not included in the cost contingencies provided for by the clause at FAR 52.222-43 [@CL-MKT-1513]. The cancellation ceilings and the clause mechanics are on [Cancellation under multiyear contracts clause](../../contract-finance/funding/ctf-multiyear-cancellation-clause.md), [Multiyear contracting: definition, term limit and approval conditions](../../industrialization/ramp/ind-multiyear-contracting.md) and [Multiyear cancellation ceilings and nonrecurring costs](../../industrialization/ramp/ind-multiyear-cancellation-ceilings.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contract buys 600 units over 3 years for known requirements, at a planned total of 9,000,000 [@CL-MKT-1354]. The Schedule sets the potential cancellation and termination costs for the later years at 2,500,000, and the first year obligates 3,000,000. The obligated funds are sufficient to cover those potential costs [@CL-MKT-1356]. A multiyear contract is contemplated and the agency head has not authorized modification, so the contracting officer inserts the cancellation clause [@CL-MKT-1358; @CL-MKT-1355]. The period of production is likely to warrant a labor and material costs contingency in the contract price, so the contracting officer normally uses an economic price adjustment clause [@CL-MKT-1359].

In the second year the contracting officer notifies the contractor that funds are not available for the third year, so the contract is canceled for the third year [@CL-MKT-1596]. Cancellation is effected between fiscal years and covers all subsequent fiscal years' quantities, here the third year only [@CL-MKT-1357; @CL-MKT-1595]. By contrast, a reduction by the contracting officer of half of the second-year quantity during that year would be a partial termination for convenience [@CL-MKT-1357; @CL-MKT-1509; @CL-MKT-1598].

## Never confuse

As contrasted with cancellation, termination can be effected at any time during the life of the contract and for the total or a partial quantity, whereas cancellation is effected between fiscal years and must be for all subsequent fiscal years' quantities [@CL-MKT-1357]. The cancellation clause is inserted when a multiyear contract is contemplated, subject to the agency head's authority to authorize modification of the requirements of the subpart and of the clause [@CL-MKT-1358; @CL-MKT-1355]. Except for cancellation under the cancellation clause—which occurs when the contracting officer notifies the contractor that funds are not available for any subsequent program year or fails to notify the contractor that funds are available—or termination under the Default clause, any reduction by the contracting officer in the requirements of the contract is considered a termination for convenience [@CL-MKT-1596; @CL-MKT-1597; @CL-MKT-1598].
