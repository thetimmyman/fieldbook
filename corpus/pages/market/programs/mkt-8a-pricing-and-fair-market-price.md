## Purpose

Shows who prices an 8(a) contract, how the fair market price is estimated, and why the SBA must concur, so the file can show that the price was set under the stated procedures [@CL-MKT-1265; @CL-MKT-1268].

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-MKT-1265] |
| Codified citation | FAR 19.806, FAR 19.807 and FAR 19.202-6 [@CL-MKT-1265; @CL-MKT-1366; @CL-MKT-1371] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-MKT-1265] |
| Applies to | 8(a) contracts, sole source or competitive [@CL-MKT-1266] |

The contracting officer prices the 8(a) contract in accordance with subpart 15.4, and the SBA obtains certified cost or pricing data from the 8(a) contractor where that subpart requires it [@CL-MKT-1265]. In a sole source acquisition, when the SBA asks for audit assistance to determine that the proposed price is fair and reasonable, the contracting activity furnishes it to the extent it is available [@CL-MKT-1365].

An 8(a) contract, sole source or competitive, may not be awarded if its price results in a cost to the contracting agency above a fair market price [@CL-MKT-1266]. The negotiated contract price and the estimated fair market price are both subject to the concurrence of the SBA, and if the contracting officer and the SBA disagree, the SBA may appeal in accordance with 19.810 [@CL-MKT-1268]. The appeal procedure is on the [subcontracting limits and SBA appeals card](mkt-8a-subcontracting-limits-and-appeals.md).

## How it works

The contracting officer estimates the fair market price of the work to be performed by the 8(a) contractor [@CL-MKT-1366]. For 8(a) contracts the contracting officer follows the 19.807 procedures both to meet the fair market price limit and to estimate the current fair market price accurately [@CL-MKT-1371]. On SBA request, the contracting officer makes the data used to estimate the fair market price available within 10 working days [@CL-MKT-1267].

For an acquisition other than a repeat purchase, the contracting officer uses cost or price analysis [@CL-MKT-1269]. The estimate also considers commercial prices for similar products and services, available in-house cost estimates, data (including certified cost or pricing data) submitted by the SBA or the 8(a) contractor, and data from any other Government agency [@CL-MKT-1270].

For a repeat purchase, the contracting officer considers recent award prices for the same items or work if quantities, conditions, terms and performance times are comparable [@CL-MKT-1271]. The estimated price should be adjusted for differences in specifications, plans, transportation costs, packaging and packing costs, and other circumstances [@CL-MKT-1367]. Price indices may be used as guides to changes in labor and material costs, and a comparison of commercial prices for similar items may also be used [@CL-MKT-1368].

Separately, the FAR ties the fair market price for other set-asides to a different standard. For total and partial small business set-asides and reserves, and for HUBZone set-asides, it is the price achieved under the reasonable price guidelines in 15.404-1(b) [@CL-MKT-1369]. The same guidelines apply to contracts using the HUBZone price evaluation preference, to set-asides for SDVOSB concerns eligible under the SDVOSB Program, and to set-asides for EDWOSB and WOSB concerns eligible under the WOSB Program [@CL-MKT-1370]. See [Small business set-asides](mkt-small-business-set-asides.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An agency proposes a sole-source 8(a) award at 1,200,000, and the contracting officer estimates the fair market price at 1,100,000 using cost or price analysis [@CL-MKT-1269]. The award price would cost the agency more than the fair market price, so the contract may not be awarded at that price [@CL-MKT-1266]. The parties negotiate 1,090,000. The negotiated price and the 1,100,000 estimate are still subject to the concurrence of the SBA, and the contracting officer gives the SBA the estimate data within 10 working days of its request [@CL-MKT-1268; @CL-MKT-1267]. If the SBA does not concur, it may appeal [@CL-MKT-1268].

## Never confuse

The fair market price limit applies to every 8(a) award, sole source or competitive [@CL-MKT-1266]. It is not the same as the reasonable price guidelines that set the fair market price for the other set-asides, because for 8(a) contracts the contracting officer follows the 19.807 procedures [@CL-MKT-1371; @CL-MKT-1369]. It is also separate from the offering and acceptance steps that come before pricing, which are on [the offering and acceptance card](mkt-8a-offering-and-acceptance.md).

## Company calibration

Whether the pricing file holds the cost or price analysis, the commercial and in-house comparisons and the data given to the SBA, and how the commercial scope of the products was defined. [@Q-019]
