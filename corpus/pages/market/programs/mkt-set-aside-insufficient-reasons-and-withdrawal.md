## Purpose

Lists the reasons that do not by themselves justify skipping a set-aside, and explains who may withdraw a set-aside before award, what happens to an unawarded set-aside, and the delivery schedule check before a new solicitation.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-MKT-1299] |
| Codified citation | FAR 19.502-5, FAR 19.502-9 and FAR 19.502-10 [@CL-MKT-1299; @CL-MKT-1303; @CL-MKT-1305] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-MKT-1299] |
| Applies to | Decisions not to set aside an acquisition, and withdrawal or dissolution of a small business set-aside [@CL-MKT-1299; @CL-MKT-1303; @CL-MKT-1305] |

None of the listed circumstances is, in itself, sufficient cause for not setting aside an acquisition [@CL-MKT-1299]. The listed circumstances are:

- A large percentage of previous contracts for the required items has been placed with small business concerns [@CL-MKT-1299].
- The item is on an established planning list under the Industrial Readiness Planning Program [@CL-MKT-1300].
- The item is on a Qualified Products List [@CL-MKT-1301].
- A period of less than 30 days is available for receipt of offers [@CL-MKT-1302].
- The acquisition is classified [@CL-MKT-1302].
- Small business concerns are already receiving a fair proportion of the agency's contracts for supplies and services [@CL-MKT-1302].
- A class small business set-aside of the item or service has been made by another contracting activity [@CL-MKT-1399].
- A brand name or equal product description will be used in the solicitation [@CL-MKT-1399].

Two of these carry a large-business exception. A total small business set-aside shall not be made when the planning list contains a large business Planned Emergency Producer of the item who has conveyed a desire to supply some or all of the required items [@CL-MKT-1300]. A total small business set-aside shall not be made if the Qualified Products List contains the products of large business, unless none of the large businesses desires to participate in the acquisition [@CL-MKT-1301].

## How it works

If, before award of a contract involving a total or partial small business set-aside, the contracting officer considers that award would be detrimental to the public interest, for example payment of more than a fair market price, the contracting officer may withdraw the set-aside, whether it was unilateral or joint [@CL-MKT-1303]. The contracting officer initiates the withdrawal by written notice, stating the reasons, to the agency small business specialist and the SBA procurement center representative or, if none is assigned, as provided in 19.402(a) [@CL-MKT-1304]. The contracting officer may modify a unilateral or joint class small business set-aside in a similar manner to withdraw one or more individual acquisitions [@CL-MKT-1400].

If the agency small business specialist does not agree to a withdrawal or modification, the case is promptly referred to the SBA procurement center representative or, if none is assigned, as provided in 19.402(a), for review [@CL-MKT-1401]. The contracting officer prepares a written statement supporting any withdrawal or modification and includes it in the contract file [@CL-MKT-1402].

If a set-aside acquisition, or a portion of one, is not awarded, the determination to set it aside is automatically dissolved for the unawarded portion [@CL-MKT-1305]. The required supplies or services for which no award was made may be acquired by sealed bidding or negotiation, as appropriate [@CL-MKT-1403]. Before issuing a solicitation for the items in a dissolved set-aside, the contracting officer ensures that the delivery schedule is realistic in the light of all relevant factors, including the capabilities of small business concerns [@CL-MKT-1404].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An agency plans a total small business set-aside, and the schedule allows only 20 days for offers. The item is also on a Qualified Products List that includes large business products, but no large business wants to participate. A period of less than 30 days is not in itself sufficient cause for skipping the set-aside, and because no large business wants to participate the list does not bar the total set-aside [@CL-MKT-1302; @CL-MKT-1301]. Later, before award, the contracting officer considers that award would cost more than a fair market price and withdraws the set-aside by written notice to the small business specialist and the SBA procurement center representative, stating the reasons [@CL-MKT-1303; @CL-MKT-1304]. If the specialist disagrees, the case goes to the SBA representative for review, and the contracting officer places a written statement in the contract file [@CL-MKT-1401; @CL-MKT-1402]. In a separate acquisition, one line item receives no award, so the set-aside is dissolved for that line item, and the contracting officer checks that the delivery schedule is realistic before a new solicitation [@CL-MKT-1305; @CL-MKT-1404].

## Never confuse

A reason that does not justify skipping a set-aside in advance is not the same as a ground for withdrawing one afterwards: the FAR example for withdrawal is a public interest concern such as paying more than a fair market price [@CL-MKT-1303]. Dissolution is also not withdrawal: it follows automatically when part of a set-aside is not awarded, and it applies only to the unawarded portion [@CL-MKT-1305]. The general set-aside rules are on [the set-aside card](mkt-small-business-set-asides.md).

## Company calibration

Whether the file records the specific reason an acquisition was not set aside and, where a set-aside was withdrawn, the notice and supporting statement. [@Q-003]
