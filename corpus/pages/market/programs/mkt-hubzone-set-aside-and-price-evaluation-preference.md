## Purpose

Gives the clause text of the two HUBZone notices: who may offer when a contract is set aside or awarded on a sole-source basis, and how the price evaluation preference changes evaluated prices. The policy rules are on the [HUBZone set-asides, sole-source awards and the price evaluation preference](mkt-hubzone-set-asides-and-price-preference.md) card.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-MKT-0984] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.219-3; FAR 52.219-4 [@CL-MKT-0984; @CL-MKT-0985] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.219-3; RFO FAR 52.219-4 [@CL-MKT-0143; @CL-MKT-0160] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-MKT-0984] |
| Applies to | The cases the set-aside clause lists, and offers evaluated under the preference clause [@CL-MKT-0982; @CL-MKT-1032; @CL-MKT-0985] |

A HUBZone small business concern means a small business concern, certified by the Small Business Administration (SBA), that appears on the List of Qualified HUBZone Small Business Concerns maintained by the SBA (13 CFR 126.103) [@CL-MKT-0984]. The set-aside notice clause at FAR 52.219-3 applies only to contracts that have been set aside, or awarded on a sole-source basis, to HUBZone small business concerns, to the part or parts of a multiple-award contract that have been set aside for HUBZone small business concerns [@CL-MKT-0982], to orders set aside for HUBZone small business concerns under multiple-award contracts as described in FAR 8.405-5 and 16.505(b)(2)(i)(F), and to orders issued directly to HUBZone small business concerns under multiple-award contracts as described in FAR 19.504(c)(1)(ii) [@CL-MKT-0982; @CL-MKT-1032]. Under that clause, offers are solicited only from HUBZone small business concerns, offers received from other concerns shall not be considered, and any award resulting from the solicitation will be made to a HUBZone small business concern [@CL-MKT-0983].

The codified introductory text of FAR 52.219-4 introduces the text as a clause [@CL-MKT-9336]. Under FAR 52.219-4, offers will be evaluated by adding a factor of 10 percent to the price of all offers, except offers from HUBZone small business concerns that have not waived the evaluation preference and otherwise successful offers from small business concerns [@CL-MKT-0985]. The factor of 10 percent shall be applied on a line item basis or to any group of items on which award may be made, and other evaluation factors described in the solicitation shall be applied before application of the factor [@CL-MKT-0986].

## How it works

A HUBZone small business concern may elect to waive the evaluation preference, in which case the factor will be added to its offer for evaluation purposes, and the clause provides a box for the offeror to mark that election [@CL-MKT-0987]. When the two highest rated offerors are a HUBZone small business concern and a large business, and the evaluated offer of the HUBZone small business concern is equal to the evaluated offer of the large business after considering the price evaluation preference, award will be made to the HUBZone small business concern [@CL-MKT-1036; @CL-MKT-1037].

A joint venture may be considered a HUBZone concern if at least one party to the joint venture is a HUBZone small business concern and complies with 13 CFR 126.616(c), and each party to the joint venture qualifies as small under the size standard for the solicitation, or the protégé is small under the size standard for the solicitation in a joint venture comprised of a mentor and protégé with an approved mentor-protégé agreement under the SBA mentor-protégé program [@CL-MKT-1033; @CL-MKT-1034]. Under both clauses, a HUBZone joint venture agrees that, in the performance of the contract, at least 40 percent of the aggregate work performed by the joint venture shall be completed by the HUBZone small business parties to the joint venture [@CL-MKT-1035; @CL-MKT-0988], and work performed by the HUBZone small business parties to the joint venture must be more than administrative functions [@CL-MKT-0989; @CL-MKT-1038]. The general limits on subcontracting are on the [Limitations on subcontracting and the nonmanufacturer rule](mkt-limitations-on-subcontracting-and-nonmanufacturer-rule.md) card.

Effective February 1, 2026, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text [@CL-CMP-9760; @CL-CMP-9762]. In RFO FAR 52.219-3, a HUBZone small business concern is a small business concern, certified by the SBA, that appears on the List of Qualified HUBZone Small Business Concerns maintained by the SBA (13 CFR 126.103) [@CL-MKT-0143]. RFO FAR 52.219-3 applies only to contracts that have been set aside, or awarded on a sole-source basis, to HUBZone small business concerns, to the part or parts of a multiple-award contract that have been set aside for HUBZone small business concerns, and to orders set aside for HUBZone small business concerns under multiple-award contracts as described in 8.4 and 16.5 [@CL-MKT-0144; @CL-MKT-0145]. Under RFO FAR 52.219-3, offers are solicited only from HUBZone small business concerns, offers received from other concerns shall not be considered, and any award resulting from the solicitation will be made to a HUBZone small business concern [@CL-MKT-0146]. Under RFO FAR 52.219-3(d), a joint venture may be considered a HUBZone concern if at least one party to the joint venture is a HUBZone small business concern and complies with 13 CFR 126.616(c), and each party to the joint venture qualifies as small under the size standard for the solicitation, or the protégé is small under the size standard for the solicitation in a joint venture comprised of a mentor and protégé with an approved mentor-protégé agreement under the SBA mentor-protégé program [@CL-MKT-0147; @CL-MKT-0148]. Under RFO FAR 52.219-3(e), a HUBZone joint venture agrees that, in the performance of the contract, at least 40 percent of the aggregate work performed by the joint venture shall be completed by the HUBZone small business parties to the joint venture, and work performed by the HUBZone small business parties to the joint venture must be more than administrative functions [@CL-MKT-0149; @CL-MKT-0159]. The introductory text of RFO FAR 52.219-4 introduces the text as a provision [@CL-MKT-0165]. Under RFO FAR 52.219-4(a)(1), offers will be evaluated by adding a factor of 10 percent to the price of all offers, except offers from HUBZone small business concerns that have not waived the evaluation preference and otherwise successful offers from small business concerns [@CL-MKT-0160]. In RFO FAR 52.219-4, paragraph (a)(1)(ii) is followed immediately by paragraph (b) on waiver of the evaluation preference [@CL-MKT-0161]. Under RFO FAR 52.219-4(b), a HUBZone small business concern may choose to waive the evaluation preference, and if the concern waives the preference, the factor will be added to its offer for evaluation purposes [@CL-MKT-0162]. Under RFO FAR 52.219-4(c), a HUBZone joint venture agrees that, in the performance of the contract, at least 40 percent of the aggregate work performed by the joint venture shall be completed by the HUBZone small business parties to the joint venture, and work performed by the HUBZone small business parties to the joint venture must be more than administrative functions [@CL-MKT-0163; @CL-MKT-0164].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An acquisition is evaluated under the preference clause, with no other evaluation factors. A HUBZone concern that has not waived the preference offers 4,000,000, and a large business offers 4,300,000. The factor adds 10 percent to the large business offer, giving 4,730,000, while the HUBZone offer stays at 4,000,000, so the HUBZone offer has the lower evaluated price. If the HUBZone concern had waived the preference, the factor would be added to its offer too, giving 4,400,000, still below 4,730,000. Under the set-aside clause the large business offer would not be considered at all.

## Never confuse

Under FAR 52.219-3, offers are solicited only from HUBZone small business concerns [@CL-MKT-0983], while under FAR 52.219-4, offers are evaluated by adding a factor of 10 percent to the price of all offers except offers from HUBZone small business concerns that have not waived the evaluation preference and otherwise successful offers from small business concerns [@CL-MKT-0985]. Under FAR 52.219-4(b), a HUBZone small business concern may elect to waive the evaluation preference, in which case the factor will be added to its offer for evaluation purposes [@CL-MKT-0987]. The general set-aside rules are on the [Small business set-asides](mkt-small-business-set-asides.md) card. Under codified FAR 52.219-4(a)(3), when the two highest rated offerors are a HUBZone small business concern and a large business and the evaluated offer of the HUBZone small business concern is equal to the evaluated offer of the large business after considering the price evaluation preference, award will be made to the HUBZone small business concern [@CL-MKT-1036; @CL-MKT-1037]. In RFO FAR 52.219-4, paragraph (a)(1)(ii) is followed immediately by paragraph (b) on waiver of the evaluation preference [@CL-MKT-0161].
