## Purpose

Gives the clause text of the two HUBZone notices: who may offer when a contract is set aside or awarded on a sole-source basis, and how the price evaluation preference changes evaluated prices. The policy rules are on the [HUBZone set-asides, sole-source awards and the price evaluation preference](mkt-hubzone-set-asides-and-price-preference.md) card.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-MKT-0984] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.219-3; FAR 52.219-4 [@CL-MKT-0984; @CL-MKT-0985] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-MKT-0984] |
| Applies to | The cases the set-aside clause lists, and offers evaluated under the preference clause [@CL-MKT-0982; @CL-MKT-1032; @CL-MKT-0985] |

A HUBZone small business concern is a small business concern, certified by the Small Business Administration (SBA), that appears on the List of Qualified HUBZone Small Business Concerns maintained by the SBA [@CL-MKT-0984]. The set-aside notice clause applies only to the listed cases, the first being contracts set aside, or awarded on a sole-source basis, to HUBZone small business concerns and the second being parts of a multiple-award contract set aside for them [@CL-MKT-0982]. It also applies to orders set aside for HUBZone small business concerns under multiple-award contracts and to orders issued directly to them [@CL-MKT-1032]. Under that clause, offers are solicited only from HUBZone small business concerns, offers from other concerns are not considered, and any award is made to a HUBZone small business concern [@CL-MKT-0983].

Under the preference clause, offers are evaluated by adding a factor of 10 percent to the price of all offers, except offers from HUBZone small business concerns that have not waived the evaluation preference and otherwise successful offers from small business concerns [@CL-MKT-0985]. The factor is applied on a line item basis or to any group of items on which award may be made, and the other evaluation factors in the solicitation are applied before it [@CL-MKT-0986].

## How it works

A HUBZone small business concern may elect to waive the evaluation preference, and the factor is then added to its own offer for evaluation purposes [@CL-MKT-0987]. The clause provides a box for the offeror to mark that election [@CL-MKT-0987]. If the two highest rated offerors are a HUBZone small business concern and a large business [@CL-MKT-1036], and the HUBZone concern's evaluated offer equals the large business's evaluated offer after the preference is considered, award is made to the HUBZone concern [@CL-MKT-1037].

A joint venture may be considered a HUBZone concern if at least one party to it is a HUBZone small business concern that complies with 13 CFR 126.616(c), and a second condition is also met [@CL-MKT-1033]. The second condition is that each party qualifies as small under the size standard for the solicitation or, in a joint venture of a mentor and protégé with an approved agreement under the SBA mentor-protégé program, that the protégé is small under that standard [@CL-MKT-1034]. Both clauses state the work share: a HUBZone joint venture agrees that at least 40 percent of the aggregate work performed by the joint venture shall be completed by its HUBZone small business parties [@CL-MKT-1035; @CL-MKT-0988]. Both also require that work to be more than administrative functions [@CL-MKT-0989; @CL-MKT-1038]. The general limits on subcontracting are on the [Limitations on subcontracting and the nonmanufacturer rule](mkt-limitations-on-subcontracting-and-nonmanufacturer-rule.md) card.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An acquisition is evaluated under the preference clause, with no other evaluation factors. A HUBZone concern that has not waived the preference offers 4,000,000, and a large business offers 4,300,000. The factor adds 10 percent to the large business offer, giving 4,730,000, while the HUBZone offer stays at 4,000,000, so the HUBZone offer has the lower evaluated price. If the HUBZone concern had waived the preference, the factor would be added to its offer too, giving 4,400,000, still below 4,730,000. Under the set-aside clause the large business offer would not be considered at all.

## Never confuse

The two notices are separate clauses. The set-aside clause limits who may offer [@CL-MKT-0983], while the preference clause adds a factor to prices in evaluation [@CL-MKT-0985]. A waiver changes only the preference: the factor is then added to the waiving concern's own offer [@CL-MKT-0987]. The general set-aside rules are on the [Small business set-asides](mkt-small-business-set-asides.md) card.
