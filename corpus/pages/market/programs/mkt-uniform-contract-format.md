## Purpose

Sets out the standard arrangement of a solicitation and the contract that follows it, so that each piece of the document sits where a reader expects it.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-MKT-1332] |
| Codified citation | FAR 15.204-1, FAR 15.204-2 and FAR 15.204-5 [@CL-MKT-1332] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-MKT-1332] |
| Applies to | Solicitations and resulting contracts prepared in the uniform contract format [@CL-MKT-1332] |

Contracting officers must prepare solicitations and resulting contracts using the uniform contract format [@CL-MKT-1332]. A solicitation in that format includes Parts I, II, III and IV, and on award the contracting officer does not physically include Part IV in the contract but retains it in the contract file [@CL-MKT-1333]. The representations and certifications are incorporated by reference in the contract by using FAR 52.204-19 or, for acquisitions of commercial products and commercial services, FAR 52.212-4(v) [@CL-MKT-1334].

Part I is the Schedule, made up of Sections A through H: solicitation or contract form, supplies or services and prices or costs, description or specifications or statement of work, packaging and marking, inspection and acceptance, deliveries or performance, contract administration data, and special contract requirements [@CL-MKT-1480]. Part II is Section I, contract clauses, and Part III is Section J, the list of documents, exhibits and other attachments [@CL-MKT-1481]. Part IV is Sections K, L and M: representations, certifications and other statements of offerors or respondents, instructions, conditions and notices to offerors or respondents, and evaluation factors for award [@CL-MKT-1482].

## How it works

The contracting officer prepares the Schedule. In Section A, Optional Form 308 or Standard Form 33 may be used to prepare requests for proposals [@CL-MKT-1483]. When a different form is used, the first page of the solicitation must give the issuing activity's name, address and location, including the room and building where proposals or information must be submitted, the solicitation number, and the date of issuance [@CL-MKT-1335]. It must also give the closing date and time, the number of pages, the requisition or other purchase authority, and a brief description of the item or service [@CL-MKT-1484]. Finally it must give the requirement for the offeror to provide its name and complete address, including electronic address if appropriate, and the offer expiration date [@CL-MKT-1485].

Section B holds a brief description of the supplies or services, such as item number, national stock number or part number if applicable, nouns, nomenclature and quantities [@CL-MKT-1486]. That description includes incidental deliverables such as manuals and reports [@CL-MKT-1601]. Section C holds any description or specifications needed in addition to Section B, and Section D holds packaging, packing, preservation and marking requirements, if any [@CL-MKT-1487]. Section E holds inspection, acceptance, quality assurance and reliability requirements [@CL-MKT-1488]. Section F specifies the requirements for time, place and method of delivery or performance [@CL-MKT-1489]. Section G holds any required accounting and appropriation data and any required contract administration information or instructions other than those on the solicitation form [@CL-MKT-1490]. It must also include a statement that the offeror should include the payment address in the proposal if it is different from the one shown for the offeror [@CL-MKT-1336]. Section H holds a clear statement of any special contract requirements not included in Section I or in other sections [@CL-MKT-1491].

In Part IV, Section K holds the solicitation provisions that require representations, certifications or other information from offerors [@CL-MKT-1337]. Section L holds the solicitation provisions and other information and instructions, not required elsewhere, that guide offerors or respondents in preparing proposals or responses to requests for information [@CL-MKT-1492]. They may be instructed to submit proposals or information in a specific format or severable parts to facilitate evaluation [@CL-MKT-1493]. The instructions may specify further organization, such as administrative, management, technical, past performance, and certified cost or pricing data or data other than certified cost or pricing data [@CL-MKT-1338].

Section M identifies all significant factors and any significant subfactors that will be considered in awarding the contract and their relative importance, and the contracting officer must insert one of the phrases in FAR 15.304(e) [@CL-MKT-1339]. Unless the exception at FAR 15.304(c)(1)(ii)(A) applies, the solicitation must also state, at a minimum, whether all evaluation factors other than cost or price, when combined, are significantly more important than, approximately equal to, or significantly less important than cost or price [@CL-MKT-1494].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A solicitation is issued on a form other than Optional Form 308 or Standard Form 33. Its first page gives the issuing office with the room and building for submissions, the solicitation number and issue date [@CL-MKT-1335]. It also gives the closing date and time, the page count, the purchase authority and a brief description of the item, the requirement for each offeror to give its name and full address, and the offer expiration date [@CL-MKT-1484; @CL-MKT-1485].

Section B lists twelve line items [@CL-MKT-1486]. Section G tells offerors to include a payment address in the proposal if it differs from the address shown for the offeror [@CL-MKT-1336]. Section L asks for three severable volumes and puts cost data in its own volume [@CL-MKT-1493; @CL-MKT-1338]. Section M lists the factors and subfactors and states that the non-price factors, when combined, are significantly more important than price [@CL-MKT-1339; @CL-MKT-1494]. On award, Part IV is kept in the contract file and is not bound into the contract, and the representations and certifications reach the contract by reference [@CL-MKT-1333; @CL-MKT-1334].

## Never confuse

Leaving Part IV out of the awarded contract is not a loss of information: the part stays in the contract file and the representations and certifications reach the contract by reference [@CL-MKT-1333; @CL-MKT-1334]. Section L tells offerors how to prepare proposals or responses; Section M tells them which factors and subfactors will be considered in the award and their relative importance [@CL-MKT-1492; @CL-MKT-1339]. Section G carries contract administration data, while Section K carries the representations and certifications that offerors give [@CL-MKT-1490; @CL-MKT-1337].
