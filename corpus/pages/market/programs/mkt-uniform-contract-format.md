## Purpose

Sets out the standard arrangement of a solicitation and the contract that follows it, so that each piece of the document sits where a reader expects it.

Contracting officers must prepare solicitations and resulting contracts using the uniform contract format [@CL-MKT-1332]. The format need not be used for construction and architect-engineer contracts or subsistence contracts [@CL-MKT-1602]. Nor need it be used for supplies or services contracts that require inconsistent special formats prescribed elsewhere in FAR part 15, for letter requests for proposals, or for contracts exempted by the agency head or designee [@CL-MKT-1603]. A solicitation in that format includes Parts I, II, III and IV, and on award the contracting officer does not physically include Part IV in the contract but retains it in the contract file [@CL-MKT-1333]. The representations and certifications are incorporated by reference in the contract by using FAR 52.204-19 or, for acquisitions of commercial products and commercial services, FAR 52.212-4(v) [@CL-MKT-1334].

Part I is the Schedule, made up of Sections A through H: solicitation or contract form, supplies or services and prices or costs, description or specifications or statement of work, packaging and marking, inspection and acceptance, deliveries or performance, contract administration data, and special contract requirements [@CL-MKT-1480]. Part II is Section I, contract clauses, and Part III is Section J, the list of documents, exhibits and other attachments [@CL-MKT-1481]. Part IV is Sections K, L and M: representations, certifications and other statements of offerors or respondents, instructions, conditions and notices to offerors or respondents, and evaluation factors for award [@CL-MKT-1482].

## How it works

The contracting officer prepares the Schedule. In Section A, Optional Form 308 or Standard Form 33 may be used to prepare requests for proposals [@CL-MKT-1483]. When a different form is used, the first page of the solicitation must give the issuing activity's name, address and location, including the room and building where proposals or information must be submitted, the solicitation number, and the date of issuance [@CL-MKT-1335]. It must also give the closing date and time, the number of pages, the requisition or other purchase authority, and a brief description of the item or service [@CL-MKT-1484]. Finally it must give the requirement for the offeror to provide its name and complete address, including electronic address if appropriate, and the offer expiration date [@CL-MKT-1485].

Section B holds a brief description of the supplies or services, such as item number, national stock number or part number if applicable, nouns, nomenclature and quantities [@CL-MKT-1486]. That description includes incidental deliverables such as manuals and reports [@CL-MKT-1601]. Section C holds any description or specifications needed in addition to Section B, and Section D holds packaging, packing, preservation and marking requirements, if any [@CL-MKT-1487]. Section E holds inspection, acceptance, quality assurance and reliability requirements [@CL-MKT-1488]. Section F specifies the requirements for time, place and method of delivery or performance [@CL-MKT-1489]. Section G holds any required accounting and appropriation data and any required contract administration information or instructions other than those on the solicitation form [@CL-MKT-1490]. It must also include a statement that the offeror should include the payment address in the proposal if it is different from the one shown for the offeror [@CL-MKT-1336]. Section H holds a clear statement of any special contract requirements not included in Section I or in other sections [@CL-MKT-1491]. In Section J, the contracting officer shall list the title, date, and number of pages for each attached document, exhibit, and other attachment [@CL-MKT-2095], and cross-references to material in other sections may be inserted, as appropriate [@CL-MKT-2096].

In Part IV, Section K holds the solicitation provisions that require representations, certifications or other information from offerors [@CL-MKT-1337]. Section L holds the solicitation provisions and other information and instructions, not required elsewhere, that guide offerors or respondents in preparing proposals or responses to requests for information [@CL-MKT-1492]. They may be instructed to submit proposals or information in a specific format or severable parts to facilitate evaluation [@CL-MKT-1493]. The instructions may specify further organization, such as administrative, management, technical, past performance, and certified cost or pricing data or data other than certified cost or pricing data [@CL-MKT-1338].

Section M identifies all significant factors and any significant subfactors that will be considered in awarding the contract and their relative importance, and the contracting officer must insert one of the phrases in FAR 15.304(e) [@CL-MKT-1339]. Unless the exception at FAR 15.304(c)(1)(ii)(A) applies, the solicitation must also state, at a minimum, whether all evaluation factors other than cost or price, when combined, are significantly more important than, approximately equal to, or significantly less important than cost or price [@CL-MKT-1494; @CL-EST-1309].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 15 text in place of the codified text under Class Deviation 2026-O0048, whose Revision 1 took effect immediately and supersedes the March 5, 2026 version [@CL-EST-3176] [@CL-EST-3177]. RFO FAR 15.102(b)(1) says contracting officers should prepare RFPs and resulting contracts using the uniform contract format at 15.109, where the codified paragraph says contracting officers shall prepare solicitations and resulting contracts using the format [@CL-MKT-2271; @CL-MKT-1332]. RFO FAR 15.102(b)(2) says agencies do not need to use the format for construction and architect-engineer contracts or for subsistence contracts [@CL-MKT-2272; @CL-MKT-1602], and also not for supplies or services contracts requiring special contract formats prescribed elsewhere in part 15 that are inconsistent with the format, for streamlined RFPs, or for contracts exempted by the agency head or designee, where the codified list names letter requests for proposals instead of streamlined RFPs [@CL-MKT-2273; @CL-MKT-1603]. RFO FAR 15.109 says RFPs using the format should include Parts I, II, III, and IV and that upon award contracting officers should not physically include Part IV in the resulting contract but should retain it in the contract file, where the codified paragraph says shall [@CL-MKT-2274; @CL-MKT-1333], and says the representations and certifications in Part IV are incorporated by reference in the contract through FAR clauses 52.204-19 and 52.212-4, where the codified paragraph distinguishes 52.204-19 from 52.212-4(v) for acquisitions of commercial products and commercial services [@CL-MKT-2275; @CL-MKT-1334]. RFO FAR 15.109-1 is headed Part I - The Schedule and begins with Section A, Solicitation/contract form [@CL-MKT-2276; @CL-MKT-1480], and RFO FAR 15.109-1(a)(1) says agencies may use OF 308 or SF 33 to prepare RFPs [@CL-MKT-2244; @CL-MKT-1483]. When other than OF 308 or SF 33 is used, RFO FAR 15.109-1(a)(2) says the first page of the RFP is to include the name, address, and location of the issuing activity, including room and building where proposals or information must be submitted, the RFP number, and the date of issuance [@CL-MKT-2277; @CL-MKT-1335], also the closing date and time, the number of RFP pages, the requisition or other purchase authority, and a brief description of the item or service [@CL-MKT-2278; @CL-MKT-1484], and also the requirement for the offeror to provide its name and complete address, including street, city, county, state, and zip code, and email address if appropriate, and the offer expiration date, where the codified paragraph says electronic address (including facsimile address) [@CL-MKT-2279; @CL-MKT-1485]. RFO FAR 15.109-1(b) says Section B, Supplies or services and prices/costs, is to include a brief description of the supplies or services, including any incidental deliverables, with no examples of the description or of incidental deliverables, where the codified paragraph gives item number, national stock number or part number, nouns, nomenclature and quantities, and manuals and reports as examples [@CL-MKT-2280; @CL-MKT-1486; @CL-MKT-1601]. RFO FAR 15.109-1(c)-(d) say Section C, Description/specifications/requirements, is to include any description or specifications needed in addition to Section B, where the codified heading reads Description/specifications/statement of work, and Section D, Packaging and marking, is to include packaging, packing, preservation, and marking requirements, if any [@CL-MKT-2281; @CL-MKT-1480; @CL-MKT-1487]. RFO FAR 15.109-1(e)-(f) say Section E, Inspection and acceptance, is to include inspection, acceptance, quality assurance, and reliability requirements, and Section F, Deliveries or performance, is to include the requirements for time, place, and method of delivery or performance [@CL-MKT-2282; @CL-MKT-1488; @CL-MKT-1489]. RFO FAR 15.109-1(g) says Section G, Contract administration data, is to include any required accounting and appropriation data and any required contract administration information or instructions not included in other sections of the uniform contract format, where the codified paragraph says other than those on the solicitation form [@CL-MKT-2283; @CL-MKT-1490], and to include a statement that the offeror should include the payment address in the proposal, if it is different from that shown for the offeror [@CL-MKT-2284; @CL-MKT-1336]. RFO FAR 15.109-1(h) says Section H, Special contract requirements, is to include a clear statement of any special contract requirements that are not included in Section I or in other sections of the uniform contract format [@CL-MKT-2285; @CL-MKT-1491]. RFO FAR 15.109-2 is headed Part II - Contract Clauses and covers Section I, Contract clauses [@CL-MKT-2247; @CL-MKT-1481], and RFO FAR 15.109-3 is headed Part III - List of Documents, Exhibits, and Other Attachments and covers Section J, List of attachments [@CL-MKT-2286; @CL-MKT-1481]. RFO FAR 15.109-4(a) says Section K, Representations, certifications, and other statements of offerors, is to include RFP provisions that require representations, certifications, or the submission of other information by offerors [@CL-MKT-2287; @CL-MKT-1337]. RFO FAR 15.109-4(b) says Section L, Instructions, conditions, and notices to offerors or respondents, is to include solicitation provisions and other information and instructions not required elsewhere to guide offerors in preparing proposals, where the codified paragraph also covers responses to requests for information [@CL-MKT-2288; @CL-MKT-1492]. RFO FAR 15.109-4(b)(1) also says Section L is to include the method(s) offerors may use to submit a proposal [@CL-MKT-2307]. RFO FAR 15.109-4(b)(2) says offerors may be instructed to submit proposals or information in a specific format or severable parts to facilitate evaluation [@CL-MKT-2289; @CL-MKT-1493], and that agencies may also require the proposal to be further organized into sections such as administrative, management, technical, past performance, and certified cost and pricing data or data other than certified cost and pricing data [@CL-MKT-2290; @CL-MKT-1338]. RFO FAR 15.109-4(c) says Section M, Evaluation factors for award, is to identify all significant factors and any significant subfactors that will be considered in awarding the contract, their relative importance, and one of the phrases in 15.104(d), as required, where the codified paragraph refers to the phrases in 15.304(e) [@CL-MKT-2291; @CL-MKT-1339]. RFO FAR 15.104(d) says that, unless the exception at paragraph (b)(1)(ii) of that section applies, the RFP must also state, at a minimum, whether all evaluation factors other than cost or price, when combined, are significantly more important than cost or price [@CL-MKT-2265; @CL-MKT-1494], approximately equal to cost or price, or significantly less important than cost or price [@CL-MKT-2266; @CL-MKT-1494; @CL-EST-1309]. The exception at RFO FAR 15.104(b)(1)(ii)(A) says that, for DoD, NASA, and the Coast Guard, contracting officers may choose not to include price or cost as an evaluation factor for award when an RFP has an estimated value above the simplified acquisition threshold, will result in multiple-award contracts for the same or similar services, and states that the Government intends to make an award to each and all qualifying offerors, where the codified text points to the exception at FAR 15.304(c)(1)(ii)(A) [@CL-MKT-2308; @CL-MKT-2309; @CL-MKT-1494]. RFO FAR 15.100 says that its subpart addresses policies and procedures for preparing requests for proposals (RFPs) and receiving proposals, and that RFPs are used in negotiated acquisitions to communicate Government requirements to prospective contractors and to request proposals [@CL-MKT-2094].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A supplies solicitation that falls in none of the excepted categories uses the uniform contract format [@CL-MKT-1332; @CL-MKT-1602; @CL-MKT-1603]. It is issued on a form other than Optional Form 308 or Standard Form 33. Its first page gives the issuing office with the room and building for submissions, the solicitation number and issue date [@CL-MKT-1335]. It also gives the closing date and time, the page count, the purchase authority and a brief description of the item, the requirement for each offeror to give its name and full address, and the offer expiration date [@CL-MKT-1484; @CL-MKT-1485].

Section B lists twelve line items [@CL-MKT-1486]. Section G tells offerors to include a payment address in the proposal if it differs from the address shown for the offeror [@CL-MKT-1336]. Section L asks for three severable volumes and puts cost data in its own volume [@CL-MKT-1493; @CL-MKT-1338]. Section M lists the factors and subfactors and states that the non-price factors, when combined, are significantly more important than price [@CL-MKT-1339; @CL-MKT-1494]. On award, Part IV is kept in the contract file and is not bound into the contract, and the representations and certifications reach the contract by reference [@CL-MKT-1333; @CL-MKT-1334]. For DoD, under the RFO text, contracting officers should use the format and should retain Part IV in the contract file [@CL-EST-3176; @CL-MKT-2271; @CL-MKT-2274].

## Never confuse

A solicitation in an excepted category, such as a construction contract, is not noncompliant for using another format [@CL-MKT-1602]. Leaving Part IV out of the awarded contract is not a loss of information: the part stays in the contract file and the representations and certifications reach the contract by reference [@CL-MKT-1333; @CL-MKT-1334]. Section L tells offerors how to prepare proposals or responses; Section M tells them which factors and subfactors will be considered in the award and their relative importance [@CL-MKT-1492; @CL-MKT-1339]. Section G carries contract administration data, while Section K carries the representations and certifications that offerors give [@CL-MKT-1490; @CL-MKT-1337]. The codified text says contracting officers shall prepare solicitations and resulting contracts using the uniform contract format and shall not physically include Part IV in the resulting contract [@CL-MKT-1332; @CL-MKT-1333], while the RFO text says they should [@CL-MKT-2271; @CL-MKT-2274].
