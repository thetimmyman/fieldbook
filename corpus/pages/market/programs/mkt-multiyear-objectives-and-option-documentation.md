## Purpose

Shows what multiyear contracting is encouraged to achieve, what the contracting officer must put in writing about an option, and when offers for option quantities or periods must be evaluated in awarding the basic contract.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-MKT-1715] |
| Codified citation | FAR 17.105-2, 17.205 and 17.206 [@CL-MKT-1715; @CL-MKT-1716; @CL-MKT-1718] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-MKT-1715] |
| Applies to | Multiyear contracting objectives, and solicitations and contracts that contain options [@CL-MKT-1715; @CL-MKT-1716] |

Use of multiyear contracting is encouraged to take advantage of one or more of the benefits that the objectives section lists [@CL-MKT-1715]. The listed benefits are lower costs, enhanced standardization, and reduced administrative burden in placing and administering contracts [@CL-MKT-1715]; substantial continuity of production or performance, which avoids annual startup costs, preproduction testing costs, make-ready expenses and phaseout costs [@CL-MKT-1765]; stabilization of contractor work forces [@CL-MKT-1765]; avoiding the need to establish quality control techniques and procedures for a new contractor each year [@CL-MKT-1766]; broadening the competitive base to firms not otherwise willing or able to compete for lesser quantities, particularly where startup costs are high [@CL-MKT-1767]; and incentives for contractors to improve productivity through investment in capital facilities, equipment and advanced technology [@CL-MKT-1768].

The contracting officer shall justify in writing the quantities or the term under option, the notification period for exercising the option, and any limitation on option price under FAR 17.203(g), and shall include the justification document in the contract file [@CL-MKT-1716]. Any justifications and approvals and any determination and findings required by FAR part 6 shall specify both the basic requirement and the increase permitted by the option [@CL-MKT-1717].

In awarding the basic contract, the contracting officer shall, except as provided in paragraph (b) of the evaluation section, evaluate offers for any option quantities or periods contained in a solicitation when it has been determined before soliciting offers that the Government is likely to exercise the options [@CL-MKT-1769; @CL-MKT-1718]. The contracting officer need not evaluate offers for any option quantities when it is determined that evaluation would not be in the best interests of the Government and this determination is approved at a level above the contracting officer [@CL-MKT-1719]. A reasonable certainty that funds will be unavailable to permit exercise of the option is an example of a circumstance that may support such a determination [@CL-MKT-1770].

## How it works

The objectives paragraph says multiyear contracting is encouraged for the listed benefits [@CL-MKT-1715]. The method, its limits and its clauses are on the linked multiyear card.

The documentation duty falls on the contracting officer: a written justification of the option quantities or term, the notification period and any option price limitation, kept in the contract file [@CL-MKT-1716]. Where part 6 requires a justification and approval or a determination and findings, it must state both the basic requirement and the increase the option permits [@CL-MKT-1717].

The evaluation duty also falls on the contracting officer and arises in awarding the basic contract. It is triggered by a determination, made before offers are solicited, that the Government is likely to exercise the options [@CL-MKT-1769; @CL-MKT-1718]. The best-interests determination does need approval above the contracting officer [@CL-MKT-1719].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A solicitation asks for a base quantity of 1,000 units and an option for 400 more. Before issuing it, the agency determines that it is likely to exercise the option. In awarding the basic contract, the contracting officer evaluates the offers for the 400-unit option quantity [@CL-MKT-1769; @CL-MKT-1718].

The contracting officer writes a justification covering the 400-unit option quantity, the 30-day notice period for exercise and any limit on option price, and places it in the contract file [@CL-MKT-1716]. If a part 6 justification and approval is needed, it states both the 1,000-unit requirement and the 400-unit increase [@CL-MKT-1717].

Suppose instead that funds for the option are reasonably certain to be unavailable. That circumstance may support a determination that evaluating the option offers would not be in the Government's best interests, and the contracting officer need not evaluate them once that determination is approved at a level above the contracting officer [@CL-MKT-1719; @CL-MKT-1770].

## Never confuse

Multiyear contracting commits to multi-year planned requirements up front to capture standardization and lower costs [@CL-MKT-1715], whereas options give the Government a unilateral right justified and evaluated at solicitation [@CL-MKT-1716; @CL-MKT-1718] rather than binding the Government until exercised under [Exercise of options](../../contract-finance/funding/ctf-exercise-of-options.md) [@CL-CTF-1311].
