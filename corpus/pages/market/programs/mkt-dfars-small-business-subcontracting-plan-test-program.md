## Purpose

Shows who takes part in the Test Program, how participants report, how the failure to meet plan goals is handled, and how this clause differs from the individual plan clause.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-MKT-0908] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 252.219-7004 [@CL-MKT-0908] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-MKT-0908] |
| Applies to | Contractors whose comprehensive subcontracting plan is approved under the Test Program, where the clause is prescribed in DFARS 219.708(b)(1)(B) [@CL-MKT-0908; @CL-MKT-0910; @CL-MKT-0947] |

The contractor's comprehensive small business subcontracting plan and its successors, authorized by and approved under the Test Program, shall be included in and made a part of the contract [@CL-MKT-0908]. To become and remain eligible, a business concern is required to have furnished supplies or services, including construction, under at least three DoD contracts during the preceding fiscal year, with an aggregate value of at least $100 million [@CL-MKT-0910]. Upon expulsion from or expiration of the Test Program, the contractor shall negotiate an individual subcontracting plan for all future contracts that meet the requirements of the cited statute [@CL-MKT-0909].

The contractor shall report semiannually for the 6-month periods ending March 31 and September 30, within 30 days after the end of the reporting period, on the items in the listed paragraphs [@CL-MKT-0911]. Item (ii) of that paragraph covers the amount of first-tier subcontract dollars awarded during the 6-month period covered by the report to covered small business concerns, set out separately by listed categories, beginning with the NAICS code [@CL-MKT-0959]. One listed breakdown is by contract number, if the contract is for maintenance, overhaul, repair, servicing, rehabilitation, salvage, modernization, or modification of supplies, systems, or equipment, and the total value of the contract, including options, exceeds $100 million [@CL-MKT-0960; @CL-MKT-0959].

Under the clause, the Summary Subcontract Report encompasses all subcontracting under prime contracts and subcontracts with the Department of Defense, regardless of the dollar value of the subcontracts, and is based on the negotiated comprehensive plan [@CL-MKT-0913]. Only subcontracts involving performance in the U.S. or its outlying areas should be included in these reports [@CL-MKT-0981]. As printed, the paragraph on Summary Subcontract Reports says the report shall be submitted semiannually for the six months ending March 31 and the twelve months ending September 30, and that reports are due 30 days after the close of each reporting period [@CL-MKT-0914]. The authority to acknowledge receipt of or reject the Summary Subcontract Report resides with the Defense Contract Management Agency [@CL-MKT-0915].

## How it works

The Summary Subcontract Report leaves out purchases from affiliates of the prime contractor or subcontractor, and its award data is limited to awards made to immediate next-tier subcontractors [@CL-MKT-0912]. Credit cannot be taken for awards made to lower-tier subcontractors unless the contractor or subcontractor has been designated to receive a small business or small disadvantaged business credit from a member firm of the Alaska Native Corporations or an Indian tribe [@CL-MKT-0970]. This card quotes the periods in the Summary Subcontract Report paragraph as printed, which read differently from the 6-month periods in the first reporting paragraph, and it does not reconcile them [@CL-MKT-0914; @CL-MKT-0911].

The failure of the contractor or a subcontractor to comply in good faith with the Utilization of Small Business Concerns clause, or with an approved plan required by the Test Program clause, is a material breach of the contract [@CL-MKT-0916]. If the contractor has failed to meet its approved subcontracting goals, the contracting officer will provide the contractor written notice specifying the failure and advising of the potential for assessment of liquidated damages [@CL-MKT-0961]. The notice permits the contractor to demonstrate what good faith efforts have been made, and the contracting officer may take the contractor's failure to respond within 15 working days, or a longer period at the contracting officer's discretion, as an admission that no valid explanation exists [@CL-MKT-0962; @CL-MKT-0961]. Failure to make a good faith effort means a willful or intentional failure to perform in accordance with the requirements of the approved plan or willful or intentional action to frustrate the plan [@CL-MKT-0964]. If the contracting officer determines, after considering all relevant information, that the contractor failed to make a good faith effort to comply with the plan, the officer issues a final decision to that effect [@CL-MKT-0917]. The contracting officer also requires the contractor to pay liquidated damages to the Government in the amount identified in the plan [@CL-MKT-0918; @CL-MKT-0917]. The contractor has the right of appeal under the Disputes clause of the contract from any final decision of the contracting officer in the liquidated damages paragraph [@CL-MKT-0965].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A Test Program participant has one comprehensive plan covering all its DoD work. For a reporting period ending September 30, its subcontract awards total 200 million, and 50 million went to covered small business concerns. Its negotiated goal was 30 percent of dollars.

Answer: the report is due within 30 days after the period closes, and the Defense Contract Management Agency accepts or rejects it. Fifty million is 25 percent of 200 million, below the goal, so the contracting officer gives written notice of the shortfall and lets the participant show its good faith efforts. If the contracting officer later decides the shortfall came from a failure to make a good faith effort, the final decision requires the liquidated damages set in the plan, and the participant may appeal under the Disputes clause.

## Never confuse

The individual plan clause sends the Individual Subcontract Report to the contracting officer at the procuring contracting office and the consolidated Summary Subcontract Report to the Department of Defense [@CL-MKT-0898; @CL-MKT-0899], and the SSR Coordinator accepts or rejects Summary Subcontract Reports [@CL-MKT-0901]. Under this clause the Summary Subcontract Report covers all DoD subcontracting under the comprehensive plan [@CL-MKT-0913], limited to subcontracts involving performance in the U.S. or its outlying areas [@CL-MKT-0981], and the Defense Contract Management Agency accepts or rejects it [@CL-MKT-0915]. The individual plan clause also directs the contractor to include this clause in subcontracts with Test Program participants where the condition on the subcontract is met [@CL-MKT-0902; @CL-MKT-0903]. The individual plan clause is on the [DFARS small business subcontracting plan clause for DoD contracts](mkt-dfars-dod-small-business-subcontracting-plan.md) card. The FAR plan clause rules are on the [scope and required elements](mkt-subcontracting-plan-clause-scope-and-required-elements.md) card and the [master and commercial plans, reporting and flowdown](mkt-subcontracting-plan-clause-master-commercial-reporting-flowdown.md) card, and good faith effort in the FAR is on the [Subcontracting plan content, good faith effort and liquidated damages](mkt-subcontracting-plan-content-and-compliance.md) card.
