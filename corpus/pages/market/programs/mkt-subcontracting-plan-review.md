## Purpose

Shows what the contracting officer checks when reviewing a subcontracting plan, including the goals and the link to the offeror's past performance.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-MKT-0443] |
| Codified citation | FAR 19.705-4 [@CL-MKT-0443; @CL-MKT-0444] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-MKT-0443] |
| Applies to | Each proposed subcontracting plan that the contracting officer reviews [@CL-MKT-0443] |

The contracting officer shall review the subcontracting plan for adequacy, ensuring that the required information, goals and assurances are included [@CL-MKT-0443]. In negotiated acquisitions, the contracting officer shall determine whether the plan is acceptable based on the negotiation of each of the 15 elements of the plan [@CL-MKT-0444]. If, under a sealed bid solicitation, a bidder submits a plan that does not cover each of the 15 required elements, the contracting officer shall advise the bidder of the deficiency and request submission of a revised plan by a specific date [@CL-MKT-0557]. If the bidder does not submit a plan that incorporates the required elements within the time allotted, the bidder shall be ineligible for award [@CL-MKT-0558].

Subcontracting goals should be set at a level the parties reasonably expect can result from the offeror expending good faith efforts to use small business subcontractors, among the listed categories of concerns [@CL-MKT-0445]. The contracting officer shall take particular care to ensure that the offeror has not submitted unreasonably low goals to minimize exposure to liquidated damages and to avoid the burden of substantiating good faith efforts [@CL-MKT-0446].

## How it works

In determining whether a proposed plan is acceptable, the contracting officer should obtain information available from the cognizant contract administration office and evaluate the offeror's past performance [@CL-MKT-0447]. The past performance to evaluate is the offeror's past performance in awarding subcontracts for the same or similar products or services to the listed small business categories, but if information is not available on a specific type of product or service, the offeror's overall past performance is evaluated and the performance of other contractors on similar efforts is considered [@CL-MKT-0556; @CL-MKT-9332]. The office that supplies that information is described on the [Responsibilities of the administrative contracting officer for subcontracting plans](mkt-subcontracting-plan-aco-responsibilities.md) card. One listed action is to ensure that the goals offered are attainable in relation to the subcontracting opportunities available to the contractor and the pool of eligible subcontractors, among other listed items [@CL-MKT-0448]. Goals are tested for attainability against listed factors that include the opportunities and the subcontractor pool available [@CL-MKT-0448], while past performance is evaluated as a separate listed action [@CL-MKT-0447]. This card does not restate what a plan must contain, which is on the [Subcontracting plan content, good faith effort and liquidated damages](mkt-subcontracting-plan-content-and-compliance.md) card, and it does not draft the other listed actions on cost data, make-or-buy policy, subcontracting potential, sources of information and SBA advice. The step after review, award, is on the [Awards involving subcontracting plans](mkt-subcontracting-plan-awards.md) card.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An offeror proposes a small business goal of 5 percent of its subcontract dollars. Its records on its last two similar contracts show 20 percent. The contracting officer gets past performance information from the contract administration office, finds that the goal looks unreasonably low, and asks the offeror to justify it or raise it.

## Never confuse

Reviewing a plan is separate from deciding whether one is required and from judging compliance after award, which are on the [When a small business subcontracting plan is required](mkt-subcontracting-plan-when-required.md) card and the [Subcontracting plan content, good faith effort and liquidated damages](mkt-subcontracting-plan-content-and-compliance.md) card. A low goal is not a safe goal, because the contracting officer must take particular care to catch unreasonably low goals [@CL-MKT-0446]. The adequacy review covers the required information, goals and assurances [@CL-MKT-0443], while in negotiated acquisitions acceptability rests on the negotiation of the 15 elements [@CL-MKT-0444]. Under sealed bidding the contracting officer instead asks a bidder with a deficient plan for a revised plan by a specific date [@CL-MKT-0557].

## Company calibration

Whether the company sets its subcontracting goals from past subcontract records, and who prepares the plan for the contracting officer's review. [@Q-040]
