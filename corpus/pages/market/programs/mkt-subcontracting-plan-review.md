## Purpose

Shows what the contracting officer checks when reviewing a subcontracting plan, including the goals and the link to the offeror's past performance.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-MKT-0443] |
| Codified citation | FAR 19.705-4 [@CL-MKT-0443; @CL-MKT-0444] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-MKT-0443] |
| Applies to | Each proposed subcontracting plan that the contracting officer reviews [@CL-MKT-0443] |

The contracting officer shall review the subcontracting plan for adequacy, ensuring that the required information, goals and assurances are included [@CL-MKT-0443]. In negotiated acquisitions, the contracting officer shall determine whether the plan is acceptable based on the negotiation of each of the 15 elements of the plan [@CL-MKT-0444]. If, under a sealed bid solicitation, a bidder submits a plan that does not cover each of the 15 required elements, the contracting officer shall advise the bidder of the deficiency and request submission of a revised plan by a specific date [@CL-MKT-0557]. If the bidder does not submit a plan that incorporates the required elements within the time allotted, the bidder shall be ineligible for award [@CL-MKT-0558].

Subcontracting goals should be set at a level that the parties reasonably expect can result from the offeror expending good faith efforts to use small business, veteran-owned small business, service-disabled veteran-owned small business, HUBZone small business, small disadvantaged business, and women-owned small business subcontractors to the maximum practicable extent [@CL-MKT-0445; @CL-MKT-9355]. The contracting officer shall take particular care to ensure that the offeror has not submitted unreasonably low goals to minimize exposure to liquidated damages and to avoid the administrative burden of substantiating good faith efforts [@CL-MKT-0446].

## How it works

In determining the acceptability of a proposed subcontracting plan, the contracting officer should obtain information available from the cognizant contract administration office, as provided for in FAR 19.706(a), and evaluate the offeror's past performance in awarding subcontracts for the same or similar products or services to small business, veteran-owned small business, service-disabled veteran-owned small business, HUBZone small business, small disadvantaged business, and women-owned small business concerns [@CL-MKT-0447; @CL-MKT-0556]. If information is not available on a specific type of product or service, the contracting officer evaluates the offeror's overall past performance and considers the performance of other contractors on similar efforts [@CL-MKT-9332]. The contract administration office responsibilities are on the [Responsibilities of the administrative contracting officer for subcontracting plans](mkt-subcontracting-plan-aco-responsibilities.md) card. In accordance with title 15 of the United States Code, section 637(d)(4)(F)(iii), the contracting officer should also ensure that the goals offered are attainable in relation to the subcontracting opportunities available to the contractor, commensurate with the efficient and economical performance of the contract, the pool of eligible subcontractors available to fulfill the subcontracting opportunities, and the offeror's actual performance in fulfilling the subcontracting goals specified in prior plans [@CL-MKT-0447; @CL-MKT-0448; @CL-MKT-1998]. What a subcontracting plan must contain is on the [Subcontracting plan content, good faith effort and liquidated damages](mkt-subcontracting-plan-content-and-compliance.md) card, and the award responsibilities are on the [Awards involving subcontracting plans](mkt-subcontracting-plan-awards.md) card.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An offeror proposes a small business goal of 5 percent of its subcontract dollars. Its records on its last two similar contracts show 20 percent. The contracting officer gets past performance information from the contract administration office, finds that the goal looks unreasonably low, and asks the offeror to justify it or raise it.

## Never confuse

Reviewing a proposed subcontracting plan is covered here, while deciding whether a plan is required and judging compliance after award are on the [When a small business subcontracting plan is required](mkt-subcontracting-plan-when-required.md) card and the [Subcontracting plan content, good faith effort and liquidated damages](mkt-subcontracting-plan-content-and-compliance.md) card. The contracting officer shall take particular care to ensure that the offeror has not submitted unreasonably low goals to minimize exposure to liquidated damages and to avoid the administrative burden of substantiating good faith efforts [@CL-MKT-0446]. The adequacy review ensures that the required information, goals, and assurances are included [@CL-MKT-0443], while in negotiated acquisitions acceptability is determined based on the negotiation of each of the 15 elements of the plan [@CL-MKT-0444]. Under a sealed bid solicitation, if a bidder submits a plan that does not cover each of the 15 required elements (see FAR 19.704), the contracting officer shall advise the bidder of the deficiency and request submission of a revised plan by a specific date [@CL-MKT-0557].

## Company calibration

Whether the company sets its subcontracting goals from past subcontract records, and who prepares the plan for the contracting officer's review. [@Q-040]
