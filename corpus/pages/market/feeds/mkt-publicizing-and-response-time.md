## Purpose

Shows how long a notice must be public before a solicitation, how long offerors must have to respond for the different kinds of action, and the extra rules the Department of Defense adds.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR and codified DFARS [@CL-MKT-0062; @CL-MKT-0073] |
| Codified citation | FAR 5.203; DFARS 205.203 [@CL-MKT-0062; @CL-MKT-0073]; FAR 2.101 [@CL-MKT-9283] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 2.101 [@CL-MKT-9284] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-MKT-0062; @CL-MKT-0073] |
| Applies to | Proposed contract actions that must be publicized, with the response time set by the kind of action [@CL-MKT-0062; @CL-MKT-0066] |

The notice must be published at least 15 days before issuance of a solicitation, or of a proposed contract action the Government intends to solicit and negotiate with only one source under the authority of FAR 6.302, with an exception for commercial products or commercial services [@CL-MKT-0062]. For those acquisitions the contracting officer may establish a shorter period for issuance of the solicitation or use the combined synopsis and solicitation procedure [@CL-MKT-0152]. All publicizing and response times are calculated based on the date of publication, which is the date the notice appears on the GPE [@CL-MKT-0063]. The FAR definitions section defines the Governmentwide point of entry (GPE) as the single point where Government business opportunities greater than $25,000, including synopses of proposed contract actions, solicitations, and associated information, can be accessed electronically by the public [@CL-MKT-9283]. The RFO text of FAR 2.101 gives the same definition without the words greater than $25,000 [@CL-MKT-9284]. The card does not restate the next sentence of the definition, which gives the location of the GPE.

Except for commercial products or commercial services, agencies shall allow at least a 30-day response time from the date of issuance of a solicitation, for a proposed action expected to exceed the simplified acquisition threshold [@CL-MKT-0066; @CL-MKT-0067]. For architect-engineer services, agencies shall allow at least a 30 day response time from the date of publication of a proper notice of intent to contract, for the actions the paragraph covers [@CL-MKT-0068]. The same paragraph also covers the time before issuance of an order under a basic ordering agreement or similar arrangement, and its closing condition is that the proposed contract action is expected to exceed the simplified acquisition threshold [@CL-MKT-0069]. For research and development actions on that condition, agencies must allow at least a 45-day response time from the date of publication of the notice [@CL-MKT-0070; @CL-MKT-0071]. For acquisitions covered by the World Trade Organization Government Procurement Agreement or a Free Trade Agreement, the period between publication of the synopsis notice and receipt of offers must be no less than 40 days [@CL-MKT-0072]. If the acquisition falls within a general category identified in an annual forecast, the availability of which is published, the contracting officer may reduce the time period to as few as 10 days [@CL-MKT-0153]. For DoD, the response time is at least 45 days when a qualifying or designated country source requests it and the request is consistent with the Government's requirement [@CL-MKT-0073]. A DoD solicitation under competitive procedures that allowed fewer than 30 days and drew only one offer must be resolicited for at least 30 more days, except as provided in the cited DFARS sections [@CL-MKT-0074].

## How it works

Publicizing and response times are calculated based on the date of publication, which is the date the notice appears on the GPE [@CL-MKT-0063], although the general 30-day response time runs from the date of issuance of a solicitation [@CL-MKT-0066]. For an action estimated to be greater than $25,000 but not greater than the simplified acquisition threshold, and for a commercial products or services action estimated to be greater than $25,000, the contracting officer must establish a response time that gives potential offerors a reasonable opportunity to respond [@CL-MKT-0064; @CL-MKT-0065]. Each fixed period applies only to the kind of action stated for it.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A notice for a non-commercial supply buy expected to exceed the simplified acquisition threshold is published on day 1. The solicitation can issue around day 16. Offers are due at least 30 days after issue, so around day 46. A research and development action of the same size would need at least 45 days from publication, so offers would be due no earlier than day 46.

## Never confuse

The wait before a solicitation issues is not the response time: the first is counted from publication of the notice and the second, for the general case, from issuance of the solicitation [@CL-MKT-0062; @CL-MKT-0066]. The 30-day general period and the 45-day research and development period also start at different points: issuance for the first and publication for the second [@CL-MKT-0066; @CL-MKT-0070]. For a sole-source notice, see also the [Only One Offer provision](../../estimating/pricing/est-only-one-offer.md) card. The dollar amount of the simplified acquisition threshold is on the [Simplified acquisition and micro-purchase thresholds](../../compliance/regulatory-framework/cmp-acquisition-thresholds.md) card.
