## Purpose

Shows how long a notice must be public before a solicitation, how long offerors must have to respond for the different kinds of action, and the extra rules the Department of Defense adds.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR and codified DFARS [@CL-MKT-0062; @CL-MKT-0073]; RFO model text [@CL-MKT-9284; @CL-MKT-1884] |
| Codified citation | FAR 5.203; DFARS 205.203 [@CL-MKT-0062; @CL-MKT-0073]; FAR 2.101 [@CL-MKT-9283; @CL-MKT-1883] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 2.101 [@CL-MKT-9284; @CL-MKT-1884] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-MKT-0062; @CL-MKT-0073; @CL-MKT-9284] |
| Applies to | Proposed contract actions that must be publicized, with the response time set by the kind of action [@CL-MKT-0062; @CL-MKT-0066] |

The notice must be published at least 15 days before issuance of a solicitation, or a proposed contract action the Government intends to solicit and negotiate with only one source under the authority of FAR 6.302, except that, for acquisitions of commercial products or commercial services, the contracting officer may (1) establish a shorter period for issuance of the solicitation, or (2) use the combined synopsis and solicitation procedure (see FAR 12.603) [@CL-MKT-0062; @CL-MKT-0152]. All publicizing and response times are calculated based on the date of publication, and the publication date is the date the notice appears on the GPE [@CL-MKT-0063]. Under FAR 2.101, the Governmentwide point of entry (GPE) means the single point where Government business opportunities greater than $25,000, including synopses of proposed contract actions, solicitations, and associated information, can be accessed electronically by the public, and the GPE is located at https://www.sam.gov [@CL-MKT-9283; @CL-MKT-1883]. Under RFO FAR 2.101, the Governmentwide point of entry (GPE) means the single point where Government business opportunities, including synopses of proposed contract actions, solicitations, and associated information, can be accessed electronically by the public, and the GPE is located at https://www.sam.gov [@CL-MKT-9284; @CL-MKT-1884].

Except for the acquisition of commercial products or commercial services (see FAR 5.203(b)), agencies shall allow at least a 30-day response time for receipt of bids or proposals from the date of issuance of a solicitation, if the proposed contract action is expected to exceed the simplified acquisition threshold [@CL-MKT-0066; @CL-MKT-0067]. Agencies shall allow at least a 30 day response time from the date of publication of a proper notice of intent to contract for architect-engineer services or before issuance of an order under a basic ordering agreement or similar arrangement if the proposed contract action is expected to exceed the simplified acquisition threshold [@CL-MKT-0068; @CL-MKT-0069]. Agencies must allow at least a 45-day response time for receipt of bids or proposals from the date of publication of the notice required in FAR 5.201 for proposed contract actions categorized as research and development if the proposed contract action is expected to exceed the simplified acquisition threshold [@CL-MKT-0070; @CL-MKT-0071]. In addition to other requirements set forth in FAR 5.203, for acquisitions covered by the World Trade Organization Government Procurement Agreement or a Free Trade Agreement (see FAR subpart 25.4), the period of time between publication of the synopsis notice and receipt of offers must be no less than 40 days [@CL-MKT-9391; @CL-MKT-0072]. However, if the acquisition falls within a general category identified in an annual forecast, the availability of which is published, the contracting officer may reduce that 40-day time period to as few as 10 days [@CL-MKT-0153]. Under DFARS 205.203(b), DoD agencies allow at least 45 days response time when requested by a qualifying or designated country source (as these terms are used in DFARS part 225) and the request is consistent with the Government's requirement [@CL-MKT-0073]. Under DFARS 205.203(S-70), when using competitive procedures, if a solicitation allowed fewer than 30 days for receipt of offers and resulted in only one offer, the contracting officer shall resolicit, allowing an additional period of at least 30 days for receipt of offers, except as provided in DFARS 215.371-4 and 215.371-5 [@CL-MKT-0074].

## How it works

All publicizing and response times are calculated based on the date of publication, which is the date the notice appears on the GPE [@CL-MKT-0063], and except for the acquisition of commercial products or commercial services, agencies shall allow at least a 30-day response time for receipt of bids or proposals from the date of issuance of a solicitation if the proposed contract action is expected to exceed the simplified acquisition threshold [@CL-MKT-0066; @CL-MKT-0067]. The contracting officer must establish a solicitation response time that will afford potential offerors a reasonable opportunity to respond to each proposed contract action in an amount estimated to be greater than $25,000, but not greater than the simplified acquisition threshold, or each contract action for the acquisition of commercial products or commercial services in an amount estimated to be greater than $25,000 [@CL-MKT-0064; @CL-MKT-0065].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A notice for a non-commercial supply buy expected to exceed the simplified acquisition threshold is published on day 1. The solicitation can issue around day 16. Offers are due at least 30 days after issue, so around day 46. A research and development action of the same size would need at least 45 days from publication, so offers would be due no earlier than day 46.

## Never confuse

The general FAR 5.203(a) presolicitation notice period of at least 15 days precedes issuance of the solicitation [@CL-MKT-0062], unlike the proposal response time after solicitation, where DoD contracting officers allow at least 45 days when requested by a qualifying or designated country source consistent with the Government's requirement [@CL-MKT-0073].
