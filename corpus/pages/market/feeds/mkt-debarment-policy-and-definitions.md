## Purpose

Shows why agencies debar and suspend, how the terms affiliate and contractor reach related entities, and what the main defined terms of the debarment subpart mean.

## The rule

| Codified citation | FAR 9.402; FAR 9.403 [@CL-MKT-0771; @CL-MKT-0772] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-MKT-0771] |
| Applies to | Agency debarment and suspension actions under the debarment subpart [@CL-MKT-0771] |

Agencies shall solicit offers from, award contracts to, and consent to subcontracts with responsible contractors only, and debarment and suspension are discretionary actions that, taken in accordance with the subpart, are appropriate means to effectuate this policy [@CL-MKT-0771]. The serious nature of debarment and suspension requires that these remedies be imposed only in the public interest for the Government's protection and not for purposes of punishment [@CL-MKT-0773].

The policy and definitions printed here do not state how a debarment of one entity affects its subsidiaries or affiliates. They define the term affiliate and widen the term contractor to reach indirect activity. The causes for debarment are on the [Causes for debarment](../../supply-chain/supplier-identity/sup-causes-for-debarment.md) card and the causes for suspension are on the [Causes for suspension](../../supply-chain/supplier-identity/sup-causes-for-suspension.md) card. How each is decided is on the [Debarment: general rules, procedures and the decision](../../supply-chain/supplier-identity/sup-debarment-decision-process.md) card and the [Suspension: general rules, procedures and the decision](../../supply-chain/supplier-identity/sup-suspension-decision-process.md) card. ## How it works

Affiliates and contractors. Business concerns, organizations, or individuals are affiliates of each other if, directly or indirectly, either one controls or has the power to control the other, or a third party controls or has the power to control both [@CL-MKT-0772]. Indicia of control include, but are not limited to, interlocking management or ownership, identity of interests among family members, shared facilities and equipment, and common use of employees [@CL-MKT-0774]. One listed indicium of control is a business entity organized following the debarment, suspension, or proposed debarment of a contractor which has the same or similar management, ownership, or principal employees as the contractor that was debarred, suspended, or proposed for debarment [@CL-MKT-0797]. In one listed limb, a contractor is any individual or other legal entity that directly or indirectly, for example through an affiliate, submits offers for or is awarded, or reasonably may be expected to submit offers for or be awarded, a Government contract [@CL-MKT-0775]. The definition of contractor includes a contract for carriage under Government or commercial bills of lading and a subcontract under a Government contract, and a second limb covers an entity that conducts business, or reasonably may be expected to conduct business, with the Government as an agent or representative of another contractor [@CL-MKT-0798]. The card on [Protecting the Government's interest when subcontracting with debarred or suspended contractors](../../supply-chain/supplier-identity/sup-debarred-suspended-subcontractors.md) covers the steps around subcontracting with a debarred or suspended party.

Other defined terms. An administrative agreement is an agreement between an agency suspending and debarring official and the contractor used to resolve a suspension or debarment proceeding, or a potential suspension or debarment proceeding [@CL-MKT-0776]. In one listed limb, a conviction is a judgment or any other determination of guilt of a criminal offense by any court of competent jurisdiction, whether entered upon a verdict or plea, including a plea of nolo contendere [@CL-MKT-0777]. In a second listed limb, a conviction is any other resolution that is the functional equivalent of a judgment establishing a criminal offense by a court of competent jurisdiction, including probation before judgment and deferred prosecution [@CL-MKT-0794]. Under the definition of conviction, a disposition without the participation of the court is the functional equivalent of a judgment only if it includes an admission of guilt [@CL-MKT-0795]. A civil judgment includes the disposition of a civil action by any court of competent jurisdiction, whether by verdict, decision, settlement, stipulation, or other disposition that creates a civil liability for the complained of wrongful acts [@CL-MKT-0778]. The definition of civil judgment also covers a final determination of liability under the Program Fraud Civil Remedies Act of 1986 [@CL-MKT-0796]. Indictment means indictment for a criminal offense, and an information or other filing by competent authority charging a criminal offense is given the same effect as an indictment [@CL-MKT-0791]. Legal proceedings means any civil judicial proceeding to which the Government is a party or any criminal proceeding, and the term includes appeals from such proceedings [@CL-MKT-0792]. Unfair trade practices means the commission by a contractor of any listed act, the first of which is a violation of section 337 of the Tariff Act of 1930 as determined by the International Trade Commission [@CL-MKT-0793]. A voluntary exclusion is a contractor's written agreement to be excluded for a period under the terms of a settlement between the contractor and the suspending and debarring official of one or more agencies, and it must have Governmentwide effect [@CL-MKT-0779]. A pre-notice letter is a written correspondence issued to a contractor in a suspension or debarment matter that does not immediately result in an exclusion or ineligibility, and it is issued at the discretion of the suspending and debarring official [@CL-MKT-0780]. The pre-notice letter is not a mandatory step in the suspension or debarment process [@CL-MKT-0799]. The section defines further terms, including agency and the Nonprocurement Common Rule, and gives further acts under the unfair trade practices term, which this card does not set out.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

Two companies share one plant, the same equipment and a common pool of employees, and one has agreed with a suspending and debarring official to be excluded under a settlement. Shared facilities and equipment and common use of employees are listed indicia of control, so the two may be treated as affiliates, though the list is not exhaustive and control is the test. The settlement is a voluntary exclusion, which must have Governmentwide effect.

## Never confuse

Debarment and suspension are both discretionary remedies imposed for protection and not for punishment [@CL-MKT-0771; @CL-MKT-0773]. A pre-notice letter is not a suspension or debarment, because it does not immediately result in an exclusion or ineligibility [@CL-MKT-0780], and it is not a mandatory step in the process [@CL-MKT-0799]. An administrative agreement resolves a proceeding [@CL-MKT-0776], while a voluntary exclusion is a written agreement to be excluded under a settlement [@CL-MKT-0779], so the two should not be treated as the same outcome.
