## Purpose

Shows why agencies debar and suspend, how the terms affiliate and contractor reach related entities, and what the main defined terms of the debarment subpart mean.

## The rule

Agencies shall solicit offers from, award contracts to, and consent to subcontracts with responsible contractors only, and debarment and suspension are discretionary actions that, taken in accordance with the subpart, are appropriate means to effectuate this policy [@CL-MKT-0771]. The serious nature of debarment and suspension requires that these remedies be imposed only in the public interest for the Government's protection and not for purposes of punishment [@CL-MKT-0773].

The causes for debarment are on the [Causes for debarment](../../supply-chain/supplier-identity/sup-causes-for-debarment.md) card and the causes for suspension are on the [Causes for suspension](../../supply-chain/supplier-identity/sup-causes-for-suspension.md) card. How each is decided is on the [Debarment: general rules, procedures and the decision](../../supply-chain/supplier-identity/sup-debarment-decision-process.md) card and the [Suspension: general rules, procedures and the decision](../../supply-chain/supplier-identity/sup-suspension-decision-process.md) card. The length and scope of a debarment are on the [Period and scope of debarment](../../supply-chain/supplier-identity/sup-debarment-period-and-scope.md) card, and the effect of a listing in the exclusion records is on the [SAM exclusion records and the effect of listing](../../supply-chain/supplier-identity/sup-sam-exclusion-records-and-effect-of-listing.md) card.

## How it works

Affiliates and contractors. Business concerns, organizations, or individuals are affiliates of each other if, directly or indirectly, either one controls or has the power to control the other, or a third party controls or has the power to control both [@CL-MKT-0772]. Indicia of control include, but are not limited to, interlocking management or ownership, identity of interests among family members, shared facilities and equipment, common use of employees [@CL-MKT-0774], or a business entity organized following the debarment, suspension, or proposed debarment of a contractor which has the same or similar management, ownership, or principal employees as the contractor that was debarred, suspended, or proposed for debarment [@CL-MKT-0797]. In one listed limb, a contractor is any individual or other legal entity that directly or indirectly (for example, through an affiliate) submits offers for or is awarded, or reasonably may be expected to submit offers for or be awarded, a Government contract [@CL-MKT-0775], including a contract for carriage under Government or commercial bills of lading, or a subcontract under a Government contract, or in a second limb conducts business, or reasonably may be expected to conduct business, with the Government as an agent or representative of another contractor [@CL-MKT-0798]. The card on [Protecting the Government's interest when subcontracting with debarred or suspended contractors](../../supply-chain/supplier-identity/sup-debarred-suspended-subcontractors.md) covers the steps around subcontracting with a debarred or suspended party.

Other defined terms. Under FAR 2.101, debarment means action taken by a suspending and debarring official under FAR 9.406 to exclude a contractor from Government contracting and Government-approved subcontracting for a reasonable, specified period, and a contractor that is "debarred" is excluded [@CL-MKT-9249; @CL-MKT-9316]. Under RFO FAR 2.101, debarment means action taken by a suspending and debarring official under 9.406 to exclude a contractor from Government contracting and Government-approved subcontracting for a reasonable, specified period, and a contractor that is "debarred" is excluded [@CL-MKT-9257]. Under FAR 2.101, suspension means action taken by a suspending and debarring official under FAR 9.407 to disqualify a contractor temporarily from Government contracting and Government-approved subcontracting, and a contractor that is "suspended" is "disqualified" [@CL-MKT-9296]. Under RFO FAR 2.101, suspension means action taken by a suspending and debarring official under 9.407 to disqualify a contractor temporarily from Government contracting and Government-approved subcontracting, and a contractor that is suspended is disqualified [@CL-MKT-9297]. An administrative agreement means an agreement between an agency suspending and debarring official and the contractor used to resolve a suspension or debarment proceeding, or a potential suspension or debarment proceeding [@CL-MKT-0776]. Agency means any executive department, military department or defense agency, or other agency or independent establishment of the executive branch [@CL-MKT-1838]. In one listed limb, a conviction is a judgment or any other determination of guilt of a criminal offense by any court of competent jurisdiction, whether entered upon a verdict or plea, including a plea of nolo contendere [@CL-MKT-0777]. In a second listed limb, a conviction is any other resolution that is the functional equivalent of a judgment establishing a criminal offense by a court of competent jurisdiction, including probation before judgment and deferred prosecution [@CL-MKT-0794]. A disposition without the participation of the court is the functional equivalent of a judgment only if it includes an admission of guilt [@CL-MKT-0795]. Civil judgment means the disposition of a civil action by any court of competent jurisdiction, whether by verdict, decision, settlement, stipulation, other disposition that creates a civil liability for the complained of wrongful acts [@CL-MKT-0778], or a final determination of liability under the Program Fraud Civil Remedies Act of 1986 (title 31 of the United States Code, sections 3801 to 3812) [@CL-MKT-0796]. Indictment means indictment for a criminal offense, and an information or other filing by competent authority charging a criminal offense is given the same effect as an indictment [@CL-MKT-0791]. Legal proceedings means any civil judicial proceeding to which the Government is a party or any criminal proceeding, and the term includes appeals from such proceedings [@CL-MKT-0792]. Nonprocurement Common Rule means the procedures used by Federal Executive Agencies to suspend, debar, or exclude individuals or entities from participation in nonprocurement transactions under Executive Order 12549 [@CL-MKT-1841], and examples of nonprocurement transactions are grants, cooperative agreements, scholarships, fellowships, contracts of assistance, loans, loan guarantees, subsidies, insurance, payments for specified use, and donation agreements (see 2 CFR part 180 and agency enacting regulations in 2 CFR subtitle B) [@CL-MKT-1842]. Unfair trade practices means the commission of any of the following acts by a contractor: (1) a violation of section 337 of the Tariff Act of 1930 (title 19 of the United States Code, section 1337) as determined by the International Trade Commission [@CL-MKT-0793], (2) a violation, as determined by the Secretary of Commerce, of any agreement of the group known as the "Coordination Committee" for purposes of the Export Administration Act of 1979 (title 50 of the United States Code Appendix, section 2401 and following) or any similar bilateral or multilateral export control agreement [@CL-MKT-1836], or (3) a knowingly false statement regarding a material element of a certification concerning the foreign content of an item of supply, as determined by the Secretary of the Department or the head of the agency to which such certificate was furnished [@CL-MKT-1837]. Voluntary exclusion means a contractor's written agreement to be excluded for a period under the terms of a settlement between the contractor and the suspending and debarring official of one or more agencies, and a voluntary exclusion must have Governmentwide effect [@CL-MKT-0779]. Pre-notice letter means a written correspondence issued to a contractor in a suspension or debarment matter, which does not immediately result in an exclusion or ineligibility, and the letter is issued at the discretion of the suspending and debarring official [@CL-MKT-0780]. The pre-notice letter is not a mandatory step in the suspension or debarment process [@CL-MKT-0799].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

Two companies share one plant, the same equipment and a common pool of employees, and one has agreed with a suspending and debarring official to be excluded under a settlement. Shared facilities and equipment and common use of employees are listed indicia of control, so the two may be treated as affiliates, though the list is not exhaustive and control is the test. The settlement is a voluntary exclusion, which must have Governmentwide effect.

## Never confuse

Debarment and suspension are discretionary actions that, taken in accordance with the subpart, are appropriate means to effectuate the policy of soliciting offers from, awarding contracts to, and consenting to subcontracts with responsible contractors only [@CL-MKT-0771], and their serious nature requires that these remedies be imposed only in the public interest for the Government's protection and not for purposes of punishment [@CL-MKT-0773]. A pre-notice letter is a written correspondence issued to a contractor in a suspension or debarment matter that does not immediately result in an exclusion or ineligibility and is issued at the discretion of the suspending and debarring official [@CL-MKT-0780], and the letter is not a mandatory step in the suspension or debarment process [@CL-MKT-0799]. An administrative agreement is an agreement between an agency suspending and debarring official and the contractor used to resolve a suspension or debarment proceeding, or a potential suspension or debarment proceeding [@CL-MKT-0776], whereas a voluntary exclusion is a contractor's written agreement to be excluded for a period under the terms of a settlement between the contractor and the suspending and debarring official of one or more agencies and must have Governmentwide effect [@CL-MKT-0779].
