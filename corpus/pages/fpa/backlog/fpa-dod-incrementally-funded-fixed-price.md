## Purpose

Shows when a fixed-price contract may be incrementally funded under the DFARS, and what the contracting officer must tell the contractor when the funding limit nears.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|

| Codified citation | DFARS 232.001; DFARS 232.702; DFARS 232.703-1; DFARS 232.704-70; DFARS 232.706-70 [@CL-FPA-0371; @CL-FPA-0372; @CL-FPA-0069; @CL-FPA-0073; @CL-FPA-0078] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-FPA-0069] |
| Applies to | Incrementally funded fixed-price contracts under the DFARS [@CL-FPA-0078; @CL-FPA-0069] |

A fixed-price contract may be incrementally funded only if either the first or the second set of conditions is met [@CL-FPA-0069; @CL-FPA-0070; @CL-FPA-0071]. Under the first set, the contract excluding options, or any exercised option, must be for severable services, must not exceed one year in length, and must be incrementally funded using funds available (unexpired) as of the date the funds are obligated [@CL-FPA-0069; @CL-FPA-0070]. Under the second set, the contract uses funds available from two or more fiscal years and either the contract is funded with research and development appropriations or Congress has otherwise authorized incremental funding [@CL-FPA-0071].

An incrementally funded fixed-price contract shall be fully funded as soon as funds are available [@CL-FPA-0072]. In the DFARS, incremental funding means the partial funding of a contract or an exercised option, with additional funds anticipated to be provided at a later time [@CL-FPA-0371]. Fixed-price contracts shall be fully funded except as permitted by DFARS 232.703-1 [@CL-FPA-0372].

## How it works

When the contractor sends its notice under the limitation of Government's obligation clause, the contracting officer shall promptly give written notice of the Government's action [@CL-FPA-0073]. The notice says that the Government is doing one of three things: allotting additional funds and increasing the limitation of obligation in a specified amount, terminating the contract, or considering whether to allot additional funds [@CL-FPA-0074]. The third alternative, considering whether to allot additional funds, also carries two statements: that the contractor is entitled by the contract terms to stop work when the limitation of obligation is reached, and that any costs expended beyond it are at the contractor's risk [@CL-FPA-0075]. Upon learning that the contract will receive no further funds, the contracting officer shall promptly give written notice and terminate for the convenience of the Government [@CL-FPA-0076]. The contracting officer shall also ensure that, in accordance with paragraph (b) of the clause, enough funds are allotted to cover the total amount payable if the contract is terminated for convenience [@CL-FPA-0077].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A fixed-price research and development contract of 3,000,000 uses funds from two fiscal years and is funded with research and development appropriations. 1,000,000 is allotted now. The planner counts 1,000,000 as funded and 2,000,000 as unfunded, and the contract is to be fully funded as soon as funds are available. When the contractor warns that the limit is near, the contracting officer tells it in writing whether more funds are coming, the contract is ending, or the Government is still deciding.

## Never confuse

This card is the fixed-price case under the DFARS, and the notices here are the contracting officer's. The clause is used in solicitations and resultant incrementally funded fixed-price contracts, and the contracting officer may revise the contractor's notification period from ninety to thirty or sixty days, as appropriate [@CL-FPA-0078; @CL-FPA-0079]. The contractor's side of the clause is on [Limitation of Government's obligation clause](fpa-limitation-of-government-obligation.md). For the cost-reimbursement clause, see [Limitation of Funds clause](../../contract-finance/funding/ctf-limitation-of-funds.md).
