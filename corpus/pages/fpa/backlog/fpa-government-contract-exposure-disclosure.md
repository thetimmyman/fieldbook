## Purpose

Shows how a public defense contractor describes any material portion of its business that may be subject to renegotiation of profits or termination of contracts or subcontracts at the election of the Government, and the material effects that compliance with government regulations may have on its capital expenditures, earnings and competitive position. Regulation S-K binds only SEC registrants (public companies), so a privately held contractor is not bound by it. For a private contractor the disclosure is a benchmark only.

Paragraph (c)(1)(iv) of Item 101 of Regulation S-K, in 17 CFR 229.101, names a description of any material portion of the business that may be subject to renegotiation of profits or termination of contracts or subcontracts at the election of the Government [@CL-FPA-0012]. It is one item in a list that disclosure may include but is not limited to, and only information material to an understanding of the business taken as a whole is required [@CL-FPA-0007].

Item 101(c)(2) tells the registrant to discuss the information in paragraphs (c)(2)(i) and (ii) with respect to, and to the extent material to an understanding of, its business taken as a whole, subject to a segment exception [@CL-FPA-0013]. If the information is material to a particular segment, the registrant should additionally identify that segment [@CL-FPA-0014]. Paragraph (c)(2)(i) names the material effects that compliance with government regulations, including environmental regulations, may have upon the capital expenditures, earnings and competitive position of the registrant and its subsidiaries [@CL-FPA-0015]. Those effects include the estimated capital expenditures for environmental control facilities for the current fiscal year and any other material subsequent period [@CL-FPA-0016].

## How it works

Where disclosure covers this item, it describes any material portion of the business that may be subject to renegotiation of profits or termination at the Government's election [@CL-FPA-0012]. Separately, the registrant discusses the material effects of regulatory compliance on capital expenditures, earnings and competitive position, to the extent material to the whole business [@CL-FPA-0013; @CL-FPA-0015]. Where those compliance effects are material to a particular segment, the registrant should additionally identify that segment [@CL-FPA-0014].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor earns 70 million of 100 million of revenue under Government contracts that carry a right to terminate. It describes that 70 million portion as material. It also notes a planned 3 million of capital spending needed to meet a new regulation, and names the segment where that spending is material.

## Never confuse

This is a description of exposure, not a settlement calculation. For how a termination settlement is built and capped, see [Termination for convenience settlement](../../contract-finance/billing-payment/ctf-termination-for-convenience-settlement.md). For which rule text governs a DoD contract, see [Acquisition regulations](../../compliance/regulatory-framework/cmp-acquisition-regulations.md). The printed text asks for a description of the exposed portion of the business and names no backlog amount. The general segment description is on [Business description by segment](../kpi/fpa-business-description-by-segment.md).
