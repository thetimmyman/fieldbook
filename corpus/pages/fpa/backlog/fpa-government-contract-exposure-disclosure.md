## Purpose

Shows how a registrant describes any material portion of the business that may be subject to renegotiation of profits or termination of contracts or subcontracts at the election of the Government under Item 101(c)(1)(iv) of Regulation S-K, and discusses the material effects that compliance with government regulations, including environmental regulations, may have upon the capital expenditures, earnings and competitive position of the registrant and its subsidiaries under Item 101(c)(2)(i) [@CL-FPA-0007; @CL-FPA-0012; @CL-FPA-0013; @CL-FPA-0014; @CL-FPA-0015; @CL-FPA-0016]. Regulation S-K states the requirements applicable to the content of the non-financial statement portions of registration statements under the Securities Act to the extent provided in the forms to be used for registration under such Act [@CL-FPA-0105], and registration statements under section 12, annual or other reports under sections 13 and 15(d), going-private transaction statements under section 13, tender offer statements under sections 13 and 14, annual reports to security holders and proxy and information statements under section 14, and any other documents required to be filed under the Exchange Act, to the extent provided in the forms and rules under that Act [@CL-FPA-0380; @CL-FPA-0381; @CL-FPA-0106].

Paragraph (c)(1)(iv) of Item 101 of Regulation S-K specifies a description of any material portion of the business that may be subject to renegotiation of profits or termination of contracts or subcontracts at the election of the Government [@CL-FPA-0012]. When describing each segment, only information material to an understanding of the business taken as a whole is required, and disclosure may include, but should not be limited to, the information specified in paragraphs (c)(1)(i) through (v) of Item 101 [@CL-FPA-0007].

Item 101(c)(2) directs the registrant to discuss the information specified in paragraphs (c)(2)(i) and (ii) of Item 101 with respect to, and to the extent material to an understanding of, the registrant's business taken as a whole, except that, if the information is material to a particular segment, the registrant should additionally identify that segment [@CL-FPA-0013; @CL-FPA-0014]. Paragraph (c)(2)(i) specifies the material effects that compliance with government regulations, including environmental regulations, may have upon the capital expenditures, earnings and competitive position of the registrant and its subsidiaries, including the estimated capital expenditures for environmental control facilities for the current fiscal year and any other material subsequent period [@CL-FPA-0015; @CL-FPA-0016].

## How it works

When describing each segment under Item 101(c)(1), only information material to an understanding of the business taken as a whole is required, and disclosure may include, but should not be limited to, the information specified in paragraphs (c)(1)(i) through (v), including a description of any material portion of the business that may be subject to renegotiation of profits or termination of contracts or subcontracts at the election of the Government [@CL-FPA-0007; @CL-FPA-0012]. Under Item 101(c)(2)(i), the registrant discusses the material effects that compliance with government regulations, including environmental regulations, may have upon the capital expenditures, earnings and competitive position of the registrant and its subsidiaries, including the estimated capital expenditures for environmental control facilities for the current fiscal year and any other material subsequent period, with respect to, and to the extent material to an understanding of, the registrant's business taken as a whole, except that, if the information is material to a particular segment, the registrant should additionally identify that segment [@CL-FPA-0013; @CL-FPA-0014; @CL-FPA-0015; @CL-FPA-0016].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor earns 70 million of 100 million of revenue under Government contracts that carry a right to terminate. It describes that 70 million portion as material. It also notes a planned 3 million of capital spending needed to meet a new regulation, and names the segment where that spending is material.
