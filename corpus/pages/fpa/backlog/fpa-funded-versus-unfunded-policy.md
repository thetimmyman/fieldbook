## Purpose

Shows the funding rule that bears on the line between funded and unfunded contract value: the limit on Government obligations and the contracting officer's two routes before executing a contract.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-FPA-0050] |
| Codified citation | FAR 32.702 [@CL-FPA-0050; @CL-FPA-0051] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 32.702 [@CL-FPA-0272; @CL-FPA-0273] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-FPA-0050] |
| Applies to | Any contract, before the contracting officer executes it [@CL-FPA-0051] |

No officer or employee of the Government may create or authorize an obligation in excess of the funds available, or in advance of appropriations, unless otherwise authorized by law [@CL-FPA-0050]. Before executing any contract, the contracting officer shall either obtain written assurance from responsible fiscal authority that adequate funds are available or expressly condition the contract upon availability of funds in accordance with FAR 32.703-2 [@CL-FPA-0051].

## How it works

The rule caps what the Government may commit: an obligation may not exceed the funds available or run ahead of appropriations, unless law says otherwise [@CL-FPA-0050]. The contracting officer then has two routes before executing a contract: written assurance from responsible fiscal authority that adequate funds are available, or an express condition that the contract depends on availability of funds, in accordance with FAR 32.703-2 [@CL-FPA-0051]. For what happens as an incrementally funded contract nears its limit, see [Contract funding: incremental funding policy](../../contract-finance/funding/ctf-incremental-funding-policy.md).

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 32 text in place of the codified text from February 1, 2026 [@CL-CTF-4046]. RFO FAR 32.702 keeps the rule that no officer or employee of the Government may create or authorize an obligation in excess of the funds available, or in advance of appropriations (Anti-Deficiency Act, section 1341 of title 31), unless otherwise authorized by law [@CL-FPA-0272; @CL-FPA-0050]. It keeps the two routes before executing any contract: the contracting officer must obtain written assurance from responsible fiscal authority that adequate funds are available or expressly condition the contract upon availability of funds in accordance with 32.703-2 [@CL-FPA-0273]. The RFO text writes the two routes in one sentence without the codified paragraph letters (a) and (b) and says "must" where the codified text says "shall" [@CL-FPA-0273; @CL-FPA-0051].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contract has a total value of 2,000,000. The file holds written assurance from fiscal authority that adequate funds are available for all 2,000,000. The planner counts the whole 2,000,000 as funded. A second contract of 500,000 is expressly conditioned on availability of funds, and no funds are available yet. The planner counts the 500,000 as unfunded until the condition is met.

## Never confuse

Written assurance of funds is not the same as a contract conditioned on availability of funds. They are the two routes in the printed text: one obtains written assurance that adequate funds are available, and the other expressly conditions the contract upon availability of funds in accordance with FAR 32.703-2 [@CL-FPA-0051]. The printed text does not address revenue recognition, so neither route is a recognition rule. The clauses and limits for conditioned contracts are on [Contracts conditioned on availability of funds](fpa-funds-availability-conditioned-contracts.md).
