## Purpose

Shows what the allotted amount caps on an incrementally funded fixed-price contract, so a planner can tell work that is covered from work that is at risk.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS clause [@CL-FPA-0081] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 252.232-7007 [@CL-FPA-0081] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-FPA-0081] |
| Applies to | Contract line items the clause identifies as incrementally funded [@CL-FPA-0080] |

The clause identifies contract line items as incrementally funded and states the sum of the total price that is presently available for payment and allotted [@CL-FPA-0080]. Paragraph (b) has the contractor agree to perform up to the point at which the total amount payable by the Government, including reimbursement in the event of termination for the Government's convenience, approximates the total amount currently allotted to the contract [@CL-FPA-0081]. The contractor is not authorized to continue work on the incrementally funded items beyond that point [@CL-FPA-0082]. The Government will not be obligated in any event to reimburse the contractor in excess of the amount allotted for those items, regardless of anything to the contrary in the termination for convenience clause [@CL-FPA-0083].

The total amount payable on termination for convenience includes costs, profit, and estimated termination settlement costs [@CL-FPA-0084]. The parties contemplate that the Government will allot funds in accordance with a schedule set out in the clause [@CL-FPA-0090].

## How it works

The contractor must notify the Contracting Officer in writing at least ninety days before the date when, in its best judgment, the work will reach the notice point [@CL-FPA-0085]. The notice point is where the total amount payable, including any termination cost, will approximate 85 percent of the total amount then allotted [@CL-FPA-0086]. The notice states the estimated date and the additional funding, if any, needed to reach the next scheduled allotment date or a mutually agreed substitute date [@CL-FPA-0087; @CL-FPA-0103]. If funds are not allotted by the date in the notice or an agreed substitute date, the Contracting Officer will terminate any items without additional funds [@CL-FPA-0088]. The clause stops applying once the contract is fully funded, except for rights and obligations concerning equitable adjustments negotiated under it [@CL-FPA-0089].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A fixed-price line item has a total price of 4,000,000, of which 1,500,000 is allotted. Costs to date plus estimated termination settlement costs reach 1,275,000, which is 85 percent of the allotment. The contractor must have given notice at least ninety days before reaching that point. If no more funds are allotted by the date it gave, the Contracting Officer terminates the item. The Government will not reimburse more than the 1,500,000 allotted.

## Never confuse

The allotted amount is part of the total price, not the total price itself [@CL-FPA-0080]. The Government is not obligated to reimburse more than the allotted amount for the items [@CL-FPA-0083], and the contractor performs only up to the point where the total amount payable approximates it [@CL-FPA-0081]. That amount counts costs, profit and estimated termination settlement costs together [@CL-FPA-0084]. The ninety days is the period as printed, and the contracting officer may revise it; see [DoD incrementally funded fixed-price contracts](fpa-dod-incrementally-funded-fixed-price.md) [@CL-FPA-0079]. For the cost-reimbursement version, with its own notice points, see [Limitation of Funds clause](../../contract-finance/funding/ctf-limitation-of-funds.md).
