## Purpose

Shows what a registrant describes about cybersecurity risk management, strategy and governance under Item 106 of Regulation S-K, and how the Item defines an incident and a threat [@CL-FPA-0119; @CL-FPA-0121; @CL-FPA-0117; @CL-FPA-0118].

| Applies to | A registrant describing its cybersecurity processes and governance [@CL-FPA-0119; @CL-FPA-0121] |

Item 106(a) defines a cybersecurity incident as an unauthorized occurrence, or a series of related unauthorized occurrences, on or conducted through a registrant's information systems that jeopardizes the confidentiality, integrity, or availability of those systems [@CL-FPA-0117]. The definition also covers an occurrence that jeopardizes the confidentiality, integrity, or availability of any information residing in those systems [@CL-FPA-0165]. Item 106(a) defines a cybersecurity threat as any potential unauthorized occurrence on or conducted through those systems that may result in adverse effects on their confidentiality, integrity, or availability or on information residing in them [@CL-FPA-0118].

Under Item 106(b)(1), the registrant describes its processes, if any, for assessing, identifying, and managing material risks from cybersecurity threats in sufficient detail for a reasonable investor to understand them [@CL-FPA-0119]. A registrant should address, as applicable, a non-exclusive list of items, one being whether and how the processes have been integrated into its overall risk management system or processes [@CL-FPA-0120]. Item 106(b)(2) requires the registrant to describe whether any risks from cybersecurity threats, including as a result of any previous cybersecurity incidents, have materially affected or are reasonably likely to materially affect the registrant, including its business strategy, results of operations, or financial condition, and if so, how [@CL-FPA-0166].

Item 106(c)(1) calls for a description of the board of directors' oversight of those risks and, if applicable, identification of any board committee or subcommittee responsible for that oversight [@CL-FPA-0121]. If applicable, the registrant also describes the processes by which the board or the responsible committee is informed about those risks [@CL-FPA-0167]. Item 106(c)(2) calls for a description of management's role in assessing and managing the registrant's material risks, addressing as applicable a non-exclusive list of disclosure items [@CL-FPA-0122].

## How it works

The registrant applies the defined terms to decide what counts as an incident and what counts as a threat [@CL-FPA-0117; @CL-FPA-0118; @CL-FPA-0165]. It describes its risk processes in enough detail for a reasonable investor [@CL-FPA-0119] and says whether cybersecurity risks have materially affected, or are reasonably likely to materially affect, the registrant [@CL-FPA-0166]. It then describes board oversight and management's role as separate parts of governance [@CL-FPA-0121; @CL-FPA-0122]. The non-exclusive lists are prompts for the description, and the registrant addresses them as applicable [@CL-FPA-0120; @CL-FPA-0122].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A registrant has an outside firm that tests its systems each year and a risk committee of its board that receives a quarterly report from the security lead. Its description says whether the testing processes sit inside the overall risk process, names the committee, and says which management role reports to the committee.

## Never confuse

A cybersecurity threat is any potential unauthorized occurrence, while an incident is an unauthorized occurrence that jeopardizes the confidentiality, integrity, or availability of the systems or of any information residing in them [@CL-FPA-0117; @CL-FPA-0118; @CL-FPA-0165]. Describing board oversight is also different from describing management's role: each has its own paragraph [@CL-FPA-0121; @CL-FPA-0122]. The question whether cybersecurity risks have materially affected the registrant is different from how the registrant manages them: the first is its own paragraph [@CL-FPA-0166], and the second is the risk management description [@CL-FPA-0119].
