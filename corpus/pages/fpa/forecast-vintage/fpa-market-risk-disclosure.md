## Purpose

Shows the three ways a registrant presents quantitative market risk information, the qualitative items and who is not required to provide the information under Item 305 of Regulation S-K [@CL-FPA-0131; @CL-FPA-0135; @CL-FPA-0137].

Item 305(a)(1) requires registrants to provide, in their reporting currency, quantitative information about market risk as of the end of the latest fiscal year, in accordance with one of three disclosure alternatives [@CL-FPA-0131]. The first alternative is a tabular presentation of information related to market risk sensitive instruments, including fair values and contract terms sufficient to determine future cash flows, categorized by expected maturity dates [@CL-FPA-0132]. The second is sensitivity analysis disclosures that express the potential loss in future earnings, fair values, or cash flows of market risk sensitive instruments resulting from one or more selected hypothetical changes including those in interest rates, foreign currency exchange rates and commodity prices [@CL-FPA-0133]. Those changes also cover other relevant market rates or prices over a selected period of time, and the magnitude of the selected changes may differ among and within market risk exposure categories [@CL-FPA-0172]. Registrants shall also provide a description of the model, assumptions, and parameters, which are necessary to understand those disclosures [@CL-FPA-0365]. The third is value at risk disclosures that express the potential loss in future earnings, fair values, or cash flows of market risk sensitive instruments over a selected period of time, with a selected likelihood of occurrence [@CL-FPA-0134]. A registrant may choose one of the three alternatives for instruments entered into for trading purposes and another for those entered into for other than trading purposes [@CL-FPA-0173]. Registrants shall also discuss material limitations that cause the information required under paragraph (a)(1) not to reflect fully the net market risk exposures of the entity [@CL-FPA-0174].

Item 305(b)(1) says that, to the extent material, the registrant shall describe its primary market risk exposures and how those exposures are managed [@CL-FPA-0135]. Another listed item is changes in either the exposures or how they are managed, compared with the most recently completed fiscal year and what is known or expected in future reporting periods [@CL-FPA-0136]. Item 305(e) says a smaller reporting company, as defined in the cited section, is not required to provide the information required by Item 305 [@CL-FPA-0137].

## How it works

The registrant presents its quantitative information under one of the three alternatives [@CL-FPA-0131]. The tabular alternative centres on fair values and contract terms by expected maturity [@CL-FPA-0132], the sensitivity alternative on loss from selected hypothetical changes [@CL-FPA-0133], and the value at risk alternative on loss over a period with a selected likelihood [@CL-FPA-0134]. A qualitative description of exposures and their management sits beside the numbers [@CL-FPA-0135]. A registrant may choose one alternative for trading instruments and another for the rest [@CL-FPA-0173], and it discusses material limitations that stop the information from fully reflecting its net exposures [@CL-FPA-0174]. The printed text sets further requirements, such as information for the preceding fiscal year, which these claims do not restate.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A registrant has variable-rate debt of 100 and chooses the sensitivity analysis alternative. It selects a hypothetical rise of one percentage point in its borrowing rate over a one-year period. The potential loss in annual earnings is 1. It then describes in words that interest rate risk is its primary exposure and how it manages that exposure.

## Never confuse

Sensitivity analysis and value at risk both express potential loss, but sensitivity analysis rests on selected hypothetical changes in rates or prices [@CL-FPA-0133; @CL-FPA-0172], while value at risk is stated over a selected period of time with a selected likelihood of occurrence [@CL-FPA-0134]. Both differ from the tabular alternative, which presents instruments with fair values and contract terms by expected maturity [@CL-FPA-0132]. The qualitative items describe exposures and their management in words and do not replace the quantitative alternatives [@CL-FPA-0131; @CL-FPA-0135].

The management discussion of liquidity is covered in [MD&A objective and liquidity for public registrants](../planning-chain/fpa-mdna-objective-and-liquidity.md), and the risk factor disclosure is covered in [Risk factor disclosure for public registrants](fpa-risk-factors.md).
