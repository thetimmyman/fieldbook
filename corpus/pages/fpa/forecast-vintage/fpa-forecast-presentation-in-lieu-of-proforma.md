## Purpose

Shows when a filing may present a financial forecast instead of pro forma condensed statements of comprehensive income, and what that forecast must contain. The rule is part of Regulation S-X, which is not FAR, DFARS or CAS.

## The rule

| Applies to | Filings that present a forecast in lieu of pro forma condensed statements of comprehensive income [@CL-FPA-0291] |

A financial forecast may be filed in lieu of the pro forma condensed statements of comprehensive income required by § 210.11-02(a)(1) [@CL-FPA-0291]. The section does not say that anyone must approve the substitution. It says only that a forecast may be filed [@CL-FPA-0291]. When the pro forma statements are required, and how they are prepared, is on [Pro forma financial information: when it is required](../../mna/diligence/mna-sx-pro-forma-presentation.md) and [Pro forma financial information: how it is prepared](../../mna/diligence/mna-sx-pro-forma-preparation.md).

Paragraph (a) gives four directions for the forecast; three use "shall" and one uses "should". It shall cover a period of at least 12 months from the latest of two dates: the most recent balance sheet included in the filing, or the consummation date or estimated consummation date of the transaction [@CL-FPA-0292]. The forecasted statement of comprehensive income shall be presented in the same degree of detail as the pro forma condensed statement required by § 210.11-02(a)(3) [@CL-FPA-0293]. Assumptions particularly relevant to the transaction and its effects should be clearly set forth [@CL-FPA-0294]. Historical condensed financial information of the registrant and of the business acquired or to be acquired, if any, shall be presented for at least a recent 12 month period in parallel columns with the forecast [@CL-FPA-0295].

Three further paragraphs follow. The forecast shall be presented in accordance with the guidelines established by the American Institute of Certified Public Accountants [@CL-FPA-0296]. Forecasted earnings per share data shall be substituted for pro forma per share data [@CL-FPA-0297]. The section does not permit a forecast in lieu of pro forma information required by United States GAAP or IFRS-IASB [@CL-FPA-0298].

## How it works

The section names no deadline and no deciding body. The duties are written in the passive voice, so the section does not say which person prepares each part.

"Shall" governs the forecast period, the level of detail, the historical columns, the American Institute of Certified Public Accountants guidelines and the substitution of forecasted per share data [@CL-FPA-0292; @CL-FPA-0293; @CL-FPA-0295; @CL-FPA-0296; @CL-FPA-0297]. "Should" governs the assumptions, so that sentence is a weaker direction than the others [@CL-FPA-0294].

The forecast period is measured from the latest of the two dates. When the transaction has not closed and its estimated consummation date is the later date, that estimate is the start [@CL-FPA-0292].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A company agrees to acquire a business. The most recent balance sheet in its filing is dated the end of March. The deal is expected to close at the end of June. Assume no United States GAAP or IFRS-IASB rule requires pro forma information here [@CL-FPA-0298].

The company files a financial forecast in lieu of the pro forma condensed statements of comprehensive income [@CL-FPA-0291]. The latest of the two dates is the estimated closing at the end of June, so the forecast runs for 18 months counted from that date. That is at least 12 months [@CL-FPA-0292]. The forecasted statement is presented in the same degree of detail as the pro forma condensed statement would be [@CL-FPA-0293]. It sets out the assumptions particularly relevant to the deal [@CL-FPA-0294]. Beside it, in parallel columns, are condensed historical results for the company and for the target for the trailing 12 months [@CL-FPA-0295]. The forecast follows the American Institute of Certified Public Accountants guidelines [@CL-FPA-0296]. It shows forecasted earnings per share in place of pro forma per share data [@CL-FPA-0297].

If the deal instead closed at the end of September, that actual consummation date would be the latest of the two dates [@CL-FPA-0292].

## Never confuse

A financial forecast is not pro forma information. The forecast may stand in for the pro forma condensed statements of comprehensive income required by § 210.11-02(a)(1) [@CL-FPA-0291]. It may not stand in for pro forma information that United States GAAP or IFRS-IASB requires [@CL-FPA-0298].

The forecast period and the historical period are two separate requirements. The forecast period is at least 12 months from the latest of the two dates [@CL-FPA-0292]. The historical period is at least a recent 12 month period [@CL-FPA-0295].
