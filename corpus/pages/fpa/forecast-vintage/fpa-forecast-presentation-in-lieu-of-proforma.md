## Purpose

Shows when a financial forecast may be filed in lieu of the pro forma condensed statements of comprehensive income required by § 210.11-02(a)(1) and how that forecast is presented under § 210.11-03 [@CL-FPA-0291; @CL-FPA-0292; @CL-FPA-0293; @CL-FPA-0294; @CL-FPA-0295].

A financial forecast may be filed in lieu of the pro forma condensed statements of comprehensive income required by § 210.11-02(a)(1) [@CL-FPA-0291]. When the pro forma statements are required, and how they are prepared, is on [Pro forma financial information: when it is required](../../mna/diligence/mna-sx-pro-forma-presentation.md) and [Pro forma financial information: how it is prepared](../../mna/diligence/mna-sx-pro-forma-preparation.md).

Under paragraph (a), the financial forecast shall cover a period of at least 12 months from the latest of the most recent balance sheet included in the filing or the consummation date or estimated consummation date of the transaction [@CL-FPA-0292]. The forecasted statement of comprehensive income shall be presented in the same degree of detail as the pro forma condensed statement of comprehensive income required by § 210.11-02(a)(3) [@CL-FPA-0293]. Assumptions particularly relevant to the transaction and effects thereof should be clearly set forth [@CL-FPA-0294]. Historical condensed financial information of the registrant and the business acquired or to be acquired, if any, shall be presented for at least a recent 12 month period in parallel columns with the financial forecast [@CL-FPA-0295].

Such financial forecast shall be presented in accordance with the guidelines established by the American Institute of Certified Public Accountants [@CL-FPA-0296]. Forecasted earnings per share data shall be substituted for pro forma per share data [@CL-FPA-0297]. This section does not permit the filing of a financial forecast in lieu of pro forma information required by U.S. (United States) generally accepted accounting principles or International Financial Reporting Standards as issued by the International Accounting Standards Board [@CL-FPA-0298].

## How it works

When a financial forecast is filed in lieu of the pro forma condensed statements of comprehensive income required by § 210.11-02(a)(1) [@CL-FPA-0291], the forecast shall cover a period of at least 12 months from the latest of the most recent balance sheet included in the filing or the consummation date or estimated consummation date of the transaction [@CL-FPA-0292], be presented in the same degree of detail as the pro forma condensed statement of comprehensive income required by § 210.11-02(a)(3) [@CL-FPA-0293], be accompanied in parallel columns by historical condensed financial information of the registrant and the business acquired or to be acquired, if any, for at least a recent 12 month period [@CL-FPA-0295], be presented in accordance with the guidelines established by the American Institute of Certified Public Accountants [@CL-FPA-0296], and substitute forecasted earnings per share data for pro forma per share data [@CL-FPA-0297], while assumptions particularly relevant to the transaction and effects thereof should be clearly set forth [@CL-FPA-0294]. A financial forecast is not permitted in lieu of pro forma information required by U.S. generally accepted accounting principles or International Financial Reporting Standards as issued by the International Accounting Standards Board [@CL-FPA-0298].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A company agrees to acquire a business. The most recent balance sheet in its filing is dated the end of March. The deal is expected to close at the end of June. Assume no United States GAAP or IFRS-IASB rule requires pro forma information here [@CL-FPA-0298].

The company files a financial forecast in lieu of the pro forma condensed statements of comprehensive income [@CL-FPA-0291]. The latest of the two dates is the estimated closing at the end of June, so the forecast runs for 18 months counted from that date. That is at least 12 months [@CL-FPA-0292]. The forecasted statement is presented in the same degree of detail as the pro forma condensed statement would be [@CL-FPA-0293]. It sets out the assumptions particularly relevant to the deal [@CL-FPA-0294]. Beside it, in parallel columns, are condensed historical results for the company and for the target for the trailing 12 months [@CL-FPA-0295]. The forecast follows the American Institute of Certified Public Accountants guidelines [@CL-FPA-0296]. It shows forecasted earnings per share in place of pro forma per share data [@CL-FPA-0297].

If the deal instead closed at the end of September, that actual consummation date would be the latest of the two dates [@CL-FPA-0292].

## Never confuse

A financial forecast may be filed in lieu of the pro forma condensed statements of comprehensive income required by § 210.11-02(a)(1) [@CL-FPA-0291], but § 210.11-03 does not permit the filing of a financial forecast in lieu of pro forma information required by U.S. generally accepted accounting principles or International Financial Reporting Standards as issued by the International Accounting Standards Board [@CL-FPA-0298].

The financial forecast shall cover a period of at least 12 months from the latest of the most recent balance sheet included in the filing or the consummation date or estimated consummation date of the transaction [@CL-FPA-0292], while historical condensed financial information of the registrant and the business acquired or to be acquired, if any, shall be presented for at least a recent 12 month period in parallel columns with the financial forecast [@CL-FPA-0295].
