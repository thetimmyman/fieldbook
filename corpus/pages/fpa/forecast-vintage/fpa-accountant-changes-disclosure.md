## Purpose

Shows when a registrant discloses a change of independent accountant, what counts as a disagreement and how the former accountant's letter works under Item 304 of Regulation S-K [@CL-FPA-0123; @CL-FPA-0125; @CL-FPA-0129].

| Applies to | A registrant whose principal accountant resigned, declined to stand for re-election or was dismissed within the period the Item looks at [@CL-FPA-0123; @CL-FPA-0124] |

Item 304(a)(1) looks at the registrant's two most recent fiscal years or any subsequent interim period and covers an independent accountant previously engaged as the principal accountant to audit the registrant's financial statements [@CL-FPA-0124]. It also covers an independent accountant previously engaged to audit a significant subsidiary and on whom the principal accountant expressed reliance in its report, who has resigned (or indicated it has declined to stand for re-election after the completion of the current audit) or was dismissed [@CL-FPA-0168]. When an accountant of the kind the paragraph covers has resigned, indicated it has declined to stand for re-election after the completion of the current audit, or was dismissed, the registrant shall state which occurred and the date [@CL-FPA-0123]. The printed text lists further statements, reportable events and rules for a newly engaged accountant, which these claims do not restate.

Instruction 4 says disagreements is interpreted broadly to include any difference of opinion concerning accounting principles or practices, financial statement disclosure, or auditing scope or procedure [@CL-FPA-0125]. The instruction limits a disagreement to a difference that, if not resolved to the former accountant's satisfaction, would have caused it to make reference to the subject matter in connection with its report, and an argument is not necessary [@CL-FPA-0126]. The disagreements contemplated are those at the decision-making level, between registrant personnel responsible for presenting its financial statements and accounting firm personnel responsible for rendering its report [@CL-FPA-0127]. The disagreements reported include both those resolved and those not resolved to the former accountant's satisfaction [@CL-FPA-0128]. The instruction also says the term disagreements does not include initial differences of opinion based on incomplete facts or preliminary information that were later resolved to the former accountant's satisfaction [@CL-FPA-0169]. That exclusion rests on resolution by obtaining additional relevant facts or information, providing the registrant and the accountant do not continue to have a difference of opinion upon obtaining them [@CL-FPA-0170].

The registrant shall request the former accountant to furnish a letter addressed to the Commission stating whether it agrees with the registrant's statements and, if not, the respects in which it does not agree [@CL-FPA-0129]. If the letter is unavailable at filing, the registrant shall request it as promptly as possible so that it can be filed within ten business days [@CL-FPA-0130]. Those ten business days run after the filing of the report or registration statement, and notwithstanding that period the registrant shall file the letter by amendment within two business days of receipt, and if the letter is received on a Saturday, Sunday or holiday on which the Commission is not open for business, the two business day period begins to run on and includes the first business day thereafter [@CL-FPA-0171; @CL-FPA-1220]. The Item and its instructions also print exceptions and further letter mechanics, such as the copy the former accountant receives and the interim letter, which these claims do not restate.

## How it works

The registrant checks the period and the accountant's role, including a significant subsidiary accountant [@CL-FPA-0124; @CL-FPA-0168]. If the accountant resigned, declined to stand for re-election or was dismissed, it states which and the date [@CL-FPA-0123]. It then tests each past matter against the broad meaning of disagreement, the decision-making level and the exclusion for early differences resolved on additional facts [@CL-FPA-0125; @CL-FPA-0127; @CL-FPA-0169; @CL-FPA-0170]. It asks the former accountant for a letter to the Commission and files it when received [@CL-FPA-0129; @CL-FPA-0130; @CL-FPA-0171].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A registrant dismisses its auditor. Before the dismissal, the finance lead and the firm's engagement partner differed on how to account for a transaction, and the firm said it would have referred to the matter in its report. The difference was not resolved. It was at the decision-making level, so it is reported as a disagreement, and the former auditor is asked for a letter.

## Never confuse

A disagreement is not only an argument: a difference of opinion is enough if it meets the report-reference test [@CL-FPA-0126]. An initial difference of opinion based on incomplete facts or preliminary information is excluded where it was later resolved to the former accountant's satisfaction on additional facts, providing no difference of opinion continues [@CL-FPA-0169; @CL-FPA-0170]. A change of accountant alone is also different from a disagreement: the change calls for the statement of what occurred and the date [@CL-FPA-0123], while a disagreement is a separate matter tested under the instruction [@CL-FPA-0125]. The former accountant's letter to the Commission is likewise different from the attestation report of the registered public accounting firm on internal control, which is provided in the annual report of an accelerated filer or large accelerated filer [@CL-FPA-0129; @CL-FPA-0143]; see [Internal control over financial reporting disclosure](fpa-icfr-disclosure.md).
