## Purpose

Shows how a public defense contractor presents the material factors that make an investment speculative or risky, including how they are organized and summarized. Regulation S-K binds only SEC registrants (public companies), so a privately held contractor is not bound by it. For a private contractor the discussion is a benchmark only.

Item 105(a) of Regulation S-K, in 17 CFR 229.105, tells the registrant, where appropriate, to provide under the caption “Risk Factors” a discussion of the material factors that make an investment in the registrant or offering speculative or risky [@CL-FPA-0041]. The discussion must be organized logically with relevant headings, and each risk factor should be set forth under a subcaption that adequately describes the risk [@CL-FPA-0042]. Risks that could apply generically to any registrant or offering are discouraged, and any generic risk factors presented are disclosed at the end of the risk factor section under the caption “General Risk Factors” [@CL-FPA-0043].

Under Item 105(b), the registrant concisely explains how each risk affects the registrant or the securities being offered [@CL-FPA-0044]. If the discussion is longer than 15 pages, the forepart of the prospectus or annual report must include a series of concise, bulleted or numbered statements of no more than two pages summarizing the principal factors [@CL-FPA-0045].

## How it works

Each risk factor should be set out under a subcaption that describes the risk, with an explanation of how it affects the registrant or the securities offered [@CL-FPA-0042; @CL-FPA-0044]. Generic risk factors, to the extent they are presented, go at the end under their own caption [@CL-FPA-0043]. A discussion longer than 15 pages needs a short summary of the principal factors in the forepart [@CL-FPA-0045].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor lists a risk that its main Government program may be terminated early. It states the effect: the program supplies most of a segment's revenue. It places a generic risk about economic conditions at the end under a general heading.

## Never confuse

A risk factor is a material factor that makes an investment speculative or risky [@CL-FPA-0041]. The printed text asks for no forecast and no reserve amount. Risk factors also differ from the critical estimates discussion, which explains estimation uncertainty in reported amounts [@CL-FPA-0036]; see [Critical accounting estimates in MD&A](fpa-critical-accounting-estimates.md).
