## Purpose

Shows what a natural person may file in lieu of the financial statements otherwise required, and when separate financial statements for businesses owned or controlled by the person must be separately presented or may be omitted under § 210.3-17 [@CL-FPA-0299; @CL-FPA-0300; @CL-FPA-0301; @CL-FPA-0304].

In lieu of the financial statements otherwise required, a natural person may file an unaudited balance sheet as of a date within 90 days of date of filing and unaudited statements of comprehensive income for each of the three most recent fiscal years [@CL-FPA-0299; @CL-FPA-0300].

Paragraph (b) requires financial statements conforming with the instructions as to financial statements of subsidiaries not consolidated and 50 percent or less owned persons under § 210.3-09(a) to be separately presented for: (1) each business owned as a sole proprietor [@CL-FPA-0301]; (2) each partnership, business trust, unincorporated association, or similar business organization of which the person holds a controlling interest [@CL-FPA-0302]; and (3) each corporation of which the person, directly or indirectly, owns securities representing more than 50 percent of the voting power [@CL-FPA-0303]. For the separate statements of a majority-owned subsidiary that is not consolidated, see [Group reporting notes and separate subsidiary statements under Regulation S-X](../../close-revrec/group-bridge/cls-sx-group-reporting-notes.md).

Paragraph (c) provides that separate financial statements may be omitted, however, for each corporation, business trust, unincorporated association, or similar business organization if the person's total investment in such entity does not exceed 5 percent of his total assets and the person's total income from such entity does not exceed 5 percent of his gross income [@CL-FPA-0304; @CL-FPA-0305]; provided, that the person's aggregate investment in and income from all such omitted entities shall not exceed 15 percent of his total assets and gross income, respectively [@CL-FPA-0306].

## How it works

Under the alternative in paragraph (a), a natural person may file an unaudited balance sheet as of a date within 90 days of the date of filing and unaudited statements of comprehensive income for each of the three most recent fiscal years [@CL-FPA-0299; @CL-FPA-0300].

Under paragraph (b), financial statements conforming with § 210.3-09(a) shall be separately presented for each business owned as a sole proprietor, each partnership, business trust, unincorporated association, or similar business organization of which the person holds a controlling interest, and each corporation of which the person, directly or indirectly, owns securities representing more than 50 percent of the voting power [@CL-FPA-0301; @CL-FPA-0302; @CL-FPA-0303]. Under paragraph (c), separate financial statements may be omitted for each corporation, business trust, unincorporated association, or similar business organization if the person's total investment in the entity does not exceed 5 percent of his total assets, the person's total income from the entity does not exceed 5 percent of his gross income, and the person's aggregate investment in and income from all such omitted entities do not exceed 15 percent of his total assets and gross income, respectively [@CL-FPA-0304; @CL-FPA-0305; @CL-FPA-0306].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A natural person must file financial statements. The person files an unaudited balance sheet as of a date 60 days before filing, and unaudited statements of comprehensive income for each of the three most recent fiscal years, in lieu of the statements otherwise required [@CL-FPA-0299; @CL-FPA-0300].

The person owns securities carrying more than 50 percent of the voting power of five corporations, A to E, so separate statements are presented for each unless paragraph (c) allows omission [@CL-FPA-0303; @CL-FPA-0304]. Total assets are 2,000,000 and gross income is 500,000. Corporations A to D each account for 80,000 of investment (4 percent of total assets) and 20,000 of income (4 percent of gross income). Each passes both entity-level tests [@CL-FPA-0304; @CL-FPA-0305]. Corporation E accounts for 140,000 of investment, which is 7 percent of total assets. It fails the investment test, so its separate statements are still required [@CL-FPA-0304].

Omitting all four of A to D would total 320,000 of investment and 80,000 of income, which is 16 percent of each total. That exceeds the 15 percent aggregate limit, so the person may not omit all four [@CL-FPA-0306]. Omitting any three totals 240,000 and 60,000, which is 12 percent of each total. That is within the limit [@CL-FPA-0306].

## Never confuse

In paragraph (b)(3), the more-than-50-percent threshold applies to securities representing voting power of a corporation [@CL-FPA-0303], whereas in paragraph (c) the 5 percent entity-level thresholds and the 15 percent aggregate proviso compare the person's investment in and income from the entity (and all such omitted entities) against 5 percent and 15 percent of his total assets and gross income, respectively [@CL-FPA-0304; @CL-FPA-0305; @CL-FPA-0306].

## Company calibration

Whether each controlled entity's investment and income are measured on the same total asset and gross income figures used in the omission test [@Q-017].
