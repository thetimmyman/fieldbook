## Purpose

Shows what a natural person may file in place of the financial statements otherwise required, and when separate statements for businesses the person owns or controls may be left out. The rule is part of Regulation S-X, which is not FAR, DFARS or CAS.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Not established. |
| Codified citation | Not established. |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Not established. |
| Applies to | Natural persons filing financial statements [@CL-FPA-0299] |

In lieu of the financial statements otherwise required, a natural person may file an unaudited balance sheet as of a date within 90 days of date of filing and unaudited statements of comprehensive income for each of the three most recent fiscal years [@CL-FPA-0299; @CL-FPA-0300]. The text says "may" and names no one who approves the alternative [@CL-FPA-0299].

Paragraph (b) requires separate presentation of financial statements that conform with the instructions for subsidiaries not consolidated and 50 percent or less owned persons under § 210.3-09(a) [@CL-FPA-0301]. Those statements shall be separately presented for three groups [@CL-FPA-0301; @CL-FPA-0302; @CL-FPA-0303]. The first is each business owned as a sole proprietor [@CL-FPA-0301]. The second is each partnership, business trust, unincorporated association, or similar business organization of which the person holds a controlling interest [@CL-FPA-0302]. The third is each corporation of which the person, directly or indirectly, owns securities representing more than 50 percent of the voting power [@CL-FPA-0303]. The text does not say who prepares them, and it gives no percentage for a controlling interest [@CL-FPA-0302]. For the separate statements of a majority-owned subsidiary that is not consolidated, see [Group reporting notes and separate subsidiary statements under Regulation S-X](../../close-revrec/group-bridge/cls-sx-group-reporting-notes.md).

Paragraph (c) then allows separate financial statements to be omitted for each corporation, business trust, unincorporated association, or similar business organization if two entity-level tests are met [@CL-FPA-0304; @CL-FPA-0305]. The person's total investment in the entity must not exceed 5 percent of his total assets [@CL-FPA-0304]. The person's total income from the entity must not exceed 5 percent of his gross income [@CL-FPA-0305]. A proviso adds an aggregate test: the person's aggregate investment in and income from all such omitted entities shall not exceed 15 percent of his total assets and gross income, respectively [@CL-FPA-0306].

## How it works

The section names no deciding body and no filing deadline. Under the alternative in paragraph (a), the balance sheet date must be within 90 days of the date of filing [@CL-FPA-0299].

The omission in paragraph (c) is permissive: separate statements "may be omitted" when both entity-level tests and the aggregate proviso are met [@CL-FPA-0304; @CL-FPA-0305; @CL-FPA-0306]. The paragraph (c) list names corporations, business trusts, unincorporated associations and similar business organizations. It does not name a partnership or a sole proprietorship, although paragraph (b) does [@CL-FPA-0304; @CL-FPA-0301; @CL-FPA-0302]. The text does not say whether a partnership is a similar business organization for paragraph (c), so this card draws no conclusion on that point.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A natural person must file financial statements. The person files an unaudited balance sheet as of a date 60 days before filing, and unaudited statements of comprehensive income for each of the three most recent fiscal years, in lieu of the statements otherwise required [@CL-FPA-0299; @CL-FPA-0300].

The person owns securities carrying more than 50 percent of the voting power of five corporations, A to E, so separate statements are presented for each unless paragraph (c) allows omission [@CL-FPA-0303; @CL-FPA-0304]. Total assets are 2,000,000 and gross income is 500,000. Corporations A to D each account for 80,000 of investment (4 percent of total assets) and 20,000 of income (4 percent of gross income). Each passes both entity-level tests [@CL-FPA-0304; @CL-FPA-0305]. Corporation E accounts for 140,000 of investment, which is 7 percent of total assets. It fails the investment test, so its separate statements are still required [@CL-FPA-0304].

Omitting all four of A to D would total 320,000 of investment and 80,000 of income, which is 16 percent of each total. That exceeds the 15 percent aggregate limit, so the person may not omit all four [@CL-FPA-0306]. Omitting any three totals 240,000 and 60,000, which is 12 percent of each total. That is within the limit [@CL-FPA-0306]. The text does not say which three.

## Never confuse

The 50 percent figure in paragraph (b) is a test of voting power, and the 5 percent and 15 percent figures in paragraph (c) are tests of investment and income [@CL-FPA-0303; @CL-FPA-0304; @CL-FPA-0305; @CL-FPA-0306]. The 5 percent and 15 percent figures are measured against the person's own total assets or gross income, not against the entity's figures [@CL-FPA-0304; @CL-FPA-0305; @CL-FPA-0306].

Passing both entity-level tests is not the end of it, because the aggregate proviso applies across all the omitted entities [@CL-FPA-0306].

## Company calibration

Whether each controlled entity's investment and income are measured on the same total asset and gross income figures used in the omission test [@Q-017].
