## Purpose

Shows what a registrant discloses about each director, executive officer, promoter and control person under Item 401 of Regulation S-K, and how far back each disclosure looks [@CL-FPA-0182; @CL-FPA-0187; @CL-FPA-0189; @CL-FPA-0191].

| Applies to | A registrant, for its directors, nominees and executive officers; the promoter and control person rules reach a registrant that has not been subject to the reporting requirements for the twelve months before the filing, and not a subsidiary of a registrant that has been reporting for those twelve months [@CL-FPA-0182; @CL-FPA-0183; @CL-FPA-0188; @CL-FPA-0190; @CL-FPA-0235] |

Item 401(a) requires the registrant to list the names and ages of all directors and of all persons nominated or chosen to become directors, to indicate all positions and offices held with the registrant, and to state each person's term of office as director [@CL-FPA-0182]. Item 401(b) asks the same of executive officers and of persons chosen to become executive officers, with the term of office as officer [@CL-FPA-0183]. Item 401(e)(1) adds a brief description of the business experience during the past five years of each director, executive officer, nominee and each person named in answer to paragraph (c) of that Item [@CL-FPA-0184]. For each director or nominee it also calls for a brief discussion of the specific experience, qualifications, attributes or skills that led to the conclusion that the person should serve as a director [@CL-FPA-0185]. Item 401(e)(2) covers other directorships held, including those held during the past five years, by each director or nominee in any company with a class of securities registered pursuant to section 12 of the Exchange Act [@CL-FPA-0186], in any company subject to the requirements of section 15(d) of the Exchange Act, and in any company registered as an investment company under the Investment Company Act of 1940, naming the company [@CL-FPA-0232]. The printed section also covers significant employees, family relationships and instructions for proxy statements, which these claims do not restate.

Item 401(f) requires the registrant to describe the listed events that occurred during the past ten years and that are material to an evaluation of the ability or integrity of a director, a nominee or an executive officer [@CL-FPA-0187]. For computing the ten-year period, the date of a reportable event is deemed the date on which the final order, judgment or decree was entered, or the date on which any rights of appeal from preliminary orders, judgments or decrees have lapsed [@CL-FPA-0233]. For a bankruptcy petition the computation date is the date of filing for an uncontested petition or the date upon which approval of a contested petition became final [@CL-FPA-0234]. The printed paragraph also lists the events themselves and gives further instructions, including one on events omitted as immaterial and one on mitigating circumstances, which these claims do not restate. Item 401(g)(1) is addressed to a registrant that has not been subject to the reporting requirements of section 13(a) or 15(d) of the Exchange Act for the twelve months immediately before the filing and that had a promoter at any time during the past five fiscal years [@CL-FPA-0188; @CL-FPA-0189]. A registrant within its scope that had a promoter at any time during the past five fiscal years describes, for any promoter, the listed events from paragraphs (f)(1) through (f)(6) that occurred during the past five years and are material to a voting or investment decision [@CL-FPA-0189]. Item 401(g)(2) addresses control persons in the same way for a registrant not subject to those reporting requirements for the twelve months before the filing [@CL-FPA-0190], and calls for a description of the same listed events that occurred during the past five years and are material to a voting or investment decision [@CL-FPA-0191]. Instruction 2 to paragraph (g) says that paragraph (g) does not apply to any subsidiary of a registrant that has been reporting pursuant to section 13(a) or 15(d) of the Exchange Act for the twelve months immediately before the filing [@CL-FPA-0235].

## How it works

The registrant lists each director, nominee and executive officer with age, positions and term [@CL-FPA-0182; @CL-FPA-0183]. It adds a five-year business history and other directorships [@CL-FPA-0184; @CL-FPA-0185; @CL-FPA-0186], and screens for the listed legal events over ten years [@CL-FPA-0187; @CL-FPA-0233]. A registrant that has not been reporting and that had a promoter at any time during the past five fiscal years screens that promoter for the events in the first six listed paragraphs over five years, and a registrant that has not been reporting screens control persons for those events over five years [@CL-FPA-0189; @CL-FPA-0190; @CL-FPA-0191].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A company that has never filed reports prepares its first registration statement. It lists a director with her age, positions and term. She served on the boards of two other public companies over the past four years, so both are named. A court order that was never reversed enjoined her from acting as a broker. It was entered eight years ago and is material, so it is described. A similar order entered twelve years ago is outside the ten-year look-back. The company also had a promoter two years ago, so it describes the promoter's listed events from the past five years that are material to a voting or investment decision.

## Never confuse

This card covers who the people are and what their record is. Transactions between the registrant and those people are a separate disclosure; see [Transactions with related persons](fpa-related-person-transactions.md). The look-back for the listed legal events is ten years for directors, nominees and executive officers, which is longer than the five-year look-back for promoters and control persons [@CL-FPA-0187; @CL-FPA-0189; @CL-FPA-0191]. What those people own is covered in the [security ownership card](fpa-security-ownership-and-section-16a-compliance.md), and director independence and board committees are covered in [Corporate governance disclosure](fpa-corporate-governance-disclosure.md).
