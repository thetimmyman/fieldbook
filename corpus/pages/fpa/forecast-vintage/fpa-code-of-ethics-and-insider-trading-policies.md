## Purpose

Shows what a registrant discloses about its code of ethics under Item 406 of Regulation S-K and about insider trading arrangements and policies under Item 408 [@CL-FPA-0199; @CL-FPA-0203; @CL-FPA-0205].

| Applies to | A registrant, for its code of ethics covering the named officers and for the trading arrangements of its directors and officers and its insider trading policies [@CL-FPA-0199; @CL-FPA-0203; @CL-FPA-0205] |

Item 406(a) requires disclosure of whether the registrant has adopted a code of ethics that applies to its principal executive officer, principal financial officer, principal accounting officer or controller, or persons performing similar functions [@CL-FPA-0199]. If the registrant has not adopted the code of ethics that the paragraph describes, it must explain why it has not done so [@CL-FPA-0239]. Item 406(b) defines a code of ethics as written standards reasonably designed to deter wrongdoing and to promote, among listed elements, honest and ethical conduct including the ethical handling of actual or apparent conflicts of interest [@CL-FPA-0200]. Item 406(c) says the registrant must do one of the alternatives it lists, and the first alternative is to file with the Commission, as an exhibit to its annual report, a copy of its code of ethics that applies to the named officers or persons performing similar functions [@CL-FPA-0201]. The second alternative is to post the text of the code on the registrant's website and disclose in the annual report its Internet address and the fact that it has posted the code there, and the list joins it by "or" to a third alternative [@CL-FPA-0202]. The third alternative is to undertake in the annual report to provide a copy of the code to any person without charge, upon request, and to explain the manner in which a request may be made [@CL-FPA-0240]. The printed section also sets out further elements of the definition, a disclosure option for amendments and waivers, and instructions, which these claims do not restate.

Item 408(a)(1) introduces disclosure of whether, during the last fiscal quarter (the fourth fiscal quarter in the case of an annual report), any director or officer adopted or terminated the listed trading arrangements [@CL-FPA-0203]. One listed arrangement is any contract, instruction or written plan for the purchase or sale of securities of the registrant intended to satisfy the affirmative defense conditions of Rule 10b5-1(c), called a Rule 10b5-1 trading arrangement [@CL-FPA-0204]. Item 408(a)(2) requires identifying whether the trading arrangement is intended to satisfy the affirmative defense of Rule 10b5-1(c) [@CL-FPA-1230] and a description of the material terms of the trading arrangement, other than terms with respect to the price at which the individual executing it is authorized to trade [@CL-FPA-0241]. Item 408(b)(1) requires disclosure of whether the registrant has adopted insider trading policies and procedures governing the purchase, sale, and/or other dispositions of its securities by directors, officers and employees, or the registrant itself [@CL-FPA-0205]. It describes them as policies and procedures reasonably designed to promote compliance with insider trading laws, rules and regulations and any applicable listing standards, and tells a registrant that has not adopted them to explain why it has not done so [@CL-FPA-0242]. Item 408(b)(2) requires a registrant that has adopted such policies and procedures to file them as an exhibit, but if all of the registrant's insider trading policies and procedures are included in its code of ethics and the code of ethics is filed as an exhibit under Item 406(c)(1), that would satisfy the exhibit requirement [@CL-FPA-0243; @CL-FPA-1222]. Under Item 408(c), a covered person has entered into a non-Rule 10b5-1 trading arrangement where two conditions both apply, joined by "and" [@CL-FPA-1231], the first being that the covered person asserts that when unaware of material nonpublic information about the security or its issuer they had adopted a written arrangement for trading [@CL-FPA-0206]. The printed section gives further conditions for that definition and the interactive data requirement, which these claims do not restate.

## How it works

The registrant says whether it has a code of ethics for the named officers [@CL-FPA-0199] and deals with one of the filing, posting or undertaking alternatives in the list for its code [@CL-FPA-0201; @CL-FPA-0202; @CL-FPA-0240]. It separately says whether directors or officers adopted or terminated a listed trading arrangement in the last fiscal quarter and describes its material terms [@CL-FPA-0203; @CL-FPA-0204; @CL-FPA-0241], and says whether it has insider trading policies and files them if it has [@CL-FPA-0205; @CL-FPA-0243].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A company's code applies to its chief executive, chief financial officer and controller. It posts the code on its website and gives the address in its annual report. In the last fiscal quarter a director adopted a written plan to sell 10,000 shares that was meant to satisfy Rule 10b5-1. The company discloses that adoption and says it has written insider trading policies.

## Never confuse

The code of ethics here is a securities disclosure item about written standards for senior financial officers. It is not the contract clause on a contractor code of business ethics; see [Contractor code of business ethics](../../compliance/regulatory-framework/cmp-contractor-code-of-business-ethics.md). A code of ethics is also not the same as insider trading policies, which are asked for in a separate item [@CL-FPA-0199; @CL-FPA-0205]. Who the directors and officers are is covered in [Directors, executive officers, promoters and control persons](fpa-directors-officers-and-control-persons.md), and board committees in [Corporate governance disclosure](fpa-corporate-governance-disclosure.md). Which documents are filed as exhibits, and how the exhibit table and index work, is on [Exhibits: the exhibit table, the index and the general instructions](fpa-exhibits-general-instructions.md).
