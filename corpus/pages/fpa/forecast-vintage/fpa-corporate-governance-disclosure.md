## Purpose

Shows what a registrant discloses about director independence, board meetings, standing committees and the audit committee financial expert under Item 407 of Regulation S-K [@CL-FPA-0207; @CL-FPA-0209; @CL-FPA-0210; @CL-FPA-0247].

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Not established. |
| Codified citation | Not established. |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Not established. |
| Applies to | A registrant, for its directors and nominees, its board meetings, its standing committees and its audit committee [@CL-FPA-0207; @CL-FPA-0209; @CL-FPA-0210; @CL-FPA-0247] |

Item 407(a) requires the registrant to identify each director, and in a proxy or information statement relating to the election of directors each nominee, that is independent under the independence standards applicable to the registrant [@CL-FPA-0207]. For each director and nominee for director identified as independent, Item 407(a)(3) requires a description, by specific category or type, of any transactions, relationships or arrangements not disclosed under Item 404(a) that were considered by the board of directors under the applicable independence definitions in determining that the director is independent [@CL-FPA-0244; @CL-FPA-0259]. Item 407(b)(1) requires the registrant to state the total number of board meetings held during the last full fiscal year and to name each incumbent director who attended fewer than 75 percent of the aggregate of the listed meetings [@CL-FPA-0209]. That aggregate is the total number of board meetings held during the period for which the director has been a director plus the total number of meetings held by all committees of the board on which the director served during the periods of service [@CL-FPA-0245].

Item 407(b)(3) requires the registrant to state whether it has standing audit, nominating and compensation committees of the board of directors, or committees performing similar functions [@CL-FPA-0210]. If it has such committees, it must identify each member, state the number of meetings each held during the last fiscal year and describe briefly each committee's functions [@CL-FPA-0211]. Item 407(b)(3) says this committee disclosure need not be provided to the extent it is duplicative of disclosure provided in accordance with paragraph (c), (d) or (e) [@CL-FPA-0246]. If the registrant has no standing nominating committee or committee performing similar functions, Item 407(c)(1) requires it to state the basis for the view of the board of directors that it is appropriate not to have such a committee and to identify each director who participates in the consideration of director nominees [@CL-FPA-0212].

Item 407(d)(5)(i)(A) requires the registrant to disclose that its board of directors has determined that the registrant either has at least one audit committee financial expert serving on its audit committee or does not have one [@CL-FPA-0247]. If it discloses that it does not have one, Item 407(d)(5)(i)(C) requires it to explain why [@CL-FPA-0248]. The printed section also covers further independence rules, the nomination process, the audit and compensation committee paragraphs, shareholder communications, board leadership, hedging and exceptions for smaller reporting companies and emerging growth companies, which these claims do not restate.

## How it works

The registrant identifies who is independent under the standards that apply to it [@CL-FPA-0207] and describes the considered relationships [@CL-FPA-0208]. It reports meeting counts and low attendance [@CL-FPA-0209], and describes its standing committees or explains why it has no nominating committee [@CL-FPA-0210; @CL-FPA-0211; @CL-FPA-0212]. It also says whether the board has determined that it has an audit committee financial expert, and explains why if it has none [@CL-FPA-0247; @CL-FPA-0248].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A board has 8 directors, and the applicable standard finds 6 independent. The company names those 6 and describes, by category, a consulting arrangement the board considered. The board met 9 times. It has determined that the company has one audit committee financial expert, so the company says so. One director attended 70 percent of the aggregate of board and committee meetings, so the company names that director. The board has no nominating committee, so it states why that is appropriate.

## Never confuse

Independence is a board finding under the applicable standard, and the transactions already disclosed as related person transactions are not part of its description [@CL-FPA-0208; @CL-FPA-0244]; see [Transactions with related persons](fpa-related-person-transactions.md). Who the directors are and what their records show is covered in [Directors, executive officers, promoters and control persons](fpa-directors-officers-and-control-persons.md), and the code of ethics in [Code of ethics and insider trading arrangements and policies](fpa-code-of-ethics-and-insider-trading-policies.md). A standing nominating committee is also different from a board with no such committee, which must state why that is appropriate [@CL-FPA-0212].
