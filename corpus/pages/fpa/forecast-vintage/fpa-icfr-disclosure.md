## Purpose

Shows what management's annual report on internal control over financial reporting contains, when an attestation report is provided and what quarterly change is disclosed under Item 308 of Regulation S-K [@CL-FPA-0138; @CL-FPA-0143; @CL-FPA-0144].

## The rule

| Field | Value |

| Applies to | A registrant providing management's report on its internal control over financial reporting [@CL-FPA-0138] |

Item 308(a) requires a report of management on the registrant's internal control over financial reporting that contains the items listed in the paragraph [@CL-FPA-0138]. One listed content is a statement identifying the framework used by management to evaluate effectiveness [@CL-FPA-0139]. Another is management's assessment of effectiveness as of the end of the most recent fiscal year, including a statement as to whether or not it is effective [@CL-FPA-0140]. The discussion must include disclosure of any material weakness in the registrant's internal control over financial reporting identified by management [@CL-FPA-0176]. Management is not permitted to conclude that internal control is effective if there are one or more material weaknesses [@CL-FPA-0141]. Another listed content is a statement of management's responsibility for establishing and maintaining adequate internal control over financial reporting for the registrant [@CL-FPA-0260]. A further listed content applies if the registrant is an accelerated filer or a large accelerated filer, or otherwise includes in its annual report a registered public accounting firm's attestation report on internal control over financial reporting [@CL-FPA-0261]. It is a statement that the registered public accounting firm that audited the financial statements included in the annual report has issued an attestation report on the registrant's internal control over financial reporting [@CL-FPA-0262].

Paragraph (b) of Item 308 is stated for a registrant, other than one that is an emerging growth company as defined in the cited rules, that is an accelerated filer or a large accelerated filer [@CL-FPA-0142]. It states that the registered public accounting firm's attestation report on internal control over financial reporting is provided in the registrant's annual report containing the disclosure required by Item 308 [@CL-FPA-0143; @CL-FPA-0142; @CL-FPA-0267].

Item 308(c) directs the registrant to disclose any change in its internal control over financial reporting identified in connection with the required evaluation that occurred during its last fiscal quarter [@CL-FPA-0144]. The change concerned is one that has materially affected, or is reasonably likely to materially affect, the registrant's internal control over financial reporting, and the fourth fiscal quarter is the period for an annual report [@CL-FPA-0145].

Instruction 1 says a registrant need not comply with paragraphs (a) and (b) until it either had been required to file an annual report under section 13(a) or 15(d) of the Securities Exchange Act of 1934 for the prior fiscal year or had filed an annual report with the Commission for the prior fiscal year [@CL-FPA-0175]. A registrant that does not comply shall include a statement in the first annual report that it files in substantially the following form [@CL-FPA-0263]. The form says the annual report does not include a report of management's assessment regarding internal control over financial reporting or an attestation report of the company's registered public accounting firm [@CL-FPA-0264], due to a transition period established by rules of the Securities and Exchange Commission for newly public companies [@CL-FPA-0266].

Instruction 2 says the registrant must maintain evidential matter, including documentation, to provide reasonable support for management's assessment of the effectiveness of its internal control over financial reporting [@CL-FPA-0265].

Item 307 is a neighbouring item on disclosure controls and procedures [@CL-FPA-1000]. It requires disclosure of the conclusions of the registrant's principal executive and principal financial officers, or persons performing similar functions, regarding the effectiveness of the registrant's disclosure controls and procedures [@CL-FPA-1000]. The disclosure controls and procedures are as defined in § 240.13a-15(e) or § 240.15d-15(e) of the same chapter, the conclusions are as of the end of the period covered by the report, and they are based on the evaluation required by paragraph (b) of § 240.13a-15 or § 240.15d-15 [@CL-FPA-1001].

## How it works

Management states the framework it used and assesses effectiveness at year end [@CL-FPA-0139; @CL-FPA-0140]. If it finds a material weakness, it cannot conclude that the control is effective [@CL-FPA-0141]. A registrant that is an accelerated filer or a large accelerated filer, and is not an emerging growth company as defined in the cited rules, has the attestation report of the registered public accounting firm provided in its annual report [@CL-FPA-0142; @CL-FPA-0143]. A registrant that has not yet reached the point described in Instruction 1 need not comply with paragraphs (a) and (b), but if it does not comply it states in the first annual report it files, in substantially the printed form, that the report omits management's assessment report and the attestation report because of the transition period for newly public companies [@CL-FPA-0175; @CL-FPA-0263; @CL-FPA-0264; @CL-FPA-0266]. Management keeps evidential matter, including documentation, that reasonably supports its assessment [@CL-FPA-0265]. Each quarter, the registrant checks for changes that have materially affected, or are reasonably likely to materially affect, its control [@CL-FPA-0144; @CL-FPA-0145].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A registrant uses a recognised control framework and reviews its controls at year end. Its review finds one material weakness in how journal entries are approved. The report says the control is not effective and describes the weakness. In the fourth quarter it also replaced its ledger system, which is likely to materially affect its control, so the change is disclosed.

Second example: a company files its first annual report soon after going public and has not yet reached the point where it must give management's report. Answer: it puts the printed form of transition-period statement in that first report, saying the report omits management's assessment and the attestation report.

## Never confuse

Management's report and the attestation report are different documents: the first is a report of management [@CL-FPA-0138], and the second is the report of the registered public accounting firm [@CL-FPA-0143]. The statement in management's report that the firm has issued an attestation report is likewise not the attestation report itself [@CL-FPA-0262]. The attestation report is also different from the disclosure of a change in the independent accountant, which is a separate Item that asks whether the former accountant resigned, declined to stand for re-election or was dismissed [@CL-FPA-0123]; see [Changes in and disagreements with accountants](fpa-accountant-changes-disclosure.md). A material weakness also differs from a change in control during the quarter: the first bars a conclusion of effectiveness [@CL-FPA-0141], and the second is disclosed when it has materially affected, or is reasonably likely to materially affect, the control [@CL-FPA-0145].
