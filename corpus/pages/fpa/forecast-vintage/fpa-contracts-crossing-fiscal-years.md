## Purpose

Shows when a contract funded by annual appropriations may run across a fiscal year, so a forecast can say which part of the value belongs to which year.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR and codified DFARS [@CL-FPA-0063] |
| Codified citation | FAR 32.703-3; DFARS 232.703-3 [@CL-FPA-0063; @CL-FPA-0066; @CL-FPA-0068] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-FPA-0063] |
| Applies to | Contracts funded by annual appropriations [@CL-FPA-0063], and contracts, options or orders for severable services for a period that does not exceed one year [@CL-FPA-0066] |

A contract that is funded by annual appropriations may not cross fiscal years, except in accordance with statutory authorization [@CL-FPA-0063]. The bar also does not apply when the contract calls for an end product that cannot feasibly be subdivided for separate performance in each fiscal year, such as a contract for expert or consultant services [@CL-FPA-0064].

Paragraph (b) of the FAR section is listed as one example of such statutory authorization [@CL-FPA-0063]. Under it, the head of an executive agency, except NASA, may enter into a contract, exercise an option, or place an order for severable services for a period that begins in one fiscal year and ends in the next [@CL-FPA-0065; @CL-FPA-0066]. The period of the contract, option or order must not exceed one year [@CL-FPA-0066]. Funds made available for a fiscal year may be obligated for the total amount of an action entered into under this authority [@CL-FPA-0067]. The DFARS states that the contracting officer may do the same for a period that does not exceed one year [@CL-FPA-0068].

## How it works

The printed default for a contract funded by annual appropriations is that it does not cross a fiscal year [@CL-FPA-0063]. The text lifts that default for statutory authorization, which includes the severable-services authority, and for an end product that cannot be subdivided [@CL-FPA-0063; @CL-FPA-0064]. The severable-services authority allows a period that straddles two fiscal years if it does not exceed one year [@CL-FPA-0066]. Because funds made available for a fiscal year may be obligated for the total amount of such an action, the whole action can be obligated from those funds [@CL-FPA-0067].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A severable-services order of 1,200,000 covers twelve months and starts in month ten of a fiscal year. Three months fall in the first fiscal year and nine in the second. Funds made available for the first fiscal year may be obligated for the whole 1,200,000. The forecast shows 300,000 of revenue in the first year and 900,000 in the second, with a note that the order rests on the severable-services authority.

## Never confuse

The bar on crossing fiscal years concerns contracts funded by annual appropriations [@CL-FPA-0063]. It is not the same as the one-year limit on a severable-services period, which is a condition of the authority to straddle two fiscal years [@CL-FPA-0066]. For contracts conditioned on availability of funds, see [Contracts conditioned on availability of funds](../backlog/fpa-funds-availability-conditioned-contracts.md). For the funding notices on contracts that are funded in steps, see [Contract funding: incremental funding policy](../../contract-finance/funding/ctf-incremental-funding-policy.md).
