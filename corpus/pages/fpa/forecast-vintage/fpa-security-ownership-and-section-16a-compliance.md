## Purpose

Shows who is listed in the beneficial ownership tables under Item 403 of Regulation S-K and which late Section 16(a) reports are disclosed under Item 405 [@CL-FPA-0192; @CL-FPA-0193; @CL-FPA-0196; @CL-FPA-0197].

## The rule

| Applies to | A registrant, for holders of more than five percent of a class of voting securities and for its directors and executive officers; Item 405 applies to a registrant with a class of equity securities registered under Section 12 and to closed-end investment companies [@CL-FPA-0192; @CL-FPA-0193; @CL-FPA-0195] |

Item 403(a) applies to any person, including any group, who is known to the registrant to be the beneficial owner of more than five percent of any class of the registrant's voting securities [@CL-FPA-0192]. Item 403(b) covers securities beneficially owned by all directors and nominees (named), each named executive officer as defined in Item 402(a)(3), and the directors and executive officers as a group (not named) [@CL-FPA-0193]. It also requires the registrant to indicate, by footnote or otherwise, the amount of shares shown that are pledged as security and the amount of shares the persons have the right to acquire beneficial ownership of [@CL-FPA-0194]. Item 403(c) requires the registrant to describe any arrangements, known to the registrant, including any pledge by any person of securities of the registrant or any of its parents, the operation of which may at a subsequent date result in a change in control of the registrant [@CL-FPA-0236]. The printed section also gives instructions on calculating percentages, determining beneficial ownership and voting trusts, which these claims do not restate.

Item 405(a) is addressed to every registrant with a class of equity securities registered under Section 12 of the Exchange Act and to every closed-end investment company registered under the Investment Company Act of 1940, and introduces a list of what each must do [@CL-FPA-0195]. Item 405(a)(1) begins its description of the persons to identify with each person who, at any time during the fiscal year, was a director, officer or beneficial owner of more than ten percent of any class of the registrant's equity securities registered under Section 12 of the Exchange Act, or any other person subject to Section 16 of the Exchange Act with respect to the registrant because of the requirements of Section 30 of the Investment Company Act [@CL-FPA-0196; @CL-FPA-0345]. The paragraph goes on to have the registrant identify a person, called a reporting person, that failed to file on a timely basis reports required by Section 16(a) of the Exchange Act during the most recent fiscal year or prior fiscal years [@CL-FPA-0197]. For each such person, Item 405(a)(2) requires the number of late reports, the number of transactions not reported on a timely basis and any known failure to file a required form [@CL-FPA-0198]. Instruction 2 to paragraph (a) says the registrant is only required to disclose a failure to file timely once [@CL-FPA-0238]. Under Item 405(b), in determining whether disclosure is required under paragraph (a), the registrant may rely only on the listed items, the first of which is a review of Forms 3 and 4 and amendments thereto filed electronically with the Commission during the registrant's most recent fiscal year [@CL-FPA-0237; @CL-FPA-0350]. The printed section also gives an instruction on the caption, the other listed items the registrant may rely on, a rule on keeping written representations and examples of a known failure to file, which these claims do not restate.

## How it works

The registrant first finds the holders above the ownership threshold and the insiders in the management group [@CL-FPA-0192; @CL-FPA-0193], and describes any known arrangement that may result in a change in control [@CL-FPA-0236]. It then identifies each reporting person that failed to file Section 16(a) reports on a timely basis [@CL-FPA-0196; @CL-FPA-0197], and counts the late reports and the transactions not reported on time [@CL-FPA-0198]. A late filing is disclosed once [@CL-FPA-0238].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A holder owns 6 percent of a class of voting securities and is listed in the ownership table because the holding is above five percent. A holder with 4 percent is not listed under that test. A director filed one report late during the year for one purchase. The registrant discloses one late report and one transaction for that director.

## Never confuse

The five percent test picks the holders in the first ownership table, and directors, nominees and named executive officers are covered by a separate table without that test [@CL-FPA-0192; @CL-FPA-0193]. The late report description starts from each director, officer and owner of more than ten percent of a class [@CL-FPA-0196]. Ownership is also not the same as a transaction with a related person; see [Transactions with related persons](fpa-related-person-transactions.md). Who the directors and officers are is covered in [Directors, executive officers, promoters and control persons](fpa-directors-officers-and-control-persons.md). What the named executive officers are paid is a separate disclosure; see [Executive compensation scope, discussion and summary compensation table](fpa-executive-compensation-scope-and-summary-compensation-table.md), [Executive equity award tables](fpa-executive-equity-award-tables.md) and [Pension, deferred compensation, termination payments and director pay](fpa-pension-deferred-compensation-termination-and-director-pay.md). The comparison of pay with the median employee and with company results is in [Pay ratio disclosure](fpa-pay-ratio-disclosure.md) and [Pay versus performance disclosure](fpa-pay-versus-performance-disclosure.md).
