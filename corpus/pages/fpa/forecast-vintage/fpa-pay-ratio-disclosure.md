## Purpose

Shows what the pay ratio compares and who counts as an employee for it under paragraph (u) of Item 402 of Regulation S-K [@CL-FPA-1113; @CL-FPA-1117; @CL-FPA-1118].

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Not established. |
| Codified citation | Not established. |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Not established. |
| Applies to | A registrant that must provide the disclosure; a smaller reporting company may provide scaled disclosure instead and an emerging growth company is not required to comply [@CL-FPA-1113; @CL-FPA-1072; @CL-FPA-1129] |

The registrant discloses the median of the annual total compensation of all employees of the registrant except the PEO, the annual total compensation of the PEO, and the ratio of the first amount to the second [@CL-FPA-1113]. For the ratio, the median amount equals one, or the ratio may be expressed narratively as the multiple that the PEO amount bears to the median amount [@CL-FPA-1114]. Total compensation for the median employee and for the PEO is determined in accordance with paragraph (c)(2)(x) [@CL-FPA-1115]. For the pay ratio, registrant means the registrant and its consolidated subsidiaries [@CL-FPA-1116].

An employee is an individual employed by the registrant or any of its consolidated subsidiaries, whether as a full-time, part-time, seasonal or temporary worker [@CL-FPA-1117]. The employee count is taken as of a date chosen by the registrant within the last three months of its last completed fiscal year [@CL-FPA-1118], and the registrant is to disclose that date [@CL-FPA-1123]. The definition does not include workers who are employed, and whose compensation is determined, by an unaffiliated third party but who provide services as independent contractors or leased workers [@CL-FPA-1119]. One exemption applies where employees outside the United States account for 5% or less of the registrant's total employees, and a registrant that excludes any under it must exclude all of them [@CL-FPA-1120].

A registrant is required to identify its median employee only once every three years and calculate total compensation for that employee each year [@CL-FPA-1121]. That applies provided that, during the last completed fiscal year, there has been no change in its employee population or employee compensation arrangements that it reasonably believes would result in a significant change to its pay ratio disclosure [@CL-FPA-1122]. Registrants may use reasonable estimates in identifying the median employee and in calculating annual total compensation for employees other than the PEO [@CL-FPA-1125], and are to briefly describe the methodology used to identify the median employee [@CL-FPA-1126]. A registrant may annualize the total compensation for permanent employees employed for less than the full fiscal year [@CL-FPA-1127]. It may not annualize it for temporary or seasonal employees or make a full-time equivalent adjustment for any employee [@CL-FPA-1128].

The required pay ratio information must in any event be filed as provided in General Instruction G(3) of Form 10-K not later than 120 days after the end of the last completed fiscal year [@CL-FPA-1124].

The printed paragraph also gives the data privacy exemption and its legal opinion, the detail of the de minimis exemption, cost-of-living adjustments, sampling, items the registrant may include, multiple PEOs, transition rules for new registrants and acquisitions, supplemental ratios and limits on personal information, which these claims do not restate.

## How it works

The registrant fixes an employee count date in the last three months of the year [@CL-FPA-1118], finds the median employee by a reasonable method and describes it [@CL-FPA-1125; @CL-FPA-1126], determines that employee's total compensation and the PEO's in accordance with paragraph (c)(2)(x) [@CL-FPA-1115], and states the ratio [@CL-FPA-1113; @CL-FPA-1114].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A company with a December year end picks a November date to count employees. Its median employee has annual total compensation of 60,000 dollars and its PEO has 6,000,000 dollars, so the ratio is 100 to 1, or the PEO is paid 100 times the median. A permanent employee hired in July has pay annualized to a full year. A seasonal worker's pay is not annualized.

## Never confuse

The pay ratio compares one executive with the median employee. It is not the disclosure that compares pay with company results, which is in [Pay versus performance disclosure](fpa-pay-versus-performance-disclosure.md). The PEO's total compensation comes from the pay reporting in [Executive compensation scope, discussion and summary compensation table](fpa-executive-compensation-scope-and-summary-compensation-table.md). Who the executive officers are is covered in [Directors, executive officers, promoters and control persons](fpa-directors-officers-and-control-persons.md).
