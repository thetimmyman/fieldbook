## Purpose

Shows how a registrant identifies and explains its critical accounting estimates in Management's Discussion and Analysis, including why each critical accounting estimate is subject to uncertainty and, to the extent material and reasonably available, how much each estimate or assumption has changed over a relevant period and the sensitivity of the reported amount to the methods, assumptions and estimates underlying its calculation [@CL-FPA-0035; @CL-FPA-0036; @CL-FPA-0037; @CL-FPA-0038]. Regulation S-K states the requirements applicable to the content of the non-financial statement portions of: (1) registration statements under the Securities Act to the extent provided in the forms to be used for registration under such Act [@CL-FPA-0105]; and (2) registration statements under section 12, annual or other reports under sections 13 and 15(d), going-private transaction statements under section 13, tender offer statements under sections 13 and 14, annual reports to security holders and proxy and information statements under section 14, and any other documents required to be filed under the Exchange Act, to the extent provided in the forms and rules under such Act [@CL-FPA-0105; @CL-FPA-0318; @CL-FPA-0319; @CL-FPA-0106].

Item 303(b)(3) of Regulation S-K defines critical accounting estimates as those estimates made in accordance with generally accepted accounting principles that involve a significant level of estimation uncertainty and have had or are reasonably likely to have a material impact on the financial condition or results of operations of the registrant [@CL-FPA-0035]. It requires qualitative and quantitative information necessary to understand the estimation uncertainty and the impact the critical accounting estimate has had or is reasonably likely to have on financial condition or results of operations to the extent the information is material and reasonably available [@CL-FPA-0036].

This information should include why each critical accounting estimate is subject to uncertainty and, to the extent the information is material and reasonably available, how much each estimate and/or assumption has changed over a relevant period, and the sensitivity of the reported amount to the methods, assumptions and estimates underlying its calculation [@CL-FPA-0037; @CL-FPA-0038]. Instruction 3 to paragraph (b) of Item 303 states that the disclosure of critical accounting estimates must supplement, but not duplicate, the description of accounting policies or other disclosures in the notes to the financial statements [@CL-FPA-0039], and that a registrant must provide the analysis in a format that facilitates easy understanding and that supplements, and does not duplicate, disclosure already provided in the filing [@CL-FPA-0040].

## How it works

An estimate is a critical accounting estimate when it is made in accordance with generally accepted accounting principles, involves a significant level of estimation uncertainty, and has had or is reasonably likely to have a material impact on the financial condition or results of operations of the registrant [@CL-FPA-0035]. For each critical accounting estimate, the registrant provides qualitative and quantitative information necessary to understand the estimation uncertainty and the impact on financial condition or results of operations to the extent material and reasonably available, which should include why the estimate is subject to uncertainty and, to the extent material and reasonably available, how much each estimate and/or assumption has changed over a relevant period and the sensitivity of the reported amount to the underlying methods, assumptions and estimates [@CL-FPA-0036; @CL-FPA-0037; @CL-FPA-0038]. The registrant must provide the analysis in a format that facilitates easy understanding and that supplements, and does not duplicate, disclosure already provided in the filing, including the description of accounting policies or other disclosures in the notes to the financial statements [@CL-FPA-0039; @CL-FPA-0040]. Contract estimates at completion are covered in [Control account estimate updates](../../program-finance/eac/prg-evms-guideline-20-eac-updates.md) and [Program manager estimate at completion](../../program-finance/eac/prg-evms-guideline-23-program-manager-eac.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor's profit on a large fixed-price program depends on its cost estimate to complete, which carries high uncertainty. A change of 2 percent in that estimate moves earnings by 3 million, which is material. The discussion explains why the estimate is uncertain, shows how it has moved over the year, and gives the 3 million sensitivity.

## Never confuse

Item 303(b)(3) defines critical accounting estimates as estimates made in accordance with generally accepted accounting principles that involve a significant level of estimation uncertainty and have had or are reasonably likely to have a material impact on the financial condition or results of operations of the registrant [@CL-FPA-0035]. Under Instruction 3 to paragraph (b) of Item 303, the disclosure of critical accounting estimates must supplement, but not duplicate, the description of accounting policies or other disclosures in the notes to the financial statements and disclosure already provided in the filing [@CL-FPA-0039; @CL-FPA-0040]. For risk factor disclosure, see [Risk factor disclosure](fpa-risk-factors.md).

## Company calibration

Which open accounting judgments management treats as critical estimates, how they are tracked with the close and audit milestones, and whether each estimate is recorded as of a stated date so that changes can be traced. [@Q-006]
