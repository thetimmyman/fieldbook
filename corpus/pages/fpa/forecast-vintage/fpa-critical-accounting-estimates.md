## Purpose

Shows how a public defense contractor identifies and explains its critical accounting estimates in the management discussion, including why they are uncertain and how sensitive reported amounts are. Regulation S-K binds only SEC registrants (public companies), so a privately held contractor is not bound by it. For a private contractor the disclosure is a benchmark only.

Item 303(b)(3) of Regulation S-K, in 17 CFR 229.303, defines critical accounting estimates as estimates made under generally accepted accounting principles that involve a significant level of estimation uncertainty and have had or are reasonably likely to have a material impact on financial condition or results of operations [@CL-FPA-0035]. It calls for qualitative and quantitative information necessary to understand the estimation uncertainty and the impact of the estimate, to the extent the information is material and reasonably available [@CL-FPA-0036].

The information should include why each critical accounting estimate is subject to uncertainty [@CL-FPA-0037]. It also lists, to the extent it is material and reasonably available, how much each estimate or assumption has changed over a relevant period and the sensitivity of the reported amount to the methods, assumptions and estimates underlying its calculation [@CL-FPA-0037; @CL-FPA-0038]. The disclosure must supplement, but not duplicate, the description of accounting policies or other disclosures in the notes to the financial statements [@CL-FPA-0039].

## How it works

An estimate is critical when it meets the definition: a significant level of estimation uncertainty together with a material impact, actual or reasonably likely [@CL-FPA-0035]. For each one the registrant explains why it is uncertain and, to the extent material and reasonably available, how much it has changed and how sensitive the reported amount is [@CL-FPA-0037; @CL-FPA-0038]. The analysis supplements, and does not duplicate, disclosure already provided in the filing [@CL-FPA-0040]. Contract estimates at completion are covered in [Control account estimate updates](../../program-finance/eac/prg-evms-guideline-20-eac-updates.md) and [Program manager estimate at completion](../../program-finance/eac/prg-evms-guideline-23-program-manager-eac.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor's profit on a large fixed-price program depends on its cost estimate to complete, which carries high uncertainty. A change of 2 percent in that estimate moves earnings by 3 million, which is material. The discussion explains why the estimate is uncertain, shows how it has moved over the year, and gives the 3 million sensitivity.

## Never confuse

A critical accounting estimate is defined by estimation uncertainty and material impact [@CL-FPA-0035]. A contract estimate at completion is one only if it meets that definition [@CL-FPA-0035]. The disclosure also differs from the note on accounting policies, which it must not duplicate [@CL-FPA-0039]. The risk discussion is separate; see [Risk factor disclosure](fpa-risk-factors.md).

## Company calibration

Which open accounting judgments management treats as critical estimates, how they are tracked with the close and audit milestones, and whether each estimate is recorded as of a stated date so that changes can be traced. [@Q-006]
