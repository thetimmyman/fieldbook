## Purpose

Shows which pending legal proceedings a registrant describes under Item 103 of Regulation S-K and which it may leave out [@CL-FPA-0107; @CL-FPA-0109].

## The rule

| Applies to | A registrant, for material pending legal proceedings to which it or any subsidiary is a party or of which any of their property is the subject [@CL-FPA-0107] |

Item 103(a) requires a brief description of material pending legal proceedings, other than ordinary routine litigation incidental to the business, to which the registrant or any of its subsidiaries is a party or of which any of their property is the subject [@CL-FPA-0107]. The description names the court or agency, the date instituted, the principal parties, the alleged factual basis and the relief sought [@CL-FPA-0108]. Item 103(a) also requires similar information as to any such proceedings known to be contemplated by governmental authorities [@CL-FPA-0163]. Information may be provided by hyperlink or cross-reference to legal proceedings disclosure elsewhere in the document, such as in Management's Discussion and Analysis, Risk Factors and notes to the financial statements [@CL-FPA-0164].

Paragraph (b) of Item 103 states that no information need be given for the proceedings it lists [@CL-FPA-0109]. One listed omission is a proceeding that involves negligence or other claims or actions if the business ordinarily results in such claims or actions, unless the claim or action departs from the normal kind of such claims or actions [@CL-FPA-0157]. Another is a proceeding that involves primarily a claim for damages when the amount involved, excluding interest and costs, does not exceed 10 percent of the current assets of the registrant and its subsidiaries on a consolidated basis [@CL-FPA-0110]. If any proceeding presents in large degree the same legal or factual issues as other proceedings pending or known to be contemplated, the amount involved in such other proceedings is included in computing that percentage [@CL-FPA-0158]. Paragraph (c) applies notwithstanding paragraph (b) and lists matters that disclosure shall include, but is not limited to them [@CL-FPA-0111].

One matter in that list is an administrative or judicial proceeding, including proceedings which present in large degree the same issues, arising under Federal, State, or local provisions that regulate the discharge of materials into the environment [@CL-FPA-0112]. The provisions covered also include those adopted primarily for the purpose of protecting the environment [@CL-FPA-0159]. Such a proceeding is not deemed ordinary routine litigation and shall be described if the listed conditions are met [@CL-FPA-0113]. One condition is that the proceeding is material to the business or financial condition of the registrant [@CL-FPA-0160]. Another applies when the proceeding involves primarily a claim for damages, or involves potential monetary sanctions, capital expenditures, deferred charges or charges to income, and the amount involved, excluding interest and costs, exceeds 10 percent of consolidated current assets [@CL-FPA-0114]. A third applies when a governmental authority is a party and the proceeding involves potential monetary sanctions, unless the registrant reasonably believes the proceeding will result in no monetary sanctions or in monetary sanctions, excluding interest and costs, of less than $300,000 [@CL-FPA-0115].

For that third condition the registrant may instead elect another threshold [@CL-FPA-0116]. The elected threshold must be one the registrant determines is reasonably designed to result in disclosure of any such proceeding that is material to the business or financial condition [@CL-FPA-0116]. The registrant must disclose it, including any change, in each annual and quarterly report [@CL-FPA-0161]. A further condition on the elected threshold is that it does not exceed the lesser of $1 million or one percent of consolidated current assets, and similar proceedings may be grouped and described generically [@CL-FPA-0162].

## How it works

The registrant first asks whether a pending proceeding is material and falls outside ordinary routine litigation [@CL-FPA-0107]. It then checks the omissions in paragraph (b), such as the damages test tied to current assets, counting related proceedings together where the same issues arise [@CL-FPA-0110; @CL-FPA-0158]. Paragraph (c) applies notwithstanding paragraph (b), so an environmental proceeding that meets a listed condition is described even if paragraph (b) would otherwise excuse it [@CL-FPA-0111; @CL-FPA-0113]. The registrant also checks the monetary sanctions threshold or its elected threshold for proceedings with a governmental authority [@CL-FPA-0115; @CL-FPA-0116].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A registrant has consolidated current assets of 500,000. A claim for damages alone is 40,000, below the 50,000 that is ten percent of current assets, so the damages omission can apply if no paragraph (c) matter is involved. A separate environmental proceeding has a governmental authority as a party and potential monetary sanctions of 400,000. That is above 300,000, so it is described unless the registrant reasonably believes the proceeding will result in no monetary sanctions or sanctions below 300,000. If the registrant had elected another threshold, the election would not help here: one percent of current assets is 5,000, which is lower than 1 million, so an elected threshold could not exceed 5,000, and 400,000 is well above it.

## Never confuse

Ordinary routine litigation incidental to the business is not the same as an environmental proceeding: a proceeding of the listed environmental kind is not deemed ordinary routine litigation [@CL-FPA-0113]. The damages omission and the environmental condition both use 10 percent of consolidated current assets, but the omission excuses a damages claim that does not exceed that level [@CL-FPA-0110], while the listed environmental condition applies when the amount involved exceeds it [@CL-FPA-0114]. Paragraph (c) applies notwithstanding paragraph (b) [@CL-FPA-0111]. A pending proceeding is also different from a transaction with a related person, which Item 404 covers when a related person has a direct or indirect material interest [@CL-FPA-0146; @CL-FPA-0147]; see [Transactions with related persons](fpa-related-person-transactions.md). Item 103 information may be provided by cross-reference to Risk Factors disclosure elsewhere in the document [@CL-FPA-0164]; see [Risk factor disclosure for public registrants](fpa-risk-factors.md).

## Company calibration

Whether the company would measure consolidated current assets and track pending proceedings, sanctions and amounts involved the way the threshold tests need. [@Q-017]
