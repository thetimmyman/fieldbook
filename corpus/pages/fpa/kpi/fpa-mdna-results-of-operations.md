## Purpose

Shows how a registrant describes the underlying reasons for material period-to-period changes in line items and explains its results of operations under Item 303(b) of Regulation S-K [@CL-FPA-0027; @CL-FPA-0028; @CL-FPA-0360; @CL-FPA-0029; @CL-FPA-0030; @CL-FPA-0031; @CL-FPA-0032; @CL-FPA-0033; @CL-FPA-0034]. Regulation S-K states the requirements applicable to the content of the non-financial statement portions of registration statements under the Securities Act to the extent provided in the forms to be used for registration under such Act [@CL-FPA-0105], and registration statements under section 12, annual or other reports under sections 13 and 15(d), going-private transaction statements under section 13, tender offer statements under sections 13 and 14, annual reports to security holders and proxy and information statements under section 14, and any other documents required to be filed under the Exchange Act, to the extent provided in the forms and rules under that Act [@CL-FPA-0380; @CL-FPA-0381; @CL-FPA-0106].

Under Item 303(b) of Regulation S-K, where the financial statements reflect material changes from period to period in one or more line items, including where material changes within a line item offset one another, the registrant describes the underlying reasons for these material changes in quantitative and qualitative terms [@CL-FPA-0027]. Paragraph (b)(2)(i) directs the registrant to describe any unusual or infrequent events or transactions or any significant economic changes that materially affected the amount of reported income from continuing operations and, in each case, indicate the extent to which income was so affected, and in addition to describe any other significant components of revenues or expenses that, in the registrant's judgment, would be material to an understanding of the registrant's results of operations [@CL-FPA-0028; @CL-FPA-0360]. Paragraph (b)(2)(ii) directs the registrant to describe any known trends or uncertainties that have had or that are reasonably likely to have a material favorable or unfavorable impact on net sales or revenues or income from continuing operations [@CL-FPA-0029].

Under paragraph (b)(2)(ii), if the registrant knows of events that are reasonably likely to cause a material change in the relationship between costs and revenues (such as known or reasonably likely future increases in costs of labor or materials or price increases or inventory adjustments), the change in the relationship must be disclosed [@CL-FPA-0030; @CL-FPA-0031]. Under paragraph (b)(2)(iii), if the statement of comprehensive income presents material changes from period to period in net sales or revenue, if applicable, the registrant describes the extent to which such changes are attributable to changes in prices or to changes in the volume or amount of goods or services being sold or to the introduction of new products or services [@CL-FPA-0032; @CL-FPA-0033]. Under Instruction 2 to paragraph (b), the discussion must not merely repeat numerical data contained in the financial statements [@CL-FPA-0034].

## How it works

Where the financial statements reflect material changes from period to period in one or more line items, including where material changes within a line item offset one another, the registrant describes the underlying reasons for these material changes in quantitative and qualitative terms [@CL-FPA-0027], and the discussion must not merely repeat numerical data contained in the financial statements [@CL-FPA-0034]. Under Item 303(b)(2)(i) and (ii), the registrant describes any unusual or infrequent events or transactions or any significant economic changes that materially affected the amount of reported income from continuing operations (indicating in each case the extent to which income was so affected), any other significant components of revenues or expenses that in the registrant's judgment would be material to an understanding of the registrant's results of operations, and any known trends or uncertainties that have had or that are reasonably likely to have a material favorable or unfavorable impact on net sales or revenues or income from continuing operations [@CL-FPA-0028; @CL-FPA-0360; @CL-FPA-0029]. If the registrant knows of events that are reasonably likely to cause a material change in the relationship between costs and revenues (such as known or reasonably likely future increases in costs of labor or materials or price increases or inventory adjustments), the change in the relationship must be disclosed [@CL-FPA-0030; @CL-FPA-0031]. If the statement of comprehensive income presents material changes from period to period in net sales or revenue, if applicable, the registrant describes the extent to which such changes are attributable to changes in prices or to changes in the volume or amount of goods or services being sold or to the introduction of new products or services [@CL-FPA-0032; @CL-FPA-0033].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

Revenue rose from 200 million to 230 million. The discussion says 10 million came from higher prices, 15 million from higher volume, and 5 million from a new product line. It also notes that a known labor cost increase will narrow the margin next year.
