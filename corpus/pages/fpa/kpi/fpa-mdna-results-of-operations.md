## Purpose

Shows how a public defense contractor explains its results of operations: unusual events, known trends, shifts in the relationship between costs and revenues, and whether a revenue change is attributable to price, volume or new products. Regulation S-K governs the non-financial statement portions of the filings its general item lists, to the extent the forms and rules provide [@CL-FPA-0105; @CL-FPA-0106], so whether it binds a contractor depends on the contractor's filing obligations, not on whether its equity is privately held. For a private contractor the discussion is a benchmark only.

Item 303(b) of Regulation S-K, in 17 CFR 229.303, tells the registrant to describe the underlying reasons for material period-to-period changes in line items in quantitative and qualitative terms [@CL-FPA-0027]. Paragraph (b)(2)(i) tells the registrant to describe unusual or infrequent events or transactions or significant economic changes that materially affected reported income from continuing operations, and the extent to which income was affected [@CL-FPA-0028]. Paragraph (b)(2)(ii) tells the registrant to describe known trends or uncertainties that have had, or are reasonably likely to have, a material favorable or unfavorable impact on net sales or revenues or income from continuing operations [@CL-FPA-0029].

The same paragraph gives known or reasonably likely future increases in costs of labor or materials, price increases and inventory adjustments as examples of events that the registrant knows of and that are reasonably likely to cause a material change in the relationship between costs and revenues [@CL-FPA-0030]. For events of that kind, the change in the relationship must be disclosed [@CL-FPA-0030; @CL-FPA-0031]. Under paragraph (b)(2)(iii), where the statement of comprehensive income presents material period-to-period changes in net sales or revenue, and if applicable, the registrant describes the extent to which they are attributable to changes in prices [@CL-FPA-0032], to changes in the volume or amount of goods or services sold, or to the introduction of new products or services [@CL-FPA-0033]. The discussion must not merely repeat numerical data contained in the financial statements [@CL-FPA-0034].

## How it works

The registrant explains the reasons behind a material change, not just the amounts [@CL-FPA-0027; @CL-FPA-0034]. It describes unusual or infrequent events or transactions in one paragraph and known trends or uncertainties in another [@CL-FPA-0028; @CL-FPA-0029]. It discloses the change in the relationship between costs and revenues when it knows of events reasonably likely to cause a material change in it, such as labor or material cost increases [@CL-FPA-0030; @CL-FPA-0031]. For a material revenue change, where applicable, it describes the extent to which the change is attributable to prices, to the volume or amount sold, or to new products [@CL-FPA-0032; @CL-FPA-0033].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

Revenue rose from 200 million to 230 million. The discussion says 10 million came from higher prices, 15 million from higher volume, and 5 million from a new product line. It also notes that a known labor cost increase will narrow the margin next year.

## Never confuse

The results discussion explains why the numbers moved, and it must not merely repeat the numbers in the financial statements [@CL-FPA-0034]. The attribution to prices, volume and new products concerns changes in net sales or revenue [@CL-FPA-0032; @CL-FPA-0033]. For the liquidity side of the same discussion, see [MD&A objective and liquidity](../planning-chain/fpa-mdna-objective-and-liquidity.md).
