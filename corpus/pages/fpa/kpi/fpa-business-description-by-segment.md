## Purpose

Shows how a registrant describes the business done and intended to be done by the registrant and its subsidiaries under Item 101(c)(1) of Regulation S-K, focusing upon the registrant's dominant segment or each reportable segment about which financial information is presented in the financial statements [@CL-FPA-0006; @CL-FPA-0007; @CL-FPA-0008; @CL-FPA-0009; @CL-FPA-0010; @CL-FPA-0358; @CL-FPA-0359; @CL-FPA-0011]. Regulation S-K states the requirements applicable to the content of the non-financial statement portions of registration statements under the Securities Act to the extent provided in the forms to be used for registration under such Act [@CL-FPA-0105], and registration statements under section 12, annual or other reports under sections 13 and 15(d), going-private transaction statements under section 13, tender offer statements under sections 13 and 14, annual reports to security holders and proxy and information statements under section 14, and any other documents required to be filed under the Exchange Act, to the extent provided in the forms and rules under that Act [@CL-FPA-0380; @CL-FPA-0381; @CL-FPA-0106].

Item 101(c)(1) of Regulation S-K directs the registrant to describe the business done and intended to be done by the registrant and its subsidiaries, focusing upon the registrant's dominant segment or each reportable segment about which financial information is presented in the financial statements [@CL-FPA-0006]. When describing each segment, only information material to an understanding of the business taken as a whole is required, and disclosure may include, but should not be limited to, the information specified in paragraphs (c)(1)(i) through (v) of Item 101 [@CL-FPA-0007].

Paragraph (c)(1)(i) specifies revenue-generating activities, products and/or services, and any dependence on revenue-generating activities, key products, services, product families or customers, including governmental customers [@CL-FPA-0008]. Paragraph (c)(1)(ii) specifies the status of development efforts for new or enhanced products, trends in market demand and competitive conditions [@CL-FPA-0009].

Instruction 1 to Item 101 states that in determining what information about the segments is material to an understanding of the registrant's business taken as a whole and therefore required to be disclosed, pursuant to paragraph (c) of Item 101, the registrant should take into account both quantitative and qualitative factors such as the significance of the matter to the registrant (e.g., whether a matter with a relatively minor impact on the registrant's business is represented by management to be important to its future profitability), the pervasiveness of the matter (e.g., whether it affects or may affect numerous items in the segment information), and the impact of the matter (e.g., whether it distorts the trends reflected in the segment information) [@CL-FPA-0010; @CL-FPA-0358; @CL-FPA-0359]. Instruction 1 adds that situations may arise when information should be disclosed about a segment, although the information in quantitative terms may not appear significant to the registrant's business taken as a whole [@CL-FPA-0011].

## How it works

The registrant describes the business done and intended to be done by the registrant and its subsidiaries, focusing upon the registrant's dominant segment or each reportable segment about which financial information is presented in the financial statements, and when describing each segment only information material to an understanding of the business taken as a whole is required, while disclosure may include, but should not be limited to, the information specified in paragraphs (c)(1)(i) through (v) of Item 101 [@CL-FPA-0006; @CL-FPA-0007]. In determining what information about the segments is material to an understanding of the registrant's business taken as a whole and therefore required to be disclosed pursuant to paragraph (c) of Item 101, the registrant should take into account both quantitative and qualitative factors such as the significance of the matter to the registrant, the pervasiveness of the matter, and the impact of the matter [@CL-FPA-0010; @CL-FPA-0358; @CL-FPA-0359]. Situations may arise when information should be disclosed about a segment, although the information in quantitative terms may not appear significant to the registrant's business taken as a whole [@CL-FPA-0011].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor has two reportable segments. One earns most of its revenue from a single Government program, worth 60 million of 100 million. The description names the dependence on that customer and the program. The second reportable segment, with 5 million of revenue, is also described because it holds the only new product in development, which is material to an understanding of the business taken as a whole.

## Never confuse

Under Item 101(c)(1), when describing each segment, only information material to an understanding of the business taken as a whole is required, and disclosure may include, but should not be limited to, revenue-generating activities, products and/or services, any dependence on revenue-generating activities, key products, services, product families or customers (including governmental customers), and the status of development efforts for new or enhanced products, trends in market demand and competitive conditions [@CL-FPA-0007; @CL-FPA-0008; @CL-FPA-0009], unlike the general development of the business under Item 101(a) on [Business development disclosure by public registrants](../planning-chain/fpa-business-development-disclosure.md) or the specific government-contract and environmental-compliance disclosures on [Government contract exposure in the business description](../backlog/fpa-government-contract-exposure-disclosure.md).
