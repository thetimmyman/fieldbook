## Purpose

Shows how a public defense contractor describes, segment by segment, its revenue-generating activities, any dependence on key products or customers including governmental customers, and the status of development efforts and trends in market demand. Regulation S-K governs the non-financial statement portions of the filings its general item lists, to the extent the forms and rules provide [@CL-FPA-0105; @CL-FPA-0106], so whether it binds a contractor depends on the contractor's filing obligations, not on whether its equity is privately held. For a private contractor the disclosure is a benchmark only.

Item 101(c)(1) of Regulation S-K, in 17 CFR 229.101, tells the registrant to describe the business done and intended to be done by the registrant and its subsidiaries, focusing on its dominant segment or each reportable segment about which financial information is presented [@CL-FPA-0006]. In describing each segment only information material to an understanding of the business taken as a whole is required, and the listed items are ones that disclosure may include but is not limited to [@CL-FPA-0007].

The first listed item is revenue-generating activities, products and services, and any dependence on revenue-generating activities, key products, services, product families or customers, including governmental customers [@CL-FPA-0008]. The second is the status of development efforts for new or enhanced products, trends in market demand and competitive conditions [@CL-FPA-0009].

Instruction 1 to Item 101 says the registrant should take into account both quantitative and qualitative factors in determining what segment information is material and therefore required to be disclosed [@CL-FPA-0010]. It adds that information about a segment may need to be disclosed although in quantitative terms it may not appear significant to the registrant's business taken as a whole [@CL-FPA-0011].

## How it works

The registrant starts from its dominant segment or each reportable segment and writes only what is material to the business taken as a whole [@CL-FPA-0006; @CL-FPA-0007]. It weighs quantitative and qualitative factors when it decides what segment information to include [@CL-FPA-0010]. A segment fact may need to be disclosed even when it looks small against the whole business [@CL-FPA-0011].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor has two reportable segments. One earns most of its revenue from a single Government program, worth 60 million of 100 million. The description names the dependence on that customer and the program. A small second segment with 5 million of revenue is also described, because it holds the only new product in development.

## Never confuse

The segment items concern revenue-generating activities, dependence, development status and market trends [@CL-FPA-0008; @CL-FPA-0009]. The printed text names no backlog figure, so a segment description is not a backlog report. For the Government election rights that the same paragraph lists, see [Government contract exposure in the business description](../backlog/fpa-government-contract-exposure-disclosure.md).
