## Purpose

Shows how a public defense contractor frames its management discussion of financial condition, known events and uncertainties, and short-term and long-term liquidity and capital resources. Regulation S-K governs the non-financial statement portions of the filings its general item lists, to the extent the forms and rules provide [@CL-FPA-0105; @CL-FPA-0106], so whether it binds a contractor depends on the contractor's filing obligations, not on whether its equity is privately held. For a private contractor the discussion is a benchmark only.

Item 303(a) of Regulation S-K, in 17 CFR 229.303, states the objective as material information relevant to an assessment of financial condition and results of operations, including an evaluation of the amounts and certainty of cash flows from operations and from outside sources [@CL-FPA-0017]. The discussion must focus specifically on material events and uncertainties known to management that are reasonably likely to cause reported financial information not to be necessarily indicative of future operating results or future financial condition [@CL-FPA-0018].

Under Item 303(b)(1), the registrant analyzes its ability to generate and obtain adequate amounts of cash to meet its requirements and its plans for cash in the short term [@CL-FPA-0019]. The short term is the next 12 months from the most recent fiscal period end required to be presented, and the long term, analyzed separately, is beyond the next 12 months [@CL-FPA-0020]. The discussion should analyze material cash requirements from known contractual and other obligations, and must specify the type of obligation and the relevant time period [@CL-FPA-0021].

The liquidity part identifies known trends, demands, commitments, events or uncertainties that will result, or are reasonably likely to result, in liquidity increasing or decreasing in any material way [@CL-FPA-0022]. If a material deficiency is identified, it indicates the course of action taken or proposed, and it identifies internal and external sources of liquidity and any material unused sources of liquid assets [@CL-FPA-0023]. The capital resources part describes material cash requirements, including capital expenditure commitments, with the anticipated source of funds and general purpose [@CL-FPA-0024], and known material trends in capital resources, with reasonably likely changes in their mix and cost [@CL-FPA-0025]. Discussion of material cash requirements from known contractual obligations may include lease obligations, purchase obligations or other liabilities reflected on the balance sheet [@CL-FPA-0026].

## How it works

Management reads its cash position in two time bands, split at the 12-month mark from the latest period end [@CL-FPA-0020]. Each material cash requirement from a known obligation is named by type and by the period in which the cash is needed [@CL-FPA-0021]. The same analysis names the trends and uncertainties that would move liquidity, and the internal and external sources of liquidity [@CL-FPA-0022; @CL-FPA-0023].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor has 30 million of cash and a 20 million purchase obligation due in month 8, plus a 15 million loan repayment due in month 18. The near-term section names the purchase obligation and its timing. The longer-term section names the repayment and its anticipated source of funds.
