## Purpose

Covers the FAR Part 32 rule and clause at FAR 52.232-39, Unenforceability of Unauthorized Obligations, on indemnification clauses in End User License Agreements (EULA), Terms of Service (TOS), or similar legal instruments or agreements [@CL-FPA-0095; @CL-FPA-0096; @CL-FPA-0104; @CL-FPA-0097].

## The rule

Under FAR 32.706-3, the contracting officer shall insert the clause at 52.232-39, Unenforceability of Unauthorized Obligations, in all solicitations and contracts [@CL-FPA-0096]. Under FAR 52.232-39(a), except as stated in paragraph (b) of the clause, when any supply or service acquired under the contract is subject to any End User License Agreement (EULA), Terms of Service (TOS), or similar legal instrument or agreement, that includes any clause requiring the Government to indemnify the Contractor or any person or entity for damages, costs, fees, or any other loss or liability that would create an Anti-Deficiency Act violation (section 1341 of title 31), the following shall govern [@CL-FPA-0104; @CL-FPA-0097]: (1) any such clause is unenforceable against the Government [@CL-FPA-0097], (2) neither the Government nor any Government authorized end user shall be deemed to have agreed to such clause by virtue of it appearing in the EULA, TOS, or similar legal instrument or agreement [@CL-FPA-0098], and if the EULA, TOS, or similar legal instrument or agreement is invoked through an "I agree" click box or other comparable mechanism (e.g., "click-wrap" or "browse-wrap" agreements), execution does not bind the Government or any Government authorized end user to such clause [@CL-FPA-0367], and (3) any such clause is deemed to be stricken from the EULA, TOS, or similar legal instrument or agreement [@CL-FPA-0368]. Under FAR 52.232-39(b), paragraph (a) of the clause does not apply to indemnification by the Government that is expressly authorized by statute and specifically authorized under applicable agency regulations and procedures [@CL-FPA-0099].

Under FAR 32.705, many supplies or services are acquired subject to supplier license agreements, which are particularly common in information technology acquisitions, though they may apply to any supply or service [@CL-FPA-0370]. For example, computer software and services delivered through the internet (web services) are often subject to license agreements, referred to as End User License Agreements (EULA), Terms of Service (TOS), or other similar legal instruments or agreements [@CL-FPA-0373]. Many of these agreements contain indemnification clauses that are inconsistent with Federal law and unenforceable, but which could create a violation of the Anti-Deficiency Act (section 1341 of title 31) if agreed to by the Government [@CL-FPA-0095].

## How it works

Where the condition in FAR 52.232-39(a) is met, any such clause requiring the Government to indemnify the Contractor or any person or entity for damages, costs, fees, or any other loss or liability that would create an Anti-Deficiency Act violation (section 1341 of title 31) is unenforceable against the Government, neither the Government nor any Government authorized end user shall be deemed to have agreed to such clause by virtue of it appearing in the EULA, TOS, or similar legal instrument or agreement (and execution through an "I agree" click box or other comparable mechanism does not bind the Government or any Government authorized end user to such clause), and any such clause is deemed to be stricken from the EULA, TOS, or similar legal instrument or agreement [@CL-FPA-0104; @CL-FPA-0097; @CL-FPA-0098; @CL-FPA-0367; @CL-FPA-0368]. Under paragraph (b), paragraph (a) of the clause does not apply to indemnification by the Government that is expressly authorized by statute and specifically authorized under applicable agency regulations and procedures [@CL-FPA-0099]. Under FAR 32.705, many of these supplier license agreements, such as EULAs, TOS, or other similar legal instruments or agreements, contain indemnification clauses that are inconsistent with Federal law and unenforceable, but which could create a violation of the Anti-Deficiency Act (section 1341 of title 31) if agreed to by the Government [@CL-FPA-0370; @CL-FPA-0373; @CL-FPA-0095]. Under Class Deviation 2026-O0022, effective February 1, 2026, DoD contracting officers shall use the revised FAR Part 32, Contract Financing published on the Revolutionary FAR Overhaul web page in lieu of the text codified at 48 CFR chapter 1 [@CL-CTF-4046]. Under RFO FAR 32.705, many supplies or services are acquired subject to supplier license agreements, which are particularly common in information technology acquisitions, though they may apply to any supply or service [@CL-FPA-0374]. ## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

The terms of service of a software service delivered under a 1,000,000 contract include a clause requiring the Government to indemnify the supplier for any loss. The clause would create an Anti-Deficiency Act violation, and no statute expressly authorizes it. Under the contract clause it is unenforceable against the Government, and the Government is not deemed to have agreed to it because it appears in the terms of service.

## Never confuse

FAR 32.706-3 and FAR 52.232-39 govern clauses in an EULA, TOS, or similar legal instrument or agreement requiring the Government to indemnify the Contractor or any person or entity for damages, costs, fees, or any other loss or liability that would create an Anti-Deficiency Act violation (section 1341 of title 31), unless expressly authorized by statute and specifically authorized under applicable agency regulations and procedures [@CL-FPA-0096; @CL-FPA-0104; @CL-FPA-0097; @CL-FPA-0099]. See [Funded versus unfunded contract value: the policy](../backlog/fpa-funded-versus-unfunded-policy.md) and [Contract funding: incremental funding policy](../../contract-finance/funding/ctf-incremental-funding-policy.md).
