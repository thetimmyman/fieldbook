## Purpose

Shows why an indemnity in a supplier license or terms of service may not be enforceable against the Government, and which clause the contract carries to say so.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-FPA-0096] |
| Codified citation | FAR 32.705; FAR 32.706-3; FAR 52.232-39 [@CL-FPA-0095; @CL-FPA-0096; @CL-FPA-0097] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 32.705; RFO FAR 32.706-3 [@CL-FPA-0288; @CL-FPA-0289; @CL-FPA-0290]; RFO FAR 52.232-39 [@CL-FPA-1028; @CL-FPA-1029; @CL-FPA-1030; @CL-FPA-1031; @CL-FPA-1032] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-FPA-0096] |
| Applies to | All solicitations and contracts [@CL-FPA-0096] |

The clause on unenforceability of unauthorized obligations is inserted in all solicitations and contracts [@CL-FPA-0096]. Except as stated in paragraph (b) of the clause, its provisions apply when any supply or service acquired under the contract is subject to any license agreement, terms of service or similar legal instrument that includes any clause requiring the Government to indemnify [@CL-FPA-0104]. Where that condition is met, any clause requiring the Government to indemnify the Contractor or any person or entity for damages, costs, fees, or any other loss or liability that would create an Anti-Deficiency Act violation is unenforceable against the Government [@CL-FPA-0097]. Neither the Government nor any Government authorized end user is deemed to have agreed to such a clause by virtue of its appearing in the agreement [@CL-FPA-0098]. The clause does not apply to indemnification by the Government that is expressly authorized by statute and specifically authorized under applicable agency regulations and procedures [@CL-FPA-0099].

The FAR explains why: many license and terms-of-service agreements contain indemnification clauses that are inconsistent with Federal law and unenforceable, but which could create an Anti-Deficiency Act violation if agreed to by the Government [@CL-FPA-0095].

## How it works

Where the scope condition is met, a Government indemnity of the kind described is unenforceable against the Government, and the Government is not deemed to have agreed to it merely because it appears in the agreement [@CL-FPA-0104; @CL-FPA-0097; @CL-FPA-0098]. The one exception is indemnification expressly authorized by statute and specifically authorized under applicable agency regulations and procedures [@CL-FPA-0099]. The FAR gives as the reason that such clauses are inconsistent with Federal law and could create an Anti-Deficiency Act violation if agreed to by the Government [@CL-FPA-0095]. The general funding rule is on [Funded versus unfunded contract value: the policy](../backlog/fpa-funded-versus-unfunded-policy.md).

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 32 text in place of the codified text from February 1, 2026 [@CL-CTF-4046]. RFO FAR 32.705 keeps the statement that many of the agreements it describes contain indemnification clauses that are inconsistent with Federal law and unenforceable, but which could create a violation of the Anti-Deficiency Act if agreed to by the Government, and adds "et seq." to its citation of section 1341 of title 31, which the codified text cites alone [@CL-FPA-0288; @CL-FPA-0095]. It says computer software and services delivered through the internet (web services) are often subject to license agreements, referred to as End User License Agreements (EULA), Terms of Service (TOS), or other similar legal instruments or agreements [@CL-FPA-0289]. RFO FAR 32.706-3 keeps the rule that the clause at 52.232-39, Unenforceability of Unauthorized Obligations, is inserted in all solicitations and contracts, and states it as an imperative where the codified text says the contracting officer shall insert it [@CL-FPA-0290; @CL-FPA-0096].

The RFO text of the clause at 52.232-39 says that, except as stated in its paragraph (b), the clause applies when any supply or service acquired under the contract is subject to any End User License Agreement, Terms of Service, or similar legal instrument or agreement that includes any clause requiring the Government to indemnify [@CL-FPA-1028; @CL-FPA-0104]. Where that condition is met, any such clause requiring the Government to indemnify the Contractor or any person or entity for damages, costs, fees, or any other loss or liability that would create an Anti-Deficiency Act violation is unenforceable against the Government [@CL-FPA-1029; @CL-FPA-0097]. Neither the Government nor any Government authorized end user is deemed to have agreed to such a clause by virtue of its appearing in the agreement [@CL-FPA-1030; @CL-FPA-0098], and any such clause is deemed to be stricken from the agreement [@CL-FPA-1031]. Paragraph (b) of the RFO clause says paragraph (a) does not apply to indemnification by the Government that is expressly authorized by statute and specifically authorized under applicable agency regulations and procedures [@CL-FPA-1032; @CL-FPA-0099].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

The terms of service of a software service delivered under a 1,000,000 contract include a clause requiring the Government to indemnify the supplier for any loss. The clause would create an Anti-Deficiency Act violation, and no statute expressly authorizes it. Under the contract clause it is unenforceable against the Government, and the Government is not deemed to have agreed to it because it appears in the terms of service.

## Never confuse

This card is about indemnities in license terms. It is not the rule on obligating beyond available funds, which is on [Funded versus unfunded contract value: the policy](../backlog/fpa-funded-versus-unfunded-policy.md). For funding notices on contracts funded in steps, see [Contract funding: incremental funding policy](../../contract-finance/funding/ctf-incremental-funding-policy.md).
