## Purpose

Explains how to read a claim, its quote and its states before relying on any figure in Fieldbook.

## The rule

Every figure on a page cites a claim. A claim states one checkable point, names the stored original and the exact place in it, and quotes the passage that proves it. A claim starts unchecked, and only the owner marks it supported, after reading the passage. Whether a claim applies to the company is a separate state, pending until the company confirms it. A citation of a source version points at a passage but is not evidence for a figure. A question reference marks something open and is never evidence.

## How it works

Open the claim from its citation and read the quote. Then open the original at the locator and read the passage around it, because conditions and exceptions often sit in the next sentence. Check the dates: when the text was retrieved, when it took effect, and which edition it is. Check the instrument: codified text, the overhaul model text or a class deviation, since DoD work often runs on the deviation. Read the verification and applicability states last, and treat an unchecked or pending claim as a lead to confirm, not a fact to sign off.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A page says a reporting threshold is 40 million and cites a claim. The claim is unchecked and pending company confirmation. You may use it to frame a question for the contracts owner, but not to approve a proposal. Once the owner verifies it and the company confirms the contract carries the clause, it can support a decision.

## Never confuse

Verification asks whether the source says it; applicability asks whether it applies to this company or contract; adoption asks whether the company has accepted it as its own practice. A claim can be verified and still not apply. A glossary definition explains a word; it is not a claim and carries no figure.
