## Purpose

Explains how the glossary and the open questions work together, so that a word means one thing and every company unknown has a home.

## The rule

Each glossary term has one definition, a boundary rule saying what it is not, and aliases that lead to it. Definitions are drafts in plain words until the owner checks them against the overhaul definitions for DoD use. An open question names what the company must answer, the role that owns it, what it blocks, and the default assumption until it is answered. Day-one questions come first. Answers never go into the public questions register; they belong in approved company storage.

## How it works

Look a word up before using it in a note or a meeting, and read its boundary rule as carefully as its definition. When a page needs a company fact, it cites the open question instead of guessing. In a meeting, take the day-one questions in order, record who answered and where the evidence is, and keep the answer in company storage. A question that turns out to have a public answer becomes a claim; a public question that keeps coming back becomes a regression case for search.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A meeting note says overhead was 18 percent last year. The glossary boundary rule separates overhead from general and administrative expense, so the first follow-up is which pool and which base the 18 percent uses. The open question on pools and bases names the rates owner, so the follow-up goes to that role.

## Never confuse

A glossary definition explains a word; a claim proves a point from a source. An open question records what is unknown; an answer is company information and stays outside the public Corpus.
