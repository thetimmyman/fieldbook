## Purpose

Build a traceable map of decisions, records, responsibilities and immediate finance outputs over three windows, each closed by a proof test.

## The rule

Treat every company answer as unconfirmed until the responsible company role supplies evidence. Keep answers and company records in approved company storage. The plan uses relative days only; the start date is entered in the browser and never stored here.

## How it works

### Day 1 to Day 30

- [ ] Agree priorities, decision rights, scope and the first deliverables.
- [ ] Hold the first finance discovery meeting and the first audit and control meeting, using their briefs.
- [ ] Walk through the close calendar, ledger-to-program reconciliation and open audit issues.
- [ ] Map contract clauses, funding notices, billing, collections and rate versions.
- [ ] Identify the owner, system of record and review route for each recurring output.
- [ ] Confirm approved IT storage and transfer routes before company data use.
- [ ] Proof test for Day 30: for every duty in the role map below, name its owner, its system of record, its calendar and its first deliverable.

### Day 31 to Day 60

- [ ] Reconcile EAC, revenue, cash, backlog and capital views using approved records.
- [ ] Test the evidence path on a representative close, proposal and program review.
- [ ] Agree the next control and reporting improvements with their owners.
- [ ] Proof test for Day 60: trace one reported program figure from the report to its source records and their versions, with each hand-off named.

### Day 61 to Day 90

- [ ] Rehearse tracing a reported figure to its assumptions, source and version.
- [ ] Review improvement evidence and unresolved decisions with the executive sponsor.
- [ ] Confirm company authorization before activating later storage or finance modules.
- [ ] Proof test for Day 90: run one close or proposal review on the evidence path, and have the sponsor accept the improvement list and its owners.

### Role map: the twelve duties

- [ ] Estimating and pricing: owner, system of record, calendar, first deliverable.
- [ ] Indirect rates: owner, system of record, calendar, first deliverable.
- [ ] Program finance and earned value: owner, system of record, calendar, first deliverable.
- [ ] Contract finance, billing and cash: owner, system of record, calendar, first deliverable.
- [ ] Compliance, CAS and business systems: owner, system of record, calendar, first deliverable.
- [ ] Close and revenue recognition: owner, system of record, calendar, first deliverable.
- [ ] Planning and analysis: owner, system of record, calendar, first deliverable.
- [ ] Supply chain and procurement finance: owner, system of record, calendar, first deliverable.
- [ ] Market and competitive intelligence: owner, system of record, calendar, first deliverable.
- [ ] Mergers and acquisitions: owner, system of record, calendar, first deliverable.
- [ ] Industrialization and capital investment: owner, system of record, calendar, first deliverable.
- [ ] Operating model, cadence and decision rights: owner, system of record, calendar, first deliverable.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A sample program report shows revenue of 120 and cash receipts of 90. The finance lead identifies their separate source records and a difference of 30; the example does not imply an overdue invoice.

## Never confuse

Completing a checklist does not verify a claim, establish clause applicability or approve a tool. A proof test checks that the map exists and is traceable; it is not a performance review.
