## Purpose

Shows who sets the extent of Government production surveillance and what that extent rests on.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-OPM-0050] |
| Codified citation | FAR 42.1103 and FAR 42.1104 [@CL-OPM-0050; @CL-OPM-0051] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 42.803 [@CL-OPM-0152; @CL-OPM-0155]; no RFO sentence matches codified 42.1103 [@CL-OPM-0050]; RFO FAR 42.801 matches codified 42.1101 [@CL-OPM-0156; @CL-CMP-2569] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-OPM-0050] |
| Applies to | Contracts whose performance the Government surveils [@CL-OPM-0050] |

The Government will maintain surveillance of contractor performance as necessary to protect its interests [@CL-OPM-0050]. When the contracting office retains a contract for administration, the contracting officer administering the contract shall determine the extent of surveillance [@CL-OPM-0050].

The contract administration office determines the extent of production surveillance on the basis of the criticality the contracting officer assigned to the supplies or services and a list of factors [@CL-OPM-0051]. Contracts at or below the simplified acquisition threshold should not normally require production surveillance [@CL-OPM-0054]. The source text does not state the dollar value of that threshold.

The FAR's general definitions say a contract administration office is an office that performs assigned postaward functions related to the administration of contracts and assigned preaward functions [@CL-OPM-1086]. The RFO model text of FAR 2.101 defines a contract administration office in the same words as the codified definition, apart from the printed form of the dash [@CL-OPM-1114; @CL-OPM-1086].

## How it works

The administering contracting officer decides the extent of surveillance when the contracting office retains the contract for administration [@CL-OPM-0050], and the contract administration office determines the extent of production surveillance on the basis of criticality and the listed factors [@CL-OPM-0051]. The first statement applies when the contracting office retains the contract for administration, while the second names the contract administration office without that condition, and the printed sections do not say how the two fit together [@CL-OPM-0050; @CL-OPM-0051]. The listed factors are contract requirements for reporting production progress and performance, the contract performance schedule and the contractor's production plan [@CL-OPM-0052], together with the contractor's history of contract performance, its experience with the contract supplies or services, its financial capability and any supplementary written instructions from the contracting office [@CL-OPM-0053]. These two sections name no item that a contractor must supply for surveillance; the contract's own reporting requirements and the contractor's production plan are inputs to the Government's decision [@CL-OPM-0052].

DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 42 text in place of the codified FAR part 42 text because DoD Class Deviation 2026-O0050 directs it, effective March 16, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9759] [@CL-CMP-9761]. RFO 42.803(a) keeps the rule of codified 42.1104(a) that the contract administration office determines the extent of production surveillance on the basis of the criticality assigned by the contracting officer and the listed factors, with the criticality cross-reference now to 42.804 [@CL-OPM-0152] [@CL-OPM-0051]. It keeps the listed factors [@CL-OPM-0153] [@CL-OPM-0052] [@CL-OPM-0154] [@CL-OPM-0053] and the rule that contracts at or below the simplified acquisition threshold should not normally require production surveillance [@CL-OPM-0155] [@CL-OPM-0054]. The RFO subpart's general section states that production surveillance is a contract administration function used to determine contractor progress and to identify any factors that may delay performance [@CL-OPM-0156], which is the first sentence of codified 42.1101 in different words [@CL-CMP-2569]. RFO 42.801 to 42.805 do not print the codified 42.1103 sentences that the Government will maintain surveillance of contractor performance as necessary to protect its interests, or that the contracting officer administering the contract determines the extent of surveillance when the contracting office retains the contract [@CL-OPM-0050]. RFO 42.803(a) names the contract administration office as the one that determines the extent [@CL-OPM-0152].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contracting officer assigns the top criticality designator to a 12-month contract for a line-critical part. The contractor has a clean delivery history and a written production plan, but the delivery schedule is short. The contract administration office weighs the criticality together with the listed factors and sets the extent of surveillance. The answer: the Government office, not the contractor, decides how much surveillance applies, and the plan and history are inputs to that decision.

## Never confuse

The extent of surveillance is not the criticality designator. The contracting officer assigns criticality, and the contract administration office uses it, with other factors, to set the extent [@CL-OPM-0051]. A small contract does not automatically escape surveillance: contracts at or below the simplified acquisition threshold should not normally require it [@CL-OPM-0054]. Under the codified text the administering contracting officer determines the extent of surveillance when the contracting office retains the contract [@CL-OPM-0050], while codified 42.1104(a) and RFO 42.803(a) both say the contract administration office determines the extent of production surveillance [@CL-OPM-0051] [@CL-OPM-0152].
