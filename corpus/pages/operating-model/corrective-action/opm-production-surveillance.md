## Purpose

Shows who sets the extent of Government production surveillance and what that extent rests on.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-OPM-0050] |
| Codified citation | FAR 42.1101 [@CL-CMP-2569]; FAR 42.1103; FAR 42.1104 [@CL-OPM-0050; @CL-OPM-0051]; FAR 2.101 [@CL-OPM-1086] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 42.801 [@CL-OPM-0156]; RFO FAR 42.803 [@CL-OPM-0152; @CL-OPM-0155]; RFO FAR 2.101 [@CL-OPM-1114] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-OPM-0050] |
| Applies to | Contracts whose performance the Government surveils [@CL-OPM-0050] |

The Government will maintain surveillance of contractor performance as necessary to protect its interests [@CL-OPM-0050]. When the contracting office retains a contract for administration, the contracting officer administering the contract shall determine the extent of surveillance [@CL-OPM-0050].

The contract administration office determines the extent of production surveillance on the basis of (1) the criticality (degree of importance to the Government) assigned by the contracting officer (see FAR 42.1105) to the supplies or services and (2) consideration of the factors listed in FAR 42.1104(a)(2) [@CL-OPM-0051]. Contracts at or below the simplified acquisition threshold should not normally require production surveillance [@CL-OPM-0054].

Under FAR 2.101, contract administration office means an office that performs assigned postaward functions related to the administration of contracts and assigned preaward functions [@CL-OPM-1086]. Under RFO FAR 2.101, contract administration office means an office that performs assigned postaward functions related to the administration of contracts and assigned preaward functions [@CL-OPM-1114].

## How it works

Under FAR 42.1101, production surveillance is a function of contract administration used to determine contractor progress and to identify any factors that may delay performance, and involves Government review and analysis of contractor performance plans, schedules, controls, and industrial processes and actual performance under them [@CL-CMP-2569; @CL-CMP-2570]. When the contracting office retains a contract for administration, the contracting officer administering the contract shall determine the extent of surveillance [@CL-OPM-0050], and under FAR 42.1104(a), the contract administration office determines the extent of production surveillance on the basis of the criticality (degree of importance to the Government) assigned by the contracting officer (see FAR 42.1105) to the supplies or services and consideration of the listed factors [@CL-OPM-0051]. Those factors are contract requirements for reporting production progress and performance, the contract performance schedule, and the contractor's production plan [@CL-OPM-0052], together with the contractor's history of contract performance, the contractor's experience with the contract supplies or services, the contractor's financial capability, and any supplementary written instructions from the contracting office [@CL-OPM-0053].

Effective March 16, 2026, DoD Class Deviation 2026-O0050 directs contracting officers to use the revised FAR part 42, Contract Administration and Audit Services, published on the Revolutionary FAR Overhaul web page in lieu of the text codified at 48 CFR chapter 1 [@CL-CMP-9759; @CL-CMP-9761]. Under RFO FAR 42.801, production surveillance is a contract administration function used to determine contractor progress and to identify any factors that may delay performance, and involves Government review and analysis of contractor performance plans, schedules, controls, and industrial processes and actual performance under them [@CL-OPM-0156; @CL-CMP-6960]. Under RFO FAR 42.803(a), the contract administration office determines the extent of production surveillance on the basis of (1) the criticality (degree of importance to the Government) assigned by the contracting officer (see RFO FAR 42.804) to the supplies or services and (2) consideration of contract requirements for reporting production progress and performance, the contract performance schedule, the contractor's production plan, the contractor's history of contract performance, the contractor's experience with the contract supplies or services, the contractor's financial capability, and any supplementary written instructions from the contracting office [@CL-OPM-0152; @CL-OPM-0153; @CL-OPM-0154]. Under RFO FAR 42.803(b), contracts at or below the simplified acquisition threshold should not normally require production surveillance [@CL-OPM-0155].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contracting officer assigns the top criticality designator to a 12-month contract for a line-critical part. The contractor has a clean delivery history and a written production plan, but the delivery schedule is short. The contract administration office weighs the criticality together with the listed factors and sets the extent of surveillance. The answer: the Government office, not the contractor, decides how much surveillance applies, and the plan and history are inputs to that decision.

## Never confuse

Under FAR 42.1104(a), the contracting officer assigns the criticality (degree of importance to the Government) to the supplies or services (see FAR 42.1105), and the contract administration office determines the extent of production surveillance on the basis of that criticality and consideration of the listed factors [@CL-OPM-0051]. Contracts at or below the simplified acquisition threshold should not normally require production surveillance [@CL-OPM-0054]. Under FAR 42.1103, when the contracting office retains a contract for administration, the contracting officer administering the contract shall determine the extent of surveillance [@CL-OPM-0050], whereas under FAR 42.1104(a) [@CL-OPM-0051] and under RFO FAR 42.803(a) [@CL-OPM-0152] the contract administration office determines the extent of production surveillance.
