## Purpose

Shows the price reduction and withholds on accepted nonconforming supplies, the written notice of rejection, and the counterfeit item and data exchange reporting rules.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-OPM-0070] |
| Codified citation | FAR 46.407(f) to (h) and FAR 46.317 [@CL-OPM-0070; @CL-OPM-0072; @CL-OPM-0074; @CL-OPM-0075] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-OPM-0070] |
| Applies to | Supplies or services accepted with critical or major nonconformances, rejected supplies or services, and contracts with the reporting nonconforming items clause [@CL-OPM-0070; @CL-OPM-0072; @CL-OPM-0075] |

When supplies or services are accepted with critical or major nonconformances as authorized, the contracting officer must modify the contract to provide for an equitable price reduction or other consideration [@CL-OPM-0070]. In the case of conditional acceptance, amounts withheld from payments generally should be at least sufficient to cover the estimated cost and related profit to correct deficiencies and complete unfinished work [@CL-OPM-0071]. Notices of rejection must include the reasons for rejection and be furnished promptly to the contractor [@CL-OPM-0072], and the notice must be in writing if the supplies or services were rejected at a place other than the contractor's plant, if the contractor persists in offering nonconforming supplies or services for acceptance, or if delivery or performance was late without excusable cause [@CL-OPM-0073]. Promptness in giving the notice is essential because, if timely nature of rejection is not furnished, acceptance may in certain cases be implied as a matter of law [@CL-OPM-0130]. The paragraph on notices sets no number of days for the notice of rejection.

The contracting officer shall provide disposition instructions for counterfeit or suspect counterfeit items in accordance with agency policy, and agency policy may require the contracting officer to direct the contractor to retain such items for investigative or evidentiary purposes [@CL-OPM-0074]. Except as provided in the exclusions paragraph, the contracting officer shall insert the reporting nonconforming items clause in solicitations and contracts in the listed cases [@CL-OPM-0075].

## How it works

The listed cases begin with an acquisition by any agency, including the Department of Defense, of any items that are subject to higher-level quality standards in accordance with the higher-level contract quality requirement clause [@CL-OPM-0126], and of any items that the contracting officer, in consultation with the requiring activity, determines to be critical items for which use of the clause is appropriate [@CL-OPM-0127]. A second listed case is the Department of Defense electronic parts condition: an acquisition that exceeds the simplified acquisition threshold and is by, or for, the Department of Defense of electronic parts or end items, components, parts, or materials containing electronic parts, whether or not covered in the first listed case [@CL-OPM-0076]. The source text does not state the dollar value of that threshold. A third listed case is the acquisition of services, if the contractor will furnish, as part of the service, any items that meet the criteria of the first two listed cases [@CL-OPM-0128]. The clause is not inserted in either of two excluded cases: when acquiring commercial products and commercial services using part 12 procedures [@CL-OPM-0077], or when acquiring medical devices subject to the Food and Drug Administration reporting requirements [@CL-OPM-0099]. If required by agency policy, the contracting officer may modify paragraph (b)(4) of the clause, but only to change the responsibility for the contractor to submit reports to the agency rather than to the Government-Industry Data Exchange Program [@CL-OPM-0078], in which case the agency instead of the contractor submits reports to that program within the mandatory 60 days [@CL-OPM-0079]. The section does not say what starts those 60 days [@CL-OPM-0079], and the clause text itself is not printed in the source.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor delivers 100 pumps and the contracting officer conditionally accepts them. The estimated cost to correct the deficiencies is 4,000 and the related profit is 500. The withhold should generally be at least 4,500. The pumps are later rejected at the customer site, so the notice of rejection must be in writing and state the reasons. The answer: the withhold covers cost plus related profit, and the rejection notice is written because the rejection was away from the contractor's plant.

## Never confuse

A price reduction is not a withhold: the price reduction is the contract modification for supplies or services accepted with critical or major nonconformances [@CL-OPM-0070], while a withhold is an amount held back from payments under conditional acceptance to cover the cost and related profit to correct [@CL-OPM-0071]. Disposition instructions for counterfeit items come from agency policy [@CL-OPM-0074], and the reporting nonconforming items clause is prescribed in a different section [@CL-OPM-0075].
