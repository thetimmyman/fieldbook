## Purpose

Shows the usual remedy for nonconforming supplies or services and who may accept a critical, major or minor nonconformance.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-OPM-0062] |
| Codified citation | FAR 46.407(a) to (d) [@CL-OPM-0062; @CL-OPM-0063; @CL-OPM-0065; @CL-OPM-0068] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-OPM-0062] |
| Applies to | Supplies or services that do not conform in all respects to contract requirements [@CL-OPM-0062] |

The contracting officer should reject supplies or services not conforming in all respects to contract requirements [@CL-OPM-0062]. The contracting officer ordinarily must give the contractor an opportunity to correct or replace nonconforming supplies or services when this can be accomplished within the required delivery schedule [@CL-OPM-0063]. Unless the contract specifies otherwise, correction or replacement must be without additional cost to the Government [@CL-OPM-0064].

For a critical or major nonconformance, the contracting officer ordinarily must reject in situations not covered by the correct-or-replace paragraph [@CL-OPM-0065], and may determine, in circumstances such as reasons of economy or urgency, that acceptance or conditional acceptance is in the best interest of the Government [@CL-OPM-0066]. Before making a decision to accept, the contracting officer must obtain the concurrence of the activity responsible for the technical requirements and, where health factors are involved, of the responsible health official of the agency concerned [@CL-OPM-0067]. For a minor nonconformance, the cognizant contract administration office may make the determination to accept or reject, except where the contracting office withholds that authority [@CL-OPM-0068].

## How it works

The first step is rejection as the starting position [@CL-OPM-0062], with an opportunity to correct or replace when the delivery schedule allows [@CL-OPM-0063]. The critical and major path comes next for situations the correct-or-replace paragraph does not cover [@CL-OPM-0065]. The printed text asks for technical concurrence in the paragraph on accepting critical or major nonconformances, and states no concurrence rule for minor ones [@CL-OPM-0067]. To assist in making the determination on a minor nonconformance, the contract administration office may set up a joint contractor-contract administrative office review group, but acceptance with critical or major nonconformances is outside the scope of that group [@CL-OPM-0068; @CL-OPM-0069].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor delivers 200 units on time and 10 of them have a minor defect. Because the schedule allows, the contracting officer first lets the contractor correct or replace the 10 units at no extra cost to the Government. Separately, because the defect is minor, the cognizant contract administration office may decide whether to accept or reject those units, unless the contracting office withheld that authority. Had the defect been critical, the contracting officer would ordinarily reject, and could accept only with the concurrence of the activity responsible for the technical requirements and, where health factors are involved, of the responsible health official. The answer: the contract administration office decides the minor case and the contracting officer decides the critical case.

## Never confuse

Correction or replacement is not the acceptance decision: correction or replacement comes first when the schedule allows [@CL-OPM-0063], and the accept-or-reject rules for critical and major nonconformances apply to situations it does not cover [@CL-OPM-0065]. The inspection clause governs the Government's inspection rights and the effect of acceptance; see [Inspection of Supplies—Fixed-Price clause](../../close-revrec/revenue/cls-inspection-of-supplies-fixed-price.md).
