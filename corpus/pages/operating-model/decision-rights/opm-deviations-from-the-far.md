## Purpose

Shows who may authorize an individual or a class deviation from the FAR, how DoD class deviations are handled, and why a deviation cannot reach the Cost Accounting Standards.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-OPM-0021] |
| Codified citation | FAR 1.400 [@CL-OPM-1020]; FAR 1.402; FAR 1.403; FAR 1.404 [@CL-OPM-0021; @CL-OPM-0023; @CL-OPM-0024] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-OPM-0021] |
| Applies to | Deviations from the FAR, unless precluded by law, executive order, or regulation [@CL-OPM-0021] |

Unless precluded by law, executive order, or regulation, deviations from the FAR may be granted as specified in the subpart when necessary to meet the specific needs and requirements of each agency [@CL-OPM-0021]. Individual deviations affect only one contract action and, unless 1.405(e) is applicable, may be authorized by the agency head, and the contracting officer must document the justification and agency approval in the contract file [@CL-OPM-0023].

Class deviations affect more than one contract action [@CL-OPM-0024]. For civilian agencies except NASA, class deviations may be authorized by agency heads or their designees, unless 1.405(e) is applicable, and that authority shall not be delegated below the head of a contracting activity [@CL-OPM-0025]. One listed limitation on authorization of class deviations by agency officials is that an agency official who may authorize one shall, before doing so, consult with the chairperson of the Civilian Agency Acquisition Council, unless the official determines that urgency precludes such consultation [@CL-OPM-0107]. For DoD, class deviations shall be controlled, processed, and approved in accordance with the Defense FAR Supplement [@CL-OPM-0026]. For NASA, class deviations shall be controlled and approved by the Assistant Administrator for Procurement [@CL-OPM-0108].

Deviations are not authorized with respect to 30.201-3 and 30.201-4 or the requirements of the Cost Accounting Standards Board rules and regulations in 48 CFR chapter 99, and the FAR refers readers to 30.201-5 for waivers pertaining to Cost Accounting Standards [@CL-OPM-0022].

The deviations subpart prescribes the policies and procedures for authorizing deviations from the FAR, and exceptions pertaining to the use of forms prescribed by the FAR are covered in FAR part 53 rather than in that subpart [@CL-OPM-1020].

## How it works

The first question is whether the deviation is individual or class, because an individual deviation affects one contract action and a class deviation more than one [@CL-OPM-0023; @CL-OPM-0024]. The deciding level follows the type and the agency: the agency head for an individual deviation, agency heads or designees for a civilian class deviation, and the Defense FAR Supplement route for DoD [@CL-OPM-0023; @CL-OPM-0025; @CL-OPM-0026]. A finance reader checks the type, checks that the approval sits at the level the FAR names for that type, and for an individual deviation checks that the justification and agency approval are in the contract file [@CL-OPM-0023]. Both civilian authorizations carry an exception for 1.405(e) [@CL-OPM-0023; @CL-OPM-0025]. That paragraph provides that, for civilian agencies other than NASA, a request for a deviation required to comply with a treaty or an executive agreement that the treaty and executive agreement paragraphs of that section do not authorize shall be processed through the FAR Secretariat to the Civilian Agency Acquisition Council [@CL-OPM-0109]. This card does not cover the other paragraphs of that section. The way the FAR, the DFARS and class deviations fit together is on [The FAR, the DFARS, the overhaul and class deviations](../../compliance/regulatory-framework/cmp-acquisition-regulations.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contract file holds an agency head's approval to set aside one FAR requirement for a single award. That is an individual deviation, and the contracting officer's justification should sit in the same file. A second award later uses the same exception. That now affects more than one contract action, so a class deviation is needed, and the single approval does not cover it. A different request, to set aside a Cost Accounting Standard, cannot be granted as a deviation.

## Never confuse

A deviation is not a waiver of the Cost Accounting Standards. Deviations are not authorized for the requirements of the Cost Accounting Standards Board, and the FAR points to a separate waiver route [@CL-OPM-0022]; see [Waiver of Cost Accounting Standards](../../compliance/cas-allowability/cmp-cas-waiver.md). A DoD class deviation is handled under the Defense FAR Supplement, not under the civilian agency rule [@CL-OPM-0026].
