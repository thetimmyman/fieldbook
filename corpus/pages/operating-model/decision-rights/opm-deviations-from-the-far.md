## Purpose

Shows who may authorize an individual or a class deviation from the FAR, how DoD class deviations are handled, and why a deviation cannot reach the Cost Accounting Standards.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-OPM-0021] |
| Codified citation | FAR 1.400 [@CL-OPM-1020]; FAR 1.402; FAR 1.403; FAR 1.404 [@CL-OPM-0021; @CL-OPM-0023; @CL-OPM-0024] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-OPM-0021] |
| Applies to | Deviations from the FAR, unless precluded by law, executive order, or regulation [@CL-OPM-0021] |

Unless precluded by law, executive order, or regulation, deviations from the FAR may be granted as specified in FAR subpart 1.4 when necessary to meet the specific needs and requirements of each agency [@CL-OPM-0021]. Individual deviations affect only one contract action and, unless FAR 1.405(e) is applicable, may be authorized by the agency head, and the contracting officer must document the justification and agency approval in the contract file [@CL-OPM-0023].

Class deviations affect more than one contract action [@CL-OPM-0024]. For civilian agencies except NASA, class deviations may be authorized by agency heads or their designees, unless FAR 1.405(e) is applicable, and delegation of that authority shall not be made below the head of a contracting activity [@CL-OPM-0025]. Authorization of class deviations by agency officials is subject to two limitations: an agency official who may authorize a class deviation, before doing so, shall consult with the chairperson of the Civilian Agency Acquisition Council (CAA Council), unless that agency official determines that urgency precludes such consultation, and recommended revisions to the FAR shall be transmitted to the FAR Secretariat by agency heads or their designees for authorizing class deviations [@CL-OPM-0177; @CL-OPM-0107; @CL-OPM-0178]. For DoD, class deviations shall be controlled, processed, and approved in accordance with the Defense FAR Supplement [@CL-OPM-0026]. For NASA, class deviations shall be controlled and approved by the Assistant Administrator for Procurement, and deviations shall be processed in accordance with agency regulations [@CL-OPM-0108; @CL-OPM-0179].

Deviations are not authorized with respect to FAR 30.201-3 and 30.201-4, or the requirements of the Cost Accounting Standards Board (CASB) rules and regulations (48 CFR chapter 99), and FAR 1.402 refers to FAR 30.201-5 for instructions concerning waivers pertaining to Cost Accounting Standards [@CL-OPM-0022].

The deviations subpart prescribes the policies and procedures for authorizing deviations from the FAR, and exceptions pertaining to the use of forms prescribed by the FAR are covered in FAR part 53 rather than in that subpart [@CL-OPM-1020].

## How it works

Individual deviations affect only one contract action and, unless FAR 1.405(e) is applicable, may be authorized by the agency head, with the contracting officer documenting the justification and agency approval in the contract file [@CL-OPM-0023], whereas class deviations affect more than one contract action [@CL-OPM-0024]. For class deviations, civilian agencies except NASA may have them authorized by agency heads or their designees (not delegated below the head of a contracting activity) unless FAR 1.405(e) is applicable [@CL-OPM-0025], DoD class deviations shall be controlled, processed, and approved in accordance with the Defense FAR Supplement [@CL-OPM-0026], and NASA class deviations shall be controlled and approved by the Assistant Administrator for Procurement and processed in accordance with agency regulations [@CL-OPM-0108; @CL-OPM-0179]. Under FAR 1.405(e), for civilian agencies other than NASA, if a deviation required to comply with a treaty or an executive agreement is not authorized by FAR 1.405(b) or (c), then the request for deviation shall be processed through the FAR Secretariat to the Civilian Agency Acquisition Council [@CL-OPM-0109]. For how the FAR, the DFARS, and class deviations fit together, see [The FAR, the DFARS, the overhaul and class deviations](../../compliance/regulatory-framework/cmp-acquisition-regulations.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contract file holds an agency head's approval to set aside one FAR requirement for a single award. That is an individual deviation, and the contracting officer's justification should sit in the same file. A second award later uses the same exception. That now affects more than one contract action, so a class deviation is needed, and the single approval does not cover it. A different request, to set aside a Cost Accounting Standard, cannot be granted as a deviation.

## Never confuse

Deviations are not authorized with respect to FAR 30.201-3 and 30.201-4, or the requirements of the Cost Accounting Standards Board (CASB) rules and regulations (48 CFR chapter 99), and FAR 1.402 refers to FAR 30.201-5 for instructions concerning waivers pertaining to Cost Accounting Standards [@CL-OPM-0022]; see [Waiver of Cost Accounting Standards](../../compliance/cas-allowability/cmp-cas-waiver.md). For DoD, class deviations shall be controlled, processed, and approved in accordance with the Defense FAR Supplement [@CL-OPM-0026], whereas for civilian agencies except NASA, class deviations may be authorized by agency heads or their designees, unless FAR 1.405(e) is applicable, and delegation of that authority shall not be made below the head of a contracting activity [@CL-OPM-0025].
