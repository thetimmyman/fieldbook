## Purpose

Shows how a contracting officer's appointment and limits are recorded in writing, who keeps the appointment files, and what a finance reader can ask for to confirm a signer's authority.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-OPM-0017] |
| Codified citation | FAR 1.603-3; FAR 1.604 [@CL-OPM-0017; @CL-OPM-0019] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-OPM-0017] |
| Applies to | Contracting officers, and the COR file for each assigned contract [@CL-OPM-0017; @CL-OPM-0019] |

Contracting officers shall be appointed in writing on an SF 1402, Certificate of Appointment, which shall state any limitations on the scope of authority to be exercised, other than limitations contained in applicable law or regulation [@CL-OPM-0017]. Appointing officials shall maintain files containing copies of all appointments that have not been terminated [@CL-OPM-0018]. The same section treats micro-purchase delegations separately, which this card does not cover.

The COR shall maintain a file for each assigned contract, which must include, at a minimum, a copy of the contracting officer's letter of designation and other documents describing the COR's duties and responsibilities [@CL-OPM-0019]. Two further listed items in the COR file are a copy of the contract administration functions delegated to a contract administration office which may not be delegated to the COR, and documentation of COR actions taken in accordance with the delegation of authority [@CL-OPM-0020].

## How it works

A finance reader can start from the signer's SF 1402, which states any limitations on the scope of authority to be exercised, other than limitations contained in applicable law or regulation [@CL-OPM-0017]. Appointing officials shall maintain files containing copies of all appointments that have not been terminated, so a reader who wants a copy can ask the appointing official [@CL-OPM-0018]. Information on the limits of a contracting officer's authority shall also be readily available to the public and agency personnel [@CL-OPM-0002]. For a COR, the check is the letter of designation, which the COR file must include [@CL-OPM-0019]. The source text sets no dollar limit on the certificate; the certificate states any limitations on the scope of authority other than those in law or regulation [@CL-OPM-0017].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contract modification that adds 2,000,000 of work is signed by a contracting officer. The finance reader asks for the signer's certificate of appointment. The certificate states a limit of 1,000,000 for any one action. The signature is above the stated limit, so the finance reader stops and asks the contracting officer's office before booking the change.

## Never confuse

A contracting officer is appointed on the certificate. A COR is designated in a letter of designation, and the COR has no authority to make commitments or changes that affect price, quality, quantity, delivery, or other terms [@CL-OPM-0019; @CL-OPM-0006].

## Company calibration

Where the certificates of appointment and COR designation letters for each executed contract are kept, and who confirms the signer's limit. [@Q-002]
