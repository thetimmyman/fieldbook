## Purpose

Shows how a contracting officer's appointment and limits are recorded in writing, who keeps the appointment files, and what a finance reader can ask for to confirm a signer's authority.

## The rule

| Codified citation | FAR 1.603-3; FAR 1.603-4; FAR 1.604 [@CL-OPM-0017; @CL-OPM-1021; @CL-OPM-1022; @CL-OPM-0019] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |

Contracting officers shall be appointed in writing on an SF 1402, Certificate of Appointment, which shall state any limitations on the scope of authority to be exercised, other than limitations contained in applicable law or regulation [@CL-OPM-0017]. Appointing officials shall maintain files containing copies of all appointments that have not been terminated [@CL-OPM-0018]. Under FAR 1.603-3(b), agency heads are encouraged to delegate micro-purchase authority to individuals who are employees of an executive agency or members of the Armed Forces of the United States who will be using the supplies or services being purchased, and individuals delegated that authority are not required to be appointed on an SF 1402, but shall be appointed in writing in accordance with agency procedures [@CL-OPM-0175; @CL-OPM-0176].

The COR shall maintain a file for each assigned contract, which must include, at a minimum, a copy of the contracting officer's letter of designation and other documents describing the COR's duties and responsibilities, a copy of the contract administration functions delegated to a contract administration office which may not be delegated to the COR (see FAR 1.602-2(d)(4)), and documentation of COR actions taken in accordance with the delegation of authority [@CL-OPM-0019; @CL-OPM-0020].

Termination of a contracting officer appointment will be by letter, unless the Certificate of Appointment contains other provisions for automatic termination, and terminations may be for reasons such as reassignment, termination of employment, or unsatisfactory performance [@CL-OPM-1021]. No termination of a contracting officer appointment shall operate retroactively [@CL-OPM-1022].

## How it works

Contracting officers shall be appointed in writing on an SF 1402, Certificate of Appointment, which shall state any limitations on the scope of authority to be exercised, other than limitations contained in applicable law or regulation [@CL-OPM-0017], and appointing officials shall maintain files containing copies of all appointments that have not been terminated [@CL-OPM-0018]. Contracting officers shall receive from the appointing authority (see FAR 1.603-1) clear instructions in writing regarding the limits of their authority, and information on the limits of the contracting officers' authority shall be readily available to the public and agency personnel [@CL-OPM-0002]. The COR shall maintain a file for each assigned contract that must include, at a minimum, a copy of the contracting officer's letter of designation and other documents describing the COR's duties and responsibilities [@CL-OPM-0019], together with a copy of the contract administration functions delegated to a contract administration office which may not be delegated to the COR (see FAR 1.602-2(d)(4)) and documentation of COR actions taken in accordance with the delegation of authority [@CL-OPM-0020].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contract modification that adds 2,000,000 of work is signed by a contracting officer. The finance reader asks for the signer's certificate of appointment. The certificate states a limit of 1,000,000 for any one action. The signature is above the stated limit, so the finance reader stops and asks the contracting officer's office before booking the change.

## Never confuse

Contracting officers shall be appointed in writing on an SF 1402, Certificate of Appointment [@CL-OPM-0017], whereas a COR's file for each assigned contract includes a copy of the contracting officer's letter of designation [@CL-OPM-0019], and a COR has no authority to make any commitments or changes that affect price, quality, quantity, delivery, or other terms and conditions of the contract nor in any way direct the contractor or its subcontractors to operate in conflict with the contract terms and conditions [@CL-OPM-0006].
