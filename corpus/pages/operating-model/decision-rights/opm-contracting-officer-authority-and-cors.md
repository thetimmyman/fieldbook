## Purpose

Shows who decides for the Government when a contract is made, administered or ended, which written instrument carries the limit, and what a contracting officer's representative may not do.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR and codified DFARS [@CL-OPM-0001; @CL-OPM-1053] |
| Codified citation | FAR 1.602-1; FAR 1.602-2; DFARS 242.200-70 [@CL-OPM-0001; @CL-OPM-0004; @CL-OPM-1053] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-OPM-0001] |
| Applies to | Contracting officers and the contracting officer's representatives they designate [@CL-OPM-0001; @CL-OPM-0004] |

Contracting officers have authority to enter into, administer, or terminate contracts and make related determinations and findings, but may bind the Government only to the extent of the authority delegated to them [@CL-OPM-0001]. The limit is set in writing: contracting officers shall receive clear written instructions from the appointing authority on the limits of their authority, and information on those limits shall be readily available to the public and agency personnel [@CL-OPM-0002].

The FAR's general definitions say contracting officer means a person with the authority to enter into, administer, and/or terminate contracts and make related determinations and findings [@CL-OPM-1083], and that the term includes certain authorized representatives of the contracting officer acting within the limits of their authority as delegated by the contracting officer [@CL-OPM-1084].

Among the listed duties, contracting officers shall ensure that the requirements of 1.602-1(b) have been met and that sufficient funds are available for obligation [@CL-OPM-0003]. Another listed duty is to designate and authorize a COR in writing and in accordance with agency procedures on all contracts and orders other than firm-fixed-price ones, and on firm-fixed-price ones as appropriate, unless the contracting officer retains and executes the COR duties [@CL-OPM-0100; @CL-OPM-0004].

A COR shall be a Government employee, unless otherwise authorized in agency regulations [@CL-OPM-0005]. One listed attribute of a COR is that the COR has no authority to make any commitments or changes that affect price, quality, quantity, delivery, or other terms and conditions of the contract, nor to direct the contractor or its subcontractors to operate in conflict with the contract terms and conditions [@CL-OPM-0006]. Another is that the COR may not be delegated responsibility to perform functions that have been delegated to a contract administration office, but may be assigned some duties by the contracting officer [@CL-OPM-0101].

The FAR's general definitions say a contracting officer's representative (COR) is an individual, including a contracting officer's technical representative (COTR), designated and authorized in writing by the contracting officer to perform specific technical or administrative functions [@CL-OPM-1085].

## How it works

The contracting officer's authority is a delegation, and the appointing authority writes down its limits [@CL-OPM-0001; @CL-OPM-0002]. The contracting officer in turn designates a COR in writing [@CL-OPM-0100]. One listed attribute of a COR is that the COR shall be designated in writing, with copies furnished to the contractor and the contract administration office, specifying the extent of the COR's authority and identifying the limitations on it [@CL-OPM-0007]. Two further listed statements in that designation are that the authority is not redelegable and that the COR may be personally liable for unauthorized acts [@CL-OPM-0008]. As the ratification subsection uses the term, an unauthorized commitment is an agreement that is not binding solely because the Government representative who made it lacked the authority to enter into that agreement on behalf of the Government [@CL-OPM-0009]. [Ratification of unauthorized commitments](opm-ratification-of-unauthorized-commitments.md) is covered on its own card.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contracting officer's written limit is 500,000 for any one action. A COR tells a contractor by email to add work worth 80,000. The COR has no authority to change price, so the instruction does not bind the Government. The contractor should ask the contracting officer for a written modification before doing the work. If the work is done anyway, it is an unauthorized commitment, and payment depends on ratification or another route, which a separate card covers.

## Never confuse

A COR is not a contracting officer. The COR has no authority to make commitments or changes that affect price, quality, quantity, delivery, or other terms [@CL-OPM-0006]. Functions that a contracting officer delegates to a contract administration office are a different delegation, and a COR may not be delegated responsibility to perform them [@CL-OPM-0101]; see [Contract administration functions](../../compliance/audit-retention/cmp-contract-administration-functions.md). The DFARS scope section 242.200-70 says the subpart does not address the contract administration role of a contracting officer's representative, and it points to 201.602 [@CL-OPM-1053].
