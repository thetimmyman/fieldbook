## Purpose

Shows who may ratify an agreement made without authority, how far that authority can be delegated, which conditions must be met, and what happens when ratification is not possible.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-OPM-0010] |
| Codified citation | FAR 1.602-3 [@CL-OPM-0010]; FAR 2.101 [@CL-OPM-1087] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 2.101 [@CL-OPM-1115] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-OPM-0010] |
| Applies to | Unauthorized commitments, meaning agreements not binding solely because the Government representative who made them lacked the authority to enter into that agreement on behalf of the Government [@CL-OPM-0009] |

Ratification, as used in FAR 1.602-3, means the act of approving an unauthorized commitment by an official who has the authority to do so [@CL-OPM-0102]. Subject to the limitations in FAR 1.602-3(c), the head of the contracting activity, unless a higher level official is designated by the agency, may ratify an unauthorized commitment [@CL-OPM-0010]. The ratification authority in FAR 1.602-3(b)(2) may be delegated in accordance with agency procedures, but in no case shall the authority be delegated below the level of chief of the contracting office [@CL-OPM-0011].

Under FAR 2.101, contracting activity means an element of an agency designated by the agency head and delegated broad authority regarding acquisition functions [@CL-OPM-1087]. Under RFO FAR 2.101, contracting activity means an element of an agency designated by the agency head and delegated broad authority regarding acquisition functions [@CL-OPM-1115].

Agencies should take positive action to preclude, to the maximum extent possible, the need for ratification actions [@CL-OPM-0103]. Although procedures are provided in FAR 1.602-3 for use in those cases where the ratification of an unauthorized commitment is necessary, those procedures may not be used in a manner that encourages such commitments being made by Government personnel [@CL-OPM-0104]. Agencies should process unauthorized commitments using the ratification authority of FAR 1.602-3 instead of referring such actions to the Government Accountability Office for resolution [@CL-OPM-0105].

The authority in FAR 1.602-3(b)(2) may be exercised only when all of the conditions in FAR 1.602-3(c) are met, beginning with the condition that supplies or services have been provided to and accepted by the Government, or the Government otherwise has obtained or will obtain a benefit resulting from performance of the unauthorized commitment [@CL-OPM-0012; @CL-OPM-0015].

## How it works

The remaining conditions in FAR 1.602-3(c) are that the ratifying official has the authority to enter into a contractual commitment, that the resulting contract would otherwise have been proper if made by an appropriate contracting officer [@CL-OPM-0013], that the contracting officer reviewing the unauthorized commitment determines the price to be fair and reasonable, that the contracting officer recommends payment and legal counsel concurs in the recommendation, unless agency procedures expressly do not require such concurrence [@CL-OPM-0014], that funds are available and were available at the time the unauthorized commitment was made, and that the ratification is in accordance with any other limitations prescribed under agency procedures [@CL-OPM-0015]. Cases that are not ratifiable under FAR 1.602-3 may be subject to resolution as recommended by the Government Accountability Office under its claim procedure (GAO Policy and Procedures Manual for Guidance of Federal Agencies, Title 4, Chapter 2), or as authorized by FAR subpart 50.1, and legal advice should be obtained in those cases [@CL-OPM-0016; @CL-OPM-0180]. Unauthorized commitments that would involve claims subject to resolution under chapter 71 of title 41 of the United States Code, Contract Disputes, should be processed in accordance with FAR subpart 33.2, Disputes and Appeals [@CL-OPM-0106].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A program office asks a contractor to start extra work worth 60,000 without contracting officer approval. The Government accepts the work. The contracting officer finds the price fair and reasonable, recommends payment, and legal counsel concurs. Funds were available on the day of the request. If the other listed conditions are also met, the head of the contracting activity, or a delegate no lower than the chief of the contracting office, can then ratify. If funds had not been available that day, the case would not be ratifiable and could only be considered through another route.

## Never confuse

An unauthorized commitment, as used in FAR 1.602-3, is an agreement that is not binding solely because the Government representative who made it lacked the authority to enter into that agreement on behalf of the Government [@CL-OPM-0009], whereas ratification, as used in FAR 1.602-3, means the act of approving an unauthorized commitment by an official who has the authority to do so [@CL-OPM-0102]. For unauthorized obligations and the Anti-Deficiency Act, see [Unauthorized obligations and Anti-Deficiency Act risk](../../fpa/planning-chain/fpa-unauthorized-obligations-anti-deficiency.md).
