## Purpose

Shows who may ratify an agreement made without authority, how far that authority can be delegated, which conditions must be met, and what happens when ratification is not possible.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-OPM-0010] |
| Codified citation | FAR 1.602-3 [@CL-OPM-0010] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-OPM-0010] |
| Applies to | Unauthorized commitments, meaning agreements made by a Government representative who lacked authority [@CL-OPM-0009] |

Ratification means the act of approving an unauthorized commitment by an official who has the authority to do so [@CL-OPM-0102]. Subject to the limitations in paragraph (c) of the subsection, the head of the contracting activity, unless the agency designates a higher level official, may ratify an unauthorized commitment [@CL-OPM-0010]. The authority to ratify may be delegated in accordance with agency procedures, but in no case below the level of chief of the contracting office [@CL-OPM-0011].

The FAR's general definitions say contracting activity means an element of an agency designated by the agency head and delegated broad authority regarding acquisition functions [@CL-OPM-1087]. The RFO model text of FAR 2.101 defines a contracting activity in the same words as the codified definition [@CL-OPM-1115; @CL-OPM-1087].

Agencies should take positive action to preclude, to the maximum extent possible, the need for ratification actions [@CL-OPM-0103]. The ratification procedures may not be used in a manner that encourages unauthorized commitments by Government personnel [@CL-OPM-0104]. Agencies should process unauthorized commitments using the ratification authority instead of referring such actions to the Government Accountability Office for resolution [@CL-OPM-0105].

The authority to ratify may be exercised only when all of the listed conditions are met, the first being that supplies or services have been provided to and accepted by the Government, or the Government otherwise has obtained or will obtain a benefit resulting from performance of the unauthorized commitment [@CL-OPM-0012].

## How it works

Two more listed conditions are that the ratifying official has the authority to enter into a contractual commitment and that the resulting contract would otherwise have been proper if made by an appropriate contracting officer [@CL-OPM-0013]. Two further conditions are that the reviewing contracting officer determines the price to be fair and reasonable, and that the contracting officer recommends payment and legal counsel concurs, unless agency procedures expressly do not require such concurrence [@CL-OPM-0014]. The remaining listed conditions are that funds are available and were available when the commitment was made, and that the ratification is in accordance with any other limitations in agency procedures [@CL-OPM-0015]. When a case is not ratifiable, it may be subject to resolution as recommended by the Government Accountability Office under its claim procedure, or as authorized by FAR Subpart 50.1 [@CL-OPM-0016]. Unauthorized commitments that would involve claims subject to resolution under the Contract Disputes chapter of title 41 of the United States Code should be processed in accordance with FAR subpart 33.2, Disputes and Appeals [@CL-OPM-0106]. Beyond those conditions, the source text does not establish what amount a contractor will be paid on ratification or when.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A program office asks a contractor to start extra work worth 60,000 without contracting officer approval. The Government accepts the work. The contracting officer finds the price fair and reasonable, recommends payment, and legal counsel concurs. Funds were available on the day of the request. If the other listed conditions are also met, the head of the contracting activity, or a delegate no lower than the chief of the contracting office, can then ratify. If funds had not been available that day, the case would not be ratifiable and could only be considered through another route.

## Never confuse

An unauthorized commitment is an agreement that is not binding solely because the person who made it lacked authority [@CL-OPM-0009]. Ratification is the act of approving it by an official who has the authority to do so [@CL-OPM-0102]. A clause in supplier license terms that requires the Government to indemnify is a different matter; see [Unauthorized obligations and Anti-Deficiency Act risk](../../fpa/planning-chain/fpa-unauthorized-obligations-anti-deficiency.md).
