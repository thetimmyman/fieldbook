## Purpose

Shows when the service contract labor standards clause goes into a contract and when the contracting officer must obtain wage determinations.

## The rule

The contracting officer shall insert the clause at FAR 52.222-41, Service Contract Labor Standards, in solicitations and contracts (except as provided in FAR 22.1006(a)(2)) if the contract is subject to the Service Contract Labor Standards statute and is either over $2,500, or for an indefinite dollar amount and the contracting officer does not know in advance that the contract amount will be $2,500 or less [@CL-OPM-0093; @CL-OPM-0094]. The contracting officer shall insert the clause at FAR 52.222-42, Statement of Equivalent Rates for Federal Hires, in solicitations and contracts if the contract amount is expected to be over $2,500 and the Service Contract Labor Standards statute is applicable [@CL-OPM-0095].

The contracting officer shall obtain wage determinations for each new service solicitation and contract in excess of $2,500 [@CL-OPM-0096], for each contract modification which brings the contract above $2,500 and either extends the existing contract pursuant to an option clause or otherwise or changes the scope of the contract whereby labor requirements are affected significantly [@CL-OPM-0096; @CL-OPM-0097], and for each multiple year contract in excess of $2,500 upon the annual anniversary date if the contract is subject to annual appropriations or the biennial anniversary date if the contract is not subject to annual appropriations and its proposed term exceeds 2 years, unless otherwise advised by the Wage and Hour Division [@CL-OPM-0098; @CL-OPM-0082].

FAR subpart 22.10 prescribes policies and procedures implementing the provisions of chapter 67 of title 41 of the United States Code, Service Contract Labor Standards (formerly known as the Service Contract Act of 1965) [@CL-OPM-1006], the applicable provisions of the Fair Labor Standards Act of 1938, as amended (section 201 and following of title 29 of the United States Code), and related Secretary of Labor regulations and instructions (29 CFR parts 4, 6, 8, and 1925) [@CL-OPM-1007]. Service contracts over $2,500 shall contain mandatory provisions regarding minimum wages and fringe benefits, safe and sanitary working conditions, notification to employees of the minimum allowable compensation, and equivalent Federal employee classifications and wage rates [@CL-OPM-1008]. Under section 6707(d) of title 41 of the United States Code, service contracts may not exceed 5 years [@CL-OPM-1009].

## How it works

If a wage determination was obtained through the e98 process, and bid opening, or commencement of work under a negotiated contract has been delayed, for whatever reason, more than 60 days from the date indicated on the previously submitted e98, the contracting officer shall submit a new e98 [@CL-OPM-1010]. Any revision of a wage determination received by the contracting agency as a result of that communication shall supersede the earlier response as the wage determination applicable to the particular acquisition subject to the time frames in FAR 22.1012-1(b) and (c) [@CL-OPM-1011]. As provided by the Service Contract Labor Standards statute, any contractor failure to comply with the requirements of the contract clauses related to the Service Contract Labor Standards statute may be grounds for termination for default (see paragraph (k) of the clause at FAR 52.222-41, Service Contract Labor Standards) [@CL-OPM-0187; @CL-OPM-1012].

The contracting officer shall cooperate with Department of Labor representatives in the examination of records, interviews with service employees, and all other aspects of investigations undertaken by the Department [@CL-OPM-1013]. When asked, agencies shall furnish the Wage and Hour Administrator or a designee any available information on contractors, subcontractors, their contracts, and the nature of the contract services [@CL-OPM-1014]. The contracting officer shall promptly refer, in writing to the appropriate regional office of the Department of Labor, apparent violations and complaints received [@CL-OPM-1015], and employee complaints shall not be disclosed to the employer [@CL-OPM-1016]. Disputes concerning labor standards requirements of the contract are handled under paragraph (t) of the contract clause at FAR 52.222-41, Service Contract Labor Standards, and not under the clause at FAR 52.233-1, Disputes [@CL-OPM-1017].

Effective February 1, 2026, DoD Class Deviation 2026-O0038 directs contracting officers to use the revised FAR part 52, Solicitation Provisions and Contract Clauses, published on the Revolutionary FAR Overhaul web page in lieu of the text codified at 48 CFR chapter 1 [@CL-CMP-9760; @CL-CMP-9762]. Under RFO FAR 52.222-41, the clause is inserted as prescribed in RFO FAR 22.1002-2(b)(1) [@CL-OPM-1063], whereas under codified FAR 22.1006(a)(1), the contracting officer shall insert the clause at FAR 52.222-41, Service Contract Labor Standards, in solicitations and contracts (except as provided in FAR 22.1006(a)(2)) when the contract is subject to the statute and meets the threshold conditions [@CL-OPM-0093; @CL-OPM-0094]. Under RFO FAR 52.222-41(p), headed Contractor's Representation, by entering into the contract, the Contractor (and officials thereof) represents that neither it nor any person or firm who has a substantial interest in the Contractor's firm is a person or firm ineligible to be awarded Government contracts by virtue of the sanctions imposed under section 6706 of title 41 of the United States Code [@CL-OPM-9002; @CL-OPM-1064]. Under codified FAR 52.222-41(p), headed Contractor's Certification, by entering into the contract, the Contractor (and officials thereof) certifies that neither it nor any person or firm who has a substantial interest in the Contractor's firm is a person or firm ineligible to be awarded Government contracts by virtue of the sanctions imposed under section 6706 of title 41 of the United States Code [@CL-OPM-9001; @CL-OPM-9000].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A service contract is awarded at 2,000 and the statute applies. Later a modification of 1,500 extends the contract through an option and brings it to 3,500. The answer: the modification brings the contract above the threshold and extends it, so the contracting officer obtains a wage determination, and the contractor does not.

## Never confuse

Under FAR 22.1006(a)(1), the contracting officer inserts the clause at FAR 52.222-41, Service Contract Labor Standards, in solicitations and contracts (except as provided in FAR 22.1006(a)(2)) if the contract is subject to the statute and is over $2,500 or for an indefinite dollar amount where the contracting officer does not know in advance that the contract amount will be $2,500 or less [@CL-OPM-0093; @CL-OPM-0094], while FAR 22.1007 sets out the service contracts for which the contracting officer shall obtain wage determinations [@CL-OPM-0096; @CL-OPM-0097; @CL-OPM-0098; @CL-OPM-0082]. Under FAR 22.305(a), the clause at FAR 52.222-4, Contract Work Hours and Safety Standards—Overtime Compensation, is not included in solicitations and contracts valued at or below $200,000 [@CL-OPM-0091; @CL-OPM-0092].
