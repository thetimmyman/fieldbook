## Purpose

Shows when the service contract labor standards clause goes into a contract and when the contracting officer must obtain wage determinations.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-OPM-0093] |
| Codified citation | FAR 22.1006 and FAR 22.1007 [@CL-OPM-0093; @CL-OPM-0096] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-OPM-0093] |
| Applies to | Contracts subject to the Service Contract Labor Standards statute that are over $2,500, or for an indefinite dollar amount where the contracting officer does not know in advance that the amount will be $2,500 or less, except as the exceptions paragraph provides [@CL-OPM-0093; @CL-OPM-0094] |

The contracting officer shall insert the Service Contract Labor Standards clause in solicitations and contracts, except as provided in the listed exceptions paragraph, if the contract is subject to the Service Contract Labor Standards statute and meets a listed condition, one of which is that the contract is over $2,500 [@CL-OPM-0093]. The listed condition is met either by a contract over $2,500 or by a contract for an indefinite dollar amount where the contracting officer does not know in advance that the contract amount will be $2,500 or less [@CL-OPM-0094]. This card does not cover the exceptions paragraph. The contracting officer shall also insert the Statement of Equivalent Rates for Federal Hires clause if the contract amount is expected to be over $2,500 and the statute is applicable [@CL-OPM-0095].

The contracting officer shall obtain wage determinations for each new service solicitation and contract in excess of $2,500, which is one of the listed service contracts [@CL-OPM-0096]. Another listed service contract is each contract modification which brings the contract above $2,500 and either extends the existing contract pursuant to an option clause or otherwise or changes the scope of the contract whereby labor requirements are affected significantly [@CL-OPM-0096; @CL-OPM-0097]. A third is each multiple year contract in excess of $2,500, upon the annual anniversary date if the contract is subject to annual appropriations [@CL-OPM-0098].

## How it works

For a multiple year contract, the list also names a biennial anniversary date if the contract is not subject to annual appropriations and its proposed term exceeds 2 years, unless otherwise advised by the Wage and Hour Division [@CL-OPM-0082]. The contracting officer decides the clause insertion [@CL-OPM-0093] and obtains the wage determinations [@CL-OPM-0096]. The printed sections state when clauses are inserted and when wage determinations are obtained. They give no wage rate that applies to a contract, and the only wage figures in them sit in an example of a price adjustment, so what a contractor must pay is not established here.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A service contract is awarded at 2,000 and the statute applies. Later a modification of 1,500 extends the contract through an option and brings it to 3,500. The answer: the modification brings the contract above the threshold and extends it, so the contracting officer obtains a wage determination, and the contractor does not.

## Never confuse

The clause prescription is not the wage determination: the clause goes in under its own prescription [@CL-OPM-0093], and wage determinations are obtained under a separate duty [@CL-OPM-0096]. The service contract threshold of $2,500 [@CL-OPM-0093] also differs from the $200,000 threshold at or below which the overtime standards clause is not included [@CL-OPM-0092].
