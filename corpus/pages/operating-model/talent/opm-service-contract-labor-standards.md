## Purpose

Shows when the service contract labor standards clause goes into a contract and when the contracting officer must obtain wage determinations.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-OPM-0093] |
| Codified citation | FAR 22.1000, FAR 22.1002-1, FAR 22.1006, FAR 22.1007, FAR 22.1014, FAR 22.1023, FAR 22.1024 and FAR 22.1026 [@CL-OPM-0093; @CL-OPM-0096; @CL-OPM-1006; @CL-OPM-1007; @CL-OPM-1008; @CL-OPM-1009; @CL-OPM-1010; @CL-OPM-1012; @CL-OPM-1013; @CL-OPM-1017] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-OPM-0093] |
| Applies to | Contracts subject to the Service Contract Labor Standards statute that are over $2,500, or for an indefinite dollar amount where the contracting officer does not know in advance that the amount will be $2,500 or less, except as the exceptions paragraph provides [@CL-OPM-0093; @CL-OPM-0094] |

The contracting officer shall insert the Service Contract Labor Standards clause in solicitations and contracts, except as provided in the listed exceptions paragraph, if the contract is subject to the Service Contract Labor Standards statute and meets a listed condition, one of which is that the contract is over $2,500 [@CL-OPM-0093]. The listed condition is met either by a contract over $2,500 or by a contract for an indefinite dollar amount where the contracting officer does not know in advance that the contract amount will be $2,500 or less [@CL-OPM-0094]. This card does not cover the exceptions paragraph. The contracting officer shall also insert the Statement of Equivalent Rates for Federal Hires clause if the contract amount is expected to be over $2,500 and the statute is applicable [@CL-OPM-0095].

The contracting officer shall obtain wage determinations for each new service solicitation and contract in excess of $2,500, which is one of the listed service contracts [@CL-OPM-0096]. Another listed service contract is each contract modification which brings the contract above $2,500 and either extends the existing contract pursuant to an option clause or otherwise or changes the scope of the contract whereby labor requirements are affected significantly [@CL-OPM-0096; @CL-OPM-0097]. A third is each multiple year contract in excess of $2,500, upon the annual anniversary date if the contract is subject to annual appropriations [@CL-OPM-0098].

Subpart 22.10 prescribes policies and procedures implementing the provisions of chapter 67 of title 41 of the United States Code, Service Contract Labor Standards (formerly known as the Service Contract Act of 1965) [@CL-OPM-1006], the applicable provisions of the Fair Labor Standards Act of 1938, as amended, and related Secretary of Labor regulations and instructions in 29 CFR parts 4, 6, 8, and 1925 [@CL-OPM-1007]. Service contracts over $2,500 shall contain mandatory provisions regarding minimum wages and fringe benefits, safe and sanitary working conditions, notification to employees of the minimum allowable compensation, and equivalent Federal employee classifications and wage rates [@CL-OPM-1008]. Under section 6707(d) of title 41 of the United States Code, service contracts may not exceed 5 years [@CL-OPM-1009].

## How it works

For a multiple year contract, the list also names a biennial anniversary date if the contract is not subject to annual appropriations and its proposed term exceeds 2 years, unless otherwise advised by the Wage and Hour Division [@CL-OPM-0082]. The contracting officer decides the clause insertion [@CL-OPM-0093] and obtains the wage determinations [@CL-OPM-0096]. The printed sections state when clauses are inserted and when wage determinations are obtained. They give no wage rate that applies to a contract, and the only wage figures in them sit in an example of a price adjustment, so what a contractor must pay is not established here.

If a wage determination came through the e98 process and bid opening, or the start of work under a negotiated contract, is delayed for whatever reason more than 60 days from the date on the earlier e98, the contracting officer must submit a new e98 [@CL-OPM-1010]. A revised wage determination received as a result supersedes the earlier response for that acquisition, subject to the time frames in 22.1012-1(b) and (c) [@CL-OPM-1011]. As provided by the Service Contract Labor Standards statute, any contractor failure to comply with the contract clauses related to that statute may be grounds for termination for default [@CL-OPM-1012].

The contracting officer must cooperate with Department of Labor representatives in examining records, interviewing service employees and the other parts of a Department investigation [@CL-OPM-1013]. When asked, agencies must give the Wage and Hour Administrator or a designee any available information on contractors, subcontractors, their contracts and the nature of the services [@CL-OPM-1014]. The contracting officer must promptly refer apparent violations and complaints received, in writing, to the appropriate regional office of the Department of Labor [@CL-OPM-1015], and employee complaints must not be disclosed to the employer [@CL-OPM-1016]. Disputes about the contract's labor standards requirements are handled under paragraph (t) of the clause at 52.222-41, not under the Disputes clause at 52.233-1 [@CL-OPM-1017].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text from February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. The RFO text of the clause at 52.222-41, Service Contract Labor Standards, says to insert it as prescribed in 22.1002-2(b)(1) [@CL-OPM-1063], where the codified prescription is in FAR 22.1006 [@CL-OPM-0093]. Under RFO paragraph (p)(1), by entering into the contract the Contractor, and officials of the Contractor, represent that neither the Contractor nor any person or firm who has a substantial interest in the Contractor's firm is a person or firm ineligible to be awarded Government contracts by virtue of the sanctions imposed under section 6706 of title 41 of the United States Code [@CL-OPM-1064]. The codified paragraph is headed Contractor's Certification and uses the verb certifies [@CL-OPM-9001; @CL-OPM-9000]; the RFO paragraph is headed Contractor's Representation and uses the verb represents [@CL-OPM-9002; @CL-OPM-1064].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A service contract is awarded at 2,000 and the statute applies. Later a modification of 1,500 extends the contract through an option and brings it to 3,500. The answer: the modification brings the contract above the threshold and extends it, so the contracting officer obtains a wage determination, and the contractor does not.

## Never confuse

The clause prescription is not the wage determination: the clause goes in under its own prescription [@CL-OPM-0093], and wage determinations are obtained under a separate duty [@CL-OPM-0096]. The service contract threshold of $2,500 [@CL-OPM-0093] also differs from the $200,000 threshold at or below which the overtime standards clause is not included [@CL-OPM-0092].
