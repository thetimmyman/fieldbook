## Purpose

Explains the overtime pay rule for laborers and mechanics, the liquidated damages and who determines them, and when the clause is not included.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-OPM-0088] |
| Codified citation | FAR 22.301, FAR 22.302, FAR 22.303, FAR 22.305 and FAR 22.403-3 [@CL-OPM-0088; @CL-OPM-0089; @CL-OPM-0091; @CL-OPM-1003; @CL-OPM-1004; @CL-OPM-1005] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-OPM-0088] |
| Applies to | Solicitations and contracts (including, for this purpose, basic ordering agreements) when the contract may require or involve the employment of laborers or mechanics, except in the cases excluded by FAR 22.305(a) through (g) [@CL-OPM-0091; @CL-OPM-0092; @CL-OPM-0183; @CL-OPM-0184] |

Under FAR 22.301, certain contracts contain a clause specifying that no laborer or mechanic doing any part of the work contemplated by the contract shall be required or permitted to work more than 40 hours in any workweek unless paid for all such overtime hours at not less than one and one-half times the basic rate of pay [@CL-OPM-0088]. When an overtime computation discloses underpayments, the responsible contractor or subcontractor must pay the affected employee any unpaid wages and pay liquidated damages to the Government [@CL-OPM-0089].

The contracting officer must assess liquidated damages at the rate specified at 29 CFR 5.5(b)(2) per affected employee for each calendar day on which the employer required or permitted the employee to work in excess of the standard workweek of 40 hours without paying overtime wages required by the statute [@CL-OPM-0181; @CL-OPM-0090]. In accordance with the Federal Civil Penalties Inflation Adjustment Act of 1990 (section 2461 note of title 28 of the United States Code), the Department of Labor adjusts that civil monetary penalty for inflation no later than January 15 each year [@CL-OPM-0182].

Under FAR 22.403-3, chapter 37 of title 40 of the United States Code, Contract Work Hours and Safety Standards, requires that certain contracts (see FAR 22.305) contain a clause (see FAR 52.222-4) specifying that no laborer or mechanic doing any part of the work contemplated by the contract shall be required or permitted to work more than 40 hours in any workweek unless paid for all additional hours at not less than 1 1/2 times the basic rate of pay (see FAR 22.301) [@CL-OPM-1004; @CL-OPM-1005]. Under FAR 22.303, the procedures and reports required for construction contracts in FAR subpart 22.4 also apply to investigations of alleged violations of the statute on other than construction contracts [@CL-OPM-1003].

## How it works

Under FAR 22.305, insert the clause at FAR 52.222-4, Contract Work Hours and Safety Standards—Overtime Compensation, in solicitations and contracts (including, for this purpose, basic ordering agreements) when the contract may require or involve the employment of laborers or mechanics [@CL-OPM-0091]. However, do not include the clause in solicitations and contracts valued at or below $200,000, for commercial products and commercial services, for transportation or the transmission of intelligence [@CL-OPM-0092], to be performed outside the United States, Puerto Rico, American Samoa, Guam, the United States Virgin Islands, Johnston Island, Wake Island, and the outer Continental Shelf as defined in the Outer Continental Shelf Lands Act (section 1331 of title 43 of the United States Code) (29 CFR 5.15) [@CL-OPM-0183], for work to be done solely in accordance with chapter 65 of title 41 of the United States Code (see FAR subpart 22.6), for supplies that include incidental services that do not require substantial employment of laborers or mechanics, or exempt under regulations of the Secretary of Labor (29 CFR 5.15) [@CL-OPM-0184]. Under FAR 22.302(d), after the contracting officer determines the liquidated damages and the contractor makes appropriate payments, any remaining assessments are disbursed in accordance with agency procedures [@CL-OPM-0125].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A covered contract of 500,000 has a mechanic who works 46 hours in a workweek and is paid straight time for all hours. The 6 overtime hours are underpaid. The contractor must pay the unpaid wages and liquidated damages to the Government, and the damages run per affected employee for each day the employee worked over the standard workweek without the overtime pay the statute requires. The answer: the contractor owes both the wages and the damages.

## Never confuse

For overtime premium approvals on cost-reimbursement, time-and-materials, and labor-hour contracts, see [Overtime premiums on cost-type, time-and-materials and labor-hour contracts](opm-overtime-premium-approval.md). Unpaid wages and liquidated damages are not the same payment: under FAR 22.302(a), when an overtime computation discloses underpayments, the responsible contractor or subcontractor must pay the affected employee any unpaid wages and pay liquidated damages to the Government [@CL-OPM-0089], and under FAR 22.302(d), after the contracting officer determines the liquidated damages and the contractor makes appropriate payments, any remaining assessments are disbursed in accordance with agency procedures [@CL-OPM-0125].
