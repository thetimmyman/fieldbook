## Purpose

Explains the overtime pay rule for laborers and mechanics, the liquidated damages and who determines them, and when the clause is not included.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-OPM-0088] |
| Codified citation | FAR 22.301, FAR 22.302, FAR 22.303, FAR 22.305 and FAR 22.403-3 [@CL-OPM-0088; @CL-OPM-0089; @CL-OPM-0091; @CL-OPM-1003; @CL-OPM-1004; @CL-OPM-1005] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-OPM-0088] |
| Applies to | Contracts that may require or involve the employment of laborers or mechanics, other than the listed excluded cases [@CL-OPM-0091; @CL-OPM-0092] |

Certain contracts contain a clause specifying that no laborer or mechanic doing any part of the work shall be required or permitted to work more than 40 hours in any workweek unless paid for all such overtime hours at not less than one and one-half times the basic rate of pay [@CL-OPM-0088]. When an overtime computation discloses underpayments, the responsible contractor or subcontractor must pay the affected employee any unpaid wages and pay liquidated damages to the Government [@CL-OPM-0089].

The contracting officer must assess liquidated damages at the rate specified in the cited regulation per affected employee for each calendar day on which the employer required or permitted the employee to work in excess of the standard workweek of 40 hours without paying overtime wages required by the statute [@CL-OPM-0090]. The section does not state that rate in dollars.

Chapter 37 of title 40 of the United States Code, Contract Work Hours and Safety Standards, requires that certain contracts (see 22.305) contain a clause (see 52.222-4) specifying that no laborer or mechanic doing any part of the work contemplated by the contract shall be required or permitted to work more than 40 hours [@CL-OPM-1004] in any workweek unless paid for all additional hours at not less than 1 1/2 times the basic rate of pay (see 22.301) [@CL-OPM-1005]. The procedures and reports required for construction contracts in subpart 22.4 also apply to investigations of alleged violations of the statute on other than construction contracts [@CL-OPM-1003].

## How it works

The contracting officer inserts the clause in solicitations and contracts, including basic ordering agreements, when the contract may require or involve the employment of laborers or mechanics [@CL-OPM-0091]. However, the clause is not included in solicitations and contracts valued at or below $200,000, for commercial products and commercial services, or for transportation or the transmission of intelligence [@CL-OPM-0092]. The printed section continues with other listed cases that this card does not cover.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A covered contract of 500,000 has a mechanic who works 46 hours in a workweek and is paid straight time for all hours. The 6 overtime hours are underpaid. The contractor must pay the unpaid wages and liquidated damages to the Government, and the damages run per affected employee for each day the employee worked over the standard workweek without the overtime pay the statute requires. The answer: the contractor owes both the wages and the damages.

## Never confuse

Paying employees the required overtime under this statute is not the same as the approval of overtime premiums under the overtime premiums clause; see [Overtime premiums on cost-type, time-and-materials and labor-hour contracts](opm-overtime-premium-approval.md) for those approval rules. The liquidated damages here are paid to the Government and the unpaid wages to the affected employee [@CL-OPM-0089], and the contracting officer determines the liquidated damages [@CL-OPM-0125].
