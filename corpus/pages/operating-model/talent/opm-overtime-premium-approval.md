## Purpose

Shows the policy to avoid overtime, who approves it, and when the overtime premiums clause applies.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-OPM-0080] |
| Codified citation | FAR 22.103-1, FAR 22.103-2, FAR 22.103-4 and FAR 22.103-5 [@CL-OPM-0080; @CL-OPM-0083; @CL-OPM-0086; @CL-OPM-1000; @CL-OPM-1001; @CL-OPM-1002] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-OPM-0080] |
| Applies to | Contracts with the overtime premiums clause, and time-and-materials and labor-hour contracts for payment approval [@CL-OPM-0084; @CL-OPM-0086] |

Contractors shall perform all contracts, so far as practicable, without using overtime, particularly as a regular employment practice, except when lower overall costs to the Government will result or when it is necessary to meet urgent program needs [@CL-OPM-0080]. Approval of the use of overtime may be granted by an agency approving official after determining in writing that overtime is necessary to meet essential delivery or performance schedules, which is one of the listed purposes [@CL-OPM-0081]. Two further listed purposes are to make up for delays beyond the control and without the fault or negligence of the contractor and to eliminate foreseeable extended production bottlenecks that cannot be eliminated in any other way [@CL-OPM-0129].

Approval by the designated official of the use and total dollar amount of overtime is required before an amount is included in the overtime premiums clause [@CL-OPM-0083]. Contracting officer approval of payment of overtime premiums is required for time-and-materials and labor-hour contracts [@CL-OPM-0084]. The section also states that no approvals are required for paying overtime premiums under other types of contracts [@CL-OPM-0085]. It does not spell out how that statement relates to the designated official's approval before an amount is included in the clause [@CL-OPM-0083].

As used in subpart 22.1, a normal workweek means, generally, a workweek of 40 hours [@CL-OPM-1000]. Outside the United States and its outlying areas, a workweek longer than 40 hours is considered normal if the workweek does not exceed the norm for the area, as determined by local custom, tradition, or law [@CL-OPM-1001], and if the hours worked in excess of 40 in the workweek are not compensated at a premium rate of pay [@CL-OPM-1002].

FAR 2.101 defines overtime as time worked by a contractor's employee in excess of the employee's normal workweek [@CL-OPM-1093]. It defines the overtime premium as the difference between the contractor's regular rate of pay to an employee for the shift involved and the higher rate paid for overtime [@CL-OPM-1094], and says the premium does not include shift premium, which is the difference between the contractor's regular rate of pay to an employee and the higher rate paid for extra-pay-shift work [@CL-OPM-1095]. The RFO text of FAR 2.101 defines overtime, the overtime premium and the exclusion of shift premium in a way that is the same in substance as the codified definitions [@CL-OPM-1118; @CL-OPM-1119; @CL-OPM-1120; @CL-OPM-1093; @CL-OPM-1094; @CL-OPM-1095].

## How it works

The contracting officer shall include the overtime premiums clause in solicitations and contracts when a cost-reimbursement contract is contemplated and the contract amount is expected to exceed the simplified acquisition threshold, unless an exception stated in the same sentence applies [@CL-OPM-0086]. That sentence names two exceptions: a contemplated cost-reimbursement contract for operation of vessels, and a contemplated cost-plus-incentive-fee contract that will provide a swing from the target fee of at least plus or minus 3 percent and a contractor's share of at least 10 percent [@CL-OPM-0087]. The source text does not state the dollar value of the simplified acquisition threshold.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A cost-plus-fixed-fee contract is expected to exceed the threshold and no listed exception applies, so the clause is included. The designated official approves the use of overtime and a total of 80,000 for the clause. During performance the contractor wants 100,000 of overtime. The answer: the extra amount is beyond the approved total, so it needs further approval, and the approval comes from Government officials, not from the contractor.

## Never confuse

Approval before an amount goes into the clause is not the same as approval of payment: the designated official's approval concerns the use and total dollar amount of overtime [@CL-OPM-0083], while contracting officer approval of payment of overtime premiums is required for time-and-materials and labor-hour contracts [@CL-OPM-0084].
