## Purpose

Shows the policy to avoid overtime, who approves it, and when the overtime premiums clause applies.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-OPM-0080] |
| Codified citation | FAR 22.103-1; FAR 22.103-2; FAR 22.103-4; FAR 22.103-5 [@CL-OPM-0080; @CL-OPM-0083; @CL-OPM-0086; @CL-OPM-1000; @CL-OPM-1001; @CL-OPM-1002]; FAR 2.101 [@CL-OPM-1093; @CL-OPM-1094; @CL-OPM-1095] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 2.101 [@CL-OPM-1118; @CL-OPM-1119; @CL-OPM-1120] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-OPM-0080] |
| Applies to | Contracts with the overtime premiums clause, and time-and-materials and labor-hour contracts for payment approval [@CL-OPM-0084; @CL-OPM-0086] |

Contractors shall perform all contracts, so far as practicable, without using overtime, particularly as a regular employment practice, except when lower overall costs to the Government will result or when it is necessary to meet urgent program needs, and any approved overtime, extra-pay shifts, and multishifts should be scheduled to achieve those objectives [@CL-OPM-0080; @CL-OPM-0185]. The contracting officer shall review the contractor's request for overtime [@CL-OPM-0186]. Approval of the use of overtime may be granted by an agency approving official after determining in writing that overtime is necessary for one of three alternatives: to meet essential delivery or performance schedules, to make up for delays beyond the control and without the fault or negligence of the contractor, or to eliminate foreseeable extended production bottlenecks that cannot be eliminated in any other way [@CL-OPM-0081; @CL-OPM-0129].

Approval by the designated official of use and total dollar amount of overtime is required before inclusion of an amount in paragraph (a) of the clause at FAR 52.222-2, Payment for Overtime Premiums [@CL-OPM-0083]. Contracting officer approval of payment of overtime premiums is required for time-and-materials and labor-hour contracts [@CL-OPM-0084]. No approvals are required for paying overtime premiums under other types of contracts [@CL-OPM-0085].

Normal workweek, as used in FAR subpart 22.1, means, generally, a workweek of 40 hours [@CL-OPM-1000]. Outside the United States and its outlying areas, a workweek longer than 40 hours is considered normal if the workweek does not exceed the norm for the area, as determined by local custom, tradition, or law [@CL-OPM-1001], and the hours worked in excess of 40 in the workweek are not compensated at a premium rate of pay [@CL-OPM-1002].

Under FAR 2.101, overtime means time worked by a contractor's employee in excess of the employee's normal workweek [@CL-OPM-1093], and overtime premium means the difference between the contractor's regular rate of pay to an employee for the shift involved and the higher rate paid for overtime [@CL-OPM-1094]; it does not include shift premium, i.e., the difference between the contractor's regular rate of pay to an employee and the higher rate paid for extra-pay-shift work [@CL-OPM-1095]. Under RFO FAR 2.101, overtime means time worked by a contractor's employee in excess of the employee's normal workweek [@CL-OPM-1118], and overtime premium means the difference between the contractor's regular rate of pay to an employee for the shift involved and the higher rate paid for overtime [@CL-OPM-1119]; it does not include shift premium, i.e., the difference between the contractor's regular rate of pay to an employee and the higher rate paid for extra-pay-shift work [@CL-OPM-1120].

## How it works

Under FAR 22.103-5(b), the contracting officer shall include the clause at FAR 52.222-2, Payment for Overtime Premiums, in solicitations and contracts when a cost-reimbursement contract is contemplated and the contract amount is expected to exceed the simplified acquisition threshold, unless either a cost-reimbursement contract for operation of vessels is contemplated, or a cost-plus-incentive-fee contract that will provide a swing from the target fee of at least plus or minus 3 percent and a contractor's share of at least 10 percent is contemplated [@CL-OPM-0086; @CL-OPM-0087]. Under FAR 22.103-4(f), during contract performance, contractor requests for overtime exceeding the amount authorized by paragraph (a) of the clause at FAR 52.222-2 shall be submitted as stated in paragraph (b) of that clause to the office designated by the contracting officer, and the office receiving the request shall review the request and report in writing its facts and recommendations to the approving official [@CL-CMP-6469; @CL-CMP-6470; @CL-CMP-6471].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A cost-plus-fixed-fee contract is expected to exceed the threshold and no listed exception applies, so the clause is included. The designated official approves the use of overtime and a total of 80,000 for the clause. During performance the contractor wants 100,000 of overtime. The answer: the extra amount is beyond the approved total, so it needs further approval, and the approval comes from Government officials, not from the contractor.

## Never confuse

Under FAR 22.103-4(b), approval by the designated official of use and total dollar amount of overtime is required before inclusion of an amount in paragraph (a) of the clause at FAR 52.222-2, Payment for Overtime Premiums [@CL-OPM-0083], whereas under FAR 22.103-4(c), contracting officer approval of payment of overtime premiums is required for time-and-materials and labor-hour contracts [@CL-OPM-0084], and under FAR 22.103-4(d), no approvals are required for paying overtime premiums under other types of contracts [@CL-OPM-0085].
