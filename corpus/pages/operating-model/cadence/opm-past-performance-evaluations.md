## Purpose

Shows which contracts get a past performance evaluation, the deadlines that run from it, who reviews disagreements, how long the information is used, and how late small business subcontract payments enter the evaluation.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-OPM-0038] |
| Codified citation | FAR 42.1502; FAR 42.1503 [@CL-OPM-0038; @CL-OPM-0046]; FAR 2.101 [@CL-OPM-1103] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 42.1102 [@CL-OPM-0131; @CL-OPM-0132; @CL-OPM-0133]; RFO FAR 42.1103 [@CL-OPM-0142; @CL-OPM-0145; @CL-OPM-0148] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-OPM-0038] |
| Applies to | Contracts and orders above the simplified acquisition threshold, subject to listed exceptions [@CL-OPM-0039] |

Past performance evaluations shall be prepared at least annually and at the time the work under a contract or order is completed [@CL-OPM-0038]. Except as provided in the listed exception paragraphs, agencies shall prepare evaluations of contractor performance for each contract that exceeds the simplified acquisition threshold and for each order that exceeds the simplified acquisition threshold [@CL-OPM-0039]. Agencies shall not evaluate performance for contracts awarded under FAR subpart 8.7 [@CL-OPM-0112]. Past performance evaluations shall also be prepared for each construction contract of $900,000 or more and each construction contract terminated for default regardless of contract value [@CL-OPM-0040]. The same applies to each architect-engineer services contract of $45,000 or more and each such contract terminated for default regardless of contract value [@CL-OPM-0041]. The threshold amount is on [Simplified acquisition and micro-purchase thresholds](../../compliance/regulatory-framework/cmp-acquisition-thresholds.md), because these sections do not state it.

The FAR definitions section describes past performance as an offeror's or contractor's performance on active and physically completed contracts, with a cross-reference to 4.804-4 [@CL-OPM-1103].

Past performance evaluations shall include an assessment of the contractor's performance against, and efforts to achieve, the goals identified in the small business subcontracting plan when the contract includes the listed clause [@CL-OPM-0042]. One listed item to assess is reduced or untimely payments made to small business subcontractors that the contracting officer determines to be unjustified [@CL-OPM-0043]. The contracting officer shall consider and evaluate the contractor's written explanation when determining whether such a payment is justified [@CL-OPM-0044]. One listed duty is to determine that a history of unjustified reduced or untimely payments has occurred when the contractor has reported three or more occasions under a single contract within a 12-month period [@CL-OPM-0045]. Three listed payment or nonpayment situations that are not considered unjustified are a contract dispute on performance, a partial payment made for amounts not in dispute, and a payment reduced due to past overpayments [@CL-OPM-0115]. Two further listed situations are an administrative mistake and late performance by the subcontractor that leads to later payment by the prime contractor [@CL-OPM-0116].

## How it works

Contractors shall be afforded up to 14 calendar days from the date of notification of availability to submit comments, rebutting statements, or additional information [@CL-OPM-0046]. Agencies shall provide for review at a level above the contracting officer to consider disagreements, and the ultimate conclusion is a decision of the contracting agency [@CL-OPM-0047]. Agencies shall prepare and submit all past performance evaluations electronically in CPARS [@CL-OPM-0113]. Past performance evaluations, including any contractor-submitted information, become available for source selection officials not later than 14 days after the date on which the contractor is notified of the evaluation's availability for comment [@CL-OPM-0048]. Evaluations for classified contracts and special access programs shall not be reported in CPARS, but will be reported as stated in the subpart and in accordance with agency procedures [@CL-OPM-0114]. Agencies shall use the past performance information in CPARS that is within three years of the completion of performance of the evaluated contract or order, or within six years for construction and architect-engineer contracts [@CL-OPM-0049]. The same sentence also names other information, which this card does not cover.

DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 42 text in place of the codified FAR part 42 text because DoD Class Deviation 2026-O0050 directs it, effective March 16, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9759] [@CL-CMP-9761]. The RFO policy section keeps the rule that evaluations are prepared at least annually and at the time the work under a contract or order is completed [@CL-OPM-0131] [@CL-OPM-0038], and keeps the construction and architect-engineer amounts, each with the case of a contract terminated for default regardless of contract value [@CL-OPM-0134] [@CL-OPM-0040] [@CL-OPM-0135] [@CL-OPM-0041]. It words the threshold rule as each contract and order that exceeds the simplified acquisition threshold, where the codified sentence says each contract, and both sentences keep the exception for paragraphs (e), (f) and (h) [@CL-OPM-0132] [@CL-OPM-0039]. Its content rules keep the assessment of performance against the small business subcontracting plan goals when the contract includes the listed clause [@CL-OPM-0136] [@CL-OPM-0042], the item on reduced or untimely payments to small business subcontractors determined by the contracting officer to be unjustified, now with the definition cross-reference to part 19 where the codified item cites 19.701 [@CL-OPM-0137] [@CL-OPM-0043], the duty to consider and evaluate the contractor's written explanation [@CL-OPM-0138] [@CL-OPM-0044], the determination that a history has occurred when three or more occasions are reported under a single contract within a 12-month period [@CL-OPM-0139] [@CL-OPM-0045], and the five situations that are not considered unjustified [@CL-OPM-0140] [@CL-OPM-0141] [@CL-OPM-0115] [@CL-OPM-0116]. The comment period of up to 14 calendar days is kept [@CL-OPM-0142] [@CL-OPM-0046], and so are electronic submission in CPARS [@CL-OPM-0144] [@CL-OPM-0113], the rule that classified contract and special access program evaluations are not reported in CPARS [@CL-OPM-0146] [@CL-OPM-0114] and the 14-day limit, counted from the contractor notification, for making the evaluation and any contractor-submitted information available to source selection officials [@CL-OPM-0145] [@CL-OPM-0048]. The review rule is worded differently: the RFO text says that while the evaluation is an agency decision, agencies must provide for review at a level above the contracting officer in the event of disagreements [@CL-OPM-0143], where the codified text says agencies shall provide for review at a level above the contracting officer to consider disagreements and that the ultimate conclusion is a decision of the contracting agency [@CL-OPM-0047]. The three-year and six-year windows for past performance information in CPARS are kept [@CL-OPM-0147] [@CL-OPM-0049], and the same RFO sentence names responsibility and qualification reports in SAM as other information [@CL-OPM-0148], where the codified sentence names the Federal Awardee Performance and Integrity Information System [@CL-OPM-0163]. Paragraph (h) of the RFO policy section says agencies must promptly report other contractor information in accordance with 42.1103(g) [@CL-OPM-0133], where codified paragraph (i) says agencies shall do so in accordance with 42.1503(h) [@CL-OPM-0164]. The codified paragraph (h) sentence that agencies shall not evaluate performance for contracts awarded under subpart 8.7 [@CL-OPM-0112] is not in RFO paragraph (h) [@CL-OPM-0133], and the RFO threshold paragraph still lists (h) among its exceptions [@CL-OPM-0132].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor is told on the first of the month that an evaluation of its 10,000,000 contract is ready for comment. It can comment for up to 14 calendar days from that notice. Source selection officials must be able to see the evaluation no later than 14 days after the notice. Separately, two late payments to small business subcontractors on one contract are found unjustified in the first six months, and a third is found in month eleven. The contractor reports all three occasions, so the contracting officer determines that a history has occurred.

## Never confuse

The comment period is not an appeal. Disagreements go to review at a level above the contracting officer, and the ultimate conclusion is a decision of the contracting agency [@CL-OPM-0047]. The comment period and the latest time by which source selection officials can see the evaluation both run from the notice to the contractor [@CL-OPM-0046; @CL-OPM-0048]. The paragraph letter (h) is not the same rule in both texts: codified 42.1502(h) is the subpart 8.7 exclusion [@CL-OPM-0112], while RFO 42.1102(h) is the duty to report other contractor information promptly, which codified 42.1502 states in paragraph (i) [@CL-OPM-0133] [@CL-OPM-0164].
