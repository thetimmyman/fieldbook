## Purpose

Shows who decides whether a postaward orientation is held, which factors the contracting officer weighs, and the rules for running the conference without changing the contract.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-OPM-0032] |
| Codified citation | FAR 42.501; FAR 42.502; FAR 42.503-2 [@CL-OPM-0032; @CL-OPM-0033; @CL-OPM-0035] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-OPM-0032] |
| Applies to | Contracts for which the contracting officer decides a postaward orientation is necessary [@CL-OPM-0032] |

Cognizant Government or contractor personnel may request the contracting officer to arrange for orientation, but it is up to the contracting officer to decide whether a postaward orientation in any form is necessary [@CL-OPM-0032]. When deciding whether it is necessary and what form it shall take, the contracting officer shall consider, as a minimum, listed factors, the first being the nature and extent of the preaward survey and any other prior discussions with the contractor [@CL-OPM-0033]. One listed factor is complex financing arrangements, such as progress payments, advance payments, or guaranteed loans [@CL-OPM-0034].

The chairperson of a postaward conference shall conduct the meeting and, unless a contract change is contemplated, shall emphasize that it is not the purpose of the meeting to change the contract [@CL-OPM-0035].

## How it works

The contracting officer decides whether to hold the orientation, and the chairperson runs the meeting [@CL-OPM-0032; @CL-OPM-0035]. At the conference the contracting officer may make commitments or give directions within the scope of the contracting officer's authority, and shall put in writing and sign any commitment or direction, whether or not it changes the contract [@CL-OPM-0036]. Any change that results shall be made only by a contract modification referencing the applicable terms of the contract, and participants without authority to bind the Government shall not take action that in any way alters the contract [@CL-OPM-0037]. Maximum benefits will be realized when orientation is conducted promptly after award, but the source text sets no day count [@CL-OPM-0110]. It does not say which finance staff attend or what finance should bring. Complex financing arrangements are a listed factor in deciding whether to hold an orientation and in what form [@CL-OPM-0034]. As this card's own suggestion, not a rule of the source, a finance reader on a contract with such arrangements can sensibly come ready to discuss the financing terms.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A fixed-price contract worth 20,000,000 has progress payments and several subcontractors. A finance lead asks the contracting officer to arrange an orientation. The contracting officer weighs the factors, including the complex financing, and decides to hold one. The chairperson opens by saying the meeting is not meant to change the contract. A participant suggests a higher billing rate. The contracting officer writes down and signs only what is within the contracting officer's authority, and any change goes through a modification.

## Never confuse

An orientation is not a contract modification. A change to the contract that results from the conference is made only by a contract modification referencing the applicable terms [@CL-OPM-0037]. Nor is an orientation to be used to alter the final agreement arrived at in any negotiations leading to contract award [@CL-OPM-0111]. Contract administration functions delegated to a contract administration office are a separate subject; see [Contract administration functions](../../compliance/audit-retention/cmp-contract-administration-functions.md).
