## Purpose

Shows who decides whether a postaward orientation is held, which factors the contracting officer weighs, and the rules for running the conference without changing the contract.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-OPM-0032] |
| Codified citation | FAR 42.500 [@CL-OPM-0165]; FAR 42.501; FAR 42.502; FAR 42.503-2 [@CL-OPM-0032; @CL-OPM-0033; @CL-OPM-0035]; FAR 42.302(a)(3) [@CL-OPM-0166] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 42.302(a)(3) [@CL-OPM-0150]; RFO FAR 42.500 [@CL-OPM-0151] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-OPM-0032] |
| Applies to | Contracts for which the contracting officer decides a postaward orientation is necessary [@CL-OPM-0032] |

While cognizant Government or contractor personnel may request the contracting officer to arrange for orientation, it is up to the contracting officer to decide whether a postaward orientation in any form is necessary [@CL-OPM-0032]. When deciding whether postaward orientation is necessary and, if so, what form it shall take, the contracting officer shall consider, as a minimum, the nature and extent of the preaward survey and any other prior discussions with the contractor [@CL-OPM-0033], the type, value, and complexity of the contract, the complexity and acquisition history of the product or service, the requirements for spare parts and related equipment, and the urgency of the delivery schedule and relationship of the product or service to critical programs [@CL-OPM-0118], the length of the planned production cycle, the extent of subcontracting, and the contractor's performance history and experience with the product or service [@CL-OPM-0119], the contractor's status, if any, as a small business, small disadvantaged, women-owned, veteran-owned, HUBZone, or service-disabled veteran-owned small business concern [@CL-OPM-0120], the contractor's performance history with small, small disadvantaged, women-owned, veteran-owned, HUBZone, and service-disabled veteran-owned small business subcontracting programs [@CL-OPM-0121], safety precautions required for hazardous materials or operations, and complex financing arrangements, such as progress payments, advance payments, or guaranteed loans [@CL-OPM-0034].

The chairperson of the conference shall conduct the meeting, and unless a contract change is contemplated, the chairperson shall emphasize that it is not the purpose of the meeting to change the contract [@CL-OPM-0035].

## How it works

It is up to the contracting officer to decide whether a postaward orientation in any form is necessary, and the chairperson of the conference shall conduct the meeting [@CL-OPM-0032; @CL-OPM-0035]. The contracting officer may make commitments or give directions within the scope of the contracting officer's authority and shall put in writing and sign any commitment or direction, whether or not it changes the contract [@CL-OPM-0036]. Any change to the contract that results from the postaward conference shall be made only by a contract modification referencing the applicable terms of the contract, and participants without authority to bind the Government shall not take action that in any way alters the contract [@CL-OPM-0037]. Maximum benefits will be realized when orientation is conducted promptly after award [@CL-OPM-0110].

Effective March 16, 2026, DoD Class Deviation 2026-O0050 directs contracting officers to use the revised FAR part 42, Contract Administration and Audit Services, published on the Revolutionary FAR Overhaul web page in lieu of the text codified at 48 CFR chapter 1 [@CL-CMP-9759; @CL-CMP-9761]. Under RFO FAR 42.500, subpart 42.5 prescribes policies and procedures for establishing billing rates and final indirect cost rates [@CL-OPM-0151], whereas codified FAR 42.500 prescribes policies and procedures for the postaward orientation of contractors and subcontractors through a conference or a letter or other form of written communication [@CL-OPM-0165]. Under RFO FAR 42.302(a)(3), conducting post-award orientation conferences is item (3) of the contract administration functions that the contracting officer normally delegates to a CAO, to the extent they apply to a specific contract [@CL-OPM-0149; @CL-CMP-0027; @CL-OPM-0150]. Under codified FAR 42.302(a)(3), to the extent they apply, the contracting officer normally delegates to a CAO the contract administration functions in FAR 42.302(a), where item (3) is to conduct post-award orientation conferences [@CL-CMP-1006; @CL-CMP-1007; @CL-OPM-0166].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A fixed-price contract worth 20,000,000 has progress payments and several subcontractors. A finance lead asks the contracting officer to arrange an orientation. The contracting officer weighs the factors, including the complex financing, and decides to hold one. The chairperson opens by saying the meeting is not meant to change the contract. A participant suggests a higher billing rate. The contracting officer writes down and signs only what is within the contracting officer's authority, and any change goes through a modification.

## Never confuse

Any change to the contract that results from the postaward conference shall be made only by a contract modification referencing the applicable terms of the contract [@CL-OPM-0037]. A postaward orientation is not a substitute for the contractor's fully understanding the work requirements at the time offers are submitted, nor is it to be used to alter the final agreement arrived at in any negotiations leading to contract award [@CL-OPM-0111]. For contract administration functions delegated to a CAO, see [Contract administration functions](../../compliance/audit-retention/cmp-contract-administration-functions.md). Under RFO FAR 42.302(a)(3), conducting post-award orientation conferences is a contract administration function that the contracting officer normally delegates to a CAO to the extent it applies to a specific contract [@CL-OPM-0149; @CL-CMP-0027; @CL-OPM-0150], whereas under FAR 42.501(c), while cognizant Government or contractor personnel may request the contracting officer to arrange for orientation, it is up to the contracting officer to decide whether a postaward orientation in any form is necessary [@CL-OPM-0032].
