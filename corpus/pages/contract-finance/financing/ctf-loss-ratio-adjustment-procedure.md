## Purpose

Explains who prepares the supplementary analysis behind a loss ratio adjustment to a progress payment request on a loss contract, and how the adjustment is recorded.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS and codified FAR [@CL-CTF-3367; @CL-CTF-3411] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 232.503-6 and FAR 32.503-6(g) [@CL-CTF-3367; @CL-CTF-3411] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CTF-3367] |
| Applies to | Progress payment requests on loss contracts [@CL-CTF-3410] |

For loss contracts, the DFARS directs use of the following loss ratio adjustment procedures for making the adjustments required by FAR 32.503-6(f) and (g) [@CL-CTF-3410]. Under the FAR, if the sum of the total costs incurred under a contract plus the estimated costs to complete the performance are likely to exceed the contract price, the contracting officer must compute a loss ratio factor and adjust future progress payments to exclude the element of loss [@CL-CTF-3411].

Except where the alternative below applies, the contracting officer must prepare a supplementary analysis of the contractor's request for progress payments and calculate the loss ratio adjustment using the procedures in FAR 32.503-6(g) [@CL-CTF-3367]. The contracting officer may instead request the contractor to prepare the supplementary analysis as an attachment to the progress payment request, when the contracting officer determines that the contractor's methods of estimating the costs to complete are reliable, accurate, and not susceptible to improper influences [@CL-CTF-3368]. So the contractor prepares the analysis only if the contracting officer asks for it, and the contracting officer makes the reliability determination [@CL-CTF-3368].

## How it works

The loss ratio factor is computed in two steps. The current contract price used in progress payment computations (the current ceiling price under fixed-price incentive contracts) is revised to include the not-to-exceed amount for any pending change orders and unpriced orders [@CL-CTF-3488]. The factor is then the revised contract price divided by the sum of the total costs incurred to date plus the estimated additional costs of completing the contract performance [@CL-CTF-3489]. If the contracting officer believes a loss is probable, future progress payment requests are modified so that the contract price is the revised amount [@CL-CTF-3490], the total costs eligible for progress payments are the sum of paid costs eligible for progress payments times the loss ratio factor [@CL-CTF-3491], and the costs applicable to items delivered, invoiced, and accepted do not include costs in excess of the contract price of the items [@CL-CTF-3492].

If the contracting officer concludes that the contractor's figures in the progress payment request are not correct, the contracting officer must prepare a supplementary analysis to be attached to the contractor's request [@CL-CTF-3412]. In the same situation, the contracting officer must also advise the contractor in writing of the differences and adjust all further progress payments in accordance with paragraph (g)(1), using the contracting officer's figures, until the difference is resolved [@CL-CTF-3413].

The loss ratio adjustments must not be made by altering or replacing data on the contractor's original request, which is the SF 1443 or a computer generated equivalent [@CL-CTF-3369]. This keeps an audit trail and lets the calculations be verified [@CL-CTF-3369]. For contractor noncompliance, the section refers the reader to 242.7503 [@CL-CTF-3370], and the text of that cross-referenced section is not among this card's sources.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A loss contract's total costs incurred plus the estimated costs to complete are likely to exceed the contract price, so a loss ratio factor applies [@CL-CTF-3411]. The contracting officer determines that the contractor's methods of estimating the costs to complete are reliable, accurate, and not susceptible to improper influences, and asks the contractor to prepare the supplementary analysis as an attachment to its progress payment request [@CL-CTF-3368]. The contractor attaches the analysis and leaves its SF 1443 figures as submitted. The adjustment appears in the attachment and never as an overwrite of the original request [@CL-CTF-3369].

The attachment shows these synthetic figures. The contract price is 900,000 and a pending change order has a not-to-exceed amount of 100,000, so the revised contract price is 1,000,000 [@CL-CTF-3488]. Total costs incurred to date are 750,000 and the estimated additional costs to complete are 500,000, a sum of 1,250,000 against the contract price of 900,000 [@CL-CTF-3411; @CL-CTF-3489]. The loss ratio factor is 1,000,000 divided by 1,250,000, which is 80% [@CL-CTF-3489]. If the contracting officer believes a loss is probable and the paid costs eligible for progress payments are 750,000, the total costs eligible for progress payments are 750,000 times 80%, or 600,000 [@CL-CTF-3490; @CL-CTF-3491].

Had the contracting officer made no such determination, the contracting officer would have prepared the supplementary analysis [@CL-CTF-3367].

## Never confuse

The loss ratio adjustment applies where costs are likely to exceed the contract price [@CL-CTF-3411]. The loss ratio factor is not the progress payment rate: the FAR example applies the factor to the total costs eligible for progress payments first, and the progress payment rate of 80.0% afterwards [@CL-CTF-3493]. The other actions that the same FAR section describes follow their own conditions; see [Suspension or reduction of progress payments](ctf-suspension-or-reduction-of-progress-payments.md) and [Progress payment administration](ctf-progress-payment-administration.md).
