## Purpose

Shows the ways the government finances work before delivery, and how each differs from payment for delivered items.

## The rule

Contract financing pays the contractor before delivery and is recovered as deliveries are paid. Advance payments are made before performance and are not measured by it; progress payments based on costs follow costs incurred as work progresses [@S-0004-v1, § 32.102]. Performance-based payments are the preferred method when practical and agreed; they are financing, not payment for accepted items, and are not used for cost-reimbursement line items [@S-0004-v1, § 32.1001]. DoD's customary progress payment rates differ for large and small business [@CL-CTF-0001; @CL-CTF-0002].

## How it works

The contract names the financing method and its terms. Progress payments are requested from costs incurred and liquidated against deliveries; performance-based payments are requested when an agreed event or measure is met. Financing payments do not earn prompt payment interest, and a business system withhold can reduce them [@CL-CMP-0004]. See [Customary progress payment rates](ctf-progress-payments.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A fixed-price contract pays an event-based amount when a design review is passed. That is a performance-based payment: the item is not yet accepted, and the amount is recovered from later delivery invoices.

## Never confuse

Financing is not payment for accepted supplies; a partial delivery payment is. A progress payment follows costs; a performance-based payment follows events or measures.
