## Purpose

Shows the statutory requirements and authorization policy for providing advance payments on non-commercial government contracts.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CTF-0255] |
| Codified citation | FAR 32.402 [@CL-CTF-0255] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 32.402 [@CL-CTF-4197; @CL-CTF-4198; @CL-CTF-4200; @CL-CTF-4201; @CL-CTF-4202; @CL-CTF-4697; @CL-CTF-4205] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CTF-0255] |
| Applies to | Non-commercial contracts where advance payments are requested [@CL-CTF-0255] |

Advance payments may be provided on any type of contract, but agencies must authorize advance payments sparingly [@CL-CTF-0255]. Except for specified contract categories, advance payment is the least preferred method of contract financing and generally should not be authorized if other financing is reasonably available in adequate amounts [@CL-CTF-0256].

## How it works

To authorize advance payments, statutory requirements demand that the contractor provide adequate security and that total advance payments not exceed the unpaid contract price [@CL-CTF-0257; @CL-CTF-0280]. In addition, the agency head or designee must make written findings that advance payments are in the public interest or facilitate national defense [@CL-CTF-0257; @CL-CTF-0280]. When necessary, advance payments may be authorized alongside progress payments or partial payments on the same contract [@CL-CTF-0258].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 32 text in place of the codified text from February 1, 2026 [@CL-CTF-4046]. RFO FAR 32.402 keeps most of the rules described above: advance payments may be provided on any type of contract, but the agency must authorize them sparingly [@CL-CTF-4197]; the statutory requirements include adequate security and advance payments that do not exceed the unpaid contract price (see 32.410(b), paragraph (a)(2)) [@CL-CTF-4200], and written findings by the agency head or designee that the payment is in the public interest (under 32.401(a) or (b)) or facilitates the national defense (under 32.401(c)) [@CL-CTF-4201]; one standard is that advance payments not exceed the contractor’s interim cash needs, based on analysis of the cash flow required for contract performance [@CL-CTF-4202], consideration of the reimbursement or other payment cycle and, to the extent possible, employment of the contractor’s own working capital [@CL-CTF-4697]; and the agency may authorize advance payments in addition to progress or partial payments on the same contract [@CL-CTF-4205]. Two differences matter. First, RFO FAR 32.402(b) says that in most situations advance payment is the least preferred method of contract financing and generally should not be authorized if other types of financing are reasonably available to the contractor in adequate amounts [@CL-CTF-4198], where the codified text begins with an exception for the contracts described in 32.403(a) and (b) [@CL-CTF-0256]; the RFO text marks 32.403 as reserved [@CL-CTF-4199]. Second, the RFO standards for advance payment determinations include that the recipient is otherwise qualified as a responsible contractor and that the Government will benefit from performance prospects or there are other practical advantages [@CL-CTF-4203], while the codified list also has a further standard that the case fits one or more of the categories described in 32.403 [@CL-CTF-4204].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor wins a 5,000,000 defense contract requiring immediate specialized mobilization. Commercial lenders decline credit on reasonable terms. The agency determines that the statutory criteria are met, receives adequate security in the form of a corporate lien, and approves an initial advance payment of 500,000 to cover interim cash needs prior to performance milestone billing.

## Never confuse

Advance payments are limited to the contractor's interim cash needs, based on analysis of the cash flow required for contract performance [@CL-CTF-0525], and may be authorized in addition to progress or partial payments on the same contract [@CL-CTF-0258], so they are not simply another form of progress payment. See [Contract financing at a glance](ctf-financing-overview.md). The codified text excepts the contracts described in 32.403(a) and (b) from the least-preferred rule [@CL-CTF-0256], while RFO FAR 32.402(b) says that advance payment is the least preferred method in most situations [@CL-CTF-4198] and the RFO text marks 32.403 as reserved [@CL-CTF-4199].
