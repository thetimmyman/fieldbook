## Purpose

Shows what the Government expects in return for contract financing, and how an agency decides whether to reduce or suspend payments when a payment request is believed to be based on fraud.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CTF-1400; @CL-CTF-1402] |
| Codified citation | FAR 32.005 and FAR 32.006-4 [@CL-CTF-1400; @CL-CTF-1402] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CTF-1400] |
| Applies to | Contract financing clauses included at the inception of a contract, and requests for advance, partial, or progress payments that a remedy coordination official finds substantial evidence to be based on fraud [@CL-CTF-1400; @CL-CTF-1402] |

When a contract financing clause is included at the inception of a contract, there is no separate consideration for the clause [@CL-CTF-1400]. Adequate new consideration is required for changes to, or the addition of, contract financing after award [@CL-CTF-1401]. The contractor may provide new consideration by monetary or nonmonetary means, provided the value is adequate [@CL-CTF-1453]. Except as provided in the subpart on advance payments for other than commercial acquisitions, the contract shall not provide for any other type of specific charges, such as interest, for contract financing [@CL-CTF-1454].

The fraud procedure applies in any case in which an agency's remedy coordination official finds substantial evidence that a contractor's request for advance, partial, or progress payments under a contract awarded by that agency is based on fraud [@CL-CTF-1402]. In that case the official shall recommend that the agency head reduce or suspend further payments to the contractor [@CL-CTF-1450]. If the agency head determines that substantial evidence exists, the agency head may reduce or suspend further payments to the contractor under the affected contracts, and the reduction or suspension must be reasonably commensurate with the anticipated loss to the Government resulting from the fraud [@CL-CTF-1403].

## How it works

The remedy coordination official recommends, and the agency head decides whether to reduce or suspend [@CL-CTF-1450; @CL-CTF-1403]. Before making that decision, the agency head must take the listed steps in accordance with agency procedures [@CL-CTF-1451]. They include notifying the contractor in writing of the proposed action and the reasons [@CL-CTF-1404], and giving the contractor an opportunity to submit information within a reasonable time in response [@CL-CTF-1405]. Not later than 180 calendar days after the reduction or suspension action, the remedy coordination official reviews the agency head's determination [@CL-CTF-1406], and also transmits a recommendation on whether the reduction or suspension should continue [@CL-CTF-1452]. For the rules on suspension or reduction of progress payments, see [Suspension or reduction of progress payments](ctf-suspension-or-reduction-of-progress-payments.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An agency's remedy coordination official finds substantial evidence that a progress payment request of 200,000 is based on fraud and recommends a suspension. The agency head sends the contractor a written notice with the reasons and allows a reasonable time to respond. The agency head then suspends further payments in an amount reasonably commensurate with an anticipated loss of 50,000. Within 180 calendar days of the action the official reviews the determination.

Answer: the agency head decides, after written notice and an opportunity to respond, and the official's review follows within 180 calendar days.

## Never confuse

No separate consideration is due for a financing clause included at the start of a contract, but financing added or changed after award needs adequate new consideration [@CL-CTF-1400; @CL-CTF-1401]. The fraud procedure is a decision of the agency head on a recommendation from the remedy coordination official [@CL-CTF-1450; @CL-CTF-1403]. For the financing the contracting officer may include in the first place, see [Providing contract financing and customary contract financing](ctf-providing-customary-contract-financing.md) and [Contract financing at a glance](ctf-financing-overview.md).
