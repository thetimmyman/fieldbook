## Purpose

Sets out how DoD fixes payment due dates for contract financing, the conditions on performance-based payments, and the standard terms for commercial financing payments and the conditions on installment payment financing.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-CTF-3356] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 232.007, 232.1001, 232.206 and 232.906(a)(i) [@CL-CTF-3356; @CL-CTF-3359; @CL-CTF-3362; @CL-CTF-3407] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CTF-3356] |
| Applies to | Contract financing payments under DoD contracts: progress, performance-based, interim and commercial financing payments [@CL-CTF-3406; @CL-CTF-3361; @CL-CTF-3362] |

DoD policy is to make contract financing payments as quickly as possible [@CL-CTF-3356]. The contracting officer generally inserts the standard due dates of 7 days for progress payments, and 14 days for performance-based payments and interim payments on cost-type contracts, in the appropriate paragraphs of the respective payment clauses [@CL-CTF-3406]. For interim payments on cost-reimbursement contracts for services, the contracting officer generally inserts a standard due date of 14 days in the Prompt Payment clause when using that clause with its Alternate I [@CL-CTF-3407].

The contracting officer should coordinate the payment terms with the offices involved in the payment process, so that the specified terms can be met [@CL-CTF-3357]. Where justified, the contracting officer may insert a due date greater than, but not less than, the standard [@CL-CTF-3357]. In setting the terms the contracting officer must consider geographical separation, workload, the contractor's ability to submit a proper request, and other factors that could affect the timing of payment [@CL-CTF-3358].

## How it works

Performance-based payments. Their purpose is to assist the contractor in paying costs incurred during performance [@CL-CTF-3408]. They must not be conditioned upon costs incurred, but on the achievement of performance outcomes, in accordance with the statute that the DFARS section cites [@CL-CTF-3359]. All companies, including nontraditional defense contractors, are eligible, subject to the criteria in the DFARS section that this one cross-references, and consistent with best commercial practices [@CL-CTF-3360]. The contracting officer must use as the standard term the contractor entitlement date, if any, stated in the contract, or 14 days after the designated billing office receives a proper request for payment, whichever is later [@CL-CTF-3361].

Commercial financing. The contracting officer must incorporate the following standard prompt payment terms for commercial product and commercial service contract financing [@CL-CTF-3409]. For commercial advance payments the standard term is the contractor entitlement date, or 30 days after the designated billing office receives a proper request for payment, whichever is later [@CL-CTF-3362]. For commercial interim payments it is the entitlement date, or 14 days after that receipt, whichever is later [@CL-CTF-3363]. Commercial delivery payments take the prompt payment standards that FAR subpart 32.9 sets for invoice payments for the item delivered [@CL-CTF-3364].

Installment payment financing. For commercial products and commercial services, installment payment financing must not be used for DoD contracts unless market research has established that this form of contract financing is both appropriate and customary in the commercial marketplace [@CL-CTF-3365]. When it is used, the contracting officer must use the ceiling percentage of contract price that is customary in the particular marketplace, not above the maximum rate in FAR 52.232-30 [@CL-CTF-3366].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A performance-based payment request reaches the designated billing office on day 10 of a month, and the contract states no entitlement date. The payment is due 14 days after receipt, which is day 24 [@CL-CTF-3361]. If the contract instead stated an entitlement date of day 30, the payment would be due on day 30, because the later of the two dates applies [@CL-CTF-3361].

A commercial advance payment request is received on day 5, and the contract states an entitlement date of day 40. Thirty days after receipt is day 35, and the entitlement date is later, so the payment is due on day 40 [@CL-CTF-3362].

For a commercial product, the contracting officer would like to use installment payment financing, but market research has not shown that it is appropriate and customary in the commercial marketplace. It must not be used [@CL-CTF-3365].

## Never confuse

Contract financing due dates are not the due dates for invoice payments, except that commercial delivery payments follow the FAR subpart 32.9 standards for invoice payments [@CL-CTF-3364]. A performance-based payment is tied to performance outcomes, not to costs incurred [@CL-CTF-3359]. The standard due date for a progress payment is generally 7 days, not the 14 days that apply to performance-based payments [@CL-CTF-3406]. The full rules are on [Progress payments](ctf-progress-payments.md), [Performance-based payments](ctf-performance-based-payments.md), [Contract financing for commercial purchases](ctf-contract-financing-for-commercial-purchases.md) and [PBP invitation and commercial financing installments](../billing-payment/ctf-pbp-invitation-and-commercial-financing-installments.md).
