## Purpose

Gives the FAR definitions that mark out a contract financing payment, and the rules for when the paying office must pay a financing request.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CTF-1500] |
| Codified citation | FAR 32.001 and FAR 32.007 [@CL-CTF-1500; @CL-CTF-1505] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CTF-1500] |
| Applies to | Contract financing requests received by a designated billing office, subject to agency policy and the separate rule for advance payments and similar arrangements [@CL-CTF-1505; @CL-CTF-1508] |

A contract financing payment is an authorized Government disbursement of monies to a contractor prior to acceptance of supplies or services by the Government [@CL-CTF-1500]. The definition lists advance payments, performance-based payments, commercial advance and interim payments, and progress payments based on cost under the Progress Payments clause among the contract financing payments [@CL-CTF-1501]. The list also includes progress payments based on a percentage or stage of completion, except those made under the Fixed-Price Construction Contracts clause or the Fixed-Price Architect-Engineer Contracts clause [@CL-CTF-1555], and interim payments under a cost reimbursement contract, except for a cost reimbursement contract for services when Alternate I of the Prompt Payment clause is used [@CL-CTF-1556]. It says contract financing payments do not include invoice payments, payments for partial deliveries, or lease and rental payments [@CL-CTF-1502].

A commercial interim payment is any payment that is not a commercial advance payment or a delivery payment, and these payments are contract financing payments for prompt payment purposes [@CL-CTF-1557]. A delivery payment is a payment for accepted supplies or services, including accepted partial deliveries; commercial financing payments are liquidated by deduction from delivery payments, and delivery payments are invoice payments for prompt payment purposes [@CL-CTF-1550]. An invoice payment is a Government disbursement of monies to a contractor under a contract or other authorization for supplies or services accepted by the Government [@CL-CTF-1551]. Invoice payments include payments for accepted partial deliveries and final cost or fee payments where amounts owed have been settled [@CL-CTF-1552]. For purposes of subpart 32.9 only, they also include all payments made under the Fixed-Price Construction Contracts clause and the Fixed-Price Architect-Engineer Contracts clause [@CL-CTF-1553]. They include interim payments under a cost-reimbursement contract for services when Alternate I of the Prompt Payment clause is used, and invoice payments do not include contract financing payments [@CL-CTF-1554].

To liquidate is to decrease a payment for an accepted supply item or service under a contract so as to recoup financing payments previously paid, and unusual contract financing is any financing not deemed customary contract financing by the agency [@CL-CTF-1503]. The designated payment office is the office designated in the contract to make invoice payments or contract financing payments, normally the Government disbursing office, and the due date is the date on which payment should be made [@CL-CTF-1504]. The designated billing office is the office or person, governmental or nongovernmental, designated in the contract where the contractor first submits invoices and contract financing requests [@CL-CTF-1558]. The definitions of contract action and customary contract financing are not restated here, so read the section for them.

## How it works

The designated payment office is due to pay a proper contract financing request by the 30th day after the designated billing office receives it, unless agency policy or the separate rule for advance payments and similar arrangements says otherwise [@CL-CTF-1505; @CL-CTF-1508]. If an audit or other review of a specific financing request is required to ensure compliance with the contract terms, the designated payment office is not compelled to pay by that date [@CL-CTF-1506]. Agency heads may prescribe shorter periods for payment based on contract pricing or administrative considerations [@CL-CTF-1559]. The text gives as an example that a shorter period may be justified when financing arrangements are integrated with agency contract pricing policies, and agency heads must not prescribe a period shorter than 7 days or longer than 30 days [@CL-CTF-1507]. For advance payments, loans, or other arrangements without recurrent financing requests, the designated payment office pays under the applicable contract financing terms or as the contracting officer directs [@CL-CTF-1508]. A proper contract financing request must comply with the terms and conditions specified by the contract, and the contractor must correct any defects in requests submitted in the manner specified in the contract or as directed by the contracting officer [@CL-CTF-1560]. The designated billing office and designated payment office must each annotate the request with the date their office received it [@CL-CTF-1561]. The Government will not pay an interest penalty to the contractor for delayed contract financing payments [@CL-CTF-1509].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A billing office receives a proper financing request for 200,000 on a given day, and the agency has set no shorter period. The payment office is due to pay on the thirtieth day after receipt. If an audit of that request is required to ensure compliance with the contract terms, the payment office is not compelled to pay by that day, and no interest penalty is owed for the delay.

Answer: payment is due on day thirty, and a required audit can defer it without an interest penalty.

## Never confuse

A contract financing payment is made prior to acceptance [@CL-CTF-1500], so it is not an invoice payment or a payment for partial deliveries, which the definition leaves out [@CL-CTF-1502]. A delivery payment is the payment for accepted supplies or services, and commercial financing payments are liquidated by deduction from delivery payments [@CL-CTF-1550]. Invoice payment due dates are a separate matter, covered on [Prompt payment: determining invoice payment due dates](../billing-payment/ctf-prompt-payment-due-dates.md). Customary and unusual financing are compared on [Providing contract financing and customary contract financing](ctf-providing-customary-contract-financing.md), and the financing types are on [Contract financing at a glance](ctf-financing-overview.md). The FAR descriptions of each financing method and the payment clauses are on [Contract financing methods and payment clauses for noncommercial purchases](ctf-financing-methods-and-noncommercial-payment-clauses.md), the progress payments clause is on [Progress payments clauses and supervision of progress payments](ctf-progress-payment-clauses-and-supervision.md), and advance payments are on [Assignment of claims under guaranteed loans and when advance payments may be considered](ctf-loan-guarantee-assignment-and-advance-payment-applicability.md).
