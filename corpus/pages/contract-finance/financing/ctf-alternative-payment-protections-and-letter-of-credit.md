## Purpose

Shows how much payment protection a contractor submits, how a supplier's nonpayment claim reaches it, and what an irrevocable letter of credit must look like, who may issue it, how it stays in force and when it can be drawn.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CTF-2483] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.228-13 and FAR 52.228-14 [@CL-CTF-2483; @CL-CTF-2485] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CTF-2483] |
| Applies to | Contracts with the alternative payment protections clause or the irrevocable letter of credit clause [@CL-CTF-2483; @CL-CTF-2485] |

The Contractor shall submit one of the payment protections that the clause lists [@CL-CTF-2742]. The Contractor submits one of the payment protections listed in the clause, the amount is 100 percent of the contract price, and it is due within a number of days of contract award that is filled in for each contract [@CL-CTF-2483]. The printed clause text gives no list of the forms, so the forms are Not established here. The payment protection shall provide protection for the full contract performance period plus a one-year period [@CL-CTF-2520]. Except for escrow agreements and payment bonds, the Contracting Officer is authorized to access funds under the payment protection when a supplier of labor or material alleges in writing that a nonpayment has occurred [@CL-CTF-2484]. Except for escrow agreements and payment bonds, the Contracting Officer is also authorized to withhold such funds pending resolution by administrative or judicial proceedings or mutual agreement of the parties [@CL-CTF-2484; @CL-CTF-2743]. When a tripartite escrow agreement is used, the Contractor shall utilize only suppliers of labor and material that signed the escrow agreement [@CL-CTF-2571].

An irrevocable letter of credit is a written commitment by a federally insured financial institution to pay all or part of a stated amount of money, until the expiration date of the letter, upon presentation by the Government, the beneficiary, of a written demand for it [@CL-CTF-2744]. Neither the financial institution nor the offeror or Contractor can revoke or condition the letter of credit [@CL-CTF-2745]. If the offeror intends to use a letter of credit in lieu of a bid bond, or to secure other types of bonds such as performance and payment bonds, the letter of credit and letter of confirmation formats in paragraphs (e) and (f) of the clause shall be used [@CL-CTF-2746].

The letter of credit shall be irrevocable, shall require presentation of no document other than a written demand and the letter of credit, including any confirming letter, and shall be issued or confirmed by an acceptable federally insured financial institution as the clause provides [@CL-CTF-2485]. Only federally insured financial institutions rated investment grade by a commercial rating service issue or confirm the letter of credit [@CL-CTF-2487]. Unless the issuer had letter of credit business of at least $25 million in the past year, a letter of credit over $5 million must be confirmed by another acceptable institution that had letter of credit business of at least $25 million in the past year [@CL-CTF-2488]. The offeror or Contractor shall provide the Contracting Officer a credit rating that indicates the financial institutions have the required credit rating as of the date of issuance of the letter of credit [@CL-CTF-2749]. The current rating for a financial institution is available through any of the listed rating services registered with the United States Securities and Exchange Commission as a Nationally Recognized Statistical Rating Organization [@CL-CTF-2750].

If a letter of credit is used as a bid guarantee, it shall expire no earlier than 60 days after the close of the bid acceptance period [@CL-CTF-2572]. When a letter of credit is used as an alternative to corporate or individual sureties as security for a performance or payment bond, the offeror or Contractor may submit one with an initial expiration date estimated to cover the entire period for which financial security is required [@CL-CTF-2577]. The offeror or Contractor may instead submit a letter of credit with an initial expiration date that is a minimum period of one year from the date of issuance [@CL-CTF-2747]. For that use, the letter of credit shall provide that, unless the issuer gives the beneficiary written notice of non-renewal at least 60 days before the current expiration date, it is automatically extended without amendment for one year from the expiration date or any future expiration date [@CL-CTF-2486]. The automatic one-year extensions continue until the period of required coverage is completed and the Contracting Officer provides the financial institution with a written statement waiving the right to payment [@CL-CTF-2748].

For contracts subject to chapter 31, subchapter III, Bonds, of title 40 of the United States Code, the period of required coverage is the later of the listed dates, the first being one year following the expected date of final payment [@CL-CTF-2573]. The later-of list for those contracts also includes, for performance bonds only, until completion of any warranty period, and, for payment bonds only, until resolution of all claims filed against the payment bond during the one-year period following final payment [@CL-CTF-2574]. For contracts not subject to that chapter, the period of required coverage is the later of 90 days following final payment or, for performance bonds only, until completion of any warranty period [@CL-CTF-2575]. If the Contractor does not furnish an acceptable extension or replacement letter of credit, or other acceptable substitute, at least 30 days before a letter of credit's scheduled expiration, the Contracting Officer has the right to draw on it immediately [@CL-CTF-2478].

The issuing financial institution shall use the format set out in paragraph (e) to create a letter of credit [@CL-CTF-2751]. The format establishes an irrevocable and transferable letter of credit in favor of the addressee for one or more drawings up to a stated amount in United States dollars [@CL-CTF-2752]. In the format the issuer undertakes to honor the addressee's or the transferee's sight drafts drawn on the issuing or, if any, the confirming financial institution, for all or any part of the credit [@CL-CTF-2753], if presented with the letter of credit and any confirmation at the office specified in paragraph 1 of the letter on or before the expiration date or any automatically extended expiration date [@CL-CTF-2754]. The format paragraph deeming the letter of credit automatically extended without amendment for one year from the expiration date, or any future expiration date, is omitted if the letter is used as a bid guarantee, and subsequent paragraphs are renumbered [@CL-CTF-2755]. In the format the automatic extension does not apply if, at least 60 days before any expiration date, the issuer notifies the beneficiary or the transferee by registered mail, or other receipted means of delivery, that it elects not to consider the letter renewed for the additional period [@CL-CTF-2756]. At the time of that notice the issuer also agrees to notify the account party, and the confirming financial institution if any, by the same means of delivery [@CL-CTF-2757]. In the format, transfers and assignments of proceeds are to be effected without charge to either the beneficiary or the transferee or assignee of proceeds [@CL-CTF-2758], and a transfer or assignment shall be only at the written direction of the Government, the beneficiary, in a form satisfactory to the issuing financial institution and the confirming financial institution, if any [@CL-CTF-2759]. In the format the letter of credit is subject to the Uniform Customs and Practice for Documentary Credits, International Chamber of Commerce publication, in the version in effect at the time of issuance [@CL-CTF-2760]. In the format the letter of credit is also subject, to the extent not inconsistent with that publication, to the laws of the State of the confirming financial institution, if any, otherwise the State of the issuing financial institution [@CL-CTF-2761]. In the format, if the credit expires during an interruption of business of the issuing financial institution as described in Article 17 of the Uniform Customs and Practice, the institution agrees to effect payment if the credit is drawn against within 30 days after the resumption of its business [@CL-CTF-2762].

The financial institution shall use the format set out in paragraph (f) to confirm a letter of credit [@CL-CTF-2763]. In the confirmation format the confirming institution confirms the indicated letter of credit, the original of which is attached, issued by the issuing financial institution for drawings of up to a stated amount in United States dollars [@CL-CTF-2764]. In the confirmation format the confirming institution undertakes to honor sight drafts drawn under and presented with the letter of credit and the confirmation at its offices as specified [@CL-CTF-2765]. In the confirmation format, drafts drawn under the letter of credit and the confirmation are payable at the confirming institution's office [@CL-CTF-2787]. The confirmation format paragraph deeming the confirmation automatically extended without amendment for one year from the expiration date, or any automatically extended expiration date, is omitted if the letter is used as a bid guarantee, and subsequent paragraphs are renumbered [@CL-CTF-2766]. In the confirmation format the extension does not apply if, at least 60 days before any expiration date, the confirming institution notifies the Contracting Officer, or the transferee and the issuing financial institution, by registered mail or other receipted means of delivery, that it elects not to consider the confirmation extended for the additional period [@CL-CTF-2767]. In the confirmation format the extension also does not apply if the issuing financial institution has exercised its right to notify the beneficiary or the transferee, the account party, and the confirming institution, of its election not to extend the expiration date of the letter of credit [@CL-CTF-2768]. In the confirmation format the confirmation is subject to the Uniform Customs and Practice for Documentary Credits, International Chamber of Commerce publication, in the version in effect at the time of issuance [@CL-CTF-2769]. In the confirmation format the confirmation is also subject, to the extent not inconsistent with that publication, to the laws of the State of the confirming financial institution [@CL-CTF-2770]. In the confirmation format, if the confirmation expires during an interruption of business of the confirming institution as described in Article 17 of the Uniform Customs and Practice, the institution agrees to effect payment if the credit is drawn against within 30 days after the resumption of its business [@CL-CTF-2771].

The Contracting Officer uses the sight draft format in the clause to draw on the letter of credit, and that format orders payment to the order of the beneficiary agency of a stated sum in United States dollars [@CL-CTF-2576].

## How it works

The payment protection is sized at 100 percent of the contract price, is due after award by the contract-specific day count, and covers the full performance period plus a one-year period [@CL-CTF-2483; @CL-CTF-2520]. A supplier's written allegation of nonpayment is the trigger for the Contracting Officer to access the protection, unless the protection is an escrow agreement or payment bond [@CL-CTF-2484]. The Contracting Officer may also withhold those funds pending resolution by proceedings or agreement, subject to the same exception [@CL-CTF-2484; @CL-CTF-2743]. A letter of credit cannot be revoked or conditioned, and it is a written commitment by a federally insured institution to pay on the Government's written demand [@CL-CTF-2744; @CL-CTF-2745]. A letter of credit is drawn on a written demand, with no other document required, using the sight draft format [@CL-CTF-2485; @CL-CTF-2576]. The issuer creates it in the paragraph (e) format, the confirming institution confirms it in the paragraph (f) format, and the offeror or Contractor gives the Contracting Officer a credit rating as of the date of issuance [@CL-CTF-2746; @CL-CTF-2751; @CL-CTF-2763; @CL-CTF-2749]. Once issued as security for a performance or payment bond, the letter renews automatically each year unless the issuer gives timely notice of non-renewal, and the renewals continue until the required coverage period is completed and the Contracting Officer waives the right to payment in writing [@CL-CTF-2486; @CL-CTF-2748]. Its issuer must meet the rating and confirmation tests, its expiry follows the use it secures, and an extension or replacement that does not arrive in time lets the Contracting Officer draw [@CL-CTF-2487; @CL-CTF-2488; @CL-CTF-2572; @CL-CTF-2574; @CL-CTF-2575; @CL-CTF-2478].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contract is priced at 4,000,000 and the contract fills in 30 days. The payment protection is 4,000,000 and is due within 30 days of award, and it covers the full performance period of 24 months plus one year, so 36 months. A supplier alleges in writing that it was not paid. If the protection is a bank-backed form other than an escrow agreement or payment bond, the Contracting Officer may access the funds, and may withhold them until administrative or judicial proceedings or an agreement of the parties resolve the claim.

A letter of credit of 6,000,000 is issued by an institution that did about 20 million in letter of credit business in the past year. That is below 25 million, and 6,000,000 is over 5 million, so another acceptable institution with at least 25 million of such business must confirm it.

A bidder uses a letter of credit as its bid guarantee, and the bid acceptance period closes on day 60. The letter must expire no earlier than day 120.

A letter of credit secures a performance bond on a contract that is not subject to chapter 31, subchapter III, of title 40. Final payment is expected on day 400 and the warranty period ends on day 450. The required coverage runs to the later of day 490, which is 90 days after final payment, and day 450, so to day 490. The letter is due to expire, and no extension, replacement or substitute arrives 30 days before expiry, so the Contracting Officer may draw at once.

The issuing institution creates a performance bond letter of credit in the paragraph (e) format and keeps the automatic extension paragraph, which a bid guarantee letter leaves out. The letter has an initial expiration date one year after issuance, and the issuer gives no notice of non-renewal 60 days before it, so it extends for another year. It keeps extending until the period of required coverage is completed and the Contracting Officer gives the financial institution a written statement waiving the right to payment. The contractor gave the Contracting Officer a credit rating for the issuer as of the issue date, and the confirming institution, if one is needed, uses the paragraph (f) format.

## Never confuse

Payment protection backs payment of suppliers of labor and material after award. A letter of credit for advance payments is a different instrument; see [Advance payment letters of credit and findings](ctf-advance-payment-letters-of-credit-and-findings.md). A payment bond and an escrow agreement carry their own protection procedures, so the access rule for other forms does not apply to them [@CL-CTF-2484].
