## Purpose

Shows how much payment protection a contractor submits, how a supplier's nonpayment claim reaches it, and what an irrevocable letter of credit must look like, who may issue it, how it stays in force and when it can be drawn.

## The rule

The Contractor shall submit one of the payment protections that the clause lists [@CL-CTF-2742]. The amount of the payment protection shall be 100 percent of the contract price, and its submission is required within the number of days of contract award that the clause fills in [@CL-CTF-2483]. The payment protection shall provide protection for the full contract performance period plus a one-year period [@CL-CTF-2520]. Except for escrow agreements and payment bonds, which provide their own protection procedures, the Contracting Officer is authorized to access funds under the payment protection when it has been alleged in writing by a supplier of labor or material that a nonpayment has occurred [@CL-CTF-2484]. Except for those, the Contracting Officer is also authorized to withhold such funds pending resolution by administrative or judicial proceedings or mutual agreement of the parties [@CL-CTF-2484; @CL-CTF-2743]. When a tripartite escrow agreement is used, the Contractor shall utilize only suppliers of labor and material that signed the escrow agreement [@CL-CTF-2571].

An irrevocable letter of credit is a written commitment by a federally insured financial institution to pay all or part of a stated amount of money, until the expiration date of the letter, upon presentation by the Government, the beneficiary, of a written demand for it [@CL-CTF-2744]. Neither the financial institution nor the offeror or Contractor can revoke or condition the letter of credit [@CL-CTF-2745]. If the offeror intends to use a letter of credit in lieu of a bid bond, or to secure other types of bonds such as performance and payment bonds, the letter of credit and letter of confirmation formats in paragraphs (e) and (f) of the clause shall be used [@CL-CTF-2746].

The letter of credit shall be irrevocable, shall require presentation of no document other than a written demand and the letter of credit, including any confirming letter, and shall be issued or confirmed by an acceptable federally insured financial institution as provided in paragraph (d) of the clause [@CL-CTF-2485]. Only federally insured financial institutions rated investment grade by a commercial rating service shall issue or confirm the letter of credit [@CL-CTF-2487]. Unless the issuer had letter of credit business of at least $25 million in the past year, a letter of credit over $5 million must be confirmed by another acceptable institution that had letter of credit business of at least $25 million in the past year [@CL-CTF-2488]. The offeror or Contractor shall provide the Contracting Officer a credit rating that indicates the financial institutions have the required credit rating as of the date of issuance of the letter of credit [@CL-CTF-2749]. The current rating for a financial institution is available through any of the listed rating services registered with the United States Securities and Exchange Commission as a Nationally Recognized Statistical Rating Organization [@CL-CTF-2750].

If a letter of credit is used as a bid guarantee, it shall expire no earlier than 60 days after the close of the bid acceptance period [@CL-CTF-2572]. When a letter of credit is used as an alternative to corporate or individual sureties as security for a performance or payment bond, the offeror or Contractor may submit one with an initial expiration date estimated to cover the entire period for which financial security is required [@CL-CTF-2577]. The offeror or Contractor may instead submit a letter of credit with an initial expiration date that is a minimum period of one year from the date of issuance [@CL-CTF-2747]. For that use, the letter of credit shall provide that, unless the issuer gives the beneficiary written notice of non-renewal at least 60 days before the current expiration date, it is automatically extended without amendment for one year from the expiration date or any future expiration date [@CL-CTF-2486]. The automatic one-year extensions continue until the period of required coverage is completed and the Contracting Officer provides the financial institution with a written statement waiving the right to payment [@CL-CTF-2748].

For contracts subject to chapter 31, subchapter III, Bonds, of title 40 of the United States Code, the period of required coverage is the later of the listed dates, the first being one year following the expected date of final payment [@CL-CTF-2573]. The later-of list for those contracts also includes, for performance bonds only, until completion of any warranty period, and, for payment bonds only, until resolution of all claims filed against the payment bond during the one-year period following final payment [@CL-CTF-2574]. For contracts not subject to that chapter, the period of required coverage is the later of 90 days following final payment or, for performance bonds only, until completion of any warranty period [@CL-CTF-2575]. Under the Additional Bond Security clause, FAR 52.228-2, one of the alternative cases in which the Contractor shall promptly furnish additional security is where a letter of credit used as security will expire before the end of the period of required security [@CL-CTF-2476; @CL-CTF-2508]. If the Contractor does not furnish an acceptable extension or replacement letter of credit, or other acceptable substitute, at least 30 days before its scheduled expiration, the Contracting Officer has the right to immediately draw on it [@CL-CTF-2478].

The issuing financial institution shall use the format set out in paragraph (e) to create a letter of credit [@CL-CTF-2751]. The format establishes an irrevocable and transferable letter of credit in favor of the addressee for one or more drawings up to a stated amount in United States dollars [@CL-CTF-2752]. In the format the issuer undertakes to honor the addressee's or the transferee's sight drafts drawn on the issuing or, if any, the confirming financial institution, for all or any part of the credit [@CL-CTF-2753], if presented with the letter of credit and any confirmation at the office specified in paragraph 1 of the letter on or before the expiration date or any automatically extended expiration date [@CL-CTF-2754]. The format paragraph deeming the letter of credit automatically extended without amendment for one year from the expiration date, or any future expiration date, is omitted if the letter is used as a bid guarantee, and subsequent paragraphs are renumbered [@CL-CTF-2755]. In the format the automatic extension does not apply if, at least 60 days before any expiration date, the issuer notifies the beneficiary or the transferee by registered mail, or other receipted means of delivery, that it elects not to consider the letter renewed for the additional period [@CL-CTF-2756]. At the time of that notice the issuer also agrees to notify the account party, and the confirming financial institution if any, by the same means of delivery [@CL-CTF-2757]. In the format, transfers and assignments of proceeds are to be effected without charge to either the beneficiary or the transferee or assignee of proceeds [@CL-CTF-2758], and a transfer or assignment shall be only at the written direction of the Government, the beneficiary, in a form satisfactory to the issuing financial institution and the confirming financial institution, if any [@CL-CTF-2759]. In the format the letter of credit is subject to the Uniform Customs and Practice for Documentary Credits, International Chamber of Commerce publication, in the version in effect at the time of issuance [@CL-CTF-2760]. In the format the letter of credit is also subject, to the extent not inconsistent with that publication, to the laws of the State of the confirming financial institution, if any, otherwise the State of the issuing financial institution [@CL-CTF-2761]. In the format, if the credit expires during an interruption of business of the issuing financial institution as described in Article 17 of the Uniform Customs and Practice, the institution agrees to effect payment if the credit is drawn against within 30 days after the resumption of its business [@CL-CTF-2762].

The financial institution shall use the format set out in paragraph (f) to confirm a letter of credit [@CL-CTF-2763]. In the confirmation format the confirming institution confirms the indicated letter of credit, the original of which is attached, issued by the issuing financial institution for drawings of up to a stated amount in United States dollars, expiring with the confirming institution's close of business on the stated expiration date or any automatically extended expiration date [@CL-CTF-2764; @CL-CTF-9526]. In the confirmation format the confirming institution undertakes to honor sight drafts drawn under and presented with the letter of credit and the confirmation at its offices as specified [@CL-CTF-2765]. In the confirmation format, drafts drawn under the letter of credit and the confirmation are payable at the confirming institution's office [@CL-CTF-2787]. The confirmation format paragraph deeming the confirmation automatically extended without amendment for one year from the expiration date, or any automatically extended expiration date, unless one of the two cases described next applies, is omitted if the letter is used as a bid guarantee, and subsequent paragraphs are renumbered [@CL-CTF-2766]. In the confirmation format the extension does not apply if, at least 60 days before any expiration date, the confirming institution notifies the Contracting Officer, or the transferee and the issuing financial institution, by registered mail or other receipted means of delivery, that it elects not to consider the confirmation extended for the additional period [@CL-CTF-2767]. In the confirmation format the extension also does not apply if the issuing financial institution has exercised its right to notify the beneficiary or the transferee, the account party, and the confirming institution, of its election not to extend the expiration date of the letter of credit [@CL-CTF-2768]. In the confirmation format the confirmation is subject to the Uniform Customs and Practice for Documentary Credits, International Chamber of Commerce publication, in the version in effect at the time of issuance [@CL-CTF-2769]. In the confirmation format the confirmation is also subject, to the extent not inconsistent with that publication, to the laws of the State of the confirming financial institution [@CL-CTF-2770]. In the confirmation format, if the confirmation expires during an interruption of business of the confirming institution as described in Article 17 of the Uniform Customs and Practice, the institution agrees to effect payment if the credit is drawn against within 30 days after the resumption of its business [@CL-CTF-2771].

The sight draft format in paragraph (g) shall be used by the Contracting Officer to draw on the letter of credit, and that format orders payment to the order of the beneficiary agency of a stated sum in United States dollars [@CL-CTF-2576].

FAR 2.101 defines an irrevocable letter of credit as a written commitment by a federally insured financial institution to pay all or part of a stated amount of money, until the expiration date of the letter, upon the Government's (the beneficiary) presentation of a written demand for payment [@CL-CTF-9410]. Neither the financial institution nor the offeror/contractor can revoke or condition the letter of credit [@CL-CTF-9411]. RFO FAR 2.101 defines an irrevocable letter of credit in the same words as the codified first sentence, apart from the printed form of the apostrophe [@CL-CTF-9445; @CL-CTF-9410], and also says that neither the financial institution nor the offeror/contractor can revoke or condition the letter of credit [@CL-CTF-9482; @CL-CTF-9411].

## How it works

The amount of the payment protection shall be 100 percent of the contract price, its submission is required within the stated number of days of contract award, and it shall provide protection for the full contract performance period plus a one-year period [@CL-CTF-2483; @CL-CTF-2520]. Except for escrow agreements and payment bonds, the Contracting Officer is authorized to access funds under the payment protection when a supplier of labor or material alleges in writing that a nonpayment has occurred [@CL-CTF-2484], and to withhold such funds pending resolution by administrative or judicial proceedings or mutual agreement of the parties [@CL-CTF-2743]. Neither the financial institution nor the offeror or Contractor can revoke or condition a letter of credit, which is a written commitment by a federally insured financial institution to pay on the Government's written demand [@CL-CTF-2744; @CL-CTF-2745]. The letter of credit shall require presentation of no document other than a written demand and the letter of credit, including any confirming letter [@CL-CTF-2485], and the Contracting Officer shall use the sight draft format to draw on it [@CL-CTF-2576]. The issuing financial institution shall use the paragraph (e) format to create a letter of credit, the financial institution shall use the paragraph (f) format to confirm it, and the offeror or Contractor shall provide the Contracting Officer a credit rating as of the date of issuance [@CL-CTF-2751; @CL-CTF-2763; @CL-CTF-2749]. The letter of credit shall provide that, unless the issuer gives the beneficiary written notice of non-renewal at least 60 days before the current expiration date, it is automatically extended without amendment for one year, until the period of required coverage is completed and the Contracting Officer provides the financial institution with a written statement waiving the right to payment [@CL-CTF-2486; @CL-CTF-2748]. Only federally insured financial institutions rated investment grade shall issue or confirm it, and a letter of credit over $5 million must be confirmed by another acceptable institution unless the issuer had letter of credit business of at least $25 million in the past year [@CL-CTF-2487; @CL-CTF-2488]. If it is used as a bid guarantee, it shall expire no earlier than 60 days after the close of the bid acceptance period [@CL-CTF-2572].

The RFO text applies to DoD contracting because Class Deviation 2026-O0038 directs DoD contracting officers to use the revised FAR part 52 in place of the codified text, effective February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. RFO FAR 52.228-13 keeps the duty to submit one of the listed payment protections, the 100 percent amount and the days for submission in paragraphs (a) to (c) [@CL-CTF-0822; @CL-CTF-0823; @CL-CTF-2742; @CL-CTF-2483], and the protection for the full contract performance period plus a one-year period in paragraph (d) [@CL-CTF-0824; @CL-CTF-2520]. It keeps the rule that, when a tripartite escrow agreement is used, the Contractor shall utilize only suppliers of labor and material that signed the escrow agreement, in paragraph (f) [@CL-CTF-9188; @CL-CTF-2571]. It keeps, in paragraph (e), the Contracting Officer's authority, except for escrow agreements and payment bonds, to access funds when a supplier of labor or material alleges nonpayment in writing, and to withhold those funds pending resolution [@CL-CTF-0825; @CL-CTF-0826; @CL-CTF-2484; @CL-CTF-2743]. RFO FAR 52.228-14 keeps the definition of an irrevocable letter of credit and the bar on revoking or conditioning it in paragraph (a) [@CL-CTF-0827; @CL-CTF-0828; @CL-CTF-2744; @CL-CTF-2745], the use of the paragraph (e) and (f) formats in paragraph (b) [@CL-CTF-0829; @CL-CTF-2746], and the content and issuer requirements in paragraph (c) [@CL-CTF-0830; @CL-CTF-2485]. It keeps the bid guarantee expiry in paragraph (c)(1) [@CL-CTF-0831; @CL-CTF-2572], the two options for the initial expiration date when a letter of credit is an alternative to corporate or individual sureties, one estimated to cover the entire period for which financial security is required and one with a minimum of one year from issuance, the automatic one-year extension with its 60-day notice of non-renewal and the continuing extensions until the period of required coverage is completed and the Contracting Officer waives the right to payment in writing in paragraph (c)(2) [@CL-CTF-9189; @CL-CTF-9190; @CL-CTF-0832; @CL-CTF-0833; @CL-CTF-2577; @CL-CTF-2747; @CL-CTF-2486; @CL-CTF-2748], and the periods of required coverage for contracts subject to and not subject to chapter 31, subchapter III, of title 40 [@CL-CTF-0834; @CL-CTF-0835; @CL-CTF-0836; @CL-CTF-2573; @CL-CTF-2574; @CL-CTF-2575]. It keeps the investment-grade rating test and the confirmation rule for letters of credit over $5 million in paragraphs (d)(1) and (d)(2) [@CL-CTF-0837; @CL-CTF-0838; @CL-CTF-2487; @CL-CTF-2488]. It keeps the credit rating that the offeror or Contractor provides to the Contracting Officer as of the date of issuance and the rating services in paragraphs (d)(3) and (d)(4) [@CL-CTF-9191; @CL-CTF-9192; @CL-CTF-2749; @CL-CTF-2750]. In the paragraph (e) format it keeps the condition that the sight drafts are honored if presented at the office specified in paragraph 1 of the letter on or before the expiration date or any automatically extended expiration date, and the 60-day notice that ends the automatic extension [@CL-CTF-9193; @CL-CTF-9194; @CL-CTF-2754; @CL-CTF-2756]. It keeps the Contracting Officer's use of the paragraph (g) sight draft format [@CL-CTF-9195; @CL-CTF-2576]. RFO FAR 52.228-14(e) keeps the irrevocable and transferable letter of credit in favor of the addressee for one or more drawings up to a stated amount [@CL-CTF-9249; @CL-CTF-2752] and the issuer's undertaking to honor the addressee's or the transferee's sight drafts drawn on the issuing or, if any, the confirming financial institution, for all or any part of the credit [@CL-CTF-9250; @CL-CTF-2753]. It keeps the omission, for a bid guarantee, of the paragraph that deems the letter automatically extended without amendment for one year, with the paragraphs that follow renumbered [@CL-CTF-9251; @CL-CTF-2755], and the issuer's agreement, at the time of its notice of non-renewal, to notify the account party and the confirming financial institution, if any, by the same means of delivery [@CL-CTF-9252; @CL-CTF-2757]. It keeps the rules that transfers and assignments of proceeds are effected without charge to the beneficiary or the transferee or assignee [@CL-CTF-9253; @CL-CTF-2758] and only at the written direction of the Government, the beneficiary, in a form satisfactory to the issuing financial institution and the confirming financial institution, if any [@CL-CTF-9254; @CL-CTF-2759]. It keeps the subjection of the letter of credit to the Uniform Customs and Practice for Documentary Credits in the version in effect at the time of issuance [@CL-CTF-9255; @CL-CTF-2760] and, to the extent not inconsistent with it, to the laws of the State of the confirming financial institution, if any, otherwise the State of the issuing financial institution [@CL-CTF-9256; @CL-CTF-2761], and the issuer's agreement to pay if the credit is drawn within 30 days after the resumption of its business following an interruption of business as described in Article 17 of the Uniform Customs and Practice [@CL-CTF-9257; @CL-CTF-2762]. In the paragraph (f) confirmation format the RFO text keeps the confirmation of the indicated letter of credit, the original of which is attached, for drawings of up to a stated amount in United States dollars, expiring with the confirming institution's close of business on the stated date or any automatically extended expiration date [@CL-CTF-9258; @CL-CTF-9259; @CL-CTF-2764], the undertaking to honor sight drafts drawn under and presented with the letter of credit and the confirmation at its offices as specified [@CL-CTF-9260; @CL-CTF-2765], and the payment of drafts at the confirming institution's office [@CL-CTF-9261; @CL-CTF-2787]. It keeps the omission, for a bid guarantee, of the paragraph that deems the confirmation automatically extended without amendment for one year [@CL-CTF-9262; @CL-CTF-2766], the two cases in which that extension does not apply, the confirming institution's notice at least 60 days before an expiration date and the issuing financial institution's notice of its election not to extend [@CL-CTF-9263; @CL-CTF-9264; @CL-CTF-2767; @CL-CTF-2768], the subjection of the confirmation to the Uniform Customs and Practice and, to the extent not inconsistent with it, to the laws of the State of the confirming financial institution [@CL-CTF-9265; @CL-CTF-9266; @CL-CTF-2769; @CL-CTF-2770], and the payment within 30 days after the resumption of business [@CL-CTF-9267; @CL-CTF-2771]. These RFO format paragraphs read in the same words as the codified text; they differ in print style, with longer blank lines, straight instead of curly quotation marks in the version example, and a space before one bracketed note [@CL-CTF-9249; @CL-CTF-9255; @CL-CTF-9265; @CL-CTF-9266].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contract is priced at 4,000,000 and the contract fills in 30 days. The payment protection is 4,000,000 and is due within 30 days of award, and it covers the full performance period of 24 months plus one year, so 36 months. A supplier alleges in writing that it was not paid. If the protection is a bank-backed form other than an escrow agreement or payment bond, the Contracting Officer may access the funds, and may withhold them until administrative or judicial proceedings or an agreement of the parties resolve the claim.

A letter of credit of 6,000,000 is issued by an institution that did about 20 million in letter of credit business in the past year. That is below 25 million, and 6,000,000 is over 5 million, so another acceptable institution with at least 25 million of such business must confirm it.

A bidder uses a letter of credit as its bid guarantee, and the bid acceptance period closes on day 60. The letter must expire no earlier than day 120.

A letter of credit secures a performance bond on a contract that is not subject to chapter 31, subchapter III, of title 40. Final payment is expected on day 400 and the warranty period ends on day 450. The required coverage runs to the later of day 490, which is 90 days after final payment, and day 450, so to day 490. The letter is due to expire, and no extension, replacement or substitute arrives 30 days before expiry, so the Contracting Officer may draw at once.

The issuing institution creates a performance bond letter of credit in the paragraph (e) format and keeps the automatic extension paragraph, which a bid guarantee letter leaves out. The letter has an initial expiration date one year after issuance, and the issuer gives no notice of non-renewal 60 days before it, so it extends for another year. It keeps extending until the period of required coverage is completed and the Contracting Officer gives the financial institution a written statement waiving the right to payment. The contractor gave the Contracting Officer a credit rating for the issuer as of the issue date, and the confirming institution, if one is needed, uses the paragraph (f) format.

## Never confuse

The payment protection is submitted within the stated number of days of contract award [@CL-CTF-2483], and the Contracting Officer is authorized to access its funds when a supplier of labor or material alleges nonpayment in writing, except for escrow agreements and payment bonds, which provide their own protection procedures [@CL-CTF-2484]. For letters of credit for advance payments, see [Advance payment letters of credit and findings](ctf-advance-payment-letters-of-credit-and-findings.md).
