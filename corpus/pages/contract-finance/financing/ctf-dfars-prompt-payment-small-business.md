## Purpose

States the DoD duty to assist small business concerns by paying as quickly as possible, and the goal of payment 15 days after receipt of proper invoices and all required documentation [@CL-CTF-3803; @CL-CTF-3804].

| Codified citation | DFARS 232.903 [@CL-CTF-3803] |

In accordance with section 3801(b) of title 10, United States Code, DoD shall assist small business concerns by providing payment as quickly as possible, to the fullest extent permitted by law [@CL-CTF-3803].

The section sets a goal of payment 15 days after receipt of proper invoices and all required documentation, including acceptance, and before the normal payment due dates established in the contract [@CL-CTF-3804].

## How it works

The duty falls on DoD and runs to small business concerns. The section names no particular office, and the duty is bounded by what the law permits [@CL-CTF-3803].

The 15 days count from receipt of proper invoices together with all required documentation [@CL-CTF-3804]. Acceptance is part of that documentation, so the count does not start until all of it has been received.

The measure is a goal, not a deadline. It sits ahead of the normal payment due dates that the contract establishes, and the section does not state that it replaces them [@CL-CTF-3804]. The FAR due-date rules are on [Prompt payment: determining invoice payment due dates](../billing-payment/ctf-prompt-payment-due-dates.md).

The section points to DFARS 232.906(a) for the normal payment due dates, and paragraph (a)(ii) of that section says the restrictions prohibiting early payment do not apply to invoice payments made to small business concerns, but contractors are not entitled to interest penalties if the Government fails to make early payment [@CL-CTF-0915]. The section does not define a small business concern and states no consequence for missing the goal.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A small business concern submits a proper invoice on day 1, and the acceptance record and all other required documentation are already in hand. The contract due date is day 30. The goal is payment 15 days after receipt, which is day 16 [@CL-CTF-3804]. DoD shall assist the concern by paying as quickly as possible, to the fullest extent permitted by law [@CL-CTF-3803].

DoD pays on day 25. That misses the goal but is before the contract due date, and the concern is not entitled to an interest penalty because DoD did not pay earlier [@CL-CTF-0915].

If acceptance has not occurred on day 1, the 15 days do not start until the acceptance and the other required documentation have been received [@CL-CTF-3804].

## Never confuse

The goal is not the payment due date. The due date is the one established in the contract [@CL-CTF-3804], while the goal is a DoD effort to pay small business concerns sooner [@CL-CTF-3803].

The 15-day goal is also not a rule that DoD must pay every contractor on that schedule, because the section addresses small business concerns [@CL-CTF-3803; @CL-CTF-3804]. For the agency-wide prompt payment duties, see [Contract financing authority and prompt payment responsibilities](ctf-financing-authority-and-prompt-payment-duties.md).

## Company calibration

Whether the company is a small business concern for each contract, and when proper invoices with all required documentation, including acceptance, reach the payment office. [@Q-009]
