## Purpose

Sets the customary progress payment rates in the FAR and the conditions for rates above them.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CTF-0950] |
| Codified citation | FAR 32.501-1 [@CL-CTF-0950] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CTF-0950] |
| Applies to | Total costs of performing the contract [@CL-CTF-0950] |

The customary progress payment rate is 80 percent, applicable to the total costs of performing the contract [@CL-CTF-0950], and the customary rate for contracts with small business concerns is 85 percent [@CL-CTF-0951]. The limit for progress payments is 80 percent on work accomplished under undefinitized contract actions [@CL-CTF-0954], and the contracting officer must not authorize a higher rate under unusual progress payments or other customary progress payments for those actions [@CL-CTF-1055].

The DFARS sets the customary rates for DoD contracts, including contracts that contain foreign military sales requirements, at 80 percent for large business concerns and 90 percent for small business concerns [@CL-CTF-0952]; see also [Progress payments](ctf-progress-payments.md).

## How it works

The contracting officer must consider any rate higher than the customary rates an unusual progress payment, and must not include a higher rate in a contract unless advance agency approval is obtained as prescribed in the section on unusual progress payments [@CL-CTF-1052]. The contracting officer may provide unusual progress payments only if the contract necessitates predelivery expenditures that are large in relation to contract price and in relation to the contractor's working capital and credit [@CL-CTF-0953], the contractor fully documents an actual need to supplement any private financing available, including guaranteed loans [@CL-CTF-1050], and the request is approved by the head of the contracting activity or a designee [@CL-CTF-1051].

The excess of the unusual rate approved over the customary rate should be the lowest amount possible under the circumstances [@CL-CTF-1053]. When advance payments and progress payments are authorized under the same contract, the contracting officer must not authorize a progress payment rate higher than the customary rate [@CL-CTF-1054].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A civilian-agency contract with a large business has total costs of performing the contract of 1,000,000 recognised for progress payments. At the customary 80 percent rate, the rate yields 800,000. On a DoD contract with a small business concern, the same costs yield 900,000 at the 90 percent rate.

## Never confuse

A customary rate is not an unusual progress payment: a higher rate is unusual and needs advance agency approval [@CL-CTF-1052]. Progress payments will not be considered unusual merely because they are on letter contracts or the definitive contracts that supersede letter contracts [@CL-CTF-1056]. These payment rates are not liquidation rates; see [Progress payment liquidation rates](ctf-progress-payment-liquidation-rates.md) and [Progress payment liquidation](ctf-progress-payment-liquidation.md). For suspension or reduction of payments, see [Suspension or reduction of progress payments](ctf-suspension-or-reduction-of-progress-payments.md).
