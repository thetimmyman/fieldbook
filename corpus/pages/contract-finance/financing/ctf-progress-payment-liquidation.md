## Purpose

Shows how progress payments are recovered from later contract payments, so that an open financing balance does not run to the end of the contract.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CTF-0101] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.232-16 [@CL-CTF-0101] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.232-16 [@CL-CTF-1112] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CTF-0101] |
| Applies to | Contracts in which the progress payments clause is inserted as prescribed in FAR 32.502-4(a) [@CL-CTF-0101] |

Under the basic clause, unless the contractor asks for a smaller amount, the Government computes each progress payment as 80 percent of the total costs incurred, whether or not actually paid, plus financing payments to subcontractors, less the sum of all previous progress payments [@CL-CTF-0102; @CL-CTF-0271]. Costs that are not reasonable, allocable to the contract and consistent with sound and generally accepted accounting principles are left out of total costs [@CL-CTF-0482]. Under Alternate I, the customary rate for a small business concern is 85 percent [@CL-CTF-0481].

Under the basic clause, total progress payments may not exceed 80 percent of the contract price [@CL-CTF-0103]. Except as provided in the Termination for Convenience of the Government clause, progress payments are recovered by deducting from payments other than advance or progress payments the unliquidated progress payments, or 80 percent of the amount invoiced if that is less [@CL-CTF-0104; @CL-CTF-0491].

## How it works

The unliquidated balance is the total of progress payments not yet recovered. Except as provided in the Termination for Convenience of the Government clause, each later payment under the contract other than an advance or progress payment absorbs the lesser of that balance or 80 percent of the amount invoiced [@CL-CTF-0104; @CL-CTF-0491].

The contractor agrees not to request a progress payment below $2,500, although the contracting officer may make exceptions [@CL-CTF-0107].

The contracting officer may reduce or suspend progress payments, increase the liquidation rate, or do both, after finding on substantial evidence one of the listed conditions [@CL-CTF-0106; @CL-CTF-0272; @CL-CTF-0273; @CL-CTF-0274; @CL-CTF-0275].

The Government may also move from the ordinary liquidation rate to an alternate rate unilaterally when it considers that appropriate for proper contract financing [@CL-CTF-0105].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text from February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. RFO FAR 52.232-16 keeps the basic computation: unless the Contractor requests a smaller amount, each progress payment is 80 percent of the total costs incurred, whether or not actually paid, plus financing payments to subcontractors, less the sum of all previous progress payments [@CL-CTF-0102; @CL-CTF-0271] [@CL-CTF-1112; @CL-CTF-1113]. It keeps the exclusion from total costs of costs that are not reasonable, allocable to the contract and consistent with sound and generally accepted accounting principles and practices [@CL-CTF-0482] [@CL-CTF-1114], and the limit that total progress payments may not exceed 80 percent of the contract price [@CL-CTF-0103] [@CL-CTF-1115]. Its Alternate I keeps the customary rate of 85 percent for a small business concern [@CL-CTF-0481] [@CL-CTF-1125]. It keeps liquidation by deducting, from payments other than advance or progress payments, the unliquidated progress payments or 80 percent of the amount invoiced if that is less, except as provided in the Termination for Convenience of the Government clause [@CL-CTF-0104; @CL-CTF-0491] [@CL-CTF-1117; @CL-CTF-1118], and the Government's right to change unilaterally to an alternate liquidation rate when it deems that appropriate for proper contract financing [@CL-CTF-0105] [@CL-CTF-1119]. It keeps the rule that the Contractor does not request a progress payment below $2,500, with exceptions allowed by the Contracting Officer [@CL-CTF-0107] [@CL-CTF-1116]. It keeps the Contracting Officer's power to reduce or suspend progress payments, increase the liquidation rate, or do both, after finding on substantial evidence one of the listed conditions [@CL-CTF-0106] [@CL-CTF-1120], and keeps those conditions in paragraphs (c)(1) through (c)(6) [@CL-CTF-0272; @CL-CTF-0273; @CL-CTF-0274; @CL-CTF-0275] [@CL-CTF-1121; @CL-CTF-1122; @CL-CTF-1123; @CL-CTF-1124].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

Costs incurred reach 400,000 and no progress payment has yet been made, so the contractor requests 320,000. A later invoice for delivered work of 100,000 is paid after deducting 80,000, because the unliquidated balance of 320,000 is larger than 80 percent of the invoice, leaving 20,000 to be paid.

## Never confuse

Progress payments are computed from costs incurred, while performance-based payments are built on events or a measurable criterion of performance [@CL-CTF-0102; @CL-CTF-0109].

The recovery mechanics also differ from a plain delivery payment: see [Progress payments](ctf-progress-payments.md).

## Company calibration

Confirm that each progress payment request is supported and that liquidation is posted against the contract financing balance. [@Q-039]
