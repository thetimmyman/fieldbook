## Purpose

Shows the methods the FAR requires for making advance payments, including the letter of credit criteria, and what written findings must support each advance payment determination.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CTF-1427; @CL-CTF-1429] |
| Codified citation | FAR 32.406 and FAR 32.410 [@CL-CTF-1427; @CL-CTF-1429] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CTF-1427] |
| Applies to | Advance payments that agencies provide to contractors [@CL-CTF-1427] |

If agencies provide advance payments to contractors, use of the methods listed in the section is required unless the agency has obtained a waiver from the Treasury Department [@CL-CTF-1427]. One listed method is a letter of credit if the contracting agency expects to have a continuing relationship with the contractor for a year or more, with advances totaling at least $120,000 a year [@CL-CTF-1428]. Another listed method is a direct Treasury check if the circumstances do not meet the criteria for the letter of credit method [@CL-CTF-1463].

Each determination concerning advance payments shall be supported by written findings [@CL-CTF-1429].

## How it works

The section requires written findings to support each determination [@CL-CTF-1429]. In the example format, the findings include that advance payments are the only adequate means of financing available to the contractor and that the designated amount is based, to the extent possible, on the use of the contractor's own working capital [@CL-CTF-1430]. The example says that finding should not be included if the contract is for experimental, developmental, or research work with a nonprofit educational or research institution, or only for management and operation of a Government-owned plant [@CL-CTF-1431]. The example format includes a signature block for a signature, a typed name and the title of an authorized official [@CL-CTF-1432]. For the security, covenants and special account, see [Advance payments: security, supervision, covenants and the special account](ctf-advance-payment-security-and-special-account.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An agency plans advance payments of 300,000 a year to a contractor and expects a continuing relationship of three years. The agency holds no Treasury waiver.

Answer: the letter of credit method applies, because the relationship is expected to last a year or more and advances total at least the listed yearly amount. The file holds written findings, and the example format provides for an authorized official to sign the findings, determination and authorization.

## Never confuse

The letter of credit and Treasury check methods govern how an advance payment is made [@CL-CTF-1427; @CL-CTF-1428; @CL-CTF-1463], while the written findings, determination and authorization support the decision to make it [@CL-CTF-1429]. The example findings are an example format, and the finding on the only adequate means of financing is not used in every case [@CL-CTF-1430; @CL-CTF-1431]. See also [Advance payments for non-commercial items](ctf-advance-payments-for-non-commercial-items.md) and [Providing contract financing and customary contract financing](ctf-providing-customary-contract-financing.md).
