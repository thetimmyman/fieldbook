## Purpose

Explains how a defense production loan guarantee is applied for, how the asset formula sizes the guarantee, and what changes its amount or maturity.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CTF-3073] |
| Codified citation | FAR 32.304-1, FAR 32.304-3 and FAR 32.304-4 [@CL-CTF-3073; @CL-CTF-3075; @CL-CTF-3174] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 32.303(f) [@CL-CTF-4528; @CL-CTF-4542]; RFO FAR 32.304 is marked reserved [@CL-CTF-4527] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CTF-3073] |
| Applies to | Guaranteed loans for contractors, subcontractors and suppliers performing contracts related to national defense [@CL-CTF-3073] |

A contractor, subcontractor or supplier that needs operating funds to perform a contract related to national defense may apply to a financing institution for a loan [@CL-CTF-3073]. If the institution is willing to extend credit but considers a Government guarantee necessary, it may apply to the Federal Reserve Bank of its district for the guarantee, and application forms and guidance are available at all Federal Reserve Banks [@CL-CTF-3167]. The Reserve Bank promptly sends a copy of the application, including a list of the relevant defense contracts held by the contractor, to the Federal Reserve Board [@CL-CTF-3168]. The Board transmits the application and the list to the interested guaranteeing agency, so that the agency can determine the contractor's eligibility [@CL-CTF-3074].

To expedite the process, the Reserve Bank may, pursuant to instructions of a guaranteeing agency, submit lists of the defense contracts to the interested contracting officers [@CL-CTF-3169]. While eligibility is being determined, the Reserve Bank makes any necessary credit investigations to supplement the information furnished by the applicant institution, in order to expedite necessary defense financing and to protect the Government against monetary loss [@CL-CTF-3170]. It sends its report and recommendation to the Board, which transmits them to the interested guaranteeing agency [@CL-CTF-3171].

Under guaranteed loans made primarily for working capital, the agency normally limits the guarantee, by an asset formula, to a specified percentage of the contractor's investment in defense production contracts, with payrolls and inventories given as examples, and the percentage is 90 percent or less [@CL-CTF-3075]. The formula may include all items under defense contracts for which the contractor would be entitled to payment on performance or termination [@CL-CTF-3172]. It excludes amounts for which the contractor has not done any work or made any expenditure, amounts that would become due as the result of later performance, and cash collateral or bank deposit balances [@CL-CTF-3076]. Progress payments are deducted from the asset formula [@CL-CTF-3077]. The agency may relax the formula to an appropriate extent for the time actually necessary for contract performance, if the contractor's working capital and credit are inadequate [@CL-CTF-3173].

The agency may change the guarantee amount or maturity date, within the limitations of the asset formula section, in two cases [@CL-CTF-3174]. If the contractor enters into additional defense production contracts after the application but before authorization of the guarantee, the agency may adjust the guarantee amount or maturity date to meet any significant increase in financing need [@CL-CTF-3078]. If the contractor enters into defense production contracts during the term of the guaranteed loan, the parties may adjust the existing guarantee agreement to provide for financing the new contracts [@CL-CTF-3079]. In that case pertinent information and the Federal Reserve Bank reports are submitted to the guaranteeing agency under the original application procedures, and normally a new certificate of eligibility is required [@CL-CTF-3175].

## How it works

The borrower applies to a financing institution, which may apply to its district Reserve Bank for a guarantee [@CL-CTF-3073; @CL-CTF-3167]. The application and the contract list pass through the Reserve Bank and the Board to the guaranteeing agency, which determines eligibility [@CL-CTF-3168; @CL-CTF-3074]. The agency then normally limits the guarantee with the asset formula, and later defense production contracts can change the amount or maturity [@CL-CTF-3075; @CL-CTF-3174].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 32 text in place of the codified text from February 1, 2026 [@CL-CTF-4046]. RFO FAR 32.304, the number under which the codified text prints the application route, the asset formula and the amount and maturity adjustments, is marked reserved [@CL-CTF-3073; @CL-CTF-3075; @CL-CTF-3174; @CL-CTF-4527]. RFO FAR 32.303(f) keeps the rule that the guaranteeing agency is responsible for certifying eligibility for the guarantee and fixing the maximum dollar amount and maturity date of the guaranteed loan to meet the contractor's requirement for financing performance of the defense production contract on hand at the time the guarantee application is submitted, as codified FAR 32.303(g) does [@CL-CTF-4528; @CL-CTF-4542; @CL-CTF-1424; @CL-CTF-1461].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor has 2,000,000 of items under defense contracts that the asset formula can include, after leaving out amounts for work not yet done, amounts that would become due as the result of later performance, and cash collateral or bank deposit balances [@CL-CTF-3172; @CL-CTF-3076]. The agency applies a 90 percent formula, so the formula amount before any progress payment deduction is 1,800,000 [@CL-CTF-3075]. Progress payments the contractor has received are also deducted from the asset formula [@CL-CTF-3077], so, unless the agency relaxes the formula because the contractor's working capital and credit are inadequate [@CL-CTF-3173], the limit is lower than 1,800,000, and the section does not give the order of the arithmetic. Before authorization, the contractor wins a further defense production contract, and the agency may adjust the amount or maturity date to meet a significant increase in financing need [@CL-CTF-3078]. If the contractor wins a contract during the loan term, the parties may adjust the existing agreement, and normally a new certificate of eligibility is required [@CL-CTF-3079; @CL-CTF-3175]. This example uses the codified text only, and RFO FAR 32.304 is marked reserved [@CL-CTF-4527].

## Never confuse

A guaranteed loan is not a progress payment. The loan comes from a financing institution, with a Government guarantee requested through the Federal Reserve Bank and sized by the asset formula, while progress payments are deducted from that formula [@CL-CTF-3167; @CL-CTF-3075; @CL-CTF-3077]. The [defense production loan guarantees](ctf-defense-production-loan-guarantees.md) card covers the rest of the regime, and the [surety, other borrowing and terminated contracts](ctf-defense-production-loan-guarantee-surety-and-termination.md) card covers the conditions that follow.

The codified text normally limits a guarantee with an asset formula of 90 percent or less and lets the agency change the guarantee amount or maturity date, within the limitations of the asset formula section, in the cases that follow it [@CL-CTF-3075; @CL-CTF-3174], but RFO FAR 32.304, the number of those codified sections, is marked reserved [@CL-CTF-4527].
