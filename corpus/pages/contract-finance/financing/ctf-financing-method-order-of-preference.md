## Purpose

Shows the order in which a contracting officer considers financing methods when a contractor requests financing, why a contractor's need for financing must not count against it in an award, and when the contracting officer consults the contract financing office.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CTF-3522] |
| Codified citation | FAR 32.106, FAR 32.107 and FAR 32.108 [@CL-CTF-3522; @CL-CTF-3525; @CL-CTF-3527] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 32.106 [@CL-CTF-4337; @CL-CTF-4338; @CL-CTF-4339], RFO FAR 32.107 [@CL-CTF-4340; @CL-CTF-4341] and RFO FAR 32.108 [@CL-CTF-4342; @CL-CTF-4343] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CTF-3522] |
| Applies to | A contractor's request for contract financing, and an offeror's or contractor's need for contract financing in an award decision [@CL-CTF-3522; @CL-CTF-3525] |

When a contractor requests contract financing, the contracting officer must consider an order of preference, unless an exception would be in the Government's best interest in a specific case [@CL-CTF-3522]. The first preference is private financing without Government guarantee [@CL-CTF-3523]. After it, the order is customary contract financing other than loan guarantees and certain advance payments, then loan guarantees, then unusual contract financing, then advance payments [@CL-CTF-3524].

It is not intended that the contracting officer require the contractor to obtain private financing at unreasonable terms or from other agencies [@CL-CTF-3523].

Advance payments come last. Except for the contracts described in paragraphs (a) and (b) of the advance payments applicability section, advance payment is the least preferred method of contract financing and generally should not be authorized if other types of financing are reasonably available to the contractor in adequate amounts [@CL-CTF-0256]. Unusual contract financing, which is any arrangement that deviates from part 32, shall be authorized only after approval by the head of the agency or as provided for in agency regulations [@CL-CTF-1410].

If the contractor or offeror meets the standards prescribed for responsible prospective contractors, the contracting officer shall not treat the contractor's need for contract financing as a handicap for a contract award, for example as a responsibility factor or evaluation criterion [@CL-CTF-3525]. The contractor should not be disqualified from contract financing solely because it failed to indicate a need for contract financing before the contract was awarded [@CL-CTF-3526].

Each contracting office should have available and use the services of contract financing personnel competent to evaluate credit and financial problems [@CL-CTF-3527]. In resolving questions about the financial capability of an offeror or contractor to perform a contract, or about what form of contract financing is appropriate in a given case, the contracting officer should consult the appropriate contract financing office [@CL-CTF-3528].

## How it works

The contracting officer must consider the order of preference when a contractor requests contract financing, unless an exception would be in the Government's best interest in a specific case [@CL-CTF-3522].

Private financing without Government guarantee leads the list, but it is not intended that the contracting officer require the contractor to obtain private financing at unreasonable terms or from other agencies [@CL-CTF-3523]. Advance payments close the list and, outside the contracts described in paragraphs (a) and (b) of the advance payments applicability section, generally should not be authorized if other financing is reasonably available to the contractor in adequate amounts [@CL-CTF-3524; @CL-CTF-0256].

If the contractor or offeror meets the standards prescribed for responsible prospective contractors, the contracting officer shall not treat its need for contract financing as a handicap for a contract award [@CL-CTF-3525]. On questions about financial capability or the right form of financing, the contracting officer should consult the appropriate contract financing office [@CL-CTF-3528].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 32 text in place of the codified text from February 1, 2026 [@CL-CTF-4046]. RFO section 32.106 keeps the order of preference: the contracting officer must consider it when a contractor requests contract financing, unless an exception would be in the Government's best interest in a specific case, and private financing without Government guarantee comes first [@CL-CTF-4337]. It is not intended that the contracting officer require the contractor to obtain private financing at unreasonable terms or from other agencies [@CL-CTF-4338]. The rest of the sequence is customary contract financing other than loan guarantees and certain advance payments, then loan guarantees, then unusual contract financing, then advance payments [@CL-CTF-4339]. RFO section 32.107(a) keeps the rule that, if the contractor or offeror meets the standards prescribed for responsible prospective contractors at 9.104, the contracting officer must not treat its need for contract financing as a handicap for a contract award, for example as a responsibility factor or evaluation criterion [@CL-CTF-4340]. Paragraph (b) keeps the statement that the contractor should not be disqualified from contract financing solely because it failed to indicate a need for contract financing before the contract was awarded [@CL-CTF-4341]. RFO section 32.108 keeps the statement that each contracting office should have available and use the services of contract financing personnel competent to evaluate credit and financial problems [@CL-CTF-4342], and that the contracting officer should consult the appropriate contract financing office on questions about the financial capability of an offeror or contractor to perform a contract or about what form of contract financing is appropriate in a given case [@CL-CTF-4343].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor requests contract financing on a fixed-price order, and the only private loan offered would carry unreasonable terms. The contracting officer does not require the contractor to take that loan [@CL-CTF-3523]. The contracting officer considers customary contract financing before loan guarantees, unusual contract financing and advance payments, unless an exception would be in the Government's best interest in this case [@CL-CTF-3522; @CL-CTF-3524]. At award the contractor met the responsibility standards, so its need for financing is not treated as a responsibility factor or an evaluation criterion [@CL-CTF-3525]. The contracting officer is unsure what form of financing is appropriate and consults the contract financing office [@CL-CTF-3528].

## Never confuse

The contracting officer must consider the sequence unless an exception would be in the Government's best interest in a specific case [@CL-CTF-3522].

If the offeror or contractor meets the standards prescribed for responsible prospective contractors, the contracting officer shall not treat its need for contract financing as a handicap for a contract award, for example as a responsibility factor or evaluation criterion [@CL-CTF-3525]. Which subparts apply to a purchase is a separate question, covered in [Scope of the contract financing part](ctf-contract-financing-scope-and-foreign-performance.md).
