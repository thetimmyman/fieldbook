## Purpose

States when the Government pays by electronic funds transfer, the exceptions the FAR lists, and how a purchase card payment works.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CTF-1532] |
| Codified citation | FAR 32.1101, FAR 32.1103 and FAR 32.1108 [@CL-CTF-3747; @CL-CTF-1532; @CL-CTF-1539]; FAR 32.1100 [@CL-CTF-9306]; FAR 2.101 [@CL-CTF-9462] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 32.1100 [@CL-CTF-3044], RFO FAR 32.1101 [@CL-CTF-3045], RFO FAR 32.1103 and RFO FAR 32.1108 [@CL-CTF-4294; @CL-CTF-4301]; RFO FAR 2.101 [@CL-CTF-9461; @CL-CTF-9463] |

| Applies to | All contract payments, subject to the listed exceptions, and payments made by Governmentwide commercial purchase card [@CL-CTF-1532; @CL-CTF-1539] |

Section 3332 of title 31 of the United States Code requires, subject to implementing regulations of the Secretary of the Treasury at 31 CFR part 208, that EFT be used to make all contract payments [@CL-CTF-3747].

The Government shall provide all contract payments through EFT except if one of the listed alternatives applies [@CL-CTF-1532]. One alternative is that the office making payment under a contract that requires payment by EFT loses the ability to release payment by EFT; in that case, to the extent authorized by 31 CFR part 208, the payment office shall make necessary payments pursuant to paragraph (a)(2) of the clause at either 52.232-33 or 52.232-34 until such time as it can make EFT payments [@CL-CTF-1532; @CL-CTF-3008]. Two further alternatives are that the payment is to be received by or on behalf of the contractor outside the United States and Puerto Rico and that a contract is paid in other than United States currency, each followed by a cross-reference to 32.1106(b) [@CL-CTF-1533]. A further alternative is that payment by EFT under a classified contract could compromise the safeguarding of classified information or national security, or arrangements for appropriate EFT payments would be impractical due to security considerations [@CL-CTF-1534].

Another alternative is that a contract is awarded by a deployed contracting officer in the course of military operations, including, but not limited to, contingency operations as defined in 2.101 [@CL-CTF-1535], or a contract is awarded by any contracting officer in the conduct of emergency operations, such as responses to natural disasters or national or civil emergencies, if EFT is not known to be possible or EFT payment would not support the objectives of the operation [@CL-CTF-1536]. Two more alternatives are that the agency does not expect to make more than one payment to the same recipient within a one-year period, and that the agency's need for supplies and services is of such unusual and compelling urgency that the Government would be seriously injured unless payment is made by a method other than EFT [@CL-CTF-1537]. The last two alternatives are that there is only one source for supplies and services and the Government would be seriously injured unless payment is made by a method other than EFT, and that the payment is otherwise authorized by Department of the Treasury Regulations at 31 CFR part 208 [@CL-CTF-1538].

Subpart 32.11 provides policy and procedures for contract financing and delivery payments to contractors by electronic funds transfer (EFT) [@CL-CTF-9306].

## How it works

The Government shall provide all contract payments through EFT except if one of the listed alternatives above applies [@CL-CTF-1532]. A Governmentwide commercial purchase card charge authorizes the third party that issued the card to make immediate payment to the contractor, and the Government reimburses the third party at a later date [@CL-CTF-1539]. For purposes of compliance with section 3332 of title 31 of the United States Code and implementing regulations at 31 CFR part 208, the term electronic funds transfer includes a Governmentwide commercial purchase card transaction [@CL-CTF-9462]. The contracting officer shall not authorize the card as a method of payment during any period the SAM indicates that the contractor has delinquent debt subject to collection under the TOP [@CL-CTF-1540]. For the clauses that carry the EFT and purchase card terms, see [EFT solicitation provision and contract clauses](ctf-eft-solicitation-provision-and-clauses.md).

Class Deviation 2026-O0022 says that, effective February 1, 2026, contracting officers shall use the revised FAR part 32, Contract Financing, published on the Revolutionary FAR Overhaul web page in lieu of the text codified at 48 CFR chapter 1 [@CL-CTF-4046]. RFO FAR 32.1100 says the subpart provides policy and procedures for contract financing and delivery payments to contractors by EFT [@CL-CTF-3044]. RFO FAR 32.1101 provides that section 3332 of title 31 of the United States Code requires, subject to implementing regulations of the Secretary of the Treasury at 31 CFR part 208, that EFT be used to make all contract payments [@CL-CTF-3045]. RFO FAR 32.1103 provides that the Government must provide all contract payments through EFT except if one of the listed alternatives applies [@CL-CTF-4294]. One alternative is that the office making payment under a contract that requires payment by EFT loses the ability to release payment by EFT, and then, to the extent authorized by 31 CFR part 208, the payment office must make necessary payments pursuant to paragraph (a)(2) of the clause at either 52.232-33 or 52.232-34 until such time as it can make EFT payments [@CL-CTF-4294; @CL-CTF-3009]. Further alternatives are that the payment is to be received by or on behalf of the contractor outside the United States and Puerto Rico and that a contract is paid in other than United States currency, each followed by a cross-reference to 32.1106(b) [@CL-CTF-4295]; that payment by EFT under a classified contract could compromise the safeguarding of classified information or national security, or arrangements for appropriate EFT payments would be impractical due to security considerations [@CL-CTF-4296]; and that a contract is awarded by a deployed contracting officer in the course of military operations, including, but not limited to, contingency operations as defined in 2.101, or a contract is awarded by any contracting officer in the conduct of emergency operations, such as responses to natural disasters or national or civil emergencies, if EFT is not known to be possible or EFT payment would not support the objectives of the operation [@CL-CTF-4297; @CL-CTF-4298]. Others are that the agency does not expect to make more than one payment to the same recipient within a one-year period, and that an agency's need for supplies and services is of such unusual and compelling urgency that the Government would be seriously injured unless payment is made by a method other than EFT [@CL-CTF-4299]. The last two alternatives are that there is only one source for supplies and services and the Government would be seriously injured unless payment is made by a method other than EFT, and that the payment is otherwise authorized by Department of the Treasury Regulations at 31 CFR part 208 [@CL-CTF-4300]. RFO FAR 32.1108 provides that a Governmentwide commercial purchase card charge authorizes the third party that issued the purchase card to make immediate payment to the contractor, and the Government reimburses the third party at a later date [@CL-CTF-4301]. The contracting officer must not authorize the Governmentwide commercial purchase card as a method of payment during any period the SAM indicates that the contractor has delinquent debt subject to collection under the TOP [@CL-CTF-4302].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An agency buys a one-time service for 8,000 and does not expect to pay the same recipient again within a year. Because the agency does not expect more than one payment to the same recipient within a one-year period, that case is a listed exception.

Answer: EFT is not required for that payment.

## Never confuse

A Governmentwide commercial purchase card charge authorizes the third party that issued the purchase card to make immediate payment to the contractor, and the Government reimburses the third party at a later date [@CL-CTF-1539]. The exceptions for payments received outside the United States and for payments in other than United States currency each carry a cross-reference to 32.1106(b) [@CL-CTF-1533]. For invoice due dates, see [Prompt payment: determining invoice payment due dates](../billing-payment/ctf-prompt-payment-due-dates.md), for earliest payment dates, see [Making payments: earliest payment date and partial deliveries](../billing-payment/ctf-making-payments.md), and for DoD electronic submission, see [DoD payment rules: prompt payment applicability, electronic submission and performance-based payment procedures](../billing-payment/ctf-dod-payment-applicability-and-electronic-submission.md).
