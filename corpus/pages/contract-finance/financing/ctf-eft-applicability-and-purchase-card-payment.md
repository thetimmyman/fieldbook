## Purpose

States when the Government pays by electronic funds transfer, the exceptions the FAR lists, and how a purchase card payment works.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CTF-1532] |
| Codified citation | FAR 32.1103 and FAR 32.1108 [@CL-CTF-1532; @CL-CTF-1539] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CTF-1532] |
| Applies to | All contract payments, subject to the listed exceptions, and payments made by Governmentwide commercial purchase card [@CL-CTF-1532; @CL-CTF-1539] |

The Government shall provide all contract payments through EFT except in the listed cases [@CL-CTF-1532]. One listed case is that the office making payment under a contract that requires payment by EFT loses the ability to release payment by EFT, and the paragraph then says what the payment office does until it can make EFT payments, which this card does not restate [@CL-CTF-1532]. Two further listed cases are a payment to be received by or on behalf of the contractor outside the United States and Puerto Rico and a contract paid in other than United States currency, each marked in the text with a cross-reference to 32.1106(b) [@CL-CTF-1533]. A further listed case is payment by EFT under a classified contract that could compromise the safeguarding of classified information or national security, or for which arrangements for appropriate EFT payments would be impractical due to security considerations [@CL-CTF-1534].

The exception for deployed and emergency operations has two parts joined by the word or. One is a contract awarded by a deployed contracting officer in the course of military operations, including, but not limited to, contingency operations as defined in 2.101 [@CL-CTF-1535]. The other is a contract awarded by any contracting officer in the conduct of emergency operations, such as responses to natural disasters or national or civil emergencies [@CL-CTF-1536]. The paragraph states one condition after both parts, introduced by if: EFT is not known to be possible, or EFT payment would not support the objectives of the operation [@CL-CTF-1536]. The text places that condition after both parts, so read the paragraph for how it applies to each [@CL-CTF-1535; @CL-CTF-1536]. Two more listed cases are that the agency does not expect to make more than one payment to the same recipient within a one-year period, and that the agency's need for supplies and services is of such unusual and compelling urgency that the Government would be seriously injured unless payment is made by a method other than EFT [@CL-CTF-1537]. The last two are that there is only one source for supplies and services and the Government would be seriously injured unless payment is made by a method other than EFT, and that EFT is otherwise not required where authorized by Department of the Treasury Regulations at 31 CFR part 208 [@CL-CTF-1538].

## How it works

EFT is the default method for contract payments, and each listed case above is a way out of that default [@CL-CTF-1532]. A Governmentwide commercial purchase card charge authorizes the third party that issued the card to make immediate payment to the contractor, and the Government reimburses the third party at a later date [@CL-CTF-1539]. The contracting officer shall not authorize the card as a method of payment during any period the SAM indicates that the contractor has delinquent debt subject to collection under the TOP [@CL-CTF-1540]. Other purchase card rules in the section, including the clause that governs a contractor's charge, what the contract must identify, and the debt check at award, are not restated on this card. The clauses that carry the EFT and purchase card terms are prescribed in a separate section, covered on [EFT solicitation provision and contract clauses](ctf-eft-solicitation-provision-and-clauses.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An agency buys a one-time service for 8,000 and does not expect to pay the same recipient again within a year. Because the agency does not expect more than one payment to the same recipient within a one-year period, that case is a listed exception.

Answer: EFT is not required for that payment.

## Never confuse

A purchase card payment runs through a third party that pays the contractor immediately and is reimbursed later [@CL-CTF-1539], while the EFT rule concerns contract payments the Government provides [@CL-CTF-1532]. The exception for payments outside the United States and for payments in other than United States currency carries a cross-reference to 32.1106(b) [@CL-CTF-1533], which this card does not cover. Invoice due dates are on [Prompt payment: determining invoice payment due dates](../billing-payment/ctf-prompt-payment-due-dates.md), earliest payment dates are on [Making payments: earliest payment date and partial deliveries](../billing-payment/ctf-making-payments.md), and DoD electronic submission is on [DoD payment rules: prompt payment applicability, electronic submission and performance-based payment procedures](../billing-payment/ctf-dod-payment-applicability-and-electronic-submission.md).
