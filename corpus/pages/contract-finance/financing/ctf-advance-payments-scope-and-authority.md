## Purpose

Shows which advance payments the non-commercial advance payment subpart covers, and the statutory authorities under which an agency may authorize them.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CTF-3556] |
| Codified citation | FAR 32.400 and 32.401 [@CL-CTF-3556; @CL-CTF-3559] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 32.400 [@CL-CTF-4206; @CL-CTF-4207], RFO FAR 32.401 [@CL-CTF-4208; @CL-CTF-4209], RFO FAR 32.402(b) [@CL-CTF-4197; @CL-CTF-4198], RFO FAR 32.403 [@CL-CTF-4199] and RFO FAR 32.404(a) [@CL-CTF-4210] |

The advance payment subpart provides policies and procedures for advance payments on prime contracts and subcontracts [@CL-CTF-3556]. It does not include policies and procedures for advance payments for the types of transactions listed in 32.404 [@CL-CTF-3557]. It does not apply to commercial advance payments, which are subject to subpart 32.2 [@CL-CTF-3558].

The agency may authorize advance payments in negotiated and sealed bid contracts if the action is appropriate under chapter 45 of title 41 of the United States Code, chapter 277 of title 10 of the United States Code, or Public Law 85-804 and Executive Order 10789 [@CL-CTF-3559]. The section refers to Subpart 50.1 for other applications of Public Law 85-804 [@CL-CTF-3560].

## How it works

Two questions decide whether the subpart applies. First, the payment must be an advance payment on a prime contract or subcontract, and the listed transaction types and commercial advance payments sit outside the subpart [@CL-CTF-3556; @CL-CTF-3557; @CL-CTF-3558]. Second, the agency may authorize advance payments if the action is appropriate under one of the named authorities [@CL-CTF-3559]. The permission reaches negotiated and sealed bid contracts alike, so the contracting method does not settle the question [@CL-CTF-3559].

Authority is not a direction to pay in advance. The agency must authorize advance payments sparingly [@CL-CTF-0255]. Except for the contracts described in 32.403(a) and (b), advance payment is the least preferred method of contract financing, and it generally should not be authorized if other types of financing are reasonably available to the contractor in adequate amounts [@CL-CTF-0256]. The statutory requirements also include adequate security from the contractor and advance payments that do not exceed the unpaid contract price [@CL-CTF-0257]. A further requirement is that the agency head or designee determines, based on written findings, that the advance payment is in the public interest or facilitates the national defense [@CL-CTF-0280].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 32 text in place of the codified text from February 1, 2026 [@CL-CTF-4046]. RFO FAR 32.400 keeps the scope: the subpart provides policies and procedures for advance payments on prime contracts and subcontracts, except for the types of transactions listed in 32.404 [@CL-CTF-4206], and it does not apply to commercial advance payments, which are subject to subpart 32.2 [@CL-CTF-4207]. RFO FAR 32.401 keeps the authority to authorize advance payments in negotiated and sealed bid contracts if the action is appropriate under chapter 45 of title 41 of the United States Code, chapter 277 of title 10 of the United States Code, or Public Law 85-804 and Executive Order 10789, as amended [@CL-CTF-4208], and it refers to Subpart 50.1 for other applications of Public Law 85-804 [@CL-CTF-4209]. RFO FAR 32.404(a) says the subpart does not apply to advance payments for the listed items, which begin with rent, tuition and insurance premiums [@CL-CTF-4210], where the codified paragraph says it does not apply to advance payments authorized by law for them [@CL-CTF-4211]. The RFO text keeps the rule that the agency must authorize advance payments sparingly [@CL-CTF-4197], but says that in most situations advance payment is the least preferred method of contract financing [@CL-CTF-4198], where the codified text excepts the contracts described in 32.403(a) and (b) [@CL-CTF-0256]; the RFO text marks 32.403 as reserved [@CL-CTF-4199].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A sealed bid supply prime contract for a non-commercial item asks for an advance payment of 500,000 against a 2,000,000 order. The transaction is not one of the types listed in 32.404 and is not a commercial advance payment, so the policies of the subpart apply to it [@CL-CTF-3556; @CL-CTF-3557; @CL-CTF-3558]. Sealed bid contracts are within the permission, so the agency may authorize the advance if the action is appropriate under chapter 45 of title 41 of the United States Code [@CL-CTF-3559]. The agency authorizes it sparingly, and a statutory requirement is that the agency head or designee determines, based on written findings, that the payment is in the public interest or facilitates the national defense [@CL-CTF-0255; @CL-CTF-0280]. The 500,000 does not exceed the unpaid contract price of 2,000,000, which meets one statutory requirement; the contractor must also give adequate security [@CL-CTF-0257].

## Never confuse

The scope rule is often confused with the commercial advance payment rules. Commercial advance payments are subject to subpart 32.2, and the advance payment subpart does not apply to them [@CL-CTF-3558]. It is also confused with the transaction types listed in 32.404, which the subpart leaves out of its policies and procedures [@CL-CTF-3557]; the [exclusions card](ctf-advance-payment-exclusions-and-interest.md) lists them. The statutory authority to authorize an advance payment is also not a preference for it [@CL-CTF-0255; @CL-CTF-0256]. The codified text begins the least-preferred rule with an exception for the contracts described in 32.403(a) and (b) [@CL-CTF-0256], while RFO FAR 32.402(b) says advance payment is the least preferred method in most situations [@CL-CTF-4198].
