## Purpose

Gives the customary progress payment rates on DoD contracts for large and small business concerns.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | DoD class deviation [@CL-CTF-0001] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 232.501-1(a) [@CL-CTF-0001] |
| RFO citation | Class Deviation 2026-O0022, DFARS 232.501-1(a) [@CL-CTF-0001] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Deviation text [@CL-CTF-0001] |
| Applies to | DoD contracts, from 2026-02-01 [@CL-CTF-0001] |

The customary progress payment rates for DoD contracts, including contracts that contain foreign military sales requirements, are 80 percent for large business concerns and 90 percent for small business concerns [@CL-CTF-0001; @CL-CTF-0002; @CL-CTF-9601]. The codified FAR customary rate for contracts with small business concerns is 85 percent, which is lower than the DoD rate for small business concerns [@CL-CTF-0951; @CL-CTF-0002].

## How it works

Progress payments based on costs are contract financing paid as costs are incurred on fixed-price work, before delivery. Each request is the customary rate times the total costs incurred to date, less all progress payments already received, and the payments are liquidated as deliveries are invoiced. The contract's progress payments clause states the rate that applies; a rate above the customary rate is an unusual financing case and needs approval.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A large business has incurred 1,000,000 of allowable costs on a fixed-price DoD contract and has received no progress payments yet. At an 80 percent rate its first request is 800,000. If it had already received 600,000, the next request would be 200,000. A small business with the same costs and no prior payments at a 90 percent rate may request 900,000.

## Never confuse

A progress payment is financing tied to costs; a performance-based payment is financing tied to events or milestones. A contract that provides for progress payments, or for an indefinite-delivery contract an order that does, cannot also use performance-based payments. Neither is a payment for delivered items.
