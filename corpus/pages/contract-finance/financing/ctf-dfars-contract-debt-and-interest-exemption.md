## Purpose

Shows which official determines and collects contract debts that arise from overpayments or erroneous payments, when that official has primary responsibility, and who may exempt contracts from the administrative interest charges that the contract debt subpart requires.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-CTF-3793] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 232.602(b) and DFARS 232.611(a) [@CL-CTF-3793; @CL-CTF-3796] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CTF-3796] |
| Applies to | Overpayments and erroneous payments under a contract, and contracts that may be exempted from the administrative interest charges [@CL-CTF-3794; @CL-CTF-3796] |

Disbursing officers are the officials designated to make payments under a contract or to receive payments of amounts due under a contract [@CL-CTF-3793]. The disbursing officer is responsible for determining the amount of, and collecting, contract debts whenever overpayments or erroneous payments have been made [@CL-CTF-3794]. The disbursing officer also has primary responsibility when the amounts due and dates for payment are contained in the contract and a copy of the contract has been furnished to the disbursing officer with notice to collect as amounts become due [@CL-CTF-3795].

The Principal Director, Defense Pricing, Contracting, and Acquisition Policy, Office of the Under Secretary of Defense (Acquisition and Sustainment), may exempt the contracts in FAR 32.611(a)(2) through (5) and other contracts [@CL-CTF-3796], in exceptional circumstances, from the administrative interest charges required by this subpart [@CL-CTF-3797].

Paragraph (a)(7) of the same section lists other exceptions: contracts for instruction of military or ROTC personnel at civilian schools, colleges, and universities, and basic agreements with telephone companies for communications services and facilities, including purchases under such agreements [@CL-CTF-3798]. It also lists transportation contracts with common carriers for common carrier services [@CL-CTF-3799].

## How it works

Disbursing officers are those officials designated to make payments under a contract or to receive payments of amounts due under a contract [@CL-CTF-3793]. The disbursing officer is responsible for determining the amount and collecting contract debts whenever overpayments or erroneous payments have been made [@CL-CTF-3794].

The disbursing officer also has primary responsibility when the amounts due and dates for payment are contained in the contract, and a copy of the contract has been furnished to the disbursing officer with notice to collect as amounts become due [@CL-CTF-3795].

The FAR roles of the contracting officer and the payment office are on [Contract debts: responsibilities and the debt determination](../cash/ctf-contract-debt-responsibilities-and-determination.md).

The Principal Director, Defense Pricing, Contracting, and Acquisition Policy, Office of the Under Secretary of Defense (Acquisition and Sustainment), may exempt the contracts in FAR 32.611(a)(2) through (5) and other contracts [@CL-CTF-3796], in exceptional circumstances, from the administrative interest charges required by this subpart [@CL-CTF-3797].

In FAR 32.611(a), item (7) of the list of categories is any other exceptions authorized under agency procedures [@CL-CTF-3201]. The FAR categories and the Interest clause are on [Contract debt final decisions and collection](../cash/ctf-contract-debt-final-decisions-and-collection.md) and [Interest on amounts due the Government](../billing-payment/ctf-interest-and-assignment-of-claims-clauses.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A payment office pays 120,000 on a contract that allowed 100,000, which is an overpayment of 20,000. The disbursing officer is responsible for determining the amount of the debt and collecting it [@CL-CTF-3794]. If the contract states the amounts due and the payment dates, and a copy went to the disbursing officer with notice to collect as amounts become due, the disbursing officer also has primary responsibility [@CL-CTF-3795].

A separate contract is a transportation contract with a common carrier for common carrier services. It is among the listed other exceptions [@CL-CTF-3799].

A third contract falls in none of the paragraph (a)(7) exceptions and in none of the FAR 32.611(a) categories. The Principal Director could exempt it from the administrative interest charges in exceptional circumstances, and this DFARS paragraph names no one else to do so [@CL-CTF-3796; @CL-CTF-3797].

## Never confuse

The Principal Director's authority differs from the disbursing officer's duty rather than overlapping with it: the Principal Director's authority is to exempt contracts, in exceptional circumstances, from the administrative interest charges required by the subpart [@CL-CTF-3797], while the disbursing officer is responsible for determining the amount and collecting contract debts whenever overpayments or erroneous payments have been made [@CL-CTF-3794]. Disbursing officers are those officials designated to make payments under a contract or to receive payments of amounts due under a contract [@CL-CTF-3793], unlike the contracting officer, whose FAR role is on [Contract debts: responsibilities and the debt determination](../cash/ctf-contract-debt-responsibilities-and-determination.md).
