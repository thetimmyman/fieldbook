## Purpose

Shows what a mentor firm can have reimbursed for advance payments to a protege firm, and what restriction the overseas clause puts on assigning claims.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-CTF-1760; @CL-CTF-1764] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 252.232-7005 and DFARS 252.232-7008 [@CL-CTF-1760; @CL-CTF-1764] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CTF-1760; @CL-CTF-1764] |
| Applies to | Solicitations and contracts where advance payments will be provided by the contractor to a subcontractor under an approved mentor-protege agreement [@CL-CTF-1794], and solicitations and contracts where contract performance will be in a foreign country [@CL-CTF-1795] |

The Government will reimburse the contractor for advance payments it makes, as a mentor firm, to a protege firm under an approved mentor-protege agreement, provided the listed conditions are met [@CL-CTF-1760]. One listed condition is that the contractor agrees that any financial loss from the protege firm's failure or inability to repay unliquidated advance payments is the contractor's sole financial responsibility [@CL-CTF-1760; @CL-CTF-1761]. The other two listed conditions are that the contractor's subcontract with the protege firm includes a provision substantially the same as the FAR advance payments clause and that the contractor has administered the advance payments in accordance with the policies of FAR subpart 32.4 [@CL-CTF-1760; @CL-CTF-1793].

DFARS directs use of the overseas clause instead of the FAR assignment of claims clause in solicitations and contracts when contract performance will be in a foreign country [@CL-CTF-1795]. Under the overseas clause, no claims for monies due or to become due shall be assigned by the contractor unless all three of these hold: the assignment is approved in writing by the Contracting Officer, made in accordance with United States law and regulation, and permitted by the laws and regulations of the contractor's country [@CL-CTF-1764].

## How it works

For a fixed price type contract, advance payments to a protege firm are paid and administered as if there were 100 percent progress payments [@CL-CTF-1762]. For cost reimbursable contracts, reimbursement of the advance payments is made through a public voucher [@CL-CTF-1763]. An overseas assignment covers all amounts payable and not already paid and is not made to more than one party, except to one party as agent or trustee for two or more parties participating in the financing [@CL-CTF-1765]. On each invoice or voucher to which an assignment applies and for which direct payment is to be made to an assignee, the contractor identifies the assignee by name and complete address [@CL-CTF-1766]. The mentor-protege clause text states no dollar limit on advance payments to the protege firm.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A mentor firm holds a fixed price contract and advances 50,000 to its protege under an approved agreement. The protege cannot repay 10,000 of it. Answer: if the listed conditions are met, the mentor firm is reimbursed for the advance, and the 10,000 loss is the mentor firm's alone. For an overseas contract, the mentor firm that wants to assign its claims first needs the written approval of the Contracting Officer.

## Never confuse

Reimbursement of the mentor firm is not a guarantee against loss: the loss from a protege firm that cannot repay stays with the contractor [@CL-CTF-1761]. The overseas assignment restriction is also not the general assignment of claims rule for United States contracts, which is on [Assignment of claims conditions and policies](../cash/ctf-assignment-of-claims.md) and [Interest on amounts due the Government and the assignment of claims clause](../billing-payment/ctf-interest-and-assignment-of-claims-clauses.md). The basic advance payments clause is on [Advance Payments clause](ctf-advance-payments-clause.md).
