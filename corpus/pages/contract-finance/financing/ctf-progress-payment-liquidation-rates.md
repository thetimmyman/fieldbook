## Purpose

Sets out ordinary and alternate progress payment liquidation rate methods and actions to correct excess unliquidated balances.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CTF-0762] |
| Codified citation | FAR 32.503-8 [@CL-CTF-0762] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 32.503-8 [@CL-CTF-4416; @CL-CTF-4417; @CL-CTF-4418] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CTF-0762] |
| Applies to | Progress payments that the Government recoups by deducting liquidations from payments for completed contract items [@CL-CTF-0854] |

The Government recoups progress payments through the deduction of liquidations from payments that would otherwise be due to the contractor for completed contract items [@CL-CTF-0854]. To determine the amount of the liquidation, the contracting officer applies a liquidation rate to the contract price of contract items delivered and accepted, and under the ordinary method the liquidation rate is the same as the progress payment rate [@CL-CTF-0762]. At the beginning of a contract, the contracting officer must use the ordinary method [@CL-CTF-0855].

The objective of the alternate method is to permit the contractor to retain the earned profit element of the contract prices for completed items in the liquidation process [@CL-CTF-0763]. The minimum liquidation rate is the expected progress payments divided by the contract price, and the expected progress payments are the estimated cost of performing the contract multiplied by the progress payment rate [@CL-CTF-0764]. Minimum rates are generally expressed to tenths of a percent, and decimals between tenths are rounded up to the next highest tenth, not necessarily the nearest tenth [@CL-CTF-0782].

The contracting officer may reduce the liquidation rate if all of the listed conditions are met, which include that the contractor requests a reduction, the rate has not been reduced in the preceding 12 months, and the delivery schedule extends at least 18 months from the contract award date [@CL-CTF-0781]. The other conditions are that data on actual costs are available for the products delivered or, if no deliveries have been made, for a performance period of at least 12 months [@CL-CTF-0864]; that the reduced rate would result in the Government recouping under each invoice the full extent of the progress payments applicable to the costs allocable to that invoice [@CL-CTF-0865]; that the contractor would not be paid for more than the costs of items delivered and accepted (less allocable progress payments) and the earned profit on those items, and the unliquidated progress payments would not exceed the limit in paragraph (a)(5) of the Progress Payments clause [@CL-CTF-0874]; and that the parties agree on an appropriate rate and the contractor agrees to certify annually, or more often if the contracting officer requests, that the alternate rate continues to meet the conditions of subsections 5, 6, and 7 above [@CL-CTF-0866; @CL-CTF-2264].

## How it works

Whenever the liquidation rate is changed, the contracting officer shall issue a contract modification to specify the new rate in the Progress Payments clause [@CL-CTF-0863]. The contracting officer shall ensure that any excess of the unliquidated progress payments over the contractual limitation is promptly corrected through one or more listed actions [@CL-CTF-0765], which are increasing the liquidation rate, reducing the progress payment rate and suspending progress payments [@CL-CTF-0872]; for those rights see [Suspension or reduction of progress payments](ctf-suspension-or-reduction-of-progress-payments.md). The excess is most likely to arise when the costs of performance exceed the contract price [@CL-CTF-0871], when the alternate method is used and actual costs exceed the estimates used to set the rate [@CL-CTF-0876], when the rate of progress or the quality of contract performance is unsatisfactory, or when the rate of rejections, waste, or spoilage is excessive [@CL-CTF-3225]. The deduction mechanics under the clause are on [Progress payment liquidation](ctf-progress-payment-liquidation.md).

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 32 text in place of the codified text from February 1, 2026 [@CL-CTF-4046]. RFO FAR 32.503-8 keeps the ordinary method: the Government recoups progress payments by deducting liquidations from payments otherwise due for completed contract items [@CL-CTF-4416], the liquidation rate is the same as the progress payment rate [@CL-CTF-4417], and the contracting officer must use that method at the beginning of a contract [@CL-CTF-4418]. RFO FAR 32.503-9 keeps the objective of the alternate method [@CL-CTF-4419], the conditions on a contractor request, no reduction in the preceding 12 months and a delivery schedule of at least 18 months [@CL-CTF-4420], the conditions on actual cost data and recoupment under each invoice [@CL-CTF-4421; @CL-CTF-4422], the conditions on payments and unliquidated progress payments [@CL-CTF-4423], the agreement on the rate and the annual certification condition [@CL-CTF-4424] and the contract modification for a changed rate [@CL-CTF-4425]. RFO FAR 32.503-9(a)(9) requires the contractor to agree to certify annually, or more often if requested by the contracting officer, that the alternate rate continues to meet the conditions of sections 32.503-5 and 32.503-6 [@CL-CTF-4424], whereas codified FAR 32.503-9(a)(9) refers to subsections 5, 6, and 7 above [@CL-CTF-4451]. RFO FAR 32.503-10 keeps the minimum rate calculation and the rounding up to the next highest tenth [@CL-CTF-4426; @CL-CTF-4427], and RFO FAR 32.503-12 keeps the actions that correct an excess of unliquidated progress payments [@CL-CTF-4428; @CL-CTF-4429] and the four circumstances in which it is most likely to arise [@CL-CTF-4430; @CL-CTF-3226].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contract price is 3,000,000 and the estimated cost of performing the contract is 2,600,000, with an 80 percent progress payment rate. Expected progress payments are 2,600,000 times 80 percent, which is 2,080,000. Dividing 2,080,000 by 3,000,000 gives 69.333 percent, so the minimum liquidation rate rounds up to the next highest tenth, 69.4 percent, not the nearest tenth of 69.3 percent. Assuming the conditions for reducing the rate are met, a delivered and accepted item priced at 300,000 is liquidated by 208,200 at 69.4 percent, against 240,000 at the ordinary 80 percent rate.

## Never confuse

Under the ordinary method, the liquidation rate is the same as the progress payment rate [@CL-CTF-0762], whereas under the alternate method the minimum liquidation rate is the expected progress payments divided by the contract price [@CL-CTF-0764]. For customary progress payment rates see [Progress payments](ctf-progress-payments.md).

Under the alternate method, the codified text requires the contractor to agree to certify annually, or more often if requested by the contracting officer, that the alternate rate continues to meet the conditions of subsections 5, 6, and 7 above [@CL-CTF-4451], while RFO FAR 32.503-9(a)(9) refers to the conditions of sections 32.503-5 and 32.503-6 [@CL-CTF-4424].
