## Purpose

Explains which DoD contracts may prohibit assignment of claims, DoD's determination of need for a no-setoff commitment, and when that commitment may be excluded.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-CTF-3800] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 232.803(b) and DFARS 232.803(d) [@CL-CTF-3800; @CL-CTF-3801] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CTF-3802] |
| Applies to | DoD contracts that may prohibit assignment of claims, and contracts with an Assignment of Claims provision under which proceeds have been assigned [@CL-CTF-3800; @CL-CTF-3801] |

Only contracts for personal services may prohibit the assignment of claims [@CL-CTF-3800]. Under FAR 32.803(b), a contract may prohibit the assignment of claims if the agency determines the prohibition to be in the Government's interest [@CL-CTF-0775].

On May 10, 1996, the Director of Defense Procurement determined that a need exists for DoD to agree not to reduce or set off any money due or to become due under the contract when the proceeds under the contract have been assigned in accordance with the Assignment of Claims provision of the contract [@CL-CTF-3801]. The Director made the determination pursuant to section 6305 of title 41, United States Code, and in accordance with a Presidential delegation dated October 3, 1995, a Secretary of Defense delegation dated February 5, 1996, and an Under Secretary of Defense (Acquisition, Technology, and Logistics) delegation dated February 23, 1996 [@CL-CTF-3887]. The determination was published in the Federal Register on June 11, 1996, as required by law [@CL-CTF-3888].

Nevertheless, if departments and agencies decide it is in the Government's interests, or if the contracting officer makes a determination in accordance with FAR 32.803(d) concerning a significantly indebted offeror, the no-setoff commitment may be excluded [@CL-CTF-3802].

## How it works

The no-setoff commitment is DoD's agreement not to reduce or set off any money due or to become due under the contract when the proceeds under the contract have been assigned in accordance with the Assignment of Claims provision of the contract [@CL-CTF-3801]. The commitment and its limits are defined on [Assignment of claims: definitions and the extent of assignee protection](../cash/ctf-assignment-of-claims-definitions-and-protection.md).

The no-setoff commitment may be excluded if departments and agencies decide it is in the Government's interests, or if the contracting officer makes a determination in accordance with FAR 32.803(d) concerning a significantly indebted offeror [@CL-CTF-3802].

Under FAR 32.803(d), in the event an offeror is significantly indebted to the United States, the contracting officer should consider whether the inclusion of the no-setoff commitment in a particular contract is in the best interests of the United States [@CL-CTF-3890]. In such an event, the contracting officer should consult with the Government officer(s) responsible for collecting the debt(s) [@CL-CTF-3891].

If an assigned contract does not include the commitment, the Government may apply against payments to the assignee any liability of the contractor arising independently of the assigned contract, if the liability existed at the time notice of the assignment was received, even though that liability had not yet matured so as to be due and payable [@CL-CTF-3495; @CL-CTF-3001]. The assignment procedure is on [Assignment of claims conditions and policies](../cash/ctf-assignment-of-claims.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor with a services contract worth 2,000,000 assigns the proceeds to a bank under the contract's Assignment of Claims provision. The contract is not for personal services, so it may not prohibit the assignment of claims [@CL-CTF-3800]. If the contract carries the no-setoff commitment, DoD has agreed not to reduce or set off the assigned money due or to become due under it [@CL-CTF-3801].

The offeror is significantly indebted to the Government on an unrelated debt. The contracting officer considers whether the commitment is in the best interests of the United States and consults the officers responsible for collecting the debt [@CL-CTF-3890; @CL-CTF-3891]. If the contracting officer then makes a determination under FAR 32.803(d) concerning the significantly indebted offeror, the commitment may be excluded [@CL-CTF-3802]. If it is excluded, the Government may apply the unrelated debt against payments to the bank, provided the debt existed when notice of the assignment was received [@CL-CTF-3495].

## Never confuse

Only contracts for personal services may prohibit the assignment of claims [@CL-CTF-3800]. DoD's agreement not to reduce or set off any money due or to become due under the contract when the proceeds have been assigned [@CL-CTF-3801] may be excluded in the cases stated in DFARS 232.803(d) [@CL-CTF-3802].

## Company calibration

Whether any contract in the portfolio is a personal services contract, whether each assigned contract includes the no-setoff commitment, and who records an exclusion. [@Q-004]
