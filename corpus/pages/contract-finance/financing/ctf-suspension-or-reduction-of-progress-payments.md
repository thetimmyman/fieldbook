## Purpose

Explains when and how the contracting officer may reduce or suspend progress payments or increase liquidation rates.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CTF-0251] |
| Codified citation | FAR 32.503-6 [@CL-CTF-0251] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 32.503-6 [@CL-CTF-4511; @CL-CTF-4512; @CL-CTF-4513; @CL-CTF-4514; @CL-CTF-4515; @CL-CTF-4516; @CL-CTF-4517; @CL-CTF-4518] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CTF-0251] |
| Applies to | Contracts containing the Progress Payments clause [@CL-CTF-0251] |

The Progress Payments clause provides the Government the right to reduce or suspend progress payments, or to increase the liquidation rate, under specified conditions [@CL-CTF-0251]. The contracting officer must adjust progress payments when necessary to ensure that the fair value of undelivered work equals or exceeds the amount of unliquidated progress payments [@CL-CTF-0253].

## How it works

The contracting officer takes these actions only in accordance with the contract terms and never precipitately or arbitrarily, and should take them only after notifying the contractor of the intended action and providing an opportunity for discussion [@CL-CTF-0521], evaluating the effect of the action on the contractor's operations, based on the contractor's financial condition, projected cash requirements, and the existing or available credit arrangements, and considering the general equities of the particular situation [@CL-CTF-0522]. The contracting officer is to take immediate unilateral action only if warranted by circumstances such as overpayments or unsatisfactory contract performance [@CL-CTF-0523]. The contractor must comply with all material requirements of the contract, including the requirement to maintain an efficient and reliable accounting system and controls, adequate for the proper administration of progress payments [@CL-CTF-0524]. If the system or controls are deemed inadequate, progress payments (or the portion associated with the unacceptable portion of the accounting system) must be suspended until the necessary changes have been made [@CL-CTF-0252]. If the contracting officer concludes that further progress payments would increase the probable loss to the Government, the contracting officer shall suspend progress payments and all other payments until the unliquidated balance of progress payments is eliminated [@CL-CTF-0254].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 32 text in place of the codified text from February 1, 2026 [@CL-CTF-4046]. RFO FAR 32.503-6 keeps the rule described above: the Progress Payments clause provides a Government right to reduce or suspend progress payments, or to increase the liquidation rate, under specified conditions [@CL-CTF-4511]. The contracting officer must take these actions only in accordance with the contract terms and never precipitately or arbitrarily, and should take them only after notifying the contractor of the intended action and providing an opportunity for discussion [@CL-CTF-4512], evaluating the effect of the action on the contractor's operations, based on the contractor's financial condition, projected cash requirements, and the existing or available credit arrangements, and considering the general equities of the particular situation [@CL-CTF-4513]. The contracting officer must take immediate unilateral action only if warranted by circumstances such as overpayments or unsatisfactory contract performance [@CL-CTF-4514]. The contractor must comply with all material requirements of the contract, including the requirement to maintain an efficient and reliable accounting system and controls, adequate for the proper administration of progress payments [@CL-CTF-4515], and if they are deemed inadequate, progress payments, or the portion associated with the unacceptable portion of the system, must be suspended until the necessary changes have been made [@CL-CTF-4516]. If the contracting officer concludes that further progress payments would increase the probable loss to the Government, the contracting officer must suspend progress payments and all other payments until the unliquidated balance of progress payments is eliminated [@CL-CTF-4517], and the contracting officer must adjust progress payments when necessary to ensure that the fair value of undelivered work equals or exceeds the amount of unliquidated progress payments [@CL-CTF-4518].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor has received 800,000 in progress payments against undelivered contract items. A government review determines that due to substantial scrap and defective manufacturing, the fair value of undelivered work conforming to contract requirements is only 600,000. The contracting officer adjusts progress payments, for example by suspending further payments or increasing the liquidation rate, until the unliquidated balance of 800,000 no longer exceeds the 600,000 fair value of undelivered work, an adjustment of 200,000.

## Never confuse

A reduction or suspension of progress payments is a Government right exercised under the Progress Payments clause only in accordance with the contract terms and never precipitately or arbitrarily [@CL-CTF-0251; @CL-CTF-0521]. It is distinct from the suspension of progress payments and all other payments that the contracting officer shall impose if the contracting officer concludes that further progress payments would increase the probable loss to the Government [@CL-CTF-0254]. See [Customary progress payment rates](ctf-progress-payments.md) and [Contract financing at a glance](ctf-financing-overview.md).
