## Purpose

Sets the limits on Government financing of contractor work: what may be held back from a construction progress payment, what contract financing may be spent on, and when performance-based payments are the preferred method.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CTF-3056] |
| Codified citation | FAR 32.103, FAR 32.105 and FAR 32.1001 [@CL-CTF-3056; @CL-CTF-3060; @CL-CTF-3063] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 32.103, RFO FAR 32.105 and RFO FAR 32.1001 [@CL-CTF-4260; @CL-CTF-4265; @CL-CTF-4269] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CTF-3056] |
| Applies to | Construction progress payments, the contract financing methods covered by the same FAR part, and performance-based payments [@CL-CTF-3156; @CL-CTF-3060; @CL-CTF-3063] |

On a construction contract, when satisfactory progress has not been achieved by a contractor during any period for which a progress payment is to be made, a percentage of the progress payment may be retained [@CL-CTF-2964; @CL-CTF-3156]. Determinations to retain and the specific amount to be withheld shall be made by the contracting officer on a case-by-case basis, and such decisions will be based on the contracting officer's assessment of past performance and the likelihood that such performance will continue [@CL-CTF-3056]. Retainage should not be used as a substitute for good contract management, and the contracting officer should not withhold funds without cause [@CL-CTF-3057]. The amount of retainage withheld shall not exceed 10 percent of the approved estimated amount in accordance with the terms of the contract, and it may be adjusted as the contract approaches completion to recognize better than expected performance, the ability to rely on alternative safeguards, and other factors [@CL-CTF-3058]. Under the payment clause at FAR 52.232-5, if satisfactory progress has not been made, the Contracting Officer may retain a maximum of 10 percent of the amount of the payment until satisfactory progress is achieved [@CL-CTF-2010]. Upon completion of all contract requirements, retained amounts shall be paid promptly [@CL-CTF-3059].

The contract financing methods covered by FAR part 32 are intended to be self-liquidating through contract performance, and consequently agencies shall only use the methods for financing of contractor working capital, not for the expansion of contractor-owned facilities or the acquisition of fixed assets [@CL-CTF-3060]. However, under loan guarantees, exceptions may be made for either facilities expansion of a minor or incidental nature, if a relatively small part of the guaranteed loan is used for the expansion and the contractor's repayment would not be delayed or impaired [@CL-CTF-3061], or other instances of facilities expansion for which contract financing is appropriate under agency procedures [@CL-CTF-3157]. The limitations in FAR 32.105 do not apply to contracts under which facilities are being acquired for Government ownership [@CL-CTF-3062].

Performance-based payments are the preferred Government financing method when the contracting officer finds them practical, and the contractor agrees to their use [@CL-CTF-3063]. They are contract financing payments that are not payment for accepted items, and they are fully recoverable, in the same manner as progress payments, in the event of default [@CL-CTF-3064]. They are contract financing payments and therefore are not subject to the interest-penalty provisions of prompt payment [@CL-CTF-3065], and these payments shall be made in accordance with agency policy [@CL-CTF-3158]. Performance-based payments shall not be used for payments under cost-reimbursement line items [@CL-CTF-3066]. They shall not be used for contracts for architect-engineer services or construction, or for shipbuilding or ship conversion, alteration, or repair, when the contracts provide for progress payments based upon a percentage or stage of completion [@CL-CTF-3159]. They shall not be used for contracts awarded through sealed bid procedures [@CL-CTF-3160].

## How it works

On a construction contract, a percentage of a progress payment may be retained when satisfactory progress has not been achieved by a contractor during any period for which a progress payment is to be made [@CL-CTF-2964; @CL-CTF-3156]. The contracting officer makes the determination to retain and the specific amount on a case-by-case basis, based on the officer's assessment of past performance and the likelihood that such performance will continue [@CL-CTF-3056]. The amount of retainage withheld shall not exceed 10 percent of the approved estimated amount [@CL-CTF-3058], and upon completion of all contract requirements, retained amounts shall be paid promptly [@CL-CTF-3059]. Agencies shall only use the financing methods for contractor working capital, not for the expansion of contractor-owned facilities or the acquisition of fixed assets [@CL-CTF-3060]. Under loan guarantees, exceptions may be made for either of the two kinds of facilities expansion described above [@CL-CTF-3061; @CL-CTF-3157], and the limitations in FAR 32.105 do not apply to contracts under which facilities are being acquired for Government ownership [@CL-CTF-3062]. Performance-based payments are contract financing payments that are not payment for accepted items and are fully recoverable in the event of default [@CL-CTF-3064]; they are not subject to the interest-penalty provisions of prompt payment [@CL-CTF-3065].

Class Deviation 2026-O0022 says that, effective February 1, 2026, contracting officers shall use the revised FAR part 32, Contract Financing, published on the Revolutionary FAR Overhaul web page in lieu of the text codified at 48 CFR chapter 1 [@CL-CTF-4046]. RFO FAR 32.103, which covers progress payments under construction contracts [@CL-CTF-2965], says that when there is unsatisfactory progress by a contractor during any period for which a progress payment is to be made, a percentage of the progress payment may be retained [@CL-CTF-4260], where the codified text says when satisfactory progress has not been achieved [@CL-CTF-3156]. RFO FAR 32.103 also says retainage should not be used as a substitute for good contract management and the contracting officer should not withhold funds without cause [@CL-CTF-4261]; determinations to retain and the specific amount to be withheld must be made by the contracting officer on a case-by-case basis, based on the contracting officer's assessment of past performance and the likelihood that such performance will continue [@CL-CTF-4262]; the amount of retainage withheld must not exceed 10 percent of the approved estimated amount in accordance with the terms of the contract and may be adjusted as the contract approaches completion to recognize improved performance, the ability to rely on alternative safeguards, and other factors [@CL-CTF-4263], where the codified text says better than expected performance [@CL-CTF-3058]; and upon completion of all contract requirements, retained amounts must be paid promptly [@CL-CTF-4264]. RFO FAR 32.105(a) says the financing methods covered in this part are intended to be self-liquidating through contract performance and agencies must only use them for financing of contractor working capital, not for the expansion of contractor-owned facilities or the acquisition of fixed assets [@CL-CTF-4265]. Under loan guarantees, exceptions may be made for facilities expansion of a minor or incidental nature, if a relatively small part of the guaranteed loan is used for the expansion and the contractor’s repayment would not be delayed or impaired [@CL-CTF-4266], or for other instances of facilities expansion for which contract financing is appropriate under agency procedures [@CL-CTF-4267]. The limitations in this section do not apply to contracts under which facilities are being acquired for Government ownership [@CL-CTF-4268]. RFO FAR 32.1001 says performance-based payments are the preferred Government financing method when the contracting officer finds them practical, and the contractor agrees to their use [@CL-CTF-4269]; they are contract financing payments that are not payment for accepted items and are fully recoverable, in the same manner as progress payments, in the event of default [@CL-CTF-4270]; they are not subject to the interest-penalty provisions of prompt payment, and these payments must be made in accordance with agency policy [@CL-CTF-4271]; and they must not be used for payments under cost-reimbursement line items [@CL-CTF-4272], for contracts for architect-engineer services or construction, or for shipbuilding or ship conversion, alteration, or repair, when the contracts provide for progress payments based upon a percentage or stage of completion, or for contracts awarded through sealed bid procedures [@CL-CTF-4273].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A construction contract has an approved estimated amount of 1,000,000. Progress in one period is unsatisfactory, and the contracting officer, judging past performance and whether it will continue, retains 10 percent of that period's 400,000 progress payment, which is 40,000 [@CL-CTF-3156; @CL-CTF-3056]. The 40,000 held is the most the clause allows from this payment, which is 10 percent of the 400,000 payment [@CL-CTF-2010]. It is also within the overall limit of 100,000, which is 10 percent of the approved estimated amount [@CL-CTF-3058]. Performance improves as the contract nears completion, so the officer may reduce the amount held [@CL-CTF-3058]. When all contract requirements are complete, the retained amounts are paid promptly [@CL-CTF-3059]. On a separate contract awarded through sealed bid procedures, performance-based payments cannot be used [@CL-CTF-3160].

## Never confuse

Retainage may be withheld when satisfactory progress has not been achieved [@CL-CTF-3156]; the contracting officer should not withhold funds without cause [@CL-CTF-3057]; and the amount of retainage withheld shall not exceed 10 percent of the approved estimated amount [@CL-CTF-3058]. Performance-based payments are contract financing payments that are not payment for accepted items [@CL-CTF-3064]. The [performance-based payment criteria](ctf-pbp-criteria.md), the [financing overview](ctf-financing-overview.md) and the [construction payments](../billing-payment/ctf-payments-fixed-price-construction-and-common-carriers.md) cards take the mechanics further.

The two texts word the retainage trigger and adjustment factor differently: the codified section allows retainage when satisfactory progress has not been achieved and lists better than expected performance as an adjustment factor [@CL-CTF-3156; @CL-CTF-3058], while the RFO section allows it when there is unsatisfactory progress by a contractor and lists improved performance [@CL-CTF-4260; @CL-CTF-4263].
