## Purpose

Sets the limits on Government financing of contractor work: what may be held back from a construction progress payment, what contract financing may be spent on, and when performance-based payments are the preferred method.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CTF-3056] |
| Codified citation | FAR 32.103, FAR 32.105 and FAR 32.1001 [@CL-CTF-3056; @CL-CTF-3060; @CL-CTF-3063] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CTF-3056] |
| Applies to | Construction progress payments, the contract financing methods covered by the same FAR part, and performance-based payments [@CL-CTF-3156; @CL-CTF-3060; @CL-CTF-3063] |

On a construction contract, when satisfactory progress has not been achieved by the contractor during a period for which a progress payment is to be made, a percentage of the progress payment may be retained [@CL-CTF-3156]. The contracting officer decides whether to retain and how much, case by case, based on the officer's assessment of past performance and the likelihood that it will continue [@CL-CTF-3056]. Retainage is not a substitute for good contract management, and the contracting officer should not withhold funds without cause [@CL-CTF-3057]. The amount withheld may not exceed 10 percent of the approved estimated amount under the terms of the contract, and it may be adjusted as the contract approaches completion to recognize better than expected performance, the ability to rely on alternative safeguards, and other factors [@CL-CTF-3058]. Under the payment clause at FAR 52.232-5, the contracting officer may retain a maximum of 10 percent of the amount of each payment until satisfactory progress is achieved [@CL-CTF-2010]. When all contract requirements are complete, retained amounts are paid promptly [@CL-CTF-3059].

The contract financing methods covered by FAR part 32 are intended to be self-liquidating through contract performance, so agencies use them only to finance contractor working capital and not to expand contractor-owned facilities or acquire fixed assets [@CL-CTF-3060]. Under loan guarantees, exceptions may be made for minor or incidental facilities expansion, if a relatively small part of the guaranteed loan is used for the expansion and the contractor's repayment would not be delayed or impaired [@CL-CTF-3061], and for other instances of facilities expansion for which contract financing is appropriate under agency procedures [@CL-CTF-3157]. The limitations in FAR 32.105 do not apply to contracts under which facilities are being acquired for Government ownership [@CL-CTF-3062].

Performance-based payments are the preferred Government financing method when the contracting officer finds them practical and the contractor agrees to their use [@CL-CTF-3063]. They are contract financing payments, not payment for accepted items, and they are fully recoverable in the same manner as progress payments if the contractor defaults [@CL-CTF-3064]. They are not subject to the prompt payment interest-penalty provisions [@CL-CTF-3065], and they are made in accordance with agency policy [@CL-CTF-3158]. They may not be used for payments under cost-reimbursement line items [@CL-CTF-3066]. They may not be used for contracts for architect-engineer services or construction, or for shipbuilding or ship conversion, alteration, or repair, when the contracts provide for progress payments based upon a percentage or stage of completion [@CL-CTF-3159]. They may not be used for contracts awarded through sealed bid procedures [@CL-CTF-3160].

## How it works

Retainage responds to unsatisfactory progress on a construction contract. The contracting officer decides each case on the contractor's past performance and the likelihood that it continues, and the ceiling and the prompt release at completion limit how much is held and for how long [@CL-CTF-3056; @CL-CTF-3058; @CL-CTF-3059]. Financing methods may not fund facilities expansion or fixed assets, except where the loan guarantee exceptions, agency procedures or Government ownership of the facilities apply [@CL-CTF-3060; @CL-CTF-3061; @CL-CTF-3157; @CL-CTF-3062]. A performance-based payment is financing rather than payment for accepted items, so it is recoverable if the contractor defaults and sits outside the prompt payment interest penalty [@CL-CTF-3064; @CL-CTF-3065].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A construction contract has an approved estimated amount of 1,000,000. Progress in one period is unsatisfactory, and the contracting officer, judging past performance and whether it will continue, retains 10 percent of that period's 400,000 progress payment, which is 40,000 [@CL-CTF-3156; @CL-CTF-3056]. The 40,000 held is the most the clause allows from this payment, which is 10 percent of the 400,000 payment [@CL-CTF-2010]. It is also within the overall limit of 100,000, which is 10 percent of the approved estimated amount [@CL-CTF-3058]. Performance improves as the contract nears completion, so the officer may reduce the amount held [@CL-CTF-3058]. When all contract requirements are complete, the retained amounts are paid promptly [@CL-CTF-3059]. On a separate contract awarded through sealed bid procedures, performance-based payments cannot be used [@CL-CTF-3160].

## Never confuse

Retainage is not a general right to hold back money. It applies when satisfactory progress has not been achieved, the contracting officer should not withhold funds without cause, and the amount is capped against the approved estimated amount [@CL-CTF-3156; @CL-CTF-3057; @CL-CTF-3058]. A performance-based payment is also not payment for accepted items, so it differs from payment for delivered work [@CL-CTF-3064].
