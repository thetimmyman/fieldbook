## Purpose

Covers the rules around a guaranteed loan: surety bonds on bonded contracts, borrowing outside the guarantee, and guarantees for terminated contracts.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CTF-3080] |
| Codified citation | FAR 32.304-7, FAR 32.304-8 and FAR 32.305 [@CL-CTF-3080; @CL-CTF-3083; @CL-CTF-3085] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 32.304 and RFO FAR 32.305 are marked reserved [@CL-CTF-4529; @CL-CTF-4530] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CTF-3080] |
| Applies to | Guaranteed loans on bonded contracts, guaranteed loans with other borrowing, and guarantees for terminated contracts [@CL-CTF-3081; @CL-CTF-3083; @CL-CTF-3085] |

Contract surety bonds are incompatible with the Government's interests under guaranteed loans, unless the interests of the surety are subordinated to the guaranteed loan [@CL-CTF-3080]. Where a substantial share of the contractor's defense contracts are covered by surety bonds, or the amount of the bond is substantial in relation to the contractor's net worth, the agency shall not authorize the guarantee of a loan on a bonded contract unless the surety enters into an agreement [@CL-CTF-3081]. That agreement is one with the financing institution to subordinate the surety's rights and claims in favor of the guaranteed loan [@CL-CTF-3176].

The agency's approval of a guarantee for a loan involving relatively substantial subcontracts covered by surety bonds also depends on a reasonable allocation agreement between the sureties and the financing institution [@CL-CTF-3082]. That agreement should give the financing institution the benefit, for payments to be made on the contract, of the portion of its loans fairly attributable to expenditures made under the bonded subcontracts before notice of default [@CL-CTF-3177].

The Federal Acquisition Regulation recognizes that prohibiting borrowings outside the guaranteed loan is preferable when practicable in a given case, but that such other borrowings should be permitted when necessary [@CL-CTF-3083]. If the agency consents to other borrowing during the guaranteed loan period, it applies restrictions that include a reasonable limit on the amount of other borrowing [@CL-CTF-3178]. It also requires, where the same financing institution makes the guaranteed and unguaranteed loans, that any collateral security the institution requests under the unguaranteed loan is also to be secondary collateral for the guaranteed loan [@CL-CTF-3179]. It also requires that the contractor provide appropriate documentation to the guaranteeing agency, at intervals not longer than 30 days, to disclose outstanding unguaranteed borrowings [@CL-CTF-3084].

The purpose of guaranteed loans, financing based on the borrower's recoverable investment in defense production contracts, may also apply to contracts that have been terminated, partially or totally, for the convenience of the Government [@CL-CTF-3085]. Guaranteed loans also may be made before termination if it is known that termination of particular contracts for the convenience of the Government is about to occur, and they are expected to provide necessary financing pending termination settlements and payments [@CL-CTF-3180]. They may also finance continuing performance of defense production contracts that are eligible for guaranteed loans [@CL-CTF-3181]. The procedure is substantially the same as the ordinary guarantee procedure, except that certificates of eligibility are not required for contracts that have been totally terminated or for the terminated portion of contracts that have been partially terminated [@CL-CTF-3086]. The agency takes the precautions necessary to avoid Government losses and to ensure that the loans will be self-liquidating from the proceeds of defense production contracts [@CL-CTF-3182]. Loan guarantees for contract termination financing are not provided before specific contract terminations are certain [@CL-CTF-3183].

## How it works

Where bonds are substantial, the agency conditions authorization on the surety's agreement with the lender to subordinate its rights and claims, and where substantial subcontracts are bonded it also conditions approval on a reasonable allocation agreement [@CL-CTF-3081; @CL-CTF-3176; @CL-CTF-3082]. If the agency consents to other borrowing, it applies a reasonable limit, secondary collateral where the same institution lends, and documentation to the guaranteeing agency at intervals not longer than 30 days [@CL-CTF-3178; @CL-CTF-3179; @CL-CTF-3084]. For terminated work, the guarantee follows the borrower's recoverable investment and needs no certificate of eligibility for a totally terminated contract or the terminated portion of a partially terminated one, and it is not provided before specific terminations are certain [@CL-CTF-3085; @CL-CTF-3086; @CL-CTF-3183].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 32 text in place of the codified text from February 1, 2026 [@CL-CTF-4046]. RFO FAR 32.304, the number under which the codified text prints the surety bond rules and the rules on other borrowing, is marked reserved [@CL-CTF-3080; @CL-CTF-3083; @CL-CTF-4529]. RFO FAR 32.305, the number of the codified section on guarantees for terminated contracts, is also marked reserved [@CL-CTF-3085; @CL-CTF-4530].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A substantial share of a contractor's defense contracts are covered by surety bonds, so the agency does not authorize the guarantee of a loan on a bonded contract until the surety enters into an agreement with the lender to subordinate its rights and claims to the guaranteed loan [@CL-CTF-3081; @CL-CTF-3176]. The contractor later asks to borrow 400,000 outside the guarantee, and the agency consents with a reasonable limit of 250,000, so the contractor may borrow no more than that [@CL-CTF-3178]. If both loans come from the same lender, any collateral the lender requests for the unguaranteed loan must also be secondary collateral for the guaranteed loan [@CL-CTF-3179]. The contractor sends documentation to the guaranteeing agency at intervals not longer than 30 days, so a 45-day gap would not meet the requirement [@CL-CTF-3084]. When a contract is later terminated in total for the convenience of the Government and the termination is certain, a guarantee for that work does not need a certificate of eligibility [@CL-CTF-3086; @CL-CTF-3183]. This example uses the codified text only, and RFO FAR 32.304 and RFO FAR 32.305 are marked reserved [@CL-CTF-4529; @CL-CTF-4530].

## Never confuse

Subordination alone does not complete the conditions for substantial bonded subcontracts, because the agency's approval also depends on a reasonable allocation agreement [@CL-CTF-3081; @CL-CTF-3082]. A guarantee for a terminated contract is not a termination settlement. The loans are expected to provide necessary financing pending termination settlements and payments [@CL-CTF-3180]. The text allows loans before termination where termination is known to be about to occur [@CL-CTF-3180], but bars termination-financing guarantees before specific terminations are certain [@CL-CTF-3183]; this card does not reconcile the two. The [defense production loan guarantees](ctf-defense-production-loan-guarantees.md) card covers the rest of the regime.

Where a substantial share of the contractor's defense contracts are covered by surety bonds, or the amount of the bond is substantial in relation to the contractor's net worth, the codified text bars authorizing a guarantee on a bonded contract unless the surety enters into an agreement with the financing institution to subordinate its rights and claims [@CL-CTF-3081; @CL-CTF-3176], and it says guaranteed loans may also apply to contracts terminated, partially or totally, for the convenience of the Government [@CL-CTF-3085], but RFO FAR 32.304 and RFO FAR 32.305, the numbers of those codified sections, are marked reserved [@CL-CTF-4529; @CL-CTF-4530].
