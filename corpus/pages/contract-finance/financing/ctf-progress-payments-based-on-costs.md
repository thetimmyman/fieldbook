## Purpose

Shows what makes a progress payment based on costs customary or unusual, which protective terms a contracting officer may add, and when the contract finance office must approve before the contracting officer acts.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CTF-3548] |
| Codified citation | FAR 32.501, FAR 32.501-5 and FAR 32.502-2 [@CL-CTF-3548; @CL-CTF-3550; @CL-CTF-3553] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 32.501 [@CL-CTF-4457; @CL-CTF-4458; @CL-CTF-4459], RFO FAR 32.501-1 [@CL-CTF-4460; @CL-CTF-4461; @CL-CTF-4462; @CL-CTF-4463], RFO FAR 32.501-2 [@CL-CTF-4464; @CL-CTF-4465], RFO FAR 32.501-5 [@CL-CTF-4466; @CL-CTF-4467; @CL-CTF-4468] and RFO FAR 32.502-2 [@CL-CTF-4469; @CL-CTF-4470; @CL-CTF-4471; @CL-CTF-4472] |

| Applies to | Progress payments based on costs [@CL-CTF-3548] |

Progress payments may be customary or unusual, and customary progress payments are those made under the general guidance in the subpart, using the customary progress payment rate, the cost base, and frequency of payment established in the Progress Payments clause [@CL-CTF-3548]. Customary progress payments also use either the ordinary liquidation method or the alternate method as provided in the liquidation sections [@CL-CTF-3549]. Any other progress payments are considered unusual, and may be used only in exceptional cases when authorized in accordance with the unusual progress payment section [@CL-CTF-3612].

The customary progress payment rate is 80 percent, applicable to the total costs of performing the contract, and the customary rate for contracts with small business concerns is 85 percent [@CL-CTF-0950; @CL-CTF-0951]. The contracting officer must consider any rate higher than those customary rates an unusual progress payment and must not include a higher rate in a contract unless advance agency approval is obtained under the unusual progress payment section [@CL-CTF-1052].

The contracting officer may provide unusual progress payments only if all of the listed conditions are met [@CL-CTF-0953; @CL-CTF-9600]. One is that the contract necessitates predelivery expenditures that are large in relation to contract price and in relation to the contractor's working capital and credit [@CL-CTF-0953]. Another is that the contractor fully documents an actual need to supplement any private financing available, including guaranteed loans [@CL-CTF-1050]. A third is that the contractor's request is approved by the head of the contracting activity or a designee [@CL-CTF-1051].

If the contracting officer considers it necessary for protection of the Government's interest, protective terms such as the listed ones may be used in addition to the Progress Payments clause of the contract [@CL-CTF-3550]. The listed terms include personal or corporate guarantees, subordinations or standbys of indebtedness, special bank accounts, and protective covenants of the kinds in the paragraph on protective covenants in the Advance Payments clause [@CL-CTF-3551]. Another listed term is a provision, included in the solicitation and resultant contract when first article testing is required, limiting progress payments on first article work by a stated amount or percentage [@CL-CTF-3552].

The contracting officer shall obtain the approval of the contract finance office or other offices designated under agency procedures before taking any of the listed actions [@CL-CTF-3553]. Those actions include providing a progress payment rate higher than the customary rate and deviating from the progress payments terms prescribed in the part [@CL-CTF-3554]. They also include providing progress payments to a contractor whose financial condition is in doubt, or who has had an advance payment request or loan guarantee denied for financial reasons, or approved but withdrawn or lapsed, within the previous 12 months [@CL-CTF-3555]. They further include providing progress payments to a contractor who is named in the consolidated list of contractors indebted to the United States, known commonly as the Hold-up List [@CL-CTF-3613].

## How it works

The customary route follows the rate, cost base, frequency and liquidation methods built into the Progress Payments clause [@CL-CTF-3548; @CL-CTF-3549]. Any other progress payments are unusual and may be used only in exceptional cases when authorized under the unusual progress payment section, which requires among its conditions that the contractor's request is approved by the head of the contracting activity or a designee [@CL-CTF-3612; @CL-CTF-1051].

If the contracting officer considers it necessary for protection of the Government's interest, protective terms such as personal or corporate guarantees, subordinations or standbys of indebtedness, special bank accounts, protective covenants, or a first article limitation provision may be used in addition to the Progress Payments clause of the contract [@CL-CTF-3550; @CL-CTF-3551; @CL-CTF-3552].

The contracting officer shall obtain the approval of the contract finance office or other offices designated under agency procedures before a rate above the customary rate is provided, a deviation from the prescribed terms, or progress payments to a contractor in one of the listed situations [@CL-CTF-3553; @CL-CTF-3554; @CL-CTF-3555; @CL-CTF-3613]. Providing a progress payment rate higher than the customary rate requires advance agency approval under FAR 32.501-2, including approval of the contractor's request by the head of the contracting activity or a designee, and approval of the contract finance office or other offices designated under agency procedures under FAR 32.502-2 [@CL-CTF-1052; @CL-CTF-1051; @CL-CTF-3553; @CL-CTF-3554].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 32 text in place of the codified text from February 1, 2026 [@CL-CTF-4046]. RFO FAR 32.501 keeps the split between customary and unusual progress payments: customary progress payments are those made under the general guidance in the subpart, using the customary progress payment rate, the cost base, and frequency of payment established in the Progress Payments clause [@CL-CTF-4457], and either the ordinary liquidation method or the alternate method [@CL-CTF-4458], and any other progress payments are considered unusual and may be used only in exceptional cases when authorized in accordance with section 32.501-2 [@CL-CTF-4459]. RFO FAR 32.501-1 keeps the 80 percent customary rate on total costs and the 85 percent rate for small business concerns [@CL-CTF-4460], the rule that the contracting officer must consider any higher rate an unusual progress payment and must not include it unless advance agency approval is obtained as prescribed in 32.501-2 [@CL-CTF-4461], the bar on a rate higher than the customary rate when advance payments and progress payments are authorized under the same contract [@CL-CTF-4462], and the 80 percent limit on work accomplished under undefinitized contract actions, with no higher rate under unusual or other customary progress payments for those actions [@CL-CTF-4463]. RFO FAR 32.501-2 keeps the rule that unusual progress payments may be provided only if the listed conditions are met, the first being that the contract necessitates predelivery expenditures that are large in relation to contract price and in relation to the contractor's working capital and credit [@CL-CTF-4464]; the conditions also include full documentation of an actual need to supplement private financing, including guaranteed loans, and approval of the request by the head of the contracting activity or a designee [@CL-CTF-4465]. RFO FAR 32.501-5 keeps the protective terms that may be used if the contracting officer considers it necessary for protection of the Government's interest [@CL-CTF-4466], including guarantees, subordinations or standbys, special bank accounts and protective covenants [@CL-CTF-4467], and the first article limit [@CL-CTF-4468]. RFO FAR 32.502-2 keeps the finance office approval [@CL-CTF-4469] before a rate higher than the customary rate or a deviation from the prescribed progress payments terms [@CL-CTF-4470], before progress payments to a contractor whose financial condition is in doubt or who has had an advance payment request or loan guarantee denied for financial reasons, or approved but withdrawn or lapsed, within the previous 12 months [@CL-CTF-4471], and before progress payments to a contractor named in the consolidated list of contractors indebted to the United States, known commonly as the Hold-up List [@CL-CTF-4472].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor that is not a small business concern asks for progress payments at 90 percent on a contract. The customary rate is 80 percent, so the higher rate is an unusual progress payment [@CL-CTF-0950; @CL-CTF-1052]. The contract is definitive and has no advance payments, so the bars on a higher rate for undefinitized actions and on a higher rate alongside advance payments do not apply [@CL-CTF-1054; @CL-CTF-1055]. The contracting officer must not include the higher rate unless advance agency approval is obtained, and unusual progress payments may be provided only if the other listed conditions are met, including approval of the contractor's request by the head of the contracting activity or a designee [@CL-CTF-1052; @CL-CTF-0953; @CL-CTF-1050; @CL-CTF-1051]. The contracting officer also obtains the approval of the contract finance office before providing the higher rate [@CL-CTF-3553; @CL-CTF-3554]. If the contract requires first article testing and the contracting officer considers it necessary for protection of the Government's interest, the contracting officer may include in the solicitation and contract a term limiting progress payments on first article work by a stated amount [@CL-CTF-3552]. Had the contractor been named on the Hold-up List, finance office approval would be needed even at the customary rate [@CL-CTF-3553; @CL-CTF-3613].

## Never confuse

Customary progress payments are those made under the general guidance in FAR Subpart 32.5 using the customary progress payment rate, the cost base, and frequency of payment established in the Progress Payments clause and either the ordinary or alternate liquidation method [@CL-CTF-3548; @CL-CTF-3549], whereas any other progress payments differ in kind rather than in size: they are considered unusual and may be used only in exceptional cases when authorized in accordance with FAR 32.501-2 [@CL-CTF-3612].

Protective terms differ from the listed finance-office actions rather than falling under them: protective terms may be used in addition to the Progress Payments clause if the contracting officer considers it necessary for protection of the Government's interest [@CL-CTF-3550], while the actions listed in FAR 32.502-2 require the approval of the contract finance office or other offices designated under agency procedures rather than the contracting officer alone [@CL-CTF-3553], and unusual progress payments instead require that the contractor's request be approved by the head of the contracting activity or a designee [@CL-CTF-1051].
