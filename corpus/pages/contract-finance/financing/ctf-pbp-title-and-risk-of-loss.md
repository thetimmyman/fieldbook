## Purpose

Addresses Government title to property under performance-based payments, the protection of that title, and the contractor's risk of loss.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CTF-0978] |
| Codified citation | FAR 32.1009 and 32.1010 [@CL-CTF-0978; @CL-CTF-0980] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 32.1009 [@CL-CTF-4387; @CL-CTF-4388; @CL-CTF-4389; @CL-CTF-4390] and RFO FAR 32.1010 [@CL-CTF-4391; @CL-CTF-4392; @CL-CTF-4393] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CTF-0978] |
| Applies to | Property described in paragraph (f) of the Performance-Based Payments clause [@CL-CTF-0978] |

Since the Performance-Based Payments clause gives the Government title to the property described in paragraph (f) of the clause, the contracting officer shall ensure that the Government title is not compromised by other encumbrances [@CL-CTF-0978]. Ordinarily, in the absence of reason to believe otherwise, the contracting officer may rely upon the contractor's certification contained in the payment request [@CL-CTF-1086]. If the contracting officer becomes aware of any arrangement or condition that would impair the Government's title to the property affected by the clause, the contracting officer shall require additional protective provisions [@CL-CTF-0979].

Except for normal spoilage, the contractor bears the risk of loss for Government property under the clause, even though title is vested in the Government, unless the Government has expressly assumed this risk [@CL-CTF-0980]. If a loss occurs in connection with property for which the contractor bears the risk, and the property is needed for performance, the contractor is obligated to repay the Government the performance-based payments related to the property [@CL-CTF-0981].

## How it works

The existence of any such encumbrance on the Government's title is a violation of the contractor's obligations under the contract, and the contracting officer may, if necessary, suspend or reduce payments under the terms of the Performance-Based Payments clause covering failure to comply with a material requirement of the contract [@CL-CTF-1087; @CL-CTF-1088]. If the contractor fails to disclose an existing encumbrance in the certification, the contracting officer should consult with legal counsel concerning possible violation of section 3729 of title 31 of the United States Code, the False Claims Act [@CL-CTF-1089].

The contractor is not obligated to pay for the loss of property for which the Government has assumed the risk of loss [@CL-CTF-1091], but a serious loss may impede the satisfactory progress of contract performance, so that the contracting officer may need to act under paragraph (e)(2) of the Performance-Based Payments clause [@CL-CTF-9491], and, although the contractor is not required to repay previous performance-based payments in the event of a loss for which the Government has assumed the risk, such a loss may prevent the contractor from making the certification required by that clause [@CL-CTF-9492]. Related title and responsibility topics are on [Government title to progress payment inventory](../../close-revrec/inventory-wip/cls-government-title-progress-payment-inventory.md) and [Payments and responsibility for supplies](../../close-revrec/revenue/cls-payments-and-responsibility-for-supplies.md), and the financing context is on [Performance-based payments](ctf-performance-based-payments.md).

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 32 text in place of the codified text from February 1, 2026 [@CL-CTF-4046]. RFO FAR 32.1009 keeps the contracting officer's duty to ensure that the Government title is not compromised by other encumbrances [@CL-CTF-4387], and keeps the rule that the contracting officer may ordinarily rely upon the contractor's certification in the absence of reason to believe otherwise [@CL-CTF-4388]. It also keeps the duty to require additional protective provisions when the contracting officer becomes aware of an arrangement or condition that would impair the title [@CL-CTF-4389] and the right to suspend or reduce payments for an encumbrance [@CL-CTF-4390]. RFO FAR 32.1010 keeps the contractor's risk of loss, except for normal spoilage and unless the Government has expressly assumed it [@CL-CTF-4391], the duty to repay the related performance-based payments for needed property [@CL-CTF-4392] and the rule that the contractor is not obligated to pay for a loss the Government has assumed [@CL-CTF-4393], with the qualifications that a serious loss may impede the satisfactory progress of contract performance, so that the contracting officer may need to act under paragraph (e)(2) of the Performance-Based Payments clause [@CL-CTF-9498], and that such a loss may prevent the contractor from making the certification required by that clause even though the contractor is not required to repay previous performance-based payments [@CL-CTF-9499].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor has received 300,000 in performance-based payments, and the Government holds title to the covered parts. The Government has not expressly assumed the risk of loss. A warehouse fire destroys parts that are needed for performance, and 100,000 of the payments relate to those parts. The contractor must repay the Government that 100,000.

## Never confuse

Except for normal spoilage, the contractor bears the risk of loss for Government property under the Performance-Based Payments clause even though title is vested in the Government, unless the Government has expressly assumed this risk [@CL-CTF-0980]. The clauses prescribed for performance-based payments, default and terminations do not constitute a Government assumption of risk [@CL-CTF-1090].
