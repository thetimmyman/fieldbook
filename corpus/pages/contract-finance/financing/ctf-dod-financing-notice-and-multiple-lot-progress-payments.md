## Purpose

Shows what a DoD solicitation tells offerors about progress payments and performance-based payments, and how the multiple-lots clause splits progress payment requests and liquidation by lot.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-CTF-1734; @CL-CTF-1740] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 252.232-7016 and DFARS 252.232-7018 [@CL-CTF-1734; @CL-CTF-1740] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CTF-1734; @CL-CTF-1740] |
| Applies to | Solicitations that contain clauses for both progress payments and performance-based payments, and contracts where separate progress payment requests are authorized for multiple lots [@CL-CTF-1784; @CL-CTF-1786] |

DFARS directs use of the notice provision in lieu of the FAR notice of progress payments provision when the solicitation contains clauses for progress payments and performance-based payments [@CL-CTF-1784]. The notice tells offerors that the resulting contract will include either performance-based payments or progress payments and not both, except as may be authorized on separate orders subject to FAR 32.1003(c) [@CL-CTF-1734]. The performance-based payments clauses are included if all of the listed conditions are met [@CL-CTF-1735; @CL-CTF-1736]. One listed condition is a positive representation from the offeror in response to the DFARS representation provision [@CL-CTF-1735]. A further listed condition is that the offeror proposes an arrangement in accordance with the FAR invitation to propose performance-based payments, including proposed events and timing, event completion criteria, event values and an expected expenditure profile [@CL-CTF-1735; @CL-CTF-1736]. Another listed condition is agreement between the offeror and the Government on all aspects of the arrangement [@CL-CTF-1735; @CL-CTF-1737].

If the performance-based payments clauses are not included, the progress payments clause in the solicitation is included in the resulting contract, modified or altered if necessary in accordance with FAR 52.232-16 and its Alternate I [@CL-CTF-1738]. Even though the progress payments clause is included in the contract, the clause shall be inoperative during any time the contractor's accounting system and controls are determined by the Government to be inadequate for segregation and accumulation of contract costs [@CL-CTF-1739]. DFARS directs use of the multiple-lots clause to authorize separate progress payment requests for multiple lots [@CL-CTF-1786].

## How it works

A lot is one or more fixed-price deliverable line items or deliverable subline items representing a single, severable group where the sum of the costs for each group is segregated and a single progress payment rate is used [@CL-CTF-1740]. Multiple lots means more than one lot on a single contract where progress payment proration is performed on a lot-wide, versus contract-wide, basis [@CL-CTF-1741]. When submitting progress payment requests under the billing instructions in FAR clause 52.232-16 or DFARS clause 252.232-7002 of this contract [@CL-CTF-1787; @CL-CTF-1742], the contractor shall, among other duties, submit separate progress payment requests for each lot identified in the contract [@CL-CTF-1742]. The contractor shall calculate each request on the basis of the price, costs (including the cost to complete), subcontractor financing, and progress payment liquidations of the lot to which it applies [@CL-CTF-1743]. The contractor shall also distribute costs among lots in a manner acceptable to the Administrative Contracting Officer [@CL-CTF-1744].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contract has two lots, each a group of fixed-price line items with its own price and progress payment rate. The contractor sends one progress payment request for lot one and a separate request for lot two. Each request uses only the price, costs, subcontractor financing and liquidations of its own lot. Answer: a payment made on lot one is liquidated against lot one, not against lot two.

## Never confuse

A lot is one or more fixed-price deliverable line items or deliverable subline items representing a single, severable group [@CL-CTF-1740].
