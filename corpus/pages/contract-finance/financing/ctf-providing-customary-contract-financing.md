## Purpose

Shows what the contracting officer weighs before offering contract financing, which financing the FAR calls customary, and why any other arrangement is unusual.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CTF-1407; @CL-CTF-1411; @CL-CTF-1410] |
| Codified citation | FAR 32.104, FAR 32.113 and FAR 32.114 [@CL-CTF-1407; @CL-CTF-1411; @CL-CTF-1410] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CTF-1407] |
| Applies to | Solicitations and contracts where contract financing is considered [@CL-CTF-1407] |

Contracting officers must consider the criteria in FAR part 32 in determining whether to include contract financing in solicitations and contracts, and must resolve reasonable doubts by including contract financing in the solicitation [@CL-CTF-1407]. The contracting officer must provide Government financing only to the extent actually needed for prompt and efficient performance, considering the availability of private financing and the probable impact on working capital of the predelivery expenditures and production lead-times [@CL-CTF-1408].

Subject to specific agency regulations and paragraph (d) of that section, the contracting officer may provide customary contract financing and must not provide unusual contract financing except as authorized [@CL-CTF-1409]. Unless otherwise authorized by agency procedures, the contracting officer may provide contract financing in the form of performance-based payments or customary progress payments if the conditions listed in paragraph (d) are met [@CL-CTF-1455]. One of the listed alternatives for a contractor that is not a small business concern is that, for an individual contract, the contract price is $3.5 million or more [@CL-CTF-1456]. One of the listed alternatives for a contractor that is a small business concern is that, for an individual contract, the contract price exceeds the simplified acquisition threshold [@CL-CTF-1457]. Paragraph (d) lists further conditions, on the contractor's billing delay and financial need and on indefinite-delivery contracts, that this card does not restate.

Unusual contract financing is any arrangement that deviates from FAR part 32, and it shall be authorized only after approval by the head of the agency or as provided for in agency regulations [@CL-CTF-1410].

## How it works

The solicitation must specify the customary contract financing offerors may propose, and the forms listed in FAR 32.113 are customary when provided in accordance with FAR part 32 and agency regulations [@CL-CTF-1411]. One listed form, for supplies or services awarded under the competitive negotiation method in FAR part 15, is either progress payments based on costs or performance-based payments, but not both [@CL-CTF-1412]. The same section lists other customary forms that this card does not restate. See [Contract financing at a glance](ctf-financing-overview.md) and, for financing through loan guarantees and advance payments, [Loan guarantees for defense production](ctf-defense-production-loan-guarantees.md) and [Advance payments for non-commercial items](ctf-advance-payments-for-non-commercial-items.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A competitively negotiated supply contract of 5,000,000 is being solicited. The solicitation names progress payments based on costs as the customary financing offerors may propose. An offeror asks to be paid under both progress payments and performance-based payments on the same contract.

Answer: for this kind of award the listed customary choice is one or the other, not both. A financing arrangement that departs from FAR part 32 is unusual and needs approval of the head of the agency or authority in agency regulations.

## Never confuse

Customary financing is a form that the FAR lists as customary when provided under FAR part 32 and agency regulations [@CL-CTF-1411], while unusual financing is any arrangement that deviates from FAR part 32 [@CL-CTF-1410]. The section on unusual financing lists no forms; it defines them only as deviations [@CL-CTF-1410]. Progress payments and performance-based payments are separate choices; see [Customary progress payment rates (DoD)](ctf-progress-payments.md) and [Performance-based payments](ctf-performance-based-payments.md). For what the Government expects in return for financing, see [Consideration for contract financing and fraud-based reduction or suspension of payments](ctf-financing-consideration-and-fraud-payment-procedures.md).
