## Purpose

Shows what the contracting officer weighs before offering contract financing, which financing the FAR calls customary, and why any other arrangement is unusual.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CTF-1407; @CL-CTF-1411; @CL-CTF-1410] |
| Codified citation | FAR 32.104, FAR 32.113 and FAR 32.114 [@CL-CTF-1407; @CL-CTF-1411; @CL-CTF-1410] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 32.104 [@CL-CTF-4473; @CL-CTF-4474; @CL-CTF-4475; @CL-CTF-4778; @CL-CTF-4476; @CL-CTF-4477; @CL-CTF-4478; @CL-CTF-4479], RFO FAR 32.113 [@CL-CTF-4480; @CL-CTF-4481] and RFO FAR 32.114 [@CL-CTF-4482] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CTF-1407] |
| Applies to | Solicitations and contracts where contract financing is considered [@CL-CTF-1407] |

Contracting officers must consider the criteria in FAR part 32 in determining whether to include contract financing in solicitations and contracts, and is to resolve reasonable doubts by including contract financing in the solicitation [@CL-CTF-1407]. The contracting officer must provide Government financing only to the extent actually needed for prompt and efficient performance, considering the availability of private financing and the probable impact on working capital of the predelivery expenditures and production lead-times associated with the contract, or groups of contracts or orders, such as those issued under indefinite-delivery contracts, basic ordering agreements, or their equivalent [@CL-CTF-1408; @CL-CTF-3227].

Subject to specific agency regulations and paragraph (d) of that section, the contracting officer may provide customary contract financing and must not provide unusual contract financing except as authorized [@CL-CTF-1409]. Unless otherwise authorized by agency procedures, the contracting officer may provide contract financing in the form of performance-based payments or customary progress payments if the conditions listed in paragraph (d) are met [@CL-CTF-1455]. Under condition (1), either the contractor will not be able to bill for the first delivery of products for a substantial time after work must begin (normally 4 months or more for small business concerns, and 6 months or more for others) and will make expenditures for contract performance during the predelivery period that have a significant impact on the contractor's working capital, or the contractor demonstrates actual financial need or the unavailability of private financing [@CL-CTF-3228; @CL-CTF-3229]. Under condition (2), if the contractor is not a small business concern, either for an individual contract the contract price is $3.5 million or more [@CL-CTF-1456], or for an indefinite-delivery contract, a basic ordering agreement or a similar ordering instrument the contracting officer expects the aggregate value of orders or contracts that individually exceed the simplified acquisition threshold to have a total value of $3.5 million or more, and the contracting officer must limit financing to those orders or contracts that exceed the simplified acquisition threshold [@CL-CTF-3230; @CL-CTF-3231]. Under condition (3), if the contractor is a small business concern, either for an individual contract the contract price exceeds the simplified acquisition threshold [@CL-CTF-1457], or for an indefinite-delivery contract, a basic ordering agreement or a similar ordering instrument the contracting officer expects the aggregate value of orders or contracts to exceed the simplified acquisition threshold [@CL-CTF-3232].

Unusual contract financing is any arrangement that deviates from FAR part 32, and it shall be authorized only after approval by the head of the agency or as provided for in agency regulations [@CL-CTF-1410].

## How it works

The solicitation must specify the customary contract financing offerors may propose, and the forms listed in FAR 32.113 are customary when provided in accordance with FAR part 32 and agency regulations [@CL-CTF-1411]. One listed form, for supplies or services awarded under the competitive negotiation method in FAR part 15, is either progress payments based on costs or performance-based payments, but not both [@CL-CTF-1412]. See [Contract financing at a glance](ctf-financing-overview.md) and, for financing through loan guarantees and advance payments, [Loan guarantees for defense production](ctf-defense-production-loan-guarantees.md) and [Advance payments for non-commercial items](ctf-advance-payments-for-non-commercial-items.md).

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 32 text in place of the codified text from February 1, 2026 [@CL-CTF-4046]. RFO FAR 32.104(a) keeps the duty to consider the criteria in the part when determining whether to include contract financing in solicitations and contracts [@CL-CTF-4473] and the limit that Government financing is provided only to the extent actually needed for prompt and efficient performance, considering the availability of private financing and the probable impact on working capital of the predelivery expenditures and production lead-times [@CL-CTF-4475], which are those associated with the contract, or groups of contracts or orders, such as orders issued under indefinite-delivery contracts, basic ordering agreements, or their equivalent [@CL-CTF-4778]. RFO FAR 32.104(c) keeps the rule that, subject to specific agency regulations and paragraph (d), the contracting officer may provide customary contract financing and must not provide unusual contract financing except as authorized in 32.114 [@CL-CTF-4476]. RFO FAR 32.104(d) keeps the $3.5 million alternative for a contractor that is not a small business concern, for an individual contract [@CL-CTF-4478], and the simplified acquisition threshold alternative for a small business concern, for an individual contract [@CL-CTF-4479]. RFO FAR 32.113 keeps the solicitation duty and the list of customary forms, including the choice of either progress payments based on costs or performance-based payments, but not both, for contracts awarded under the competitive negotiation method in part 15 [@CL-CTF-4480; @CL-CTF-4481]. RFO FAR 32.114 keeps the definition of unusual contract financing and the rule that it must be authorized only after approval by the head of the agency or as provided for in agency regulations [@CL-CTF-4482]. In RFO FAR 32.104(a) the contracting officer is told to resolve doubts that lack of financing could affect performance by including contract financing in the solicitation [@CL-CTF-4474]. In RFO FAR 32.104(d) the opening is "Unless prohibited by agency procedures" [@CL-CTF-4477].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A competitively negotiated supply contract of 5,000,000 is being solicited. The solicitation names progress payments based on costs as the customary financing offerors may propose. An offeror asks to be paid under both progress payments and performance-based payments on the same contract.

Answer: for this kind of award the listed customary choice is one or the other, not both. A financing arrangement that departs from FAR part 32 is unusual and needs approval of the head of the agency or authority in agency regulations.

## Never confuse

Customary financing is a form that the FAR lists as customary when provided under FAR part 32 and agency regulations [@CL-CTF-1411], while unusual financing is any arrangement that deviates from FAR part 32 [@CL-CTF-1410]. The section on unusual financing defines unusual contract financing as any arrangement that deviates from FAR part 32 [@CL-CTF-1410]. Progress payments and performance-based payments are separate choices; see [Customary progress payment rates (DoD)](ctf-progress-payments.md) and [Performance-based payments](ctf-performance-based-payments.md). For what the Government expects in return for financing, see [Consideration for contract financing and fraud-based reduction or suspension of payments](ctf-financing-consideration-and-fraud-payment-procedures.md).

The codified text says to resolve reasonable doubts by including contract financing in the solicitation [@CL-CTF-1407], while the RFO text says to resolve doubts that lack of financing could affect performance by including it [@CL-CTF-4474]. The codified text opens paragraph (d) with "Unless otherwise authorized by agency procedures" [@CL-CTF-1455], while the RFO text opens it with "Unless prohibited by agency procedures", and under the RFO text the contracting officer may provide performance-based payments or customary progress payments if the listed conditions are met, unless agency procedures prohibit it [@CL-CTF-4477].
