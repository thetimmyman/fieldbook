## Purpose

Shows who certifies a defense production loan guarantee, who prepares the certificate of eligibility, and what that certificate must state.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CTF-1423; @CL-CTF-1425] |
| Codified citation | FAR 32.303 and FAR 32.304-2 [@CL-CTF-1424; @CL-CTF-1425] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CTF-1423] |
| Applies to | Loan guarantees for contract performance or other operations related to national defense under section 301 of the Defense Production Act [@CL-CTF-1423] |

Section 301 of the Defense Production Act authorizes loan guarantees for contract performance or other operations related to national defense, subject to amounts annually authorized by Congress on the maximum obligation of any guaranteeing agency [@CL-CTF-1423]. The guaranteeing agency is responsible for certifying eligibility for the guarantee and fixing the maximum dollar amount and maturity date of the guaranteed loan [@CL-CTF-1424]. The amount and maturity date are fixed to meet the contractor's requirement for financing performance of the defense production contract on hand at the time the guarantee application is submitted [@CL-CTF-1461].

The contracting officer shall prepare the certificate of eligibility for a contract that the contracting officer deems to be of material consequence, when the circumstances listed in the paragraph apply [@CL-CTF-1462]. The certificate of eligibility shall include the determinations that the supplies or services to be acquired are essential to the national defense and that the contractor has the facilities and the technical and management ability required for contract performance [@CL-CTF-1425]. The third listed determination is the statement that there is no practicable alternate source for the acquisition without prejudice to the national defense, and that statement shall not be included if the contractor is a small business concern [@CL-CTF-1426].

## How it works

The guaranteeing agency certifies eligibility and fixes the maximum dollar amount and maturity date [@CL-CTF-1424]. The contracting officer prepares the certificate of eligibility when the listed circumstances apply to a contract the contracting officer deems of material consequence [@CL-CTF-1462]. The certificate carries the listed determinations [@CL-CTF-1425; @CL-CTF-1426]. This card does not cover how the guarantee agreement is executed.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor with a defense production contract asks its lender for a loan of 2,000,000. The guaranteeing agency certifies eligibility and fixes a maximum amount of 1,500,000 and a maturity date. The certificate for a large business states that the supplies are essential, that the contractor has the facilities and ability, and that there is no practicable alternate source.

Answer: if the contractor were a small business concern, the alternate source statement would be left out and the other two determinations would stay.

## Never confuse

The small business exception removes only the alternate source statement; the essentiality and ability determinations remain required [@CL-CTF-1426; @CL-CTF-1425]. The guarantee authority is also limited by the amounts Congress authorizes each year [@CL-CTF-1423]. The guaranteeing agency certifies eligibility for the guarantee [@CL-CTF-1424], while the certificate of eligibility is prepared by the contracting officer [@CL-CTF-1462]. For the other forms of contract financing, see [Contract financing at a glance](ctf-financing-overview.md) and [Providing contract financing and customary contract financing](ctf-providing-customary-contract-financing.md).
