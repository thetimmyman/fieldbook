## Purpose

Shows the insurance a contractor keeps for work on a Government installation, the notice rule when a policy changes, the insurance kept under the third persons clause, and which third person liabilities the Government reimburses.

## The rule

|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CTF-2497] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.228-5 and FAR 52.228-7 [@CL-CTF-2497; @CL-CTF-2499] |

The Contractor provides and maintains at its own expense, during the entire performance of the contract, at least the kinds and minimum amounts of insurance required in the schedule or elsewhere in the contract [@CL-CTF-2497]. The policies evidencing required insurance shall contain an endorsement to the effect that any cancellation or any material change adversely affecting the Government's interest shall not be effective for the periods that follow [@CL-CTF-2609]. That cancellation or change shall not be effective for the period that the laws of the State in which the contract is to be performed prescribe, or until 30 days after the insurer or the Contractor gives written notice to the Contracting Officer, whichever period is longer [@CL-CTF-2498]. Before commencing work under the contract, the Contractor shall notify the Contracting Officer in writing that the required insurance has been obtained [@CL-CTF-2732]. The Contractor shall insert the substance of the clause in subcontracts that require work on a Government installation and shall require subcontractors to provide and maintain the insurance required in the Schedule or elsewhere in the contract [@CL-CTF-2610]. The Contractor shall maintain a copy of all subcontractors' proofs of required insurance, and shall make copies available to the Contracting Officer upon request [@CL-CTF-2733].

Under the third persons clause, and except as provided in subparagraph (a)(2), the Contractor shall provide and maintain workers' compensation, employer's liability, comprehensive general liability (bodily injury) and comprehensive automobile liability (bodily injury and property damage) insurance [@CL-CTF-2499]. The Contractor shall also provide and maintain such other insurance as the Contracting Officer may require under the contract [@CL-CTF-2734]. The Contractor may, with the approval of the Contracting Officer, maintain a self-insurance program, provided that, with respect to workers' compensation, the Contractor is qualified pursuant to statutory authority [@CL-CTF-2735]. All insurance required by paragraph (a) shall be in a form and amount and for those periods as the Contracting Officer may require or approve and with insurers approved by the Contracting Officer [@CL-CTF-2736]. The Contractor agrees to submit for the Contracting Officer's approval, to the extent and in the manner required by the Contracting Officer, any other insurance that it maintains in connection with performance of the contract and for which it seeks reimbursement [@CL-CTF-2737].

The Contractor shall be reimbursed for that portion of the reasonable cost of insurance allocable to the contract and required or approved under the clause, and for a second item that follows [@CL-CTF-2585]. The second item is certain liabilities, and expenses incidental to them, to third persons not compensated by insurance or otherwise, without regard to and as an exception to the limitation of cost or limitation of funds clause [@CL-CTF-2500]. The reimbursable liabilities must arise out of performance of the contract, whether or not caused by the negligence of the Contractor or of the Contractor's agents, servants, or employees, and must be represented by final judgments or settlements approved in writing by the Government [@CL-CTF-2501]. One kind of reimbursable third person liability is loss of or damage to property, other than property owned, occupied, or used by the Contractor, rented to the Contractor, or in the care, custody, or control of the Contractor [@CL-CTF-2738]. The other kind of reimbursable third person liability is death or bodily injury [@CL-CTF-2739]. The Government's liability under paragraph (c) is subject to the availability of appropriated funds at the time a contingency occurs, and nothing in the contract implies that Congress will later appropriate funds sufficient to meet deficiencies [@CL-CTF-2586].

The Contractor shall not be reimbursed for liabilities, and expenses incidental to them, for which the Contractor is otherwise responsible under the express terms of any clause specified in the Schedule or elsewhere in the contract [@CL-CTF-2587]. Another listed case is a liability for which the Contractor has failed to insure or to maintain insurance as required by the Contracting Officer [@CL-CTF-2588]. A further listed case is liabilities that result from willful misconduct or lack of good faith on the part of any of the Contractor's directors, officers, managers, superintendents, or other representatives who have supervision or direction of all or substantially all of the Contractor's business or of the other operations listed after it [@CL-CTF-2589]. Those representatives also include those who have supervision or direction of all or substantially all of the Contractor's operations at any one plant or separate location in which the contract is being performed, or of a separate and complete major industrial operation in connection with the performance of the contract [@CL-CTF-2590]. The provisions of paragraph (e) shall not restrict the right of the Contractor to be reimbursed for the cost of insurance maintained in connection with the performance of the contract, other than insurance required in accordance with the clause, subject to the proviso that follows [@CL-CTF-2591]. The proviso is that the cost of that other insurance is allowable under the Allowable Cost and Payment clause of the contract [@CL-CTF-2592].

If any suit or action is filed or any claim is made against the Contractor, the cost and expense of which may be reimbursable under the contract and the risk of which is uninsured or insured for less than the amount claimed, the Contractor shall take the actions that the clause then lists [@CL-CTF-2593]. In that case the Contractor shall immediately notify the Contracting Officer and promptly furnish copies of all pertinent papers received [@CL-CTF-2594]. The Contractor shall also authorize Government representatives to collaborate with counsel for the insurance carrier in settling or defending the claim when the amount of the liability claimed exceeds the amount of coverage [@CL-CTF-2595]. The Contractor shall also authorize Government representatives to settle or defend the claim and to represent the Contractor in or to take charge of any litigation, if required by the Government, when the liability is not insured or covered by bond [@CL-CTF-2596]. The Contractor may, at its own expense, be associated with the Government representatives in any such claim or litigation [@CL-CTF-2740].

FAR 2.101 defines insurance as a contract that provides that for a stipulated consideration, one party undertakes to indemnify another against loss, damage, or liability arising from an unknown or contingent event [@CL-CTF-9413]. The RFO model text of FAR 2.101 defines insurance in the same words as the codified definition [@CL-CTF-9444; @CL-CTF-9413].

## How it works

For work on an installation, the schedule or contract sets the kinds and minimum amounts, and the Contractor carries at least those kinds and amounts for the whole period of performance [@CL-CTF-2497]. The policies carry an endorsement so that a cancellation or material change adverse to the Government cannot take effect before the longer of the period the State's laws prescribe or 30 days after written notice to the Contracting Officer, and the Contractor passes the insurance duty to subcontractors on the installation [@CL-CTF-2609; @CL-CTF-2498; @CL-CTF-2610]. The Contractor tells the Contracting Officer in writing before work starts that the insurance is in place, and it keeps copies of subcontractors' proofs of insurance for the Contracting Officer on request [@CL-CTF-2732; @CL-CTF-2733]. Under the third persons clause, the insured risks include the listed liability lines and any other insurance the Contracting Officer requires, and the form, amount, periods and insurers are subject to the Contracting Officer's requirement or approval [@CL-CTF-2499; @CL-CTF-2734; @CL-CTF-2736]. Self-insurance needs the Contracting Officer's approval and, for workers' compensation, statutory qualification, and other insurance for which reimbursement is sought goes to the Contracting Officer for approval [@CL-CTF-2735; @CL-CTF-2737]. Reimbursement of a third person liability not compensated by insurance or otherwise needs a link to performance and a final judgment or a settlement the Government approved in writing, and it is not allowed in the listed excluded cases, and the liability must be for property damage of the listed kind or for death or bodily injury [@CL-CTF-2500; @CL-CTF-2501; @CL-CTF-2738; @CL-CTF-2739; @CL-CTF-2587; @CL-CTF-2588; @CL-CTF-2589]. When a suit or claim could be reimbursable and is uninsured or underinsured, the Contractor notifies the Contracting Officer at once and authorizes Government representatives to collaborate in, settle or defend it as the clause provides, and the Contractor may be associated with them at its own expense [@CL-CTF-2593; @CL-CTF-2594; @CL-CTF-2595; @CL-CTF-2596; @CL-CTF-2740].

The RFO text applies to DoD contracting because Class Deviation 2026-O0038 directs DoD contracting officers to use the revised FAR part 52 in place of the codified text, effective February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. The RFO text of FAR 52.228-5 keeps the duty to carry the required insurance during the entire performance in paragraph (a) [@CL-CTF-0993; @CL-CTF-2497], the written notice before commencing work and the endorsement period in paragraph (b) [@CL-CTF-0994; @CL-CTF-0995; @CL-CTF-0996; @CL-CTF-2732; @CL-CTF-2609; @CL-CTF-2498], and the insertion of the substance of the clause in subcontracts in paragraph (c) [@CL-CTF-0997; @CL-CTF-2610]. It also keeps, in paragraph (c), the duty to maintain a copy of all subcontractors' proofs of required insurance and make copies available to the Contracting Officer upon request [@CL-CTF-9203; @CL-CTF-2733]. The RFO text of FAR 52.228-7 says "paragraph (a)(2) of this clause" in paragraph (a)(1) where the codified text says "subparagraph (a)(2) of this clause", with the same listed lines of insurance [@CL-CTF-0998; @CL-CTF-2499]. It keeps the other insurance that the Contracting Officer may require under the contract [@CL-CTF-9204; @CL-CTF-2734]. It keeps the self-insurance rule in paragraph (a)(2) [@CL-CTF-0999; @CL-CTF-2735], the rule in paragraph (a)(3) that all required insurance is in a form and amount and for those periods as the Contracting Officer may require or approve and with insurers the Contracting Officer approves [@CL-CTF-9205; @CL-CTF-2736], the submission for approval of other insurance for which reimbursement is sought in paragraph (b) [@CL-CTF-9206; @CL-CTF-2737], the reimbursable third person liabilities and their conditions in paragraph (c)(2), including the two kinds of liability, loss of or damage to property other than the listed property of or in the care of the Contractor and death or bodily injury [@CL-CTF-1020; @CL-CTF-1021; @CL-CTF-9207; @CL-CTF-2500; @CL-CTF-2501; @CL-CTF-2738; @CL-CTF-2739], the availability of appropriated funds in paragraph (d) [@CL-CTF-1022; @CL-CTF-2586], and the cases of no reimbursement in paragraphs (e)(1) and (e)(2) [@CL-CTF-1023; @CL-CTF-1024; @CL-CTF-2587; @CL-CTF-2588]. It keeps the case in paragraph (e)(3) of willful misconduct or lack of good faith of the Contractor's directors, officers, managers, superintendents or other representatives who have supervision or direction of its business or the other listed operations [@CL-CTF-9208; @CL-CTF-9209; @CL-CTF-2589; @CL-CTF-2590], and paragraph (f), which leaves the right to be reimbursed for the cost of other insurance that is allowable under the Allowable Cost and Payment clause [@CL-CTF-9210; @CL-CTF-9211; @CL-CTF-2591; @CL-CTF-2592]. It keeps the opening of paragraph (g), which applies when a suit or claim may be reimbursable and the risk is uninsured or insured for less than the amount claimed [@CL-CTF-1025; @CL-CTF-2593], and the three actions in paragraphs (g)(1) to (g)(3), notice and papers, collaboration with the insurance carrier's counsel, and settlement or defense by Government representatives [@CL-CTF-9212; @CL-CTF-9213; @CL-CTF-9214; @CL-CTF-2594; @CL-CTF-2595; @CL-CTF-2596]. It keeps, in paragraph (c)(1), the reimbursement of that portion of the reasonable cost of insurance allocable to the contract and required or approved under the clause [@CL-CTF-9268; @CL-CTF-2585], and, in the last sentence of paragraph (g)(3), the Contractor's right to be associated with the Government representatives at its own expense in any such claim or litigation [@CL-CTF-9269; @CL-CTF-2740]. The RFO text has the same words as the codified text in both places and differs only in print style, with hyphens and capital letters in the paragraph (c)(1) list [@CL-CTF-9268].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

The schedule requires general liability insurance of 1,000,000. The contractor buys it and keeps it for the whole period of performance. The endorsement says a change adverse to the Government takes effect at the later of the State period, here 20 days, or 30 days after written notice to the Contracting Officer. The later date is 30 days. Before work starts the contractor notifies the Contracting Officer in writing that the insurance has been obtained, and it keeps a copy of each subcontractor's proof of insurance and gives copies to the Contracting Officer on request.

A third person wins a final judgment of 400,000 that arose out of performance of the contract, and insurance does not pay it. No excluded case applies, so it meets the conditions stated above and is reimbursable, as an exception to the limitation of cost clause, subject to the availability of appropriated funds at the time the contingency occurs. A second claim is settled for 90,000 without the Government's written approval, so it is not a settlement approved in writing and does not meet the condition. The 400,000 judgment is for injury to a third person, which is a listed kind. A judgment for damage to property in the contractor's own care, custody or control is not property damage of the listed kind. A third liability arose because the contractor let a required policy lapse, so it is a case in which the contractor failed to maintain insurance as the Contracting Officer required, and it is not reimbursed.

A suit is filed against the contractor for 500,000, and its insurance covers 300,000. The cost and expense of the suit may be reimbursable under the contract and the risk is insured for less than the amount claimed, so the contractor notifies the Contracting Officer at once, furnishes copies of the papers received, and authorizes Government representatives to collaborate with the carrier's counsel because the claim exceeds the coverage. The contractor may, at its own expense, be associated with them in the litigation.

The Contracting Officer also requires one more kind of cover, and the contractor buys it from an insurer the Contracting Officer approves. The contractor also carries extra insurance of its own choosing and submits it to the Contracting Officer for approval because it wants reimbursement. A self-insurance program needs the Contracting Officer's approval, and for workers' compensation the contractor must be qualified under statutory authority.

## Never confuse

Reimbursement of a third person liability under the clause is different from allowability of insurance cost under the cost principles; see [Insurance and indemnification](../../compliance/cas-allowability/cmp-insurance-and-indemnification.md). Insurance required for work on an installation is also different from liability to third persons under the other clause, which adds a reimbursement right for certain third person liabilities not compensated by insurance or otherwise [@CL-CTF-2500]. Limits on liability elsewhere are on [Limitation of liability clauses](../../supply-chain/sourcing-evidence/sup-limitation-of-liability-clauses.md).
