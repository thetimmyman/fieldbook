## Purpose

Shows what a contractor must obtain from an individual surety, how long the pledged security is kept for a performance bond, and when security for a bid guarantee is released.

## The rule

| Codified citation | FAR 52.228-11 and FAR 52.228-17 [@CL-CTF-2219; @CL-CTF-2480; @CL-CTF-2518]; FAR 2.101 [@CL-CTF-9425; @CL-CTF-2222; @CL-CTF-2223] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.228-11(a) [@CL-CTF-2220; @CL-CTF-0896]; RFO FAR 52.228-11(b) [@CL-CTF-0897; @CL-CTF-0898; @CL-CTF-9202; @CL-CTF-0899]; RFO FAR 52.228-11(c) [@CL-CTF-0984; @CL-CTF-0985; @CL-CTF-0986; @CL-CTF-0987; @CL-CTF-0988]; RFO FAR 52.228-11(d) [@CL-CTF-0989]; RFO FAR 52.228-17 [@CL-CTF-0990; @CL-CTF-0991; @CL-CTF-0992]; RFO FAR 2.101 [@CL-CTF-9455; @CL-CTF-9483; @CL-CTF-9484] |

| Text status | Final [@CL-CTF-2480] |
| Applies to | Contracts with an individual surety on a performance or payment bond, and offers with an individual surety on a bid guarantee [@CL-CTF-2219; @CL-CTF-2480; @CL-CTF-2518] |

The Contractor shall obtain from each person acting as an individual surety on a performance bond or a payment bond (1) a pledge of assets that meets the eligibility, valuation, and security requirements described in the Federal Acquisition Regulation (FAR) 28.203-1, and (2) Standard Form 28, Affidavit of Individual Surety [@CL-CTF-2219; @CL-CTF-2480].

FAR 2.101 defines a surety as an individual or corporation legally liable for the debt, default, or failure of a principal to satisfy a contractual obligation [@CL-CTF-9425]. The types of sureties referred to are as follows: (1) an individual surety is one person, as distinguished from a business entity, who is liable for the entire penal amount of the bond, (2) a corporate surety is licensed under various insurance laws and, under its charter, has legal power to act as surety for others, and (3) a cosurety is one of two or more sureties that are jointly liable for the penal sum of the bond, and a limit of liability for each surety may be stated [@CL-CTF-2222; @CL-CTF-2223]. Under RFO FAR 2.101, a surety is an individual or corporation legally liable for the debt, default, or failure of a principal to satisfy a contractual obligation, and the types of sureties referred to are as follows: (1) an individual surety is one person, as distinguished from a business entity, who is liable for the entire penal amount of the bond, (2) a corporate surety is licensed under various insurance laws and, under its charter, has legal power to act as surety for others, and (3) a cosurety is one of two or more sureties that are jointly liable for the penal sum of the bond, and a limit of liability for each surety may be stated [@CL-CTF-9455; @CL-CTF-9483; @CL-CTF-9484].

For a performance bond, the Contracting Officer may release a portion of the security interest on the individual surety's assets based upon substantial performance of the Contractor's obligations under its performance bond, and the security interest in support of a performance bond shall be maintained (1) for construction, alteration, or repair contracts for a public building or public work of the Federal Government exceeding $150,000, until completion of any warranty period, or for 1 year following final payment, whichever is later, (2) for contracts subject to alternative payment protection (see FAR 28.102-1(b)(1)), for the full contract performance period plus 1 year, and (3) for other contracts not subject to the requirements of paragraph (b)(1) of the clause, until completion of any warranty period, or for 90 days following final payment, whichever is later [@CL-CTF-2509; @CL-CTF-2481; @CL-CTF-2510; @CL-CTF-2511].

For a payment bond, a surety's assets pledged in support of the bond may be released to a subcontractor or supplier upon Government receipt of a Federal district court judgment, or a sworn statement by the subcontractor or supplier that the claim is correct along with a notarized authorization of the release by the surety stating that it approves of such release [@CL-CTF-2512; @CL-CTF-2513]. The security interest on the individual surety's assets in support of a payment bond shall be maintained (1) for construction, alteration, or repair contracts for a public building or public work of the Federal Government exceeding $150,000 which require performance and payment bonds, for 1 year following final payment, or until resolution of all pending claims filed against the payment bond during the 1-year period following final payment, whichever is later, (2) for contracts subject to alternative payment protection (see FAR 28.102-1(b)(1)), for the full contract performance period plus 1 year, and (3) for other contracts not subject to the requirements of paragraph (c)(1) of the clause, for 90 days following final payment [@CL-CTF-2513; @CL-CTF-2514; @CL-CTF-2515; @CL-CTF-2516].

The Contracting Officer may allow the Contractor to substitute an individual surety, for a performance or payment bond, after contract award, and the Contractor shall comply with the requirements of paragraph (a) within the timeframe established by the Contracting Officer [@CL-CTF-2517].

For a bid guarantee, offerors shall obtain from each person acting as an individual surety on the guarantee a pledge of assets that meets the eligibility, valuation and security requirements described in the Federal Acquisition Regulation, and Standard Form 28, Affidavit of Individual Surety [@CL-CTF-2518]. The Offeror shall include with its offer the pledge of assets and the affidavit required by paragraph (a) within the timeframe specified in the Bid Guarantee provision, or as otherwise established by the Contracting Officer [@CL-CTF-2519]. The Contracting Officer may release the security interest on the individual surety's assets supporting a bid guarantee based upon evidence that the offer the surety supports will not result in contract award [@CL-CTF-2482].

## How it works

The pledge and the affidavit come from each person acting as an individual surety, and the Contractor obtains them for a performance or payment bond, while Offerors obtain them for a bid guarantee and include them with the offer [@CL-CTF-2219; @CL-CTF-2480; @CL-CTF-2518; @CL-CTF-2519]. For a performance bond or a payment bond the clause sets how long the security interest is maintained by contract type, and the Contracting Officer may release a portion of the security interest based upon substantial performance of the Contractor's obligations under its performance bond, or a surety's assets pledged in support of a payment bond may be released to a subcontractor or supplier upon Government receipt of a Federal district court judgment or a sworn statement by the subcontractor or supplier that the claim is correct along with a notarized authorization of the release by the surety [@CL-CTF-2509; @CL-CTF-2481; @CL-CTF-2510; @CL-CTF-2511; @CL-CTF-2512; @CL-CTF-2513; @CL-CTF-2514; @CL-CTF-2515; @CL-CTF-2516]. For a bid guarantee, the Contracting Officer may release the security interest on the individual surety's assets based upon evidence that the offer supported by the individual surety will not result in contract award [@CL-CTF-2482]. After contract award the Contracting Officer may allow the Contractor to substitute an individual surety for a performance or payment bond, and the Contractor shall comply with the requirements of paragraph (a) within the timeframe established by the Contracting Officer [@CL-CTF-2517].

Class Deviation 2026-O0038 directs DoD contracting officers to use the revised FAR part 52 in place of the codified text, effective February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760; @CL-CMP-9762]. Under RFO FAR 52.228-11(a), the Contractor shall obtain from each person acting as an individual surety on a performance bond or a payment bond a pledge of assets that meets the eligibility, valuation, and security requirements described in FAR 28.203-1, and Standard Form 28, Affidavit of Individual Surety [@CL-CTF-2220; @CL-CTF-0896]. Under RFO FAR 52.228-11(b), the Contracting Officer may release a portion of the security interest based upon substantial performance of the Contractor's obligations under its performance bond, and the security interest shall be maintained for the periods in paragraphs (b)(1) through (b)(3), including the full contract performance period plus 1 year for contracts subject to alternative payment protection [@CL-CTF-0897; @CL-CTF-0898; @CL-CTF-9202; @CL-CTF-0899]. Under RFO FAR 52.228-11(c) and (d), a surety's assets pledged in support of a payment bond may be released to a subcontractor or supplier upon Government receipt of a Federal district court judgment, or a sworn statement by the subcontractor or supplier that the claim is correct along with the surety's notarized authorization, the security interest shall be maintained for the periods in paragraphs (c)(1) through (c)(3) [@CL-CTF-0984; @CL-CTF-0985; @CL-CTF-0986; @CL-CTF-0987; @CL-CTF-0988], and the Contracting Officer may allow the Contractor to substitute an individual surety after contract award [@CL-CTF-0989]. Under RFO FAR 52.228-17, offerors shall obtain the pledge of assets and the affidavit for a bid guarantee, include them with the offer within the specified timeframe, and the Contracting Officer may release the security interest based upon evidence that the offer supported by the individual surety will not result in contract award [@CL-CTF-0990; @CL-CTF-0991; @CL-CTF-0992].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A construction contract for a public building is worth 3,000,000, above the 150,000 line, and requires performance and payment bonds. An individual surety on each bond gives a pledge of assets and the affidavit. Final payment is made in month 0. For the performance bond, the warranty period ends in month 6, so the later date is one year after final payment, month 12, and the security interest is kept to month 12. If the warranty instead ended in month 18, the interest would be kept to month 18. For the payment bond, a claim is filed against it in month 10 and is resolved in month 15. The interest is kept to the later of month 12 and month 15, so to month 15.

A supplier with a valid claim gives a sworn statement that the claim is correct, together with the surety's notarized authorization of the release, so the pledged assets may be released to it.

A contract that is not construction above the line and has no alternative payment protection reaches final payment on day 0, and its warranty period ends on day 120. The performance bond interest is kept to day 120, the later of day 90 and day 120. A contract under alternative payment protection with a full performance period of 24 months keeps the interest for 36 months.

For a separate bid, the offer the surety supports loses, and the Contracting Officer may release the security once there is evidence it will not result in award.

## Never confuse

A pledge of assets for a performance or payment bond is kept for the period the clause sets by contract type, which can run from final payment, warranty or pending claims to the full performance period plus 1 year [@CL-CTF-2509; @CL-CTF-2481; @CL-CTF-2510; @CL-CTF-2511; @CL-CTF-2515; @CL-CTF-2516], while a pledge for a bid guarantee may be released by the Contracting Officer on evidence that the offer will not result in award [@CL-CTF-2482]. See [Surety-takeover agreements after a default termination](../../close-revrec/revenue/cls-surety-takeover-agreements.md) and [Default clauses for fixed-price contracts](../../close-revrec/revenue/cls-fixed-price-default-clauses.md).
