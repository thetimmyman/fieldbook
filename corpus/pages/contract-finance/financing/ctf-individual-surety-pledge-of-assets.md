## Purpose

Shows what a contractor must obtain from an individual surety, how long the pledged security is kept for a performance bond, and when security for a bid guarantee is released.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CTF-2480] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.228-11 and FAR 52.228-17 [@CL-CTF-2480; @CL-CTF-2518]; FAR 2.101 [@CL-CTF-9425] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.228-11(a) [@CL-CTF-0896]; RFO FAR 52.228-11(b) [@CL-CTF-0897; @CL-CTF-0898; @CL-CTF-0899]; RFO FAR 52.228-11(c) [@CL-CTF-0984; @CL-CTF-0985; @CL-CTF-0986; @CL-CTF-0987; @CL-CTF-0988]; RFO FAR 52.228-11(d) [@CL-CTF-0989]; RFO FAR 52.228-17 [@CL-CTF-0990; @CL-CTF-0991; @CL-CTF-0992]; RFO FAR 2.101 [@CL-CTF-9455] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CTF-2480] |
| Applies to | Contracts with an individual surety on a performance or payment bond, and offers with an individual surety on a bid guarantee [@CL-CTF-2480; @CL-CTF-2518] |

The Contractor shall obtain from each individual surety on a performance bond or a payment bond a pledge of assets that meets the eligibility, valuation and security requirements described in the Federal Acquisition Regulation, and Standard Form 28, Affidavit of Individual Surety [@CL-CTF-2480]. The clause points to another section of the Federal Acquisition Regulation for which assets are eligible and how they are valued, so this card does not list the assets [@CL-CTF-2480]. The eligible assets are Not established.

The FAR definitions section describes a surety as an individual or corporation legally liable for the debt, default, or failure of a principal to satisfy a contractual obligation [@CL-CTF-9425]. The RFO text of FAR 2.101 defines a surety as an individual or corporation legally liable for the debt, default, or failure of a principal to satisfy a contractual obligation, the same in substance as the codified definition [@CL-CTF-9455; @CL-CTF-9425], and goes on to say that the types of sureties referred to are as follows: (1) an individual surety is one person, as distinguished from a business entity, who is liable for the entire penal amount of the bond, (2) a corporate surety is licensed under various insurance laws and, under its charter, has legal power to act as surety for others, and (3) a cosurety is one of two or more sureties that are jointly liable for the penal sum of the bond, and a limit of liability for each surety may be stated [@CL-CTF-9455; @CL-CTF-9483; @CL-CTF-9484].

For a performance bond, the Contracting Officer may release a portion of the security interest on the individual surety's assets based upon substantial performance of the Contractor's obligations under its performance bond, and the security interest in support of a performance bond shall be maintained as the list that follows provides [@CL-CTF-2509]. For construction, alteration or repair contracts for a public building or public work of the Federal Government exceeding $150,000, the period is until completion of any warranty period, or for 1 year following final payment, whichever is later [@CL-CTF-2481]. For contracts subject to alternative payment protection, the period is the full contract performance period plus 1 year [@CL-CTF-2510]. For other contracts not subject to the requirements of paragraph (b)(1), the period is until completion of any warranty period, or for 90 days following final payment, whichever is later [@CL-CTF-2511].

For a payment bond, a surety's assets pledged in support of the bond may be released to a subcontractor or supplier upon Government receipt of a Federal district court judgment, or a sworn statement by the subcontractor or supplier that the claim is correct [@CL-CTF-2512]. A sworn statement by the subcontractor or supplier that the claim is correct comes along with a notarized authorization of the release by the surety stating that it approves of such release, and the security interest on the individual surety's assets in support of a payment bond shall be maintained as the list that follows provides [@CL-CTF-2513]. That list covers construction, alteration or repair contracts for a public building or public work of the Federal Government exceeding $150,000 which require performance and payment bonds [@CL-CTF-2514]. For those contracts the security interest is maintained for 1 year following final payment, or until resolution of all pending claims filed against the payment bond during the 1-year period following final payment, whichever is later [@CL-CTF-2515]. For contracts subject to alternative payment protection it is maintained for the full contract performance period plus 1 year, and for other contracts not subject to the requirements of paragraph (c)(1) it is maintained for 90 days following final payment [@CL-CTF-2516].

The Contracting Officer may allow the Contractor to substitute an individual surety, for a performance or payment bond, after contract award, and the Contractor shall comply with the requirements of paragraph (a) within the timeframe established by the Contracting Officer [@CL-CTF-2517].

For a bid guarantee, offerors shall obtain from each person acting as an individual surety on the guarantee a pledge of assets that meets the eligibility, valuation and security requirements described in the Federal Acquisition Regulation, and Standard Form 28, Affidavit of Individual Surety [@CL-CTF-2518]. The Offeror shall include with its offer the pledge of assets and the affidavit required by paragraph (a) within the timeframe specified in the Bid Guarantee provision, or as otherwise established by the Contracting Officer [@CL-CTF-2519]. The Contracting Officer may release the security interest on the individual surety's assets supporting a bid guarantee based upon evidence that the offer the surety supports will not result in contract award [@CL-CTF-2482].

## How it works

The pledge and the affidavit come from each individual surety, and the Contractor obtains them for a bond, while the Offeror obtains them for a bid guarantee and files them with its offer [@CL-CTF-2480; @CL-CTF-2518; @CL-CTF-2519]. The pledged assets stay under a security interest. For a performance bond or a payment bond the clause sets how long the interest is kept, by contract type, and the Contracting Officer may release part of it after substantial performance, or the surety's assets may be released to a subcontractor or supplier on the stated proof [@CL-CTF-2509; @CL-CTF-2512; @CL-CTF-2513]. For a bid guarantee, the release depends on evidence that the supported offer will not lead to award, and the Contracting Officer decides [@CL-CTF-2482]. After award the Contracting Officer may allow a substitute individual surety, who must meet the pledge and affidavit requirements within the timeframe the Contracting Officer sets [@CL-CTF-2517].

The RFO text applies to DoD contracting because Class Deviation 2026-O0038 directs DoD contracting officers to use the revised FAR part 52 in place of the codified text, effective February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. The RFO text of FAR 52.228-11 keeps the pledge of assets and the affidavit in paragraph (a) [@CL-CTF-0896; @CL-CTF-2480]. It keeps the release of part of the security interest after substantial performance and the periods in paragraphs (b)(1) to (b)(3), including the full contract performance period plus 1 year for contracts subject to alternative payment protection [@CL-CTF-0897; @CL-CTF-0898; @CL-CTF-9202; @CL-CTF-0899; @CL-CTF-2509; @CL-CTF-2481; @CL-CTF-2510; @CL-CTF-2511]. It keeps the release of pledged assets to a subcontractor or supplier on a judgment or sworn statement and the periods for a payment bond in paragraphs (c)(1) to (c)(3) [@CL-CTF-0984; @CL-CTF-0985; @CL-CTF-0986; @CL-CTF-0987; @CL-CTF-0988; @CL-CTF-2512; @CL-CTF-2513; @CL-CTF-2514; @CL-CTF-2515; @CL-CTF-2516], and the substitution of an individual surety in paragraph (d) [@CL-CTF-0989; @CL-CTF-2517]. The RFO text of FAR 52.228-17 keeps the pledge of assets and the affidavit for a bid guarantee, the timeframe for including the information with the offer, and the release of the security interest when the offer will not result in award [@CL-CTF-0990; @CL-CTF-0991; @CL-CTF-0992; @CL-CTF-2518; @CL-CTF-2519; @CL-CTF-2482].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A construction contract for a public building is worth 3,000,000, above the 150,000 line, and requires performance and payment bonds. An individual surety on each bond gives a pledge of assets and the affidavit. Final payment is made in month 0. For the performance bond, the warranty period ends in month 6, so the later date is one year after final payment, month 12, and the security interest is kept to month 12. If the warranty instead ended in month 18, the interest would be kept to month 18. For the payment bond, a claim is filed against it in month 10 and is resolved in month 15. The interest is kept to the later of month 12 and month 15, so to month 15.

A supplier with a valid claim gives a sworn statement that the claim is correct, together with the surety's notarized authorization of the release, so the pledged assets may be released to it.

A contract that is not construction above the line and has no alternative payment protection reaches final payment on day 0, and its warranty period ends on day 120. The performance bond interest is kept to day 120, the later of day 90 and day 120. A contract under alternative payment protection with a full performance period of 24 months keeps the interest for 36 months.

For a separate bid, the offer the surety supports loses, and the Contracting Officer may release the security once there is evidence it will not result in award.

## Never confuse

A pledge of assets for a performance or payment bond is kept for the period the clause sets by contract type, which can run from final payment, warranty or pending claims to the full performance period plus 1 year [@CL-CTF-2509; @CL-CTF-2481; @CL-CTF-2510; @CL-CTF-2511; @CL-CTF-2515; @CL-CTF-2516], while a pledge for a bid guarantee may be released by the Contracting Officer on evidence that the offer will not result in award [@CL-CTF-2482]. A surety takeover after a default termination is a different arrangement; see [Surety-takeover agreements after a default termination](../../close-revrec/revenue/cls-surety-takeover-agreements.md). The default clauses are on [Default clauses for fixed-price contracts](../../close-revrec/revenue/cls-fixed-price-default-clauses.md).
