## Purpose

Lists the bases on which FAR 32.1002 says performance-based payments may be made on noncommercial purchases [@CL-CTF-3429; @CL-CTF-3445]. It also states two DoD points: DFARS 232.202-4(a)(2) on an offeror's financial condition as security, and DFARS 232.072 on determining the financial capability of current or prospective contractors [@CL-CTF-3431; @CL-CTF-3432]. These are three separate sections. The DFARS security point concerns commercial contract financing [@CL-CTF-3431].

Performance-based payments may be made on any of the following bases: performance measured by objective, quantifiable methods, accomplishment of defined events, or other quantifiable measures of results [@CL-CTF-3429]. The section does not say who chooses the basis [@CL-CTF-3429]. It sits in a subpart that covers performance-based payments under noncommercial purchases [@CL-CTF-3445].

When determining whether an offeror's financial condition is adequate security, DFARS 232.202-4(a)(2) says to see DFARS 232.072-2 and 232.072-3 for guidance [@CL-CTF-3430]. It also notes that an offeror's financial condition may be sufficient to make the contractor responsible for award purposes but may not be adequate security for commercial contract financing [@CL-CTF-3431].

DFARS 232.072 says to use its policies and procedures in determining the financial capability of current or prospective contractors [@CL-CTF-3432]. The sentence does not name who must use them [@CL-CTF-3432].

## How it works

The section lists three bases and says performance-based payments may be made on any of them [@CL-CTF-3429]. It sets no threshold, deadline or exception [@CL-CTF-3429].

On security for commercial contract financing, the decision belongs to the contracting officer under the FAR: subject to agency regulations, the contracting officer may determine the offeror's financial condition to be adequate security, provided the offeror agrees to provide additional security should that condition become inadequate [@CL-CTF-1413]. The DFARS paragraph adds the pointer to financial responsibility guidance and the warning that responsibility for award and adequate security are different questions [@CL-CTF-3430; @CL-CTF-3431]. The [commercial financing security](ctf-commercial-financing-security-and-clauses.md) card covers the FAR rules on security.

The stored DFARS text of 232.202-4 prints only paragraph (a)(2) [@CL-CTF-3430].

The financial responsibility section is the policy base. Its reviews, information requests and cash flow forecasts are on the [required financial reviews](../cash/ctf-dod-required-financial-reviews.md), [financial review information](../cash/ctf-dod-financial-review-information.md) and [cash flow forecasts](../cash/ctf-dod-cash-flow-forecasts.md) cards.

From February 1, 2026, DoD contracting officers use the RFO text of FAR part 32 in place of the codified text [@CL-CTF-4046]. RFO FAR 32.1002 has the same three bases as the codified section [@CL-CTF-3444; @CL-CTF-3429].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A noncommercial purchase contract could set a performance-based payment of 100,000 on completion of a defined test event. Accomplishment of defined events is one of the bases on which performance-based payments may be made [@CL-CTF-3429]. The section does not say whether any particular contract must use that basis.

Separately, an offeror shows 2,000,000 of working capital and is found responsible for award. That finding does not settle whether the offeror's financial condition is adequate security for 1,500,000 of commercial contract financing, because the two are separate questions [@CL-CTF-3431]. Subject to agency regulations, the contracting officer may determine the financial condition to be adequate security if the offeror agrees to provide additional security should that condition become inadequate [@CL-CTF-1413]. The guidance for that determination is at DFARS 232.072-2 and 232.072-3 [@CL-CTF-3430]. The example does not conclude either way.

## Never confuse

Responsibility for award is a different test from adequate security. An offeror's financial condition may be sufficient for the first and still not be adequate for the second [@CL-CTF-3431].

FAR 32.1002 gives bases for noncommercial purchases, while the DFARS security paragraph concerns commercial contract financing [@CL-CTF-3445; @CL-CTF-3431]. They are not one rule.

DFARS 232.072 is the policy for determining financial capability [@CL-CTF-3432]. DFARS 232.202-4(a)(2) points to two of its subsections for guidance on security and adds the warning that responsibility for award and adequate security differ [@CL-CTF-3432; @CL-CTF-3430; @CL-CTF-3431].

The [performance-based payments](ctf-performance-based-payments.md) card covers the payment mechanics. The bases here are measures of performance or results [@CL-CTF-3429].
