## Purpose

A fixed-price contract with economic price adjustment provides for revising the stated contract price upward or downward upon the occurrence of specified market or labor contingencies. The stated price moves only when the specified contingencies occur.

## The rule

A fixed-price contract with economic price adjustment provides for upward and downward revision of the stated contract price upon the occurrence of specified contingencies [@CL-EST-1155]. Economic price adjustments are of three general types: adjustments based on established prices of specific items or contract end items, adjustments based on actual costs of labor or material experienced during performance, and adjustments based on cost standards or indexes of labor or material specifically identified in the contract [@CL-EST-1156; @CL-EST-1157; @CL-EST-1158].

The contract type may be used when there is serious doubt concerning the stability of market or labor conditions during an extended period of performance, and contingencies that would otherwise be included in the contract price can be identified and covered separately [@CL-EST-1159]. It shall not be used unless the contracting officer determines that it is necessary either to protect the contractor and the Government against significant fluctuations in labor or material costs or to provide for price adjustment in the event of changes in the contractor's established prices [@CL-EST-1162].

## How it works

In establishing the base level from which adjustment is made, the contracting officer must ensure that contingency allowances are not duplicated by inclusion in both the base price and the adjustment requested under the clause [@CL-EST-1160]. Adjustments based on established prices should normally be restricted to industry-wide contingencies, and adjustments based on labor and material costs should be limited to contingencies beyond the contractor's control [@CL-EST-1205]. Only the occurrence of the specified contingencies moves the stated price [@CL-EST-1155].

Prescribed clauses include FAR 52.216-2 for standard supplies, FAR 52.216-3 for semistandard supplies, and FAR 52.216-4 for actual costs of labor and material [@CL-EST-1207; @CL-EST-1208; @CL-EST-1206]. For standard supplies under FAR 52.216-2, the contracting officer may modify the 10 percent limit on aggregate increases upon approval by the chief of the contracting office [@CL-EST-1166]. See [Fixed-price contracts with economic price adjustment: types and use](../../estimating/escalation/est-fixed-price-epa-types-and-use.md), [Prescriptions for the economic price adjustment clauses](../../estimating/escalation/est-epa-clause-prescriptions.md), and [Economic Price Adjustment—Labor and Material](ctf-economic-price-adjustment-labor-and-material.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contract has a stated price of 500,000 dollars, which includes an identified labor component of 200,000 dollars tied to a specified labor cost index. When the index rises 5 percent over the agreed baseline level as defined in the contract, the labor component adjusts upward by 5 percent or 10,000 dollars to 210,000 dollars, increasing the total contract price to 510,000 dollars. A cost increase experienced outside the specified index contingency does not change the price and is absorbed by the contractor. This example is illustrative only, and the terms of the contract itself govern.

## Never confuse

A fixed-price contract with economic price adjustment must not be confused with a plain firm-fixed-price contract, where the price is not subject to any adjustment on the basis of the contractor's cost experience [@CL-EST-1155; @CL-EST-1150]. It also differs from a fixed-price incentive contract, which adjusts profit and establishes the final contract price by a formula based on total final negotiated cost relative to target cost, rather than revising the price for specified market or labor contingencies [@CL-EST-1568].
