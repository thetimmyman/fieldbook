## Purpose

Shows the paragraphs that Alternate I of the commercial products and services terms clause substitutes when a time-and-materials or labor-hour contract is contemplated: inspection, acceptance and correction, definitions, payment of hourly rates and materials, the ceiling price and the notice duties, the Government's access to records, overpayments, interest, the completion invoice, the release of claims, prompt payment and discounts, and termination for convenience and for cause.

Alternate I says that when a time-and-materials or labor-hour contract is contemplated, its paragraphs (a), (e), (i), (l), and (m) are substituted for those in the basic clause [@CL-CTF-2668].

The Government has the right to inspect and test all materials furnished and services performed under the contract, to the extent practicable at all places and times, including the period of performance, and in any event before acceptance [@CL-CTF-2669]. The Government may also inspect the plant or plants of the contractor or any subcontractor engaged in contract performance, and will perform inspections and tests in a manner that will not unduly delay the work [@CL-CTF-2670]. If the Government performs inspection or tests on the premises of the contractor or a subcontractor, the contractor shall furnish, and shall require subcontractors to furnish, all reasonable facilities and assistance for the safe and convenient performance of these duties [@CL-CTF-2671]. Unless otherwise specified in the contract, the Government will accept or reject services and materials at the place of delivery as promptly as practicable after delivery, and they will be presumed accepted 60 days after the date of delivery, unless accepted earlier [@CL-CTF-2672].

At any time during contract performance, but not later than 6 months, or such other time as the contract specifies, after acceptance of the services or materials last delivered, the Government may require the contractor to replace or correct services or materials, as limited in the next sentence [@CL-CTF-2673]. The services or materials that may be required to be replaced or corrected are those that at time of delivery failed to meet contract requirements, and, except as otherwise specified in paragraph (a)(6), the cost of replacement or correction shall be determined under paragraph (i) of Alternate I [@CL-CTF-2674]. The hourly rate for labor hours incurred in the replacement or correction shall be reduced to exclude the portion of the rate attributable to profit, and unless otherwise specified in that paragraph the portion of the hourly rate attributable to profit shall be 10 percent [@CL-CTF-2675]. The contractor shall not tender for acceptance materials and services required to be replaced or corrected without disclosing the former requirement for replacement or correction, and, when required, shall disclose the corrective action taken [@CL-CTF-2676].

If the contractor fails to proceed with reasonable promptness to perform required replacement or correction, and the replacement or correction can be performed within the ceiling price (or the ceiling price as increased by the Government), the Government may take the listed actions [@CL-CTF-2677]. The Government may, by contract or otherwise, perform the replacement or correction, charge to the contractor any increased cost, or deduct such increased cost from any amounts paid or due under the contract [@CL-CTF-2678]. The other listed action is to terminate the contract for cause, and failure to agree to the amount of increased cost to be charged to the contractor shall be a dispute under the Disputes clause of the contract [@CL-CTF-2679]. Notwithstanding paragraphs (a)(4) and (5), the Government may at any time require the contractor to remedy by correction or replacement, without cost to the Government, any failure by the contractor to comply with the requirements of the contract, if the failure is due to the listed causes [@CL-CTF-2680]. One listed cause is fraud, lack of good faith, or willful misconduct on the part of the contractor's managerial personnel [@CL-CTF-2681]. The other listed cause is the conduct of one or more of the contractor's employees selected or retained by the contractor after any of the contractor's managerial personnel has reasonable grounds to believe that the employee is habitually careless or unqualified [@CL-CTF-2682].

The clause applies in the same manner and to the same extent to corrected or replacement materials or services as to materials and services originally delivered under the contract [@CL-CTF-2683]. The contractor has no obligation or liability under the contract to correct or replace materials and services that at time of delivery do not meet contract requirements, except as provided in the clause or as may be otherwise specified in the contract [@CL-CTF-2684]. Unless otherwise specified in the contract, the contractor's obligation to correct or replace Government-furnished property shall be governed by the clause pertaining to Government property [@CL-CTF-2685].

The clause at FAR 52.202-1, Definitions, is incorporated by reference, and as used in the clause direct materials means those materials that enter directly into the end product, or that are used or consumed directly in connection with the furnishing of the end product or service [@CL-CTF-2719]. Hourly rate means the rate or rates prescribed in the contract for payment for labor that meets the labor category qualifications of a labor category specified in the contract and that is performed by the contractor [@CL-CTF-2720]. The hourly rate also covers such labor that is performed by the subcontractors, or that is transferred between divisions, subsidiaries, or affiliates of the contractor under a common control [@CL-CTF-2841]. Materials means direct materials, including supplies transferred between divisions, subsidiaries, or affiliates of the contractor under a common control [@CL-CTF-2842]. Materials also include subcontracts for supplies and incidental services for which there is not a labor category specified in the contract, and other direct costs such as incidental services without a specified labor category, travel and computer usage charges [@CL-CTF-2843]. Materials also include the subcontracts for services that are specifically excluded from the hourly rate (the clause has a blank for subcontracts to be excluded from the hourly rates prescribed in the schedule), and indirect costs specifically provided for in the clause [@CL-CTF-2844]. A subcontract is any contract, as defined in FAR Subpart 2.1, entered into with a subcontractor to furnish supplies or services for performance of the prime contract or a subcontract, including transfers between divisions, subsidiaries, or affiliates of a contractor or subcontractor, and it includes, but is not limited to, purchase orders, and changes and modifications to purchase orders [@CL-CTF-2845; @CL-CTF-2846].

The Government will pay the contractor as follows, upon the submission of commercial invoices approved by the Contracting Officer [@CL-CTF-2847]. On payment for work performed, the amounts shall be computed by multiplying the appropriate hourly rates prescribed in the contract by the number of direct labor hours performed, and fractional parts of an hour shall be payable on a prorated basis [@CL-CTF-2697]. The rates shall be paid for all labor performed on the contract that meets the labor qualifications specified in the contract [@CL-CTF-2698]. Labor hours incurred to perform tasks for which labor qualifications were specified in the contract will not be paid to the extent the work is performed by individuals that do not meet the qualifications specified in the contract, unless specifically authorized by the Contracting Officer [@CL-CTF-2699]. Invoices may be submitted once each month, or at more frequent intervals if approved by the Contracting Officer, to the Contracting Officer or the authorized representative [@CL-CTF-2700]. When requested by the Contracting Officer or the authorized representative, the contractor shall substantiate invoices, including any subcontractor hours reimbursed at the hourly rate in the schedule, by evidence of actual payment [@CL-CTF-2848], and the substantiation may also be by individual daily job timecards, records that verify the employees meet the qualifications for the labor categories specified in the contract, or other substantiation specified in the contract [@CL-CTF-2849]. Unless the Schedule prescribes otherwise, the hourly rates in the Schedule shall not be varied by virtue of the contractor having performed work on an overtime basis [@CL-CTF-2850]. If no overtime rates are provided in the Schedule and the Contracting Officer approves overtime work in advance, overtime rates shall be negotiated, and failure to agree upon these overtime rates shall be treated as a dispute under the Disputes clause of the contract [@CL-CTF-2851]. If the Schedule provides rates for overtime, the premium portion of those rates will be reimbursable only to the extent the overtime is approved by the Contracting Officer [@CL-CTF-2852].

If the contractor furnishes materials that meet the definition of a commercial product at FAR 2.101, the price to be paid for such materials shall not exceed the contractor's established catalog or market price, adjusted to reflect the items listed next [@CL-CTF-2701]. The adjustments are for the quantities being acquired and for any modifications necessary because of contract requirements [@CL-CTF-2702]. Except as provided for in paragraphs (i)(1)(ii)(A) and (D)(2), the Government will reimburse the contractor the actual cost of materials, less any rebates, refunds, or discounts received by the contractor that are identifiable to the contract, provided the contractor meets one of the listed conditions [@CL-CTF-2703]. One listed condition is that the contractor has made payments for materials in accordance with the terms and conditions of the agreement or invoice [@CL-CTF-2704]. The other listed condition is that the contractor makes these payments within 30 days of the submission of the contractor's payment request to the Government and such payment is in accordance with the terms and conditions of the agreement or invoice [@CL-CTF-2705]. To the extent able, the contractor shall obtain materials at the most advantageous prices available with due regard to securing prompt delivery of satisfactory materials [@CL-CTF-2853], and, to the same extent, shall also give credit to the Government for cash and trade discounts, rebates, scrap, commissions, and other amounts that are identifiable to the contract [@CL-CTF-2853; @CL-CTF-2854]. Unless listed in the clause, other direct and indirect costs will not be reimbursed [@CL-CTF-2855]. The Government will reimburse the contractor on the basis of actual cost for the listed other direct costs, such as travel and computer usage charges, provided such costs comply with the requirements in paragraph (i)(1)(ii)(B) of the clause, with each element to be inserted where the clause provides a blank [@CL-CTF-2856], and None is to be inserted if no reimbursement for other direct costs will be provided [@CL-CTF-2890]. The Government will reimburse the contractor for indirect costs, such as material handling and subcontract administration, on a pro-rata basis over the period of contract performance at the listed fixed price, with a fixed amount and payment schedule to be inserted where the clause provides a blank [@CL-CTF-2857].

It is estimated that the total cost to the Government for performance of the contract shall not exceed the ceiling price set forth in the Schedule [@CL-CTF-2706]. The contractor agrees to use its best efforts to perform the work specified in the Schedule and all obligations under the contract within such ceiling price [@CL-CTF-2707]. If at any time the contractor has reason to believe that the hourly rate payments and material costs that will accrue in the next succeeding 30 days, added to all other payments and costs previously accrued, will exceed 85 percent of the ceiling price in the Schedule, the following notice duty arises [@CL-CTF-2708]. The contractor shall then notify the Contracting Officer giving a revised estimate of the total price to the Government for performing the contract with supporting reasons and documentation [@CL-CTF-2709]. If at any time during the performance of the contract the contractor has reason to believe that the total price to the Government for performing the contract will be substantially greater or less than the then stated ceiling price, a further notice duty arises [@CL-CTF-2858]. The contractor shall then so notify the Contracting Officer, giving a revised estimate of the total price for performing the contract, with supporting reasons and documentation [@CL-CTF-2859]. If at any time during performance of the contract the Government has reason to believe that the work to be required will be substantially greater or less than the stated ceiling price, a Government advice duty arises [@CL-CTF-2860]. The Contracting Officer will then so advise the contractor, giving the then revised estimate of the total amount of effort to be required under the contract [@CL-CTF-2861].

The Government will not be obligated to pay the contractor any amount in excess of the ceiling price in the Schedule, and the contractor shall not be obligated to continue performance if to do so would exceed the ceiling price set forth in the Schedule [@CL-CTF-2710]. The ceiling price limits apply unless and until the Contracting Officer notifies the contractor in writing that the ceiling price has been increased and specifies in the notice a revised ceiling that shall constitute the ceiling price for performance under the contract [@CL-CTF-2711]. When and to the extent that the ceiling price set forth in the Schedule has been increased, any hours expended and material costs incurred by the contractor in excess of the ceiling price before the increase shall be allowable [@CL-CTF-2712]. They are allowable to the same extent as if the hours expended and material costs had been incurred after the increase in the ceiling price [@CL-CTF-2713].

At any time before final payment, the Contracting Officer or authorized representative will have access to the records listed next, and access shall be limited to that listing unless otherwise agreed to by the contractor and the Contracting Officer [@CL-CTF-2714]. One listed item is records that verify that the employees whose time has been included in any invoice meet the qualifications for the labor categories specified in the contract [@CL-CTF-2715]. For labor hours, including any subcontractor hours reimbursed at the hourly rate in the schedule, when timecards are required as substantiation for payment, the listed items include the original timecards, paper-based or electronic, and the contractor's timekeeping procedures [@CL-CTF-2716]. The listed items for labor hours, when timecards are required as substantiation for payment, also include contractor records that show the distribution of labor between jobs or contracts, and employees whose time has been included in any invoice for the purpose of verifying that these employees have worked the hours shown on the invoices [@CL-CTF-2717]. For material and subcontract costs that are reimbursed on the basis of actual cost, the listed items are any invoices or subcontract agreements substantiating material costs and any documents supporting payment of those invoices [@CL-CTF-2718].

Each payment previously made shall be subject to reduction to the extent of amounts, on preceding invoices, that are found by the Contracting Officer not to have been properly payable, and shall also be subject to reduction for overpayments or to increase for underpayments [@CL-CTF-2862]. The contractor shall promptly pay any such reduction within 30 days unless the parties agree otherwise, and the Government within 30 days will pay any such increases, unless the parties agree otherwise [@CL-CTF-2863]. The contractor's payment will be made by check, and if the contractor becomes aware of a duplicate invoice payment or that the Government has otherwise overpaid on an invoice payment, the contractor shall take the steps that follow [@CL-CTF-2864]. The contractor shall remit the overpayment amount to the payment office cited in the contract along with a description of the overpayment including the circumstances of the overpayment, such as duplicate payment, erroneous payment, liquidation errors or the date or dates of overpayment [@CL-CTF-2865]. The description of the overpayment also includes the affected contract number and delivery order number, if applicable, the affected line item or subline item, if applicable, and the contractor point of contact, and the contractor shall provide a copy of the remittance and supporting documentation to the Contracting Officer [@CL-CTF-2866].

All amounts that become payable by the contractor to the Government under the contract shall bear simple interest from the date due until paid unless paid within 30 days of becoming due, at the interest rate established by the Secretary of the Treasury [@CL-CTF-2867]. That interest rate is as provided in section 7109 of title 41, United States Code, which is applicable to the period in which the amount becomes due, and then the rate applicable for each six month period as established by the Secretary until the amount is paid [@CL-CTF-2868]. The Government may issue a demand for payment to the contractor upon finding a debt is due under the contract [@CL-CTF-2869]. The Contracting Officer will issue a final decision as required by 33.211 if the Contracting Officer and the contractor are unable to reach agreement on the existence or amount of a debt in a timely manner [@CL-CTF-2870]. The Contracting Officer will also issue a final decision if the contractor fails to liquidate a debt previously demanded by the Contracting Officer within the timeline specified in the demand for payment, unless the amounts were not repaid because the contractor has requested an installment payment agreement [@CL-CTF-2871], or if the contractor requests a deferment of collection on a debt previously demanded by the Contracting Officer [@CL-CTF-2872]. If a demand for payment was previously issued for the debt, the demand for payment included in the final decision shall identify the same due date as the original demand for payment [@CL-CTF-2873]. Amounts shall be due at the earliest of the date fixed under the contract and the date of the first written demand for payment, including any demand for payment resulting from a default termination [@CL-CTF-2874]. The interest charge shall be computed for the actual number of calendar days involved beginning on the due date and ending on the date on which the designated office receives payment from the contractor [@CL-CTF-2875], or on the date of issuance of a Government check to the contractor from which an amount otherwise payable has been withheld as a credit against the contract debt, or on the date on which an amount withheld and applied to the contract debt would otherwise have become payable to the contractor [@CL-CTF-2876]. The interest charge made under the clause may be reduced under the procedures prescribed in FAR 32.608-2 in effect on the date of the contract [@CL-CTF-2877].

Upon receipt and approval of the invoice designated by the contractor as the completion invoice and supporting documentation, and upon compliance by the contractor with all terms of the contract, any outstanding balances will be paid within 30 days unless the parties agree otherwise [@CL-CTF-2878]. The completion invoice and supporting documentation shall be submitted by the contractor as promptly as practicable following completion of the work [@CL-CTF-2879], and shall in no event be submitted later than 1 year, or such longer period as the Contracting Officer may approve in writing, from the date of completion [@CL-CTF-2880]. The contractor, and each assignee under an assignment entered into under the contract and in effect at the time of final payment, shall execute and deliver, at the time of and as a condition precedent to final payment, a release [@CL-CTF-2881]. The release discharges the Government, its officers, agents, and employees of and from all liabilities, obligations, and claims arising out of or under the contract, subject only to the listed exceptions [@CL-CTF-2882]. One exception is specified claims in stated amounts, or in estimated amounts if the amounts are not susceptible to exact statement by the contractor [@CL-CTF-2883]. A second exception is claims, together with reasonable incidental expenses, based upon the liabilities of the contractor to third parties arising out of performing the contract, that are not known to the contractor on the date of the execution of the release [@CL-CTF-2884]. That second exception also requires that the contractor give notice in writing to the Contracting Officer not more than 6 years after the date of the release or the date of any notice to the contractor that the Government is prepared to make final payment, whichever is earlier [@CL-CTF-2885]. A third exception is claims for reimbursement of costs, other than expenses of the contractor by reason of its indemnification of the Government against patent liability, including reasonable incidental expenses, incurred by the contractor under the terms of the contract relating to patents [@CL-CTF-2886].

The Government will make payment in accordance with the Prompt Payment Act and prompt payment regulations at 5 CFR part 1315, and if the Government makes payment by electronic funds transfer, see 52.212-5(b) for the appropriate electronic funds transfer clause [@CL-CTF-2887]. In connection with any discount offered for early payment, time shall be computed from the date of the invoice [@CL-CTF-2888]. For the purpose of computing the discount earned, payment shall be considered to have been made on the date that appears on the payment check, or the specified payment date if an electronic funds transfer payment is made [@CL-CTF-2889].

The Government reserves the right to terminate the contract, or any part of it, for its sole convenience, and on such termination the contractor shall immediately stop all work and immediately cause its suppliers and subcontractors to cease work [@CL-CTF-2686]. Subject to the terms of the contract, on a termination for convenience the contractor shall be paid an amount for direct labor hours, as defined in the Schedule, determined by multiplying the number of direct labor hours expended before the effective date of termination by the hourly rates in the contract [@CL-CTF-2687]. The termination payment is reduced by any hourly rate payments already made to the contractor and increased by reasonable charges that the contractor can demonstrate to the satisfaction of the Government, using its standard record keeping system, have resulted from the termination [@CL-CTF-2688]. The contractor shall not be required to comply with the cost accounting standards or contract cost principles for this purpose, and the paragraph does not give the Government any right to audit the contractor's records [@CL-CTF-2689]. The contractor shall not be paid for any work performed or costs incurred that reasonably could have been avoided [@CL-CTF-2690].

The Government may terminate the contract, or any part of it, for cause in the event of any default by the contractor or if the contractor fails to comply with any contract terms and conditions [@CL-CTF-2691]. The Government may also terminate for cause if the contractor fails to provide the Government, upon written request, with adequate assurances of future performance [@CL-CTF-2692]. Subject to the terms of the contract, on a termination for cause the contractor shall be paid an amount computed under paragraph (i), Payments, of Alternate I [@CL-CTF-2693]. The hourly rate for labor hours expended in furnishing work not delivered to or accepted by the Government shall be reduced to exclude that portion of the rate attributable to profit [@CL-CTF-2694]. Unless otherwise specified in paragraph (a)(4), the portion of the hourly rate attributable to profit shall be 10 percent [@CL-CTF-2695]. In the event of termination for cause the contractor shall be liable to the Government for any and all rights and remedies provided by law, and if it is determined that the Government improperly terminated the contract for default, the termination shall be deemed a termination for convenience [@CL-CTF-2696].

## How it works

Alternate I replaces the listed basic paragraphs on a time-and-materials or labor-hour contract [@CL-CTF-2668]. The Government inspects and tests before acceptance, may inspect the contractor's and subcontractors' plants, and must not unduly delay the work, while the contractor furnishes facilities and assistance when inspection is on its premises or a subcontractor's [@CL-CTF-2669; @CL-CTF-2670; @CL-CTF-2671]. Services and materials are accepted or rejected at the place of delivery, and are presumed accepted 60 days after delivery unless accepted earlier or the contract says otherwise [@CL-CTF-2672]. The Government may require replacement or correction up to 6 months after acceptance of the last delivery, or another time the contract specifies, for services or materials that failed to meet contract requirements at delivery [@CL-CTF-2673; @CL-CTF-2674]. The cost is determined under paragraph (i), with the hourly rate cut by the profit portion, which is 10 percent unless otherwise specified [@CL-CTF-2674; @CL-CTF-2675]. If the contractor does not proceed with reasonable promptness and the replacement or correction can be performed within the ceiling price (or the ceiling price as increased by the Government), the Government may do the work itself and charge or deduct the increased cost, or terminate for cause, and failure to agree to the amount of increased cost is a dispute [@CL-CTF-2677; @CL-CTF-2678; @CL-CTF-2679]. Notwithstanding paragraphs (a)(4) and (5), the Government may at any time require correction without cost to the Government of a failure due to fraud, lack of good faith or willful misconduct by managerial personnel, or due to the conduct of employees selected or retained after managerial personnel had reasonable grounds to believe the employee is habitually careless or unqualified [@CL-CTF-2680; @CL-CTF-2681; @CL-CTF-2682].

Payment is hourly rate times direct labor hours performed, with fractions prorated, and hours worked by individuals who do not meet specified qualifications are not paid unless the Contracting Officer specifically authorizes them [@CL-CTF-2697; @CL-CTF-2698; @CL-CTF-2699]. Commercial product materials are priced at no more than the contractor's established catalog or market price as adjusted, and other materials are reimbursed at actual cost less identifiable rebates, refunds and discounts if the payment conditions are met [@CL-CTF-2701; @CL-CTF-2702; @CL-CTF-2703; @CL-CTF-2704; @CL-CTF-2705]. The contractor watches the ceiling price: when it has reason to believe that the hourly rate payments and material costs accruing in the next 30 days, added to all other payments and costs previously accrued, will exceed 85 percent of the ceiling price, it notifies the Contracting Officer with a revised estimate of the total price [@CL-CTF-2708; @CL-CTF-2709], the Government owes nothing above the ceiling and the contractor need not continue past it until the Contracting Officer's written notice raises it [@CL-CTF-2710; @CL-CTF-2711], and, when and to the extent the ceiling price has been increased, hours and costs over the old ceiling before the increase are allowable as if incurred afterward [@CL-CTF-2712; @CL-CTF-2713]. The access of the Contracting Officer (or authorized representative) to records before final payment is limited to the listed records unless the contractor and the Contracting Officer otherwise agree [@CL-CTF-2714; @CL-CTF-2715; @CL-CTF-2716; @CL-CTF-2717; @CL-CTF-2718]. Subject to the terms of the contract, a convenience termination pays hours expended before the effective date at the contract hourly rates, less hourly rate payments already made, plus reasonable charges the contractor can demonstrate to the satisfaction of the Government, using its standard record keeping system, that have resulted from the termination, but not for work performed or costs incurred that reasonably could have been avoided [@CL-CTF-2687; @CL-CTF-2688; @CL-CTF-2690]. Subject to the terms of the contract, a cause termination pays under paragraph (i) with the profit portion removed from the hourly rate for work not delivered or accepted [@CL-CTF-2693; @CL-CTF-2694; @CL-CTF-2695]. The hourly rate covers qualifying labor performed by the contractor or its subcontractors or transferred between divisions, subsidiaries or affiliates under common control, and materials include direct materials, subcontracts for supplies and incidental services and other direct costs without a specified labor category, excluded service subcontracts and the indirect costs that the clause provides for [@CL-CTF-2719; @CL-CTF-2720; @CL-CTF-2841; @CL-CTF-2842; @CL-CTF-2843; @CL-CTF-2844; @CL-CTF-2845; @CL-CTF-2846]. Invoices are approved by the Contracting Officer and may have to be substantiated on request, and the Schedule's hourly rates are not varied for overtime unless the Schedule prescribes otherwise, with overtime rates negotiated when none are provided and the Contracting Officer approves the overtime in advance [@CL-CTF-2847; @CL-CTF-2848; @CL-CTF-2849; @CL-CTF-2850; @CL-CTF-2851; @CL-CTF-2852]. To the extent able, the contractor buys materials at the most advantageous prices available, with due regard to securing prompt delivery of satisfactory materials, and credits the Government with identifiable discounts and rebates, and other direct and indirect costs are reimbursed only as listed [@CL-CTF-2853; @CL-CTF-2854; @CL-CTF-2855; @CL-CTF-2856; @CL-CTF-2857]. Beyond the 85 percent notice, the contractor and the Government each give a revised estimate when they expect the work or price to be substantially greater or less than the ceiling price [@CL-CTF-2708; @CL-CTF-2858; @CL-CTF-2859; @CL-CTF-2860; @CL-CTF-2861]. Each previous payment is subject to reduction for amounts on preceding invoices that the Contracting Officer finds were not properly payable, and for overpayments, or to increase for underpayments, settled within 30 days unless the parties agree otherwise [@CL-CTF-2862; @CL-CTF-2863], and a contractor that learns of an overpayment remits it with a description and a copy to the Contracting Officer [@CL-CTF-2864; @CL-CTF-2865; @CL-CTF-2866]. Amounts the contractor owes bear simple interest from the date due unless paid within 30 days of becoming due, computed over the actual calendar days from the due date, and the Government may issue a demand for payment on finding a debt is due, with the Contracting Officer issuing a final decision in one of the listed cases [@CL-CTF-2867; @CL-CTF-2868; @CL-CTF-2869; @CL-CTF-2870; @CL-CTF-2871; @CL-CTF-2872; @CL-CTF-2873; @CL-CTF-2874; @CL-CTF-2875; @CL-CTF-2876], and the interest charge may be reduced [@CL-CTF-2877]. Outstanding balances are paid within 30 days of the approved completion invoice unless the parties agree otherwise, and the completion invoice is submitted as promptly as practicable and no later than 1 year after completion unless the Contracting Officer approves a longer period in writing [@CL-CTF-2878; @CL-CTF-2879; @CL-CTF-2880]. Final payment is conditioned on a release subject only to the listed exceptions [@CL-CTF-2881; @CL-CTF-2882; @CL-CTF-2883; @CL-CTF-2884; @CL-CTF-2885; @CL-CTF-2886]. Payment follows the Prompt Payment Act, and an early payment discount runs from the invoice date, with payment treated as made on the payment check date or the specified electronic funds transfer date [@CL-CTF-2887; @CL-CTF-2888; @CL-CTF-2889].

Under the RFO text, which DoD contracting officers use in place of the codified text because Class Deviation 2026-O0038 directs it, effective February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762], the comparison with the codified text is as follows. The RFO Alternate I substitutes paragraphs (a), (b), (i), (l) and (m) for those in the basic clause, where the codified Alternate I lists (a), (e), (i), (l) and (m) [@CL-CTF-2668; @CL-CTF-0017]. In the RFO text the definitions are paragraph (a), which incorporates FAR 52.202-1 by reference as the codified text does at paragraph (e)(1) [@CL-CTF-2719; @CL-CTF-0029], and inspection and acceptance is paragraph (b) [@CL-CTF-0018]. RFO paragraph (b)(1) lets the Government inspect and test at all places and times before acceptance without the codified words "to the extent practicable", "including the period of performance" and "in any event", and it does not print the codified sentence on inspecting the plant of the Contractor or a subcontractor [@CL-CTF-2669; @CL-CTF-2670; @CL-CTF-0018]. The RFO text keeps the 60-day presumed acceptance rule [@CL-CTF-2672; @CL-CTF-0019], the time limit of 6 months or such other time as the contract specifies for requiring replacement or correction [@CL-CTF-2673; @CL-CTF-0020; @CL-CTF-0021], and the 10 percent profit portion unless otherwise specified [@CL-CTF-2675; @CL-CTF-0023]. RFO paragraph (b)(4) points to paragraph (b)(6) for the exception to the cost rule where the codified text points to paragraph (a)(6) [@CL-CTF-2674; @CL-CTF-0021; @CL-CTF-0022], and RFO paragraph (b)(6) cites paragraphs (b)(4) and (5) [@CL-CTF-2680; @CL-CTF-0026]. The RFO definition also says that a subcontract includes, but is not limited to, purchase orders, and changes and modifications to purchase orders [@CL-CTF-9501]. The RFO text keeps the hourly rate computation [@CL-CTF-2697; @CL-CTF-0031], the rule on labor hours worked by unqualified individuals [@CL-CTF-2699; @CL-CTF-0032] and the overtime rule [@CL-CTF-2850; @CL-CTF-0033]. It also keeps the reimbursement of the actual cost of materials, less rebates, refunds or discounts identifiable to the contract and except as provided in paragraphs (i)(1)(ii)(A) and (D)(2), provided the Contractor has made the payments in accordance with the agreement or invoice or makes them within 30 days of its payment request to the Government in accordance with the agreement or invoice [@CL-CTF-2703; @CL-CTF-2704; @CL-CTF-2705; @CL-CTF-9002; @CL-CTF-9003; @CL-CTF-9004]. The RFO total cost sentence says the total cost shall not exceed the ceiling price without the codified opening words "It is estimated that" [@CL-CTF-2706; @CL-CTF-0034]. It keeps the rule that, when and to the extent the ceiling price has been increased, hours expended and material costs incurred above the earlier ceiling before the increase are allowable to the same extent as if incurred after it [@CL-CTF-2712; @CL-CTF-2713; @CL-CTF-9005; @CL-CTF-9006]. It keeps the Contracting Officer's access to records before final payment, limited to the listed records unless the Contractor and the Contracting Officer otherwise agree [@CL-CTF-2714; @CL-CTF-0039]. The listed records are those that verify that the employees whose time is invoiced meet the labor category qualifications [@CL-CTF-2715; @CL-CTF-9018], for labor hours, when timecards are required as substantiation for payment, the original timecards, the timekeeping procedures, the records of the distribution of labor and the employees whose time is invoiced [@CL-CTF-2716; @CL-CTF-2717; @CL-CTF-9019; @CL-CTF-9020], and for material and subcontract costs reimbursed on the basis of actual cost the invoices, subcontract agreements and payment documents [@CL-CTF-2718; @CL-CTF-9021]. On interest, the RFO text keeps simple interest unless paid within 30 days of becoming due [@CL-CTF-2867; @CL-CTF-0041], at the rate provided in 41 U.S.C. 7109 for the period in which the amount becomes due and then for each six-month period until paid [@CL-CTF-2868; @CL-CTF-9017], and the due date rule [@CL-CTF-2874; @CL-CTF-0043], but its final decision sentence omits the codified words "as required by 33.211" [@CL-CTF-2870; @CL-CTF-0042]. ## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A time-and-materials contract uses Alternate I and has a ceiling price of 50,000 in the Schedule and an hourly rate of 100. The contractor performs 120.5 direct labor hours on tasks with specified labor qualifications, of which 10 hours were worked by an individual who does not meet the qualifications, and the Contracting Officer has not authorized those hours. The payable hours are 110.5, so the labor payment is 11,050, with the half hour prorated. The contractor also furnishes a commercial product at an established catalog price of 2,000 for the quantities and requirements involved, so the price cannot exceed 2,000, and other materials with an actual cost of 3,000 less a 100 rebate identifiable to the contract. The Government reimburses 2,900 for those materials if the contractor has paid for them or pays within 30 days of submitting its payment request. Costs and payments accrued so far are 38,000, and the contractor has reason to believe that the next 30 days of hourly rate payments and material costs will add 6,000. The total, 44,000, exceeds 85 percent of the ceiling price, which is 42,500, so the contractor notifies the Contracting Officer with a revised estimate, supporting reasons and documentation. The Government owes nothing above 50,000 and the contractor need not continue past it. Later the Contracting Officer notifies the contractor in writing that the ceiling price is increased to 60,000. The 2,000 of hours and material costs the contractor incurred above 50,000 before that notice are allowable as if incurred after it. Services are delivered on Day 1 and neither accepted nor rejected, so they are presumed accepted 60 days after delivery. Within 6 months after acceptance of the last delivery the Government requires 8 hours of correction of services that failed to meet requirements at delivery. Assume no other specification and no fraud or similar cause. The hourly rate is reduced by the 10 percent profit portion to 90, so the correction hours are costed at 720 under the payment paragraph. At a later point the Government terminates for convenience. The contractor had expended 300 direct labor hours before the effective date, which at 100 is 30,000, less 25,000 of hourly rate payments already made, which is 5,000. It also demonstrates 1,500 of reasonable charges from its standard record keeping system, of which 200 reasonably could have been avoided, so 1,300 is paid. Answer for the termination: 6,300, with no cost accounting standards required and no Government audit right under that paragraph. Had the Government instead terminated for cause with 40 hours of work not delivered or accepted, those hours would be paid at the rate of 90 under paragraph (i), 3,600, and if the default termination were found improper it would be deemed a termination for convenience. Separately, the Government finds that 500 of an earlier payment was not properly payable, so that payment is reduced by 500 and the contractor must pay the 500 within 30 days unless the parties agree otherwise. The first written demand for payment is on Day 20 and no earlier date is fixed under the contract, so the 500 is due on Day 20. If the contractor pays on Day 40, which is within 30 days of becoming due, no interest accrues, but had it paid on Day 60, simple interest would run for the actual calendar days from Day 20 until the designated office received payment. After the work is complete the contractor submits the completion invoice within 1 year of completion, and at final payment it and each assignee must deliver a release as a condition precedent, with a claim for a specified stated amount preserved as an exception. If the Schedule had no overtime rates and the Contracting Officer had approved 4 of the hours as overtime in advance, overtime rates would be negotiated rather than the rate of 100 being varied, and a failure to agree would be a dispute. Under the RFO text paragraph (m) also says the Government will send a cure notice to the Contractor, unless the reason for the termination is late delivery [@CL-CTF-0049].

## Never confuse

Alternate I substitutes paragraphs (a), (e), (i), (l), and (m) for those in the basic clause [@CL-CTF-2668]. Its cause termination pays under paragraph (i), with the profit portion removed from the hourly rate for labor hours expended in furnishing work not delivered to or accepted by the Government, while the basic clause says the Government shall not be liable for any amount for supplies or services not accepted [@CL-CTF-2693; @CL-CTF-2694; @CL-CTF-2449]. If the contractor has reason to believe that the hourly rate payments and material costs accruing in the next succeeding 30 days, added to all other payments and costs previously accrued, will exceed 85 percent of the ceiling price, it notifies the Contracting Officer with a revised estimate [@CL-CTF-2708; @CL-CTF-2709], and the ceiling price limits apply unless and until the Contracting Officer notifies the contractor in writing that the ceiling price has been increased [@CL-CTF-2711]. A further notice is owed if the contractor has reason to believe that the total price will be substantially greater or less than the then stated ceiling price [@CL-CTF-2858]. Interest on an amount the contractor owes is not charged when it is paid within 30 days of becoming due, and the due date is the earlier of the date fixed under the contract and the date of the first written demand [@CL-CTF-2867; @CL-CTF-2874]. Correction of defects is costed at the hourly rate reduced by the profit portion, while correction of failures caused by the listed fraud or unqualified-employee causes is without cost to the Government [@CL-CTF-2675; @CL-CTF-2680]. See [Payments under time-and-materials and labor-hour contracts](ctf-tm-and-lh-payments.md), [Time-and-materials contracts](ctf-time-and-materials-contracts.md) and [Inspection under time-and-materials and labor-hour contracts](../../supply-chain/sourcing-evidence/sup-inspection-time-and-materials-labor-hour.md). The codified text lets the Government inspect the plant of the Contractor or a subcontractor, but the RFO paragraph (b)(1) does not print that sentence [@CL-CTF-2670; @CL-CTF-0018]. The RFO cause termination adds a sentence that the Government will send a cure notice to the Contractor, unless the reason for the termination is late delivery [@CL-CTF-0049]. The codified electronic funds transfer paragraph is not printed in the RFO text, where prompt payment is followed by discount [@CL-CTF-2887; @CL-CTF-0047].
