## Purpose

A fixed-price incentive (firm target) contract shares cost risk between the government and the contractor by adjusting profit and establishing the final contract price through a negotiated formula based on cost performance against target. It caps government expenditure at a stated price ceiling while providing a financial incentive for effective cost control.

## The rule

A fixed-price incentive contract is a fixed-price contract that provides for adjusting profit and establishing the final contract price by applying a formula based on the relationship of total final negotiated cost to total target cost [@CL-EST-1568]. A fixed-price incentive (firm target) contract specifies a target cost, a target profit, a price ceiling without any profit ceiling or floor, and a profit adjustment formula, all negotiated at the outset [@CL-EST-1076].

The price ceiling is the maximum that may be paid to the contractor, except for any adjustment under other contract clauses [@CL-EST-1077]. Under the negotiated formula, a final cost below target cost yields a final profit above target profit, whereas a final cost above target cost yields a final profit below target profit or even a net loss [@CL-EST-1078]. If the final negotiated cost exceeds the price ceiling, the contractor absorbs the difference as a loss [@CL-EST-1105].

## How it works

The contract type is appropriate when a firm-fixed-price contract is not suitable and the parties can negotiate at the outset a firm target cost, target profit, and profit adjustment formula that provide a fair and reasonable incentive, under a ceiling where the contractor assumes an appropriate share of the risk [@CL-EST-1569; @CL-EST-1079]. It may be used only when the contractor's accounting system is adequate to support negotiation of final cost and incentive price revision, and adequate cost or pricing information is available at initial negotiation to establish reasonable firm targets [@CL-EST-1080; @CL-EST-1081]. Fixed-price incentive contracts are preferred when contract costs and performance requirements are reasonably certain, allowing the contractor to assume substantial cost responsibility and an appropriate share of cost risk [@CL-EST-1054].

Solicitations and contracts contemplating this contract type insert the clause at FAR 52.216-16, Incentive Price Revision—Firm Target [@CL-CTF-1248]. Billing prices may be adjusted within ceiling limits upon request of either party when it becomes apparent that final negotiated cost will differ substantially from target cost [@CL-EST-1571]. See [Fixed-price incentive (firm target) contracts](../../estimating/pricing/est-fpi-firm-target.md), [Fixed-price incentive (successive targets) contracts](../../estimating/pricing/est-fpi-successive-targets.md), and [Incentive price revision clause with a firm target: data, final price and billing prices](../../estimating/pricing/est-incentive-price-revision-firm-target-clause.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contract specifies a target cost of 800,000 dollars, a target profit of 80,000 dollars, and a ceiling price of 920,000 dollars, using an invented sharing arrangement where the contractor bears 20 percent of any cost over or under target. In an underrun where final cost finishes at 700,000 dollars, the 100,000 dollar underrun provides the contractor with 20 percent or 20,000 dollars in additional profit, resulting in a profit of 100,000 dollars and a final price of 800,000 dollars. In an overrun where final cost finishes at 900,000 dollars, the 100,000 dollar overrun reduces profit by 20 percent or 20,000 dollars to a formula profit of 60,000 dollars, yielding a formula price of 960,000 dollars; however, the ceiling price caps the price at 920,000 dollars, so profit is 20,000 dollars. This sharing ratio is invented, the example is illustrative only, and the contract formula itself governs.

## Never confuse

A fixed-price incentive (firm target) contract is not a firm-fixed-price contract, which provides for a price not subject to any adjustment on the basis of cost experience [@CL-EST-1150]. It also differs from a fixed-price incentive contract with successive targets, where initial target cost and target profit are negotiated at the outset and firm targets are established later at an agreed production point [@CL-EST-1082; @CL-EST-1084]. Finally, it is not a cost-plus-incentive-fee contract, which is a cost-reimbursement contract that adjusts fee within minimum and maximum fee bounds rather than establishing a firm ceiling price [@CL-EST-1090; @CL-EST-1093; @CL-EST-1077].
