## Purpose

Explains the excise tax on payments to foreign persons under certain foreign procurements, who counts as a foreign person, and how the withholding is applied at payment.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CTF-2553] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.229-11 and FAR 52.229-12 [@CL-CTF-2553; @CL-CTF-2556] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.229-11 and RFO FAR 52.229-12 [@CL-CTF-0370; @CL-CTF-0375] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CTF-2553] |
| Applies to | Foreign persons receiving specified Federal procurement payments [@CL-CTF-2553; @CL-CTF-2554] |

Unless exempted, a 2 percent tax applies to the amount of a specified Federal procurement payment received by a foreign person [@CL-CTF-2553]. A foreign person is any person other than a United States person [@CL-CTF-2554].

A specified Federal procurement payment is a payment under a contract with a foreign contracting party for goods manufactured or produced, or services provided, in a foreign country that is not a party to an international procurement agreement with the United States [@CL-CTF-2555]. In that definition, for purposes of the prior sentence, a foreign country does not include an outlying area [@CL-CTF-2824]. Taxes imposed under section 5000C of title 26 of the United States Code may not be included in the contract price and may not be reimbursed [@CL-CTF-2559].

The definition of a United States person in section 7701(a)(30) of title 26 of the United States Code lists a citizen or resident of the United States, a domestic partnership, a domestic corporation, and any estate other than a foreign estate [@CL-CTF-2825]. It also lists any trust if a court within the United States is able to exercise primary supervision over the administration of the trust and one or more United States persons have the authority to control all substantial decisions of the trust [@CL-CTF-2826]. The clause applies only to foreign persons and implements section 5000C of title 26 of the United States Code and its implementing regulations at 26 CFR 1.5000C-1 through 1.5000C-7 [@CL-CTF-2833]. The clause repeats that definition: a United States person is a citizen or resident of the United States, a domestic partnership, a domestic corporation, or any estate other than a foreign estate [@CL-CTF-2895]. The clause also lists any trust if a court within the United States is able to exercise primary supervision over the administration of the trust and one or more United States persons have the authority to control all substantial decisions of the trust [@CL-CTF-2896].

## How it works

In the offer provision, the Offeror represents, for purposes of withholding under section 5000C of title 26 of the United States Code, whether it is or is not a foreign person [@CL-CTF-2829]. An Offeror that indicates it is a foreign person represents that it is claiming on the IRS Form W-14 either a full exemption or a partial or no exemption from the excise tax, and selects one [@CL-CTF-2830]. If the Offeror represents in paragraph (d)(1) that it is a foreign person, the clause on this tax will be included in any resulting contract [@CL-CTF-2639]. That Offeror submits the IRS Form W-14 with its offer, and if the form is not submitted with the offer, exemptions will not be applied to any resulting contract and the Government will withhold a full 2 percent of each payment [@CL-CTF-2560]. An Offeror that selects “is” in paragraph (d)(1) and “partial or no exemption” in paragraph (d)(2) will be subject to withholding in accordance with the clause at FAR 52.229-12 in any resulting contract [@CL-CTF-2831].

Withholding is applied at the payment level, not at the contract level [@CL-CTF-2556]. The Government withholds a full 2 percent of each payment unless the Contractor claims an exemption [@CL-CTF-2834]. A Contractor that is a foreign person with only a partial or no exemption to the withholding [@CL-CTF-2640] includes IRS Form W-14 with each voucher or invoice submitted under the contract throughout the period in which this status is applicable [@CL-CTF-2641]. The Contractor should revise each IRS Form W-14 submission to reflect the exemption, if any, that applies to that particular invoice, such as a different exemption applying [@CL-CTF-2897]. In the absence of a completed IRS Form W-14 accompanying a payment request, the default withholding percentage is 2 percent for that payment request [@CL-CTF-2557].

If the Contractor enters a ratio in Line 12 of the IRS Form W-14, the result of Line 11 divided by Line 10, the Government withholds from each payment an amount equal to 2 percent multiplied by the contract ratio [@CL-CTF-2644]. If the Contractor marks box 9 of the form rather than completing Lines 10 through 12, the Contractor must identify and enter the specific exempt and nonexempt amounts in Line 15, and the Government then withholds 2 percent only from the nonexempt amount [@CL-CTF-2645].

A Contractor that is a foreign person and has indicated in its offer in the offer provision that it is fully exempt from the withholding [@CL-CTF-2642] may lose that exemption. This happens if it certified the full exemption on the form and a change in circumstances during contract performance causes it to become subject to the withholding for the 2 percent excise tax [@CL-CTF-2643]. It then notifies the Contracting Officer within 30 days of the change in circumstances and complies with paragraph (c)(1) of the clause [@CL-CTF-2558].

Exemptions from withholding under the offer provision are described at 26 CFR 1.5000C-1(d)(5) through (7), and the Offeror would claim an exemption from the withholding by using the Department of the Treasury Internal Revenue Service Form W-14 [@CL-CTF-2827]. Any exemption claimed and self-certified on the IRS Form W-14 is subject to audit by the IRS, and any disputes regarding the imposition and collection of the tax under section 5000C of title 26 of the United States Code are adjudicated by the IRS because that tax is a tax matter, not a contract issue [@CL-CTF-2828]. The clause says the same [@CL-CTF-2835]. The offer provision states that the IRS Form W-14 is provided to the acquiring agency rather than to the IRS [@CL-CTF-2898]. The offer provision also has an informational paragraph: a taxpayer may, for a fee, seek advice from the IRS as to the proper tax treatment of a transaction, which is called a private letter ruling [@CL-CTF-2832]. The same paragraph says the IRS may publish a revenue ruling, which is an official interpretation by the IRS of the Internal Revenue Code, related statutes, tax treaties, and regulations, and is the conclusion of the IRS on how the law is applied to a specific set of facts [@CL-CTF-2899]. That paragraph also directs questions on the interpretation of the IRS regulations to the IRS tax law questions web page [@CL-CTF-2900].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text from February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. The RFO text of FAR 52.229-11 keeps the 2 percent tax, unless exempted, on a specified Federal procurement payment received by a foreign person [@CL-CTF-2553; @CL-CTF-0370], the rule that, if the Offeror represents it is a foreign person in paragraph (d)(1), the clause at FAR 52.229-12 will be included in any resulting contract [@CL-CTF-2639; @CL-CTF-9077] and the Offeror submits the IRS Form W-14 with its offer or exemptions are not applied to any resulting contract and the Government withholds a full 2 percent of each payment [@CL-CTF-2560; @CL-CTF-0373], and the withholding under FAR 52.229-12 for an Offeror that selects "is" and "partial or no exemption" [@CL-CTF-2831; @CL-CTF-0374]. It says the Offeror "may claim" an exemption by using the IRS Form W-14, where the codified text says the Offeror "would claim" it [@CL-CTF-0372; @CL-CTF-2827]. The RFO text of FAR 52.229-12 keeps the rules that the clause applies only to foreign persons [@CL-CTF-2833; @CL-CTF-0375], that the withholding is applied at the payment level [@CL-CTF-2556; @CL-CTF-0376], that the default withholding percentage is 2 percent without a completed form [@CL-CTF-2557; @CL-CTF-0377], that the Government withholds a full 2 percent of each payment unless the Contractor claims an exemption [@CL-CTF-2834; @CL-CTF-0381], and that section 5000C taxes may not be included in the contract price or reimbursed [@CL-CTF-2559; @CL-CTF-0382]. For a Contractor that loses a full exemption, it lists the conditions as items (i) to (iii) [@CL-CTF-0378; @CL-CTF-2642] [@CL-CTF-0379; @CL-CTF-2643] and numbers the two duties, notice within 30 days and compliance with paragraph (c)(1), as (A) and (B) where the codified text numbers them (i) and (ii) [@CL-CTF-0380; @CL-CTF-2558].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A foreign contractor with only a partial exemption invoices 100,000 and sends a completed IRS Form W-14 with a Line 12 contract ratio of one half. The Government withholds 2 percent multiplied by that ratio, which is 1,000. A second invoice of 80,000 arrives with no completed form, so the default withholding is 2 percent of that payment request, which is 1,600. A third invoice of 60,000 arrives with box 9 marked, and Line 15 shows 20,000 as exempt and 40,000 as nonexempt, so the Government withholds 2 percent of the 40,000, which is 800. Each result is worked out per payment, not per contract.

A different contractor certified a full exemption in its offer. Midway through performance a change in circumstances makes it subject to the withholding, so it notifies the Contracting Officer within 30 days and sends a form with each invoice from then on. A payment of 25,000 to a contractor that has claimed no exemption is withheld at a full 2 percent, which is 500. A trust whose administration a court within the United States can supervise, and whose substantial decisions United States persons control, is a United States person, so it is not a foreign person. The clause lists a domestic partnership among United States persons, so a domestic partnership is not a foreign person either. When the partial-exemption contractor's second invoice differs from its first, it should revise the form to show the exemption that applies to that invoice, and the form goes to the acquiring agency, not to the IRS. An offeror selects “is” and “partial or no exemption”, so withholding under the clause follows in the resulting contract. A payment for goods made in an outlying area is not for goods produced in a foreign country, because a foreign country does not include an outlying area. The IRS may audit an exemption that the contractor self-certified on the form, and a dispute about the tax goes to the IRS, not to the contracting officer. An offeror that represents it is a foreign person but submits no form with its offer has no exemption applied, and the Government withholds 2 percent of each payment, which is 1,000 on a 50,000 payment. None of this tax is added to the contract price or reimbursed.

## Never confuse

The withholding is a payment-level collection of a Federal excise tax on foreign persons, not a price adjustment and not a tax cost that can be added back into the price or reimbursed [@CL-CTF-2556; @CL-CTF-2559]. It is also different from the host-country taxes handled in the foreign fixed-price and foreign cost-reimbursement clauses, which turn on what the price includes or what is allowable. See [Taxes under foreign fixed-price contracts](ctf-foreign-fixed-price-taxes.md). The same tax is barred from the price in the general [Federal, State, and Local Taxes clause](ctf-federal-state-local-taxes.md). Invoice mechanics sit at [Proper invoice content](ctf-proper-invoice-content.md).

The codified definition says a foreign country does not include an outlying area [@CL-CTF-2824], while the RFO text says it does not include an outlying area of the United States [@CL-CTF-0371].
