## Purpose

Shows which fuel purchases qualify for relief in the United Kingdom, what the contractor must do and show, and how the price and any rebate are treated.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-CTF-1887] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 252.229-7009 and 252.229-7010 [@CL-CTF-1887; @CL-CTF-1891] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CTF-1887] |
| Applies to | Contracts that include the passenger vehicle fuel relief clause [@CL-CTF-1924] or the fuel customs duty relief clause [@CL-CTF-1891] |

Under the passenger vehicle clause, pursuant to an agreement between the United States Government and Her Majesty's Customs and Excise, fuels and lubricants used by passenger vehicles (except taxis) in the performance of the contract will be exempt from customs duty and value added tax [@CL-CTF-1887]. Therefore, the procedures outlined in the named customs notice, and any amendment to it, shall be used to obtain relief from both customs duty and value added tax for fuel used under the contract [@CL-CTF-1913]. Those procedures apply to both loaded and unloaded miles [@CL-CTF-1888].

Under the other fuel clause, if vehicle fuels and lubricants are used in support of the contract, the contractor must seek relief from customs duty in accordance with the cited customs notice [@CL-CTF-1891]. Application for that relief should be sent to the contractor's local Customs and Excise Office [@CL-CTF-1914]. Within 30 days after award, the contractor must give the Contracting Officer evidence that an attempt to obtain relief has been initiated [@CL-CTF-1892].

## How it works

In the passenger vehicle clause, unit prices are based on the recoupment by the contractor of customs duty under the listed allowances [@CL-CTF-1888]. The allowances are one gallon for every 27 miles for vehicles (except taxis) with a seating capacity of less than 29, one gallon for every 13 miles for vehicles with a seating capacity of 29-53, and one gallon for every 10 miles for vehicles with a seating capacity of 54 or more [@CL-CTF-1889]. If route mileage is increased or decreased within 10 percent with no change in route price, the customs duty is reclaimed on actual mileage performed [@CL-CTF-1890]. In the other clause, if the contractor does not attempt relief in time, the Contracting Officer may deduct from the contract price the relief that would have been allowed if the customs authority had favorably considered the request [@CL-CTF-1893]. Under that clause, any rebate granted by the customs authority is paid in full to the Government by check payable to the Treasurer of the United States, sent to the Administrative Contracting Officer [@CL-CTF-1894].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

Part one, passenger vehicle clause. A route is run by a vehicle with an assumed seating capacity of 20, so the allowance is one gallon for every 27 miles. For 2,700 assumed miles that is 100 gallons. The route mileage then rises by an assumed 5 percent with no change in route price, so the customs duty is reclaimed on the actual mileage performed.

Part two, the other fuel clause, on a different assumed contract. The contractor must show evidence of an application within 30 days of award. If it files nothing, the Contracting Officer may deduct the assumed 6,000 of relief that would have been granted. Any rebate it does receive is paid in full to the Government.

## Never confuse

The passenger vehicle clause covers both customs duty and value added tax, while the other fuel clause has the contractor seek relief from customs duty [@CL-CTF-1887; @CL-CTF-1891]. The allowances are the basis on which unit prices recoup customs duty [@CL-CTF-1888; @CL-CTF-1889]. Under the other fuel clause, a rebate granted by the customs authority is paid in full to the Government [@CL-CTF-1894]. If the contractor does not attempt to obtain relief within the time specified, the Contracting Officer may deduct from the contract price the amount of relief that would have been allowed [@CL-CTF-1893]. For the FAR tax clause, see [Federal, State, and Local Taxes clause](ctf-federal-state-local-taxes.md). Value added tax on the price itself and duty on imported items are on [United Kingdom VAT exclusion and relief from import duty](ctf-dfars-uk-vat-exclusion-and-import-duty-relief.md).
