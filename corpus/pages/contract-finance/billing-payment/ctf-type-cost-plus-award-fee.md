## Purpose

A cost-plus-award-fee contract reimburses allowable incurred costs and provides a fee consisting of a fixed base amount and a judgmental award amount. It provides an incentive for excellence across cost, schedule and technical performance where objective formulas cannot be devised.

## The rule

A cost-plus-award-fee contract is a cost-reimbursement contract providing for a fee consisting of a base amount, which may be zero, fixed at inception and an award amount based upon a judgmental evaluation by the Government [@CL-EST-1064]. The award amount is one that the contractor may earn in whole or in part during performance and that is sufficient to motivate excellence in cost, schedule, and technical performance [@CL-EST-1067; @CL-EST-9744].

The amount of award fee earned must be commensurate with the contractor's overall cost, schedule, and technical performance as measured against contract requirements in accordance with the criteria stated in the award-fee plan [@CL-EST-1058]. Award fee shall not be earned if the contractor's overall cost, schedule, and technical performance in the aggregate is below satisfactory [@CL-EST-1059].

## How it works

The contract type is suitable when the work is such that it is neither feasible nor effective to devise predetermined objective incentive targets applicable to cost, schedule, and technical performance [@CL-EST-1056], and additional administrative effort and cost required to monitor and evaluate performance are justified by expected benefits in a risk and cost benefit analysis [@CL-EST-1057]. A determination and finding signed by the head of the contracting activity must be completed for all award-fee contracts, justifying that the use of this type of contract is in the best interest of the Government [@CL-EST-1055]. Conditions that must be met before award include complying with cost-reimbursement limitations and completing an award-fee plan [@CL-EST-1063].

All contracts providing for award fees must be supported by an award-fee plan that establishes procedures for evaluating award fee and an Award-Fee Board for conducting the evaluation [@CL-EST-1060], and the plan must be approved by the fee determining official unless otherwise authorized by agency procedures [@CL-EST-1061]. Award-fee provisions may also be used in fixed-price contracts when contractor performance cannot be measured objectively [@CL-EST-1065], establishing a fixed price including normal profit paid for satisfactory performance with earned award fee paid in addition to that price [@CL-EST-1066]. See [Award-fee contracts: cost-plus-award-fee and fixed-price with award fee](../../estimating/pricing/est-award-fee-contracts.md), [DoD cost-plus-award-fee contracts: final-period fee, base fee and health or safety](../../estimating/pricing/est-dod-cost-plus-award-fee-requirements.md) and [Incentive contracts: when they fit and how they are structured](../../estimating/pricing/est-incentive-contracts-general.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A cost-plus-award-fee contract has an estimated cost of 5,000,000, an invented base fee of 100,000 (representing 2 percent of estimated cost), and an invented award-fee pool of 300,000. At the end of an evaluation period, the Award-Fee Board evaluates contractor performance and the fee determining official awards an invented 60 percent of the available award-fee pool, which equals 180,000. The total fee earned for that period equals the base fee of 100,000 plus the awarded fee of 180,000, for a total fee of 280,000 paid on top of allowable costs reimbursed. The base fee, award-fee pool and 60 percent rating are invented. This example is illustrative only and the contract itself governs.

## Never confuse

A cost-plus-award-fee contract differs from a cost-plus-incentive-fee contract, where fee is adjusted by a predetermined formula based on total allowable costs relative to target costs [@CL-EST-1090; @CL-EST-1092], and from a fixed-price contract with award fee, which pays a fixed price including normal profit for satisfactory performance and pays any earned award fee in addition to that price [@CL-EST-1065; @CL-EST-1066].
