## Purpose

Shows when title passes to the Government, how and when the contractor is paid under a ship repair job order, and what the contractor must do about liens.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-CTF-1955; @CL-CTF-1956] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 252.217-7006, DFARS 252.217-7007 and DFARS 252.217-7014 [@CL-CTF-1955; @CL-CTF-1956; @CL-CTF-1961] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CTF-1955] |
| Applies to | Job orders under a ship repair Master Agreement that includes the Title, Payments and Discharge of Liens clauses [@CL-CTF-1955; @CL-CTF-1956; @CL-CTF-1961] |

Unless otherwise provided, title to all materials and equipment to be incorporated in a vessel in performing a job order vests in the Government on delivery at the location specified for the work [@CL-CTF-1955]. The Government makes progress payments as work progresses under the job order, on invoices in the form and number of copies directed and approved by the Contracting Officer [@CL-CTF-1956]. Generally the contractor may submit invoices on a semi-monthly basis, unless expenditures justify more frequent submission, and the Government need not make progress payments for invoices aggregating less than $5,000 [@CL-CTF-1957]. Upon request, the contractor will furnish the Contracting Officer any reports concerning expenditures on the work to date that the Contracting Officer may require [@CL-CTF-2383]. The Contracting Officer may direct that progress payments be based on the job order price as adjusted by change orders under the Changes clause [@CL-CTF-2384]. If the Contracting Officer does not so direct, payments of any increases shall be made from time to time after the amount of the increase is determined under the Changes clause of the Master Agreement [@CL-CTF-2385], and reductions resulting from decreases shall be made for the purposes of subsequent progress payments as soon as the amounts are determined under the Changes clause of the Master Agreement [@CL-CTF-2386].

The Contracting Officer shall approve progress payments based on the value, computed on the price of the job order, of labor and materials incorporated in the work, materials suitably stored at the site of the work and preparatory work completed, less the aggregate of any previous payments [@CL-CTF-1982]. The Government will retain, until final completion and acceptance of all work covered by the job order, an amount estimated or approved by the Contracting Officer under paragraph (b) of the Payments clause [@CL-CTF-1958]. The amount retained will be in accordance with the rate authorized by Congress for Naval vessel repair contracts at the time of job order award [@CL-CTF-2163]. Upon completion of the work under a job order and final inspection and acceptance, and upon submission of invoices in such form and with such copies as the Contracting Officer may prescribe [@CL-CTF-1983], the contractor shall be paid the price of the job order, as adjusted pursuant to the Changes clause of the Master Agreement, less any performance reserves deemed necessary by the Contracting Officer, and less the amount of any previous payments [@CL-CTF-1959].

## How it works

Property covered by progress payments becomes the sole property of the Government when those payments are made, and is subject to the Title clause [@CL-CTF-1960]. Upon completion of the job order, or with the approval of the Contracting Officer during performance of the job order, all contractor-furnished materials and equipment not incorporated in, or placed on, any vessel shall become the property of the contractor, unless the Government has reimbursed the contractor for the cost of the materials and equipment [@CL-CTF-2165; @CL-CTF-2164]. The vessel, its equipment, movable stores, cargo, or other ship's materials shall not be considered Government-furnished property [@CL-CTF-2382]. The contractor shall immediately discharge, or cause to be discharged, any lien or right in rem of any kind, other than in favor of the Government, that exists or arises in connection with work done or material furnished under any job order under the agreement [@CL-CTF-1961]. If any lien or right in rem is not immediately discharged, the Government, at the expense of the contractor, may discharge, or cause to be discharged, the lien or right [@CL-CTF-1962].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A job order is priced at 200,000, and the Government has paid 150,000 in progress payments, each approved on the value of labor, materials and preparatory work in the job. The Government holds back the retention at the rate authorized by Congress for Naval vessel repair contracts when the job order was awarded, and keeps it until final completion and acceptance. At completion, final inspection and acceptance are done and the invoices are in the form the Contracting Officer prescribed. The Contracting Officer deems a performance reserve of 6,000 necessary. The final payment is 200,000 less 6,000 less 150,000, which is 44,000. The Contracting Officer asks for a report of expenditures to date, and the contractor furnishes it. Had the Contracting Officer directed payments on the adjusted price, a 20,000 increase from a change order would be paid once its amount was determined. Separately, invoices that add up to only 3,000 have not reached the 5,000 level, so the Government need not make a progress payment on them yet.

## Never confuse

Unless otherwise provided, title to all materials and equipment to be incorporated in a vessel in the performance of a job order shall vest in the Government upon delivery at the location specified for the performance of the work [@CL-CTF-1955]. Materials, equipment, or any other property or work in process covered by the progress payments shall become the sole property of the Government upon the making of those progress payments [@CL-CTF-1960]. The Government will retain until final completion and acceptance of all work covered by the job order an amount estimated or approved by the Contracting Officer, at the rate authorized by Congress for Naval vessel repair contracts at the time of job order award [@CL-CTF-1958; @CL-CTF-2163]; upon completion of the work under a job order and final inspection and acceptance, and upon submission of invoices in such form and with such copies as the Contracting Officer may prescribe [@CL-CTF-1983], the contractor shall be paid the price of the job order, as adjusted pursuant to the Changes clause of the Master Agreement, less any performance reserves deemed necessary by the Contracting Officer, and less the amount of any previous payments [@CL-CTF-1959]. If the Government terminates the job order in whole or in part as provided in paragraph (a) of the Default clause [@CL-CTF-1846], the Government, in addition to any other rights provided in the clause [@CL-CTF-1847], may require the contractor to transfer title and delivery to the Government, in the manner and to the extent directed by the Contracting Officer, any completed supplies and such partially completed supplies and materials [@CL-CTF-1979], parts, tools, dies, jigs, fixtures, plans, drawings, information and contract rights [@CL-CTF-2317] as the contractor has specifically produced or specifically acquired for the performance of the terminated part of the job order [@CL-CTF-1980]; see [Ship repair master agreement: bonds and default (DFARS)](ctf-dfars-ship-repair-bonds-and-default.md). For the Changes clause that adjusts the job order price, see [Ship repair master agreement: property exchange, changes and job orders (DFARS)](ctf-dfars-ship-repair-exchange-changes-and-job-orders.md). For customary progress payment financing on other contracts, see [Customary progress payment rates (DoD)](../financing/ctf-progress-payments.md).
