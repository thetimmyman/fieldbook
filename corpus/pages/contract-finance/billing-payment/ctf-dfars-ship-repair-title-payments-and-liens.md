## Purpose

Shows when title passes to the Government, how and when the contractor is paid under a ship repair job order, and what the contractor must do about liens.

## The rule

|---|---|

| Codified citation | DFARS 252.217-7006, DFARS 252.217-7007 and DFARS 252.217-7014 [@CL-CTF-1955; @CL-CTF-1956; @CL-CTF-1961] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CTF-1955] |
| Applies to | Job orders under a ship repair Master Agreement that includes the Title, Payments and Discharge of Liens clauses [@CL-CTF-1955; @CL-CTF-1956; @CL-CTF-1961] |

Unless otherwise provided, title to all materials and equipment to be incorporated in a vessel in performing a job order vests in the Government on delivery at the location specified for the work [@CL-CTF-1955]. The Government makes progress payments as work progresses under the job order, on invoices in the form and number of copies directed and approved by the Contracting Officer [@CL-CTF-1956]. Generally the contractor may submit invoices on a semi-monthly basis, unless expenditures justify more frequent submission, and the Government need not make progress payments for invoices aggregating less than $5,000 [@CL-CTF-1957].

The Contracting Officer approves progress payments based on the value, computed on the price of the job order, of labor and materials incorporated in the work, materials suitably stored at the site of the work and preparatory work completed, less the aggregate of any previous payments [@CL-CTF-1982]. The Government retains an amount estimated or approved by the Contracting Officer until final completion and acceptance of all work covered by the job order [@CL-CTF-1958]. The amount retained follows the rate authorized by Congress for Naval vessel repair contracts at the time of job order award [@CL-CTF-2163]. Final payment is tied to completion of the work, final inspection and acceptance, and submission of invoices in the form and with the copies the Contracting Officer may prescribe [@CL-CTF-1983]. The contractor is then paid the job order price as adjusted for changes, less any performance reserves the Contracting Officer deems necessary and less previous payments [@CL-CTF-1959].

## How it works

Property covered by progress payments becomes the sole property of the Government when those payments are made, and is subject to the Title clause [@CL-CTF-1960]. Contractor-furnished materials and equipment not incorporated in, or placed on, any vessel become the contractor's property, unless the Government has reimbursed their cost, upon completion of the job order or with the Contracting Officer's approval during performance [@CL-CTF-2164; @CL-CTF-2165]. The contractor must immediately discharge any lien or right in rem, other than in favor of the Government, that arises in connection with work done or material furnished under a job order [@CL-CTF-1961]. If it does not, the Government may discharge the lien or right at the contractor's expense [@CL-CTF-1962].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A job order is priced at 200,000, and the Government has paid 150,000 in progress payments, each approved on the value of labor, materials and preparatory work in the job. The Government holds back the retention at the rate authorized by Congress for Naval vessel repair contracts when the job order was awarded, and keeps it until final completion and acceptance. At completion, final inspection and acceptance are done and the invoices are in the form the Contracting Officer prescribed. The Contracting Officer deems a performance reserve of 6,000 necessary. The final payment is 200,000 less 6,000 less 150,000, which is 44,000. Separately, invoices that add up to only 3,000 have not reached the 5,000 level, so the Government need not make a progress payment on them yet.

## Never confuse

Title to materials to be incorporated passes on delivery at the location of the work [@CL-CTF-1955]. Title to property covered by progress payments passes when the Government makes those payments [@CL-CTF-1960]. These are two different triggers. The amount retained until completion, which follows the rate authorized by Congress when the job order was awarded, is different from the performance reserve deducted at final payment, because the first is held during the job and the second reduces the final payment [@CL-CTF-1958; @CL-CTF-2163; @CL-CTF-1959]. The Government's right to require transfer of title to completed and partially completed items after a default termination is a separate mechanism [@CL-CTF-1979]; see [Ship repair master agreement: bonds and default (DFARS)](ctf-dfars-ship-repair-bonds-and-default.md). The price used at final payment is the job order price as adjusted under the Changes clause [@CL-CTF-1959]; see [Ship repair master agreement: property exchange, changes and job orders (DFARS)](ctf-dfars-ship-repair-exchange-changes-and-job-orders.md). For customary progress payment financing on other contracts, see [Customary progress payment rates (DoD)](../financing/ctf-progress-payments.md).
