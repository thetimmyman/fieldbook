## Purpose

Shows how the DFARS tax relief clause keeps exempt taxes out of the price, how the German customs exemption works, and what the receipt statement must say.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-CTF-1851] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 252.229-7001, 252.229-7002 and 252.229-7007 [@CL-CTF-1851; @CL-CTF-1859; @CL-CTF-1860] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CTF-1851] |
| Applies to | Contracts that include the tax relief clause [@CL-CTF-1916], its Alternate I [@CL-CTF-1855], the German customs exemption clause [@CL-CTF-1859] or the receipt verification clause [@CL-CTF-1860] |

Contract prices are exclusive of all taxes and duties from which the United States Government is exempt by virtue of tax agreements between the United States Government and the contractor's government [@CL-CTF-1851]. The clause identifies the taxes or duties excluded from the contract price by name of tax and rate (percentage), and the offeror inserts both [@CL-CTF-1901]. Invoices submitted in accordance with the terms and conditions of the contract must be exclusive of all taxes or duties for which relief is available, and the invoice must list separately the gross price, the amount of tax deducted and the net price charged [@CL-CTF-1852]. For items made to United States Government specifications, the contractor must identify the materials or components intended to be imported, so that relief from import duties is obtained [@CL-CTF-1853]. If the contractor intends to use imported products from inventories on hand whose price includes a factor for import duties, it must ensure the Government's exemption from those taxes [@CL-CTF-1854]. The contractor may obtain a refund of the import duties from its government, or request the duty-free import of an amount of supplies or components corresponding to that used from inventory for the contract [@CL-CTF-1902].

The German customs exemption clause authorizes the contractor to acquire duty-free the imported products required for the direct benefit of the United States Forces [@CL-CTF-1859]. It does so in accordance with the provisions of the agreement between the United States of America and Germany concerning tax relief to be accorded by Germany to United States expenditures in the interest of common defense [@CL-CTF-1903]. The receipt verification clause requires the contractor to insert, for Contracting Officer approval, a statement certifying that the items listed on the invoice have been received by the United States on all Material Inspection and Receiving Reports (DD Form 250 series) [@CL-CTF-1860].

## How it works

Alternate I of the tax relief clause adds a paragraph that the basic clause does not include [@CL-CTF-1855]. That paragraph says tax relief will be claimed in Germany under the agreement between the United States and Germany on tax relief for United States expenditures in the interest of common defense [@CL-CTF-1856]. Under that paragraph, the contractor must use the German performance certificate for tax-free deliveries, or other documentary evidence acceptable to the German tax authorities [@CL-CTF-1857]. Under the same paragraph, purchases made and paid for on a tax-free basis during a 30-day period may be accumulated, totaled and reported as tax-free [@CL-CTF-1858].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contract includes Alternate I. The offeror inserted one tax in the clause at an assumed rate of 10 percent. The delivery is priced at 100,000 before relief, of which 10,000 is a tax from which the Government is exempt. The invoice shows a gross price of 100,000, tax deducted of 10,000 and a net price charged of 90,000. In one 30-day period the contractor makes tax-free purchases of 5,000, 7,000 and 8,000, and it reports 20,000 as tax-free in one total. Each Material Inspection and Receiving Report carries the receipt statement for Contracting Officer approval.

## Never confuse

Under the customs exemption clause, imported products required for the direct benefit of the United States Forces are authorized to be acquired duty-free by the contractor [@CL-CTF-1859]. Under the tax relief clause, prices are exclusive of the taxes and duties from which the United States Government is exempt [@CL-CTF-1851], and invoices are exclusive of all taxes or duties for which relief is available [@CL-CTF-1852]. The receipt statement certifies that the items listed on the invoice have been received by the United States [@CL-CTF-1860]. Alternate I adds a paragraph (d), the German paragraph, that is not included in the basic clause [@CL-CTF-1855]. The FAR clause on applicable taxes is on [Federal, State, and Local Taxes clause](ctf-federal-state-local-taxes.md), and whether a tax is an allowable cost is on [Taxes](../../compliance/cas-allowability/cmp-taxes.md). The DoD clause on electronic submission of payment requests and receiving reports is on [DoD clause on electronic submission of payment requests and receiving reports](../financing/ctf-dod-electronic-payment-request-clause.md), and invoice content is on [Proper invoice: required content and payment documentation](ctf-proper-invoice-content.md). Clauses for other host countries are on [Italy tax exemptions and the matching representation](ctf-dfars-italy-tax-exemptions-and-representation.md) and [United Kingdom VAT exclusion and relief from import duty](ctf-dfars-uk-vat-exclusion-and-import-duty-relief.md).
