## Purpose

Shows how the North Carolina sales and use tax clause and the State of New Mexico gross receipts and compensating tax clause treat tax on materials and on contract payments, and what the Contractor furnishes, registers or pays under each.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CTF-2529] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.229-2 and FAR 52.229-10 [@CL-CTF-2529; @CL-CTF-2534] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.229-2 and RFO FAR 52.229-10 [@CL-CTF-0393; @CL-CTF-0398] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CTF-2529] |
| Applies to | North Carolina clause: construction to be performed in North Carolina [@CL-CTF-2529]. New Mexico clause: payments for performance within the State of New Mexico [@CL-CTF-2619] |

The North Carolina clause is inserted in solicitations and contracts for construction to be performed in North Carolina [@CL-CTF-2529]. In a fixed-price contract, the contract price includes North Carolina State and local sales and use taxes to be paid on materials, notwithstanding any other provision of the contract [@CL-CTF-2530]. In a cost-reimbursement contract, North Carolina State and local sales and use taxes the Contractor pays on materials are an allowable cost under the contract [@CL-CTF-2531]. Materials means building materials, supplies, fixtures, and equipment that become a part of or are annexed to any building or structure erected, altered, or repaired under the contract [@CL-CTF-2795]. If the requirement is for vessel repair to be performed in North Carolina, Alternate I substitutes its paragraph (a) for paragraph (a) of the basic clause [@CL-CTF-2802]. Under Alternate I, materials means materials, supplies, fixtures, and equipment that become a part of or are annexed to any vessel altered or repaired under the contract [@CL-CTF-2803].

At the time set by paragraph (d), the Contractor furnishes the Contracting Officer certified statements setting forth the cost of the materials purchased from each vendor and the amount of North Carolina State and local sales and use taxes paid [@CL-CTF-2532]. If the contract is completed before the next October 1, the certified statements are submitted within 60 days after completion [@CL-CTF-2533]. If it is not completed before the next October 1, the certified statements are submitted on or before November 30 of each year and cover taxes paid during the 12-month period that ended the preceding September 30 [@CL-CTF-2618].

If the Contractor makes several purchases from the same vendor, the certified statement indicates the invoice numbers, the inclusive dates of the invoices, the total amount of the invoices, and the North Carolina State and local sales and use taxes paid [@CL-CTF-2796]. The statement also includes the cost of any tangible personal property withdrawn from the Contractor's warehouse stock and the amount of North Carolina State and local sales or use tax paid on this property by the Contractor [@CL-CTF-2797]. Any local sales or use taxes included in the Contractor's statements must be shown separately from the State sales or use taxes [@CL-CTF-2798]. The Contractor furnishes any additional information the Commissioner of Revenue of the State of North Carolina may require to substantiate a refund claim for sales or use taxes [@CL-CTF-2799]. The Contractor also obtains and furnishes to the Contracting Officer similar certified statements by its subcontractors [@CL-CTF-2800]. The certified statements are in a form in which the Contractor or subcontractor certifies, for a stated period, that it paid North Carolina State and local sales and use taxes aggregating a stated State amount and a stated local amount [@CL-CTF-2801]. The form also states that the taxes were paid with respect to building materials, supplies, fixtures, and equipment that have become a part of or annexed to a building or structure erected, altered, or repaired for the United States of America [@CL-CTF-2891]. The form also certifies that the vendors from whom the property was purchased, the dates and numbers of the invoices covering the purchases, the total amount of the invoices of each vendor [@CL-CTF-2892], the North Carolina State and local sales and use taxes paid on the property (shown separately), and the cost of property withdrawn from warehouse stock and the taxes paid on it are as set forth in the attachments [@CL-CTF-2893].

In New Mexico, the Contractor advises the State of New Mexico of the contract within 30 days after award by registering with the State of New Mexico, Taxation and Revenue Department, Revenue Division, pursuant to the Tax Administration Act of the State of New Mexico, and identifies the contract number [@CL-CTF-2534; @CL-CTF-9621]. The Contractor pays the New Mexico gross receipts taxes assessed against the contract fee and costs paid for performance of the contract, or of any part or portion of it, within the State of New Mexico [@CL-CTF-2619]. The allowability of gross receipts taxes or local option taxes lawfully paid to the State of New Mexico by the Contractor or its subcontractors is determined under the Allowable Cost and Payment clause, except as provided in paragraph (d) of the New Mexico clause [@CL-CTF-2535].

## How it works

The Contractor provides Type 15 Nontaxable Transaction Certificates to each vendor in New Mexico that sells it tangible personal property for use in performing the contract [@CL-CTF-2536]. If the Contractor fails to provide a certificate to a vendor, the vendor becomes liable for the gross receipts taxes, and the taxes then passed on to the Contractor are not reimbursable as an allowable cost [@CL-CTF-2537]. The Contractor pays the New Mexico compensating user tax on tangible personal property bought under a Nontaxable Transaction Certificate if the property is not used for Federal purposes [@CL-CTF-2538]. Out-of-state purchases of tangible personal property by the Contractor that would otherwise be subject to compensation tax are governed by the principles of the clause, so compensating tax is due from the Contractor only if the property is not used for Federal purposes [@CL-CTF-2806].

The Contractor submits applications for Nontaxable Transaction Certificates, Form CSR-3C, to the State of New Mexico Taxation and Revenue Department, Revenue Division, Post Office Box 630, Santa Fe, New Mexico 87509 [@CL-CTF-2804]. When the Revenue Division issues the Type 15 Nontaxable Transaction Certificate, the Contractor uses these certificates strictly in accordance with the contract and the agreement between the agency named in the contract and the New Mexico Taxation and Revenue Department [@CL-CTF-2805]. The agency named in the contract may receive information regarding the Contractor from the Revenue Division of the New Mexico Taxation and Revenue Department and, at its discretion, may participate in any matters or proceedings pertaining to the clause or the agreement [@CL-CTF-2807]. That paragraph does not preclude the Contractor from having its own representative and does not obligate the agency to represent the Contractor [@CL-CTF-2894]. The clause leaves the agency name blank for each contract to fill in [@CL-CTF-2805].

The Contractor agrees to insert the substance of the clause, including paragraph (h), in each subcontract that meets the criteria in 29.401-4(b)(1) through (3) of the Federal Acquisition Regulation, 48 CFR part 29 [@CL-CTF-2808]. Paragraphs (a) through (h) of the clause are null and void should the Agreement referred to in paragraph (c) be terminated, but the termination does not nullify obligations already incurred before the date of termination [@CL-CTF-2809].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text from February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. The RFO prescription line for the North Carolina clause says to insert the clause as prescribed in 29.401-2 and does not state where the construction is performed, where the codified prescription says it is inserted in solicitations and contracts for construction to be performed in North Carolina [@CL-CTF-0392; @CL-CTF-2529]. The RFO text of FAR 52.229-2 keeps the fixed-price rule [@CL-CTF-2530; @CL-CTF-0393], the cost-reimbursement rule [@CL-CTF-2531; @CL-CTF-0394] and the time for the certified statements set by paragraph (d) [@CL-CTF-2532; @CL-CTF-0395]. It says the Contractor shall submit the statements within 60 days after completion if the contract is completed before the next October 1 [@CL-CTF-0396; @CL-CTF-2533], and, if it is not, on or before November 30 of each year, with statements that include taxes paid during the 12-month period that ended the preceding September 30 [@CL-CTF-0397; @CL-CTF-2618]. In paragraph (d) the RFO text names the Contractor as the one who shall submit the statements, where the codified text says the statements shall be submitted, and says the statements "include" taxes paid where the codified text says they "shall cover" them [@CL-CTF-0396; @CL-CTF-2533] [@CL-CTF-0397; @CL-CTF-2618]. In FAR 52.229-10 the RFO text says the Contractor shall register the contract, including the contract number, with the State of New Mexico, Taxation and Revenue Department, Revenue Division, within 30 days after award, pursuant to the Tax Administration Act of the State of New Mexico [@CL-CTF-0398; @CL-CTF-2534], and that allowability of the gross receipts taxes or local option taxes will be determined by the contracting officer under the Allowable Cost and Payment clause [@CL-CTF-0399; @CL-CTF-2535], except as provided in paragraph (d) [@CL-CTF-0400]. The sentence on certificates is now paragraph (c)(2) [@CL-CTF-0433; @CL-CTF-2805]. In paragraph (d), the vendor will be liable for the gross receipt taxes, which it will pass on to the Contractor, if the Contractor does not provide a Type 15 certificate [@CL-CTF-0434; @CL-CTF-2537]; those taxes are not an allowable cost under the contract and the Government will not reimburse the Contractor for the taxes [@CL-CTF-0435], where the codified text says the passed-on taxes shall not be reimbursable as an allowable cost by the Government [@CL-CTF-2537]. The RFO text keeps the compensating user tax on property bought under a certificate and not used for Federal purposes [@CL-CTF-2538; @CL-CTF-0436]. For out-of-state purchases it says the purchase is subject to the principles of the clause and Contractors shall pay compensating tax if the property is not used for Federal purposes [@CL-CTF-0437; @CL-CTF-2806]. Paragraph (h) ends after the Federal Acquisition Regulation reference to 29.401-4(b)(1) through (3), with no reference to 48 CFR part 29 [@CL-CTF-0438; @CL-CTF-2808], and paragraph (i) points to the Agreement referred to in paragraph (c)(2) [@CL-CTF-0439; @CL-CTF-2809].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A cost-reimbursement construction contract performed in North Carolina buys materials, and the Contractor pays 14,000 of North Carolina State and local sales and use tax on them. The tax is an allowable cost. A fixed-price contract for the same work carries 12,000 of that tax inside its price, and the price is not increased for it. The cost-reimbursement contract is completed before the next October 1, so the Contractor furnishes its certified statements within 60 days after completion. A contract still running on that October 1 furnishes statements by November 30 covering taxes paid in the 12 months that ended September 30.

One vendor's statement covers three purchases and lists the three invoice numbers, their dates, their 30,000 total and the tax paid, and it shows 400 of local tax apart from 1,100 of State tax. The statement also includes 2,000 of property withdrawn from warehouse stock with the tax paid on it. The Contractor furnishes extra information that the State Commissioner of Revenue asks for to support a refund claim, and it obtains a certified statement from each subcontractor. The certification form it signs says the taxes were paid on materials that became part of a building built for the United States of America, and it points to attachments that show each vendor, each invoice's date and number, each vendor's invoice total, the tax paid shown separately, and the warehouse-stock cost with its tax. For vessel repair in North Carolina under Alternate I, materials are the items that become part of or are annexed to the vessel, not to a building.

On New Mexico work, the Contractor registers with the State 20 days after award, which is inside the 30-day limit. A New Mexico vendor never received a Type 15 certificate and is liable for 9,000 of gross receipts tax, which it passes on to the Contractor. That 9,000 is not reimbursable as an allowable cost. The Contractor later buys 5,000 of equipment under a certificate and uses it for a purpose that is not Federal, so the Contractor pays the New Mexico compensating user tax on it. It also buys 3,000 of supplies out of state that would otherwise be subject to compensation tax and uses them for a Federal purpose, so, under the codified text, no compensating tax is due from it [@CL-CTF-2806]; the RFO text says Contractors shall pay compensating tax if the property is not used for Federal purposes [@CL-CTF-0437]. It sends applications for certificates to the Revenue Division and uses the certificates only as the contract and the agency's agreement allow. The Contractor keeps its own representative in a matter before the agency, and the agency is not obliged to represent the Contractor. The Contractor puts the substance of the clause into a qualifying subcontract. If the agreement ends, paragraphs (a) through (h) are void, but 2,000 of tax obligations incurred before the end date stay in force.

## Never confuse

North Carolina tax on materials follows contract type: a fixed-price contract carries it in the price and a cost-reimbursement contract treats it as an allowable cost. New Mexico gross receipts tax is not allowed or disallowed by the type of contract: its allowability is decided under the Allowable Cost and Payment clause, and a tax passed on because a certificate was not provided is not reimbursable.

Neither clause is the general exclusion of State and local taxes, which is on the [State and local tax exclusions](ctf-state-and-local-tax-exclusions.md) card. Invoice mechanics for both sit at [Proper invoice content](ctf-proper-invoice-content.md).

The codified New Mexico rule says compensating tax on an out-of-state purchase is due from the Contractor only if the property is not used for Federal purposes [@CL-CTF-2806], while the RFO text says Contractors shall pay compensating tax if such property is not used for Federal purposes [@CL-CTF-0437]. The codified rule says the allowability of gross receipts taxes is determined under the Allowable Cost and Payment clause without naming who determines it [@CL-CTF-2535], while the RFO text says the contracting officer determines it [@CL-CTF-0399].
