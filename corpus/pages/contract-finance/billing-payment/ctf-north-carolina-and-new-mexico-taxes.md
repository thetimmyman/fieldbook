## Purpose

Shows how the North Carolina sales and use tax clause and the State of New Mexico gross receipts and compensating tax clause treat tax on materials and on contract payments, and what the Contractor furnishes, registers or pays under each.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CTF-2529] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.229-2 and FAR 52.229-10 [@CL-CTF-2529; @CL-CTF-2534] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.229-2 and RFO FAR 52.229-10 [@CL-CTF-0393; @CL-CTF-0398] |
| Edition date | FAR 52.229-2: APR 1984 [@CL-CTF-9701]; FAR 52.229-10: APR 2003 [@CL-CTF-9702] |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CTF-2529] |
| Applies to | North Carolina clause: construction to be performed in North Carolina [@CL-CTF-2529]. New Mexico clause: payments for performance within the State of New Mexico [@CL-CTF-2619] |

The North Carolina clause is inserted in solicitations and contracts for construction to be performed in North Carolina [@CL-CTF-2529]. In a fixed-price contract, the contract price includes North Carolina State and local sales and use taxes to be paid on materials, notwithstanding any other provision of the contract [@CL-CTF-2530]. In a cost-reimbursement contract, North Carolina State and local sales and use taxes the Contractor pays on materials are an allowable cost under the contract [@CL-CTF-2531]. Materials means building materials, supplies, fixtures, and equipment that become a part of or are annexed to any building or structure erected, altered, or repaired under the contract [@CL-CTF-2795]. If the requirement is for vessel repair to be performed in North Carolina, Alternate I substitutes its paragraph (a) for paragraph (a) of the basic clause [@CL-CTF-2802]. Under Alternate I, materials means materials, supplies, fixtures, and equipment that become a part of or are annexed to any vessel altered or repaired under the contract [@CL-CTF-2803].

At the time specified in paragraph (d), the Contractor must furnish the Contracting Officer certified statements setting forth the cost of the materials purchased from each vendor and the amount of North Carolina State and local sales and use taxes paid [@CL-CTF-2532]. If the contract is completed before the next October 1, the certified statements to be furnished pursuant to paragraph (c) must be submitted within 60 days after completion [@CL-CTF-2533]. If it is not completed before the next October 1, the certified statements must be submitted on or before November 30 of each year and must cover taxes paid during the 12-month period that ended the preceding September 30 [@CL-CTF-2618].

In the event the Contractor makes several purchases from the same vendor, the certified statement must indicate the invoice numbers, the inclusive dates of the invoices, the total amount of the invoices, and the North Carolina State and local sales and use taxes paid [@CL-CTF-2796]. The statement must also include the cost of any tangible personal property withdrawn from the Contractor's warehouse stock and the amount of North Carolina State and local sales or use tax paid on this property by the Contractor [@CL-CTF-2797]. Any local sales or use taxes included in the Contractor's statements must be shown separately from the State sales or use taxes [@CL-CTF-2798]. The Contractor must furnish any additional information the Commissioner of Revenue of the State of North Carolina may require to substantiate a refund claim for sales or use taxes [@CL-CTF-2799]. The Contractor must also obtain and furnish to the Contracting Officer similar certified statements by its subcontractors [@CL-CTF-2800]. The certified statements must be in the form in which the named Contractor or subcontractor certifies, for a stated period, that it paid North Carolina State and local sales and use taxes aggregating a stated State amount and a stated local amount [@CL-CTF-2801], with respect to building materials, supplies, fixtures, and equipment that have become a part of or annexed to a building or structure erected, altered, or repaired by the named Contractor or subcontractor for the United States of America [@CL-CTF-2891], and that the vendors from whom the property was purchased, the dates and numbers of the invoices covering the purchases, the total amount of the invoices of each vendor [@CL-CTF-2892], the North Carolina State and local sales and use taxes paid on the property (shown separately), and the cost of property withdrawn from warehouse stock and North Carolina State and local sales or use taxes paid on this property are as set forth in the attachments [@CL-CTF-2893].

In New Mexico, within 30 days after award of the contract, the Contractor must advise the State of New Mexico of the contract by registering with the State of New Mexico, Taxation and Revenue Department, Revenue Division, pursuant to the Tax Administration Act of the State of New Mexico, and must identify the contract number [@CL-CTF-2534; @CL-CTF-9621]. The Contractor must pay the New Mexico gross receipts taxes, pursuant to the Gross Receipts and Compensating Tax Act of New Mexico, assessed against the contract fee and costs paid for performance of the contract, or of any part or portion thereof, within the State of New Mexico [@CL-CTF-2619]. The allowability of any gross receipts taxes or local option taxes lawfully paid to the State of New Mexico by the Contractor or its subcontractors will be determined in accordance with the Allowable Cost and Payment clause of the contract, except as provided in paragraph (d) of the clause [@CL-CTF-2535].

## How it works

The Contractor must provide Type 15 Nontaxable Transaction Certificates to each vendor in New Mexico selling tangible personal property to the Contractor for use in the performance of the contract [@CL-CTF-2536]. Failure to provide a Type 15 Nontaxable Transaction Certificate to vendors will result in the vendor's liability for the gross receipt taxes, and those taxes, which are then passed on to the Contractor, are not reimbursable as an allowable cost by the Government [@CL-CTF-2537]. The Contractor must pay the New Mexico compensating user tax for any tangible personal property purchased pursuant to a Nontaxable Transaction Certificate if such property is not used for Federal purposes [@CL-CTF-2538]. Out-of-state purchase of tangible personal property by the Contractor that would otherwise be subject to compensation tax is governed by the principles of the clause, and accordingly compensating tax is due from the Contractor only if such property is not used for Federal purposes [@CL-CTF-2806].

The Contractor must submit applications for Nontaxable Transaction Certificates, Form CSR-3C [@CL-CTF-2804], to the State of New Mexico Taxation and Revenue Department, Revenue Division, P.O. Box 630, Santa Fe, New Mexico 87509 [@CL-CTF-2804]. When the Type 15 Nontaxable Transaction Certificate is issued by the Revenue Division, the Contractor must use these certificates strictly in accordance with the contract and the agreement between the agency named in the contract and the New Mexico Taxation and Revenue Department [@CL-CTF-2805]. The agency named in the contract may receive information regarding the Contractor from the Revenue Division of the New Mexico Taxation and Revenue Department and, at its discretion, may participate in any matters or proceedings pertaining to the clause or the above-mentioned agreement [@CL-CTF-2807]. This does not preclude the Contractor from having its own representative and does not obligate the agency named in the contract to represent its Contractor [@CL-CTF-2894].

The Contractor agrees to insert the substance of the clause, including paragraph (h), in each subcontract that meets the criteria in 29.401-4(b)(1) through (3) of the Federal Acquisition Regulation, 48 CFR part 29 [@CL-CTF-2808]. Paragraphs (a) through (h) of the clause are null and void should the Agreement referred to in paragraph (c) of the clause be terminated; provided, however, that such termination does not nullify obligations already incurred prior to the date of termination [@CL-CTF-2809].

Effective February 1, 2026, DoD contracting officers must use the revised FAR part 52 published on the Revolutionary FAR Overhaul web page in place of the text codified at 48 CFR chapter 1 [@CL-CMP-9760; @CL-CMP-9762]. RFO FAR 52.229-2 says to insert the clause as prescribed in 29.401-2 [@CL-CTF-0392], where the codified prescription directs insertion in solicitations and contracts for construction to be performed in North Carolina [@CL-CTF-2529]. RFO FAR 52.229-2 provides that in a fixed-price contract the contract price includes North Carolina State and local sales and use taxes to be paid on materials, notwithstanding any other provision of the contract [@CL-CTF-0393], that in a cost-reimbursement contract any North Carolina State and local sales and use taxes paid by the Contractor on materials constitute an allowable cost under the contract [@CL-CTF-0394], and that at the time specified in paragraph (d) of the clause the Contractor must furnish the Contracting Officer certified statements setting forth the cost of the materials purchased from each vendor and the amount of North Carolina State and local sales and use taxes paid [@CL-CTF-0395]. Under RFO FAR 52.229-2(d), if the contract is completed before the next October 1, the Contractor must submit the certified statements to be furnished pursuant to paragraph (c) of the clause within 60 days after completion [@CL-CTF-0396], and if the contract is not completed before the next October 1, the Contractor must submit certified statements on or before November 30 of each year that include taxes paid during the 12-month period that ended the preceding September 30 [@CL-CTF-0397], whereas the codified paragraph (d) states that the certified statements shall be submitted and shall cover those taxes [@CL-CTF-2533; @CL-CTF-2618]. RFO FAR 52.229-10 provides that within 30 days after award of the contract the Contractor must register the contract, including the contract number, with the State of New Mexico, Taxation and Revenue Department, Revenue Division, pursuant to the Tax Administration Act of the State of New Mexico [@CL-CTF-0398], and that the allowability of any gross receipts taxes or local option taxes lawfully paid to the State of New Mexico by the Contractor or its subcontractors will be determined by the contracting officer in accordance with the Allowable Cost and Payment clause of the contract [@CL-CTF-0399] except as provided in paragraph (d) of the clause [@CL-CTF-0400]. Under RFO FAR 52.229-10(c)(2), when the Type 15 Nontaxable Transaction Certificate is issued by the Revenue Division, the Contractor must use these certificates strictly in accordance with the contract and the agreement between the agency named in the contract and the New Mexico Taxation and Revenue Department [@CL-CTF-0433]. Under RFO FAR 52.229-10(d), if the Contractor does not provide a Type 15 Nontaxable Transaction Certificate to a vendor, the vendor will be liable for the gross receipt taxes, which the vendor will pass on to the Contractor [@CL-CTF-0434], and those taxes are not an allowable cost under the contract and the Government will not reimburse the Contractor for the taxes [@CL-CTF-0435], where the codified paragraph says those taxes shall not be reimbursable as an allowable cost by the Government [@CL-CTF-2537]. RFO FAR 52.229-10 provides that the Contractor must pay the New Mexico compensating user tax for any tangible personal property purchased pursuant to a Nontaxable Transaction Certificate if such property is not used for Federal purposes [@CL-CTF-0436], that the Contractor's out-of-state purchase of tangible personal property that would otherwise be subject to compensation tax is subject to the principles of the clause and accordingly Contractors must pay compensating tax if such property is not used for Federal purposes [@CL-CTF-0437], that the Contractor agrees to insert the substance of the clause, including paragraph (h), in each subcontract that meets the criteria in 29.401-4(b)(1) through (3) of the Federal Acquisition Regulation [@CL-CTF-0438], and that paragraphs (a) through (h) of the clause are null and void should the Agreement referred to in paragraph (c)(2) of the clause be terminated, provided that such termination does not nullify obligations already incurred prior to the date of termination [@CL-CTF-0439].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A cost-reimbursement construction contract performed in North Carolina buys materials, and the Contractor pays 14,000 of North Carolina State and local sales and use tax on them. The tax is an allowable cost. A fixed-price contract for the same work carries 12,000 of that tax inside its price, and the price is not increased for it. The cost-reimbursement contract is completed before the next October 1, so the Contractor furnishes its certified statements within 60 days after completion. A contract still running on that October 1 furnishes statements by November 30 covering taxes paid in the 12 months that ended September 30.

One vendor's statement covers three purchases and lists the three invoice numbers, their dates, their 30,000 total and the tax paid, and it shows 400 of local tax apart from 1,100 of State tax. The statement also includes 2,000 of property withdrawn from warehouse stock with the tax paid on it. The Contractor furnishes extra information that the State Commissioner of Revenue asks for to support a refund claim, and it obtains a certified statement from each subcontractor. The certification form it signs says the taxes were paid on materials that became part of a building built for the United States of America, and it points to attachments that show each vendor, each invoice's date and number, each vendor's invoice total, the tax paid shown separately, and the warehouse-stock cost with its tax. For vessel repair in North Carolina under Alternate I, materials are the items that become part of or are annexed to the vessel, not to a building.

On New Mexico work, the Contractor registers with the State 20 days after award, which is inside the 30-day limit. A New Mexico vendor never received a Type 15 certificate and is liable for 9,000 of gross receipts tax, which it passes on to the Contractor. That 9,000 is not reimbursable as an allowable cost. The Contractor later buys 5,000 of equipment under a certificate and uses it for a purpose that is not Federal, so the Contractor pays the New Mexico compensating user tax on it. It also buys 3,000 of supplies out of state that would otherwise be subject to compensation tax and uses them for a Federal purpose, so no compensating tax is due from it. It sends applications for certificates to the Revenue Division and uses the certificates only as the contract and the agency's agreement allow. The Contractor keeps its own representative in a matter before the agency, and the agency is not obliged to represent the Contractor. The Contractor puts the substance of the clause into a qualifying subcontract. If the agreement ends, paragraphs (a) through (h) are void, but 2,000 of tax obligations incurred before the end date stay in force.

## Never confuse

Under the North Carolina clause, in a fixed-price contract the contract price includes North Carolina State and local sales and use taxes to be paid on materials, notwithstanding any other provision of the contract [@CL-CTF-2530], and in a cost-reimbursement contract any North Carolina State and local sales and use taxes paid by the Contractor on materials constitute an allowable cost under the contract [@CL-CTF-2531]. Under the New Mexico clause, the allowability of any gross receipts taxes or local option taxes lawfully paid to the State of New Mexico by the Contractor or its subcontractors is determined in accordance with the Allowable Cost and Payment clause of the contract, except as provided in paragraph (d) of the clause [@CL-CTF-2535], under which gross receipt taxes passed on to the Contractor after a failure to provide a Type 15 Nontaxable Transaction Certificate to vendors are not reimbursable as an allowable cost by the Government [@CL-CTF-2537].

For State and local tax exclusions, see [State and local tax exclusions](ctf-state-and-local-tax-exclusions.md). For invoice content, see [Proper invoice content](ctf-proper-invoice-content.md).

The codified New Mexico rule says compensating tax on an out-of-state purchase is due from the Contractor only if such property is not used for Federal purposes [@CL-CTF-2806], while RFO FAR 52.229-10(f) says Contractors shall pay compensating tax if such property is not used for Federal purposes [@CL-CTF-0437]. The codified rule says the allowability of gross receipts taxes or local option taxes lawfully paid to the State of New Mexico by the Contractor or its subcontractors will be determined in accordance with the Allowable Cost and Payment clause of the contract, except as provided in paragraph (d) [@CL-CTF-2535], while RFO FAR 52.229-10(b) says the contracting officer will determine it in accordance with the Allowable Cost and Payment clause, except as provided in paragraph (d) [@CL-CTF-0399; @CL-CTF-0400].
