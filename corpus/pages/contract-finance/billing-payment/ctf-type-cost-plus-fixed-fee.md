## Purpose

A cost-plus-fixed-fee contract reimburses allowable incurred costs and pays a predetermined fee that does not change with actual cost.

## The rule

Cost-reimbursement contracts provide for the payment of allowable incurred costs to the extent prescribed in the contract [@CL-CLS-0567]. These contracts establish an estimate of total cost for obligating funds and establishing a ceiling that the contractor may not exceed, except at its own risk, without contracting officer approval [@CL-CLS-0680].

A cost-plus-fixed-fee contract is a cost-reimbursement contract that pays a negotiated fee fixed at contract inception, which does not vary with actual cost but may be adjusted for changes in the work [@CL-CLS-0576]. The contract type is suitable when the general conditions for cost-reimbursement contracts are present and, for example, the work involves research or preliminary exploration or study where the required level of effort is unknown [@CL-CLS-0577].

## How it works

Cost-reimbursement contracts may be used only when requirements cannot be defined sufficiently for a fixed-price contract [@CL-CLS-0568] or uncertainties do not permit costs to be estimated with sufficient accuracy [@CL-CLS-0681]. In addition, they require consideration of the factors in FAR 16.104, an acquisition plan signed at least one level above the contracting officer, and an adequate contractor accounting system [@CL-CLS-0569], and their use is prohibited for commercial products and commercial services [@CL-CLS-0571]. No such contract is awarded unless the contracting officer complies with all limitations in FAR 15.404-4(c)(4)(i) and FAR 16.301-3 [@CL-CLS-0691], and solicitations and contracts other than for construction must insert clause 52.216-8, Fixed Fee [@CL-CTF-1243]. Because the fee is fixed at inception, it does not rise when cost falls or fall when cost rises [@CL-CLS-0576].

The contract may take a completion form or a term form. Under the completion form, the scope states a definite goal and specifies an end product [@CL-CLS-0578], normally requiring delivery within the estimated cost for payment of the full fee [@CL-CLS-0687]; if the work cannot be finished within that estimate, the Government may require additional effort without more fee provided it increases the estimated cost [@CL-CLS-0688]. The term form describes scope generally and obligates the contractor to devote a specified level of effort for a stated time period [@CL-CLS-0579], paying the fixed fee upon satisfactory completion and the contractor's statement that the required effort was expended [@CL-CLS-0689], but it may only be used when the contractor is obligated to a specific level of effort within a definite time period [@CL-CLS-0690]. The completion form is preferred over the term form whenever work or specific milestones can be defined well enough to permit estimates within which the contractor can be expected to complete the work [@CL-CLS-0580]. See [Cost-plus-fixed-fee contracts](../../close-revrec/revenue/cls-cost-plus-fixed-fee-contracts.md), [Fixed Fee clause](ctf-fixed-fee.md) and [Cost-reimbursement contracts](../../close-revrec/revenue/cls-cost-reimbursement-contracts.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor is awarded a cost-plus-fixed-fee completion contract with an estimated cost of 2,000,000 and a negotiated fixed fee of 150,000. If the contractor completes the work with allowable incurred costs of 1,900,000, it is paid total allowable costs of 1,900,000 plus the fixed fee of 150,000, which equals 2,050,000. If the work requires additional effort and the Government increases the estimated cost, resulting in allowable incurred costs of 2,100,000, the fee stays at 150,000 and total payment equals 2,100,000 plus 150,000, for 2,250,000. In both cases the fixed fee remains exactly 150,000 because fee does not vary with actual cost. This example is illustrative only and the contract itself governs.

## Never confuse

A cost-plus-fixed-fee contract differs from a cost-plus-incentive-fee contract, where the initially negotiated fee is adjusted by a formula based on the relationship of total allowable costs to total target costs [@CL-EST-1090], and from a firm-fixed-price contract, which places maximum risk and full responsibility for all costs and resulting profit or loss on the contractor [@CL-EST-1150]. It is also distinct from a cost contract, which is a cost-reimbursement contract in which the contractor receives no fee [@CL-CLS-0572].
