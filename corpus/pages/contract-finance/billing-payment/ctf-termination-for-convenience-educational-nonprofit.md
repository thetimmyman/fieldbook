## Purpose

Shows what an educational or other nonprofit contractor can recover on a termination for convenience, how the settlement proposal works, how costs are paid along the way, and which cost principles govern the amounts by type of institution.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CTF-2069] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.249-5 [@CL-CTF-2069] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CTF-2069] |
| Applies to | Solicitations and contracts when either a fixed-price or cost-reimbursement contract is contemplated for research and development work with an educational or nonprofit institution [@CL-CLS-1021], on a no-profit or no-fee basis [@CL-CLS-1056] |

The Government may terminate performance of work in whole or from time to time in part if the contracting officer determines that a termination is in the Government's interest [@CL-CTF-2069]. After termination, the contractor must submit a final termination settlement proposal in the form and with the certification the contracting officer prescribes [@CL-CTF-2070]. The proposal is due promptly but no later than 1 year from the effective date of termination, unless the contracting officer extends the period in writing on the contractor's written request made within that period [@CL-CTF-2071].

If the contractor fails to submit the proposal within the time allowed, the contracting officer may determine, on the basis of information available, the amount, if any, due and must pay the amount determined [@CL-CTF-2072]. Subject to the settlement proposal paragraph, the contractor and the contracting officer may agree on the whole or any part of the amount to be paid because of the termination, and that amount may include reasonable cancellation charges incurred by the contractor [@CL-CTF-2073]. The amount also may include any reasonable loss on outstanding commitments for personal services that the contractor is unable to cancel, provided that the contractor exercised reasonable diligence in diverting such commitments to other operations [@CL-CTF-2120]. The contractor must also terminate all applicable subcontracts and cancel or divert applicable commitments covering personal services that extend beyond the effective date of termination [@CL-CTF-2237].

## How it works

Partial payments against costs incurred by the contractor for the terminated portion of the contract may be made, under terms and conditions prescribed, if the contracting officer believes the total of these payments will not exceed the amount to which the contractor will be entitled [@CL-CTF-2074]. See [Partial payments and final payment under a terminated contract](../../close-revrec/revenue/cls-termination-partial-payments-and-final-payment.md). The contractor has the right of appeal under the Disputes clause, except that there is no right of appeal if it failed to submit the settlement proposal within the time provided and failed to request a time extension [@CL-CTF-2075].

The clause names the cost principles that govern all costs claimed, agreed to or determined, and they depend on the type of institution. The base rule names the cost principles and procedures in subpart 31.3 of the Federal Acquisition Regulation, Contracts with Educational Institutions, as in effect on the date of the contract, with educational institutions defined as institutions of higher education in the OMB Uniform Guidance in 2 CFR part 200, subpart A, and title 20 of the United States Code, section 1001 [@CL-CTF-2121]. If the contractor is not an educational institution and is a nonprofit organization, as defined in the OMB Uniform Guidance at 2 CFR part 200, the cost principles and procedures in subpart 31.7 of the Federal Acquisition Regulation, Contracts with Nonprofit Organizations, apply, except for a nonprofit institution listed as exempt in appendix VIII of 2 CFR part 200 [@CL-CTF-2122; @CL-CTF-2123]. If the contractor is a nonprofit institution listed in the OMB Uniform Guidance at 2 CFR part 200, appendix VIII, as exempted from the cost principles in subpart E, the cost principles at FAR 31.2 for commercial organizations apply to that contractor [@CL-CTF-2123]. The commercial-organization principles are on [Cost principles for contracts with commercial organizations](../../compliance/cas-allowability/cmp-cost-principles-commercial-organizations.md), and the shared definitions are on [Definitions used in the FAR cost principles](../../compliance/cas-allowability/cmp-far-cost-principles-definitions.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A university has a research agreement that is terminated in part. It has a commitment of 30,000 for a visiting researcher that it cannot cancel, and it moves 10,000 of that work to other projects with reasonable diligence. The university has also terminated its applicable subcontracts and tried to cancel or divert the commitment. Assume the remaining 20,000 is a reasonable loss on the commitment, so it can be part of the agreed amount. The university's costs are judged under the educational institution cost principles. A nonprofit research institute that is not an educational institution would instead use the nonprofit principles, unless it is on the exempt list, in which case the commercial-organization principles apply.

If the university never sends the settlement proposal and never asks for more time, the contracting officer may decide the amount from available information, and the university has no appeal.

## Never confuse

Partial payments are not the settlement. They are payments against costs incurred, and they depend on the contracting officer's belief that the total will not exceed what the contractor will be entitled to [@CL-CTF-2074]. A late proposal is also not just a missed formality: without a request for more time, the right of appeal is lost [@CL-CTF-2075]. The cost principles follow the type of institution, and a listed exempt nonprofit uses the commercial-organization principles rather than the nonprofit ones [@CL-CTF-2123]. Fixed-price research and development contracts with these institutions on a no-profit basis are outside the fixed-price short-form and default prescriptions [@CL-CLS-1051; @CL-CLS-1059], and the short-form clauses are on [Termination for convenience: fixed-price and services short forms](ctf-termination-for-convenience-short-forms.md). For the general fixed-price version, see [Termination for Convenience of the Government (Fixed-Price): settlement](ctf-termination-for-convenience-settlement.md). A similar long-form clause for other work is on [Termination for convenience: dismantling, demolition or removal of improvements](ctf-termination-for-convenience-dismantling-demolition-removal.md).
