## Purpose

Shows how progress payments are requested, certified, retained and refunded under the fixed-price construction payments clause, how final payment is made, and how common carriers are paid for communication services.

### Request and certification

Progress payments are made monthly as the work proceeds, or at more frequent intervals as the Contracting Officer determines, on estimates of work accomplished that meet the quality standards of the contract, as approved by the Contracting Officer [@CL-CTF-2006]. The request includes substantiation, the first item of which is an itemization of the amounts requested, related to the elements of work covered by the payment [@CL-CTF-2007]. It also lists the amount included for work performed by each subcontractor, the total amount of each subcontract and the amounts previously paid to each such subcontractor [@CL-CTF-2190], and carries additional supporting data in the form and detail the Contracting Officer requires [@CL-CTF-2191]. In preparing estimates, the Contracting Officer may authorize material delivered on the site and preparatory work done to be taken into consideration [@CL-CTF-2136]. Material delivered to the Contractor elsewhere may also be taken into consideration if the contract specifically authorizes it and the Contractor furnishes satisfactory evidence that it has acquired title and that the material will be used to perform the contract [@CL-CTF-2137].

Along with each request the Contractor furnishes the certification, or payment is not made [@CL-CTF-2008]. The certification includes statements that the amounts requested are only for performance in accordance with the contract and that all payments due to subcontractors and suppliers from previous payments have been made [@CL-CTF-2009]. It states that timely payments will be made from the proceeds of the payment covered by the certification, in accordance with subcontract agreements and the requirements of chapter 39 of Title 31, United States Code [@CL-CTF-2189]. It also states that the request does not include amounts the prime contractor intends to withhold or retain from a subcontractor or supplier under the subcontract, and that the certification is not final acceptance of a subcontractor's performance [@CL-CTF-2133]. If the Contractor elects to delete that last statement, paragraph (c)(4), from the certification, the certification is still acceptable [@CL-CTF-2188].

### Retainage, limits and title

If the Contracting Officer finds that satisfactory progress was achieved during the period for which a progress payment is to be made, the Contracting Officer authorizes payment in full [@CL-CTF-2186]. If satisfactory progress has not been made, the Contracting Officer may retain a maximum of 10 percent of the payment until satisfactory progress is achieved [@CL-CTF-2010]. When the work is substantially complete, the Contracting Officer may keep the amount considered adequate to protect the Government and releases the remaining withheld funds [@CL-CTF-2011]. On completion and acceptance of each separately priced building, public work or other division of the contract, payment is made for the completed work without retention of a percentage [@CL-CTF-2012]. The Government also reimburses, on request, the premiums the Contractor paid for performance and payment bonds, after the Contractor furnishes evidence of full payment to the surety [@CL-CTF-2134]. The retainage provisions do not apply to the portion of progress payments attributable to bond premiums [@CL-CTF-2187].

Progress payments shall not exceed 80 percent on work accomplished on undefinitized contract actions [@CL-CTF-2126]. A contract action there is any action resulting in a contract, including modifications for additional supplies or services, but not modifications within the scope and under the terms of the contract [@CL-CTF-2128], such as modifications issued under the Changes clause or funding and other administrative changes [@CL-CTF-2193]. Material and work covered by progress payments become the sole property of the Government at the time of payment, but that does not relieve the Contractor of sole responsibility for the material and work or for restoring damaged work [@CL-CTF-2138].

### Refund of unearned amounts

The refund paragraph applies when the Contractor, after a certified request, discovers that part or all of it pays for performance that fails to conform to the contract [@CL-CTF-2129]. The Contractor is then obligated to pay interest on the unearned amount, computed by the Contracting Officer, from the 8th day after receipt of the unearned amount [@CL-CTF-2130]. The Contractor must also notify the Contracting Officer of the performance deficiency [@CL-CTF-2192]. The interest runs until the Contractor notifies the Contracting Officer that the deficiency has been corrected, or reduces a later certified request by the unearned amount [@CL-CTF-2131]. It is computed at the average bond equivalent rate of 91-day Treasury bills at the most recent auction before receipt, and is deducted from the next available payment [@CL-CTF-2132].

### Final payment and common carriers

Final payment follows completion and acceptance of all work, a properly executed voucher and a release of all claims against the Government arising by virtue of the contract [@CL-CTF-2013]. Claims that the Contractor has specifically excepted, in stated amounts, are outside that release [@CL-CTF-2014]. A common carrier is paid in arrears on invoices for services and facilities furnished under the terms of the CSAs issued under the contract, at the rates and charges set out in the clause entitled Rates, Charges and Services [@CL-CTF-2015].

## How it works

A progress payment starts with the Contractor's request, which carries the itemization and the certification [@CL-CTF-2007; @CL-CTF-2008]. The Contracting Officer approves the estimate of work accomplished and may retain part of the payment where satisfactory progress has not been made [@CL-CTF-2006; @CL-CTF-2010]. If the Contractor finds that part of a certified request paid for nonconforming work, the Contractor owes interest on that part until the deficiency is corrected or a later request is reduced [@CL-CTF-2129; @CL-CTF-2131]. Withheld funds are released when the work is substantially complete, apart from the amount the Contracting Officer considers adequate for protection of the Government [@CL-CTF-2011]. Final payment waits for acceptance of all work, a voucher and the release, while a common carrier instead invoices in arrears [@CL-CTF-2013; @CL-CTF-2015].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text from February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. The RFO construction clause keeps the rule that the Government makes progress payments monthly as the work proceeds, or at more frequent intervals as the contracting officer determines, on estimates of work accomplished that meet the quality standards of the contract, as approved by the contracting officer [@CL-CTF-0502] [@CL-CTF-2006]. It keeps the first item of substantiation, an itemization of the amounts requested related to the elements of work covered by the payment [@CL-CTF-0503] [@CL-CTF-2007], and the rule that the contractor furnishes the certification with each request or payment is not made [@CL-CTF-0504] [@CL-CTF-2008]. The certification keeps the statements that the amounts requested are only for performance in accordance with the contract and that all payments due to subcontractors and suppliers from previous payments have been made [@CL-CTF-0505] [@CL-CTF-2009], and that timely payments will be made from the proceeds in accordance with subcontract agreements and the requirements of chapter 39 of Title 31, United States Code [@CL-CTF-0506] [@CL-CTF-2189]. The RFO clause keeps the rule that the contracting officer authorizes payment in full where satisfactory progress was achieved [@CL-CTF-0507] [@CL-CTF-2186], and may retain a maximum of 10 percent of the payment where satisfactory progress has not been made, until satisfactory progress is achieved [@CL-CTF-0508] [@CL-CTF-2010]. It keeps the rule that, when the work is substantially complete, the contracting officer may keep the amount considered adequate for protection of the Government and releases the remaining withheld funds [@CL-CTF-0509] [@CL-CTF-2011]. It keeps the reimbursement of bond premiums on request after evidence of full payment to the surety [@CL-CTF-0510] [@CL-CTF-2134], and the rule that the retainage provisions do not apply to the portion attributable to bond premiums [@CL-CTF-0511] [@CL-CTF-2187]. It keeps the refund paragraph's trigger, which is that the contractor, after making a certified request for progress payments, discovers that a portion or all of the request constitutes a payment for performance that fails to conform to the specifications, terms and conditions of the contract [@CL-CTF-9100] [@CL-CTF-2129], and the duty that the contractor then notify the contracting officer of the performance deficiency [@CL-CTF-9102] [@CL-CTF-2192]. It keeps the interest on the unearned amount from the 8th day after receipt, computed by the contracting officer in the manner the clause provides [@CL-CTF-0512] [@CL-CTF-2130], running until the contractor notifies the contracting officer that the deficiency has been corrected or reduces a later certified request by the unearned amount [@CL-CTF-0513] [@CL-CTF-2131], at the average bond equivalent rate of 91-day Treasury bills at the most recent auction before receipt [@CL-CTF-0514] [@CL-CTF-2132], deducted from the next available payment [@CL-CTF-9101] [@CL-CTF-2132]. It keeps the conditions for final payment, which are completion and acceptance of all work, a properly executed voucher and a release of all claims against the Government arising by virtue of the contract [@CL-CTF-0515] [@CL-CTF-2013], other than claims, in stated amounts, that the contractor has specifically excepted [@CL-CTF-0516] [@CL-CTF-2014]. It keeps the limit that progress payments shall not exceed 80 percent on work accomplished on undefinitized contract actions [@CL-CTF-0517] [@CL-CTF-2126], defining a contract action as any action resulting in a contract, including modifications for additional supplies or services but not modifications within the scope and under the terms of the contract [@CL-CTF-0518] [@CL-CTF-2128], such as modifications issued pursuant to the Changes clause or funding and other administrative changes [@CL-CTF-0519] [@CL-CTF-2193]. The RFO common carrier clause keeps the rule that the Government pays the contractor, in arrears, on invoices for services and facilities furnished in accordance with the terms of the CSAs issued under the contract, at the rates and charges in the clause entitled Rates, Charges and Services [@CL-CTF-0520] [@CL-CTF-2015].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A builder submits a monthly request, with the itemization, the subcontractor listings and the certification, for an approved estimate of 500,000, of which 10,000 is reimbursement of bond premiums. The Contracting Officer finds progress unsatisfactory for the period, so the Contracting Officer may retain up to 10 percent of the 490,000 not attributable to bond premiums, which is 49,000, and pay 451,000. Had the Contracting Officer found satisfactory progress, payment would be authorized in full. Later the work is substantially complete, and the Contracting Officer keeps only what is adequate for protection of the Government and releases the rest of the withheld funds.

In a later month the builder finds that 20,000 of an earlier certified payment was for work that did not conform to the contract. The builder notifies the Contracting Officer. Interest on the 20,000 runs from the 8th day after receipt until the builder reports the deficiency corrected or cuts a later request by 20,000, and the Contracting Officer deducts it from the next available payment.

A modification that adds work beyond the scope and terms of the contract is not yet definitized. Work accomplished on it is 100,000, so progress payments for that work cannot exceed 80,000.

## Never confuse

The retainage of up to 10 percent here applies where satisfactory progress has not been made [@CL-CTF-2010], and not to the portion attributable to bond premiums [@CL-CTF-2187], whereas the architect-engineer clause requires up to 10 percent to be withheld only if the Contracting Officer finds it necessary [@CL-CTF-2023]. See [Payments under fixed-price architect-engineer contracts](ctf-payments-fixed-price-architect-engineer.md). The limit on undefinitized work is a cap on payments for that work, not the customary progress payment rate, which is on [Progress payment rates and unusual progress payments](../financing/ctf-progress-payment-rates.md). A common carrier is paid in arrears on invoices for services and facilities furnished [@CL-CTF-2015], whereas construction progress payments are made as the work proceeds on estimates of work accomplished [@CL-CTF-2006]. For due dates of construction progress payments and retained amounts, see [Prompt payment clause for construction contracts](ctf-prompt-payment-construction-contracts.md). For the rules on assigning claims under a contract, see [Assignment of claims conditions and policies](../cash/ctf-assignment-of-claims.md). The payment clauses for other contract types are on [Payments under fixed-price research and development, personal services and transportation contracts](ctf-payments-rd-personal-services-and-transportation.md). The FAR part 32 prescription says the clause for communication services by common carriers is inserted appropriately modified with respect to payment due dates in accordance with agency regulations [@CL-CTF-1514], in solicitations and contracts for regulated communication services by common carriers [@CL-CTF-1519], whereas the prescription line printed in the RFO common carrier clause says only to insert the following clause [@CL-CTF-9099].
