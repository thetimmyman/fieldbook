## Purpose

Shows the compliance duties in the commercial terms clause, how inconsistencies in a commercial contract are ranked from the schedule down to the specification, how a clause in an end user license agreement that makes the Government indemnify the contractor is treated, and how the contractor's representations and certifications become part of the contract.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CTF-2451] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.212-4(q), (r), (s), (t), (u) and (v) [@CL-CTF-2659; @CL-CTF-2660; @CL-CTF-2451; @CL-CTF-2663; @CL-CTF-2453; @CL-CTF-2667] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CTF-2451] |
| Applies to | Contracts that contain the commercial products and services terms clause, and supplies or services under them that are subject to an end user license or similar agreement [@CL-CTF-2451; @CL-CTF-2453] |

The contractor shall comply with all applicable Federal, State and local laws, executive orders, rules and regulations applicable to its performance under the contract [@CL-CTF-2659]. The contractor agrees to comply with the statutes on limitations on the use of appropriated funds to influence certain Federal contracts (section 1352 of title 31 of the United States Code), officials not to benefit (section 431 of title 18) and Contract Work Hours and Safety Standards (chapter 37 of title 40) [@CL-CTF-2660]. The contractor also agrees to comply with the statutes on Kickbacks (chapter 87 of title 41 of the United States Code), Fly American (section 40118 of title 49) and procurement integrity (chapter 21 of title 41) [@CL-CTF-2661].

Any inconsistencies in the solicitation or contract shall be resolved by giving precedence in a stated order, which begins with the schedule of supplies and services [@CL-CTF-2451]. Next in precedence come the Assignments, Disputes, Payments, Invoice, Other Compliances, Compliance with Laws Unique to Government Contracts, and Unauthorized Obligations paragraphs of the clause, then the clause at 52.212-5, then addenda to the solicitation or contract, including any license agreements for computer software [@CL-CTF-2452; @CL-CTF-2662]. After the addenda come solicitation provisions if this is a solicitation, other paragraphs of the clause, the Standard Form 1449, other documents, exhibits, and attachments, and last the specification [@CL-CTF-2662]. Paragraph (t) of the clause is reserved [@CL-CTF-2663].

Except as stated in paragraph (u)(2), the Unauthorized Obligations rule is stated for any supply or service acquired under the contract that is subject to any End User License Agreement, Terms of Service, or similar legal instrument or agreement that includes a clause of the kind described next [@CL-CTF-2453]. A clause in such an agreement requiring the Government to indemnify the Contractor or any person or entity for damages, costs, fees, or any other loss or liability that would create an Anti-Deficiency Act violation is unenforceable against the Government [@CL-CTF-2454]. Neither the Government nor any Government authorized end user shall be deemed to have agreed to such a clause by virtue of it appearing in the End User License Agreement, Terms of Service, or similar legal instrument or agreement [@CL-CTF-2664]. If the license agreement is invoked through an "I agree" click box or other comparable mechanism, such as a click-wrap or browse-wrap agreement, execution does not bind the Government or any Government authorized end user to such a clause [@CL-CTF-2665]. Any such clause is deemed to be stricken from the End User License Agreement, Terms of Service, or similar legal instrument or agreement [@CL-CTF-2666]. The Unauthorized Obligations rule does not apply to indemnification by the Government that is expressly authorized by statute and specifically authorized under applicable agency regulations and procedures [@CL-CTF-2455].

The contractor's representations and certifications, including those completed electronically via the System for Award Management, are incorporated by reference into the contract [@CL-CTF-2667].

## How it works

When two documents conflict, the schedule of supplies and services wins first, then the listed paragraphs of the clause, then the clause at 52.212-5, and addenda rank below those, with any license agreements for computer software among the addenda [@CL-CTF-2451; @CL-CTF-2452; @CL-CTF-2662]. Below the addenda come solicitation provisions if this is a solicitation, other paragraphs of the clause, the Standard Form 1449, other documents, exhibits, and attachments, and last the specification [@CL-CTF-2662]. Separately, when a supply or service is subject to an end user license or similar agreement, a clause in it that requires the Government to indemnify for a loss that would create an Anti-Deficiency Act violation is unenforceable against the Government [@CL-CTF-2453; @CL-CTF-2454]. The Government and its authorized end users are not treated as having agreed to such a clause because it appears in the agreement, a click-through or similar execution does not bind them to it, and the clause is deemed stricken [@CL-CTF-2664; @CL-CTF-2665; @CL-CTF-2666]. The exception covers indemnification that a statute expressly authorizes and agency regulations and procedures specifically authorize [@CL-CTF-2455].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contract schedule lists 10 licences at 1,000 each, a total of 10,000, and an addendum sets a different delivery date. The schedule is first in the order, so it governs the inconsistency. The vendor's computer software license agreement is an addendum, and one of its terms conflicts with the Invoice paragraph of the clause. The Invoice paragraph ranks second and the addenda, including license agreements for computer software, rank fourth, so the Invoice paragraph prevails. The license agreement also says the agency must indemnify the vendor for any loss up to 50,000, and it is accepted through an "I agree" click box. That clause would create an Anti-Deficiency Act violation, so it is unenforceable against the Government, the click box does not bind the Government or its authorized end users to it, and it is deemed stricken. If a statute expressly authorized that indemnification and agency regulations and procedures specifically authorized it, the rule would not apply to it. The contractor also complies with the applicable Federal, State and local laws that apply to its performance, and its representations and certifications completed in the System for Award Management are part of the contract. Answer: the schedule wins the date conflict, the Invoice paragraph wins over the license term, and the indemnification clause is unenforceable unless both authorizations exist.

## Never confuse

Ranking documents is not the same as striking a clause. The order of precedence decides which provision wins an inconsistency [@CL-CTF-2451; @CL-CTF-2452; @CL-CTF-2662], while the license rule makes a particular indemnification clause unenforceable against the Government and deemed stricken [@CL-CTF-2454; @CL-CTF-2666]. The Other Compliances paragraph and the Compliance with Laws Unique to Government Contracts paragraph state duties of the contractor [@CL-CTF-2659; @CL-CTF-2660; @CL-CTF-2661], and they are also named among the paragraphs in the second rank [@CL-CTF-2452]. See [Commercial computer software license](../../compliance/regulatory-framework/cmp-commercial-computer-software-license.md) and [Unauthorized obligations and anti-deficiency](../../fpa/planning-chain/fpa-unauthorized-obligations-anti-deficiency.md).
