## Purpose

Explains how a termination for the convenience of the Government under a fixed-price contract is carried out, how the settlement is agreed or determined, and what caps it.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CTF-0351] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.249-2 [@CL-CTF-0351] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.249-2 [@CL-CTF-0725] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CTF-0351] |
| Applies to | Contracts that include the fixed-price termination for convenience clause, which is inserted as prescribed in FAR 49.502(b)(1)(i) [@CL-CTF-0351] |

Under the clause, the Government may terminate performance of work in whole or in part when the contracting officer determines that a termination is in the Government's interest [@CL-CTF-0352]. Following termination, the contractor must submit complete termination inventory schedules no later than 120 days from the effective date of termination, unless the contracting officer extends the period in writing on the contractor's written request made within that 120-day period [@CL-CTF-0353].

The contractor must submit its final termination settlement proposal promptly, but no later than 1 year from the effective date of termination, unless the contracting officer extends the period in writing on the contractor's written request made within that 1-year period [@CL-CTF-0354]. The contracting officer may still receive and act on a proposal after 1 year or any extension if the facts justify it [@CL-CTF-0600]. The agreed amount, exclusive of settlement costs, may not exceed the total contract price as reduced by previous payments and the contract price of work not terminated [@CL-CTF-0355].

FAR 2.101 defines the effective date of termination as the date on which the notice of termination requires the contractor to stop performance under the contract, or, if the contractor receives the notice after the date fixed for termination, the date the contractor receives the notice [@CL-CTF-9409]. The RFO model text definition of the effective date of termination in FAR 2.101 reads the same as the codified definition, including the rule that a notice received after the date fixed for termination takes effect on the date the contractor receives it [@CL-CTF-9446; @CL-CTF-9481; @CL-CTF-9409].

## How it works

After receiving a Notice of Termination, and except as directed by the contracting officer, the contractor immediately proceeds with its listed obligations, beginning with stopping work as specified in the notice [@CL-CTF-0601]. The same list also covers terminating subcontracts to the extent they relate to the terminated work and protecting contract property in the contractor's possession in which the Government has or may acquire an interest.

The contractor and the contracting officer may agree on the whole or any part of the amount to be paid, and the agreed amount may include a reasonable allowance for profit on work done [@CL-CTF-0603], but the agreed amount, exclusive of the costs shown in paragraph (g)(3), may not exceed the total contract price as reduced by previous payments and the contract price of work not terminated [@CL-CTF-0355]. If they fail to agree on the whole amount, the contracting officer pays the amounts the contracting officer determines, without duplicating any agreed amounts [@CL-CTF-0602]. If it appears that the contractor would have sustained a loss on the entire contract had it been completed, the contracting officer allows no profit under the profit subdivision of that determination and reduces the settlement to reflect the indicated rate of loss [@CL-CTF-0356].

All costs claimed, agreed to, or determined under the clause are governed by the cost principles and procedures of FAR Part 31 in effect on the date of the contract [@CL-CTF-0357]. In a partial termination, the contractor may request an equitable adjustment of the continued portion within 90 days from the effective date of termination unless the contracting officer extends the period in writing [@CL-CTF-0358].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text because DoD's class deviation directs it, effective February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. RFO FAR 52.249-2 keeps the termination ground [@CL-CTF-0725; @CL-CTF-0352] and the duty to stop work as specified in the notice, regardless of any delay in determining or adjusting any amounts due [@CL-CTF-0726; @CL-CTF-0601]. It keeps the 120-day deadline for inventory schedules [@CL-CTF-0727; @CL-CTF-0353] and the 1-year deadline for the final settlement proposal, each unless the contracting officer extends it in writing on the contractor's written request made within that period [@CL-CTF-0728; @CL-CTF-0354], and the rule that a proposal may be received and acted on after 1 year or any extension if the facts justify it [@CL-CTF-0729; @CL-CTF-0600]. It keeps agreement on the amount, including a reasonable allowance for profit on work done [@CL-CTF-0730; @CL-CTF-0603], the cap on the agreed amount, exclusive of costs shown in paragraph (g)(3) [@CL-CTF-0731; @CL-CTF-0355], and the payment of the amounts the contracting officer determines, without duplicating agreed amounts [@CL-CTF-0732; @CL-CTF-0733; @CL-CTF-0602]. It keeps the part 31 cost principles [@CL-CTF-0735; @CL-CTF-0357]. It keeps the loss rule: if the contractor would have sustained a loss on the entire contract, the contracting officer allows no profit under the profit subdivision and reduces the settlement to reflect the indicated rate of loss [@CL-CTF-0734; @CL-CTF-0356]. The RFO text names that subdivision (g)(2)(iii), where the codified paragraph says this subdivision (iii) [@CL-CTF-0734; @CL-CTF-0356].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor holds a fixed-price contract with a total contract price of 1,000,000. The Government terminates the whole contract for convenience, and no work remains that was not terminated. Payments previously made are 200,000 for completed work. The contractor has incurred 400,000 in costs that relate only to the terminated work, not to the completed work already paid for, and the parties agree on 40,000 of profit. The cap on the agreed amount is the total price of 1,000,000 minus the previous payments of 200,000, which leaves 800,000. The agreed amount of 440,000 is well below that cap. The contractor also has 30,000 of reasonable costs of settling the terminated work, which sit outside the cap, so the total final settlement payment is 470,000.

## Never confuse

A convenience settlement may include a reasonable allowance for profit on work done when the parties agree [@CL-CTF-0603], within the cap that the agreed amount, exclusive of settlement costs, may not exceed the total contract price as reduced by previous payments and the contract price of work not terminated [@CL-CTF-0355], but when the contracting officer determines the amount and it appears that the contractor would have sustained a loss on the entire contract, no profit is allowed and the settlement is reduced to reflect the indicated rate of loss [@CL-CTF-0356].

The cap on the agreed amount leaves out the settlement costs, so those costs are not counted against it [@CL-CTF-0355]. For regular invoice payments and terms, see [Prompt payment](ctf-prompt-payment.md) and [Contract types](ctf-contract-types.md).
