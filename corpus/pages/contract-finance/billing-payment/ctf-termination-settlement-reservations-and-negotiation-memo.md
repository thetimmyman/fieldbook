## Purpose

How a settlement agreement reserves the issues it does not settle, how one contractor's proposals under several contracts are settled together, and what the settlement negotiation memorandum records.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CTF-3117] |
| Codified citation | FAR 49.109-2, FAR 49.109-6 and FAR 49.110 [@CL-CTF-3117; @CL-CTF-3120; @CL-CTF-3254] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CTF-3117] |
| Applies to | Termination settlements negotiated by a termination contracting officer, including joint settlements of one contractor's proposals under different contracts [@CL-CTF-3117; @CL-CTF-3120; @CL-CTF-3254] |

The termination contracting officer (TCO) shall (1) reserve in the settlement agreement any rights or demands of the parties that are excepted from the settlement, and (2) ensure that the wording of the reservation does not create any rights for the parties beyond those in existence before execution of the settlement agreement [@CL-CTF-3117]. The TCO shall also (3) mark each applicable settlement agreement with "This settlement agreement contains a reservation" and retain the contract file until the reservation is removed [@CL-CTF-3117; @CL-CTF-3118]. The TCO shall (4) ensure that sufficient funds are retained to cover complete settlement of the reserved items and (5) at the appropriate time, prepare a separate settlement of reserved items and include it in a separate settlement agreement [@CL-CTF-3117; @CL-CTF-3119].

With the consent of the contractor, the TCO or TCOs concerned may negotiate jointly two or more termination settlement proposals of the same contractor under different contracts, even though the contracts are with different contracting offices or agencies [@CL-CTF-3120]. In such cases, accounting work shall be consolidated to the greatest extent practical [@CL-CTF-3251]. The resulting settlement may be evidenced by one settlement agreement covering all contracts involved or by a separate agreement for each contract involved [@CL-CTF-3252].

## How it works

When the settlement agreement covers more than one contract, it shall (1) clearly identify the contracts involved, (2) assign an amendment modification number to each contract, and (3) apportion the total amount of the settlement among the several contracts on some reasonable basis [@CL-CTF-3121]. It shall also (4) have attached or incorporated a schedule showing the apportionment, and (5) be distributed and attached to each contract involved in the same manner as other contract modifications [@CL-CTF-3121; @CL-CTF-3253].

The TCO shall, at the conclusion of negotiations, prepare a settlement negotiation memorandum describing the principal elements of the settlement for inclusion in the termination case file and for use by reviewing authorities [@CL-CTF-3254]. Pricing aspects of the settlement shall be documented in accordance with FAR 15.406-3, and the memorandum shall be distributed in accordance with FAR 15.406-3 [@CL-CTF-3255].

If the settlement was negotiated on the basis of individual items, the TCO shall specify the factors considered for each item [@CL-CTF-3122]. If the settlement was negotiated on an overall lump-sum basis, it is not necessary to evaluate each item or group of items individually, but the total amount of the recommended settlement shall be supported in reasonable detail [@CL-CTF-3123]. The memorandum shall include explanations of matters involving differences and doubtful questions settled by agreement, and the factors considered [@CL-CTF-3124]. The TCO should include any other matters that will assist reviewing authorities in understanding the basis for the settlement [@CL-CTF-3256].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor has termination settlement proposals under two contracts held by contracting offices of different agencies. With the contractor's consent, the TCOs negotiate the two proposals jointly and consolidate the accounting work. They agree on a total settlement of 150,000. The single agreement identifies both contracts, gives each its own amendment modification number, and apportions 90,000 and 60,000 on a reasonable basis shown in an attached schedule. It is distributed and attached to each contract like any other modification.

One item, a disputed warranty claim, is excepted from the settlement. The TCO reserves it in the agreement without creating rights beyond those that existed before, marks the agreement as containing a reservation and keeps the contract file. The TCO retains sufficient funds to cover complete settlement of that item and settles it later in a separate settlement agreement.

The parties negotiated the other items one by one, so the memorandum specifies the factors considered for each item and explains the differences and doubtful questions settled by agreement. Had they negotiated an overall lump sum, the memorandum would not need to evaluate each item but would support the 150,000 total in reasonable detail.

## Never confuse

When a settlement agreement reserves rights or demands of the parties that are excepted from the settlement, the TCO shall ensure that sufficient funds are retained to cover complete settlement of the reserved items and, at the appropriate time, prepare a separate settlement of reserved items and include it in a separate settlement agreement [@CL-CTF-3117; @CL-CTF-3119].

With the consent of the contractor, the TCO or TCOs concerned may negotiate jointly two or more termination settlement proposals of the same contractor under different contracts [@CL-CTF-3120], and the resulting settlement may be evidenced by one settlement agreement covering all contracts involved or by a separate agreement for each contract involved [@CL-CTF-3252]. When the settlement agreement covers more than one contract, it shall clearly identify the contracts involved, assign an amendment modification number to each contract, and apportion the total amount of the settlement among the several contracts on some reasonable basis [@CL-CTF-3121].

If the settlement was negotiated on an overall lump-sum basis, it is not necessary to evaluate each item or group of items individually, but the total amount of the recommended settlement shall be supported in reasonable detail [@CL-CTF-3123].
