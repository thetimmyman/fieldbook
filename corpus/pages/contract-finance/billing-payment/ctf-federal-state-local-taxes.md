## Purpose

Shows how the Federal, State, and Local Taxes clause puts applicable taxes inside the contract price, when later Federal tax changes adjust that price, and the exception for taxes imposed under section 5000C of title 26 of the United States Code [@CL-CTF-0428].

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CTF-0427] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.229-3 [@CL-CTF-0427] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CTF-0427] |
| Applies to | Contracts that include the Federal, State, and Local Taxes clause [@CL-CTF-0427] |

The contract price includes all applicable Federal, State, and local taxes and duties, with one exception: taxes imposed under section 5000C of title 26 of the United States Code may not be included in the price and may not be reimbursed [@CL-CTF-0427; @CL-CTF-0428].

After-imposed Federal tax increases the price, but only if the contractor warrants in writing that no amount for the new tax or rate increase was carried in the price as a contingency reserve or otherwise [@CL-CTF-0429].

After-relieved Federal tax decreases the price [@CL-CTF-0430]. No adjustment is made unless the amount of the adjustment exceeds $250 [@CL-CTF-0431]. The definition of an after-imposed Federal tax does not include social security tax or other employment taxes [@CL-CTF-0620].

## How it works

The clause measures from the contract date, which is the bid opening date or, for a negotiated contract or a modification, its effective date [@CL-CTF-0432].

Taxes and duties in effect on that date that the taxing authority is imposing and collecting on the transactions or property covered by the contract sit inside the price [@CL-CTF-0619]. Later Federal tax changes are trued up through the price, upward for an after-imposed tax and downward for an after-relieved tax [@CL-CTF-0429; @CL-CTF-0430].

An upward adjustment needs the written warranty that the tax was not already built in [@CL-CTF-0429], and an adjustment that does not exceed $250 is not made [@CL-CTF-0431].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contract is priced with all applicable taxes in effect on the contract date. A new Federal excise tax later adds 4,000 to the contractor's cost, and the contractor warrants in writing that nothing for that tax was included in the price, so the price rises by 4,000. A later change relieves 1,000 of Federal excise tax, so the price falls by 1,000. A separate adjustment of 180 is not made, because it does not exceed the clause threshold.

## Never confuse

The adjustments in this clause are for Federal tax, not employment taxes: an after-imposed Federal tax raises the price and an after-relieved Federal tax lowers it [@CL-CTF-0429; @CL-CTF-0430; @CL-CTF-0620]. The clause states no similar adjustment for a State or local tax change.

Taxes imposed under section 5000C of title 26 of the United States Code are the exception to the general rule that applicable taxes sit in the price, and they are neither priced in nor reimbursed [@CL-CTF-0428].
