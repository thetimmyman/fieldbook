## Purpose

Shows what the contractor is paid when the Government terminates a commercial contract for convenience, what it may not recover, the remedies when the Government terminates for cause, and where a time-and-materials or labor-hour contract replaces these paragraphs.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CTF-2444] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.212-4(l) and (m) [@CL-CTF-2444; @CL-CTF-2448] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.212-4(l) [@CL-CTF-0145; @CL-CTF-0146; @CL-CTF-0147; @CL-CTF-0148]; RFO FAR 52.212-4(m) [@CL-CTF-0149; @CL-CTF-0150; @CL-CTF-0151; @CL-CTF-0152]; RFO FAR 52.212-4 Alternate I [@CL-CTF-0068; @CL-CTF-0153] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CTF-2444] |
| Applies to | Contracts that contain the commercial products and services terms clause, as to the paragraphs quoted [@CL-CTF-2444] |

The Government reserves the right to terminate the contract, or any part of it, for its sole convenience, and on such termination the contractor shall immediately stop all work and immediately cause its suppliers and subcontractors to cease work [@CL-CTF-2444]. Subject to the terms of the contract, on a termination for convenience the contractor shall be paid a percentage of the contract price reflecting the percentage of the work performed prior to the notice of termination [@CL-CTF-2445]. The termination payment also includes reasonable charges that the contractor can demonstrate to the satisfaction of the Government, using its standard record keeping system, have resulted from the termination, and the contractor is not required to comply with the cost accounting standards or contract cost principles for this purpose [@CL-CTF-2446]. The convenience termination paragraph does not give the Government any right to audit the contractor's records, and the contractor shall not be paid for any work performed or costs incurred which reasonably could have been avoided [@CL-CTF-2447].

The Government may terminate the contract, or any part of it, for cause in the event of any default by the contractor, or if the contractor fails to comply with any contract terms and conditions, or fails to provide the Government, upon request, with adequate assurances of future performance [@CL-CTF-2448]. In the event of termination for cause, the Government shall not be liable to the contractor for any amount for supplies or services not accepted, and the contractor shall be liable to the Government for any and all rights and remedies provided by law [@CL-CTF-2449]. If it is determined that the Government improperly terminated the contract for default, the termination shall be deemed a termination for convenience [@CL-CTF-2450].

The clause says that when a time-and-materials or labor-hour contract is contemplated, its Alternate I paragraphs (a), (e), (i), (l), and (m) are substituted for those in the basic clause [@CL-CTF-2668]. Those substituted termination paragraphs are stated on [Commercial contract terms, Alternate I](ctf-commercial-terms-alternate-i-time-and-materials.md).

## How it works

On a convenience termination the payment, subject to the terms of the contract, has two parts: the share of the contract price matching the share of work performed before the notice, and the termination charges the contractor can demonstrate from its standard record keeping system [@CL-CTF-2445; @CL-CTF-2446]. Those charges need no cost accounting standards or contract cost principles, the paragraph gives no audit right, and avoidable work or costs are not paid [@CL-CTF-2446; @CL-CTF-2447]. On a termination for cause the Government owes nothing for supplies or services it did not accept, and the contractor answers for the remedies the law provides [@CL-CTF-2449]. If it is determined that the Government improperly terminated the contract for default, the termination is deemed a termination for convenience [@CL-CTF-2450]. On a time-and-materials or labor-hour contract the Alternate I termination paragraphs are substituted for these [@CL-CTF-2668].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text because Class Deviation 2026-O0038 directs it, effective February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. It keeps the convenience termination rules in paragraph (l) [@CL-CTF-0145; @CL-CTF-0146; @CL-CTF-0147; @CL-CTF-0148; @CL-CTF-2444; @CL-CTF-2445; @CL-CTF-2446; @CL-CTF-2447]. It keeps the grounds for termination for cause, the effect of termination for cause and the rule that an improper default termination is deemed a termination for convenience in paragraph (m) [@CL-CTF-0149; @CL-CTF-0151; @CL-CTF-0152; @CL-CTF-2448; @CL-CTF-2449; @CL-CTF-2450]. The RFO paragraph (m) also includes a sentence that the Government will send a cure notice to the Contractor, unless the reason for the termination is late delivery, which the codified paragraph (m) does not print [@CL-CTF-0150; @CL-CTF-2448; @CL-CTF-2449]. For a time-and-materials or labor-hour contract, the RFO text says Alternate I substitutes paragraphs (a), (b), (i), (l), and (m) for those in the basic clause [@CL-CTF-0068; @CL-CTF-2668], and its paragraph (m) also says the Government will send a cure notice to the Contractor, unless the reason for the termination is late delivery [@CL-CTF-0153].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contract has a price of 100,000. The Government terminates it for convenience after 40 percent of the work is performed before the notice. The contractor is paid 40,000 for the work performed. It also shows 3,000 of termination charges from its standard record keeping system, and 500 of those charges reasonably could have been avoided, so only 2,500 is paid. It need not apply cost accounting standards or contract cost principles to the charges. Total payment: 42,500. In a second contract the Government terminates for cause for default, and 10,000 worth of supplies were not accepted. The Government owes nothing for those supplies. If it is later determined that the default termination was improper, it is deemed a termination for convenience and the first calculation applies. If the RFO text applies under the class deviation, the Government will also send a cure notice to the Contractor, unless the reason for the termination is late delivery [@CL-CTF-0150].

## Never confuse

Subject to the terms of the contract, a convenience termination pays a percentage of the contract price reflecting the percentage of the work performed prior to the notice of termination, plus the demonstrated reasonable charges [@CL-CTF-2445; @CL-CTF-2446]. On a termination for cause, the Government shall not be liable for any amount for supplies or services not accepted, and the contractor shall be liable for any and all rights and remedies provided by law [@CL-CTF-2449]. If it is determined that the Government improperly terminated the contract for default, the termination is deemed a termination for convenience [@CL-CTF-2450]. See [Termination for convenience settlement](ctf-termination-for-convenience-settlement.md) and [Termination for default, effect and repurchase](../../close-revrec/revenue/cls-termination-for-default-effect-and-repurchase.md).

Under the RFO text the Government will send a cure notice to the Contractor on a termination for cause, unless the reason for the termination is late delivery [@CL-CTF-0150], while the codified paragraph (m) states the grounds and the effect of the termination without such a sentence [@CL-CTF-2448; @CL-CTF-2449].
