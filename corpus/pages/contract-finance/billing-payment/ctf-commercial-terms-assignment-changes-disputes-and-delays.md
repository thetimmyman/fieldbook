## Purpose

Shows how the commercial terms clause treats assignment of payment rights, changes, disputes, definitions and excusable delay, what occurrences it gives as examples of excusable delay, and what the contractor must do when a delay is excusable.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CTF-2427] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.212-4(b) to (f) [@CL-CTF-2427; @CL-CTF-2428; @CL-CTF-2429; @CL-CTF-2430; @CL-CTF-2431; @CL-CTF-2456] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.212-4(a) [@CL-CTF-0052]; RFO FAR 52.212-4(c) [@CL-CTF-0053; @CL-CTF-0054]; RFO FAR 52.212-4(d) [@CL-CTF-0055]; RFO FAR 52.212-4(e) [@CL-CTF-0056; @CL-CTF-0057; @CL-CTF-0058]; RFO FAR 52.212-4(f) [@CL-CTF-0059; @CL-CTF-0060; @CL-CTF-0061] |
| Edition date | NOV 2023 [@CL-CTF-9643] |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CTF-2427] |
| Applies to | Contracts that contain the commercial products and services terms clause, as to the paragraphs quoted [@CL-CTF-2427] |

The contractor or its assignee may assign its rights to receive payment due as a result of performance to a bank, trust company, or other financing institution, including any Federal lending agency, in accordance with the Assignment of Claims Act [@CL-CTF-2427]; however, when a third party makes payment, for example through the Governmentwide commercial purchase card, the contractor may not assign its rights to receive payment under the contract [@CL-CTF-2428]. Changes in the terms and conditions of the contract may be made only by written agreement of the parties [@CL-CTF-2428].

The contract is subject to the Contract Disputes statute, and a failure of the parties to reach agreement on any request for equitable adjustment, claim, appeal or action arising under or relating to the contract is a dispute [@CL-CTF-2429]. A dispute is to be resolved in accordance with the Disputes clause at FAR 52.233-1, which the paragraph incorporates by reference, and the contractor shall proceed diligently with performance pending final resolution of any dispute arising under the contract [@CL-CTF-2430]. The clause at FAR 52.202-1, Definitions, is incorporated by reference [@CL-CTF-2431]. The contractor is liable for default unless nonperformance is caused by an occurrence beyond its reasonable control and without its fault or negligence [@CL-CTF-2431].

The excusable delays paragraph gives as examples acts of God or the public enemy, acts of the Government in either its sovereign or contractual capacity, fires, floods, epidemics, quarantine restrictions, strikes, unusually severe weather, and delays of common carriers [@CL-CTF-2456]. The contractor shall notify the Contracting Officer in writing as soon as it is reasonably possible after the commencement of any excusable delay, setting forth the full particulars in connection with the delay [@CL-CTF-2432]. The contractor shall remedy the occurrence causing the delay with all reasonable dispatch and shall promptly give written notice to the Contracting Officer of the cessation of the occurrence [@CL-CTF-2433].

## How it works

Changes to the terms of the contract may be made only by written agreement of the parties [@CL-CTF-2428]. A failure of the parties to reach agreement on any request for equitable adjustment, claim, appeal or action arising under or relating to the contract is a dispute, and the contractor shall proceed diligently with performance pending final resolution of any dispute arising under the contract [@CL-CTF-2429; @CL-CTF-2430]. On default, the test is whether the nonperformance was caused by an occurrence beyond the contractor's reasonable control and without its fault or negligence [@CL-CTF-2431]. The paragraph gives as examples of such occurrences acts of God or the public enemy, acts of the Government in either its sovereign or contractual capacity, fires, floods, epidemics, quarantine restrictions, strikes, unusually severe weather, and delays of common carriers [@CL-CTF-2456]. For an excusable delay the contractor has three duties. It shall notify the Contracting Officer in writing as soon as it is reasonably possible after the commencement of the delay, setting forth the full particulars [@CL-CTF-2432]. It shall remedy the occurrence with all reasonable dispatch [@CL-CTF-2433]. It shall promptly give the Contracting Officer written notice of the cessation of the occurrence [@CL-CTF-2433].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text because Class Deviation 2026-O0038 directs it, effective February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. The RFO text says in paragraph (a) that the clause at FAR 52.202-1, Definitions, is incorporated by reference, where the codified text has this in paragraph (e) [@CL-CTF-0052; @CL-CTF-2431]. It keeps the assignment rule in paragraph (c), the written-agreement rule for changes in paragraph (d) and the disputes rule in paragraph (e), where the codified text has them in paragraphs (b), (c) and (d) [@CL-CTF-0053; @CL-CTF-0054; @CL-CTF-0055; @CL-CTF-0056; @CL-CTF-0057; @CL-CTF-0058; @CL-CTF-2427; @CL-CTF-2428; @CL-CTF-2429; @CL-CTF-2430]. It keeps, in paragraph (f), the test for default and the list of example occurrences, which it introduces with "Examples of occurrences include" where the codified paragraph says "such as" [@CL-CTF-0059; @CL-CTF-0060; @CL-CTF-2431; @CL-CTF-2456]. The list of duties when an excusable delay occurs is reworded and shorter: the RFO text says the contractor shall notify the Contracting Officer in writing as soon as possible, remedy the delay as quickly as possible, and notify the Contracting Officer when the occurrence is over [@CL-CTF-0061]. The RFO items print no words about the commencement of the delay, no word "reasonably", no duty to set out the full particulars, no "all reasonable dispatch", and neither "promptly" nor "written" in the notice that the occurrence is over, all of which appear in the codified paragraph [@CL-CTF-0061; @CL-CTF-2432; @CL-CTF-2433].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor holds a contract worth 200,000 and assigns its right to be paid to a bank. That assignment is allowed. A separate 20,000 order is paid with the Governmentwide commercial purchase card, so the contractor may not assign its rights to receive payment under that order. The contractor then files a 15,000 request for equitable adjustment and the parties do not agree. The failure to agree is a dispute resolved under the Disputes clause, and the contractor proceeds diligently with performance while it is resolved. A fire, one of the listed examples, stops work for a time, and assume it was beyond the contractor's reasonable control and without its fault or negligence. Answer: the contractor is not liable for default for that nonperformance. It notifies the Contracting Officer in writing as soon as reasonably possible after the fire begins, with the full particulars, remedies the occurrence with all reasonable dispatch, and promptly gives written notice when it ends. If the RFO text applies under the class deviation, the contractor would instead notify the Contracting Officer in writing as soon as possible, remedy the delay as quickly as possible, and notify the Contracting Officer when the occurrence is over [@CL-CTF-0061]. Any change to the delivery terms needs a written agreement of both parties.

## Never confuse

The contractor or its assignee may assign its rights to receive payment to a bank, trust company, or other financing institution [@CL-CTF-2427], and changes in the terms and conditions of the contract may be made only by written agreement of the parties [@CL-CTF-2428]. The contractor shall proceed diligently with performance pending final resolution of any dispute arising under the contract [@CL-CTF-2430]. The contractor is liable for default unless nonperformance is caused by an occurrence beyond its reasonable control and without its fault or negligence [@CL-CTF-2431], and the paragraph gives examples of such occurrences [@CL-CTF-2456]. When an excusable delay occurs, the contractor shall give the written notices and remedy the occurrence [@CL-CTF-2432; @CL-CTF-2433]. See [Assignment of claims](../cash/ctf-assignment-of-claims.md) and [Disputes clause](../../close-revrec/revenue/cls-disputes-clause.md).

The excusable delay duties read differently in the two texts. The codified paragraph asks for written notice setting forth the full particulars, a remedy with all reasonable dispatch, and prompt written notice of the cessation [@CL-CTF-2432; @CL-CTF-2433], while the RFO paragraph asks for notice in writing as soon as possible, a remedy as quickly as possible, and notice when the occurrence is over [@CL-CTF-0061]. The RFO items do not print a duty to set out the full particulars or to give prompt written notice of the end of the occurrence [@CL-CTF-0061].
