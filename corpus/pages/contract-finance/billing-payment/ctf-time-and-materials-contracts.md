## Purpose

Shows when a time-and-materials contract may be used, the determination and ceiling price it needs, who decides, and what finance tracks against the ceiling.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CTF-1229] |
| Codified citation | FAR 16.600 [@CL-CTF-2359] and FAR 16.601 [@CL-CTF-1229] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CTF-1229] |
| Applies to | Time-and-materials contracts and orders [@CL-CTF-1229; @CL-CTF-1231] |

Time-and-materials contracts and labor-hour contracts are not fixed-price contracts [@CL-CTF-2359].

A time-and-materials contract provides for acquiring supplies or services on the basis of both direct labor hours at specified fixed hourly rates that include wages, overhead, general and administrative expenses, and profit, and actual cost for materials, except as provided for in 31.205-26(e) and (f) [@CL-CTF-1228; @CL-CTF-1259]. It may be used only when it is not possible at the time of placing the contract to estimate accurately the extent or duration of the work or to anticipate costs with any reasonable degree of confidence [@CL-CTF-1229].

A time-and-materials contract or order may be used only if (1) the contracting officer prepares a determination and findings that no other contract type is suitable and (2) the contract or order includes a ceiling price that the contractor exceeds at its own risk [@CL-CTF-1231; @CL-CTF-1232]. The determination and finding shall be (i) signed by the contracting officer prior to the execution of the base period or any option periods of the contracts [@CL-CTF-1233; @CL-CTF-1231], and (ii) approved by the head of the contracting activity prior to the execution of the base period when the base period plus any option periods exceeds three years [@CL-CTF-1258; @CL-CTF-1231].

## How it works

The contracting officer prepares the determination and findings, and the head of the contracting activity approves them when the base period plus any option periods exceeds three years [@CL-CTF-1231; @CL-CTF-1258]. A time-and-materials contract provides no positive profit incentive to the contractor for cost control or labor efficiency [@CL-CTF-1230], so appropriate Government surveillance of contractor performance is required to give reasonable assurance that efficient methods and effective cost controls are being used [@CL-CTF-1257]. Prior to an increase in the ceiling price of a time-and-materials or labor-hour contract or order, the contracting officer shall (1) conduct an analysis of pricing and other relevant factors to determine if the action is in the best interest of the Government, (2) document the decision in the contract or order file, and (3) when making a change that modifies the general scope of (i) a contract, follow the procedures at FAR 6.303, (ii) an order issued under the Federal Supply Schedules, follow the procedures at FAR 8.405-6, or (iii) an order issued under multiple award task and delivery order contracts, follow the procedures at FAR 16.505(b)(2) [@CL-CTF-1234; @CL-CTF-1260; @CL-CTF-2205; @CL-CTF-2206]. The contract or order includes a ceiling price that the contractor exceeds at its own risk [@CL-CTF-1232].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A time-and-materials order has a ceiling price of 600,000. Billed labor and materials reach 480,000 with work still ahead, and the contractor asks for a higher ceiling. The answer: finance reports billings against the 600,000 ceiling each period, and the contracting officer analyses pricing and other relevant factors before any increase. Until a ceiling increase is made, costs above the ceiling are at the contractor's own risk.

## Never confuse

How vouchers, withholds and the notice before the ceiling are handled is on [Payments under time-and-materials and labor-hour contracts](ctf-tm-and-lh-payments.md). A time-and-materials contract provides for acquiring supplies or services on the basis of (1) direct labor hours at specified fixed hourly rates that include wages, overhead, general and administrative expenses, and profit, and (2) actual cost for materials (except as provided for in FAR 31.205-26(e) and (f)) [@CL-CTF-1228; @CL-CTF-1259]. Under FAR 16.307(a)(1), the contracting officer shall insert the clause at FAR 52.216-7, Allowable Cost and Payment, in solicitations and contracts when a cost-reimbursement contract or a time-and-materials contract (other than a contract for a commercial product or commercial service) is contemplated [@CL-CTF-1242], and if the contract is a time-and-materials contract, the clause at FAR 52.216-7 applies in conjunction with the clause at FAR 52.232-7, but only to the portion of the contract that provides for reimbursement of materials (as defined in the clause at FAR 52.232-7) at actual cost, and the clause at FAR 52.216-7 does not apply to labor-hour contracts [@CL-CTF-1263; @CL-CTF-1264]. See [Clause prescriptions for cost-reimbursement and incentive contracts](ctf-cost-reimbursement-and-incentive-clause-prescriptions.md), [Negotiating and selecting contract type](../funding/ctf-contract-type-negotiation-and-selection.md), and [Contract types and what they mean for finance](ctf-contract-types.md).
