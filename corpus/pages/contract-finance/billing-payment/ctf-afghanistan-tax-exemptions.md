## Purpose

Sets out which Afghan taxes, customs, duties, fees and similar charges a Contractor excludes from the contract price, which persons the agreements exempt, and what the Contractor withholds and remits for Afghan employees.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CTF-2561] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.229-13 and FAR 52.229-14 [@CL-CTF-2561; @CL-CTF-2564] |
| RFO citation | No RFO equivalent for either clause: RFO part 52 lists FAR 52.229-13 and FAR 52.229-14 as [Reserved] [@CL-CTF-0004; @CL-CTF-0005] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CTF-2561] |
| Applies to | Activities in Afghanistan on behalf of or in support of United States Forces under the security agreement clause, or NATO Forces under the status of forces clause [@CL-CTF-2646; @CL-CTF-2649] |

The clause based on the Security and Defense Cooperation Agreement says that the Agreement exempts the United States Government, and its contractors and subcontractors other than those that are Afghan legal entities or residents [@CL-CTF-2561]. The exemption is from paying any tax or similar charge assessed by the Government of Afghanistan on activities associated with the contract within Afghanistan if the activities are on behalf of or in support of United States Forces [@CL-CTF-2646]. The Contractor excludes any Afghan taxes, customs, duties, fees, or similar charges from the contract price, other than those charged to Afghan legal entities or residents [@CL-CTF-2562]. In that clause, United States Forces means the entity comprising the members of the force and of the civilian component, and all property, equipment, and materiel of the United States Armed Forces present in the territory of Afghanistan [@CL-CTF-2836]. The Agreement also exempts the acquisition, importation, exportation, reexportation, transportation, and use of supplies and services in Afghanistan, on behalf of or in support of United States Forces, from any taxes, customs, duties, fees, or similar charges imposed by the Government of Afghanistan [@CL-CTF-2838].

The status of forces agreement clause says that the Status of Forces Agreement exempts NATO Forces and its contractors and subcontractors other than those that are Afghan legal entities or residents [@CL-CTF-2564]. The exemption is from paying any tax or similar charge assessed by the Government of Afghanistan within Afghanistan if the activities are on behalf of or in support of NATO Forces [@CL-CTF-2649]. Its Contractor excludes any Afghan taxes, customs, duties, fees or similar charges from the contract price on the same carve-out for Afghan legal entities or residents [@CL-CTF-2565]. In that clause, NATO Forces means the Members of the Force, Members of the Civilian Component, NATO Personnel and all property, equipment, and materiel of NATO, NATO Member States, and Operational Partners present in the territory of Afghanistan [@CL-CTF-2837]. The Status of Forces Agreement also exempts the acquisition, importation, exportation, reexportation, transportation, and use of supplies and services in Afghanistan on behalf of or in support of NATO Forces from all Afghan taxes, customs, duties, fees, or similar charges [@CL-CTF-2839].

## How it works

The clause that applies depends on who the work supports, United States Forces or NATO Forces, because the two clauses rest on different agreements [@CL-CTF-2646; @CL-CTF-2649]. In both clauses the Contractor's duty to exclude charges from the price does not reach charges to Afghan legal entities or residents [@CL-CTF-2562; @CL-CTF-2565].

The Agreement does not exempt Afghan employees of Government contractors and subcontractors from Afghan tax laws [@CL-CTF-2647]. To the extent required by Afghan law, the Contractor withholds tax from the wages of these employees and remits those payments to the appropriate Afghan taxing authority, and these withholdings are an individual's liability, not a tax against the Contractor [@CL-CTF-2563]. Under the status of forces clause, Afghan citizens employed by NATO contractors and subcontractors are subject to Afghan tax laws, and to the extent Afghan law requires, the Contractor withholds tax from their wages and remits the withholdings to the appropriate Afghan taxing authority [@CL-CTF-2566]. Under the status of forces clause, the withholdings from the wages of those employees are an individual's liability, not a tax against the Contractor [@CL-CTF-2840].

Each clause requires the Contractor to include its substance, including its paragraph on subcontracts, in all subcontracts, including subcontracts for commercial products or commercial services [@CL-CTF-2648; @CL-CTF-2650].

Under the RFO text, which DoD contracting officers use in place of the codified text because Class Deviation 2026-O0038 directs it, effective February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762], the comparison with the codified text is as follows. The RFO text lists FAR 52.229-13 and FAR 52.229-14 as [Reserved] [@CL-CTF-0004; @CL-CTF-0005], where the codified text has the Security and Defense Cooperation Agreement tax clause and the status of forces agreement tax clause [@CL-CTF-2561; @CL-CTF-2564].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contract supporting United States Forces imports 80,000 of supplies into Afghanistan, and Afghanistan assesses a 5,000 customs charge on them. The Contractor excludes the 5,000 from the contract price. A separate 2,000 charge is assessed on an Afghan legal entity, so the exclusion duty does not reach it. The Contractor withholds 900 of Afghan tax from an Afghan employee's wages as Afghan law requires and remits it to the Afghan taxing authority. The 900 is the employee's liability, not a tax against the Contractor. The importation and use of the supplies in Afghanistan on behalf of United States Forces are exempt from Afghan taxes, customs, duties, fees, or similar charges, and equipment of the United States Armed Forces present in Afghanistan counts as part of United States Forces.

On a contract supporting NATO Forces, the Contractor excludes a 3,000 Afghan fee from the price in the same way. It withholds 700 from an Afghan citizen employee's wages as Afghan law requires and remits it, and the 700 is the individual's liability, not a tax against the Contractor. The acquisition of 12,000 of services in Afghanistan on behalf of NATO Forces is exempt from all Afghan taxes, customs, duties, fees, or similar charges, and the property of NATO Member States present in Afghanistan counts as part of NATO Forces. The two clause numbers that the example applies, FAR 52.229-13 and FAR 52.229-14, are listed as [Reserved] in the RFO text [@CL-CTF-0004; @CL-CTF-0005].

## Never confuse

The exemption covers the United States Government or NATO Forces and their contractors and subcontractors, other than Afghan legal entities or residents, so charges to those entities and residents are outside the exclusion duty [@CL-CTF-2561; @CL-CTF-2564]. Tax withheld from an Afghan employee's wages under the security agreement clause is the individual's liability, not a tax against the Contractor [@CL-CTF-2563]. A view of host-country tax relief elsewhere sits at [Germany tax relief, customs and receipt verification](ctf-dfars-germany-tax-relief-customs-and-receipt-verification.md). The codified text carries two Afghanistan tax clauses with the rules described above [@CL-CTF-2561; @CL-CTF-2564], but the RFO text prints no clause at either number: both are listed as [Reserved] [@CL-CTF-0004; @CL-CTF-0005].
