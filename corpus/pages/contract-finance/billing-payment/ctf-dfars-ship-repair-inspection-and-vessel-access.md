## Purpose

Shows the Government's inspection rights, the standards the work must meet, and who may be admitted to the contractor's facilities and the vessel under a ship repair Master Agreement.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-CTF-1950; @CL-CTF-1953] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 252.217-7005 and DFARS 252.217-7011 [@CL-CTF-1950; @CL-CTF-1953] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CTF-1950] |
| Applies to | Job orders under a ship repair Master Agreement that includes the Inspection and Manner of Doing Work clause or the Access to Vessel clause [@CL-CTF-1950; @CL-CTF-1953] |

The Government may inspect and test all material and workmanship at any time during the contractor's performance of the work [@CL-CTF-1950]. Subject to the Navy-specification exception, and unless the job order specifically provides otherwise, the standards paragraph covers all operational practices of the contractor and all workmanship, material, equipment and articles used in the work under the Master Agreement [@CL-CTF-1947; @CL-CTF-1949]. Those practices, workmanship, material, equipment and articles are to be in accordance with the best commercial marine practices and the rules and requirements of the American Bureau of Shipping, the United States Coast Guard and the Institute of Electrical and Electronic Engineers, as in effect at the time of the contractor's submission of bid, or acceptance of the job order if negotiated [@CL-CTF-1948; @CL-CTF-2173]. Where Navy specifications are specified in the job order, the contractor follows Navy standards of material and workmanship [@CL-CTF-1949].

## How it works

If the Government finds, before delivery, material or workmanship that is defective or not in accordance with the job order, it may reject the defective or nonconforming material, in addition to its rights under the Guarantees clause [@CL-CTF-1951], and may require the contractor to correct or replace it at the contractor's expense [@CL-CTF-2174]. If the contractor fails to proceed promptly with the replacement or correction, the Government may replace or correct the material or workmanship and charge the contractor the excess costs incurred [@CL-CTF-2175]. The Government does not guarantee the correctness of dimensions, sizes and shapes in the job order, sketches, drawings, plans or specifications it furnished, unless the job order requires the contractor to perform the work before any opportunity to inspect [@CL-CTF-2176]. Otherwise, other than parts furnished by the Government, the contractor is responsible for the correctness of the dimensions, sizes and shapes of parts it furnishes [@CL-CTF-2177]. As specified in the job order, the contractor shall provide and maintain an inspection system acceptable to the Government [@CL-CTF-2369]. The contractor must keep complete records of all inspection work and make them available during performance and for 90 days after all work required is complete [@CL-CTF-1952].

The clause also sets fire controls for the manner of doing work. The contractor shall exercise reasonable care to protect the vessel from fire [@CL-CTF-2326], shall maintain a reasonable system of inspection over activities taking place in the vicinity of the vessel's magazines, fuel oil tanks, or storerooms containing flammable materials [@CL-CTF-2332], and shall maintain a reasonable number of hose lines ready for immediate use on the vessel at all times while the vessel is berthed alongside the contractor's pier or in dry dock or on a marine railway [@CL-CTF-2327]. Unless a job order provides otherwise, it shall provide sufficient security patrols to reasonably maintain a fire watch for protection of the vessel when it is in the contractor's custody [@CL-CTF-2328]. To the extent necessary, it shall clean, wash, and steam out or otherwise make safe all tanks under alteration or repair [@CL-CTF-2329]. Before any hot work is done on a tank, it shall furnish the Contracting Officer or designated representative with a copy of the gas-free or safe-for-hotwork certificate provided by a Marine Chemist or Coast Guard authorized person, in accordance with the Occupational Safety and Health Administration regulations at 29 CFR 1915.14 [@CL-CTF-2330; @CL-CTF-2331]. It shall treat the contents of any tank as Government property in accordance with the Government Property clause [@CL-CTF-2333], and dispose of them only at the direction, or with the concurrence, of the Contracting Officer [@CL-CTF-2334].

The contractor shall not permit any welder to work on a vessel unless the welder is, at the time of the work, qualified to the standards established by the United States Coast Guard, American Bureau of Shipping, or Department of the Navy for the type of welding being performed [@CL-CTF-2361], and the welder's qualifications are as specified in the job order [@CL-CTF-2362]. Except as otherwise provided in the job order, when the vessel is in the contractor's custody or in dry dock or on a marine railway and the temperature is expected to go as low as 35 degrees Fahrenheit, the contractor shall take all necessary steps to keep all hose pipe lines, fixtures, traps, tanks, and other receptacles on the vessel from freezing and to protect the stern tube and propeller hubs from frost damage [@CL-CTF-2363; @CL-CTF-2364]. The contractor shall at all times keep the site of the work on the vessel free from accumulation of waste material or rubbish caused by its employees or the work [@CL-CTF-2367], and at the completion of the work, unless the job order specifies otherwise, it shall remove all rubbish from the site and leave the immediate vicinity of the work area broom clean [@CL-CTF-2368].

On the Contracting Officer's request, the contractor must grant admission to its facilities and access to the vessel, on a non-interference basis and as necessary for their respective responsibilities, to a reasonable number of the listed people [@CL-CTF-1953]. Those people are Government and other Government contractor employees, in addition to Government employees attached to the vessel, and representatives of offerors on other contemplated Government work [@CL-CTF-1954]. All personnel granted access must comply with the contractor's rules governing personnel at its shipyard [@CL-CTF-1954]. Whenever practicable, the contractor shall perform the work so that it does not interfere with the berthing and messing of Government personnel attached to the vessel, and shall give those personnel access to the vessel at all times [@CL-CTF-2365]; in turn, Government personnel attached to the vessel shall not interfere with the contractor's work or workers [@CL-CTF-2366].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A job order covers a pump replacement and does not specify Navy standards. The yard follows the best commercial marine practices and the classification and Coast Guard rules in effect when it submitted its bid. Before delivery the inspector finds a pump that does not meet the job order. The Government may reject it, in addition to its guarantee rights, and may require the yard to correct or replace it at the yard's expense. If the yard does not proceed promptly, the Government may have the pump replaced or corrected and charge the yard the excess costs. The contractor keeps its inspection records for the work available for 90 days after all work is complete. Separately, the Contracting Officer asks the yard to admit a few employees of another Government contractor to the vessel. The yard admits a reasonable number without interfering with its work, and those visitors follow the yard's shipyard rules. The job order also calls for welding a bracket inside a fuel tank. The yard keeps an inspection system over the work going on near that tank and the vessel's magazines. It treats the fuel drained from the tank as Government property and disposes of it only as the Contracting Officer directs or agrees. The yard runs the inspection system the job order specifies and keeps its inspection records for 90 days after all work is complete. The welder the yard assigns is qualified, at the time of the work, to the Coast Guard, American Bureau of Shipping or Navy standard that the job order specifies for that type of welding. Before any hot work on that tank, the yard makes the tank safe and gives the Contracting Officer's representative a copy of the gas-free or safe-for-hotwork certificate from a Marine Chemist or Coast Guard authorized person. While the vessel is in dry dock, hose lines stay ready for immediate use and security patrols keep a fire watch. The forecast shows 30 degrees Fahrenheit while the vessel is in dry dock, and the job order says nothing different, so the yard protects the hose lines, tanks and other receptacles from freezing and the stern tube and propeller hubs from frost. Crew members still berthed aboard keep access to the vessel, and the yard does not let its work interfere with their berthing and messing where practicable. At completion the yard removes all rubbish and leaves the work area broom clean.

## Never confuse

Inspection before delivery is separate from the guarantee after redelivery. The right to reject before delivery is in addition to the Government's rights under the Guarantees clause [@CL-CTF-1951]; for the guarantee, see [Ship repair master agreement: performance, guarantees and over and above work (DFARS)](ctf-dfars-ship-repair-performance-guarantees-and-over-and-above.md). The admission clause is also separate from the Government employees already attached to the vessel, because it covers further Government and other Government contractor employees and offeror representatives [@CL-CTF-1954]. For how job orders are issued and changed, see [Ship repair master agreement: property exchange, changes and job orders (DFARS)](ctf-dfars-ship-repair-exchange-changes-and-job-orders.md). For the general inspection clause for fixed-price supplies, see [Inspection of Supplies—Fixed-Price clause](../../close-revrec/revenue/cls-inspection-of-supplies-fixed-price.md), and for liability for loss of or damage to the vessel, see [Liability and insurance for vessel repair (DFARS)](../../supply-chain/sourcing-evidence/sup-dfars-ship-repair-liability-and-insurance.md).
