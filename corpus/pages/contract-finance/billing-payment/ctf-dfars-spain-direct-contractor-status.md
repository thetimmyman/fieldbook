## Purpose

Shows what direct-contractor status in Spain gives a contractor, what it must do to obtain it, and the guaranty or fund that protects duty-free imports.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-CTF-1868] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 252.229-7004 [@CL-CTF-1868] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | JUN 1997 [@CL-CTF-9683] |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CTF-1868] |
| Applies to | Contracts that include the direct contractor status clause for Spain [@CL-CTF-1919], which designates the contractor a Direct Contractor [@CL-CTF-1869] |

In the clause, a Direct Contractor is an individual, company, or entity with whom a Department of Defense agency has executed a written agreement that allows duty-free import of equipment, materials, and supplies into Spain [@CL-CTF-1868]. The clause describes that duty-free import as being for the construction, development, maintenance, and operation of Spanish-American installations and facilities [@CL-CTF-1911]. The contractor is designated a Direct Contractor under the named complementary agreement and articles of the Agreement on Friendship, Defense and Cooperation between the United States and Spain [@CL-CTF-1869].

The contractor shall apply to the appropriate Spanish authorities for approval of the status, in order to complete duty-free import of non-Spanish equipment, materials, and supplies represented as necessary for contract performance by the Contracting Officer [@CL-CTF-1870]. The contractor must submit its documentation in sufficient time to permit processing by the appropriate United States and Spanish Government agencies prior to the arrival of the equipment, material, or supplies in Spain [@CL-CTF-1907]. Orders placed before official notification of approval are at the contractor's own risk [@CL-CTF-1871]. Any delay or expense arising directly or indirectly from this process shall not excuse untimely performance (except as expressly allowed in other provisions of the contract), constitute a direct or constructive change, or otherwise provide a basis for additional compensation or adjustment of any kind [@CL-CTF-1872].

## How it works

To ensure that all duty-free imports are properly accounted for, exported, or disposed of in accordance with Spanish law, the contractor shall obtain a written bank letter of guaranty payable to the Treasurer of the United States, or such other authority as the Contracting Officer may designate [@CL-CTF-9590; @CL-CTF-1873]. The guaranty is to be in the amount set forth in paragraph (g) of the clause, prior to effecting any duty-free imports for the performance of the contract [@CL-CTF-1908]. The amount required under paragraph (d), (e), or (f) is to be inserted by the Contracting Officer at the time of contract award [@CL-CTF-1910]. If the contractor fails to obtain the required guaranty, the contractor agrees that the Contracting Officer may withhold a portion of the contract payments in order to establish a fund in the amount set forth in paragraph (g) of the clause [@CL-CTF-1874]. The fund shall be used for the payment of import taxes in the event that the contractor fails to properly account for, export, or dispose of duty-free imports [@CL-CTF-1909]. The amount of the bank letter of guaranty or size of the fund required under paragraph (d) or (e) normally shall be 5 percent of the contract value [@CL-CTF-1875]. However, if the contractor demonstrates to the Contracting Officer's satisfaction that the amount retained by the United States Government or guaranteed by the bank is excessive, the amount shall be reduced to an amount commensurate with contingent import tax and duty-free liability [@CL-CTF-1876]. This bank guaranty or fund shall not be released to the contractor until the Spanish General Directorate of Customs verifies the accounting, export, or disposition of the equipment, material, or supplies imported on a duty-free basis [@CL-CTF-1877]. The contractor agrees to insert the provisions of the clause, including paragraph (h), in all subcontracts [@CL-CTF-1878].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contract is worth an assumed 2,000,000. The Contracting Officer inserted the normal amount of 5 percent, which is 100,000. The contractor shows to the Contracting Officer's satisfaction that its contingent import tax and duty-free liability is only 40,000, so the amount is reduced to 40,000. If it had not obtained the guaranty, the Contracting Officer could have withheld payments to build a fund of that amount. Either way, the guaranty or fund is released only after Spanish customs verifies the imports were accounted for, exported or disposed of.

## Never confuse

A Direct Contractor is an individual, company, or entity with whom an agency of the United States Department of Defense has executed a written agreement that allows duty-free import of equipment, materials, and supplies into Spain [@CL-CTF-1868]. The contractor shall apply to the appropriate Spanish authorities for approval of status as a Direct Contractor in order to complete duty-free import of non-Spanish equipment, materials, and supplies represented as necessary for contract performance by the Contracting Officer [@CL-CTF-1870]. In accordance with tax relief agreements between the United States Government and the Spanish Government, and because the incumbent contract arises from the activities of the United States Forces in Spain, the contract will be exempt from the excise, luxury, and transaction taxes listed in the Spanish tax exemptions clause [@CL-CTF-1880]; see [Spain tax exemptions and the matching representation](ctf-dfars-spain-tax-exemptions-and-representation.md). Any delay or expense arising directly or indirectly from the approval process shall not excuse untimely performance (except as expressly allowed in other provisions of the contract), constitute a direct or constructive change, or otherwise provide a basis for additional compensation or adjustment of any kind [@CL-CTF-1872]. The bank guaranty or fund shall not be released to the contractor until the Spanish General Directorate of Customs verifies the accounting, export, or disposition of the equipment, material, or supplies imported on a duty-free basis [@CL-CTF-1877]. For the FAR tax clause, see [Federal, State, and Local Taxes clause](ctf-federal-state-local-taxes.md). Another host country's tax clause is on [Italy tax exemptions and the matching representation](ctf-dfars-italy-tax-exemptions-and-representation.md).
