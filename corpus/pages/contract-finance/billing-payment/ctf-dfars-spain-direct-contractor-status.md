## Purpose

Shows what direct-contractor status in Spain gives a contractor, what it must do to obtain it, and the guaranty or fund that protects duty-free imports.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-CTF-1868] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 252.229-7004 [@CL-CTF-1868] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CTF-1868] |
| Applies to | Contracts that include the direct contractor status clause for Spain [@CL-CTF-1919], which designates the contractor a Direct Contractor [@CL-CTF-1869] |

In the clause, a Direct Contractor is an individual, company, or entity with whom a Department of Defense agency has executed a written agreement that allows duty-free import of equipment, materials, and supplies into Spain [@CL-CTF-1868]. The clause describes that duty-free import as being for the construction, development, maintenance, and operation of Spanish-American installations and facilities [@CL-CTF-1911]. The contractor is designated a Direct Contractor under the named complementary agreement and articles of the Agreement on Friendship, Defense and Cooperation between the United States and Spain [@CL-CTF-1869].

The contractor must apply to the appropriate Spanish authorities for approval of the status, in order to complete duty-free import of non-Spanish equipment, materials, and supplies represented as necessary for contract performance by the Contracting Officer [@CL-CTF-1870]. The contractor must submit its documentation in sufficient time to permit processing by the appropriate United States and Spanish Government agencies prior to the arrival of the equipment, material, or supplies in Spain [@CL-CTF-1907]. Orders placed before official notification of approval are at the contractor's own risk [@CL-CTF-1871]. Delay or expense from the approval process does not excuse untimely performance, is not a change, and is not a basis for additional compensation or adjustment, except as other provisions of the contract expressly allow [@CL-CTF-1872].

## How it works

The contractor must obtain a written bank letter of guaranty payable to the Treasurer of the United States, or such other authority as the Contracting Officer may designate [@CL-CTF-1873]. The guaranty is required in the amount set forth in the paragraph that states the amount, before any duty-free imports for the performance of the contract [@CL-CTF-1908]. The Contracting Officer inserts that amount at the time of contract award [@CL-CTF-1910]. If the contractor fails to obtain the guaranty, the Contracting Officer may withhold part of the contract payments to build a fund of that amount [@CL-CTF-1874]. The fund is used to pay import taxes if the contractor fails to properly account for, export, or dispose of duty-free imports [@CL-CTF-1909]. The amount normally is 5 percent of the contract value [@CL-CTF-1875]. If the contractor demonstrates to the Contracting Officer's satisfaction that the amount is excessive, it is reduced to an amount commensurate with contingent import tax and duty-free liability [@CL-CTF-1876]. The guaranty or fund is not released until the Spanish General Directorate of Customs verifies the accounting, export, or disposition of the duty-free imports [@CL-CTF-1877]. The contractor must insert the provisions of the clause in all subcontracts [@CL-CTF-1878].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contract is worth an assumed 2,000,000. The Contracting Officer inserted the normal amount of 5 percent, which is 100,000. The contractor shows to the Contracting Officer's satisfaction that its contingent import tax and duty-free liability is only 40,000, so the amount is reduced to 40,000. If it had not obtained the guaranty, the Contracting Officer could have withheld payments to build a fund of that amount. Either way, the guaranty or fund is released only after Spanish customs verifies the imports were accounted for, exported or disposed of.

## Never confuse

Direct-contractor status rests on the written agreement and the approval that permits duty-free import [@CL-CTF-1868; @CL-CTF-1870]. It is not the list of taxes from which the contract is exempt, which has its own clause, see [Spain tax exemptions and the matching representation](ctf-dfars-spain-tax-exemptions-and-representation.md) [@CL-CTF-1880]. The status does not protect the contractor's schedule or price: approval delay gives no basis for adjustment unless another provision of the contract expressly allows it [@CL-CTF-1872]. The guaranty or fund is held and is not released to the contractor until Spanish customs verifies the imports [@CL-CTF-1877]. For the FAR tax clause, see [Federal, State, and Local Taxes clause](ctf-federal-state-local-taxes.md). Another host country's tax clause is on [Italy tax exemptions and the matching representation](ctf-dfars-italy-tax-exemptions-and-representation.md).
