## Purpose

Explains how the State and Local Taxes clause takes State and local taxes out of the contract price, and how the Federal, State, and Local Taxes clause with State and local adjustments puts applicable taxes in the price and then moves the price when a tax is imposed, relieved or borne through the Contractor's fault.

## The rule

|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CTF-2521] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.229-1 and FAR 52.229-4 [@CL-CTF-2521; @CL-CTF-2523]; FAR 2.101 [@CL-CTF-9466] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.229-1 [@CL-CTF-0660; @CL-CTF-0661]; RFO FAR 52.229-4 [@CL-CTF-0662; @CL-CTF-0663; @CL-CTF-0664; @CL-CTF-0665; @CL-CTF-0666; @CL-CTF-0667; @CL-CTF-0668; @CL-CTF-0669; @CL-CTF-0670; @CL-CTF-0671; @CL-CTF-0672; @CL-CTF-0673; @CL-CTF-0674; @CL-CTF-0675; @CL-CTF-0676; @CL-CTF-0677; @CL-CTF-0678; @CL-CTF-9127; @CL-CTF-9128; @CL-CTF-9129; @CL-CTF-9130]; RFO FAR 2.101 [@CL-CTF-9467] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CTF-2521] |
| Applies to | Contracts that include the State and Local Taxes clause or the clause with State and local adjustments [@CL-CTF-2521; @CL-CTF-2523] |

The State and Local Taxes clause says that, notwithstanding the terms of the Federal, State, and Local Taxes clause, the contract price excludes all State and local taxes levied on or measured by the contract or sales price of the services or completed supplies furnished under the contract [@CL-CTF-2521]. The Contractor must state the excluded taxes separately on its invoices, and the Government agrees either to pay the amount of the taxes to the Contractor or to provide evidence necessary to sustain an exemption [@CL-CTF-2522]. The FAR definitions section defines State and local taxes as taxes levied by the States, the District of Columbia, outlying areas of the United States, or their political subdivisions [@CL-CTF-9466], and the RFO text of FAR 2.101 has the same sentence word for word [@CL-CTF-9467; @CL-CTF-9466].

The clause with State and local adjustments takes the opposite default: unless the contract provides otherwise, the contract price includes all applicable Federal, State, and local taxes and duties, except as provided in subparagraph (b)(2)(i) [@CL-CTF-2523]. Taxes imposed under section 5000C of title 26 of the United States Code may not be included in the contract price and may not be reimbursed [@CL-CTF-2524].

In that clause, all applicable Federal, State, and local taxes and duties means all taxes and duties, in effect on the contract date, that the taxing authority is imposing and collecting on the transactions or property covered by the contract [@CL-CTF-2791]. The contract date is the effective date of the contract and, for any modification to the contract, the effective date of the modification [@CL-CTF-2792]. Local taxes includes taxes imposed by a possession or territory of the United States, Puerto Rico, or the Northern Mariana Islands, if the contract is performed wholly or partly in any of those areas [@CL-CTF-2793].

## How it works

The clause with State and local adjustments moves the price for an after-imposed tax or an after-relieved tax, and decreases the price for a tax that the Contractor bears through its own fault.

An after-imposed tax includes any new or increased Federal, State, or local tax or duty, or a tax that was excluded on the contract date but whose exclusion was later revoked or whose exemption was reduced during the contract period, other than an excepted tax [@CL-CTF-2612]. It is a tax on the transactions or property covered by the contract that the Contractor is required to pay or bear as the result of legislative, judicial, or administrative action taking effect after the contract date [@CL-CTF-2613].

An after-relieved tax is any amount of Federal, State, or local tax or duty, other than an excepted tax, that would otherwise have been payable on the transactions or property covered by the contract [@CL-CTF-2614]. The Contractor is not required to pay or bear it, or obtains a refund or drawback of it, as the result of legislative, judicial, or administrative action taking effect after the contract date [@CL-CTF-2615].

Excepted tax means social security or other employment taxes, net income and franchise taxes, excess profits taxes, capital stock taxes, transportation taxes, unemployment compensation taxes, and property taxes [@CL-CTF-2616]. It does not include gross income taxes levied on or measured by sales or receipts from sales, property taxes assessed on completed supplies covered by the contract, or any tax assessed on the Contractor's possession of, interest in, or use of property, title to which is in the Government [@CL-CTF-2617].

The contract price is increased by the amount of any after-imposed tax, or of any tax or duty specifically excluded from the contract price by a term or condition of the contract that the Contractor is required to pay or bear, including any interest or penalty [@CL-CTF-2567]. The increase applies if the Contractor states in writing that the contract price does not include any contingency for the tax, and if liability for the tax, interest, or penalty was not incurred through the Contractor's fault, negligence, or failure to follow instructions of the Contracting Officer [@CL-CTF-2525].

The contract price is decreased by the amount of any after-relieved tax, and the Government is entitled to interest received by the Contractor incident to a tax refund to the extent that the interest was earned after the Contractor was paid by the Government for the taxes [@CL-CTF-2526]. The Government is also entitled to repayment of any penalty refunded to the Contractor to the extent that the penalty was paid by the Government [@CL-CTF-2794]. The contract price is also decreased by the amount of any Federal, State, or local tax, other than an excepted tax, that was included in the contract price and that the Contractor is required to pay or bear, or does not obtain a refund of [@CL-CTF-2568], through the Contractor's fault, negligence, or failure to follow instructions of the Contracting Officer [@CL-CTF-2569].

No adjustment is made in the contract price under the clause unless the amount of the adjustment exceeds $250 [@CL-CTF-2527].

The Contractor must promptly notify the Contracting Officer of all matters relating to Federal, State, and local taxes and duties that reasonably may be expected to result in an increase or decrease in the contract price, and must take appropriate action as the Contracting Officer directs [@CL-CTF-2528]. The contract price is equitably adjusted to cover the costs of action taken at the direction of the Contracting Officer, including any interest, penalty, and reasonable attorneys' fees [@CL-CTF-2570].

The Government must furnish evidence appropriate to establish exemption from any Federal, State, or local tax when the Contractor requests the exemption and states in writing that it applies to a tax excluded from the contract price, and a reasonable basis exists to sustain the exemption [@CL-CTF-2611].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text from February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. RFO FAR 52.229-1 keeps the rule that, notwithstanding the terms of the Federal, State, and Local Taxes clause, the contract price excludes all State and local taxes levied on or measured by the contract or sales price of the services or completed supplies furnished under the contract [@CL-CTF-0660; @CL-CTF-2521], and the rule that the contractor states the excluded taxes separately on its invoices while the Government agrees either to pay the taxes or provide evidence necessary to sustain an exemption [@CL-CTF-0661; @CL-CTF-2522]. RFO FAR 52.229-4 keeps the definitions of an after-imposed tax [@CL-CTF-0662; @CL-CTF-0663; @CL-CTF-2612; @CL-CTF-2613], an after-relieved tax [@CL-CTF-0664; @CL-CTF-0665; @CL-CTF-2614; @CL-CTF-2615] and an excepted tax [@CL-CTF-0666; @CL-CTF-0667; @CL-CTF-2616; @CL-CTF-2617], and the definitions of all applicable Federal, State, and local taxes and duties [@CL-CTF-9129; @CL-CTF-2791], contract date [@CL-CTF-9127; @CL-CTF-2792] and local taxes [@CL-CTF-9128; @CL-CTF-2793]. It keeps the rule that, unless the contract provides otherwise, the contract price includes all applicable Federal, State, and local taxes and duties, except as provided in subparagraph (b)(2)(i) of the clause [@CL-CTF-0668; @CL-CTF-2523], and the rule that taxes imposed under section 5000C of title 26 of the United States Code may not be included in the price or reimbursed [@CL-CTF-0669; @CL-CTF-2524]. Where the codified paragraph (c) says the contract price shall be increased by the amount of an after-imposed tax or specifically excluded tax or duty [@CL-CTF-2567], the RFO paragraph (c) says the Government will increase the contract price by that amount [@CL-CTF-0670]. The RFO paragraph (c) keeps the conditions that the contractor states in writing that the price carries no contingency for the tax and that liability was not incurred through the contractor's fault, negligence, or failure to follow the Contracting Officer's instructions [@CL-CTF-0671; @CL-CTF-2525]. Where the codified paragraph (d) says the contract price shall be decreased by the amount of an after-relieved tax [@CL-CTF-2526], the RFO paragraph (d) says the Government will decrease the contract price by that amount [@CL-CTF-0672], and it keeps the Government's entitlement to interest received by the contractor incident to a tax refund, to the extent the interest was earned after the Government paid the contractor for the taxes [@CL-CTF-9130; @CL-CTF-2526]. It keeps the Government's entitlement to repayment of a refunded penalty to the extent the Government paid the penalty [@CL-CTF-0673; @CL-CTF-2794]. Where the codified paragraph (e) says the contract price shall be decreased by the amount of a tax, other than an excepted tax, that was included in the price and that the contractor is required to pay or bear or does not obtain a refund of [@CL-CTF-2568], the RFO paragraph (e) says the Government will decrease the contract price by that amount [@CL-CTF-0674], and it keeps the condition that this is through the contractor's fault, negligence, or failure to follow the Contracting Officer's instructions [@CL-CTF-0675; @CL-CTF-2569]. Where the codified paragraph (f) says no adjustment shall be made in the contract price unless the amount of the adjustment exceeds $250 [@CL-CTF-2527], the RFO paragraph (f) says the Government will not adjust the contract price unless the amount of the adjustment exceeds $250 [@CL-CTF-0676]. RFO paragraph (g) keeps the contractor's duty to notify the Contracting Officer promptly of all matters relating to Federal, State, and local taxes and duties that reasonably may be expected to increase or decrease the contract price and to take appropriate action as the Contracting Officer directs [@CL-CTF-0677; @CL-CTF-2528]. RFO paragraph (h) keeps the Government's duty to furnish evidence appropriate to establish exemption when the contractor requests it and states in writing that it applies to a tax excluded from the price and a reasonable basis exists to sustain the exemption [@CL-CTF-0678; @CL-CTF-2611].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A firm-fixed-price service contract with the clause with State and local adjustments has 40,000 of Federal and State taxes in its price. After the contract date a State law adds 3,000 of tax on the same transactions. The Contractor must pay it, states in writing that the price carries no contingency for it, and did not incur the liability through its own fault, negligence, or failure to follow the Contracting Officer's instructions. The price increases by 3,000, which exceeds 250. A later court ruling relieves 200 of tax, an after-relieved tax, and no adjustment is made because 200 does not exceed 250. A later change in law gives the Contractor a refund of 1,000 of tax, with 40 of interest earned after the Government paid the Contractor for the tax, so the price decreases by 1,000 and the Government is entitled to the 40. The Contractor also receives a refund of a 300 penalty, of which the Government had paid 120, so the Government is entitled to repayment of 120.

A tax of 5,000 that the State was already imposing and collecting on the covered transactions on the contract date is among the applicable taxes already in the price, so the price does not move for it. On a contract performed partly in Puerto Rico, a tax imposed by Puerto Rico is a local tax. For a later modification, the contract date is the modification's effective date.

An increase in social security tax is an excepted tax, so it is not an after-imposed tax. The Contractor also pays 1,500 of State tax that was in the price because it failed to follow the Contracting Officer's instructions, so the price decreases by 1,500.

Under the State and Local Taxes clause, the Contractor shows 2,000 of State sales tax separately on an invoice. That tax is outside the price, and the Government either pays the 2,000 or provides evidence that sustains an exemption.

## Never confuse

The State and Local Taxes clause leaves State and local taxes out of the price and settles them invoice by invoice, while the clause with State and local adjustments puts applicable taxes in the price and then moves the price as taxes change. An excepted tax, such as social security tax, falls outside the definitions of an after-imposed tax and an after-relieved tax [@CL-CTF-2612; @CL-CTF-2614; @CL-CTF-2616].

The separate Federal, State, and Local Taxes clause is on the [Federal, State, and local taxes](ctf-federal-state-local-taxes.md) card. This clause also reaches State and local taxes.
