## Purpose

Shows which foreign taxes and duties are not an allowable cost under the foreign cost-reimbursement clause and the cost-reimbursement clause with a foreign government, and where the amount of a foreign tax credit goes.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CTF-2549] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.229-8 and FAR 52.229-9 [@CL-CTF-2549; @CL-CTF-2551] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.229-8 and RFO FAR 52.229-9 [@CL-CTF-0336; @CL-CTF-0340] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CTF-2549] |
| Applies to | Not established. |

Under the foreign cost-reimbursement clause, a tax or duty from which the United States Government is exempt by agreement with the foreign government [@CL-CTF-2635], or from which the Contractor or any subcontractor is exempt under the laws of the foreign country [@CL-CTF-2549], is not an allowable cost under the contract. The clause for cost-reimbursement contracts with a foreign government states the same rule but names only the subcontractor as the party exempt under the laws of the foreign country [@CL-CTF-2637; @CL-CTF-2551].

Under the foreign cost-reimbursement clause, paragraph (b) applies if the Contractor or a subcontractor obtains a foreign tax credit that reduces its Federal income tax liability under the United States Internal Revenue Code because of the payment of a tax or duty that was reimbursed under the contract [@CL-CTF-2550]. The amount of the reduction is then paid or credited at the time of the offset to the Government of the United States as the Contracting Officer directs [@CL-CTF-2636].

Under the clause with a foreign government, paragraph (b) applies if any subcontractor obtains such a credit on a reimbursed tax or duty [@CL-CTF-2638]. The amount of the reduction is paid, not credited to the contract, to the Treasurer of the United States at the time the Federal income tax return is filed [@CL-CTF-2552].

## How it works

Under the foreign cost-reimbursement clause, a tax or duty that the Government is exempt from by agreement with the foreign government, or that the Contractor or any subcontractor is exempt from under the foreign country's laws, shall not constitute an allowable cost [@CL-CTF-2635; @CL-CTF-2549]. Under the clause with a foreign government, the local-law exemption named is that of any subcontractor [@CL-CTF-2637; @CL-CTF-2551].

The general clause covers a credit obtained by the Contractor or a subcontractor and sends the amount to the Government of the United States as the Contracting Officer directs [@CL-CTF-2550; @CL-CTF-2636]. The foreign-government clause covers a credit obtained by a subcontractor and sends the amount, not credited to the contract, to the Treasurer of the United States when the Federal income tax return is filed [@CL-CTF-2638; @CL-CTF-2552].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text from February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. Both RFO clauses keep the exemption test in paragraph (a): in the clause for foreign cost-reimbursement contracts, a tax or duty from which the United States Government is exempt by agreement with the foreign government [@CL-CTF-2635; @CL-CTF-0335], or from which the Contractor or any subcontractor is exempt under the laws of the foreign country, is not an allowable cost [@CL-CTF-2549; @CL-CTF-0336]; in the clause with a foreign government, the same applies to the agreement rule [@CL-CTF-2637; @CL-CTF-0339] and to a tax or duty from which any subcontractor is exempt under the laws of the foreign country [@CL-CTF-2551; @CL-CTF-0340]. Both keep the paragraph (b) trigger, a foreign tax credit that reduces Federal income tax liability because of the payment of a tax or duty that was reimbursed [@CL-CTF-2550; @CL-CTF-0337] [@CL-CTF-2638; @CL-CTF-0341]. The RFO text of paragraph (b) of FAR 52.229-8 says the Contractor shall pay or credit the amount of the reduction at the time of the offset to the Government of the United States as the Contracting Officer directs [@CL-CTF-0338; @CL-CTF-2636], and that of FAR 52.229-9 says the subcontractor shall pay, not credit to the contract, the amount of the reduction to the Treasurer of the United States at the time the Federal income tax return is filed [@CL-CTF-0342; @CL-CTF-2552].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A cost-reimbursement contract performed abroad pays 25,000 of a local tax from which the subcontractor is exempt under that country's laws, so the 25,000 is not an allowable cost. A separate 10,000 tax is one from which the United States Government is exempt by agreement with the foreign government, so it is not an allowable cost either.

A different 8,000 tax is reimbursed. The Contractor later obtains a foreign tax credit that cuts its Federal income tax by 8,000, so under the foreign cost-reimbursement clause the 8,000 is paid or credited to the Government at the time of the offset, as the Contracting Officer directs. Under the clause with a foreign government, a subcontractor obtains a credit of 4,000 on a reimbursed tax. It pays the 4,000 to the Treasurer of the United States when it files its Federal income tax return, and it does not credit the amount to the contract.

## Never confuse

For taxes under foreign fixed-price contracts, see [Taxes under foreign fixed-price contracts](ctf-foreign-fixed-price-taxes.md).

The foreign-government version also differs from the general version in two places: only the subcontractor is named as the party exempt under local law, and only a subcontractor's credit is covered and it is paid to the Treasurer of the United States at filing, not credited to the contract [@CL-CTF-2551; @CL-CTF-2638; @CL-CTF-2552].

In the RFO text each paragraph (b) names who pays the amount of the reduction: the Contractor under the foreign cost-reimbursement clause [@CL-CTF-0338] and the subcontractor under the clause with a foreign government [@CL-CTF-0342], where the codified paragraphs say only that the amount is paid or credited [@CL-CTF-2636] or paid, not credited to the contract [@CL-CTF-2552].
