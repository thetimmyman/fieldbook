## Purpose

Shows the contractor's performance duty, the guarantee on repaired work, and how over and above work is identified, approved and priced.

Upon the award of a job order, the contractor shall promptly start the work specified and diligently prosecute the work to completion [@CL-CTF-1967]. If work performed or materials furnished prove defective or deficient within 90 days from the date of redelivery of the vessel, the contractor, as directed by the Contracting Officer and at its own expense, shall correct and repair the deficiency [@CL-CTF-1968]. If the contractor or any subcontractor has a guarantee that exceeds the 90 day period, the Government is entitled to rely upon the longer guarantee until its expiration [@CL-CTF-1995]. For any individual work item identified as incomplete when the vessel is redelivered, the guarantee period runs from the date the item is completed, not from redelivery [@CL-CTF-2153].

Over and above work is work discovered during overhaul, maintenance and repair efforts that is within the general scope of the contract, not covered by the line items for the basic work, and necessary [@CL-CTF-1969].

## How it works

### Guarantee

If the Contracting Officer determines it is not practicable or otherwise not advisable to return the vessel to the contractor, or the contractor fails to proceed with the repairs promptly, the Contracting Officer may direct that the repairs be performed elsewhere, at the contractor's expense [@CL-CTF-1996]. If correction and repairs are performed by someone other than the contractor, the Contracting Officer may discharge the contractor's liability by making an equitable deduction in the price of the job order [@CL-CTF-1997]. The contractor's liability extends for an additional 90 day guarantee period on the defects or deficiencies that it corrected [@CL-CTF-1998]. At the Contracting Officer's option, defects and deficiencies may be left uncorrected, and the parties then negotiate an equitable reduction in the job price [@CL-CTF-1999].

### Over and above work

The clause calls for mutually agreed procedures for Government administration and contractor performance of over and above work requests, and if the parties cannot agree, the Administrative Contracting Officer has the unilateral right to direct the procedures to be followed [@CL-CTF-1990]. The Government must promptly review the work request, verify that the proposed work is required and not covered under the basic line items, verify that the proposed corrective action is appropriate, and authorize the over and above work as necessary [@CL-CTF-1970]. The contractor must promptly submit a proposal for the over and above work, the Government and the contractor then negotiate a settlement, and contract modifications are executed to definitize it [@CL-CTF-1971]. Failure to agree on the price is a dispute within the meaning of the Disputes clause [@CL-CTF-1991].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A vessel is redelivered, and a valve fails 60 days later, inside the 90 day period, so the contractor corrects it as directed and at its own expense. Its liability for that corrected defect then runs for a further 90 days. In another case the contractor does not proceed promptly with a repair, so the Contracting Officer has the repair done elsewhere at the contractor's expense and may discharge the contractor's liability by an equitable deduction in the job order price. During an overhaul, workers also find a corroded pipe that is within the contract's scope, not covered by the basic line items and needed to finish the job. The contractor prepares a work request under the agreed procedures, the Government verifies and authorizes it, and the contractor submits a proposal. The parties negotiate, and a modification definitizes the price. If they cannot agree on the price, it is a dispute under the Disputes clause.

## Never confuse

Over and above work is not a change. It is work discovered during the job [@CL-CTF-1969], while a change is made by written change order of the Contracting Officer [@CL-CTF-1938]; for changes, see [Ship repair master agreement: property exchange, changes and job orders (DFARS)](ctf-dfars-ship-repair-exchange-changes-and-job-orders.md). The guarantee is also a different mechanism: it covers defects found within the period after redelivery and the contractor corrects them at its own expense [@CL-CTF-1968], whereas over and above work is priced through a proposal, a negotiated settlement and a modification [@CL-CTF-1971]. A repair done elsewhere is charged to the contractor, and a defect left uncorrected instead brings a negotiated reduction in the job price [@CL-CTF-1996; @CL-CTF-1999]. The guarantee after redelivery is also separate from inspection before delivery; see [Ship repair master agreement: inspection, manner of work and vessel access (DFARS)](ctf-dfars-ship-repair-inspection-and-vessel-access.md). For warranties generally, see [Warranties: criteria, terms and remedies](../../supply-chain/sourcing-evidence/sup-warranties.md).
