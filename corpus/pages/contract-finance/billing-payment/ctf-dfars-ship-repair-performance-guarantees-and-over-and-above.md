## Purpose

Shows the contractor's performance duty, the guarantee on repaired work, and how over and above work is identified, approved and priced.

## The rule

Upon the award of a job order, the contractor shall promptly start the work specified and diligently prosecute the work to completion [@CL-CTF-1967]. The contractor may not start work until the job order has been awarded, except for emergency work ordered by the Contracting Officer under the Job Orders and Compensation clause of the Master Agreement [@CL-CTF-2372]. If work performed or materials furnished prove defective or deficient within 90 days from the date of redelivery of the vessel, the contractor, as directed by the Contracting Officer and at its own expense, shall correct and repair the deficiency to the satisfaction of the Contracting Officer [@CL-CTF-1968; @CL-CTF-2178]. If the contractor or any subcontractor has a guarantee that exceeds the 90 day period, the Government is entitled to rely upon the longer guarantee until its expiration [@CL-CTF-1995]. For any individual work item identified as incomplete when the vessel is redelivered, the guarantee period runs from the date the item is completed, not from redelivery [@CL-CTF-2153].

Over and above work is work discovered during overhaul, maintenance and repair efforts that is within the general scope of the contract, not covered by the line items for the basic work, and necessary in order to satisfactorily complete the contract [@CL-CTF-1969; @CL-CTF-2172].

DFARS subpart 217.77, Over and Above Work, which is separate from the ship repair provisions on this card, says to follow the procedures at PGI 217.7701 when acquiring over and above work [@CL-CTF-9281]. It also says to use the clause at 252.217-7028, Over and Above Work, in solicitations and contracts containing requirements for over and above work, except as provided for in subpart 217.71 [@CL-CTF-9282]. The text names no official who follows the procedures or uses the clause [@CL-CTF-9281; @CL-CTF-9282].

## How it works

### Performance

The Government delivers the vessel at the time and location the job order specifies and, on completion of the work, accepts delivery at that time and location [@CL-CTF-2373]. Without charge and without specific requirement in a job order, the contractor makes available at the plant to personnel of the vessel in dry dock or on a marine railway sanitary lavatory and similar facilities acceptable to the Contracting Officer [@CL-CTF-2180]. The list of items provided without charge also includes supplying and maintaining suitable brows and gangways from the pier, dry dock or marine railway to the vessel [@CL-CTF-2374]. It includes treating salvage, scrap or other ship's material of the Government resulting from the work as Government-furnished property under the Government Property clause [@CL-CTF-2375]. It also includes performing, or paying the cost of, any repair, reconditioning or replacement made necessary by the contractor's use of the vessel's machinery, equipment or fittings [@CL-CTF-2181], and furnishing suitable offices, office equipment and telephones at or near the site of the work for the Government's use [@CL-CTF-2376]. The job order states whether dock and sea trials are required to determine whether the contractor has satisfactorily performed the work [@CL-CTF-2418]. When the job order requires dock and sea trials, the vessel is under the control of the vessel's commander and crew [@CL-CTF-2370]. The contractor provides and installs all fittings and appliances necessary for the trials and is responsible for care, installation and removal of the instruments and apparatus that the Government furnishes for them [@CL-CTF-2371]. The contractor may not conduct dock and sea trials not specified in the job order without advance approval of the Contracting Officer, and such trials are at its expense and risk [@CL-CTF-2182].

### Guarantee

If practicable, the Government gives the contractor an opportunity to correct the deficiency [@CL-CTF-2179]. If the Contracting Officer determines it is not practicable or otherwise not advisable to return the vessel to the contractor, or the contractor fails to proceed with the repairs promptly, the Contracting Officer may direct that the repairs be performed elsewhere, at the contractor's expense [@CL-CTF-1996]. If correction and repairs are performed by someone other than the contractor, the Contracting Officer may discharge the contractor's liability by making an equitable deduction in the price of the job order [@CL-CTF-1997]. The contractor's liability extends for an additional 90 day guarantee period on the defects or deficiencies that it corrected [@CL-CTF-1998]. At the Contracting Officer's option, defects and deficiencies may be left uncorrected, and the parties then negotiate an equitable reduction in the job price [@CL-CTF-1999]. Failure to agree on that reduction is a dispute under the Disputes clause [@CL-CTF-2377].

### Over and above work

The clause calls for mutually agreed procedures for Government administration and contractor performance of over and above work requests, and if the parties cannot agree, the Administrative Contracting Officer has the unilateral right to direct the procedures to be followed [@CL-CTF-1990]. Upon discovery of the need for over and above work, the contractor prepares and furnishes a work request under the agreed procedures [@CL-CTF-2378], and those procedures cover, as a minimum, the format, content and submission of work requests, which must give the type and location of the discrepancy and the estimated labor hours and material to correct it [@CL-CTF-2379]. The request data must be sufficient to satisfy contract requirements and obtain the Contracting Officer's authorization to perform the proposed work, and the procedures also cover Government review, verification and authorization of the work and proposal pricing, submission, negotiation and definitization [@CL-CTF-2420]. The Government must promptly review the work request, verify that the proposed work is required and not covered under the basic line items, verify that the proposed corrective action is appropriate, and authorize the over and above work as necessary [@CL-CTF-1970]. The contractor must promptly submit a proposal for the over and above work, the Government and the contractor then negotiate a settlement, and contract modifications are executed to definitize it [@CL-CTF-1971]. Failure to agree on the price is a dispute within the meaning of the Disputes clause [@CL-CTF-1991].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

The yard may not start a job order's work before award, except emergency work that the Contracting Officer orders, and the Government delivers and accepts the vessel at the times and places the job order sets. A vessel is redelivered, and a valve fails 60 days later, inside the 90 day period, so the contractor corrects it as directed and at its own expense. Its liability for that corrected defect then runs for a further 90 days. In another case the Government, as the clause requires where practicable, gives the contractor the chance to correct a deficiency, but the contractor does not proceed promptly with the repair, so the Contracting Officer has the repair done elsewhere at the contractor's expense and may discharge the contractor's liability by an equitable deduction in the job order price. During an overhaul, workers also find a corroded pipe that is within the contract's scope, not covered by the basic line items and needed to finish the job. The contractor prepares a work request under the agreed procedures that gives the type and location of the corrosion and the estimated labor hours and material, the Government verifies and authorizes it, and the contractor submits a proposal. The parties negotiate, and a modification definitizes the price. Separately, the job order states that dock and sea trials are required to decide whether the work was done satisfactorily, and it requires a dock trial, so the vessel stays under the control of its commander and crew while the contractor installs the fittings and appliances the trial needs and looks after the Government's test instruments. The contractor also runs a second dock trial that the job order did not specify and did not get advance approval for, so that trial is at its own expense and risk. If the parties cannot agree on the price of the over and above work, it is a dispute under the Disputes clause.

## Never confuse

Over and above work is not a change. It is work discovered during the job [@CL-CTF-1969], while a change is made by written change order of the Contracting Officer [@CL-CTF-1938]; for changes, see [Ship repair master agreement: property exchange, changes and job orders (DFARS)](ctf-dfars-ship-repair-exchange-changes-and-job-orders.md). The guarantee is also a different mechanism: it covers defects found within the period after redelivery and the contractor corrects them at its own expense [@CL-CTF-1968], whereas over and above work is priced through a proposal, a negotiated settlement and a modification [@CL-CTF-1971]. A repair done elsewhere is charged to the contractor, and a defect left uncorrected instead brings a negotiated reduction in the job price [@CL-CTF-1996; @CL-CTF-1999]. The guarantee after redelivery is also separate from inspection before delivery; see [Ship repair master agreement: inspection, manner of work and vessel access (DFARS)](ctf-dfars-ship-repair-inspection-and-vessel-access.md). For warranties generally, see [Warranties: criteria, terms and remedies](../../supply-chain/sourcing-evidence/sup-warranties.md).
