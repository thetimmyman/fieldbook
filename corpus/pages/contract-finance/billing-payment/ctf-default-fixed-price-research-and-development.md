## Purpose

Shows when the Government may terminate a fixed-price research and development contract for default, what completed work it may take, what it pays for, and when a failure is excused.

Subject to paragraphs (c) and (d) of the clause, the Government may by written Notice of Default terminate the contract in whole or in part if the contractor fails to perform the work under the contract within the time specified in the contract or any extension [@CL-CTF-2089]. The two further grounds are failing to prosecute the work so as to endanger performance of the contract and failing to perform any of the other provisions of the contract, and each is followed by a cross-reference to subparagraph (a)(2) of the clause [@CL-CTF-2090]. The Government's right to terminate on those two grounds may be exercised if the contractor does not cure the failure within 10 days, or more if the contracting officer authorizes it in writing, after receiving the notice [@CL-CTF-2091]. The cure period runs after receipt of the notice from the contracting officer specifying the failure [@CL-CTF-2248].

Except for defaults of subcontractors at any tier, the contractor is not liable for any excess costs if the failure to perform arises from causes beyond the control and without the fault or negligence of the contractor [@CL-CTF-2093]. Examples of such causes include acts of God or of the public enemy, acts of the Government in either its sovereign or contractual capacity, fires, floods, epidemics, quarantine restrictions, strikes, freight embargoes and unusually severe weather [@CL-CTF-2246]. In each instance the failure to perform must be beyond the control and without the fault or negligence of the contractor [@CL-CTF-2247].

## How it works

If the contract is terminated for default, the Government may require the contractor to transfer title and deliver to the Government, as directed by the contracting officer, any completed or partially completed work not previously delivered to, and accepted by, the Government [@CL-CTF-2092]. The list also covers other property, including contract rights, specifically produced or acquired for the terminated portion of the contract [@CL-CTF-2109]. Upon direction of the contracting officer, the contractor must also protect and preserve property in its possession in which the Government has an interest [@CL-CTF-2249].

If the Government terminates the contract in whole or in part, it may acquire, under the terms and in the manner the contracting officer considers appropriate, work similar to the work terminated, and the contractor will be liable to the Government for any excess costs for the similar work [@CL-CTF-2244]. However, the contractor shall continue the work not terminated [@CL-CTF-2245].

On payment, the Government pays the contract price, if separately stated, for completed work it has accepted, and the amount agreed upon by the contractor and the contracting officer for completed work for which no separate price is stated and for partially completed work [@CL-CTF-2106]. The same list also includes other property described above that the Government accepts and the protection and preservation of the property [@CL-CTF-2250]. Failure to agree will be a dispute under the Disputes clause, and the Government may withhold from these amounts any sum the contracting officer determines to be necessary to protect the Government against loss from outstanding liens or claims of former lien holders [@CL-CTF-2108]. The Disputes clause is on [Disputes clause](../../close-revrec/revenue/cls-disputes-clause.md).

In the paragraph on a subcontractor's default, the conditions are that the failure to perform is caused by the default of a subcontractor at any tier, the cause of the default is beyond the control of both the contractor and the subcontractor, and the cause is without the fault or negligence of either [@CL-CTF-2110]. Then the contractor is not liable for any excess costs for failure to perform, unless the subcontracted supplies or services were obtainable from other sources in sufficient time for the contractor to meet the required delivery schedule or other performance requirements [@CL-CTF-2111]. The excess-cost excuse in paragraph (c) turns on causes beyond the control and without the fault or negligence of the contractor [@CL-CTF-2093].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text because Class Deviation 2026-O0038 directs it, effective February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. It keeps the Notice of Default grounds, which are failing to perform the work in time, failing to prosecute the work so as to endanger performance, and failing to perform any other provision [@CL-CTF-0154; @CL-CTF-0155; @CL-CTF-2089; @CL-CTF-2090], and the 10-day cure period after receipt of the notice for the last two grounds [@CL-CTF-0156; @CL-CTF-0157; @CL-CTF-2091; @CL-CTF-2248]. It keeps the right to acquire similar work with the contractor liable for excess costs, and the duty to continue the work not terminated [@CL-CTF-0158; @CL-CTF-0159; @CL-CTF-2244; @CL-CTF-2245], the excess-cost excuse for causes beyond the contractor's control and without its fault or negligence, with the same examples [@CL-CTF-0160; @CL-CTF-0161; @CL-CTF-0162; @CL-CTF-2093; @CL-CTF-2246; @CL-CTF-2247], and the subcontractor default rule [@CL-CTF-0163; @CL-CTF-0164; @CL-CTF-2110; @CL-CTF-2111]. It keeps the transfer of title to completed and partially completed work and other property, and the duty to protect and preserve property [@CL-CTF-0165; @CL-CTF-0166; @CL-CTF-0167; @CL-CTF-2092; @CL-CTF-2109; @CL-CTF-2249], and the payment, dispute and withholding rules [@CL-CTF-0168; @CL-CTF-0169; @CL-CTF-0170; @CL-CTF-2106; @CL-CTF-2250; @CL-CTF-2108].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor holds a fixed-price development contract and stops working on the prototype. The contracting officer sends a Notice of Default for failing to prosecute the work. The contractor does not cure within 10 days after receiving the contracting officer's notice specifying the failure, so the Government terminates. The prototype is partly built and has not been delivered or accepted, so the Government may require title to it and its delivery. The Government may then acquire similar work elsewhere, and the contractor is liable for the excess costs of that similar work unless the failure is excused. Assume the contractor and the contracting officer agree on 60,000 for the partly built prototype. If they cannot agree, the failure to agree is a dispute under the Disputes clause, and the Government may withhold any sum the contracting officer determines is necessary to protect against outstanding liens.

Had unusually severe weather beyond the contractor's control and without its fault stopped the work, the contractor would owe no excess costs. If a subcontractor had defaulted, the contractor would owe no excess costs only if the subcontractor's default met the stated conditions and the subcontracted work could not have been obtained from other sources in time.

## Never confuse

The Government may terminate if the contractor fails to perform the work under the contract within the time specified in the contract or any extension [@CL-CTF-2089], and may require transfer of completed or partially completed work not previously delivered to, and accepted by, the Government [@CL-CTF-2092]. The subcontractor-default rule refers to the required delivery schedule or other performance requirements [@CL-CTF-2111]. The supply and service clause is on [Default under fixed-price supply and service contracts](ctf-default-fixed-price-supply-and-service.md) and [Default clauses for fixed-price contracts and when each goes in](../../close-revrec/revenue/cls-fixed-price-default-clauses.md), and the effect of a default termination is on [Termination for default: effect, notices and repurchase against the contractor](../../close-revrec/revenue/cls-termination-for-default-effect-and-repurchase.md). Termination for convenience for research and development work with an educational or nonprofit institution on a no-profit or no-fee basis is on [Termination for convenience: educational and other nonprofit institutions](ctf-termination-for-convenience-educational-nonprofit.md) [@CL-CLS-1056].
