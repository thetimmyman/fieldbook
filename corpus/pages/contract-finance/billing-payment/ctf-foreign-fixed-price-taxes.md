## Purpose

Sets out how the foreign fixed-price clause and the fixed-price clause with a foreign government treat taxes in the contract price, which taxes stay out, and how the price changes when a tax changes after the contract date.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CTF-2539] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.229-6 and FAR 52.229-7 [@CL-CTF-2539; @CL-CTF-2546] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.229-6 and RFO FAR 52.229-7 [@CL-CTF-0343; @CL-CTF-0366] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CTF-2539] |
| Applies to | Foreign fixed-price clause: supplies furnished or services performed outside the United States and its outlying areas [@CL-CTF-2539]. Clause with a foreign government: Not established. |

To the extent that the contract provides for furnishing supplies or performing services outside the United States and its outlying areas, the foreign fixed-price clause applies in lieu of any Federal, State, and local taxes clause of the contract [@CL-CTF-2539]. Unless the contract provides otherwise, the contract price includes all applicable taxes and duties [@CL-CTF-2540], except taxes and duties that the Government of the United States and the government of the country concerned have agreed shall not be applicable to expenditures in such country by or on behalf of the United States [@CL-CTF-2620], and except as provided in subparagraph (c)(2) of the clause [@CL-CTF-2266].

Taxes imposed under section 5000C of title 26 of the United States Code may not be included in the contract price and may not be reimbursed [@CL-CTF-2541]. The contract price may not be increased to offset those taxes [@CL-CTF-2542].

In the foreign fixed-price clause, contract date means the date set for bid opening or, if the contract is negotiated or a modification, the effective date of the contract or modification, and country concerned means any country, other than the United States and its outlying areas, in which expenditures under the contract are made [@CL-CTF-2810]. Tax and taxes include fees and charges for doing business that are levied by the government of the country concerned or by its political subdivisions [@CL-CTF-2811]. All applicable taxes and duties means all taxes and duties, in effect on the contract date, that the taxing authority is imposing and collecting on the transactions or property covered by the contract, pursuant to written ruling or regulation in effect on the contract date [@CL-CTF-2812].

Under the clause for fixed-price contracts with foreign governments, the contract price, including the prices in any subcontracts, does not include any tax or duty that the Government of the United States and the foreign government have agreed shall not apply to expenditures made by the United States [@CL-CTF-2546], or any tax or duty not applicable to the contract or any subcontracts under the laws of the foreign country [@CL-CTF-2631]. Taxes imposed under section 5000C of title 26 of the United States Code may not be included in the contract price [@CL-CTF-2632].

## How it works

An after-imposed tax means any new or increased tax or duty, or tax that was exempted or excluded on the contract date but whose exemption was later revoked or reduced during the contract period, other than an excepted tax [@CL-CTF-2813], on the transactions or property covered by the contract that the Contractor is required to pay or bear as the result of legislative, judicial, or administrative action taking effect after the contract date [@CL-CTF-2814]. An after-relieved tax means any amount of tax or duty, other than an excepted tax, that would otherwise have been payable on the transactions or property covered by the contract [@CL-CTF-2815], but which the Contractor is not required to pay or bear, or for which the Contractor obtains a refund, as the result of legislative, judicial, or administrative action taking effect after the contract date [@CL-CTF-2816]. Excepted tax means social security or other employment taxes, net income and franchise taxes, excess profits taxes, capital stock taxes, transportation taxes, unemployment compensation taxes, and property taxes [@CL-CTF-2817]. It does not include gross income taxes levied on or measured by sales or receipts from sales, or property taxes assessed on completed supplies covered by the contract [@CL-CTF-2818], or any tax assessed on the Contractor's possession of, interest in, or use of property, title to which is in the United States Government [@CL-CTF-2819].

Under the foreign fixed-price clause, except as provided in subparagraph (d)(2) of the clause [@CL-CTF-9616], the contract price is increased by the amount of any after-imposed tax, or of any tax or duty specifically excluded from the contract price by a provision of the contract that the Contractor is required to pay or bear, including any interest or penalty [@CL-CTF-2621]. The increase applies if the Contractor states in writing that the contract price does not include any contingency for the tax [@CL-CTF-2622], and if liability for the tax, interest, or penalty was not incurred through the Contractor's fault, negligence, or failure to follow instructions of the Contracting Officer or to comply with the provisions of paragraph (i) [@CL-CTF-2623].

The contract price is decreased by the amount of any after-relieved tax, including any interest or penalty [@CL-CTF-2543]. The Government of the United States is entitled to interest received by the Contractor incident to a refund of taxes to the extent that the interest was earned after the Contractor was paid by the Government of the United States for the taxes [@CL-CTF-2820], and to repayment of any penalty refunded to the Contractor to the extent that the penalty was paid by the Government [@CL-CTF-2821]. The contract price is also decreased by the amount of any tax or duty, other than an excepted tax, that was included in the contract and that the Contractor is required to pay or bear, or does not obtain a refund of [@CL-CTF-2624], through the Contractor's fault, negligence, or failure to follow instructions of the Contracting Officer or to comply with the provisions of paragraph (i) [@CL-CTF-2625]. No adjustment is made in the contract price under the foreign fixed-price clause unless the amount of the adjustment exceeds $250 [@CL-CTF-2544].

If the Contractor obtains a reduction in tax liability under the United States Internal Revenue Code because of the payment of a tax or duty that either was included in the contract price or was the basis of an increase in the contract price [@CL-CTF-2626], the amount of the reduction is paid or credited to the Government of the United States as the Contracting Officer directs [@CL-CTF-2627].

The Contractor must take all reasonable action to obtain exemption from or refund of any taxes or duties, including interest or penalty [@CL-CTF-2545], from which the United States Government, the Contractor, any subcontractor, or the transactions or property covered by the contract are exempt under the laws of the country concerned or its political subdivisions [@CL-CTF-2628], or which the governments of the United States and of the country concerned have agreed shall not be applicable to expenditures in such country by or on behalf of the United States [@CL-CTF-2629].

The Contractor must promptly notify the Contracting Officer of all matters relating to taxes or duties that reasonably may be expected to result in an increase or decrease in the contract price, and must take appropriate action as the Contracting Officer directs [@CL-CTF-2630]. The contract price is equitably adjusted to cover the costs of action taken by the Contractor at the direction of the Contracting Officer, including any interest, penalty, and reasonable attorneys' fees [@CL-CTF-2822].

In the clause for fixed-price contracts with foreign governments, the contract date is the date set for bid opening or, if the contract is negotiated or a modification, the effective date of the contract or modification [@CL-CTF-2823]. Under that clause, if a tax or duty that the price does not include, because the two governments agreed it shall not apply or because it is not applicable under the laws of the foreign country, has been included in the contract price through error or otherwise, the price is correspondingly reduced [@CL-CTF-2546; @CL-CTF-2631; @CL-CTF-2547]. If, after the contract date, the Government of the United States and the foreign government agree that a tax or duty included in the contract price shall not apply to expenditures by the United States in the country [@CL-CTF-2633], the contract price is reduced accordingly [@CL-CTF-2634]. No adjustment is made in the contract price under that clause unless the amount of the adjustment exceeds $250 [@CL-CTF-2548].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text from February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. RFO FAR 52.229-6 keeps the rule that, to the extent the contract provides for furnishing supplies or performing services outside the United States and its outlying areas, the clause applies in lieu of any Federal, State, and local taxes clause [@CL-CTF-2539; @CL-CTF-0343]. It says that, except as provided in paragraph (d)(2) of the clause [@CL-CTF-9078], the Government will increase the contract price by the amount of any after-imposed tax, or of any tax or duty specifically excluded from the contract price that the Contractor is required to pay or bear, including any interest or penalty [@CL-CTF-0344; @CL-CTF-2621], where the codified text says the contract price shall be increased. The conditions are the Contractor's written statement that the price includes no contingency for the tax [@CL-CTF-0345; @CL-CTF-2622] and that liability was not incurred through the Contractor's fault, negligence, or failure to follow instructions of the Contracting Officer or to comply with the requirements of paragraph (i) of the clause [@CL-CTF-0346; @CL-CTF-2623]. It says the Government will not increase the contract price to offset taxes imposed under section 5000C [@CL-CTF-0347; @CL-CTF-2542], will decrease the contract price by the amount of any after-relieved tax, including any interest or penalty [@CL-CTF-0348; @CL-CTF-2543], and will not adjust the contract price unless the amount of the adjustment exceeds $250 [@CL-CTF-0349; @CL-CTF-2544]. Paragraph (f) likewise says the Government will decrease the contract price by the amount of any tax or duty, other than an excepted tax, that was included in the contract and that the Contractor is required to pay or bear, or does not obtain a refund of [@CL-CTF-9080; @CL-CTF-2624], through the Contractor's fault, negligence, or failure to follow instructions of the Contracting Officer or to comply with the requirements of paragraph (i) of the clause [@CL-CTF-9081; @CL-CTF-2625]. If the Contractor obtains a reduction in tax liability under the United States Internal Revenue Code [@CL-CTF-9079] because of the payment of a tax or duty that was included in the contract price or was the basis of an increase in the contract price, RFO paragraph (h) says the Contractor shall pay or credit the amount of the reduction to the Government of the United States as the Contracting Officer directs [@CL-CTF-0350], where the codified paragraph says the amount of the reduction shall be paid or credited to the Government of the United States as the Contracting Officer directs [@CL-CTF-2627]. The Government will equitably adjust the contract price to cover the costs of action the Contractor takes at the direction of the Contracting Officer, including any interest, penalty, and reasonable attorneys' fees [@CL-CTF-0365; @CL-CTF-2822]. In paragraphs (d)(1), (d)(2), (e), (g) and (j) the RFO paragraphs name the Government as the actor with "will", where the codified paragraphs use "shall" or "may not" [@CL-CTF-0344; @CL-CTF-2621] [@CL-CTF-0347; @CL-CTF-2542] [@CL-CTF-0348; @CL-CTF-2543] [@CL-CTF-0349; @CL-CTF-2544] [@CL-CTF-0365; @CL-CTF-2822], and paragraph (f) does the same [@CL-CTF-9080; @CL-CTF-2624]. In FAR 52.229-7 the RFO text keeps the rule that taxes imposed under section 5000C may not be included in the contract price [@CL-CTF-2632; @CL-CTF-0366]. It says that, if after the contract date the Government of the United States and the foreign government agree that a tax or duty included in the contract price shall not apply to expenditures by the United States in the country [@CL-CTF-0367; @CL-CTF-2633], the Government of the United States will reduce the contract price accordingly [@CL-CTF-0368; @CL-CTF-2634], and that it will not adjust the contract price unless the amount of the adjustment exceeds $250 [@CL-CTF-0369; @CL-CTF-2548]; the codified paragraphs say the contract price shall be reduced accordingly [@CL-CTF-2634] and that no adjustment shall be made unless the amount exceeds $250 [@CL-CTF-2548].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A fixed-price supply contract performed in a country concerned carries 30,000 of local taxes in its price. After the contract date a legislative change adds a 2,000 tax that the Contractor must pay. The Contractor states in writing that the price carries no contingency for it and did not incur the liability through fault, negligence, or failure to follow the Contracting Officer's instructions or to comply with paragraph (i). The price increases by 2,000. A separate new tax of 150 does not exceed 250, so no adjustment is made. A 1,000 tax imposed under section 5000C is not included in the price and is not reimbursed, and the price is not increased to offset it.

A later change relieves 800 of tax, so the price decreases by 800. The Contractor also obtains a 1,200 reduction in its United States income tax because of a tax included in the price, so it pays or credits 1,200 to the Government as the Contracting Officer directs. A 500 fee for doing business that the country concerned levies counts as a tax. A 600 tax that the country was imposing and collecting under a written ruling on the contract date is an applicable tax already in the price. A refund of 900 of tax carries 60 of interest earned after the Government paid the Contractor for the tax and a 100 penalty that the Government had paid, so the Government is entitled to the 60 and to repayment of the 100. A tax on net income is an excepted tax, so a rise in it is not an after-imposed tax. At the Contracting Officer's direction the Contractor contests a tax, and the price is equitably adjusted for the 3,000 cost of that action, including interest, penalty and reasonable attorneys' fees.

Under a fixed-price contract with a foreign government, the two governments agree after the contract date that a 4,000 tax in the price does not apply to expenditures by the United States, so the price is reduced by 4,000. A 7,000 tax that the two governments had already agreed shall not apply, but that was included in the price through error, is removed by reducing the price by 7,000; a 5,000 tax that is applicable under the foreign country's laws and was not agreed away stays in the price. An agreement that affects only 100 produces no adjustment because 100 does not exceed 250. On a negotiated contract with a foreign government, the contract date is the effective date of the contract, or of a later modification for that modification.

## Never confuse

The foreign fixed-price clause differs from the general [Federal, State, and Local Taxes clause](ctf-federal-state-local-taxes.md) rather than repeating it, and applies in its place to the extent that the contract provides for furnishing supplies or performing services outside the United States and its outlying areas [@CL-CTF-2539]. For foreign taxes under cost-reimbursement contracts, see [Foreign taxes under cost-reimbursement contracts](ctf-foreign-cost-reimbursement-taxes.md), for the excise tax on certain foreign procurements, see [excise tax on certain foreign procurements](ctf-foreign-procurement-excise-tax.md), and for foreign tax reporting, see [Foreign tax reporting and excise tax exemption](ctf-dfars-foreign-tax-reporting-and-excise-tax-exemption.md).

## Company calibration

Whether each foreign-performance contract carries the foreign fixed-price clause or the clause with a foreign government, and what the two governments have agreed about taxes. [@Q-004]
