## Purpose

Sets out how the foreign fixed-price clause and the fixed-price clause with a foreign government treat taxes in the contract price, which taxes stay out, and how the price changes when a tax changes after the contract date.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CTF-2539] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.229-6 and FAR 52.229-7 [@CL-CTF-2539; @CL-CTF-2546] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CTF-2539] |
| Applies to | Foreign fixed-price clause: supplies furnished or services performed outside the United States and its outlying areas [@CL-CTF-2539]. Clause with a foreign government: Not established. |

Where the contract provides for furnishing supplies or performing services outside the United States and its outlying areas, the foreign fixed-price clause applies in lieu of any Federal, State, and local taxes clause of the contract [@CL-CTF-2539]. Unless the contract provides otherwise, the contract price includes all applicable taxes and duties [@CL-CTF-2540]. The contract price does not include taxes and duties that the Government of the United States and the government of the country concerned have agreed shall not be applicable to expenditures in such country by or on behalf of the United States [@CL-CTF-2620].

Taxes imposed under section 5000C of title 26 of the United States Code may not be included in the contract price and may not be reimbursed [@CL-CTF-2541]. The contract price may not be increased to offset those taxes [@CL-CTF-2542].

In the foreign fixed-price clause, contract date means the date set for bid opening or, if the contract is negotiated or a modification, the effective date of the contract or modification, and country concerned means any country, other than the United States and its outlying areas, in which expenditures under the contract are made [@CL-CTF-2810]. Tax and taxes include fees and charges for doing business that are levied by the government of the country concerned or by its political subdivisions [@CL-CTF-2811]. All applicable taxes and duties means all taxes and duties, in effect on the contract date, that the taxing authority is imposing and collecting on the transactions or property covered by the contract, pursuant to written ruling or regulation in effect on the contract date [@CL-CTF-2812].

The clause for fixed-price contracts with foreign governments states what the price leaves out. The contract price, including the prices in any subcontracts, does not include any tax or duty that the Government of the United States and the foreign government have agreed shall not apply to expenditures made by the United States [@CL-CTF-2546], or any tax or duty not applicable to the contract or any subcontracts under the laws of the foreign country [@CL-CTF-2631]. Taxes imposed under section 5000C of title 26 of the United States Code may not be included in the contract price [@CL-CTF-2632]. The clause names the foreign government and the country in blanks that each contract fills in [@CL-CTF-2546].

## How it works

An after-imposed tax includes any new or increased tax or duty, or tax that was exempted or excluded on the contract date but whose exemption was later revoked or reduced during the contract period, other than an excepted tax [@CL-CTF-2813]. It is a tax on the transactions or property covered by the contract that the Contractor is required to pay or bear as the result of legislative, judicial, or administrative action taking effect after the contract date [@CL-CTF-2814]. An after-relieved tax is any amount of tax or duty, other than an excepted tax, that would otherwise have been payable on the transactions or property covered by the contract [@CL-CTF-2815]. The Contractor is not required to pay or bear it, or obtains a refund of it, as the result of legislative, judicial, or administrative action taking effect after the contract date [@CL-CTF-2816]. Excepted tax means social security or other employment taxes, net income and franchise taxes, excess profits taxes, capital stock taxes, transportation taxes, unemployment compensation taxes, and property taxes [@CL-CTF-2817]. It does not include gross income taxes levied on or measured by sales or receipts from sales, or property taxes assessed on completed supplies covered by the contract [@CL-CTF-2818], or any tax assessed on the Contractor's possession of, interest in, or use of property, title to which is in the United States Government [@CL-CTF-2819].

Under the foreign fixed-price clause, the contract price is increased by the amount of any after-imposed tax, or of any tax or duty specifically excluded from the contract price by a provision of the contract that the Contractor is required to pay or bear, including any interest or penalty [@CL-CTF-2621]. The increase applies if the Contractor states in writing that the contract price does not include any contingency for the tax [@CL-CTF-2622], and if liability for the tax, interest, or penalty was not incurred through the Contractor's fault, negligence, or failure to follow instructions of the Contracting Officer or to comply with the provisions of paragraph (i) [@CL-CTF-2623].

The contract price is decreased by the amount of any after-relieved tax, including any interest or penalty [@CL-CTF-2543]. The Government of the United States is entitled to interest received by the Contractor incident to a refund of taxes to the extent that the interest was earned after the Contractor was paid by the Government of the United States for the taxes [@CL-CTF-2820], and to repayment of any penalty refunded to the Contractor to the extent that the penalty was paid by the Government [@CL-CTF-2821]. The contract price is also decreased by the amount of any tax or duty, other than an excepted tax, that was included in the contract and that the Contractor is required to pay or bear, or does not obtain a refund of [@CL-CTF-2624], through the Contractor's fault, negligence, or failure to follow instructions of the Contracting Officer or to comply with the provisions of paragraph (i) [@CL-CTF-2625]. No adjustment is made in the contract price under the foreign fixed-price clause unless the amount of the adjustment exceeds $250 [@CL-CTF-2544].

If the Contractor obtains a reduction in tax liability under the United States Internal Revenue Code because of the payment of a tax or duty that either was included in the contract price or was the basis of an increase in the contract price [@CL-CTF-2626], the amount of the reduction is paid or credited to the Government of the United States as the Contracting Officer directs [@CL-CTF-2627].

The Contractor takes all reasonable action to obtain exemption from or refund of any taxes or duties, including interest or penalty [@CL-CTF-2545]. The taxes and duties covered include those from which the United States Government, the Contractor, any subcontractor, or the transactions or property covered by the contract are exempt under the laws of the country concerned or its political subdivisions [@CL-CTF-2628]. They also include those which the governments of the United States and of the country concerned have agreed shall not be applicable to expenditures in such country by or on behalf of the United States [@CL-CTF-2629].

The Contractor promptly notifies the Contracting Officer of all matters relating to taxes or duties that reasonably may be expected to result in an increase or decrease in the contract price, and takes appropriate action as the Contracting Officer directs [@CL-CTF-2630]. The contract price is equitably adjusted to cover the costs of action taken by the Contractor at the direction of the Contracting Officer, including any interest, penalty, and reasonable attorneys' fees [@CL-CTF-2822].

In the clause for fixed-price contracts with foreign governments, the contract date is the date set for bid opening or, if the contract is negotiated or a modification, the effective date of the contract or modification [@CL-CTF-2823]. Under that clause, a tax or duty included in the contract price through error or otherwise is removed by a corresponding reduction of the price [@CL-CTF-2547]. If, after the contract date, the Government of the United States and the foreign government agree that a tax or duty included in the contract price shall not apply to expenditures by the United States in the country [@CL-CTF-2633], the contract price is reduced accordingly [@CL-CTF-2634]. No adjustment is made in the contract price under that clause unless the amount of the adjustment exceeds $250 [@CL-CTF-2548].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A fixed-price supply contract performed in a country concerned carries 30,000 of local taxes in its price. After the contract date a legislative change adds a 2,000 tax that the Contractor must pay. The Contractor states in writing that the price carries no contingency for it and did not incur the liability through fault, negligence, or failure to follow the Contracting Officer's instructions or to comply with paragraph (i). The price increases by 2,000. A separate new tax of 150 does not exceed 250, so no adjustment is made. A 1,000 tax imposed under section 5000C is not included in the price and is not reimbursed, and the price is not increased to offset it.

A later change relieves 800 of tax, so the price decreases by 800. The Contractor also obtains a 1,200 reduction in its United States income tax because of a tax included in the price, so it pays or credits 1,200 to the Government as the Contracting Officer directs. A 500 fee for doing business that the country concerned levies counts as a tax. A 600 tax that the country was imposing and collecting under a written ruling on the contract date is an applicable tax already in the price. A refund of 900 of tax carries 60 of interest earned after the Government paid the Contractor for the tax and a 100 penalty that the Government had paid, so the Government is entitled to the 60 and to repayment of the 100. A tax on net income is an excepted tax, so a rise in it is not an after-imposed tax. At the Contracting Officer's direction the Contractor contests a tax, and the price is equitably adjusted for the 3,000 cost of that action, including interest, penalty and reasonable attorneys' fees.

Under a fixed-price contract with a foreign government, the two governments agree after the contract date that a 4,000 tax in the price does not apply to expenditures by the United States, so the price is reduced by 4,000. A 7,000 tax included through error is removed by reducing the price by 7,000. An agreement that affects only 100 produces no adjustment because 100 does not exceed 250. On a negotiated contract with a foreign government, the contract date is the effective date of the contract, or of a later modification for that modification.

## Never confuse

The foreign fixed-price clause is about what the price includes and how the price moves, while the foreign cost-reimbursement clauses ask whether a tax is an allowable cost at all. See [Foreign taxes under cost-reimbursement contracts](ctf-foreign-cost-reimbursement-taxes.md).

The foreign fixed-price clause displaces the general [Federal, State, and Local Taxes clause](ctf-federal-state-local-taxes.md) where the work is performed outside the United States and its outlying areas [@CL-CTF-2539]. The excise tax on payments to foreign persons is a separate withholding that is described on the [excise tax on certain foreign procurements](ctf-foreign-procurement-excise-tax.md) card. A related view of foreign tax reporting sits at [Foreign tax reporting and excise tax exemption](ctf-dfars-foreign-tax-reporting-and-excise-tax-exemption.md).
