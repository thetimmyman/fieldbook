## Purpose

Tells what each of the three progress payment notices in a solicitation says to offerors and bidders, and when each is inserted.

The Notice of Progress Payments provision is inserted in invitations for bids and requests for proposals that include a Progress Payments clause [@CL-CTF-2148]. It states that the need for customary progress payments conforming to the regulations in subpart 32.5 of the Federal Acquisition Regulation (FAR) will not be considered as a handicap or adverse factor in the award of the contract [@CL-CTF-2027]. It also states that, even though the clause is included in the contract, the clause shall be inoperative during any time the contractor's accounting system and controls are determined by the Government to be inadequate for segregation and accumulation of contract costs [@CL-CTF-2028].

## How it works

The Notice of Availability of Progress Payments Exclusively for Small Business Concerns provision is inserted in invitations for bids if it is anticipated that both small business concerns and others may submit bids in response to the same invitation and only the small business bidders would need progress payments [@CL-CTF-2149]. It states that the Progress Payments clause will be available only to small business concerns and that any bid conditioned upon inclusion of a progress payment clause in the resulting contract will be rejected as nonresponsive if the bidder is not a small business concern [@CL-CTF-2029].

The Progress Payments Not Included provision is inserted in invitations for bids if the solicitation will not contain one of the provisions prescribed in paragraphs (a) and (b) of FAR 32.502-3 [@CL-CTF-2150]. It states that a progress payments clause is not included in the solicitation and will not be added to the resulting contract at the time of award, and that bids conditioned upon inclusion of a progress payment clause in the resulting contract will be rejected as nonresponsive [@CL-CTF-2030].

DoD contracting officers must use the revised FAR part 52 published on the Revolutionary FAR Overhaul web page in place of the FAR part 52 text codified at 48 CFR chapter 1, effective February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760; @CL-CMP-9762]. Under RFO FAR 52.232-13, the need for customary progress payments conforming to the regulations in subpart 32.5 of the FAR will not be considered as a handicap or adverse factor in the award of the contract [@CL-CTF-0586], and even though the clause is included in the contract, the clause shall be inoperative during any time the contractor's accounting system and controls are determined by the Government to be inadequate for segregation and accumulation of contract costs [@CL-CTF-0587], matching the codified provision [@CL-CTF-2027; @CL-CTF-2028]. Under RFO FAR 52.232-14, the Progress Payments clause will be available only to small business concerns and any bid conditioned upon inclusion of a progress payment clause in the resulting contract will be rejected as nonresponsive if the bidder is not a small business concern [@CL-CTF-0589], matching the codified provision [@CL-CTF-2029]. Under RFO FAR 52.232-15, a progress payments clause is not included in the solicitation and will not be added to the resulting contract at the time of award, and bids conditioned upon inclusion of a progress payment clause in the resulting contract will be rejected as nonresponsive [@CL-CTF-0591], matching the codified provision [@CL-CTF-2030]. RFO FAR 52.232-13 is prescribed in FAR 32.502-3(a) [@CL-CTF-0588], whereas the codified prescription names invitations for bids and requests for proposals that include a Progress Payments clause [@CL-CTF-2148]; RFO FAR 52.232-14 is prescribed in FAR 32.502-3(b)(2) [@CL-CTF-0590], whereas the codified prescription names invitations for bids if it is anticipated that both small business concerns and others may submit bids in response to the same invitation and only the small business bidders would need progress payments [@CL-CTF-2149]; and RFO FAR 52.232-15 is prescribed in FAR 32.502-3(c) [@CL-CTF-0592], whereas the codified prescription names invitations for bids if the solicitation will not contain one of the provisions prescribed in FAR 32.502-3(a) and (b) [@CL-CTF-2150].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An agency issues three invitations for bids. The first includes a Progress Payments clause, so it carries the first notice. A bidder there is told that its need for progress payments is no handicap, and that the clause stops operating while its accounting system is judged inadequate. The second is expected to draw both small and large bidders, and only the small ones need progress payments, so it carries the small business notice. A large business that conditions its bid on a progress payment clause is rejected as nonresponsive. The third has no progress payments clause and neither of the other notices, so it carries the not-included notice, and any bid conditioned on such a clause is rejected.

## Never confuse

The codified prescription lines state that FAR 52.232-13 is inserted in invitations for bids and requests for proposals that include a Progress Payments clause [@CL-CTF-2148], FAR 52.232-14 is inserted in invitations for bids if it is anticipated that both small business concerns and others may submit bids in response to the same invitation and only the small business bidders would need progress payments [@CL-CTF-2149], and FAR 52.232-15 is inserted in invitations for bids if the solicitation will not contain one of the provisions prescribed in FAR 32.502-3(a) and (b) [@CL-CTF-2150], whereas the RFO prescription lines direct insertion as prescribed in FAR 32.502-3(a), FAR 32.502-3(b)(2), and FAR 32.502-3(c) [@CL-CTF-0588; @CL-CTF-0590; @CL-CTF-0592].
