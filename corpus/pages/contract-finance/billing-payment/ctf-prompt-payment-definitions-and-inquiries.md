## Purpose

Gives the prompt payment definitions that set payment dates, and shows where a contractor takes an inquiry about a late, short or disputed payment.

Subpart 32.9 prescribes policies, procedures, and clauses for implementing Office of Management and Budget (OMB) prompt payment regulations at 5 CFR part 1315 [@CL-CTF-9305]. The subpart applies to invoice payments on all contracts, except contracts with payment terms and late payment penalties established by other governmental authority (e.g., tariffs) [@CL-CTF-3745]. It does not apply to contract financing payments (see definition at FAR 32.001) [@CL-CTF-3746].

As used in subpart 32.9, discount for prompt payment means an invoice payment reduction offered by the contractor for payment prior to the due date [@CL-CTF-9632; @CL-CTF-3584]. Mixed invoice means an invoice that contains items with different payment due dates [@CL-CTF-3585]. Payment date means the date on which a check for payment is dated or, for an electronic funds transfer (EFT), the settlement date [@CL-CTF-3586]. Settlement date, as it applies to electronic funds transfer, means the date on which an electronic funds transfer payment is credited to the contractor's financial institution [@CL-CTF-3587].

Under FAR 2.101, Electronic Funds Transfer (EFT) means any transfer of funds, other than a transaction originated by cash, check, or similar paper instrument, that is initiated through an electronic terminal, telephone, computer, or magnetic tape, for the purpose of ordering, instructing, or authorizing a financial institution to debit or credit an account [@CL-CTF-9407; @CL-CTF-9408]. The term includes Automated Clearing House transfers, Fedwire transfers, and transfers made at automatic teller machines and point-of-sale terminals [@CL-CTF-3209], and for purposes of compliance with 31 U.S.C. 3332 and implementing regulations at 31 CFR part 208, the term electronic funds transfer includes a Governmentwide commercial purchase card transaction [@CL-CTF-9462].

## How it works

Under FAR 32.909(a), questions involving delinquent payments are directed to the designated billing office or designated payment office [@CL-CTF-3588]. Questions involving disagreements in payment amount or timing are directed to the contracting officer for resolution, and the contracting officer must coordinate within appropriate contracting channels and seek the advice of other offices as necessary to resolve disagreements [@CL-CTF-3589]. Small business concerns may contact the agency's local small business specialist or representative from the Office of Small and Disadvantaged Business Utilization to obtain additional assistance related to payment issues, late payment interest penalties, and information on the Prompt Payment Act [@CL-CTF-3590]. For due dates, see [Prompt payment: determining invoice payment due dates](ctf-prompt-payment-due-dates.md), and for interest penalties, see [Prompt payment interest and clauses](ctf-prompt-payment-interest-and-clauses.md).

Effective February 1, 2026, DoD contracting officers must use the revised FAR part 32 published on the Revolutionary FAR Overhaul web page in place of the FAR part 32 text codified at 48 CFR chapter 1 [@CL-CTF-4046]. Under RFO FAR 32.900, the subpart prescribes policies, procedures, and clauses for implementing Office of Management and Budget (OMB) prompt payment regulations at 5 CFR part 1315 [@CL-CTF-3041], matching the codified section [@CL-CTF-9305]. Under RFO FAR 32.901, the subpart applies to invoice payments on all contracts, except contracts with payment terms and late payment penalties established by other governmental authority (e.g., tariffs), and does not apply to contract financing payments (see definition at 32.001) [@CL-CTF-3042], matching the codified section [@CL-CTF-3745; @CL-CTF-3746]. RFO FAR 32.902 defines discount for prompt payment [@CL-CTF-4055], mixed invoice [@CL-CTF-4056], payment date [@CL-CTF-4057], and settlement date [@CL-CTF-4058], matching the codified definitions [@CL-CTF-3584; @CL-CTF-3585; @CL-CTF-3586; @CL-CTF-3587]. Under RFO FAR 2.101, Electronic Funds Transfer (EFT) means any transfer of funds, other than a transaction originated by cash, check, or similar paper instrument, that is initiated through an electronic terminal, telephone, computer, or magnetic tape, for the purpose of ordering, instructing, or authorizing a financial institution to debit or credit an account [@CL-CTF-9464; @CL-CTF-9465], the term includes Automated Clearing House transfers, Fedwire transfers, and transfers made at automatic teller machines and point-of-sale terminals [@CL-CTF-9486], and for purposes of compliance with 31 U.S.C. 3332 and implementing regulations at 31 CFR part 208, the term electronic funds transfer includes a Governmentwide commercial purchase card transaction [@CL-CTF-9487], matching the codified definition [@CL-CTF-9407; @CL-CTF-9408; @CL-CTF-3209; @CL-CTF-9462]. Under RFO FAR 32.909, questions involving delinquent payments are directed to the designated billing office or designated payment office [@CL-CTF-4059], questions involving disagreements in payment amount or timing are directed to the contracting officer for resolution, who must coordinate within appropriate contracting channels and seek the advice of other offices as necessary to resolve disagreements [@CL-CTF-4060], and small business concerns may contact the agency's local small business specialist or representative from the Office of Small and Disadvantaged Business Utilization to obtain additional assistance related to payment issues, late payment interest penalties, and information on the Prompt Payment Act [@CL-CTF-4061], matching the codified section [@CL-CTF-3588; @CL-CTF-3589; @CL-CTF-3590].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor sends one invoice covering two line items with different due dates, so it is a mixed invoice. For one item the agency pays by check dated on the 10th of the month, and the contractor's bank receives it on the 14th. The payment date is the 10th, the date on the check. For the other item the agency pays by electronic funds transfer that is credited to the contractor's bank on the 14th, so the settlement date and the payment date are both the 14th. When the contractor does not receive a payment, it directs the question about the delinquent payment to the designated billing office or the designated payment office. Later the contractor and the agency disagree on the amount paid, so the contractor directs the disagreement to the contracting officer, who coordinates to resolve it. If the contractor is a small business concern, it may also ask the local small business specialist for additional help.

## Never confuse

Payment date means the date on which a check for payment is dated or, for an electronic funds transfer (EFT), the settlement date [@CL-CTF-3586], and settlement date, as it applies to electronic funds transfer, means the date on which an electronic funds transfer payment is credited to the contractor's financial institution [@CL-CTF-3587]. Questions involving delinquent payments are directed to the designated billing office or designated payment office [@CL-CTF-3588], whereas disagreements in payment amount or timing are directed to the contracting officer for resolution [@CL-CTF-3589]. Discount for prompt payment means an invoice payment reduction offered by the contractor for payment prior to the due date [@CL-CTF-3584]. For due dates, see [Prompt payment: determining invoice payment due dates](ctf-prompt-payment-due-dates.md).

## Company calibration

Which office the company contacts about a late payment, who owns a disagreement on amount or timing before it reaches the contracting officer, and whether the company qualifies for the small business route. [@Q-009]
