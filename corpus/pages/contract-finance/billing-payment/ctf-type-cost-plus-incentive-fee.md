## Purpose

A cost-plus-incentive-fee contract reimburses allowable incurred costs and adjusts an initially negotiated fee by formula based on cost performance against a target. The fee moves with cost performance inside a range set by minimum and maximum fees.

## The rule

A cost-plus-incentive-fee contract is a cost-reimbursement contract providing for payment of allowable incurred costs to the extent prescribed in the contract [@CL-CLS-0567] and an initially negotiated fee that is adjusted later by a formula based on the relationship of total allowable costs to total target costs [@CL-EST-1090]. The contract specifies a target cost, a target fee, minimum and maximum fees, and a fee adjustment formula [@CL-EST-1091].

Within limits, the formula provides for fee above the target fee when total allowable costs are less than target costs, and fee below the target fee when total allowable costs exceed target costs [@CL-EST-1092]. Meeting target cost results in target fee, while costs exceeding or falling below target adjust fee downward or upward [@CL-EST-1071]. When total allowable cost is outside the range within which the fee-adjustment formula operates, the contractor is paid total allowable costs plus the minimum or maximum fee [@CL-EST-1093].

## How it works

The two basic categories of incentive contracts are fixed-price incentive contracts and cost-reimbursement incentive contracts [@CL-EST-1053]. A determination and finding signed by the head of the contracting activity must be completed for all incentive contracts, justifying that the use of this contract type is in the best interest of the Government [@CL-EST-1055]. A cost-plus-incentive-fee contract is appropriate for services or development and test programs when a cost-reimbursement contract is necessary and a target cost and fee adjustment formula can be negotiated that are likely to motivate the contractor to manage effectively [@CL-EST-1094].

Except for award-fee contracts, incentive contracts include a target cost, a target fee, and a fee adjustment formula that operates within minimum and maximum fee limits [@CL-EST-1070], and most include only cost incentives intended to motivate the contractor to manage costs effectively [@CL-EST-1068]. If a high maximum fee is negotiated, the contract must also provide for a low minimum fee that may be a zero fee or, in rare cases, a negative fee [@CL-EST-1095]. The contracting officer shall insert the clause at 52.216-10, Incentive Fee, in solicitations and contracts when a cost-plus-incentive-fee contract is contemplated [@CL-CTF-1245]. See [Cost-plus-incentive-fee contracts: fee adjustment, minimum fee and maximum fee](../../estimating/pricing/est-cpif-fee-adjustment.md), [Incentive Fee clause](ctf-incentive-fee.md) and [Incentive contracts: when they fit and how they are structured](../../estimating/pricing/est-incentive-contracts-general.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contract sets a target cost of 1,000,000, a target fee of 80,000, a minimum fee of 40,000, a maximum fee of 120,000, and an invented formula where the contractor receives an invented 20 percent of any cost savings below target as added fee and gives up 20 percent of any cost overrun above target. If actual allowable cost is 900,000, the cost underrun is 100,000; the contractor earns 20 percent of that saving, or 20,000, bringing fee to 80,000 plus 20,000, which equals 100,000, and total payment to 900,000 plus 100,000, which equals 1,000,000. If actual allowable cost is 1,100,000, the cost overrun is 100,000; fee decreases by 20 percent of that overrun, or 20,000, leaving a fee of 80,000 minus 20,000, which equals 60,000, and total payment of 1,100,000 plus 60,000, which equals 1,160,000. If actual allowable cost is 1,500,000, the 500,000 overrun would reduce fee by 20 percent, or 100,000, yielding a negative calculated fee of 80,000 minus 100,000; because this falls below the floor, the minimum fee of 40,000 applies and total payment is 1,500,000 plus 40,000, which equals 1,540,000. The 20 percent share rate is invented; this example is illustrative only and the contract itself governs.

## Never confuse

A cost-plus-incentive-fee contract differs from a cost-plus-fixed-fee contract, where the negotiated fee does not vary with actual cost [@CL-CLS-0576], and from a cost-plus-award-fee contract, where the fee includes an award amount based on a judgmental evaluation by the Government [@CL-EST-1064]. It also differs from a fixed-price incentive firm target contract, which establishes a price ceiling where the contractor absorbs any cost exceeding the ceiling as a loss [@CL-EST-1076; @CL-EST-1105]; under cost-plus-incentive-fee, when allowable cost is outside the formula range, the contractor is paid allowable costs plus the minimum or maximum fee [@CL-EST-1093].
