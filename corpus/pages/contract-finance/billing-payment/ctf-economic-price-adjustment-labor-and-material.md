## Purpose

Shows when a contract's unit prices adjust for changes in scheduled labor rates and material prices, and the limits on those adjustments.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CTF-0301] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.216-4 [@CL-CTF-0301] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.216-4 [@CL-CTF-0189] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CTF-0301] |
| Applies to | Contracts with the Economic Price Adjustment—Labor and Material clause [@CL-CTF-0301] |

The Contractor must notify the Contracting Officer of any increase or decrease in the scheduled labor rates or material unit prices [@CL-CTF-0302]. The notice must be furnished within 60 days after the increase or decrease, or within any additional period the Contracting Officer approves in writing, but not later than the date of final payment [@CL-CTF-0303]. The parties then negotiate a price adjustment in the contract unit prices and its effective date [@CL-CTF-0304], although the Contracting Officer may postpone the negotiations until an accumulation of increases and decreases in the scheduled labor rates and material unit prices results in an adjustment allowable under subparagraph (c)(3) of the clause [@CL-CTF-9494].

Any adjustment is limited to the effect on unit prices of the scheduled changes [@CL-CTF-0700]. There is no adjustment for a change that would not result in a net change of at least 3 percent of the then-current total contract price [@CL-CTF-0306]. That limitation does not apply if, after final delivery of all line items, either party requests an adjustment [@CL-CTF-0307]. The aggregate of increases in any contract unit price will not exceed 10 percent of the original unit price, and there is no percentage limitation on decreases [@CL-CTF-0308].

## How it works

The Contractor watches the scheduled labor rates and material unit prices during performance and reports any change to the Contracting Officer [@CL-CTF-0302]. The Contracting Officer and the Contractor negotiate the adjustment and its effective date [@CL-CTF-0304], although the Contracting Officer may postpone the negotiations until an accumulation of increases and decreases in the scheduled rates and prices results in an adjustment allowable under subparagraph (c)(3) of the clause [@CL-CTF-9494], and the Contractor keeps performing while the adjustment is open [@CL-CTF-0305]. The adjustment is limited to the effect on unit prices of the scheduled changes, and a change that nets less than 3 percent of the then-current total contract price produces no adjustment, subject to the request after final delivery [@CL-CTF-0700; @CL-CTF-0306; @CL-CTF-0307]. Increases in any one unit price are capped at 10 percent of the original unit price, while decreases are not capped [@CL-CTF-0308]. The Contracting Officer may examine the supporting cost records until the end of 3 years after final payment, or the periods in FAR subpart 4.7, whichever is earlier [@CL-CTF-0309; @CL-CTF-0310].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text from February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. The contractor notifies the contracting officer if the scheduled labor rates or material unit prices increase or decrease during contract performance [@CL-CTF-0189] [@CL-CTF-0302], within 60 days after the change or any additional period the contracting officer approves in writing, but not later than the date of final payment [@CL-CTF-0190] [@CL-CTF-0303]. The parties negotiate a price adjustment in the contract unit prices and its effective date [@CL-CTF-0191] [@CL-CTF-0304], although the contracting officer may postpone the negotiations until an accumulation of increases and decreases in the scheduled labor rates and material unit prices results in an adjustment allowable under paragraph (c)(3) of the clause [@CL-CTF-9025] [@CL-CTF-9494], and the contractor continues performance pending agreement or determination [@CL-CTF-0192] [@CL-CTF-0305].

The limits are the same: any adjustment is limited to the effect on unit prices of the scheduled changes [@CL-CTF-0193] [@CL-CTF-0700], and there is no adjustment for the three listed cases [@CL-CTF-0194] [@CL-CTF-0701]. There is no adjustment for a change that would not result in a net change of at least 3 percent of the then-current total contract price [@CL-CTF-0195] [@CL-CTF-0306], a limit that does not apply if, after final delivery of all line items, either party requests an adjustment [@CL-CTF-0196] [@CL-CTF-0307]. The aggregate of increases in any contract unit price may not exceed 10 percent of the original unit price, with no percentage limit on decreases [@CL-CTF-0197] [@CL-CTF-0308]. The contracting officer may examine the supporting records during all reasonable times [@CL-CTF-0198] [@CL-CTF-0309], until the end of 3 years after final payment or the periods in FAR subpart 4.7, whichever is earlier [@CL-CTF-0199] [@CL-CTF-0310].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

Labor rates shown in the Schedule rise during performance. The effect on the unit prices is a net change of 5 percent of the then-current total contract price, so it clears the 3 percent floor. The Contractor notifies the Contracting Officer within 60 days, and the parties negotiate an adjustment to the unit prices and its effective date. A unit price that started at 100 can rise by at most 10 in total across all adjustments. Had the net change been 2 percent, no adjustment would apply unless, after final delivery of all line items, either party requested one.

## Never confuse

An economic price adjustment is not a general renegotiation of the contract. The adjustment is limited to the effect on unit prices of changes in the scheduled labor rates and material unit prices [@CL-CTF-0700]. There is no adjustment for supplies or services whose production cost is not affected, for changes in rates or unit prices other than those shown in the Schedule, or for changes in the quantities of labor or material used [@CL-CTF-0701].
