## Purpose

Says when a contractor pays the Government under the dismantling and demolition payment clauses, both the lump-sum and the incremental version, and whether the source sets interest on late payment.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CTF-1698; @CL-CTF-1699] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.237-5 [@CL-CTF-1698]; FAR 52.237-6 [@CL-CTF-1699] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CTF-1698; @CL-CTF-1699] |
| Applies to | Contracts with the clause, in the cases described in the prescriptions for the payment clause and the incremental payment clause [@CL-CTF-1697; @CL-CTF-1724] |

Under the payment clause, the contractor shall perform the work called for under the contract and, within the number of days of receipt of notice of award stated in the clause unless otherwise provided in the Schedule and before proceeding with the work, shall pay the amount stated in the clause [@CL-CTF-1698]. The days limit attaches to the payment, not to performing the work. The prescription for the payment clause refers to the case where the contractor is to receive title to dismantled or demolished property and a net amount of compensation is due to the Government, except if the contracting officer determines that it would be advantageous to the Government for the contractor to pay in increments [@CL-CTF-1697]. The prescription for the incremental payment clause names, as one condition, that the contracting officer determines that it would be advantageous to the Government for the contractor to pay in increments, and for the Government to transfer title to the contractor for increments of property, only upon receipt of those payments [@CL-CTF-1724].

Under the incremental clause, after the initial payment the contractor shall make payment to the Government in the amount and frequency specified in the Schedule [@CL-CTF-1699]. The days and the amount are blanks in the clause text, so the source does not establish a number of days or an amount. The source also does not establish interest on late payment under either clause.

## How it works

The contractor pays the stated amount within the days after receipt of notice of award that the clause states and before proceeding with the work [@CL-CTF-1698]. Under the incremental version, the initial payment is followed by payments in the amount and frequency in the Schedule [@CL-CTF-1699]. The prescription for the payment clause excepts the case where the contracting officer determines that payment in increments would be advantageous to the Government [@CL-CTF-1697].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A demolition contract leaves a net amount of 90,000 due to the Government. Under the payment clause, the contractor pays 90,000 within the stated number of days after notice of award and before starting work. If the incremental clause is used instead, the Schedule might call for 30,000 at the start and 30,000 in each of the next two months.

## Never confuse

The payment clause and the incremental clause are alternatives, and the choice turns on whether the contracting officer determines that payment in increments would be advantageous to the Government [@CL-CTF-1697; @CL-CTF-1724]. These two clauses, as printed, state no interest of their own on late payment. Interest on amounts that become payable by the contractor to the Government under a contract that contains the interest clause is on [Interest on amounts due the Government and the assignment of claims clause](ctf-interest-and-assignment-of-claims-clauses.md) [@CL-CTF-1671].
