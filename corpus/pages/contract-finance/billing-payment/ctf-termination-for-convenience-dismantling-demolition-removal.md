## Purpose

Shows what a contractor must do after a termination notice on a dismantling, demolition or removal contract, how the settlement amount is bounded and adjusted, what is deducted, how partial payments and partial terminations work, how long records are kept, and who holds title to the salvage.

## The rule

|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CTF-2058] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.249-3 [@CL-CTF-2058] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.249-3 [@CL-CTF-0696; @CL-CTF-0713] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CTF-2058] |
| Applies to | Solicitations and contracts for dismantling, demolition or removal of improvements [@CL-CLS-1017], when a fixed-price contract is contemplated and the contract amount is expected to exceed the simplified acquisition threshold [@CL-CLS-1018] |

The Government may terminate performance of work under the contract, in whole or, from time to time, in part if the contracting officer determines that a termination is in the Government's interest [@CL-CTF-2058]. The contracting officer shall terminate by delivering to the contractor a Notice of Termination specifying the extent of termination and the effective date [@CL-CTF-2059]. Upon receipt of the notice, if title to property is vested in the contractor under the contract, it shall revest in the Government regardless of any other clause of the contract, except for property that the contractor disposed of by bona fide sale or removed from the site [@CL-CTF-2060].

Subject to paragraph (e) of the clause, the contractor and the contracting officer may agree upon the whole or any part of the amount to be paid because of the termination, and the amount may include a reasonable allowance for profit on work done [@CL-CTF-2065]. However, the agreed amount, whether under paragraph (f) or paragraph (g) of the clause, exclusive of settlement costs, may not exceed the total contract price as reduced by (1) the amount of payments previously made and (2) the contract price of work not terminated [@CL-CTF-2066]. If the contractor and the contracting officer fail to agree on the whole amount to be paid because of the termination of work, the contracting officer shall pay the contractor the amounts determined by the contracting officer, without duplication of any amounts agreed on under paragraph (f) of the clause [@CL-CTF-2067; @CL-CTF-2211], which for contract work performed before the effective date of termination is the total (without duplication of any items) of (i) the cost of this work and (ii) the cost of settling and paying termination settlement proposals under terminated subcontracts that are properly chargeable to the terminated portion of the contract, if not included in subdivision (g)(1)(i) of the clause [@CL-CTF-2068; @CL-CTF-1480].

For that work the determination also includes (iii) a sum, as profit on subdivision (g)(1)(i) of the clause, determined by the contracting officer under section 49.202 of the Federal Acquisition Regulation, in effect on the date of the contract, to be fair and reasonable [@CL-CTF-2112]. However, if it appears that the contractor would have sustained a loss on the entire contract had it been completed, the contracting officer shall allow no profit under subdivision (iii) and shall reduce the amount of the settlement to reflect the indicated rate of loss [@CL-CTF-2113]. The profit and loss rules are also on [Termination settlements: profit allowance and adjustment for loss](../../close-revrec/revenue/cls-termination-settlements-profit-and-loss.md).

## How it works

After receipt of a Notice of Termination, and except as directed by the contracting officer, the contractor shall immediately proceed with its listed obligations, regardless of delay in determining or adjusting any amounts due under the clause, beginning with stopping work as specified in the notice [@CL-CTF-2061]. As directed by the contracting officer, the contractor shall transfer title and deliver to the Government (i) the fabricated or unfabricated parts, work in process, completed work, supplies, and other material produced or acquired for the work terminated [@CL-CTF-2062], and (ii) the completed or partially completed plans, drawings, information, and other property that, if the contract has been completed, would be required to be furnished to the Government [@CL-CTF-9577]. The contractor shall also use its best efforts to sell, as directed or authorized by the contracting officer, any property of the types referred to in subparagraph (b)(6) of the clause, provided that the contractor (i) is not required to extend credit to any purchaser and (ii) may acquire the property under the conditions prescribed by, and at prices approved by, the contracting officer [@CL-CTF-2107; @CL-CTF-2212]. The proceeds of any transfer or disposition will be applied to reduce any payments to be made by the Government under the contract, credited to the price or cost of the work, or paid in any other manner directed by the contracting officer [@CL-CTF-1481]. Inventory handling is on [Termination inventory: disposal schedules and government property accounting](../../close-revrec/inventory-wip/cls-termination-inventory-schedules-and-property-accounting.md).

The contractor shall submit complete termination inventory schedules no later than 120 days from the effective date of termination, unless extended in writing by the contracting officer upon written request of the contractor within this 120-day period [@CL-CTF-2063]. The contractor shall submit the final termination settlement proposal promptly, but no later than 1 year from the effective date of termination, unless extended in writing by the contracting officer upon written request of the contractor within this 1-year period [@CL-CTF-2064]. However, if the contracting officer determines that the facts justify it, a termination settlement proposal may be received and acted on after 1 year or any extension [@CL-CTF-2231]. If the contractor fails to submit the proposal within the time allowed, the contracting officer may determine, on the basis of information available, the amount, if any, due the contractor because of the termination and shall pay the amount determined [@CL-CTF-2232]. The contractor shall have the right of appeal, under the Disputes clause, from any determination made by the contracting officer under paragraph (e), (g), or (l) of the clause [@CL-CTF-9578], except that if the contractor failed to submit the termination settlement proposal within the time provided in paragraph (e) or (f) and failed to request a time extension, there is no right of appeal [@CL-CTF-2233].

In arriving at the amount due the contractor under the clause, there shall be deducted (1) all unliquidated advance or other payments to the contractor under the terminated portion of the contract, (2) any claim which the Government has against the contractor under the contract, and (3) the agreed price for, or the proceeds of sale of, materials, supplies, or other things acquired by the contractor or sold under the provisions of the clause and not recovered by or credited to the Government [@CL-CTF-2114; @CL-CTF-1482]. The deduction and limitation rules are also on [Termination settlements: deductions, completed end items, and settlement limitation](../../close-revrec/revenue/cls-termination-settlement-deductions-and-limitations.md).

If the termination is partial, the contractor may file a proposal with the contracting officer for an equitable adjustment of the price or prices of the continued portion of the contract [@CL-CTF-2115]. The contracting officer shall make any equitable adjustment agreed upon [@CL-CTF-1486]. Any proposal by the contractor for an equitable adjustment under the clause shall be requested within 90 days from the effective date of termination unless extended in writing by the contracting officer [@CL-CTF-2116]. See [Partial termination: equitable adjustment and partial settlements](../../close-revrec/revenue/cls-partial-termination-equitable-adjustment-and-settlements.md).

Under FAR 52.249-3(m)(1), the Government may, under the terms and conditions it prescribes, make partial payments and payments against cost incurred by the contractor for the terminated portion of the contract, if the contracting officer believes the total of these payments will not exceed the amount to which the contractor will be entitled [@CL-CTF-1487; @CL-CTF-2117]. If the total payments exceed the amount finally determined to be due, the contractor shall repay the excess to the Government upon demand, together with interest computed at the rate established by the Secretary of the Treasury under 50 USC App 1215(b)(2) [@CL-CTF-2118]. Interest shall be computed for the period from the date the excess payment is received by the contractor to the date the excess is repaid [@CL-CTF-2234]. Interest shall not be charged on any excess payment due to a reduction in the contractor's termination settlement proposal because of retention or other disposition of termination inventory until 10 days after the date of the retention or disposition [@CL-CTF-2235], or a later date determined by the contracting officer because of the circumstances [@CL-CTF-2256]. Under Alternate I, if the contract is with an agency of the United States Government or with State, local, or foreign governments or their agencies, and if the contracting officer determines that the requirement to pay interest on excess partial payments is inappropriate, subparagraph (m)(2) is deleted from the basic clause [@CL-CTF-2236]. See [Partial payments and final payment under a terminated contract](../../close-revrec/revenue/cls-termination-partial-payments-and-final-payment.md).

Unless otherwise provided in the contract or by statute, the contractor shall maintain all records and documents relating to the terminated portion of the contract for 3 years after final settlement [@CL-CTF-2119]. Retention policy is on [Contractor records retention policy](../../compliance/audit-retention/cmp-contractor-records-retention-policy.md).

Effective February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9762], DoD contracting officers shall use the revised FAR part 52 solicitation provisions and contract clauses published on the Revolutionary FAR Overhaul web page in lieu of the text codified at 48 CFR chapter 1 [@CL-CMP-9760]. Under RFO FAR 52.249-3(a)-(c) and (e), the Government may terminate performance of work under the contract, in whole or, from time to time, in part if the contracting officer determines that a termination is in the Government's interest [@CL-CTF-0696], upon receipt of the notice, if title to property is vested in the contractor under the contract, it shall revest in the Government regardless of any other clause of the contract, except for property that the contractor disposed of by bona fide sale or removed from the site [@CL-CTF-0697], after receipt of a Notice of Termination, and except as directed by the contracting officer, the contractor shall immediately proceed with its listed obligations, regardless of delay in determining or adjusting any amounts due under the clause, beginning with stopping work as specified in the notice [@CL-CTF-0698], the contractor shall submit complete termination inventory schedules no later than 120 days from the effective date of termination, unless extended in writing by the contracting officer upon written request of the contractor within this 120-day period [@CL-CTF-0699], and the contractor shall submit the proposal promptly, but no later than 1 year from the effective date of termination, unless extended in writing by the contracting officer upon written request of the contractor within this 1-year period [@CL-CTF-0705], except that if the contracting officer determines that the facts justify it, a termination settlement proposal may be received and acted on after 1 year or any extension [@CL-CTF-9504]. Under RFO FAR 52.249-3(f), (g)(1)(iii), (j), (l), (m), (n), and Alternate I, the agreed amount, whether under paragraph (f) or paragraph (g) of the clause, exclusive of settlement costs, may not exceed the total contract price as reduced by (1) the amount of payments previously made and (2) the contract price of work not terminated [@CL-CTF-0706], if it appears that the contractor would have sustained a loss on the entire contract had it been completed, the contracting officer shall allow no profit under subdivision (iii) and shall reduce the amount of the settlement to reflect the indicated rate of loss [@CL-CTF-0707], if the contractor failed to submit the termination settlement proposal within the time provided in paragraph (e) or (l) and failed to request a time extension, there is no right of appeal [@CL-CTF-0708], any proposal by the contractor for an equitable adjustment under the clause shall be requested within 90 days from the effective date of termination unless extended in writing by the contracting officer [@CL-CTF-0709], the Government may, under the terms and conditions it prescribes, make partial payments and payments against cost incurred by the contractor for the terminated portion of the contract, if the contracting officer believes the total of these payments will not exceed the amount to which the contractor will be entitled [@CL-CTF-1488; @CL-CTF-0710], if the total payments exceed the amount finally determined to be due, the contractor shall repay the excess to the Government upon demand, together with interest computed at the rate established by the Secretary of the Treasury under 50 USC App 1215(b)(2) [@CL-CTF-0711], unless otherwise provided in the contract or by statute, the contractor shall maintain all records and documents relating to the terminated portion of the contract for 3 years after final settlement [@CL-CTF-0712], and under Alternate I, if the contract is with an agency of the United States Government or with State, local, or foreign governments or their agencies, and if the contracting officer determines that the requirement to pay interest on excess partial payments is inappropriate, paragraph (m)(2) is deleted from the basic clause [@CL-CTF-0713].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor has a fixed-price contract to remove a structure for 500,000. Before the notice it sold 20,000 of scrap in a bona fide sale. The Government terminates the whole contract. The scrap sold stays with the contractor, and any salvage still on the site, with title vested in the contractor, revests in the Government. Payments already made are 200,000, and no work remains that was not terminated. The agreed amount, excluding settlement costs, can be at most 500,000 less 200,000, which is 300,000.

If instead the parties cannot agree, the contracting officer determines the amount, starting from the cost of the work done. Suppose the contractor's cost of work performed is 150,000 and the contracting officer finds that the contractor would have lost money on the whole contract had it been completed. Then no profit is added and the amount is reduced to reflect the indicated rate of loss. Any advance payments not yet liquidated under the terminated portion are deducted from what is due.

## Never confuse

Under FAR 52.249-3(a), upon receipt of the notice, if title to property is vested in the contractor under the contract, it shall revest in the Government regardless of any other clause of the contract, except for property that the contractor disposed of by bona fide sale or removed from the site [@CL-CTF-2060]. Under FAR 52.249-3(f), the agreed amount, whether under paragraph (f) or paragraph (g) of the clause, exclusive of settlement costs, may not exceed the total contract price as reduced by (1) the amount of payments previously made and (2) the contract price of work not terminated [@CL-CTF-2066]. Under FAR 52.249-3(g)(1)(iii), if it appears that the contractor would have sustained a loss on the entire contract had it been completed, the contracting officer shall allow no profit under subdivision (iii) and shall reduce the amount of the settlement to reflect the indicated rate of loss [@CL-CTF-2113]. Under FAR 52.249-3(m)(2), if the total payments exceed the amount finally determined to be due, the contractor shall repay the excess to the Government upon demand, together with interest computed at the rate established by the Secretary of the Treasury under 50 USC App 1215(b)(2) [@CL-CTF-2118], and interest shall not be charged on any excess payment due to a reduction in the contractor's termination settlement proposal because of retention or other disposition of termination inventory until 10 days after the date of the retention or disposition [@CL-CTF-2235], or a later date determined by the contracting officer because of the circumstances [@CL-CTF-2256]. If a fixed-price contract not expected to exceed the simplified acquisition threshold is for dismantling, demolition, or removal of improvements, the contracting officer shall use the clause at FAR 52.249-1 with its Alternate I, on [Termination for convenience: fixed-price and services short forms](ctf-termination-for-convenience-short-forms.md) [@CL-CLS-1014; @CL-CLS-1053]. For the general fixed-price convenience settlement, see [Termination for Convenience of the Government (Fixed-Price): settlement](ctf-termination-for-convenience-settlement.md). Under FAR 52.249-3(j), there is no right of appeal if the contractor failed to submit the termination settlement proposal within the time provided in paragraph (e) or (f) and failed to request a time extension [@CL-CTF-2233], where paragraph (f) is the paragraph under which the contractor and the contracting officer may agree upon the whole or any part of the amount to be paid because of the termination [@CL-CTF-2065], whereas under RFO FAR 52.249-3(j), there is no right of appeal if the contractor failed to submit the termination settlement proposal within the time provided in paragraph (e) or (l) and failed to request a time extension [@CL-CTF-0708], where paragraph (l) provides the 90-day period for requesting an equitable adjustment [@CL-CTF-0709].
