## Purpose

Shows what a contractor must do after a termination notice on a dismantling, demolition or removal contract, how the settlement amount is bounded and adjusted, what is deducted, how partial payments and partial terminations work, how long records are kept, and who holds title to the salvage.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CTF-2058] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.249-3 [@CL-CTF-2058] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.249-3 [@CL-CTF-0696; @CL-CTF-0713] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CTF-2058] |
| Applies to | Solicitations and contracts for dismantling, demolition or removal of improvements [@CL-CLS-1017], when a fixed-price contract is contemplated and the contract amount is expected to exceed the simplified acquisition threshold [@CL-CLS-1018] |

The Government may terminate performance of work in whole or from time to time in part if the contracting officer determines that a termination is in the Government's interest [@CL-CTF-2058]. The contracting officer terminates by delivering a Notice of Termination specifying the extent of termination and the effective date [@CL-CTF-2059]. On receipt of the notice, title to property vested in the contractor under the contract revests in the Government regardless of any other clause of the contract, except for property that the contractor disposed of by bona fide sale or removed from the site [@CL-CTF-2060].

The contractor and the contracting officer may agree on the whole or any part of the amount to be paid, subject to the settlement proposal paragraph, and that amount may include a reasonable allowance for profit on work done [@CL-CTF-2065]. The agreed amount, exclusive of settlement costs, may not exceed the total contract price as reduced by payments previously made and the contract price of work not terminated [@CL-CTF-2066]. If they fail to agree on the whole amount, the contracting officer pays the amounts the contracting officer determines, but without duplication of any amounts agreed on under paragraph (f) of the clause [@CL-CTF-2067; @CL-CTF-2211], which for contract work performed before the effective date of termination include the cost of that work [@CL-CTF-2068].

For that work the determination also includes a sum as profit on the cost of the work, determined under section 49.202 of the Federal Acquisition Regulation as in effect on the date of the contract to be fair and reasonable, subject to the loss rule [@CL-CTF-2112]. If it appears that the contractor would have sustained a loss on the entire contract had it been completed, the contracting officer allows no profit under that subdivision and reduces the amount of the settlement to reflect the indicated rate of loss [@CL-CTF-2113]. The profit and loss rules are also on [Termination settlements: profit allowance and adjustment for loss](../../close-revrec/revenue/cls-termination-settlements-profit-and-loss.md).

## How it works

After the notice, and except as the contracting officer directs, the contractor immediately stops work as specified in the notice and proceeds with its other listed obligations, regardless of delay in determining or adjusting any amounts due [@CL-CTF-2061]. As directed by the contracting officer, the contractor transfers title to the Government and delivers the fabricated or unfabricated parts, work in process, completed work, supplies and other material produced or acquired for the work terminated [@CL-CTF-2062]. The contractor must also use its best efforts to sell, as directed or authorized by the contracting officer, property of the types listed for transfer and delivery, but is not required to extend credit to any purchaser, and may acquire the property under the conditions prescribed by, and at prices approved by, the contracting officer [@CL-CTF-2107; @CL-CTF-2212]. Inventory handling is on [Termination inventory: disposal schedules and government property accounting](../../close-revrec/inventory-wip/cls-termination-inventory-schedules-and-property-accounting.md).

Complete termination inventory schedules are due no later than 120 days from the effective date of termination, unless the contracting officer extends the period in writing on the contractor's written request made within that period [@CL-CTF-2063]. The final termination settlement proposal is due promptly, and no later than 1 year from the effective date of termination, unless extended in the same way [@CL-CTF-2064]. However, if the contracting officer determines that the facts justify it, a settlement proposal may be received and acted on after 1 year or any extension [@CL-CTF-2231]. If the contractor fails to submit the proposal within the time allowed, the contracting officer may determine, on the basis of information available, the amount, if any, due the contractor and pays the amount determined [@CL-CTF-2232]. If the contractor failed to submit the proposal within the time provided and also failed to request a time extension, there is no right of appeal [@CL-CTF-2233].

In arriving at the amount due, the listed deductions include all unliquidated advance or other payments to the contractor under the terminated portion of the contract and any claim the Government has against the contractor under the contract [@CL-CTF-2114]. The deduction and limitation rules are also on [Termination settlements: deductions, completed end items, and settlement limitation](../../close-revrec/revenue/cls-termination-settlement-deductions-and-limitations.md).

If the termination is partial, the contractor may file a proposal with the contracting officer for an equitable adjustment of the price or prices of the continued portion of the contract [@CL-CTF-2115]. Any such proposal must be requested within 90 days from the effective date of termination, unless the contracting officer extends the period in writing [@CL-CTF-2116]. See [Partial termination: equitable adjustment and partial settlements](../../close-revrec/revenue/cls-partial-termination-equitable-adjustment-and-settlements.md).

The clause provides for partial payments and payments against cost incurred by the contractor for the terminated portion of the contract, under terms and conditions prescribed, if the contracting officer believes the total of these payments will not exceed the amount to which the contractor will be entitled [@CL-CTF-2117]. If the total payments exceed the amount finally determined to be due, the contractor must repay the excess to the Government on demand, together with interest computed at the rate established by the Secretary of the Treasury [@CL-CTF-2118]. Interest is computed for the period from the date the contractor receives the excess payment to the date it is repaid [@CL-CTF-2234]. Interest is not charged on an excess payment caused by a reduction in the settlement proposal because of retention or other disposition of termination inventory until 10 days after the retention or disposition [@CL-CTF-2235], or a later date the contracting officer determines because of the circumstances [@CL-CTF-2256]. Under Alternate I, if the contract is with an agency of the United States Government or with State, local or foreign governments or their agencies, and the contracting officer determines that the requirement to pay interest on excess partial payments is inappropriate, the interest subparagraph is deleted from the basic clause [@CL-CTF-2236]. See [Partial payments and final payment under a terminated contract](../../close-revrec/revenue/cls-termination-partial-payments-and-final-payment.md).

Unless the contract or a statute provides otherwise, the contractor must maintain all records and documents relating to the terminated portion of the contract for 3 years after final settlement [@CL-CTF-2119]. Retention policy is on [Contractor records retention policy](../../compliance/audit-retention/cmp-contractor-records-retention-policy.md).

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text because DoD's class deviation directs it, effective February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. RFO FAR 52.249-3 keeps the termination ground [@CL-CTF-0696; @CL-CTF-2058] and the revesting of title in the Government, except for property that the contractor disposed of by bona fide sale or removed from the site [@CL-CTF-0697; @CL-CTF-2060]. It keeps the duty to stop work as specified in the notice, regardless of delay in determining or adjusting any amounts due [@CL-CTF-0698; @CL-CTF-2061], the 120-day deadline for inventory schedules [@CL-CTF-0699; @CL-CTF-2063] and the 1-year deadline for the final settlement proposal [@CL-CTF-0705; @CL-CTF-2064], each unless the contracting officer extends it in writing on the contractor's written request made within that period, and the rule that a termination settlement proposal may be received and acted on after 1 year or any extension if the contracting officer determines that the facts justify it [@CL-CTF-9504]. It keeps the cap on the agreed amount, exclusive of settlement costs [@CL-CTF-0706; @CL-CTF-2066], and the rule that, if the contractor would have sustained a loss on the entire contract, no profit is allowed and the settlement is reduced to reflect the indicated rate of loss [@CL-CTF-0707; @CL-CTF-2113]. It keeps the 90-day request period for an equitable adjustment [@CL-CTF-0709; @CL-CTF-2116], partial payments [@CL-CTF-0710; @CL-CTF-2117], repayment of excess payments on demand with interest [@CL-CTF-0711; @CL-CTF-2118], the deletion of the interest paragraph under Alternate I in the listed cases [@CL-CTF-0713; @CL-CTF-2236] and the 3-year records rule [@CL-CTF-0712; @CL-CTF-2119]. One cross-reference differs: RFO FAR 52.249-3(j) says there is no right of appeal if the contractor failed to submit the termination settlement proposal within the time provided in paragraph (e) or (l) and failed to request a time extension [@CL-CTF-0708], where the codified paragraph says paragraph (e) or (f) [@CL-CTF-2233].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor has a fixed-price contract to remove a structure for 500,000. Before the notice it sold 20,000 of scrap in a bona fide sale. The Government terminates the whole contract. The scrap sold stays with the contractor, and any salvage still on the site, with title vested in the contractor, revests in the Government. Payments already made are 200,000, and no work remains that was not terminated. The agreed amount, excluding settlement costs, can be at most 500,000 less 200,000, which is 300,000.

If instead the parties cannot agree, the contracting officer determines the amount, starting from the cost of the work done. Suppose the contractor's cost of work performed is 150,000 and the contracting officer finds that the contractor would have lost money on the whole contract had it been completed. Then no profit is added and the amount is reduced to reflect the indicated rate of loss. Any advance payments not yet liquidated under the terminated portion are deducted from what is due.

## Never confuse

Revesting of title is not a forfeiture of every item. Property the contractor disposed of by bona fide sale or removed from the site stays outside it [@CL-CTF-2060]. The cap on the agreed amount leaves out settlement costs, so those costs are not counted against it [@CL-CTF-2066]. Profit on the cost of work is not guaranteed: the same subdivision that allows it takes it away when the contract would have run at a loss [@CL-CTF-2113]. Partial payments are payments against cost, and any excess over the final amount comes back on demand with interest [@CL-CTF-2118], although interest on an excess caused by retention or disposition of termination inventory is held off for the period described above [@CL-CTF-2235; @CL-CTF-2256]. The short-form clause with Alternate I is a different clause, on [Termination for convenience: fixed-price and services short forms](ctf-termination-for-convenience-short-forms.md) [@CL-CLS-1053]. For the general fixed-price convenience settlement, see [Termination for Convenience of the Government (Fixed-Price): settlement](ctf-termination-for-convenience-settlement.md). The paragraph that sets the time whose miss ends the right of appeal is a real change between the two texts, not a renumbering: the codified text names paragraph (e) or (f) [@CL-CTF-2233], and the RFO text names paragraph (e) or (l) [@CL-CTF-0708]. Codified paragraph (f) is where the parties may agree on the amount to be paid [@CL-CTF-2065], while RFO paragraph (l) sets the 90-day period for requesting an equitable adjustment [@CL-CTF-0709].
