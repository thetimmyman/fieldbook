## Purpose

Shows how a modification adds or changes line items, what must happen before a modification that lowers the obligated amount, and in what order modifications take effect.

## The rule

Under the section on contract modifications, if new items are added, new contract line or subline item numbers or exhibit line item numbers are assigned under the numbering procedures [@CL-CTF-1596]. If a modification relates to existing line items or exhibit line items, it shall refer to those item numbers [@CL-CTF-1597]. FAR 4.1006 says that when a new item, such as an increased quantity, is added to the procurement instrument, a new line item number is to be assigned [@CL-CTF-1397], and that a modification that relates to existing line items shall refer to those items [@CL-CTF-1398]. If the contracting officer decides to assign new identifications to existing items, the listed rules apply [@CL-CTF-1598].

One listed rule is that the original line item or subline item number may be used if the modification applies to the total quantity of the original line item or subline [@CL-CTF-1598; @CL-CTF-1599]. If the changes in the specifications make the item significantly distinguishable from the original item or the resulting changes in unit price cannot be averaged, a new line item is created [@CL-CTF-1598; @CL-CTF-1632]. Another rule applies if the modification affects only a partial quantity of an existing item and the change does not involve either the delivery date or the ship-to/mark-for data [@CL-CTF-1631]. In that case the original item number shall remain with the unchanged quantity, and the changed quantity is assigned the next available number [@CL-CTF-1631; @CL-CTF-1600]. For undefinitized items, additional rules apply, including that a modification that is undefinitized and increases the quantity of an existing definitized item has its undefinitized quantity assigned the next available number [@CL-CTF-1633].

If a modification will decrease the amount obligated, there shall be coordination between the administrative and procuring contracting offices before issuance [@CL-CTF-1601]. A further listed requirement is that the contracting officer shall not issue it unless sufficient unliquidated obligation exists or the purpose is to recover monies owed to the Government [@CL-CTF-1601; @CL-CTF-1602].

Circumstances may exist in which the numeric order of the modifications to a contract is not the order in which the changes actually take effect [@CL-CTF-1634]. To determine the sequence, modifications are applied first in order of the effective date on the modification [@CL-CTF-1603]. When two or more modifications have the same effective date, they are applied in signature date order [@CL-CTF-1603; @CL-CTF-1604]. When they have the same effective date and the same signature date, procuring contracting office modifications are applied in numeric order, followed by contract administration office modifications in numeric order [@CL-CTF-1603; @CL-CTF-1605].

## How it works

If the modification relates to existing contract line items or exhibit line items, it shall refer to those item numbers [@CL-CTF-1597], and if the contracting officer decides to assign new identifications to existing items, the listed rules apply [@CL-CTF-1598]. If new items are added, new numbers are assigned [@CL-CTF-1596]. Modifications are applied first in order of the effective date on the modification [@CL-CTF-1603]. If a modification will decrease the amount obligated, there shall be coordination between the administrative and procuring contracting offices before issuance [@CL-CTF-1601], and the contracting officer shall not issue it unless sufficient unliquidated obligation exists or the purpose is to recover monies owed to the Government [@CL-CTF-1602].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A line item covers 100 units. A modification affects only 30 of them, and the change does not involve either the delivery date or the ship-to/mark-for data. If the contracting officer assigns new identifications, the original item number stays with the 70 unchanged units, and the 30 changed units take the next available number.

A second modification, numbered later, has the earlier effective date. It is applied before the one with the later effective date, whatever the numbers say.

## Never confuse

Circumstances may exist in which the numeric order of the modifications to a contract is not the order in which the changes actually take effect [@CL-CTF-1634]. When two or more modifications have the same effective date and the same signature date, procuring contracting office modifications are applied in numeric order, followed by contract administration office modifications in numeric order [@CL-CTF-1605]. How a changed price is settled is on [Contract definitization](../funding/ctf-contract-definitization.md) and [Equitable adjustment for fixed-price changes](../funding/ctf-changes-fixed-price-equitable-adjustment.md). The numbering procedures themselves are on [DFARS line item and subline item numbering, exhibits and attachments](ctf-dfars-line-item-numbering-and-exhibits.md), and the tests for a line item are on [DFARS criteria for contract line items and subline items](ctf-dfars-line-item-and-subline-item-criteria.md).
