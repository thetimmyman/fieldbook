## Purpose

What a prime contractor does with its subcontracts after a termination notice, how the reasonableness of a subcontract settlement is measured, and when the Government takes an assignment of subcontract rights or settles a subcontractor's proposal itself.

## The rule

|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CTF-3106] |
| Codified citation | FAR 49.108-2 and FAR 49.108-8 [@CL-CTF-3106; @CL-CTF-3110] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CTF-3106] |
| Applies to | Prime contracts with a termination for convenience clause; the assignment and direct settlement provisions do not apply to the short-form clauses [@CL-CTF-3106; @CL-CTF-3110; @CL-CTF-3239] |

The termination for convenience clauses provide that, after receipt of a termination notice, the prime contractor shall terminate all subcontracts to the extent that they relate to the performance of prime work terminated, except as otherwise directed by the termination contracting officer (TCO) [@CL-CTF-3106]. Therefore, prime contractors should include a termination clause in their subcontracts for their own protection [@CL-CTF-3236].

A prime contractor's failure to include an appropriate termination clause in a subcontract, or to exercise the clause rights, does not affect the Government's right to require the termination of the subcontract [@CL-CTF-3107]. The same failure does not increase the obligation of the Government beyond what it would have been if the subcontract had contained an appropriate clause [@CL-CTF-3237].

The reasonableness of the prime contractor's settlement with a subcontractor should normally be measured by the aggregate amount due under paragraph (f) of the subcontract termination clause suggested in FAR 49.502(e) [@CL-CTF-3108]. The TCO shall allow reimbursement in excess of that amount only in unusual cases and then only to the extent that the terms of the subcontract did not unreasonably increase the rights of the subcontractor [@CL-CTF-3109].

## How it works

Assignment. The termination for convenience clauses in FAR 52.249, except the short-form clauses, require the prime contractor to assign to the Government, as directed by the TCO, all rights, titles, and interest under any subcontract terminated because of termination of the prime contract [@CL-CTF-3110]. The TCO shall not require the assignment unless it is in the Government's interest [@CL-CTF-3238].

Direct settlement. The termination for convenience clauses in FAR 52.249, except the short-form clauses, give the Government the right, in its discretion, to settle and pay any settlement proposal arising out of the termination of subcontracts [@CL-CTF-3239]. However, the clauses do not obligate the Government to settle and pay settlement proposals of subcontractors [@CL-CTF-3240], and as a general rule, the prime contractor is obligated to settle and pay these proposals [@CL-CTF-3111]. When the TCO determines that it is in the Government's interest, the TCO shall, after notifying the contractor, settle the subcontractor's proposal using the procedures for settlement of prime contracts [@CL-CTF-3241]. An example in which the Government's interest would be served is when a subcontractor is a sole source and it appears that a delay by the prime contractor in settlement or payment of the subcontractor's proposal will jeopardize the financial position of the subcontractor [@CL-CTF-3112]. Direct settlements with subcontractors are not encouraged [@CL-CTF-3242].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A prime contract with a full termination for convenience clause is terminated. The prime contractor holds a subcontract for a part it no longer needs, and the TCO gives no other direction, so the contractor terminates the subcontract. The subcontract itself has no termination clause, but the Government can still require termination and owes no more than it would have owed with a clause.

The subcontractor proposes 80,000. The aggregate amount due under paragraph (f) of the suggested subcontract termination clause is 70,000, and the TCO finds no unusual case, so 70,000 is the measure of a reasonable settlement and the extra 10,000 is not reimbursed. As the general rule, the contractor settles and pays the subcontractor.

Suppose instead that the subcontractor is a sole source and a delay by the contractor would jeopardize its financial position. The TCO may then determine that the Government's interest requires it to settle the proposal directly, and does so after notifying the contractor. If the prime contract had a short-form clause, that clause would not provide for either assignment or direct settlement.

## Never confuse

The termination for convenience clauses in FAR 52.249, except the short-form clauses, require the prime contractor to assign to the Government, as directed by the TCO, all rights, titles, and interest under any subcontract terminated because of termination of the prime contract [@CL-CTF-3110], whereas when the TCO determines that it is in the Government's interest, the TCO shall, after notifying the contractor, settle the subcontractor's proposal using the procedures for settlement of prime contracts [@CL-CTF-3241].

After receipt of a termination notice, the prime contractor shall terminate all subcontracts to the extent that they relate to the performance of prime work terminated, except as otherwise directed by the TCO [@CL-CTF-3106], and the reasonableness of the prime contractor's settlement with a subcontractor should normally be measured by the aggregate amount due under paragraph (f) of the subcontract termination clause suggested in FAR 49.502(e) [@CL-CTF-3108].
