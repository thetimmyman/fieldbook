## Purpose

A labor-hour contract pays for direct labor hours at specified fixed hourly rates without the contractor supplying materials. Profit sits inside the fixed hourly rates rather than in a separate fee, and the ceiling price is one the contractor exceeds at its own risk.

## The rule

A labor-hour contract is a variation of the time-and-materials contract, differing only in that materials are not supplied by the contractor [@CL-EST-2981]. Like time-and-materials contracts, labor-hour contracts are not fixed-price contracts [@CL-CTF-2359].

The application and limitations for time-and-materials contracts in 12.207(b), 16.601(c), and 16.601(d) also apply to labor-hour contracts, meaning a labor-hour contract may be used only when it is not possible at the time of placement to estimate accurately the extent or duration of the work or anticipate costs with any reasonable degree of confidence [@CL-EST-2982; @CL-CTF-1229]. The clause at 52.216-7 does not apply to labor-hour contracts [@CL-CTF-1264].

## How it works

Payment is made for direct labor hours delivered at specified fixed hourly rates that incorporate wages, overhead, general and administrative expenses, and profit [@CL-CTF-1228]. Because the contract structure provides no positive profit incentive to the contractor for cost control or labor efficiency, appropriate government surveillance of performance is required to give reasonable assurance of efficient operation [@CL-CTF-1230].

A labor-hour contract must include a ceiling price that the contractor exceeds at its own risk [@CL-CTF-1232]. Before increasing the ceiling price of a labor-hour contract or order, the contracting officer must conduct an analysis of pricing and other relevant factors to determine if the action is in the best interest of the government [@CL-CTF-1234]. See [Labor-hour contracts and the description of letter contracts](../../estimating/pricing/est-labor-hour-and-letter-contracts.md), [Payments under time-and-materials and labor-hour contracts](../../contract-finance/billing-payment/ctf-tm-and-lh-payments.md), and [Time-and-materials contracts: use, ceiling and surveillance](../../contract-finance/billing-payment/ctf-time-and-materials-contracts.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor performs under a labor-hour contract with an established ceiling price of 200,000 dollars and an agreed fixed rate of 95 dollars per hour, with no materials supplied by the contractor. During performance, the contractor delivers 2,000 direct labor hours, which multiplied by 95 dollars per hour results in total labor billings of 190,000 dollars. Because materials are not supplied by the contractor, there is no materials line, and the total billed amount of 190,000 dollars remains 10,000 dollars below the 200,000 dollar ceiling price. Any hours delivered beyond the ceiling price are at the contractor's own risk unless the contracting officer increases the ceiling. This example is illustrative only, and the terms of the contract itself govern.

## Never confuse

A labor-hour contract must not be confused with a time-and-materials contract, which acquires supplies or services on the basis of both fixed hourly rates and actual cost for materials [@CL-EST-2981; @CL-CTF-1259]. Nor is it equivalent to a firm-fixed-price level-of-effort term contract, which is a fixed-price contract requiring a specified level of effort over a stated period for a fixed dollar amount rather than billing hours at fixed rates under a ceiling [@CL-CLS-0562].
