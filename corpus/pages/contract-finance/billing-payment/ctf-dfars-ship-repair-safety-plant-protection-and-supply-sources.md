## Purpose

Shows the contractor's safety and plant protection duties under a ship repair Master Agreement, how the Government pays for additional protection, and the supply-source information an apparently successful offeror gives before award.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-CTF-1972; @CL-CTF-1973; @CL-CTF-1975] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 252.217-7015, DFARS 252.217-7016 and DFARS 252.217-7026 [@CL-CTF-1972; @CL-CTF-1973; @CL-CTF-1975] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CTF-1972] |
| Applies to | Contracts that include the Safety and Health clause or the Plant Protection clause, and solicitations that use the supply-source provision [@CL-CTF-1972; @CL-CTF-1973; @CL-CTF-1975] |

Nothing contained in the Master Agreement or any job order relieves the contractor of obligations it may have under the Occupational Safety and Health Act of 1970 and the Safety and Health Regulations for Ship Repairing [@CL-CTF-1972]. The contractor must provide, for the plant and work in process, reasonable safeguards against all hazards, including unauthorized entry, malicious mischief, theft, vandalism and fire [@CL-CTF-1973]. It must also provide whatever additional safeguards are necessary to protect the plant and work in process from espionage, sabotage and enemy action [@CL-CTF-1992].

The apparently successful offeror agrees to complete and submit before award a table whose columns are line items, national stock number, commercial item, source of supply, actual mfg, company, address and part number [@CL-CTF-1975].

## How it works

The Government reimburses the portion of the costs of the additional safeguards that is allocable to the contract, in the same manner as if the Contracting Officer had issued a change order for them [@CL-CTF-1974]. The costs reimbursed do not include any overhead allowance, unless the overhead is incident to the construction or installation of necessary security devices or equipment [@CL-CTF-1993]. Upon payment by the Government of the cost of any device or equipment required or approved under the additional-safeguards paragraph, title vests in the Government [@CL-CTF-1994]. The reimbursement attaches to the additional safeguards and not to the reasonable safeguards the contractor provides for the plant and work in process [@CL-CTF-1973; @CL-CTF-1974; @CL-CTF-1992].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A yard adds extra guard posts and a security camera system during a job order to protect the plant from sabotage. The additional safeguards cost 50,000, of which 30,000 is allocable to the contract. The Government reimburses the 30,000 as if it had issued a change order, but without any overhead allowance, because the overhead is not incident to building or installing the security equipment. Once the Government pays for the camera system, title to that equipment vests in the Government. The yard's ordinary safeguards against fire and theft for the plant remain its own duty.

## Never confuse

The safety obligations come from the laws the clause names and are not removed by the contract [@CL-CTF-1972]. Plant protection concerns hazards to the plant and work in process [@CL-CTF-1973]. The reimbursed additional safeguards are a different matter, because they cover espionage, sabotage and enemy action and are paid as if by change order [@CL-CTF-1992; @CL-CTF-1974]; for how change orders adjust price, see [Ship repair master agreement: property exchange, changes and job orders (DFARS)](ctf-dfars-ship-repair-exchange-changes-and-job-orders.md). Title to security devices passes on payment under this clause, which is a different trigger from title to property covered by progress payments [@CL-CTF-1994; @CL-CTF-1960]; see [Ship repair master agreement: title, progress payments and discharge of liens (DFARS)](ctf-dfars-ship-repair-title-payments-and-liens.md). The supply-source table is a different matter, because it is completed by the apparently successful offeror before award [@CL-CTF-1975]. For liability for loss of or damage to the vessel, see [Liability and insurance for vessel repair (DFARS)](../../supply-chain/sourcing-evidence/sup-dfars-ship-repair-liability-and-insurance.md).
