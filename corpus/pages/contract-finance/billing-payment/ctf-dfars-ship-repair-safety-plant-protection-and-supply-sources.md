## Purpose

Shows the contractor's safety and plant protection duties under a ship repair Master Agreement, how the Government pays for additional protection, and the supply-source information an apparently successful offeror gives before award.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-CTF-1972; @CL-CTF-1973; @CL-CTF-1975] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 252.217-7015, DFARS 252.217-7016 and DFARS 252.217-7026 [@CL-CTF-1972; @CL-CTF-1973; @CL-CTF-1975] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CTF-1972] |
| Applies to | Contracts that include the Safety and Health clause or the Plant Protection clause, and solicitations that use the supply-source provision [@CL-CTF-1972; @CL-CTF-1973; @CL-CTF-1975] |

Nothing contained in the Master Agreement or any job order shall relieve the contractor of any obligations it may have to comply with the Occupational Safety and Health Act of 1970, the Safety and Health Regulations for Ship Repairing (29 CFR part 1915), or any other applicable Federal, State, and local laws, codes, ordinances, and regulations [@CL-CTF-1972; @CL-CTF-2183]. The contractor shall provide, for the plant and work in process, reasonable safeguards against all hazards, including unauthorized entry, malicious mischief, theft, vandalism and fire [@CL-CTF-1973]. The contractor shall also provide whatever additional safeguards are necessary to protect the plant and work in process from espionage, sabotage and enemy action [@CL-CTF-1992].

The apparently successful offeror agrees to complete and submit before award a table whose columns are line items, national stock number, commercial item (Y or N), source of supply, actual mfg, company, address and part number [@CL-CTF-1975]. The offeror uses “Y” if the item is a commercial product or commercial service and otherwise uses “N”; if “Y” is listed, the offeror need not complete the remaining columns in the table [@CL-CTF-2184].

## How it works

The Government shall reimburse the contractor for that portion of the costs of the additional safeguards that is allocable to the contract, in the same manner as if the Contracting Officer had issued a change order for them [@CL-CTF-1974]. The costs reimbursed shall not include any overhead allowance, unless the overhead is incident to the construction or installation of necessary security devices or equipment [@CL-CTF-1993]. Upon payment by the Government of the cost of any device or equipment required or approved under paragraph (b) of the Plant Protection clause, title shall vest in the Government [@CL-CTF-1994]. The contractor shall comply with the instructions of the Contracting Officer concerning its identification and disposition, and no such device or equipment shall become a fixture as a result of its being affixed to realty not owned by the Government [@CL-CTF-2185].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A yard adds extra guard posts and a security camera system during a job order to protect the plant from sabotage. The additional safeguards cost 50,000, of which 30,000 is allocable to the contract. The Government reimburses the 30,000 as if it had issued a change order, but without any overhead allowance, because the overhead is not incident to building or installing the security equipment. Once the Government pays for the camera system, title to that equipment vests in the Government. The yard's ordinary safeguards against fire and theft for the plant remain its own duty.

## Never confuse

The ordinary safeguards and the additional safeguards differ in who bears the cost: the contractor shall provide reasonable safeguards for the plant and work in process against all hazards [@CL-CTF-1973], and shall also provide whatever additional safeguards are necessary to protect them from espionage, sabotage and enemy action [@CL-CTF-1992], but the Government shall reimburse the allocable portion of the costs of the additional safeguards in the same manner as if the Contracting Officer had issued a change order [@CL-CTF-1974] rather than leaving those costs with the contractor. Nothing in the Master Agreement or any job order relieves the contractor of obligations to comply with the laws and regulations the Safety and Health clause names [@CL-CTF-1972; @CL-CTF-2183]. For how change orders adjust price, see [Ship repair master agreement: property exchange, changes and job orders (DFARS)](ctf-dfars-ship-repair-exchange-changes-and-job-orders.md). Title to a device or equipment required or approved under paragraph (b) of the Plant Protection clause shall vest in the Government upon payment of its cost [@CL-CTF-1994], unlike materials, equipment, or any other property or work in process covered by the progress payments, which become the sole property of the Government upon the making of those progress payments, and are subject to the provisions of the Title clause of the Master Agreement [@CL-CTF-1960]; see [Ship repair master agreement: title, progress payments and discharge of liens (DFARS)](ctf-dfars-ship-repair-title-payments-and-liens.md). The apparently successful offeror agrees to complete and submit the supply-source table before award [@CL-CTF-1975]. For liability for loss of or damage to the vessel, see [Liability and insurance for vessel repair (DFARS)](../../supply-chain/sourcing-evidence/sup-dfars-ship-repair-liability-and-insurance.md).
