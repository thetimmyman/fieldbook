## Purpose

Shows what the commercial products and services terms clause lets the Government do about inspection and nonconforming items, what warranty and damages limit it sets for accepted items, and where a time-and-materials or labor-hour contract replaces the inspection paragraph.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CTF-2421] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.212-4(a), (o) and (p) [@CL-CTF-2421; @CL-CTF-2425; @CL-CTF-2426] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.212-4(b) [@CL-CTF-0062; @CL-CTF-0063; @CL-CTF-0064; @CL-CTF-0065]; RFO FAR 52.212-4(o) [@CL-CTF-0066]; RFO FAR 52.212-4(p) [@CL-CTF-0067]; RFO FAR 52.212-4 Alternate I [@CL-CTF-0068; @CL-CTF-0069; @CL-CTF-0171] |
| Edition date | NOV 2023 [@CL-CTF-9644] |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CTF-2421] |
| Applies to | Contracts that contain the commercial products and services terms clause, as to the paragraphs quoted [@CL-CTF-2421] |

The contractor shall tender for acceptance only items that conform to the requirements of the contract, and the Government reserves the right to inspect or test any supplies or services that have been tendered for acceptance [@CL-CTF-2421]. The Government may require repair or replacement of nonconforming supplies, or reperformance of nonconforming services, at no increase in contract price [@CL-CTF-2422]. If repair, replacement or reperformance will not correct the defects or is not possible, the Government may seek an equitable price reduction or adequate consideration for acceptance of nonconforming supplies or services [@CL-CTF-2423].

The Government must exercise its postacceptance rights within a reasonable time after the defect was discovered or should have been discovered, and before any substantial change occurs in the condition of the item, unless the change is due to the defect in the item [@CL-CTF-2424]. The contractor warrants and implies that the items delivered are merchantable and fit for use for the particular purpose described in the contract [@CL-CTF-2425]. Except as otherwise provided by an express warranty, the contractor will not be liable to the Government for consequential damages resulting from any defect or deficiencies in accepted items [@CL-CTF-2426].

The clause also says that when a time-and-materials or labor-hour contract is contemplated, its Alternate I paragraphs (a), (e), (i), (l), and (m) are substituted for those in the basic clause [@CL-CTF-2668]. Those substituted paragraphs are stated on [Commercial contract terms, Alternate I](ctf-commercial-terms-alternate-i-time-and-materials.md).

## How it works

The contractor tenders only conforming items, and the Government may inspect or test them [@CL-CTF-2421]. For a nonconforming item the Government may require repair or replacement of supplies, or reperformance of services, at no increase in contract price [@CL-CTF-2422]. If repair, replacement or reperformance will not correct the defects or is not possible, the Government may seek an equitable price reduction or adequate consideration for acceptance of nonconforming supplies or services [@CL-CTF-2423]. The postacceptance rights must be used within a reasonable time after the Government found or should have found the defect, and before any substantial change in the item's condition unless the defect caused the change [@CL-CTF-2424]. The contractor warrants and implies that the items delivered are merchantable and fit for the particular purpose described in the contract [@CL-CTF-2425]. Except as otherwise provided by an express warranty, the contractor will not be liable to the Government for consequential damages resulting from any defect or deficiencies in accepted items [@CL-CTF-2426]. When a time-and-materials or labor-hour contract is contemplated, paragraphs (a), (e), (i), (l), and (m) of Alternate I are substituted for those in the basic clause [@CL-CTF-2668], and Alternate I paragraph (a)(1) gives the Government the right to inspect and test all materials furnished and services performed [@CL-CTF-2669].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text because Class Deviation 2026-O0038 directs it, effective February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. It keeps the inspection and acceptance rules, now in paragraph (b) after a new paragraph (a) on definitions, where the codified text has them in paragraph (a) [@CL-CTF-0052; @CL-CTF-0062; @CL-CTF-0063; @CL-CTF-0064; @CL-CTF-0065; @CL-CTF-2421; @CL-CTF-2422; @CL-CTF-2423; @CL-CTF-2424]. It keeps the implied warranty in paragraph (o) and the limitation of liability in paragraph (p) [@CL-CTF-0066; @CL-CTF-0067; @CL-CTF-2425; @CL-CTF-2426]. For a time-and-materials or labor-hour contract, the RFO text says Alternate I substitutes paragraphs (a), (b), (i), (l), and (m) for those in the basic clause [@CL-CTF-0068; @CL-CTF-2668]. In the RFO Alternate I, paragraph (a) incorporates the clause at FAR 52.202-1, Definitions, and paragraph (b) is Inspection/Acceptance [@CL-CTF-0171; @CL-CTF-0069]. That inspection paragraph says the Government has the right to inspect and test all materials furnished and services performed under this contract at all places and times before acceptance, and will perform inspections and tests in a manner that will not unduly delay the work [@CL-CTF-0069]. It does not print the codified Alternate I words "to the extent practicable" and "including the period of performance", or the sentence that the Government may also inspect the plant of the contractor or any subcontractor engaged in contract performance [@CL-CTF-0069; @CL-CTF-2669; @CL-CTF-2670].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor tenders 100 units under a firm fixed price of 50,000. On inspection the Government finds 10 units nonconforming. The Government may require repair or replacement of those 10 units at no increase in the 50,000 price. If repair or replacement will not correct the defects or is not possible, the Government may seek an equitable price reduction or adequate consideration for accepting them. After acceptance, 2 units show a defect. The Government must exercise its postacceptance rights within a reasonable time after it discovered or should have discovered the defect, and before any substantial change in the condition of the item, unless the change is due to the defect. One accepted unit then damages 5,000 of other Government property. Assume that damage is consequential damage resulting from the defect, and that no express warranty says otherwise. Answer: the contractor is not liable for that 5,000 of consequential damage. If the same work were a time-and-materials contract, the Alternate I inspection paragraph would replace the basic one.

## Never confuse

The warranty paragraph (o) says the contractor warrants and implies that the items delivered are merchantable and fit for use for the particular purpose described in the contract [@CL-CTF-2425]. The limitation paragraph (p) says that, except as otherwise provided by an express warranty, the contractor will not be liable to the Government for consequential damages resulting from any defect or deficiencies in accepted items [@CL-CTF-2426]. The Government may require repair or replacement of nonconforming supplies, or reperformance of nonconforming services, at no increase in contract price [@CL-CTF-2422]. For the general warranty rules see [Warranties](../../supply-chain/sourcing-evidence/sup-warranties.md), and for the fixed-price inspection clause see [Inspection of supplies](../../close-revrec/revenue/cls-inspection-of-supplies-fixed-price.md).

The inspection paragraph is lettered (a) in the codified clause and (b) in the RFO text [@CL-CTF-2421; @CL-CTF-0062]. Codified Alternate I substitutes paragraphs (a), (e), (i), (l), and (m), while the RFO Alternate I substitutes (a), (b), (i), (l), and (m) [@CL-CTF-2668; @CL-CTF-0068].
