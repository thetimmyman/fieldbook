## Purpose

Shows when the Government may exercise the foreign military sales option and the surge option, how the surge option is scheduled and priced, and what the contractor must do after exercise.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-CTF-1926; @CL-CTF-1928] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 252.217-7000 and DFARS 252.217-7001 [@CL-CTF-1926; @CL-CTF-1928] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | DFARS 252.217-7000: NOV 2014 [@CL-CTF-9652]; DFARS 252.217-7001: DEC 2018 [@CL-CTF-9653] |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CTF-1926] |
| Applies to | Contracts that include the foreign military sales option clause or the surge option clause [@CL-CTF-1926; @CL-CTF-1928] |

The Government may exercise the option or options of the contract to fulfill foreign military sales commitments [@CL-CTF-1926]. In the basic clause, paragraph (b) states the commitments by an inserted country name and applicable contract line item number [@CL-CTF-1926]. Under Alternate I of that clause, the Government identifies the foreign country on the date the option is exercised so that any equitable adjustment attributable to foreign military sales can be negotiated, and failure to agree is treated as a dispute under the Disputes clause [@CL-CTF-1927].

The Government has the option to increase the quantity called for by no more than the percent or the quantity inserted in the clause [@CL-CTF-1928], and/or to accelerate the rate of delivery at a price or cost set before award or negotiated as the clause provides [@CL-CTF-1929]. The Contracting Officer may exercise it at any time before the Government accepts the final scheduled delivery [@CL-CTF-1930].

## How it works

When a Capabilities Analysis Plan (CAP) is included in the contract, the option delivery schedule is the production rate provided with the Plan, and if the Plan was negotiated before contract award, the negotiated schedule is used [@CL-CTF-2000]. Where the contract has no CAP, the contractor must give the Contracting Officer a delivery schedule showing its maximum sustainable rate within 30 days of award [@CL-CTF-1933]. That schedule must provide acceleration by month up to the maximum sustainable rate achievable within the contractor's existing facilities, equipment and subcontracting structure [@CL-CTF-2166]. The contractor must not revise the option delivery schedule without the Contracting Officer's approval [@CL-CTF-1930].

The Contracting Officer first gives a preliminary oral or written notice stating the quantities to be added or accelerated, followed by a contract modification incorporating the transmitted information and instructions [@CL-CTF-2167]. The notice and modification under the surge option set a not-to-exceed price equal to the highest contract unit price or cost of the added or accelerated items as of the date of the notice [@CL-CTF-1931]. The contractor need not deliver at a rate greater than the maximum sustainable delivery rate under the schedule paragraph for contracts without a CAP, and exercise cannot extend delivery more than 24 months beyond the scheduled final delivery [@CL-CTF-1932; @CL-CTF-1933]. Unless the price was already agreed, the contractor must submit a cost or price proposal with a cost breakdown within 30 days of exercise [@CL-CTF-1934]. Failure to agree on a cost or price in negotiations resulting from the exercise of the option is a dispute concerning a question of fact within the meaning of the Disputes clause [@CL-CTF-1935]. However, nothing in the clause excuses the contractor from proceeding with the performance of the contract, as modified, while any resulting claim is being settled [@CL-CTF-1936].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contract has a surge option, and the Contracting Officer exercises it to add 10 units. The highest contract unit price is 500, so the notice sets a not-to-exceed price of 5,000. The price was not agreed in advance, so the contractor sends a cost breakdown within 30 days. The final price is then negotiated and may be below 5,000. The contractor keeps delivering the modified contract while any claim is settled.

## Never confuse

Under the foreign military sales option clause, the Government may exercise the option(s) of the contract to fulfill foreign military sales commitments [@CL-CTF-1926]. Under the surge option clause, the Government has the option to increase the quantity and/or accelerate the rate of delivery [@CL-CTF-1928; @CL-CTF-1929]. The notice and modification establish a not-to-exceed price equal to the highest contract unit price or cost of the added or accelerated items as of the date of the notice [@CL-CTF-1931]. Unless the option cost or price was previously agreed upon, the contractor shall submit a cost or price proposal within 30 days from the date of option exercise [@CL-CTF-1934], and failure to agree on a cost or price in negotiations shall constitute a dispute concerning a question of fact [@CL-CTF-1935]. For options in general, see [Exercise of options](../funding/ctf-exercise-of-options.md) and [Option clauses and provisions](../funding/ctf-option-clauses-and-provisions.md). For foreign military sales progress payments, see [DoD small business progress payment rate and FMS progress payment requests](ctf-dfars-progress-payment-rates-and-fms.md).
