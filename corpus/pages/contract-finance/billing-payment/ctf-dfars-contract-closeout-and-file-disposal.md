## Purpose

Shows what the DFARS adds to FAR closeout, including a path for old contracts that cannot be reconciled, and how long DoD holds completed and pricing review files.

Except as provided in paragraph (3) of the DFARS closeout section, contracting officers shall close out contracts in accordance with the procedures at PGI 204.804, and the procuring contracting officer determines and documents the closeout date for file purposes [@CL-CTF-1611]. The FAR closeout steps and time standards are on [Procedures for closing out contract files](../../compliance/audit-retention/cmp-contract-closeout-procedures.md) and [Time standards for closing contract files](../../compliance/audit-retention/cmp-contract-file-closeout-time-standards.md).

Contracting officers may close out contracts or groups of contracts through modifications without completing a reconciliation audit or other corrective action under FAR 4.804-5(a)(3) through (15), as appropriate, if each contract meets the listed conditions [@CL-CTF-1612]. One listed condition is an award date at least 10 fiscal years before the current fiscal year for military construction or shipbuilding, or at least 7 fiscal years before it for all other contracts [@CL-CTF-1612; @CL-CTF-1613]. Another is that performance or delivery was completed at least 4 years prior to the current fiscal year [@CL-CTF-1612; @CL-CTF-1614]. A further listed condition is a determination by a contracting official at least one level above the contracting officer that the contract is not otherwise reconcilable because, as one of the alternatives, the contract or related payment records have been destroyed or lost [@CL-CTF-1612; @CL-CTF-1615]. The other alternative is that, although contract or related payment records are available, the time or effort required to establish the exact amount owed to the Government or the contractor is disproportionate to the amount at issue [@CL-CTF-1615; @CL-CTF-1642].

Any contract or group of contracts meeting the requirements of paragraph (3)(i) may be closed out through a negotiated settlement with the contractor [@CL-CTF-1643]. Except as provided in paragraph (3)(ii)(B), the closeout process shall include a bilateral modification of the affected contract, including contracts closed out in accordance with a negotiated settlement [@CL-CTF-1644]. For contract closeout actions under paragraph (3), remaining contract balances may be offset with balances in other contract line items within the same contract, regardless of the year or type of appropriation obligated to fund each contract line item and regardless of whether the appropriation obligated to fund such contract line item has closed [@CL-CTF-1645; @CL-CTF-9587].

Completed contract files are held in the office responsible for maintaining them for 12 months after completion and are then sent to the local records holding or staging area until they are eligible for destruction [@CL-CTF-1616]; if no space is available locally, the files are to be transferred to the General Services Administration Federal Records Center that services the area [@CL-CTF-9522]. Pricing review files, containing documents related to reviews of the contractor's price proposals subject to certified cost or pricing data, are retained for six years [@CL-CTF-1617]. If it is impossible to determine the final payment date in order to measure the six year period, the files are retained for nine years [@CL-CTF-1646]. The FAR storage and disposal table is on [Contract file storage and disposal](../../compliance/audit-retention/cmp-contract-file-storage-and-disposal.md).

## How it works

Except as provided in paragraph (3), contracting officers shall close out contracts in accordance with the procedures at PGI 204.804, and the procuring contracting officer determines and documents the closeout date for file purposes [@CL-CTF-1611]. Contracting officers may close out contracts or groups of contracts through modifications without completing a reconciliation audit or other corrective action if each contract meets the conditions on award date [@CL-CTF-1613], completion of performance or delivery [@CL-CTF-1614] and a determination that it is not otherwise reconcilable [@CL-CTF-1615], as paragraph (3)(i) provides [@CL-CTF-1612]. A contract or group of contracts meeting the requirements of paragraph (3)(i) may be closed out through a negotiated settlement [@CL-CTF-1643], and, except as provided in paragraph (3)(ii)(B), the closeout process shall include a bilateral modification of the affected contract [@CL-CTF-1644]. Completed contract files are held for 12 months after completion [@CL-CTF-1616]. Pricing review files are retained for six years [@CL-CTF-1617], or for nine years if it is impossible to determine the final payment date in order to measure the six year period [@CL-CTF-1646].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An ordinary contract was awarded 8 fiscal years before the current fiscal year, and its delivery was completed 5 years before the current fiscal year. A contracting official one level above the contracting officer determines that its payment records were lost.

The answer: the award and completion ages meet the two time conditions in the source text, and the determination covers the third, so the contracting officer may close it out by modification without a reconciliation audit.

## Never confuse

Except as provided in paragraph (3) of the DFARS closeout section, contracting officers shall close out contracts in accordance with the procedures at PGI 204.804 [@CL-CTF-1611]. Completed contract files are held in the office responsible for maintaining them for 12 months after completion [@CL-CTF-1616], and pricing review files are retained for six years [@CL-CTF-1617]. The FAR time standards for closing contract files are on [Time standards for closing contract files](../../compliance/audit-retention/cmp-contract-file-closeout-time-standards.md).
