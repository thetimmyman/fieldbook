## Purpose

When vouchers stop on a completely terminated cost-reimbursement contract, when and how the contractor proposes its fee, and how the termination contracting officer works out the adjusted fee.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CTF-3125] |
| Codified citation | FAR 49.302, FAR 49.305-1 and FAR 49.305-2 [@CL-CTF-3125; @CL-CTF-3127; @CL-CTF-3129] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CTF-3125] |
| Applies to | Cost-reimbursement contracts: the voucher cutoff and fee proposal rules for a complete termination (a partial termination goes to FAR 49.304), the adjusted fee for the contract or the terminated portion, and the weighted-factor method for cost-reimbursement construction or professional services contracts [@CL-CTF-3125; @CL-CTF-3127; @CL-CTF-3129; @CL-CTF-3260] |

When the contract has been completely terminated, the contractor does not use Standard Form 1034 after the last day of the sixth month following the month in which the termination is effective [@CL-CTF-3125]. The contractor may elect to stop using vouchers at any time during the 6-month period [@CL-CTF-3257]. When the use of vouchers is discontinued, the contractor submits all unvouchered costs and the proposed fee, if any, as specified in FAR 49.303 [@CL-CTF-3259]. When the contract is partially terminated, FAR 49.304 applies [@CL-CTF-3260].

When the contractor has vouchered out all costs within the 6-month period, a proposal for fee, if any, may be submitted on Standard Form 1437 or by letter appropriately certified [@CL-CTF-3126]. The contractor must submit a substantiated proposal for fee to the termination contracting officer (TCO) within 1 year from the effective date of termination, unless the TCO extends the period [@CL-CTF-3258].

## How it works

The TCO determines the adjusted fee to be paid, if any, in the manner provided by the contract [@CL-CTF-3261]. The determination is generally based on a percentage of completion of the contract or of the terminated portion, using factors such as the extent and difficulty of the work performed by the contractor [@CL-CTF-3127]. The work weighed includes planning, scheduling, technical study, engineering work production and supervision, placing and supervising subcontracts, and work performed in stopping performance, settling terminated subcontracts and disposing of termination inventory [@CL-CTF-3262]. Those factors are compared with the total work required by the contract or by the terminated portion [@CL-CTF-3128]. The contractor's adjusted fee does not include an allowance for fee for subcontract effort included in subcontractors' settlement proposals [@CL-CTF-3263].

The ratio of costs incurred to the total estimated cost of performing the contract or the terminated portion is only one factor in computing the percentage of completion [@CL-CTF-3264]. The percentage may be greater or less than that ratio indicates, depending on the TCO's evaluation of other pertinent factors [@CL-CTF-3265].

On construction contracts the percentage of completion basis refers to the contractor's total effort and not solely to the actual construction work [@CL-CTF-3266]. The effort under a cost-reimbursement construction or professional services contract can generally be segregated into factors such as mobilization including organization, use of finances, and contracting for and receipt of materials [@CL-CTF-3129], and placement of subcontracts, preparation of shop drawings, work in place performed by own forces, supervision of subcontractors' work, job administration and demobilization [@CL-CTF-3267]. Each applicable factor is assigned a weighted value depending on its importance and difficulty, and the total weight value of all factors should be easily divisible so that percentages can be determined [@CL-CTF-3130]. The percentage of completion of each factor must be established based upon the specific facts of each contract [@CL-CTF-3268]. When totaled, the percentage of completion of each factor applied to its weighted value gives the overall percentage of contract completion [@CL-CTF-3269]. That percentage is then applied to the total contract fee, or to the fee applicable to the terminated portion, to arrive at an equitable adjustment [@CL-CTF-3270].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A cost-reimbursement construction contract is completely terminated with an effective date in the middle of March. The sixth month following March is September, so the contractor stops using Standard Form 1034 after the last day of September [@CL-CTF-3125]. It has vouchered out all costs by the end of August, so it may submit its fee proposal on Standard Form 1437 or by certified letter [@CL-CTF-3126]. The substantiated proposal for fee is due to the TCO within 1 year from the effective date, unless the TCO extends the period [@CL-CTF-3258].

The fee applicable to the terminated portion is 200,000, and it carries no allowance for fee on subcontract effort in subcontractors' settlement proposals [@CL-CTF-3263]. Costs incurred are 600,000 against an estimated cost of 1,000,000, a ratio of 60 percent, but that ratio is only one factor [@CL-CTF-3264]. The TCO uses the weighted-factor method, with weights that total 100 [@CL-CTF-3130]. The weights are mobilization 10, use of finances 10, materials 15, subcontract placement 10, shop drawings 5, work in place by own forces 30, supervision of subcontractors 10, job administration 5 and demobilization 5 [@CL-CTF-3129; @CL-CTF-3267].

From the facts of the contract, the percentages complete for those factors are 100, 80, 100, 100, 100, 50, 50, 60 and 0 [@CL-CTF-3268]. Applied to their weights, they give 10, 8, 15, 10, 5, 15, 5, 3 and 0, which total 71, so the overall percentage of completion is 71 percent, above the 60 percent cost ratio [@CL-CTF-3269; @CL-CTF-3265]. Applied to the 200,000 fee, the equitable adjustment is 142,000 [@CL-CTF-3270].

## Never confuse

The cost ratio is not the percentage of completion. Costs incurred against estimated cost are only one factor, and the percentage may be greater or less than the ratio [@CL-CTF-3264; @CL-CTF-3265].

The adjusted fee on a termination is not the fee reduction on a default termination. Settlement of a cost-reimbursement contract terminated for default follows the principles in FAR subparts 49.1 and 49.3 as for a convenience termination, except that the costs of preparing the settlement proposal are not allowable and, while the contractor is reimbursed the allowable costs, an appropriate reduction is made in the total fee, if any [@CL-CTF-3146; @CL-CTF-3295]; see [the default termination card](ctf-default-termination-reinstatement-and-damages.md).

The voucher cutoff applies to a complete termination, and a partial termination goes to FAR 49.304 [@CL-CTF-3260]. The wider settlement mechanics for cost-reimbursement contracts are on [the cost-reimbursement termination settlement card](ctf-termination-cost-reimbursement-settlement.md).
