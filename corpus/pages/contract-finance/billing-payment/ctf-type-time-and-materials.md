## Purpose

A time-and-materials contract pays for direct labor hours at specified fixed hourly rates and reimburses materials at actual cost up to an agreed ceiling price. Profit sits inside the fixed hourly rates rather than in a separate fee, and the ceiling price is one the contractor exceeds at its own risk.

## The rule

Time-and-materials contracts and labor-hour contracts are not fixed-price contracts [@CL-CTF-2359]. A time-and-materials contract provides for acquiring supplies or services on the basis of direct labor hours at specified fixed hourly rates that include wages, overhead, general and administrative expenses, and profit, as well as actual cost for materials except as provided for in 31.205-26(e) and (f) [@CL-CTF-1228; @CL-CTF-1259].

The contract type may be used only when it is not possible at the time of placing the contract to estimate accurately the extent or duration of the work or to anticipate costs with any reasonable degree of confidence [@CL-CTF-1229].

## How it works

Because a time-and-materials contract provides no positive profit incentive to the contractor for cost control or labor efficiency, appropriate government surveillance of performance is required to provide reasonable assurance that efficient methods and effective cost controls are being used [@CL-CTF-1230; @CL-CTF-1257]. Use of the type requires a determination and findings by the contracting officer that no other contract type is suitable, signed prior to executing the base period or any option periods, and approved by the head of the contracting activity prior to executing the base period when the base period plus options exceeds three years [@CL-CTF-1231; @CL-CTF-1233; @CL-CTF-1258].

The contract must include a ceiling price that the contractor exceeds at its own risk, and before increasing that ceiling, the contracting officer must conduct an analysis of pricing and other relevant factors to determine if the action is in the government's best interest [@CL-CTF-1232; @CL-CTF-1234]. Profit is included within the fixed hourly labor rates rather than delivered through a separate fee, and the clause at 52.216-7 applies in conjunction with 52.232-7 only to the portion providing reimbursement of materials at actual cost [@CL-CTF-1228; @CL-CTF-1263]. See [Time-and-materials contracts: use, ceiling and surveillance](../../contract-finance/billing-payment/ctf-time-and-materials-contracts.md), [Payments under time-and-materials and labor-hour contracts](../../contract-finance/billing-payment/ctf-tm-and-lh-payments.md), and [DoD time-and-materials contracts: determination and findings approval](../../estimating/pricing/est-dod-time-and-materials-limitations.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor performs under a time-and-materials order with an established ceiling price of 160,000 dollars and a fixed labor rate of 120 dollars per hour, which incorporates direct wages, overhead, general and administrative expenses, and profit. The contractor performs 1,000 direct labor hours, generating 120,000 dollars in labor billings from multiplying 1,000 hours by 120 dollars per hour, and incurs 30,000 dollars in allowable materials at actual cost. Adding 120,000 dollars for labor and 30,000 dollars for materials yields a total billed amount of 150,000 dollars, which remains 10,000 dollars below the 160,000 dollar ceiling price. Any additional hours or costs incurred beyond the 160,000 dollar ceiling price are at the contractor's own risk unless the contracting officer increases the ceiling. This example is illustrative only, and the terms of the contract itself govern.

## Never confuse

A time-and-materials contract must not be confused with a firm-fixed-price contract, which places maximum risk and full responsibility for all costs and resulting profit or loss on the contractor, as time-and-materials and labor-hour contracts are not fixed-price contracts [@CL-EST-1150; @CL-CTF-2359]. Nor is it a cost-reimbursement contract, where the government pays allowable incurred costs to the extent prescribed in the contract [@CL-CLS-0567]. Under a time-and-materials contract, the fixed hourly labor rate already includes wages, overhead, general and administrative expenses, and profit rather than billing indirect costs or fee separately, and the allowable cost and payment clause at 52.216-7 applies only to the materials-at-actual-cost portion of the contract [@CL-CTF-1228; @CL-CTF-1263].
