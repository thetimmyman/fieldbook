## Purpose

Shows how a cost-reimbursement contract is terminated and settled: the grounds and the notice, the two submission clocks, the agreement on the amount, and the fee on a convenience termination.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CTF-0418] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.249-6 [@CL-CTF-0418] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.249-6 [@CL-CTF-0681; @CL-CTF-0682] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CTF-0418] |
| Applies to | Contracts that include the cost-reimbursement termination clause, which is inserted as prescribed in FAR 49.503(a)(1) [@CL-CTF-0612] |

The Government may terminate in whole, or from time to time in part, when the contracting officer determines that a termination is in the Government's interest [@CL-CTF-0418], or when the contractor defaults and fails to cure the default within 10 days (unless extended by the contracting officer) after receiving a notice specifying the default [@CL-CTF-0611]. The contracting officer terminates by a Notice of Termination that specifies whether the termination is for default or for convenience, the extent, and the effective date [@CL-CTF-0419].

After the notice, and except as the contracting officer directs, the contractor must immediately stop work as specified in the notice, regardless of any delay in determining amounts due [@CL-CTF-0420]. Termination inventory schedules are due no later than 120 days from the effective date, unless the contracting officer extends the period in writing on the contractor's written request made within that 120-day period [@CL-CTF-0421]. The final settlement proposal is due promptly and no later than 1 year from the effective date, unless the contracting officer extends the period in writing on the contractor's written request made within that 1-year period [@CL-CTF-0422]. The contracting officer may still receive and act on a proposal after 1 year or any extension if the facts justify it [@CL-CTF-0613].

Subject to the final settlement proposal requirements, the parties may agree on the whole or any part of the amount, including an allowance for fee, and the contract is then amended and the agreed amount paid [@CL-CTF-0423]. If they fail to agree in whole or in part, the contracting officer determines the amount due on the basis of information available [@CL-CTF-0614]. In that case a convenience termination settlement includes a percentage of the fee equal to the percentage of completion of work contemplated under the contract, less previous payments for fee [@CL-CTF-0424; @CL-CTF-0615], and part 31 cost principles in effect on the contract date govern the costs [@CL-CTF-0425]. The fee provisions are inapplicable if the contract includes no fee [@CL-CTF-0616].

## How it works

Once the notice arrives, the contractor's duty to proceed with its obligations does not wait for a determination of amounts due, and it begins with stopping work as specified in the notice [@CL-CTF-0420]. The same list of obligations also covers terminating related subcontracts, protecting contract property and completing the work not terminated, all subject to the contracting officer's direction.

The inventory schedule and the settlement proposal are separate deadlines. Each moves only by a written extension from the contracting officer on the contractor's written request made within the period, except that a late proposal may still be received and acted on if the facts justify it [@CL-CTF-0421; @CL-CTF-0422; @CL-CTF-0613].

Settlement can close by agreement, which amends the contract and pays the agreed amount, including any allowance for fee [@CL-CTF-0423].

The Government may, under the terms and conditions it prescribes, make partial payments and payments against costs incurred by the contractor for the terminated portion of the contract [@CL-CTF-0617]. These may be made if the contracting officer believes the total will not exceed the amount to which the contractor will be entitled [@CL-CTF-0426].

When the parties fail to agree and the contracting officer determines the amount, the fee on a convenience termination is the completion percentage of the fee, less previous payments for fee [@CL-CTF-0614; @CL-CTF-0424; @CL-CTF-0615]. The percentage excludes subcontract effort included in subcontractors' termination proposals, and previous payments for fee are deducted [@CL-CTF-0615].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text because DoD's class deviation directs it, effective February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. RFO FAR 52.249-6 keeps the grounds for termination, including the 10-day cure period for a default [@CL-CTF-0681; @CL-CTF-0682; @CL-CTF-0418; @CL-CTF-0611]. It keeps the Notice of Termination [@CL-CTF-0683; @CL-CTF-0419] and the immediate duty to stop work as specified in the notice [@CL-CTF-0684; @CL-CTF-0420]. It keeps the 120-day deadline for inventory schedules [@CL-CTF-0685; @CL-CTF-0421] and the 1-year deadline for the final settlement proposal, each unless the contracting officer extends it in writing on the contractor's written request made within that period [@CL-CTF-0686; @CL-CTF-0422], and it keeps the rule that a proposal may be received and acted on after 1 year or any extension if the facts justify it [@CL-CTF-0687; @CL-CTF-0613]. It keeps settlement by agreement [@CL-CTF-0688; @CL-CTF-0423], the determination on the basis of information available when the parties fail to agree, and payment of the amount determined [@CL-CTF-0689; @CL-CTF-9147; @CL-CTF-0614], the convenience-termination fee percentage [@CL-CTF-0690; @CL-CTF-0691; @CL-CTF-0424; @CL-CTF-0615] and the default-termination fee proportion [@CL-CTF-0692; @CL-CTF-9141; @CL-CTF-0618]. It also keeps the part 31 cost principles [@CL-CTF-0693; @CL-CTF-0425], the condition on partial payments [@CL-CTF-0694; @CL-CTF-0426; @CL-CTF-0617] and the rule that the fee provisions are inapplicable when the contract includes no fee [@CL-CTF-0695; @CL-CTF-0616].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A cost-reimbursement contract is terminated for convenience at 60 percent complete, with 900,000 of costs incurred and unpaid, 100,000 of subcontract settlement costs, and 50,000 of settlement expenses. The contract fee is 200,000. The parties fail to agree, so the contracting officer determines the amount. With the 60 percent completion measured excluding subcontract effort already in subcontractors' termination proposals, the fee portion is 60 percent of 200,000, or 120,000. Of that fee, 40,000 was already paid, so 80,000 of fee remains. The settlement is 900,000 plus 100,000 plus 50,000 plus 80,000, or 1,130,000, and the inventory schedules are due 120 days after the effective date of termination.

## Never confuse

The fee rule depends on the ground for termination. On a convenience termination the fee portion follows the percentage of completion of the work contemplated, less previous payments for fee [@CL-CTF-0424; @CL-CTF-0615]. On a default termination the total fee payable is instead the proportionate part of the fee that the number of articles (or amount of services) delivered to and accepted by the Government bears to the total number of articles (or amount of services) of a like kind required by the contract [@CL-CTF-0618; @CL-CTF-1483].

Nor is the settlement an open-ended advance: partial payments depend on the contracting officer's belief that the total will not exceed what the contractor will be entitled to [@CL-CTF-0426].
