## Purpose

Shows how job orders are issued and priced under a ship repair Master Agreement, how changes to a job order are handled, and what the property exchange provision says.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-CTF-1938; @CL-CTF-1942] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 252.217-7002, DFARS 252.217-7003 and DFARS 252.217-7004 [@CL-CTF-1937; @CL-CTF-1938; @CL-CTF-1942] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CTF-1938] |
| Applies to | Job orders under a ship repair Master Agreement that includes the Changes clause and the Job Orders and Compensation clause, and solicitations that use the property exchange provision [@CL-CTF-1937; @CL-CTF-1938; @CL-CTF-1942] |

The Contracting Officer solicits bids or proposals and awards job orders, a job order signed by the Contracting Officer constitutes award, and each job order carries the terms of the Master Agreement [@CL-CTF-1942]. A separate provision states that property described by item number is offered under the exchange provisions of 40 United States Code 503 [@CL-CTF-1937], and the provision gives the address where the property is located and the dates and hours during which offerors may inspect it [@CL-CTF-2381].

Where a vessel, its cargo or stores would be endangered by delay, or military necessity requires immediate work, the Contracting Officer may issue a written order [@CL-CTF-1943]. In the clause the contractor agrees to comply with the order and to perform work on the vessel within its capabilities [@CL-CTF-1989]. As soon as practicable after the written order, the parties negotiate a price and the Contracting Officer issues a job order covering the work [@CL-CTF-1944]. Failure to agree on that price is a dispute within the meaning of the Disputes clause, and in the meantime the contractor must diligently proceed with the work ordered [@CL-CTF-1988]. On request the contractor must furnish a breakdown of costs incurred and an estimate of costs expected to be incurred for that work [@CL-CTF-2169], and must maintain, and make available for inspection by the Contracting Officer or the Contracting Officer's representative, records supporting its cost [@CL-CTF-2170].

## How it works

Where the extent and probable cost of repairs cannot be ascertained readily, the Contracting Officer may issue a job order, on a sealed bid or negotiated basis, to determine the nature and extent of required repairs [@CL-CTF-1945]. Once the Contracting Officer determines what work is necessary, the contractor, if requested, negotiates prices for that work, and the agreed prices are set out in a modification of the job order [@CL-CTF-1946]. Failure to agree on that price is a dispute under the Disputes clause, and in the meantime the contractor must diligently proceed to perform the work ordered [@CL-CTF-2168].

By written change order the Contracting Officer may change a job order within its general scope without notice to the sureties [@CL-CTF-1938]. The change may be to the drawings, designs, plans and specifications, the work itemized, the place of performance, the time of commencement or completion, or any other requirement of the job order [@CL-CTF-2380]. A change that raises or lowers cost or time brings an equitable adjustment in price or date of completion and a written modification of the job order [@CL-CTF-1939]. The contractor must submit a request for price adjustment with a written estimate within ten days after notification of the change [@CL-CTF-1940]. The Contracting Officer may extend that period if the contractor asks for the extension within the ten days, so a request filed within a granted extension is not a late request [@CL-CTF-2154]. If the circumstances justify it, the Contracting Officer may accept a later request at any time before final payment under the job order, but the contractor may not receive profit on a payment under a late request [@CL-CTF-1987]. If the contractor includes in its claim the cost of property made obsolete or excess as a result of a change, the Contracting Officer has the right to prescribe how that property is disposed of [@CL-CTF-2171]. Failure to agree on an adjustment is a dispute under the Disputes clause, and the contractor must proceed with the job order as changed [@CL-CTF-1941].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A job order is priced at 400,000. A change order adds work, and the contractor submits a written estimate of 30,000 of added cost within ten days of notice. The parties agree an adjustment of 28,000, so the job order price becomes 428,000 by written modification. Had the request come after the ten days with no extension granted, the Contracting Officer could still have accepted it before final payment, but the contractor would earn no profit on a payment under that late request. Had the parties not agreed, the matter would be a dispute, and the contractor would still proceed with the job order as changed.

## Never confuse

An emergency order is not a job order. The written order covers immediate work, and the job order covering the work follows once the price is negotiated [@CL-CTF-1944]. A change order changes a job order already issued [@CL-CTF-1938], while an exploratory job order only determines the nature and extent of required repairs [@CL-CTF-1945]. Work discovered during overhaul, maintenance and repair that meets the over and above definition, which includes being necessary in order to satisfactorily complete the contract, has its price settled by negotiation and contract modification [@CL-CTF-1969; @CL-CTF-2172; @CL-CTF-1971]; see [Ship repair master agreement: performance, guarantees and over and above work (DFARS)](ctf-dfars-ship-repair-performance-guarantees-and-over-and-above.md). The job order price as adjusted under the Changes clause is the price used at final payment [@CL-CTF-1959]; see [Ship repair master agreement: title, progress payments and discharge of liens (DFARS)](ctf-dfars-ship-repair-title-payments-and-liens.md). For the general fixed-price changes clause, see [Changes, Fixed-Price clause and equitable adjustment](../funding/ctf-changes-fixed-price-equitable-adjustment.md), and for disputes, see [Disputes clause, claims and certification](../../close-revrec/revenue/cls-disputes-clause.md).
