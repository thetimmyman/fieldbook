## Purpose

Shows what the contracting officer must do and determine before exercising an option, including the funds test and the price comparison, and what the contractor should watch.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CTF-1311] |
| Codified citation | FAR 17.207 [@CL-CTF-1311] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CTF-1311] |
| Applies to | Exercise of an option by the contracting officer [@CL-CTF-1311] |

When exercising an option, the contracting officer shall provide written notice to the contractor within the time period specified in the contract [@CL-CTF-1311]. The contracting officer may exercise options only after determining that funds are available, the requirement covered by the option fulfills an existing Government need [@CL-CTF-1313], the exercise of the option is the most advantageous method of fulfilling the Government's need, price and other factors (see paragraphs (d) and (e) of FAR 17.207) considered [@CL-CTF-1396], the option was synopsized in accordance with part 5 unless exempted by 5.202(a)(10) or other appropriate exemptions in 5.202, the contractor does not have an active exclusion record in the System for Award Management (see FAR 9.405-1) [@CL-CTF-1314], the contractor's past performance evaluations on other contract actions have been considered, and the contractor's performance on this contract has been acceptable, e.g., received satisfactory ratings [@CL-CTF-0447].

For the determination under paragraph (c)(3), the contracting officer, after considering price and other factors, shall make the determination on the basis of one of the following [@CL-CTF-1315]: (1) a new solicitation fails to produce a better price or a more advantageous offer than that offered by the option [@CL-CTF-1315]; (2) an informal analysis of prices or an examination of the market indicates that the option price is better than prices available in the market or that the option is the more advantageous offer [@CL-CTF-1317]; or (3) the time between the award of the contract containing the option and the exercise of the option is so short that it indicates the option price is the lowest price obtainable or the more advantageous offer [@CL-CTF-1318], and the contracting officer shall take into consideration such factors as market stability and comparison of the time since award with the usual duration of contracts for such supplies or services [@CL-CTF-1378]. Under paragraph (e), the determination of other factors under paragraph (c)(3) should take into account the Government's need for continuity of operations and potential costs of disrupting operations, and may consider the effect on small business [@CL-CTF-0575].

## How it works

The contracting officer may exercise options only after determining that funds are available and that the other conditions in FAR 17.207(c) are met [@CL-CTF-1313; @CL-CTF-1396; @CL-CTF-1314; @CL-CTF-0447]. When the contract provides for economic price adjustment and the contractor requests a revision of the price, the contracting officer shall determine the effect of the adjustment on prices under the option before the option is exercised [@CL-CTF-1312].

If it is anticipated that the best price available is the option price or that this is the more advantageous offer, the contracting officer should not use the new-solicitation method of testing the market [@CL-CTF-1315; @CL-CTF-1316]. The contracting officer must provide written notice to the contractor within the time period specified in the contract, determine the effect of a requested economic price adjustment on option prices before exercise, and determine that the contractor does not have an active exclusion record in the System for Award Management [@CL-CTF-1311; @CL-CTF-1312; @CL-CTF-1314].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contract has an option year priced at 1,000,000. Eight months after award the contracting officer compares the option price with an informal review of market prices, which shows the option price is better. Funds are available, the need still exists, the contractor has no active exclusion record, and the other required determinations are made.

Answer: the contracting officer may exercise the option by written notice within the period set in the contract, based on the informal price analysis. A new solicitation is not required.

## Never confuse

When exercising an option, the contracting officer shall provide written notice to the contractor within the time period specified in the contract, and may exercise options only after making the determinations in FAR 17.207(c), including the determination under paragraph (d) [@CL-CTF-1311; @CL-CTF-1313; @CL-CTF-1396; @CL-CTF-1314; @CL-CTF-0447; @CL-CTF-1315]. Under paragraph (d)(1), a new solicitation is one of the three bases for the determination, and if it is anticipated that the best price available is the option price or that this is the more advantageous offer, the contracting officer should not use this method of testing the market [@CL-CTF-1315; @CL-CTF-1316; @CL-CTF-1317; @CL-CTF-1318].
