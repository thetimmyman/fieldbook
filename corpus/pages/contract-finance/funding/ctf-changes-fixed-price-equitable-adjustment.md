## Purpose

Shows how a written change order becomes an equitable adjustment in a fixed-price contract, and when the Contractor must assert its right.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CTF-0311] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.243-1 [@CL-CTF-0311] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.243-1 [@CL-CTF-1126; @CL-CTF-1128] |
| Edition date | AUG 1987 [@CL-CTF-9726] |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CTF-0311] |
| Applies to | Contracts containing the Changes—Fixed-Price clause [@CL-CTF-0311] |

The Contracting Officer may at any time, by written order, and without notice to the sureties, if any, make changes within the general scope of the contract in any one or more of the following: drawings, designs, or specifications when the supplies to be furnished are to be specially manufactured for the Government in accordance with the drawings, designs, or specifications; method of shipment or packing; or place of delivery [@CL-CTF-0312; @CL-CTF-0443]. If any such change causes an increase or decrease in the cost of, or the time required for, performance of any part of the work under the contract, whether or not changed by the order, the Contracting Officer shall make an equitable adjustment in the contract price, the delivery schedule, or both, and shall modify the contract [@CL-CTF-0313; @CL-CTF-0314].

The Contractor must assert its right to an adjustment under the clause within 30 days from the date of receipt of the written order [@CL-CTF-0315]. The 30-day period may be varied according to agency procedures [@CL-CTF-0702]. However, if the Contracting Officer decides that the facts justify it, the Contracting Officer may receive and act upon a proposal submitted before final payment of the contract [@CL-CTF-0316].

## How it works

The Contracting Officer may at any time, by written order and without notice to the sureties, if any, make changes within the general scope of the contract in drawings, designs, or specifications when the supplies to be furnished are to be specially manufactured for the Government in accordance with them, method of shipment or packing, or place of delivery [@CL-CTF-0312; @CL-CTF-0443]. If any such change causes an increase or decrease in the cost of, or the time required for, performance of any part of the work under the contract, whether or not changed by the order, the Contracting Officer shall make an equitable adjustment in the contract price, the delivery schedule, or both, and shall modify the contract [@CL-CTF-0313; @CL-CTF-0314]. The Contractor must assert its right to an adjustment under the clause within 30 days from the date of receipt of the written order, although if the Contracting Officer decides that the facts justify it, the Contracting Officer may receive and act upon a proposal submitted before final payment of the contract [@CL-CTF-0315; @CL-CTF-0316]. Failure to agree to any adjustment shall be a dispute under the Disputes clause, and nothing in the clause shall excuse the Contractor from proceeding with the contract as changed [@CL-CTF-0317].

Effective February 1, 2026, DoD contracting officers shall use the revised FAR Part 52 solicitation provisions and contract clauses published on the Revolutionary FAR Overhaul web page in lieu of the text codified at 48 CFR chapter 1 [@CL-CMP-9760; @CL-CMP-9762]. Under RFO FAR 52.243-1, at any time, the Contracting Officer may issue a written order making changes within the scope of the contract related to drawings, designs, or specifications which require special manufacturing of supplies for the Government, the method of shipment or packing, or place of delivery [@CL-CTF-1126]. If there are any sureties, the Contracting Officer does not need to notify them of a written order [@CL-CTF-1127]. Whether or not changed by the order, if any of the changes cause an increase or decrease in the cost of, or the time required for, performance of the work under the contract, the Contracting Officer shall make an equitable adjustment in the contract price, the delivery schedule, or both, and shall modify the contract [@CL-CTF-1128; @CL-CTF-1129]. The Contractor must assert its right to an adjustment under the clause within 30 days from the date of receipt of the written order [@CL-CTF-1130], agency procedures may vary the 30-day period [@CL-CTF-1133], and if the Contracting Officer decides that the facts justify it, the Contracting Officer may receive and act upon a proposal submitted before final payment of the contract [@CL-CTF-1131]. Failure to agree to any adjustment shall be a dispute under the Disputes clause, and nothing in the clause shall excuse the Contractor from proceeding with the contract as changed [@CL-CTF-1132].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A written change order revises a specification, raising the cost of a fixed-price contract by 40,000 and adding two weeks to the schedule. The 30-day period has not been varied. The Contractor asserts its right to an adjustment within 30 days of receiving the order, and the Contracting Officer makes an equitable adjustment to the price and the delivery schedule, then modifies the contract.

## Never confuse

The codified and RFO change clauses differ in wording rather than in the power to order changes: under FAR 52.243-1(a), the Contracting Officer may make changes within the general scope of the contract in drawings, designs, or specifications when the supplies to be furnished are to be specially manufactured for the Government in accordance with the drawings, designs, or specifications, method of shipment or packing, or place of delivery [@CL-CTF-0312; @CL-CTF-0443], whereas under RFO FAR 52.243-1(a)(1), the Contracting Officer may instead issue a written order making changes within the scope of the contract related to drawings, designs, or specifications which require special manufacturing of supplies for the Government, the method of shipment or packing, or place of delivery [@CL-CTF-1126].

The adjustment triggers differ in wording as well rather than in the duty to adjust: under FAR 52.243-1(b), the Contracting Officer shall make an equitable adjustment if any such change causes an increase or decrease in the cost of, or the time required for, performance of any part of the work under the contract [@CL-CTF-0313; @CL-CTF-0314], whereas under RFO FAR 52.243-1(b), the Contracting Officer shall make an equitable adjustment if any of the changes cause an increase or decrease in the cost of, or the time required for, performance of the work under the contract rather than of any part of it [@CL-CTF-1128; @CL-CTF-1129].
