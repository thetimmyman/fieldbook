## Purpose

Shows how a written change order becomes an equitable adjustment in a fixed-price contract, and when the Contractor must assert its right.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CTF-0311] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.243-1 [@CL-CTF-0311] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.243-1 [@CL-CTF-1126; @CL-CTF-1128] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CTF-0311] |
| Applies to | Contracts containing the Changes—Fixed-Price clause [@CL-CTF-0311] |

The Contracting Officer may at any time, by written order and without notice to the sureties, if any, make changes within the general scope of the contract in one or more of the areas the clause lists [@CL-CTF-0312]. If such a change causes an increase or decrease in the cost of, or the time required for, performance of any part of the work, whether or not changed by the order, the Contracting Officer makes an equitable adjustment in the contract price, the delivery schedule, or both [@CL-CTF-0313], and modifies the contract [@CL-CTF-0314].

The Contractor must assert its right to an adjustment within 30 days from the date of receipt of the written order [@CL-CTF-0315]. The 30-day period may be varied according to agency procedures [@CL-CTF-0702]. If the Contracting Officer decides that the facts justify it, the Contracting Officer may receive and act upon a proposal submitted before final payment of the contract [@CL-CTF-0316].

## How it works

The Contracting Officer orders a change in writing, within the general scope of the contract [@CL-CTF-0312]. If the change moves cost or time, the Contracting Officer makes an equitable adjustment in the price, the schedule, or both, and modifies the contract [@CL-CTF-0313; @CL-CTF-0314]. The Contractor must assert its right to an adjustment within 30 days of receiving the written order, although the Contracting Officer may act on a later proposal if the facts justify it [@CL-CTF-0315; @CL-CTF-0316]. If the parties cannot agree, it becomes a dispute under the Disputes clause, but the Contractor must still proceed with the contract as changed [@CL-CTF-0317].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text from February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. RFO FAR 52.243-1 lets the Contracting Officer, at any time, issue a written order making changes within the scope of this contract related to the listed areas, and paragraph (a)(1) does not use the word general before scope [@CL-CTF-0312] [@CL-CTF-1126]. It puts the surety rule in its own subparagraph, saying that if there are any sureties the Contracting Officer does not need to notify them of a written order [@CL-CTF-0312] [@CL-CTF-1127]. In paragraph (b) it speaks of a change in the cost or time of performance of the work under the contract, where the codified text says performance of any part of the work [@CL-CTF-0313] [@CL-CTF-1128]. It keeps the Contracting Officer's modification of the contract to include the equitable adjustment in the price, the delivery schedule, or both [@CL-CTF-0314] [@CL-CTF-1129]. It keeps the rule that the Contractor must assert its right to an adjustment within 30 days from the date of receipt of the written order [@CL-CTF-0315] [@CL-CTF-1130], the Contracting Officer's discretion to act on a proposal submitted before final payment if the facts justify it [@CL-CTF-0316] [@CL-CTF-1131], and the rule that agency procedures may vary the 30-day period [@CL-CTF-0702] [@CL-CTF-1133]. It also keeps the dispute rule: failure to agree to an adjustment is a dispute under the Disputes clause, but the Contractor must still proceed with the contract as changed [@CL-CTF-0317] [@CL-CTF-1132].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A written change order revises a specification, raising the cost of a fixed-price contract by 40,000 and adding two weeks to the schedule. The 30-day period has not been varied. The Contractor asserts its right to an adjustment within 30 days of receiving the order, and the Contracting Officer makes an equitable adjustment to the price and the delivery schedule, then modifies the contract. Under the RFO text, the order is described as a change within the scope of this contract, without the word general [@CL-CTF-1126], and the cost and time effect is measured on performance of the work, not performance of any part of the work [@CL-CTF-1128].

## Never confuse

The written order under the clause makes changes within the general scope of the contract, in one or more of the areas the clause lists [@CL-CTF-0312].

The codified text has the written order make changes within the general scope of the contract in one or more of the areas the clause lists [@CL-CTF-0312]; the RFO text says changes within the scope of this contract related to the listed areas and does not use the word general [@CL-CTF-1126]. The codified text ties the adjustment to a change in the cost or time of performance of any part of the work [@CL-CTF-0313], while the RFO text ties it to performance of the work [@CL-CTF-1128].
