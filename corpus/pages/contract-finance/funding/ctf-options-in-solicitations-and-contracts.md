## Purpose

Shows how a solicitation states the evaluation of options and the price rule, and which limits apply to option quantities and to the total contract period.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CTF-1305] |
| Codified citation | FAR 17.203 and 17.204 [@CL-CTF-1305; @CL-CTF-1309] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CTF-1305] |
| Applies to | Solicitations containing option provisions, and contracts whose basic and option periods or quantities are limited by the total-period rule [@CL-CTF-1305; @CL-CTF-1309] |

A solicitation containing option provisions shall state the basis of evaluation, either exclusive or inclusive of the option, and when appropriate shall inform offerors that it is anticipated that the Government may exercise the option at time of award [@CL-CTF-1305]. Solicitations normally should allow option quantities to be offered without limitation as to price, and there shall be no limitation as to price if the option quantity is to be considered in the evaluation for award [@CL-CTF-1306].

Unless otherwise approved in accordance with agency procedures, the total of the basic and option periods shall not exceed 5 years in the case of services, and the total of the basic and option quantities shall not exceed the requirement for 5 years in the case of supplies [@CL-CTF-1309]. These limitations do not apply to information technology contracts, although statutes applicable to various classes of contracts may place additional restrictions on the length of contracts [@CL-CTF-1309; @CL-CTF-1310]. The sections also cover how offerors may vary option prices, how option quantities and extensions are expressed, the period within which an option may be exercised, and the value of options for trade agreement thresholds; this card does not state those rules.

FAR 2.101 defines an option as a unilateral right in a contract by which, for a specified time, the Government may elect to purchase additional supplies or services called for by the contract, or may elect to extend the term of the contract [@CL-CTF-9412]. The RFO text of FAR 2.101 defines an option as a unilateral right in a contract by which, for a specified time, the Government may elect to purchase additional supplies or services called for by the contract, or may elect to extend the term of the contract, the same in substance as the codified definition [@CL-CTF-9451; @CL-CTF-9412].

## How it works

This card covers two limits. The first applies only to solicitations that require the offering of an option at prices no higher than those for the initial requirement, which a solicitation may do in unusual circumstances [@CL-CTF-1379]. Those solicitations shall meet the listed requirements [@CL-CTF-1307]. One of them caps option quantities for additional supplies at not more than 50 percent of the initial quantity of the same line item, and an authorized person at a level above the contracting officer may approve a greater percentage in unusual circumstances [@CL-CTF-1307; @CL-CTF-1308]. The second is the total-period limit, which may be exceeded when approved in accordance with agency procedures and which does not apply to information technology contracts [@CL-CTF-1309; @CL-CTF-1310]. This card does not state who gives the approval under agency procedures.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

In unusual circumstances, a solicitation requires options at prices no higher than the base price. A line item has a base quantity of 200 units. Separately, a services contract has a 2-year base period and options for 3 more years.

Answer: the option quantity for additional supplies on the line item is limited to 100 units unless an authorized person above the contracting officer approves more in unusual circumstances. The services contract totals 5 years, which sits at the limit; a further option year would need approval in accordance with agency procedures unless it is an information technology contract.

## Never confuse

The price rule is not the same as the quantity cap. Offerors are normally allowed to offer option quantities without limitation as to price, and the no-higher-than-initial-price requirement is a separate case that brings the quantity cap with it [@CL-CTF-1306; @CL-CTF-1307; @CL-CTF-1308]. The total-period limit is also separate from the quantity cap, because it counts basic and option periods or quantities together [@CL-CTF-1309]. For contract types see [Contract types](../billing-payment/ctf-contract-types.md). For when options are used, how they are exercised and which clauses carry them, see [Use of options](ctf-use-of-options.md), [Exercise of options](ctf-exercise-of-options.md) and [Option clauses and provisions](ctf-option-clauses-and-provisions.md).
