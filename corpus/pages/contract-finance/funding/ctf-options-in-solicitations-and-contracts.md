## Purpose

Shows how a solicitation states the evaluation of options and the price rule, and which limits apply to option quantities and to the total contract period.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CTF-1305] |
| Codified citation | FAR 2.101 [@CL-CTF-9412], FAR 17.203 and FAR 17.204 [@CL-CTF-1305; @CL-CTF-1309] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 2.101 [@CL-CTF-9451] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CTF-1305] |
| Applies to | Solicitations containing option provisions, and contracts whose basic and option periods or quantities are limited by the total-period rule [@CL-CTF-1305; @CL-CTF-1309] |

Solicitations containing option provisions shall state the basis of evaluation, either exclusive or inclusive of the option, and when appropriate shall inform offerors that it is anticipated that the Government may exercise the option at time of award [@CL-CTF-1305]. Solicitations normally should allow option quantities to be offered without limitation as to price, and there shall be no limitation as to price if the option quantity is to be considered in the evaluation for award [@CL-CTF-1306].

Unless otherwise approved in accordance with agency procedures, the total of the basic and option periods shall not exceed 5 years in the case of services, and the total of the basic and option quantities shall not exceed the requirement for 5 years in the case of supplies [@CL-CTF-1309]. These limitations do not apply to information technology contracts; however, statutes applicable to various classes of contracts (such as the Service Contract Labor Standards statute (see 22.1002-1)) may place additional restrictions on the length of contracts [@CL-CTF-1309; @CL-CTF-1310].

FAR 2.101 defines an option as a unilateral right in a contract by which, for a specified time, the Government may elect to purchase additional supplies or services called for by the contract, or may elect to extend the term of the contract [@CL-CTF-9412]. RFO FAR 2.101 defines an option as a unilateral right in a contract by which, for a specified time, the Government may elect to purchase additional supplies or services called for by the contract, or may elect to extend the term of the contract [@CL-CTF-9451].

## How it works

Solicitations may, in unusual circumstances, require that options be offered at prices no higher than those for the initial requirement; for example, when the option cannot be evaluated under FAR 17.206, or future competition for the option is impracticable [@CL-CTF-1379]. Solicitations that require the offering of an option at prices no higher than those for the initial requirement shall: (1) specify that the Government will accept an offer containing an option price higher than the base price only if the acceptance does not prejudice any other offeror [@CL-CTF-1307; @CL-CTF-1282]; and (2) limit option quantities for additional supplies to not more than 50 percent of the initial quantity of the same line item, although in unusual circumstances an authorized person at a level above the contracting officer may approve a greater percentage of quantity [@CL-CTF-1307; @CL-CTF-1308]. Separately, unless otherwise approved in accordance with agency procedures, the total of the basic and option periods shall not exceed 5 years for services and the total of the basic and option quantities shall not exceed the requirement for 5 years for supplies, and these limitations do not apply to information technology contracts [@CL-CTF-1309; @CL-CTF-1310].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

In unusual circumstances, a solicitation requires options at prices no higher than the base price. A line item has a base quantity of 200 units. Separately, a services contract has a 2-year base period and options for 3 more years.

Answer: the option quantity for additional supplies on the line item is limited to 100 units unless an authorized person above the contracting officer approves more in unusual circumstances. The services contract totals 5 years, which sits at the limit; a further option year would need approval in accordance with agency procedures unless it is an information technology contract.

## Never confuse

Solicitations normally should allow option quantities to be offered without limitation as to price, and there shall be no limitation as to price if the option quantity is to be considered in the evaluation for award [@CL-CTF-1306]. Solicitations that require the offering of an option at prices no higher than those for the initial requirement must specify that the Government will accept an offer containing an option price higher than the base price only if the acceptance does not prejudice any other offeror, and must limit option quantities for additional supplies to not more than 50 percent of the initial quantity of the same line item unless a greater percentage is approved in unusual circumstances [@CL-CTF-1307; @CL-CTF-1282; @CL-CTF-1308]. The 5-year limit counts the total of the basic and option periods for services or the total of the basic and option quantities for supplies [@CL-CTF-1309]. For contract types see [Contract types](../billing-payment/ctf-contract-types.md). For when options are used, how they are exercised and which clauses carry them, see [Use of options](ctf-use-of-options.md), [Exercise of options](ctf-exercise-of-options.md) and [Option clauses and provisions](ctf-option-clauses-and-provisions.md).
