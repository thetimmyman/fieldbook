## Purpose

Sets out contracting officer responsibilities, written notices to the contractor, and funding controls when contracts contain the limitation of cost or limitation of funds clauses.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CTF-0359] |
| Codified citation | FAR 32.704 [@CL-CTF-0359] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 32.704 [@CL-CTF-4519; @CL-CTF-4520; @CL-CTF-4521; @CL-CTF-4522; @CL-CTF-4523; @CL-CTF-4524; @CL-CTF-4525; @CL-CTF-4526] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CTF-0359] |
| Applies to | Contracts containing the Limitation of Cost or Limitation of Funds clause [@CL-CTF-9530] |

When a contract contains the clause at 52.232-20, Limitation of Cost, or 52.232-22, Limitation of Funds, the contracting officer, upon learning that the contractor is approaching the estimated cost of the contract or the limit of the funds allotted, shall promptly obtain funding and programming information pertinent to the contract's continuation [@CL-CTF-9530; @CL-CTF-0359]. The contracting officer shall also notify the contractor in writing of one of the following: (i) additional funds have been allotted, or the estimated cost has been increased, in a specified amount [@CL-CTF-9561]; (ii) the contract is not to be further funded and that the contractor should submit a proposal for an adjustment of fee, if any, based on the percentage of work completed in relation to the total work called for under the contract [@CL-CTF-0604]; (iii) the contract is to be terminated [@CL-CTF-9572]; or (iv) the Government is considering whether to allot additional funds or increase the estimated cost, the contractor is entitled by the contract terms to stop work when the funding or cost limit is reached, and any work beyond the funding or cost limit will be at the contractor's risk [@CL-CTF-9572; @CL-CTF-0679].

Upon learning that a partially funded contract containing any of the clauses referenced in FAR 32.704(a)(1) will receive no further funds, the contracting officer shall promptly give the contractor written notice of the decision not to provide funds [@CL-CTF-0360]. Government personnel encouraging a contractor to continue work in the absence of funds will incur a violation of Revised Statutes section 3679 (title 31, United States Code, section 1341) that may subject the violator to civil or criminal penalties [@CL-CTF-0364].

## How it works

Under a cost-reimbursement contract, the contracting officer may issue a change order, a direction to replace or repair defective items or work, or a termination notice without immediately increasing the funds available [@CL-CTF-0361]. Since a contractor is not obligated to incur costs in excess of the estimated cost in the contract, the contracting officer shall ensure availability of funds for directed actions [@CL-CTF-0362]. The contracting officer may direct that any increase in the estimated cost or amount allotted to a contract be used for the sole purpose of funding termination or other specified expenses [@CL-CTF-0363]. Upon learning that a partially funded contract containing any of the clauses referenced in FAR 32.704(a)(1) will receive no further funds, the contracting officer shall promptly give the contractor written notice of the decision not to provide funds [@CL-CTF-0360].

Effective February 1, 2026, DoD contracting officers shall use the revised FAR Part 32, Contract Financing, published on the Revolutionary FAR Overhaul web page in lieu of the text codified at 48 CFR chapter 1 [@CL-CTF-4046]. Under RFO FAR 32.704(a)(1), when a contract contains the clause at 52.232-20, Limitation of Cost, or 52.232-22, Limitation of Funds, the contracting officer, upon learning that the contractor is approaching the estimated cost of the contract or the limit of the funds allotted, will promptly obtain funding and programming information pertinent to the contract's continuation [@CL-CTF-4519; @CL-CTF-4520], and notify the contractor in writing of one of the following [@CL-CTF-4520]: (i) additional funds have been allotted, or the estimated cost has been increased, in a specified amount [@CL-CTF-0680]; (ii) the contract is not to be further funded and that the contractor should submit a proposal for an adjustment of fee, if any, based on the percentage of work completed in relation to the total work called for under the contract [@CL-CTF-4521]; (iii) the contract is to be terminated [@CL-CTF-0802]; or (iv) the Government is considering whether to allot additional funds or increase the estimated cost, the contractor is entitled by the contract terms to stop work when the funding or cost limit is reached, and any work beyond the funding or cost limit will be at the contractor's risk [@CL-CTF-0802; @CL-CTF-0803]. Upon learning that a partially funded contract containing any of the clauses referenced in subparagraph (a)(1) will receive no further funds, the contracting officer must promptly give the contractor written notice of the decision not to provide funds [@CL-CTF-4522]. Under a cost-reimbursement contract, the contracting officer may issue a change order, a direction to replace or repair defective items or work, or a termination notice without immediately increasing the funds available [@CL-CTF-4523], shall ensure availability of funds for directed actions [@CL-CTF-4524], and may direct that any increase in the estimated cost or amount allotted to a contract be used for the sole purpose of funding termination or other specified expenses [@CL-CTF-4525]. Under RFO FAR 32.704(c), Government personnel encouraging a contractor to continue work in the absence of funds (title 31, United States Code, section 1341 et seq.) may subject the violator to civil or criminal penalties [@CL-CTF-4526].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A cost-reimbursement research contract with an estimated cost of 5,000,000 is incrementally funded with 3,000,000 allotted. When the contractor reports that incurred costs and near-term projections are approaching the 3,000,000 allotment, the contracting officer reviews program requirements and determines that agency budget constraints preclude additional funding. Assuming the contract contains the Limitation of Funds clause, the contracting officer promptly issues a written notice advising the contractor that the contract is not to be further funded and that it should submit a proposal for an adjustment of fee, if any, based on the percentage of work completed in relation to the total work called for under the contract.

## Never confuse

FAR 32.704 sets out the contracting officer's duties to obtain funding and programming information, notify the contractor in writing, and ensure availability of funds for directed actions [@CL-CTF-0359; @CL-CTF-9561; @CL-CTF-0362]. For the contractor notice mechanisms and thresholds under the individual clauses, see [Limitation of cost](ctf-limitation-of-cost.md) and [Limitation of funds](ctf-limitation-of-funds.md).

Under FAR 32.704(c), Government personnel encouraging a contractor to continue work in the absence of funds will incur a violation of Revised Statutes section 3679 (title 31, United States Code, section 1341) that may subject the violator to civil or criminal penalties [@CL-CTF-0364]. Under RFO FAR 32.704(c), Government personnel encouraging a contractor to continue work in the absence of funds (title 31, United States Code, section 1341 et seq.) may subject the violator to civil or criminal penalties [@CL-CTF-4526].
