## Purpose

Sets out contracting officer responsibilities, written notices to the contractor, and funding controls when contracts contain the limitation of cost or limitation of funds clauses.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CTF-0359] |
| Codified citation | FAR 32.704 [@CL-CTF-0359] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CTF-0359] |
| Applies to | Contracts containing the Limitation of Cost or Limitation of Funds clause [@CL-CTF-0359] |

When a contract contains the Limitation of Cost or Limitation of Funds clause, the contracting officer, upon learning that the contractor is approaching the estimated cost or the limit of the funds allotted, must promptly obtain funding and programming information pertinent to the contract's continuation [@CL-CTF-0359]. The written notice that follows can state that the contract is not to be further funded and that the contractor should submit a proposal for an adjustment of fee, if any, based on the percentage of work completed in relation to the total work called for under the contract [@CL-CTF-0604].

Upon learning that a partially funded contract containing the Limitation of Cost or Limitation of Funds clause will receive no further funds, the contracting officer shall promptly give the contractor written notice of the decision not to provide funds [@CL-CTF-0360]. Government personnel encouraging a contractor to continue work in the absence of funds incur a statutory violation that may subject the violator to civil or criminal penalties [@CL-CTF-0364].

## How it works

Under cost-reimbursement contracts, the contracting officer may issue change orders, directions to replace defective items, or termination notices without immediately increasing available funds [@CL-CTF-0361]. Because contractors are not obligated to incur costs beyond estimated amounts in the contract, the contracting officer must ensure the availability of funds for directed actions [@CL-CTF-0362]. The contracting officer may also direct that any increase in estimated cost or allotted funding be reserved for the sole purpose of funding termination or other specified expenses [@CL-CTF-0363]. Written notice is required when the contracting officer learns that a partially funded contract containing the Limitation of Cost or Limitation of Funds clause will receive no further funds [@CL-CTF-0360].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A cost-reimbursement research contract with an estimated cost of 5,000,000 is incrementally funded with 3,000,000 allotted. When the contractor reports that incurred costs and near-term projections are approaching the 3,000,000 allotment, the contracting officer reviews program requirements and determines that agency budget constraints preclude additional funding. Assuming the contract contains the Limitation of Funds clause, the contracting officer promptly issues a written notice advising the contractor that the contract is not to be further funded and that it should submit a proposal for an adjustment of fee, if any, based on the percentage of work completed in relation to the total work called for under the contract.

## Never confuse

This policy sets out duties of the contracting officer for contracts containing the Limitation of Cost or Limitation of Funds clause, such as obtaining funding information, giving written notices to the contractor and ensuring funds for directed actions; it does not itself state the contractor's own notice obligations, which are in the clauses. For the contractor notice mechanisms and thresholds under the individual clauses, see [Limitation of cost](ctf-limitation-of-cost.md) and [Limitation of funds](ctf-limitation-of-funds.md).

## Company calibration

Confirming which contracts are incrementally funded, tracking formal agency funding notifications, and identifying responsible personnel for funding surveillance. [@Q-008]
