## Purpose

Sets out contracting officer responsibilities, written notices to the contractor, and funding controls when contracts contain the limitation of cost or limitation of funds clauses.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CTF-0359] |
| Codified citation | FAR 32.704 [@CL-CTF-0359] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 32.704 [@CL-CTF-4519; @CL-CTF-4520; @CL-CTF-4521; @CL-CTF-4522; @CL-CTF-4523; @CL-CTF-4524; @CL-CTF-4525; @CL-CTF-4526] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CTF-0359] |
| Applies to | Contracts containing the Limitation of Cost or Limitation of Funds clause [@CL-CTF-0359] |

When a contract contains the Limitation of Cost or Limitation of Funds clause, the contracting officer, upon learning that the contractor is approaching the estimated cost or the limit of the funds allotted, must promptly obtain funding and programming information pertinent to the contract's continuation [@CL-CTF-0359]. The same sentence has the contracting officer notify the contractor in writing of one of the alternatives the paragraph lists: that additional funds have been allotted or the estimated cost increased in a specified amount [@CL-CTF-9561]; that the contract is not to be further funded and that the contractor should submit a proposal for an adjustment of fee, if any, based on the percentage of work completed in relation to the total work called for under the contract [@CL-CTF-0604]; that the contract is to be terminated; or a further alternative [@CL-CTF-9572].

Upon learning that a partially funded contract containing the Limitation of Cost or Limitation of Funds clause will receive no further funds, the contracting officer shall promptly give the contractor written notice of the decision not to provide funds [@CL-CTF-0360]. Government personnel encouraging a contractor to continue work in the absence of funds incur a statutory violation that may subject the violator to civil or criminal penalties [@CL-CTF-0364].

## How it works

Under cost-reimbursement contracts, the contracting officer may issue change orders, directions to replace defective items, or termination notices without immediately increasing available funds [@CL-CTF-0361]. Because contractors are not obligated to incur costs beyond estimated amounts in the contract, the contracting officer must ensure the availability of funds for directed actions [@CL-CTF-0362]. The contracting officer may also direct that any increase in estimated cost or allotted funding be reserved for the sole purpose of funding termination or other specified expenses [@CL-CTF-0363]. Written notice is required when the contracting officer learns that a partially funded contract containing the Limitation of Cost or Limitation of Funds clause will receive no further funds [@CL-CTF-0360].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 32 text in place of the codified text from February 1, 2026 [@CL-CTF-4046]. RFO FAR 32.704 keeps the scope: it applies when a contract contains the Limitation of Cost or Limitation of Funds clause and the contracting officer learns that the contractor is approaching the estimated cost of the contract or the limit of the funds allotted [@CL-CTF-4519]. The RFO text has the contracting officer notify the contractor in writing of the matters the paragraph lists [@CL-CTF-4520], and it keeps, among them, that the contract is not to be further funded and that the contractor should submit a proposal for an adjustment of fee, if any, based on the percentage of work completed in relation to the total work called for under the contract [@CL-CTF-4521]. It also keeps the written notice of the decision not to provide funds to a partially funded contract that will receive no further funds, which the RFO text words with "must" in place of "shall" [@CL-CTF-4522]. Under a cost-reimbursement contract, it keeps the permissions to issue a change order, a direction to replace or repair defective items or work, or a termination notice without immediately increasing the funds available [@CL-CTF-4523], the duty to ensure availability of funds for directed actions [@CL-CTF-4524], and the permission to direct that an increase be used for the sole purpose of funding termination or other specified expenses [@CL-CTF-4525]. Two points differ. The RFO text says the contracting officer "will" promptly obtain funding and programming information, where the codified text says "shall" [@CL-CTF-4520; @CL-CTF-0359]. And RFO paragraph (c) says that Government personnel encouraging a contractor to continue work in the absence of funds may subject the violator to civil or criminal penalties [@CL-CTF-4526], citing 31 U.S.C. 1341 et seq., without the codified words that such personnel "will incur a violation" [@CL-CTF-0364].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A cost-reimbursement research contract with an estimated cost of 5,000,000 is incrementally funded with 3,000,000 allotted. When the contractor reports that incurred costs and near-term projections are approaching the 3,000,000 allotment, the contracting officer reviews program requirements and determines that agency budget constraints preclude additional funding. Assuming the contract contains the Limitation of Funds clause, the contracting officer promptly issues a written notice advising the contractor that the contract is not to be further funded and that it should submit a proposal for an adjustment of fee, if any, based on the percentage of work completed in relation to the total work called for under the contract.

## Never confuse

This policy sets out duties of the contracting officer for contracts containing the Limitation of Cost or Limitation of Funds clause, such as obtaining funding information, giving written notices to the contractor and ensuring funds for directed actions; it does not itself state the contractor's own notice obligations, which are in the clauses. For the contractor notice mechanisms and thresholds under the individual clauses, see [Limitation of cost](ctf-limitation-of-cost.md) and [Limitation of funds](ctf-limitation-of-funds.md).

The codified paragraph says that Government personnel encouraging a contractor to continue work in the absence of funds incur a statutory violation that may subject the violator to civil or criminal penalties [@CL-CTF-0364], while the RFO paragraph says only that the encouragement, citing 31 U.S.C. 1341 et seq., may subject the violator to those penalties [@CL-CTF-4526].

## Company calibration

Confirming which contracts are incrementally funded, tracking formal agency funding notifications, and identifying responsible personnel for funding surveillance. [@Q-008]
