## Purpose

Shows who selects and negotiates the contract type, which factors the contracting officer considers, and what the contract file must record about the choice, so a finance reader knows what to supply.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CTF-1200] |
| Codified citation | FAR 16.103 and FAR 16.104 [@CL-CTF-1200; @CL-CTF-1205] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CTF-1200] |
| Applies to | Selecting and negotiating the contract type, with file documentation subject to listed exceptions [@CL-CTF-1202; @CL-CTF-1203; @CL-CTF-1250] |

Selecting the contract type is generally a matter for negotiation that requires sound judgment, and it should be considered together with price negotiation [@CL-CTF-1200]. A firm-fixed-price contract shall be used when the risk involved is minimal or can be predicted with an acceptable degree of certainty [@CL-CTF-1201]. When a reasonable basis for firm pricing does not exist, other contract types should be considered, and negotiations should be directed toward a contract type or combination of types that will appropriately tie profit to contractor performance [@CL-CTF-1251].

Each contract file shall include documentation to show why the particular contract type was selected, documented in the acquisition plan, or in the contract file if a written acquisition plan is not required by agency procedures [@CL-CTF-1202]. Exceptions to the requirements at FAR 16.103(d)(1) are fixed-price acquisitions made under simplified acquisition procedures, contracts on a firm-fixed-price basis other than those for major systems or research and development, and awards on the set-aside portion of sealed bid partial set-asides for small business [@CL-CTF-1203; @CL-CTF-1250]. For other than a firm-fixed-price contract, at a minimum the documentation should include an analysis of why the use of other than a firm-fixed-price contract (e.g., cost reimbursement, time and materials, labor hour) is appropriate [@CL-CTF-1204]; rationale that detail the particular facts and circumstances (e.g., complexity of the requirements, uncertain duration of the work, contractor's technical capability and financial responsibility, or adequacy of the contractor's accounting system), and associated reasoning essential to support the contract type selection [@CL-CTF-0444; @CL-CTF-0445]; an assessment regarding the adequacy of Government resources that are necessary to properly plan for, award, and administer other than firm-fixed-price contracts [@CL-CTF-0445]; and a discussion of the actions planned to minimize the use of other than firm-fixed-price contracts on future acquisitions for the same requirement and to transition to firm-fixed-price contracts to the maximum extent practicable [@CL-CTF-0446].

## How it works

There are many factors that the contracting officer should consider in selecting and negotiating the contract type [@CL-CTF-1205]. Under price competition, normally effective price competition results in realistic pricing, and a fixed-price contract is ordinarily in the Government's interest [@CL-CTF-1205]. Before agreeing on a contract type other than firm-fixed-price, the contracting officer shall ensure that the contractor's accounting system will permit timely development of all necessary cost data in the form required by the proposed contract type [@CL-CTF-1206].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A development requirement is valued at about 8 million, and its scope may change as the work proceeds, so there is no reasonable basis for firm pricing. The contracting officer considers a cost-reimbursement type and negotiates it together with price. Before agreeing, the contracting officer asks the contractor to show that its accounting system can report cost by contract line in the form the contract needs. The file also records why a firm-fixed-price contract was not used. The answer: the type and price are negotiated together, finance supplies the accounting system evidence, and the rationale sits in the acquisition plan or the contract file.
