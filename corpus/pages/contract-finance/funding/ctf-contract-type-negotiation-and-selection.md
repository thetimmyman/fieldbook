## Purpose

Shows who selects and negotiates the contract type, which factors the contracting officer considers, and what the contract file must record about the choice, so a finance reader knows what to supply.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CTF-1200] |
| Codified citation | FAR 16.103 and FAR 16.104 [@CL-CTF-1200; @CL-CTF-1205] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CTF-1200] |
| Applies to | Selecting and negotiating the contract type, with file documentation subject to listed exceptions [@CL-CTF-1202; @CL-CTF-1203; @CL-CTF-1250] |

Selecting the contract type is generally a matter for negotiation that requires sound judgment, and it should be considered together with price negotiation [@CL-CTF-1200]. A firm-fixed-price contract shall be used when the risk involved is minimal or can be predicted with an acceptable degree of certainty [@CL-CTF-1201]. When a reasonable basis for firm pricing does not exist, other contract types should be considered, and negotiations should be directed toward a contract type or combination of types that will appropriately tie profit to contractor performance [@CL-CTF-1251].

Each contract file shall include documentation of why the particular contract type was selected, recorded in the acquisition plan or, if a written acquisition plan is not required by agency procedures, in the contract file [@CL-CTF-1202]. The requirement has three exceptions: fixed-price acquisitions made under simplified acquisition procedures, contracts on a firm-fixed-price basis other than those for major systems or research and development, and awards on the set-aside portion of sealed bid partial set-asides for small business [@CL-CTF-1203; @CL-CTF-1250]. For a contract other than firm-fixed-price, the documentation should at a minimum include an analysis of why the use of that other type is appropriate [@CL-CTF-1204].

## How it works

The contracting officer should consider factors in selecting and negotiating the contract type [@CL-CTF-1205]. The first listed factor is price competition: effective price competition normally results in realistic pricing, and a fixed-price contract is then ordinarily in the Government's interest [@CL-CTF-1205]. Before agreeing on a contract type other than firm-fixed-price, the contracting officer shall ensure that the contractor's accounting system will permit timely development of all necessary cost data in the form required by the proposed contract type [@CL-CTF-1206]. A finance reader should therefore expect to be asked for evidence that the accounting system can produce that cost data [@CL-CTF-1206]. This card does not state the other listed factors, and the printed section does not prescribe a checklist for that evidence.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A development requirement is valued at about 8 million, and its scope may change as the work proceeds, so there is no reasonable basis for firm pricing. The contracting officer considers a cost-reimbursement type and negotiates it together with price. Before agreeing, the contracting officer asks the contractor to show that its accounting system can report cost by contract line in the form the contract needs. The file also records why a firm-fixed-price contract was not used. The answer: the type and price are negotiated together, finance supplies the accounting system evidence, and the rationale sits in the acquisition plan or the contract file.
