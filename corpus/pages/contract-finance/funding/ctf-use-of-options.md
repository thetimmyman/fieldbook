## Purpose

Shows when the contracting officer may and may not include options in a contract, and how the decision to include an option relates to the funds test at exercise.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CTF-1300] |
| Codified citation | FAR 17.202 [@CL-CTF-1300] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CTF-1300] |
| Applies to | Contracts awarded by sealed bidding or by negotiation, when the contracting officer decides whether to include options, subject to the limitations in the section [@CL-CTF-1300] |

The contracting officer may include options in contracts, for both sealed bidding and contracting by negotiation, when it is in the Government's interest, subject to the limitations in the paragraphs on circumstances where options are normally not in the Government's interest and where options shall not be employed [@CL-CTF-1300; @CL-CTF-1301; @CL-CTF-1303]. Inclusion of an option is normally not in the Government's interest when, in the judgment of the contracting officer, the circumstances listed in that paragraph exist [@CL-CTF-1301]. One listed circumstance is that an indefinite quantity or requirements contract would be more appropriate than a contract with options, and the section adds that this does not preclude using an indefinite quantity or requirements contract with options [@CL-CTF-1301; @CL-CTF-1302]. The paragraph lists another circumstance that this card does not state.

The contracting officer shall not employ options in the cases listed in paragraph (c) of the section [@CL-CTF-1303]. Those cases include one where the contractor will incur undue risks, for example because the price or availability of necessary materials or labor is not reasonably foreseeable, and one where market prices for the supplies or services involved are likely to change substantially [@CL-CTF-1303]. Another listed case is an option that represents known firm requirements for which funds are available, unless the basic quantity is a learning or testing quantity and competition for the option is impracticable once the initial contract is awarded [@CL-CTF-1303; @CL-CTF-1304]. The section also has a rule for sealed bidding and a rule for service contracts; this card does not state them.

## How it works

The decision to include an option rests with the contracting officer and turns on the Government's interest, within the limits above [@CL-CTF-1300]. This card does not state what an option commits in dollars. The contracting officer may exercise an option only after determining, among other things, that funds are available [@CL-CTF-1313]. The paragraph on cases where options shall not be employed also bars an option for known firm requirements for which funds are available, unless the basic quantity is a learning or testing quantity and competition for the option is impracticable once the initial contract is awarded [@CL-CTF-1303; @CL-CTF-1304]. A finance reader should therefore treat the funds determination at exercise as a separate step from the decision to include the option.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contract buys 100 units of a part. The buyer also wants 100 more units next year. The need for the extra units is known and firm, and funds are already available for them. The basic quantity is a normal production quantity, not a learning or testing quantity.

Answer: the contracting officer does not employ an option for the extra units, because the option would represent known firm requirements for which funds are available and the learning or testing exception does not apply.

## Never confuse

An option is not an indefinite quantity or requirements contract. The section lists one circumstance in which such a contract would be more appropriate than a contract with options, yet it says this does not preclude using such a contract with options [@CL-CTF-1301; @CL-CTF-1302]. See [Indefinite delivery and indefinite quantity contracts](ctf-indefinite-delivery-and-indefinite-quantity-contracts.md) and [Requirements contracts](ctf-requirements-contracts.md). For what a solicitation, an exercise and the option clauses require, see [Options in solicitations and contracts](ctf-options-in-solicitations-and-contracts.md), [Exercise of options](ctf-exercise-of-options.md) and [Option clauses and provisions](ctf-option-clauses-and-provisions.md).
