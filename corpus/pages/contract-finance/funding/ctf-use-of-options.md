## Purpose

Shows when the contracting officer may and may not include options in a contract, and how the decision to include an option relates to the funds test at exercise.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CTF-1300] |
| Codified citation | FAR 17.202 [@CL-CTF-1300] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CTF-1300] |
| Applies to | Contracts awarded by sealed bidding or by negotiation, when the contracting officer decides whether to include options, subject to the limitations in the section [@CL-CTF-1300] |

Subject to the limitations of paragraphs (b) and (c) of FAR 17.202, for both sealed bidding and contracting by negotiation, the contracting officer may include options in contracts when it is in the Government's interest [@CL-CTF-1300]. Inclusion of an option is normally not in the Government's interest when, in the judgment of the contracting officer: (1) the foreseeable requirements involve minimum economic quantities (i.e., quantities large enough to permit the recovery of startup costs and production of the required supplies at a reasonable price) and delivery requirements far enough into the future to permit competitive acquisition, production, and delivery [@CL-CTF-1301; @CL-CTF-1286; @CL-CTF-1287]; or (2) an indefinite quantity or requirements contract would be more appropriate than a contract with options, although this does not preclude the use of an indefinite quantity contract or requirements contract with options [@CL-CTF-1301; @CL-CTF-1302].

The contracting officer shall not employ options if: (1) the contractor will incur undue risks, e.g., the price or availability of necessary materials or labor is not reasonably foreseeable [@CL-CTF-1303]; (2) market prices for the supplies or services involved are likely to change substantially [@CL-CTF-1303]; or (3) the option represents known firm requirements for which funds are available unless (i) the basic quantity is a learning or testing quantity and (ii) competition for the option is impracticable once the initial contract is awarded [@CL-CTF-1303; @CL-CTF-1304].

## How it works

Subject to the limitations of paragraphs (b) and (c) of FAR 17.202, the contracting officer may include options in contracts when it is in the Government's interest [@CL-CTF-1300]. Under FAR 17.207(c), the contracting officer may exercise options only after determining, among other requirements, that funds are available [@CL-CTF-1313]. Under FAR 17.202(c)(3), the contracting officer shall not employ options if the option represents known firm requirements for which funds are available unless the basic quantity is a learning or testing quantity and competition for the option is impracticable once the initial contract is awarded [@CL-CTF-1303; @CL-CTF-1304].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contract buys 100 units of a part. The buyer also wants 100 more units next year. The need for the extra units is known and firm, and funds are already available for them. The basic quantity is a normal production quantity, not a learning or testing quantity.

Answer: the contracting officer does not employ an option for the extra units, because the option would represent known firm requirements for which funds are available and the learning or testing exception does not apply.

## Never confuse

Inclusion of an option is normally not in the Government's interest when, in the judgment of the contracting officer, an indefinite quantity or requirements contract would be more appropriate than a contract with options, although this does not preclude the use of an indefinite quantity contract or requirements contract with options [@CL-CTF-1301; @CL-CTF-1302]. See [Indefinite delivery and indefinite quantity contracts](ctf-indefinite-delivery-and-indefinite-quantity-contracts.md) and [Requirements contracts](ctf-requirements-contracts.md). For what a solicitation, an exercise and the option clauses require, see [Options in solicitations and contracts](ctf-options-in-solicitations-and-contracts.md), [Exercise of options](ctf-exercise-of-options.md) and [Option clauses and provisions](ctf-option-clauses-and-provisions.md).
