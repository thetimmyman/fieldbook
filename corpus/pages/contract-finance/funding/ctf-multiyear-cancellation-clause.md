## Purpose

Shows when a multiyear contract is cancelled, what the cancellation charge may cover, how the ceiling limits it and when the claim is due.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CTF-2276] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.217-2 [@CL-CTF-2276] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.217-2 [@CL-CTF-1183] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CTF-2276] |
| Applies to | Contracts that include the cancellation clause [@CL-CTF-2276] |

Cancellation means that the Government is canceling its requirements for all supplies or services in program years subsequent to that in which notice of cancellation is provided [@CL-CTF-2276]. Cancellation shall occur by the date or within the time period specified in the Schedule, unless a later date is agreed to, if the Contracting Officer notifies the Contractor that funds are not available for contract performance for any subsequent program year, or fails to notify the Contractor that funds are available for performance of the succeeding program year requirement [@CL-CTF-2277; @CL-CTF-2278].

If cancellation under the clause occurs, the Contractor will be paid a cancellation charge not to exceed the cancellation ceiling specified in the Schedule as applicable at the time of cancellation [@CL-CTF-2280]. Excessively high charges are not authorized: the cancellation charge will cover only (1) costs reasonably necessary for performance of the contract that would have been equitably amortized over the entire multiyear contract period, and incurred by the Contractor and/or subcontractor, because of the cancellation are not so amortized, and (2) a reasonable profit or fee on the costs [@CL-CTF-2281; @CL-CTF-2282; @CL-CTF-2283].

## How it works

The cancellation charge shall be computed and the claim made for it as if the claim were being made under the Termination for Convenience of the Government clause of the contract [@CL-CTF-2284]. The Contractor shall submit the claim promptly, but no later than 1 year from the date of notification of the nonavailability of funds, or from the date specified in the Schedule by which notification of the availability of additional funds for the next succeeding program year is required to be issued, whichever is earlier, unless extensions in writing are granted by the Contracting Officer [@CL-CTF-2285; @CL-CTF-2286].

What the claim may include [@CL-CTF-2287; @CL-CTF-2288; @CL-CTF-2289]:

- Reasonable nonrecurring costs (see Subpart 15.4 of the Federal Acquisition Regulation) that are applicable to and normally would have been amortized in all supplies or services that are multiyear requirements [@CL-CTF-2287].
- Allocable portions of the costs of facilities acquired or established for the conduct of the work, to the extent that it is impracticable for the Contractor to use the facilities in its commercial work, and if the costs are not charged to the contract through overhead or otherwise depreciated [@CL-CTF-2288].
- Costs incurred for the assembly, training, and transportation to and from the job site of a specialized work force, and costs not amortized solely because the cancellation had precluded anticipated benefits of Contractor or subcontractor learning [@CL-CTF-2289].

The claim shall not include labor, material, or other expenses incurred by the Contractor or subcontractors for performance of the canceled work, any cost already paid to the Contractor, or anticipated profit or unearned fee on the canceled work [@CL-CTF-2290]. For service contracts, the Contractor's claim shall also exclude the remaining useful commercial life of facilities [@CL-CTF-2291]; "useful commercial life" means the commercial utility of the facilities rather than their physical life, with due consideration given to such factors as location of facilities, their specialized nature, and obsolescence [@CL-CTF-2292].

The contract may include an Option clause with the period for exercising the option limited to the date in the contract for notification that funds are available for the next succeeding program year [@CL-CTF-2313]. If so, the Contractor agrees not to include in option quantities any costs of a startup or nonrecurring nature that have been fully set forth in the contract [@CL-CTF-2314], and the quantities of items executable under the Option clause shall be only for the recurring costs and a reasonable profit or fee necessary to furnish the additional option quantities [@CL-CTF-2315]. Quantities added because of the exercise of the Option clause shall be included in the quantity canceled for the purpose of computing allowable cancellation charges [@CL-CTF-2293].

FAR 52.217-2 is inserted as prescribed in FAR 17.109(a) [@CL-CTF-9163]. Effective February 1, 2026, DoD contracting officers shall use the revised FAR Part 52 solicitation provisions and contract clauses published on the Revolutionary FAR Overhaul web page in lieu of the text codified at 48 CFR chapter 1 [@CL-CMP-9760; @CL-CMP-9762]. Under RFO FAR 52.217-2, the clause is inserted as prescribed in 17.106(a) [@CL-CTF-1183], cancellation means that the Government is canceling its requirements for all supplies or services in program years subsequent to that in which notice of cancellation is provided [@CL-CTF-1184], and cancellation shall occur by the date or within the time period specified in the Schedule, unless a later date is agreed to, if the Contracting Officer notifies the Contractor that funds are not available for contract performance for any subsequent program year or fails to notify the Contractor that funds are available for performance of the succeeding program year requirement [@CL-CTF-1185; @CL-CTF-1186]. Except for cancellation under the clause or termination under the Default clause, any reduction by the Contracting Officer in the requirements of the contract shall be considered a termination under the Termination for Convenience of the Government clause [@CL-CTF-1187]. If cancellation under the clause occurs, the Contractor will be paid a cancellation charge not to exceed the cancellation ceiling specified in the Schedule as applicable at the time of cancellation, covering only costs reasonably necessary for performance of the contract that would have been equitably amortized over the entire multiyear contract period, and incurred by the Contractor and/or subcontractor, that because of the cancellation are not so amortized, and a reasonable profit or fee on the costs [@CL-CTF-1188; @CL-CTF-1189; @CL-CTF-1190]. The cancellation charge shall be computed and the claim made for it as if the claim were being made under the Termination for Convenience of the Government clause, and the Contractor shall submit the claim promptly, but no later than 1 year from the earlier of the date of notification of the nonavailability of funds or the Schedule date by which notification of the availability of additional funds for the next succeeding program year is required to be issued, unless extensions in writing are granted by the Contracting Officer [@CL-CTF-1191; @CL-CTF-1192; @CL-CTF-1193]. The claim may include reasonable nonrecurring costs (see part 15 of the Federal Acquisition Regulation) that are applicable to and normally would have been amortized in all multiyear requirements, the allocable facility costs, and the specialized work force and learning costs [@CL-CTF-1194; @CL-CTF-1195; @CL-CTF-1196], and shall not include the excluded expenses, already-paid costs, anticipated profit or unearned fee, or, for service contracts, the remaining useful commercial life of facilities [@CL-CTF-1197; @CL-CTF-1198; @CL-CTF-1199]. Paragraph (h) states the same Option clause rules, and paragraph (i) includes quantities added by exercise of the Option clause in the quantity canceled [@CL-CTF-1265; @CL-CTF-1266; @CL-CTF-1267; @CL-CTF-1268].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

The Schedule states a cancellation ceiling of 600,000. The Contracting Officer notifies the Contractor on day 0 that funds are not available for the next program year. The Schedule date by which notification of additional funds is required to be issued is day 90, so the earlier date is day 0. The Contractor has 560,000 of costs that were reasonably necessary for performance and would have been equitably amortized over the multiyear period but are not so amortized because of the cancellation. They include costs of quantities added earlier through the Option clause, which count in the quantity canceled. Of the 560,000, 60,000 was already paid to the Contractor, which the claim leaves out, so 500,000 remains. The claim adds a reasonable profit of 40,000 on those costs. It includes no expenses for performing the canceled work and no anticipated profit on that work.

Answer: the claim is 540,000, which is the 500,000 plus the 40,000 profit, computed as under the termination for convenience clause. It is under the 600,000 ceiling, so the ceiling does not cut it. It is due promptly and no later than day 365, which is 1 year after day 0, unless the Contracting Officer grants an extension in writing. Had the claim come to 700,000, the Contractor would be paid no more than the 600,000 ceiling.

## Never confuse

Except for cancellation under this clause or termination under the Default clause, any reduction by the Contracting Officer in the requirements of the contract shall be considered a termination under the Termination for Convenience of the Government clause [@CL-CTF-2279]. The cancellation charge is computed as if the claim were being made under the Termination for Convenience of the Government clause, and the claim shall not include anticipated profit or unearned fee on the canceled work [@CL-CTF-2284; @CL-CTF-2290]. For how ceilings are set, see [Multiyear cancellation ceilings](../../industrialization/ramp/ind-multiyear-cancellation-ceilings.md); for the option clauses and provisions, see [Option solicitation provisions and contract clauses](ctf-option-clauses-and-provisions.md).
