## Purpose

Shows when a multiyear contract is cancelled, what the cancellation charge may cover, how the ceiling limits it and when the claim is due.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CTF-2276] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.217-2 [@CL-CTF-2276] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CTF-2276] |
| Applies to | Contracts that include the cancellation clause [@CL-CTF-2276] |

Cancellation means that the Government is canceling its requirements for all supplies or services in program years after the one in which notice of cancellation is provided [@CL-CTF-2276]. Cancellation occurs by the date or within the time period specified in the Schedule, unless a later date is agreed to, if the Contracting Officer notifies the Contractor that funds are not available for contract performance for any subsequent program year [@CL-CTF-2277]. The clause also provides for cancellation if the Contracting Officer fails to notify the Contractor that funds are available for performance of the succeeding program year requirement [@CL-CTF-2278]. The cancellation date or period is therefore a Schedule entry and is not fixed by the clause [@CL-CTF-2277].

On cancellation the Contractor is paid a cancellation charge not over the cancellation ceiling specified in the Schedule as applicable at the time of cancellation [@CL-CTF-2280]. The ceiling is a Schedule figure, and the clause does not state it [@CL-CTF-2280]. Apart from a reasonable profit or fee on the costs, the charge covers only costs incurred by the Contractor and/or subcontractor, reasonably necessary for performance of the contract, and that would have been equitably amortized over the entire multiyear contract period [@CL-CTF-2281; @CL-CTF-2283]. Those costs are the ones that, because of the cancellation, are not so amortized [@CL-CTF-2282]. A reasonable profit or fee on the costs is also covered [@CL-CTF-2283].

## How it works

The cancellation charge is computed, and the claim for it made, as if the claim were being made under the Termination for Convenience of the Government clause [@CL-CTF-2284]. The Contractor submits the claim promptly but no later than 1 year from the date of notification of the nonavailability of funds, or from the date specified in the Schedule by which notification of the availability of additional funds for the next succeeding program year is required to be issued [@CL-CTF-2285]. Whichever of those two dates is earlier starts the year, unless the Contracting Officer grants extensions in writing [@CL-CTF-2286]. The second date is a Schedule entry.

What the claim may include [@CL-CTF-2287; @CL-CTF-2288; @CL-CTF-2289]:

- Reasonable nonrecurring costs that are applicable to and normally would have been amortized in all supplies or services that are multiyear requirements [@CL-CTF-2287].
- Allocable portions of the costs of facilities acquired or established for the conduct of the work, but only to the extent that it is impracticable for the Contractor to use the facilities in its commercial work, and only if the costs are not charged to the contract through overhead or otherwise depreciated [@CL-CTF-2288].
- Costs incurred for the assembly, training, and transportation to and from the job site of a specialized work force, and costs not amortized solely because the cancellation had precluded anticipated benefits of Contractor or subcontractor learning [@CL-CTF-2289].

The claim shall not include labor, material or other expenses incurred by the Contractor or subcontractors for performance of the canceled work, any cost already paid to the Contractor, or anticipated profit or unearned fee on the canceled work [@CL-CTF-2290]. For service contracts it also excludes the remaining useful commercial life of facilities [@CL-CTF-2291], which means their commercial utility rather than their physical life, with due consideration given to factors such as location of facilities, their specialized nature, and obsolescence [@CL-CTF-2292].

The contract may include an Option clause whose exercise period is limited to the date in the contract for notification that funds are available for the next succeeding program year [@CL-CTF-2313]. If so, the Contractor agrees not to include in option quantities any costs of a startup or nonrecurring nature that have been fully set forth in the contract [@CL-CTF-2314], and the option quantities reflect only the recurring costs and a reasonable profit or fee necessary to furnish the additional option quantities [@CL-CTF-2315]. Quantities added through the Option clause are included in the quantity canceled when computing allowable cancellation charges [@CL-CTF-2293].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

The Schedule states a cancellation ceiling of 600,000. The Contracting Officer notifies the Contractor on day 0 that funds are not available for the next program year. The Schedule date by which notification of additional funds is required to be issued is day 90, so the earlier date is day 0. The Contractor has 560,000 of costs that were reasonably necessary for performance and would have been equitably amortized over the multiyear period but are not so amortized because of the cancellation. They include costs of quantities added earlier through the Option clause, which count in the quantity canceled. Of the 560,000, 60,000 was already paid to the Contractor, which the claim leaves out, so 500,000 remains. The claim adds a reasonable profit of 40,000 on those costs. It includes no expenses for performing the canceled work and no anticipated profit on that work.

Answer: the claim is 540,000, which is the 500,000 plus the 40,000 profit, computed as under the termination for convenience clause. It is under the 600,000 ceiling, so the ceiling does not cut it. It is due promptly and no later than day 365, which is 1 year after day 0, unless the Contracting Officer grants an extension in writing. Had the claim come to 700,000, the Contractor would be paid no more than the 600,000 ceiling.

## Never confuse

Cancellation is not an ordinary reduction in requirements. Except for cancellation under this clause or termination under the Default clause, any reduction by the Contracting Officer in the requirements is considered a termination for convenience [@CL-CTF-2279]. The cancellation charge is computed like a termination claim but excludes anticipated profit or unearned fee on the canceled work [@CL-CTF-2284; @CL-CTF-2290]. For how ceilings are set, see [Multiyear cancellation ceilings](../../industrialization/ramp/ind-multiyear-cancellation-ceilings.md); for the option clauses and provisions, see [Option solicitation provisions and contract clauses](ctf-option-clauses-and-provisions.md).
