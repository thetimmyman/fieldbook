## Purpose

Shows that a basic agreement and a basic ordering agreement are not contracts, what each must or must not contain, when an order becomes a binding contract, and how order prices are set.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CTF-1235] |
| Codified citation | FAR 16.701 [@CL-CTF-2360; @CL-CTF-2788], FAR 16.702 and FAR 16.703 [@CL-CTF-1235; @CL-CTF-1238] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CTF-1235] |
| Applies to | Basic agreements and basic ordering agreements, neither of which is a contract [@CL-CTF-1235; @CL-CTF-1238] |

Subpart 16.7 prescribes policies and procedures for establishing and using basic agreements and basic ordering agreements [@CL-CTF-2360]. Its scope section refers the reader to 13.303 for blanket purchase agreements and to 35.015(b) for additional coverage of basic agreements with educational institutions and nonprofit organizations [@CL-CTF-2788].

A basic agreement is not a contract [@CL-CTF-1235]. Each basic agreement shall be reviewed annually before the anniversary of its effective date and revised as necessary to conform to the requirements of the regulation [@CL-CTF-1236]. Basic agreements may need to be revised before the annual review due to mandatory statutory requirements, and a basic agreement may be changed only by modifying the agreement itself and not by a contract incorporating the agreement [@CL-CTF-0440]. A basic agreement shall not cite appropriations or obligate funds, state or imply any agreement by the Government to place future contracts or orders with the contractor, or be used in any manner to restrict competition [@CL-CTF-1237].

A basic ordering agreement is not a contract [@CL-CTF-1238]. A basic ordering agreement shall not state or imply any agreement by the Government to place future contracts or orders with the contractor or be used in any manner to restrict competition [@CL-CTF-1262]. Each basic ordering agreement shall describe the method for determining prices to be paid to the contractor for the supplies or services [@CL-CTF-1239], and shall specify the point at which each order becomes a binding contract (e.g., issuance of the order, acceptance of the order in a specified manner, or failure to reject the order within a specified number of days) [@CL-CTF-1239; @CL-CTF-1240].

## How it works

An order under a basic ordering agreement becomes a binding contract at the point the agreement specifies [@CL-CTF-1239; @CL-CTF-1240]. Each basic ordering agreement describes the method for determining prices to be paid to the contractor for the supplies or services [@CL-CTF-1239]. The contracting officer shall neither make any final commitment nor authorize the contractor to begin work on an order under a basic ordering agreement until prices have been established, unless the order establishes a ceiling price limiting the Government's obligation and either the basic ordering agreement provides adequate procedures for timely pricing of the order early in its performance period or the need for the supplies or services is compelling and unusually urgent (i.e., when the Government would be seriously injured, financially or otherwise, if the requirement is not met sooner than would be possible if prices were established before the work began) [@CL-CTF-1241; @CL-CTF-1261; @CL-CTF-0441]. The contracting officer shall proceed with pricing as soon as practical, and in no event shall an entire order be priced retroactively [@CL-CTF-0442]. A basic agreement shall not cite appropriations or obligate funds [@CL-CTF-1237].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A basic ordering agreement says an order becomes binding when the contractor accepts it in writing, and prices are set by an agreed catalog method. The Government sends an order for 200,000 of parts. The answer: the order becomes a binding contract when the contractor accepts it as the agreement specifies, the price follows the catalog method, and no final commitment or work starts until the price is established unless the order has a ceiling price limiting the Government's obligation and one of the two listed conditions applies.

## Never confuse

Neither agreement is a contract [@CL-CTF-1235; @CL-CTF-1238]. A basic agreement shall not state or imply any agreement by the Government to place future contracts or orders with the contractor or be used in any manner to restrict competition [@CL-CTF-1237], and a basic ordering agreement shall not state or imply any agreement by the Government to place future contracts or orders with the contractor or be used in any manner to restrict competition [@CL-CTF-1262]. An indefinite-quantity contract requires the Government to order and the contractor to furnish at least a stated minimum quantity of supplies or services [@CL-CTF-1209]; see [Indefinite-delivery and indefinite-quantity contracts](ctf-indefinite-delivery-and-indefinite-quantity-contracts.md). A basic agreement shall not cite appropriations or obligate funds [@CL-CTF-1237]; for incremental funding see [Contract funding: incremental funding policy](ctf-incremental-funding-policy.md). Who may sign orders and contracts for the Government is on [Contracting officer authority and contracting officer's representatives](../../operating-model/decision-rights/opm-contracting-officer-authority-and-cors.md).
