## Purpose

Shows that a basic agreement and a basic ordering agreement are not contracts, what each must or must not contain, when an order becomes a binding contract, and how order prices are set.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CTF-1235] |
| Codified citation | FAR 16.701 [@CL-CTF-2360; @CL-CTF-2788], FAR 16.702 and FAR 16.703 [@CL-CTF-1235; @CL-CTF-1238] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CTF-1235] |
| Applies to | Basic agreements and basic ordering agreements, neither of which is a contract [@CL-CTF-1235; @CL-CTF-1238] |

Subpart 16.7 prescribes policies and procedures for establishing and using basic agreements and basic ordering agreements [@CL-CTF-2360]. Its scope section refers the reader to 13.303 for blanket purchase agreements and to 35.015(b) for additional coverage of basic agreements with educational institutions and nonprofit organizations [@CL-CTF-2788].

A basic agreement is not a contract [@CL-CTF-1235]. Each basic agreement shall be reviewed annually before the anniversary of its effective date and revised as necessary to conform to the requirements of the regulation [@CL-CTF-1236]. A basic agreement shall not cite appropriations or obligate funds, state or imply any Government agreement to place future contracts or orders with the contractor, or be used in any manner to restrict competition [@CL-CTF-1237].

A basic ordering agreement is not a contract [@CL-CTF-1238]. Each basic ordering agreement shall describe the method for determining prices to be paid to the contractor for the supplies or services, and this is the first of its listed required contents [@CL-CTF-1239]. Another listed required content is a specified point at which each order becomes a binding contract, for example issuance of the order, acceptance in a specified manner, or failure to reject the order within a specified number of days [@CL-CTF-1240; @CL-CTF-1239].

## How it works

An order under a basic ordering agreement becomes a binding contract at the point the agreement specifies [@CL-CTF-1240; @CL-CTF-1239]. Its price is set by the method the agreement describes [@CL-CTF-1239]. The contracting officer shall neither make any final commitment nor authorize the contractor to begin work on an order until prices have been established, unless the order establishes a ceiling price limiting the Government's obligation and either the agreement provides adequate procedures for timely pricing of the order early in its performance period or the need is compelling and unusually urgent [@CL-CTF-1241; @CL-CTF-1261]. A basic agreement shall not cite appropriations or obligate funds [@CL-CTF-1237]. This card does not state the separate review requirement for ordering agreements.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A basic ordering agreement says an order becomes binding when the contractor accepts it in writing, and prices are set by an agreed catalog method. The Government sends an order for 200,000 of parts. The answer: the order becomes a binding contract when the contractor accepts it as the agreement specifies, the price follows the catalog method, and no final commitment or work starts until the price is established unless the order has a ceiling price limiting the Government's obligation and one of the two listed conditions applies.

## Never confuse

Neither agreement is a contract [@CL-CTF-1235; @CL-CTF-1238], and neither promises future work: a basic agreement shall not state or imply any Government agreement to place future contracts or orders [@CL-CTF-1237], and the same limit is stated for a basic ordering agreement [@CL-CTF-1262]. An indefinite-quantity contract, by contrast, must require the Government to order at least a stated minimum [@CL-CTF-1209]; see [Indefinite-delivery and indefinite-quantity contracts](ctf-indefinite-delivery-and-indefinite-quantity-contracts.md). A basic agreement does not obligate funds [@CL-CTF-1237], so a reader should look to the individual contracts or orders for funding terms; for incremental funding see [Contract funding: incremental funding policy](ctf-incremental-funding-policy.md). Who may sign orders and contracts for the Government is on [Contracting officer authority and contracting officer's representatives](../../operating-model/decision-rights/opm-contracting-officer-authority-and-cors.md).
