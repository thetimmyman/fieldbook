## Purpose

Explains the estimated cost ceiling in a contract that carries the Limitation of Cost clause, and the written notice and revised estimate the Contractor owes the Contracting Officer when costs near that ceiling or the total cost is expected to be greater or substantially less than estimated.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CTF-2916] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.232-20 [@CL-CTF-2916] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.232-20 [@CL-CTF-1152] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CTF-2916] |
| Applies to | Contracts in which the clause is inserted under its own prescription [@CL-CTF-2916] |

The parties estimate that performance, exclusive of any fee, will not cost the Government more than the estimated cost in the Schedule or, in a cost-sharing contract, the Government's share of that estimated cost [@CL-CTF-2918]. The Contractor agrees to use its best efforts to perform the work in the Schedule and all contract obligations within the estimated cost, which in a cost-sharing contract includes both the Government's and the Contractor's share of the cost [@CL-CTF-2967].

The Contractor must notify the Contracting Officer in writing whenever it has reason to believe that a condition listed in paragraph (b) exists [@CL-CTF-2968]. One condition is that the costs the Contractor expects to incur in the next 60 days, added to all costs previously incurred, will exceed 75 percent of the estimated cost in the Schedule [@CL-CTF-2919]. The other is that the total cost of performance, exclusive of any fee, will be either greater or substantially less than had been previously estimated [@CL-CTF-2920].

As part of the notification, the Contractor must give the Contracting Officer a revised estimate of the total cost of performing the contract [@CL-CTF-2921].

The codified prescription lets the notice period be varied from 30 to 90 days and the percentage from 75 to 85 percent, so the figures in the contract may differ from 60 days and 75 percent [@CL-CTF-2917]. The codified prescription also lets "Task Order" or another appropriate designation replace "Schedule" wherever that word appears in the clause [@CL-CTF-2966].

## How it works

The notice has two separate triggers. The first asks whether the costs expected in the next 60 days plus all costs previously incurred will exceed 75 percent of the estimated cost in the Schedule, and the second asks whether total cost will be either greater or substantially less than previously estimated [@CL-CTF-2919; @CL-CTF-2920]. Either one calls for the written notice and the revised estimate of total cost [@CL-CTF-2968; @CL-CTF-2921].

Paragraph (d) applies except as required by other provisions of the contract that specifically cite the clause and are stated to be an exception to it [@CL-CTF-2969]. Subject to that, the Government is not obligated to reimburse costs incurred above the estimated cost in the Schedule or, in a cost-sharing contract, above the estimated cost to the Government in the Schedule [@CL-CTF-2922]. Also subject to that, the Contractor is not obligated to continue performance, including actions under the Termination clause, or otherwise incur costs above the estimated cost in the Schedule, until the Contracting Officer notifies the Contractor in writing that the estimated cost has been increased and provides a revised estimated total cost of performing the contract [@CL-CTF-2923; @CL-CTF-2924]. In a cost-sharing contract the increase is allocated in accordance with the formula specified in the Schedule [@CL-CTF-2970].

No notice, communication or representation in any form other than that specified in paragraph (d)(2), or from anyone other than the Contracting Officer, affects the contract's estimated cost to the Government [@CL-CTF-2925]. Without the specified notice, the Government is not obligated to reimburse costs above the estimated cost or, in a cost-sharing contract, above the estimated cost to the Government in the Schedule, whether they were incurred during the course of the contract or as a result of termination [@CL-CTF-2971; @CL-CTF-2972]. What follows an increase, including change orders and termination, is on the [costs beyond the estimate](../funding/ctf-limitation-of-cost-costs-beyond-estimate.md) page.

Effective February 1, 2026, DoD contracting officers shall use the revised FAR Part 52 solicitation provisions and contract clauses published on the Revolutionary FAR Overhaul web page in lieu of the text codified at 48 CFR chapter 1 [@CL-CMP-9760; @CL-CMP-9762]. Under RFO FAR 52.232-20, the clause is inserted as prescribed in 32.706-2(a) [@CL-CTF-1152], the parties estimate that performance of the contract, exclusive of any fee, will not cost the Government more than the estimated cost specified in the Schedule or, if it is a cost-sharing contract, the Government's share of the estimated cost specified in the Schedule [@CL-CTF-1153], and the Contractor agrees to use its best efforts to perform the work specified in the Schedule and all obligations under the contract within the estimated cost, which, if it is a cost-sharing contract, includes both the Government's and the Contractor's share of the cost [@CL-CTF-1154]. The Contractor shall notify the Contracting Officer in writing whenever it has reason to believe that the costs the Contractor expects to incur under the contract in the next 60 days, when added to all costs previously incurred, will exceed 75 percent of the estimated cost specified in the Schedule, or that the total cost for the performance of the contract, exclusive of any fee, will be either greater or substantially less than had been previously estimated [@CL-CTF-1155; @CL-CTF-1156; @CL-CTF-1157], and as part of the notification shall provide the Contracting Officer a revised estimate of the total cost of performing the contract [@CL-CTF-1158]. Except as required by other provisions of the contract, specifically citing and stated to be an exception to the clause, the Government is not obligated to reimburse the Contractor for costs incurred in excess of the estimated cost specified in the Schedule or, if it is a cost-sharing contract, the estimated cost to the Government specified in the Schedule [@CL-CTF-1159; @CL-CTF-1160], the Contractor is not obligated to continue performance under the contract (including actions under the Termination clause) or otherwise incur costs in excess of the estimated cost specified in the Schedule until the Contracting Officer notifies the Contractor in writing that the estimated cost has been increased and provides a revised estimated total cost of performing the contract [@CL-CTF-1161; @CL-CTF-1162], and if it is a cost-sharing contract, the increase shall be allocated in accordance with the formula specified in the Schedule [@CL-CTF-1163]. Under RFO FAR 52.232-20(e), no notice, communication, or representation in any form other than that specified in subparagraph (d)(2) in the clause, or from any person other than the Contracting Officer, shall affect the contract's estimated cost to the Government, and in the absence of the specified notice, the Government is not obligated to reimburse the Contractor for any costs in excess of the estimated cost or, if it is a cost-sharing contract, for any costs in excess of the estimated cost to the Government specified in the Schedule, whether those excess costs were incurred during the course of the contract or as a result of termination [@CL-CTF-1164; @CL-CTF-1165; @CL-CTF-1166]. Change orders shall not be considered an authorization to exceed the estimated cost to the Government specified in the Schedule, unless they contain a statement increasing the estimated cost [@CL-CTF-1169].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A cost-reimbursement contract, not cost-sharing, carries an estimated cost of 500,000 in the Schedule. By mid-year the Contractor has incurred 300,000 and expects another 100,000 in the next 60 days. The two figures total 400,000, which is 80 percent of the estimated cost and above the 375,000 that is 75 percent, so a written notice is due. With the notice the Contractor sends a revised estimate of total cost, say 620,000. A program office employee then tells the Contractor by email to keep working. That email does not change the estimated cost, because a communication in any form other than the specified notice, or from anyone other than the Contracting Officer, does not affect it. Costs reach 540,000. The Government is not obligated to reimburse the 40,000 above 500,000, and the Contractor is not obligated to continue performance, unless another provision of the contract specifically cites the clause and states an exception. The Contractor's non-obligation to continue lasts until the Contracting Officer notifies the Contractor in writing that the estimated cost has been increased and provides a revised estimated total cost of performing the contract.

## Never confuse

Limitation of Cost is measured against the estimated cost specified in the Schedule or, in a cost-sharing contract, the Government's share of the estimated cost specified in the Schedule [@CL-CTF-2918; @CL-CTF-2922]. The separate clause for incrementally funded contracts is on the [limitation of funds](../funding/ctf-limitation-of-funds.md) page, and a shorter card on the notice alone is the [limitation of cost notice](../funding/ctf-limitation-of-cost.md) page.

Sending the notice and the revised estimate does not increase the estimated cost; the Contractor is not obligated to continue performance or incur costs in excess of the estimated cost until the Contracting Officer notifies the Contractor in writing that the estimated cost has been increased and provides a revised estimated total cost, and no notice, communication, or representation in any other form or from any person other than the Contracting Officer affects the contract's estimated cost to the Government [@CL-CTF-2923; @CL-CTF-2924; @CL-CTF-2925].

Change orders shall not be considered an authorization to exceed the estimated cost to the Government specified in the Schedule, unless they contain a statement increasing the estimated cost [@CL-CTF-2928].

Under FAR 52.232-20, the 60-day period may be varied from 30 to 90 days, the 75 percent from 75 to 85 percent, and "Task Order" or other appropriate designation may be substituted for "Schedule" wherever that word appears in the clause [@CL-CTF-2917; @CL-CTF-2966]. Under RFO FAR 32.706-2(a), the 60-day period may be varied from 30 to 90 days, the 75 percent from 75 to 85 percent, and "Task Order" or other appropriate designation may be substituted for "Schedule" wherever that word appears in the clause [@CL-FPA-0270; @CL-FPA-0271].
