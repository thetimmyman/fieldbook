## Purpose

Explains the estimated cost ceiling in a contract that carries the Limitation of Cost clause, and the written notice and revised estimate the Contractor owes the Contracting Officer when costs near that ceiling or the total cost is expected to be greater or substantially less than estimated.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CTF-2916] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.232-20 [@CL-CTF-2916] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.232-20 [@CL-CTF-1152] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CTF-2916] |
| Applies to | Contracts in which the clause is inserted under its own prescription [@CL-CTF-2916] |

The parties estimate that performance, exclusive of any fee, will not cost the Government more than the estimated cost in the Schedule or, in a cost-sharing contract, the Government's share of that estimated cost [@CL-CTF-2918]. The Contractor agrees to use its best efforts to perform the work in the Schedule and all contract obligations within the estimated cost, which in a cost-sharing contract includes both the Government's and the Contractor's share of the cost [@CL-CTF-2967].

The Contractor must notify the Contracting Officer in writing whenever it has reason to believe that a condition listed in paragraph (b) exists [@CL-CTF-2968]. One condition is that the costs the Contractor expects to incur in the next 60 days, added to all costs previously incurred, will exceed 75 percent of the estimated cost in the Schedule [@CL-CTF-2919]. The other is that the total cost of performance, exclusive of any fee, will be either greater or substantially less than had been previously estimated [@CL-CTF-2920].

As part of the notification, the Contractor must give the Contracting Officer a revised estimate of the total cost of performing the contract [@CL-CTF-2921].

The codified prescription lets the notice period be varied from 30 to 90 days and the percentage from 75 to 85 percent, so the figures in the contract may differ from 60 days and 75 percent [@CL-CTF-2917]. The codified prescription also lets "Task Order" or another appropriate designation replace "Schedule" wherever that word appears in the clause [@CL-CTF-2966].

## How it works

The notice has two separate triggers. The first asks whether the costs expected in the next 60 days plus all costs previously incurred will exceed 75 percent of the estimated cost in the Schedule, and the second asks whether total cost will be either greater or substantially less than previously estimated [@CL-CTF-2919; @CL-CTF-2920]. Either one calls for the written notice and the revised estimate of total cost [@CL-CTF-2968; @CL-CTF-2921].

Paragraph (d) applies except as required by other provisions of the contract that specifically cite the clause and are stated to be an exception to it [@CL-CTF-2969]. Subject to that, the Government is not obligated to reimburse costs incurred above the estimated cost in the Schedule or, in a cost-sharing contract, above the estimated cost to the Government in the Schedule [@CL-CTF-2922]. Also subject to that, the Contractor is not obligated to continue performance, including actions under the Termination clause, or otherwise incur costs above the estimated cost in the Schedule, until the Contracting Officer notifies the Contractor in writing that the estimated cost has been increased and provides a revised estimated total cost of performing the contract [@CL-CTF-2923; @CL-CTF-2924]. In a cost-sharing contract the increase is allocated in accordance with the formula specified in the Schedule [@CL-CTF-2970].

No notice, communication or representation in any form other than that specified in paragraph (d)(2), or from anyone other than the Contracting Officer, affects the contract's estimated cost to the Government [@CL-CTF-2925]. Without the specified notice, the Government is not obligated to reimburse costs above the estimated cost or, in a cost-sharing contract, above the estimated cost to the Government in the Schedule, whether they were incurred during the course of the contract or as a result of termination [@CL-CTF-2971; @CL-CTF-2972]. What follows an increase, including change orders and termination, is on the [costs beyond the estimate](../funding/ctf-limitation-of-cost-costs-beyond-estimate.md) page.

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text under Class Deviation 2026-O0038, effective February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. The RFO clause keeps the estimated cost in the Schedule and the best-efforts duty of paragraph (a) [@CL-CTF-1153; @CL-CTF-1154; @CL-CTF-2918; @CL-CTF-2967]. It keeps the written notice, the two conditions in paragraph (b) and the revised estimate of total cost in paragraph (c) [@CL-CTF-1155; @CL-CTF-1156; @CL-CTF-1157; @CL-CTF-1158; @CL-CTF-2968; @CL-CTF-2919; @CL-CTF-2920; @CL-CTF-2921]. It keeps the paragraph (d) rules, which are the exception for provisions that specifically cite the clause, the Government's non-obligation to reimburse costs above the estimated cost, the Contractor's non-obligation to continue until the written increase and revised estimate, and the allocation formula in a cost-sharing contract [@CL-CTF-1159; @CL-CTF-1160; @CL-CTF-1161; @CL-CTF-1162; @CL-CTF-1163; @CL-CTF-2969; @CL-CTF-2922; @CL-CTF-2923; @CL-CTF-2924; @CL-CTF-2970]. Paragraph (e) says that no notice, communication or representation in any form other than that specified in paragraph (d)(2), or from anyone other than the Contracting Officer, affects the estimated cost, and that without the specified notice costs above the estimate are not reimbursed whether incurred during the course of the contract or as a result of termination [@CL-CTF-1164; @CL-CTF-1165; @CL-CTF-1166; @CL-CTF-2925; @CL-CTF-2971; @CL-CTF-2972]. The RFO text points to paragraph (d)(2) with the words "in this clause" where the codified text says "above" [@CL-CTF-1164; @CL-CTF-2925]. The RFO text keeps the rule that a change order does not authorize costs above the estimated cost unless it contains a statement increasing the estimated cost [@CL-CTF-1169; @CL-CTF-2928]. The RFO prescription line prints no sentence that lets the 60-day period or the 75 percent be varied or lets "Task Order" or another designation replace "Schedule" [@CL-CTF-1152; @CL-CTF-2917; @CL-CTF-2966].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A cost-reimbursement contract, not cost-sharing, carries an estimated cost of 500,000 in the Schedule and uses the clause as printed. By mid-year the Contractor has incurred 300,000 and expects another 100,000 in the next 60 days. The two figures total 400,000, which is 80 percent of the estimated cost and above the 375,000 that is 75 percent, so a written notice is due. With the notice the Contractor sends a revised estimate of total cost, say 620,000. A program office employee then tells the Contractor by email to keep working. That email does not change the estimated cost, because a communication in any form other than the specified notice, or from anyone other than the Contracting Officer, does not affect it. Costs reach 540,000. The Government is not obligated to reimburse the 40,000 above 500,000, and the Contractor is not obligated to continue performance, unless another provision of the contract specifically cites the clause and states an exception. The Contractor's non-obligation to continue lasts until the Contracting Officer notifies the Contractor in writing that the estimated cost has been increased and provides a revised estimated total cost of performing the contract.

## Never confuse

Limitation of Cost is not Limitation of Funds. This clause is measured against the estimated cost in the Schedule or, in a cost-sharing contract, the Government's share of it [@CL-CTF-2918; @CL-CTF-2922]. The separate clause for incrementally funded contracts is on the [limitation of funds](../funding/ctf-limitation-of-funds.md) page, and a shorter card on the notice alone is the [limitation of cost notice](../funding/ctf-limitation-of-cost.md) page.

Sending the notice and the revised estimate does not raise the estimated cost. The increase comes through the Contracting Officer's written notice, and a representation in any other form leaves the estimated cost unchanged [@CL-CTF-2924; @CL-CTF-2925].

A change order does not authorize costs above the estimated cost to the Government in the Schedule unless it contains a statement increasing the estimated cost [@CL-CTF-2928].

The codified prescription lets the notice period be varied from 30 to 90 days, the percentage from 75 to 85 percent and "Schedule" be replaced [@CL-CTF-2917; @CL-CTF-2966]. The RFO prescription line prints none of those sentences, although paragraph (b)(1) of the RFO clause still prints 60 days and 75 percent [@CL-CTF-1152; @CL-CTF-1156].
