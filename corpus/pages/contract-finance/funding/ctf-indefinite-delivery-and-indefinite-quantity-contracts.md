## Purpose

Shows the three types of indefinite-delivery contract, how an indefinite-quantity contract sets its minimum and maximum, and when the multiple-award preference applies.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CTF-1207] |
| Codified citation | FAR 16.501-2 and FAR 16.504 [@CL-CTF-1207; @CL-CTF-1209] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CTF-1207] |
| Applies to | Indefinite-delivery contracts, and indefinite-quantity contracts for the minimum, maximum and multiple-award rules [@CL-CTF-1207; @CL-CTF-1209; @CL-CTF-1211] |

There are three types of indefinite-delivery contracts: definite-quantity contracts, requirements contracts, and indefinite-quantity contracts [@CL-CTF-1207]. The appropriate type may be used to acquire supplies or services when the exact times and/or exact quantities of future deliveries are not known at the time of contract award [@CL-CTF-1208].

An indefinite-quantity contract must require the Government to order and the contractor to furnish at least a stated minimum quantity, and the contractor must furnish any additional quantities, if ordered, up to the stated maximum [@CL-CTF-1209]. To ensure that the contract is binding, the minimum quantity must be more than a nominal quantity, but it should not exceed the amount that the Government is fairly certain to order [@CL-CTF-1210]. Except for indefinite-quantity contracts for advisory and assistance services as provided in a separate paragraph, the contracting officer must, to the maximum extent practicable, give preference to making multiple awards of indefinite-quantity contracts [@CL-CTF-1211]. For an indefinite-quantity contract for advisory and assistance services that exceeds 3 years and $20 million, including all options, the contracting officer must make multiple awards unless a listed circumstance applies [@CL-CTF-1252]. That requirement does not apply if the contracting officer or other official designated by the head of the agency determines that the advisory and assistance services are incidental and not a significant component of the contract [@CL-CTF-1253].

## How it works

The minimum is the quantity the Government is bound to order, and the maximum is the most the contractor must furnish if ordered [@CL-CTF-1209]. Among other listed conditions, the contracting officer must not use the multiple award approach if only one contractor is capable of providing performance at the level of quality required because the supplies or services are unique or highly specialized [@CL-CTF-1212], or if the total estimated value of the contract is at or below the simplified acquisition threshold [@CL-CTF-1213; @CL-CTF-1212]. No task or delivery order contract estimated to exceed $150 million, including all options, may be awarded to a single source unless the head of the agency determines in writing that one of the listed findings applies [@CL-CTF-1214]. That determination requirement is not applicable for architect-engineer services awarded pursuant to Subpart 36.6 [@CL-CTF-1254]. This card does not describe definite-quantity contracts beyond naming them as a type.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An indefinite-quantity contract for spare parts states a minimum of 100 units and a maximum of 1,000 units. The Government places a first order for 40 units and a second order for 150 units. The answer: the contract bound the Government to order at least 100 units, which the two orders already exceed, and the contractor must furnish further quantities, if ordered, only up to 1,000 units. Finance checks the contract and each order for the funds actually obligated.

## Never confuse

An indefinite-quantity contract requires the Government to order at least a stated minimum [@CL-CTF-1209], whereas a requirements contract provides for filling all actual purchase requirements of designated Government activities from one contractor [@CL-CTF-1215]; see [Requirements contracts](ctf-requirements-contracts.md). Neither is the same as an incrementally funded contract. Funding limits are on [Contract funding: incremental funding policy](ctf-incremental-funding-policy.md) and [Limitation of Funds clause](ctf-limitation-of-funds.md). The clauses that go into a solicitation for each type are on [Indefinite-delivery solicitation provisions and clauses](ctf-indefinite-delivery-solicitation-clauses.md), and how the contract type is chosen is on [Negotiating and selecting contract type](ctf-contract-type-negotiation-and-selection.md). The effect of contract type on billing and risk is on [Contract types and what they mean for finance](../billing-payment/ctf-contract-types.md).
