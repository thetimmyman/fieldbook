## Purpose

Shows which ordering, order-limitation, definite-quantity, requirements and indefinite-quantity clauses go into a solicitation or contract, and what triggers each.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CTF-1222] |
| Codified citation | FAR 16.506 [@CL-CTF-1222] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CTF-1222] |
| Applies to | Solicitations and contracts that contemplate an indefinite-delivery contract [@CL-CTF-1222; @CL-CTF-1226] |

The clause at 52.216-18, Ordering, is to be inserted in solicitations and contracts when a definite-quantity contract, a requirements contract, or an indefinite-quantity contract is contemplated [@CL-CTF-1222]. A clause substantially the same as the clause at 52.216-19, Order Limitations, is to be inserted in the same cases [@CL-CTF-1223]. The clause at 52.216-20, Definite Quantity, is to be inserted when a definite-quantity contract is contemplated [@CL-CTF-1224].

The clause at 52.216-21, Requirements, is to be inserted when a requirements contract is contemplated [@CL-CTF-1225]. The clause at 52.216-22, Indefinite Quantity, is to be inserted when an indefinite-quantity contract is contemplated [@CL-CTF-1226]. The provision at 52.216-27, Single or Multiple Awards, is to be inserted in solicitations for indefinite-quantity contracts that may result in multiple contract awards, modified to specify the estimated number of awards [@CL-CTF-1227]. That provision is not to be used for advisory and assistance services contracts that exceed 3 years and $20 million, including all options [@CL-CTF-1227; @CL-CTF-1256].

## How it works

The ordering and order-limitations clauses are keyed to all three contract types [@CL-CTF-1222; @CL-CTF-1223]. The definite-quantity clause is tied to a definite-quantity contract [@CL-CTF-1224], the requirements clause to a requirements contract [@CL-CTF-1225], and the indefinite-quantity clause to an indefinite-quantity contract [@CL-CTF-1226]. The provision on single or multiple awards belongs in the solicitation and is modified to state the estimated number of awards [@CL-CTF-1227]. The section also has further sentences and paragraphs, including alternates, a provision for advisory and assistance task-order contracts and an ombudsman clause, that this card does not state, so the list above is not complete.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A solicitation contemplates a requirements contract for repair parts. The ordering clause and the order-limitations clause go in, and so does the requirements clause. The definite-quantity clause and the indefinite-quantity clause do not apply to this solicitation. The answer: the contract type in the solicitation decides which type-specific clause is inserted.

## Never confuse

A provision is not a clause: the single-or-multiple-awards item is prescribed as a provision for solicitations [@CL-CTF-1227], while the other items on this card are prescribed as clauses for solicitations and contracts [@CL-CTF-1222]. The order-limitations clause is prescribed as a clause substantially the same as the one named, not as the exact text [@CL-CTF-1223]. The contract types themselves are on [Indefinite-delivery and indefinite-quantity contracts](ctf-indefinite-delivery-and-indefinite-quantity-contracts.md) and [Requirements contracts](ctf-requirements-contracts.md). What each contract type means for billing and risk is on [Contract types and what they mean for finance](../billing-payment/ctf-contract-types.md).
