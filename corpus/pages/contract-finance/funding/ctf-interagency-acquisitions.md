## Purpose

Shows who must sign and approve what when one agency buys through another, who may charge or pay what under an Economy Act order, and who answers for compliance when the servicing agency awards the contract.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CTF-1328] |
| Codified citation | FAR 17.502-1, 17.502-2 and 17.503 [@CL-CTF-1328; @CL-CTF-1330; @CL-CTF-1336] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CTF-1328] |
| Applies to | Assisted acquisitions, direct acquisitions and Economy Act orders, as each claim states [@CL-CTF-1328; @CL-CTF-1329; @CL-CTF-1330] |

The FAR's general definitions say an assisted acquisition is a type of interagency acquisition where a servicing agency performs acquisition activities on a requesting agency's behalf, such as awarding and administering a contract, task order, or delivery order [@CL-CTF-9392]. The RFO model text of FAR 2.101 defines an assisted acquisition in the same words as the codified definition [@CL-CTF-9442; @CL-CTF-9392].

For assisted acquisitions, prior to the issuance of a solicitation, the servicing agency and the requesting agency shall both sign a written interagency agreement that establishes the general terms and conditions governing the relationship between the parties [@CL-CTF-1328]. For direct acquisitions the requesting agency administers the order, so no written agreement with the servicing agency is required [@CL-CTF-1329].

Each Economy Act order shall be supported by a determination and findings (D&F), which shall state that use of an interagency acquisition is in the best interest of the Government [@CL-CTF-1330]. The D&F shall also state that the supplies or services cannot be obtained as conveniently or economically by contracting directly with a private source, and shall include a statement that at least one of the listed circumstances applies [@CL-CTF-1330; @CL-CTF-1331]. The first listed circumstance is that the acquisition will appropriately be made under an existing contract of the servicing agency, entered into before placement of the order, to meet the requirements of the servicing agency for the same or similar supplies or services [@CL-CTF-1331; @CL-CTF-1332]. The other two are that the servicing agency has the capability or expertise to enter into a contract for such supplies or services that is not available within the requesting agency, and that the servicing agency is specifically authorized by law or regulation to purchase such supplies or services on behalf of other agencies [@CL-CTF-1331; @CL-CTF-1333].

In the payment paragraph of the Economy Act section, the servicing agency may ask the requesting agency, in writing, for advance payment for all or part of the estimated cost of furnishing the supplies or services, and adjustment on the basis of actual costs shall be made as agreed to by the agencies [@CL-CTF-1380]. In no event shall the servicing agency require, or the requesting agency pay, any fee or charge in excess of the actual cost (or estimated cost if the actual cost is not known) of entering into and administering the contract or other agreement under which the order is filled [@CL-CTF-1381].

## How it works

Who decides: the D&F shall be approved by a contracting officer of the requesting agency with authority to contract for the supplies or services to be ordered, or by another official designated by the agency head, except where the servicing agency is not covered by the FAR [@CL-CTF-1334]. In that exception, approval of the D&F may not be delegated below the senior procurement executive of the requesting agency [@CL-CTF-1335]. When an interagency acquisition requires the servicing agency to award a contract, further procedures apply [@CL-CTF-1377], and the servicing agency is responsible for compliance with all other legal or regulatory requirements applicable to the contract, including adequate statutory authority and the competition requirements of part 6 [@CL-CTF-1336]. However, if the servicing agency is not subject to the FAR, the requesting agency shall verify that contracts utilized to meet its requirements contain provisions protecting the Government from inappropriate charges (for example, provisions mandated for FAR agencies by part 31), and that adequate contract administration will be provided [@CL-CTF-9562; @CL-CTF-9563]. This card does not state the rest of the agreement contents, the order contents, the procedures for resolving disagreements, the business-case rules for multi-agency and governmentwide contracts, or the use of research centers.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

Agency A wants a service that Agency B can buy under a contract B already holds. The work is an assisted acquisition, and A orders under the Economy Act. A contracting officer of A with authority to contract for the service signs the D&F.

Answer: before B issues a solicitation, A and B sign a written interagency agreement, and the D&F states the best-interest finding and the existing-contract circumstance. If B were not covered by the FAR, approval of the D&F could not sit below A's senior procurement executive.

## Never confuse

An assisted acquisition needs a written interagency agreement before the solicitation, while a direct acquisition does not, because the requesting agency administers the order [@CL-CTF-1328; @CL-CTF-1329]. The D&F is required for each Economy Act order, and it is approved on the requesting agency side [@CL-CTF-1330; @CL-CTF-1334]. For determinations and findings in general see [Determinations and findings](../../operating-model/decision-rights/opm-determinations-and-findings.md).
