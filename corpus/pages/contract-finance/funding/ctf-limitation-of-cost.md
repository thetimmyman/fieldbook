## Purpose

Shows when a contractor must warn the contracting officer that costs are approaching the estimated cost of a fully funded cost-reimbursement contract.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR clause [@CL-CTF-0003] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.232-20(b)(1) [@CL-CTF-0003] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.232-20(b)(1) [@CL-CTF-0003] |
| Edition date | 1984-04 [@CL-CTF-0003] |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CTF-0003] |
| Applies to | Fully funded cost-reimbursement contracts with the clause [@CL-CTF-0003] |

The contractor must notify the contracting officer when costs expected in the next 60 days, added to costs incurred, will exceed 75 percent of the estimated cost [@CL-CTF-2919; @CL-CTF-0003]. The contractor must also notify the contracting officer when it expects the total cost to be greater, or substantially less, than previously estimated [@CL-CTF-0003]. The prescription lets a contract change both figures, so read the clause in the contract [@CL-CTF-0003].

## How it works

Whenever the contractor has reason to believe a notice point is near, add the costs incurred to date and the costs expected over the notice window, and compare the total with the notice percentage of the estimated cost. When the total crosses it, send the notice with a revised estimate. The government is not obliged to pay costs above the estimated cost, and the contractor is not obliged to keep working past it, unless the contracting officer raises the estimate in writing.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contract has an estimated cost of 4,000,000. Costs incurred are 2,500,000, and 600,000 more is expected over the next 60 days. The total, 3,100,000, is above 75 percent of the estimate, 3,000,000, so the notice is due now.

## Never confuse

Limitation of cost applies to a fully funded contract; limitation of funds applies to an incrementally funded one and is measured against the funds allotted. Funding is the money obligated; the estimated cost is the expected total.
