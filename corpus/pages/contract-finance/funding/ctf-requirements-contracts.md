## Purpose

Shows when a requirements contract is used, what the stated estimated quantity is and is not, and how maximum and order limits and ordering work.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CTF-1215] |
| Codified citation | FAR 16.503 [@CL-CTF-1215] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CTF-1215] |
| Applies to | Requirements contracts [@CL-CTF-1215] |

A requirements contract provides for filling all actual purchase requirements of designated Government activities for supplies or services during a specified contract period from one contractor, with deliveries or performance scheduled by placing orders with the contractor [@CL-CTF-1215]. It may be appropriate for acquiring any supplies or services when the Government anticipates recurring requirements but cannot predetermine the precise quantities of supplies or services that designated Government activities will need during a definite period [@CL-CTF-1220]. A separate paragraph limits the use of requirements contracts for advisory and assistance services, and this card does not state its terms [@CL-CTF-1255].

A realistic estimated total quantity shall be stated in the solicitation and resulting contract, and that estimate is not a representation to an offeror or contractor that the estimated quantity will be required or ordered or that conditions affecting requirements will be stable or normal [@CL-CTF-1216]. The contracting officer may obtain the estimate from records of previous requirements and consumption, or by other means, and should base the estimate on the most current information available [@CL-CTF-1217].

## How it works

The contract shall state, if feasible, the maximum limit of the contractor's obligation to deliver and the Government's obligation to order [@CL-CTF-1218]. The contract may also specify maximum or minimum quantities that the Government may order under each individual order and the maximum that it may order during a specified period of time [@CL-CTF-1219]. The printed requirements-contract section does not set a minimum total quantity that the Government must order. No requirements contract estimated to exceed $150 million, including all options, may be awarded to a single source unless a determination is executed in accordance with the single-award determination paragraph of the indefinite-quantity section [@CL-CTF-1221]. That paragraph says its determination requirement is not applicable for architect-engineer services awarded pursuant to Subpart 36.6 [@CL-CTF-1254].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A requirements contract for filters states an estimate of 5,000 units a year and a maximum of 8,000 units. The Government orders 3,000 units in the year. The answer: the estimate was not a representation that 5,000 units would be ordered, so finance plans on the orders actually placed and treats 8,000 units as the limit of both sides' obligations.

## Never confuse

The stated estimate, the maximum limit and the per-order limits are three different things. The estimate is a realistic total quantity stated in the solicitation and contract [@CL-CTF-1216], the maximum limit caps the obligations to deliver and to order [@CL-CTF-1218], and the per-order and per-period limits cap single orders [@CL-CTF-1219]. A requirements contract is also not an indefinite-quantity contract, which must require the Government to order at least a stated minimum [@CL-CTF-1209]; see [Indefinite-delivery and indefinite-quantity contracts](ctf-indefinite-delivery-and-indefinite-quantity-contracts.md). The clauses for a solicitation that contemplates a requirements contract are on [Indefinite-delivery solicitation provisions and clauses](ctf-indefinite-delivery-solicitation-clauses.md). Funding limits are on [Contract funding: incremental funding policy](ctf-incremental-funding-policy.md), and the effect of contract type on billing is on [Contract types and what they mean for finance](../billing-payment/ctf-contract-types.md).
