## Purpose

Shows when a requirements contract is used, what the stated estimated quantity is and is not, and how maximum and order limits and ordering work.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CTF-1215] |
| Codified citation | FAR 16.503 [@CL-CTF-1215] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CTF-1215] |
| Applies to | Requirements contracts [@CL-CTF-1215] |

A requirements contract provides for filling all actual purchase requirements of designated Government activities for supplies or services during a specified contract period (from one contractor), with deliveries or performance to be scheduled by placing orders with the contractor [@CL-CTF-1215]. It may be appropriate for acquiring any supplies or services when the Government anticipates recurring requirements but cannot predetermine the precise quantities of supplies or services that designated Government activities will need during a definite period [@CL-CTF-1220]. Under FAR 16.503(d), except as provided in paragraph (d)(2), no solicitation for a requirements contract for advisory and assistance services in excess of three years and $20 million (including all options) may be issued unless the contracting officer or other official designated by the head of the agency determines in writing that the services required are so unique or highly specialized that it is not practicable to make multiple awards using the procedures in FAR 16.504 [@CL-CTF-1255; @CL-CTF-1283; @CL-CTF-1284]. That limitation is not applicable to an acquisition of supplies or services that includes the acquisition of advisory and assistance services, if the contracting officer or other official designated by the head of the agency determines that the advisory and assistance services are necessarily incident to, and not a significant component of, the contract [@CL-CTF-1284; @CL-CTF-1285].

A realistic estimated total quantity shall be stated in the solicitation and resulting contract, and that estimate is not a representation to an offeror or contractor that the estimated quantity will be required or ordered, or that conditions affecting requirements will be stable or normal [@CL-CTF-1216]. The contracting officer may obtain the estimate from records of previous requirements and consumption, or by other means, and should base the estimate on the most current information available [@CL-CTF-1217].

## How it works

The contract shall state, if feasible, the maximum limit of the contractor's obligation to deliver and the Government's obligation to order [@CL-CTF-1218]. The contract may also specify maximum or minimum quantities that the Government may order under each individual order and the maximum that it may order during a specified period of time [@CL-CTF-1219]. No requirements contract in an amount estimated to exceed $150 million (including all options) may be awarded to a single source unless a determination is executed in accordance with FAR 16.504(c)(1)(ii)(D) [@CL-CTF-1221]. That paragraph states that the requirement for a determination for a single-award contract greater than $150 million is in addition to any applicable requirements of Subpart 6.3 and is not applicable for architect-engineer services awarded pursuant to Subpart 36.6 [@CL-CTF-1254].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A requirements contract for filters states an estimate of 5,000 units a year and a maximum of 8,000 units. The Government orders 3,000 units in the year. The answer: the estimate was not a representation that 5,000 units would be ordered, so finance plans on the orders actually placed and treats 8,000 units as the limit of both sides' obligations.

## Never confuse

The realistic estimated total quantity is stated in the solicitation and resulting contract [@CL-CTF-1216], the contract shall state, if feasible, the maximum limit of the contractor's obligation to deliver and the Government's obligation to order [@CL-CTF-1218], and the contract may also specify maximum or minimum quantities that the Government may order under each individual order and the maximum that it may order during a specified period of time [@CL-CTF-1219]. A requirements contract is also not an indefinite-quantity contract, which must require the Government to order and the contractor to furnish at least a stated minimum quantity [@CL-CTF-1209]; see [Indefinite-delivery and indefinite-quantity contracts](ctf-indefinite-delivery-and-indefinite-quantity-contracts.md). The clauses for a solicitation that contemplates a requirements contract are on [Indefinite-delivery solicitation provisions and clauses](ctf-indefinite-delivery-solicitation-clauses.md). Funding limits are on [Contract funding: incremental funding policy](ctf-incremental-funding-policy.md), and the effect of contract type on billing is on [Contract types and what they mean for finance](../billing-payment/ctf-contract-types.md).
