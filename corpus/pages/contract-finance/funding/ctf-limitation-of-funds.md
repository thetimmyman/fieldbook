## Purpose

Explains how an incrementally funded contract limits the work the contractor must perform to the funds actually allotted, and which notices apply.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CTF-0114] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.232-22 [@CL-CTF-0114] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.232-22 [@CL-CTF-1171] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CTF-0114] |
| Applies to | Contracts in which the Limitation of Funds clause is inserted as prescribed in FAR 32.706-2(b) [@CL-CTF-0114] |

The parties estimate that performance will not cost the Government more than the estimated cost stated in the Schedule or, for a cost-sharing contract, the Government's share of that cost [@CL-CTF-0115]. The parties contemplate that the Government will allot funds incrementally up to the full estimated cost, exclusive of any fee, and the contractor agrees to perform, or have performed, work up to the point at which the total amount paid and payable approximates but does not exceed the total amount actually allotted [@CL-CTF-0116; @CL-CTF-0117].

Except as required by other contract provisions that specifically cite and are stated to be an exception to the clause, the Government is not obligated to reimburse costs incurred above the total amount allotted [@CL-CTF-0122].

## How it works

In the clause as printed, when the contractor has reason to believe that the cost it expects in the next 60 days, added to cost already incurred, will exceed 75 percent of the amount so far allotted, plus its share where the contract shares cost, it must notify the contracting officer in writing [@CL-CTF-0118; @CL-CTF-0276].

In the clause as printed, sixty days before the end of the period in the Schedule the contractor must notify the contracting officer in writing of the estimated additional funds, if any, required to continue timely performance and when they will be required [@CL-CTF-0119; @CL-CTF-0277].

If, after notification, funds are not allotted by the end of the scheduled period or another agreed-upon date, then upon the contractor's written request the contracting officer will terminate the contract on that date [@CL-CTF-0120]. If the contractor estimates that its funds last longer, it may specify a later date in its request, and the contracting officer may terminate on that later date [@CL-CTF-0483].

The codified prescription permits the 60-day period to be varied from 30 to 90 days and the 75 percent figure from 75 to 85 percent [@CL-CTF-0121].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text under Class Deviation 2026-O0038, effective February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. The RFO clause keeps the parties' estimate in paragraph (a) [@CL-CTF-1172; @CL-CTF-0115]. In paragraph (b) it says the Government will allot additional funds incrementally up to the fully estimated cost to the Government specified in the Schedule, exclusive of any fee, where the codified text says the full estimated cost [@CL-CTF-1173; @CL-CTF-0116], and it keeps the Contractor's agreement to perform up to the point at which the total amount paid and payable approximates but does not exceed the total amount actually allotted [@CL-CTF-1174; @CL-CTF-0117]. It keeps the written notice when the costs expected in the next 60 days, added to costs already incurred, will exceed 75 percent of the allotted amount, or of the allotted amount plus the Contractor's corresponding share in a cost-sharing contract [@CL-CTF-1175; @CL-CTF-1176; @CL-CTF-0118; @CL-CTF-0276]. It keeps the written notice sixty days before the end of the period in the Schedule of the estimated additional funds, if any, and when they will be required [@CL-CTF-1177; @CL-CTF-1178; @CL-CTF-0119; @CL-CTF-0277]. It keeps termination on that date on the Contractor's written request when funds are not allotted by the end of the period or another agreed-upon date, and the option to specify a later date [@CL-CTF-1179; @CL-CTF-1180; @CL-CTF-1181; @CL-CTF-0120; @CL-CTF-0483]. It keeps the Government's non-obligation to reimburse costs above the total amount allotted, except as required by other provisions that specifically cite the clause and are stated to be an exception to it [@CL-CTF-1182; @CL-CTF-0122]. The RFO prescription line prints no sentence that lets the 60-day period or the 75 percent be varied [@CL-CTF-1171; @CL-CTF-0121].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An incrementally funded contract has 500,000 allotted against an estimated cost of 1,200,000. Costs of 300,000 are incurred and the contractor expects 100,000 more in the next 60 days, so the projected 400,000 exceeds 75 percent of the 500,000 allotted and the written notice is due.

## Never confuse

The Limitation of Funds clause ties the contractor's effort to the amount actually allotted and requires notices as that amount is used up, and, unless another provision specifically cites and is stated to be an exception to the clause, it does not oblige the Government to reimburse cost above the allotted amount [@CL-CTF-0117; @CL-CTF-0122].

A related but separate clause is covered on the [limitation of cost](../funding/ctf-limitation-of-cost.md) page.

The codified prescription permits the 60-day period to be varied from 30 to 90 days and the 75 percent figure from 75 to 85 percent [@CL-CTF-0121]. The RFO prescription line prints neither sentence, although paragraph (c) of the RFO clause still prints 60 days and 75 percent and paragraph (d) prints sixty days [@CL-CTF-1171; @CL-CTF-1175; @CL-CTF-1177].
