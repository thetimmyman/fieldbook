## Purpose

Names each option provision and clause and the trigger that prescribes it, so a reader can match the clauses in a contract to the option type it contains.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CTF-1319] |
| Codified citation | FAR 17.208 [@CL-CTF-1319] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CTF-1319] |
| Applies to | Solicitations and contracts that include option clauses, as each paragraph of the section states [@CL-CTF-1319] |

Three solicitation provisions deal with evaluation. A provision substantially the same as 52.217-3, Evaluation Exclusive of Options, is inserted in solicitations when the solicitation includes an option clause and does not include one of the other two provisions [@CL-CTF-1319]. A provision substantially the same as 52.217-4, Evaluation of Options Exercised at Time of Contract Award, is inserted in solicitations when the solicitation includes an option clause [@CL-CTF-1320]; the other conditions are that the contracting officer has determined that there is a reasonable likelihood that the option will be exercised and that the option may be exercised at the time of contract award [@CL-CTF-1375]. A provision substantially the same as 52.217-5, Evaluation of Options, has four listed conditions: the solicitation contains an option clause, and an option is not to be exercised at the time of contract award [@CL-CTF-1321]; and a firm-fixed-price contract, a fixed-price contract with economic price adjustment, or other type of contract approved under agency procedures is contemplated, and the contracting officer has determined that there is a reasonable likelihood that the option will be exercised [@CL-CTF-1322]. The section adds a writing requirement for sealed bids, which this card does not state.

Four clauses deal with the option itself. A clause substantially the same as 52.217-6, Option for Increased Quantity, is inserted in solicitations and contracts other than those for services when the inclusion of an option is appropriate [@CL-CTF-1323]; a further condition is that the option quantity is expressed as a percentage of the basic contract quantity or as an additional quantity of a specific line item [@CL-CTF-1376]. A clause substantially the same as 52.217-7, Option for Increased Quantity, Separately Priced Line Item, is inserted in solicitations and contracts other than those for services when the inclusion of an option is appropriate [@CL-CTF-1324]; a further condition is that the option quantity is identified as a separately priced line item having the same nomenclature as a corresponding line item [@CL-CTF-1325]. A clause substantially the same as 52.217-8, Options to Extend Services, is inserted in solicitations and contracts for services when the inclusion of an option is appropriate [@CL-CTF-1326]. A clause substantially the same as 52.217-9, Option to Extend the Term of the Contract, is inserted in solicitations and contracts when the inclusion of an option is appropriate and it is necessary to include in the contract any or all of the items listed in that paragraph [@CL-CTF-1327]. The listed items are a requirement of preliminary written notice of intent to extend the contract, a statement that an extension of the contract includes an extension of the option, and a specified limitation on the total duration of the contract [@CL-CTF-1393].

## How it works

The provisions go into solicitations and deal with evaluation; the clauses go into solicitations and contracts and deal with the option itself [@CL-CTF-1319; @CL-CTF-1323]. The evaluation provisions turn on whether an option may be exercised at award and on the contracting officer's determination of likelihood [@CL-CTF-1375; @CL-CTF-1321]. The increased-quantity clauses exclude contracts for services and turn on how the option quantity is written, the services extension clause is for service contracts, and the term extension clause turns on the listed items [@CL-CTF-1323; @CL-CTF-1324; @CL-CTF-1325; @CL-CTF-1326; @CL-CTF-1327].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A supply contract is firm-fixed-price. It has an option for 20 percent of the basic contract quantity, the solicitation contains an option clause, and no option will be exercised at award. The contracting officer has determined that exercise is reasonably likely, and the inclusion of the option is appropriate.

Answer: the evaluation provision for options that will not be exercised at award is prescribed, because the listed conditions are met. The increased-quantity clause for a percentage option is prescribed, because the quantity is expressed as a percentage of the basic contract quantity, the contract is not for services and the inclusion of an option is appropriate.

## Never confuse

A provision belongs to the solicitation and deals with evaluation, while a clause belongs to the solicitation and the contract and deals with the option itself [@CL-CTF-1319; @CL-CTF-1323]. The two increased-quantity clauses differ in how the option quantity is written: as a percentage or additional quantity of a specific line item, or as a separately priced line item with matching nomenclature [@CL-CTF-1376; @CL-CTF-1325]. The clause to extend services, for service contracts, is not the same as the clause to extend the term, which is inserted when it is necessary to include any or all of the listed items [@CL-CTF-1326; @CL-CTF-1327]. For price adjustment see [Economic price adjustment, labor and material](../billing-payment/ctf-economic-price-adjustment-labor-and-material.md). For when options are used, what a solicitation and a contract state, and how an option is exercised, see [Use of options](ctf-use-of-options.md), [Options in solicitations and contracts](ctf-options-in-solicitations-and-contracts.md) and [Exercise of options](ctf-exercise-of-options.md).
