## Purpose

Shows what the Limitation of Cost clause does when costs pass the estimated cost: the reimbursement and performance limits, how a written increase works, and where change orders and termination leave the ceiling.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CTF-2916] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.232-20 [@CL-CTF-2916] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.232-20 [@CL-CTF-1152] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CTF-2916] |
| Applies to | Contracts in which the clause is inserted under its own prescription [@CL-CTF-2916] |

Paragraph (d) applies except as required by other provisions of the contract that specifically cite the clause and are stated to be an exception to it [@CL-CTF-2969]. Subject to that, the Government is not obligated to reimburse costs incurred above the estimated cost in the Schedule or, in a cost-sharing contract, above the estimated cost to the Government in the Schedule [@CL-CTF-2922].

If the estimated cost in the Schedule is increased, any costs the Contractor incurs before the increase that exceed the previously estimated cost are allowable to the same extent as if incurred afterward [@CL-CTF-2926], unless the Contracting Officer issues a termination or other notice directing that the increase is solely to cover termination or other specified expenses [@CL-CTF-2927].

## How it works

Subject to the exception in paragraph (d), the Contractor is not obligated to continue performance, including actions under the Termination clause, or otherwise incur costs above the estimated cost in the Schedule, until the Contracting Officer notifies the Contractor in writing that the estimated cost has been increased and provides a revised estimated total cost of performing the contract [@CL-CTF-2969; @CL-CTF-2923; @CL-CTF-2924]. In a cost-sharing contract the increase is allocated in accordance with the formula specified in the Schedule [@CL-CTF-2970].

Other forms do not change the estimate: no notice, communication or representation in any form other than that specified in paragraph (d)(2), or from anyone other than the Contracting Officer, affects the contract's estimated cost to the Government [@CL-CTF-2925]. In the absence of the specified notice, the Government is not obligated to reimburse costs above the estimated cost or, in a cost-sharing contract, above the estimated cost to the Government in the Schedule [@CL-CTF-2971]. That holds whether the excess costs were incurred during the course of the contract or as a result of termination [@CL-CTF-2972].

When the written increase arrives, excess costs incurred earlier are allowable as if incurred afterward, except where the Contracting Officer's termination or other notice directs that the increase is solely to cover termination or other specified expenses [@CL-CTF-2926; @CL-CTF-2927].

If the contract is terminated or the estimated cost is not increased, the Government and the Contractor must negotiate an equitable distribution of all property produced or purchased under the contract, based upon the share of costs incurred by each [@CL-CTF-2929].

A change order is not an authorization to exceed the estimated cost to the Government in the Schedule unless it contains a statement increasing the estimated cost [@CL-CTF-2928]. The related clauses on changes are on the [notification of changes](../../close-revrec/revenue/cls-notification-of-changes.md) and [changes in cost-reimbursement contracts](../../close-revrec/revenue/cls-changes-cost-reimbursement.md) pages.

Effective February 1, 2026, DoD contracting officers shall use the revised FAR Part 52 solicitation provisions and contract clauses published on the Revolutionary FAR Overhaul web page in lieu of the text codified at 48 CFR chapter 1 [@CL-CMP-9760; @CL-CMP-9762]. Under RFO FAR 52.232-20, except as required by other provisions of the contract, specifically citing and stated to be an exception to the clause, the Government is not obligated to reimburse the Contractor for costs incurred in excess of the estimated cost specified in the Schedule or, if it is a cost-sharing contract, the estimated cost to the Government specified in the Schedule [@CL-CTF-1159; @CL-CTF-1160]. The Contractor is not obligated to continue performance under the contract (including actions under the Termination clause of the contract) or otherwise incur costs in excess of the estimated cost specified in the Schedule, until the Contracting Officer notifies the Contractor in writing that the estimated cost has been increased and provides a revised estimated total cost of performing the contract [@CL-CTF-1161; @CL-CTF-1162], and if it is a cost-sharing contract, the increase shall be allocated in accordance with the formula specified in the Schedule [@CL-CTF-1163]. Under RFO FAR 52.232-20(e), no notice, communication, or representation in any form other than that specified in subparagraph (d)(2) in the clause, or from any person other than the Contracting Officer, shall affect the contract's estimated cost to the Government, and in the absence of the specified notice, the Government is not obligated to reimburse the Contractor for any costs in excess of the estimated cost or, if it is a cost-sharing contract, for any costs in excess of the estimated cost to the Government specified in the Schedule, whether those excess costs were incurred during the course of the contract or as a result of termination [@CL-CTF-1164; @CL-CTF-1165; @CL-CTF-1166]. If the estimated cost specified in the Schedule is increased, any costs the Contractor incurs before the increase that are in excess of the previously estimated cost shall be allowable to the same extent as if incurred afterward, unless the Contracting Officer issues a termination or other notice directing that the increase is solely to cover termination or other specified expenses [@CL-CTF-1167; @CL-CTF-1168]. Change orders shall not be considered an authorization to exceed the estimated cost to the Government specified in the Schedule, unless they contain a statement increasing the estimated cost [@CL-CTF-1169], and if the contract is terminated or the estimated cost is not increased, the Government and the Contractor shall negotiate an equitable distribution of all property produced or purchased under the contract, based upon the share of costs incurred by each [@CL-CTF-1170].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A cost-reimbursement contract, not cost-sharing, carries an estimated cost of 800,000 in the Schedule. The Contractor incurs 820,000 before any increase and sends the notice with a revised estimate of 900,000. Until the Contracting Officer's written notice, the Government is not obligated to reimburse the 20,000 above 800,000, unless another provision of the contract specifically cites the clause as an exception. The Contracting Officer then notifies the Contractor in writing that the estimated cost has been increased to 900,000 and provides a revised estimated total cost of performing the contract. The notice does not direct that the increase is solely to cover termination expenses, so the 20,000 incurred before the increase is allowable as if incurred afterward. Had the notice directed that the increase was solely to cover termination expenses, that rule would not pick up the 20,000. Later a change order adds work without any statement increasing the estimated cost. It does not authorize costs above 900,000.

## Never confuse

A change order differs from the written increase rather than matching it: change orders shall not be considered an authorization to exceed the estimated cost to the Government specified in the Schedule, unless they contain a statement increasing the estimated cost [@CL-CTF-2928].

In the absence of the specified notice, the Government is not obligated to reimburse the Contractor for any costs in excess of the estimated cost or, if it is a cost-sharing contract, for any costs in excess of the estimated cost to the Government specified in the Schedule, whether those excess costs were incurred during the course of the contract or as a result of termination rather than only during performance [@CL-CTF-2971; @CL-CTF-2972].

When the estimated cost specified in the Schedule is increased, the rule differs rather than repeating the no-notice one: any costs the Contractor incurs before the increase that are in excess of the previously estimated cost shall be allowable to the same extent as if incurred afterward, unless the Contracting Officer issues a termination or other notice directing that the increase is solely to cover termination or other specified expenses [@CL-CTF-2926; @CL-CTF-2927].
