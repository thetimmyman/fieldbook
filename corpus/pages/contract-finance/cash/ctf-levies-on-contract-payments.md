## Purpose

Explains contractor notification duties and DoD evaluation standards when continuous tax levies are imposed on contract payments.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-CTF-0776] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 252.232-7010 [@CL-CTF-0776] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CTF-0776] |
| Applies to | Contracts that use the Levies on Contract Payments clause as prescribed in DFARS 232.7102 [@CL-CTF-0776] |

The clause at DFARS 252.232-7010, Levies on Contract Payments, is used as prescribed in DFARS 232.7102 [@CL-CTF-0776]. Under paragraph (a) of the clause, section 6331(h) of title 26 of the United States Code authorizes the Internal Revenue Service (IRS) to continuously levy up to 100 percent of contract payments, up to the amount of tax debt [@CL-CTF-0777].

When a levy is imposed on a payment under the contract and the contractor believes that the levy may result in an inability to perform the contract, the contractor shall promptly notify the Procuring Contracting Officer in writing, with a copy to the Administrative Contracting Officer, and shall provide: (1) the total dollar amount of the levy, (2) a statement that the contractor believes that the levy may result in an inability to perform the contract, including rationale and adequate supporting documentation, and (3) advice as to whether the inability to perform may adversely affect national security, including rationale and adequate supporting documentation [@CL-CTF-0778; @CL-CTF-9570; @CL-CTF-0868; @CL-CTF-9571].

## How it works

DoD shall promptly review the contractor's assessment, and the Procuring Contracting Officer shall provide a written notification to the contractor [@CL-CTF-0869]. That written notification includes: (1) a statement as to whether DoD agrees that the levy may result in an inability to perform the contract [@CL-CTF-9528], and (2)(i) if the levy may result in an inability to perform the contract and the lack of performance will adversely affect national security, the total amount of the monies collected that should be returned to the contractor, or (2)(ii) if the levy may result in an inability to perform the contract but will not impact national security, a recommendation that the contractor promptly notify the IRS to attempt to resolve the tax situation [@CL-CTF-0779; @CL-CTF-9529; @CL-CTF-0870]. Any DoD determination under the clause is not subject to appeal under the Contract Disputes Act [@CL-CTF-0780].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

The IRS levies 100 percent of a 200,000 payment under a defense contract [@CL-CTF-0777]. The contractor promptly notifies the Procuring Contracting Officer in writing, with a copy to the Administrative Contracting Officer, and provides the total dollar amount of the levy, a statement with rationale and adequate supporting documentation that the levy may result in an inability to perform the contract, and advice with rationale and adequate supporting documentation that the inability to perform may adversely affect national security [@CL-CTF-0778; @CL-CTF-9570; @CL-CTF-0868; @CL-CTF-9571]. DoD promptly reviews the assessment, agrees that the levy may result in an inability to perform and that the lack of performance will adversely affect national security, and the Procuring Contracting Officer provides a written notification stating that 150,000 of the monies collected should be returned to the contractor [@CL-CTF-0869; @CL-CTF-9528; @CL-CTF-0779].

## Never confuse

Under DFARS 252.232-7010, section 6331(h) of title 26 of the United States Code authorizes the IRS to continuously levy up to 100 percent of contract payments, up to the amount of tax debt [@CL-CTF-0777], and any DoD determination under the clause is not subject to appeal under the Contract Disputes Act [@CL-CTF-0780]. For payment timing see [Prompt payment](../billing-payment/ctf-prompt-payment.md), and for tax costs see [Federal, state, and local taxes](../billing-payment/ctf-federal-state-local-taxes.md).
