## Purpose

Explains what a designated agency and a no-setoff commitment are, and how far the protection of an assignee of claims reaches.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CTF-3574] |
| Codified citation | FAR 32.801 and 32.804 [@CL-CTF-3574; @CL-CTF-3576] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 32.800 [@CL-CTF-4087]; RFO FAR 32.801 [@CL-CTF-4088; @CL-CTF-4089]; RFO FAR 32.803 [@CL-CTF-4097; @CL-CTF-4098; @CL-CTF-4099]; RFO FAR 32.804 [@CL-CTF-4090; @CL-CTF-4091; @CL-CTF-4092; @CL-CTF-4093; @CL-CTF-4094; @CL-CTF-4095; @CL-CTF-4096] |

The assignment of claims subpart prescribes policies and procedures for the assignment of claims under the Assignment of Claims Act of 1940, as amended, which the subpart calls the Act [@CL-CTF-3686]. In that subpart, designated agency means any department or agency of the executive branch of the United States Government [@CL-CTF-3574]. A no-setoff commitment means a contractual undertaking that, to the extent the Act permits, payments by the designated agency to the assignee under an assignment of claims will not be reduced to liquidate the indebtedness of the contractor to the Government [@CL-CTF-3575].

No payment the Government makes to the assignee under a contract assigned in accordance with the Act may be recovered on account of any liability of the contractor to the Government [@CL-CTF-3576]. That immunity applies whether the liability of the contractor arises from the assigned contract or independently of it [@CL-CTF-3577].

## How it works

The immunity concerns payments already made to the assignee [@CL-CTF-3576; @CL-CTF-3577]. The no-setoff commitment concerns contract payments still to be made [@CL-CTF-3578]. Except as the exceptions below provide, a no-setoff commitment entitles the assignee to receive contract payments free of reduction or setoff for any liability of the contractor to the Government arising independently of the contract [@CL-CTF-3578]. It also protects against liabilities arising from the assigned contract for renegotiation and fines [@CL-CTF-3579], penalties other than amounts that may be collected or withheld under, or for failure to comply with, the terms of the contract [@CL-CTF-3580], and taxes or social security contributions and their withholding or nonwithholding [@CL-CTF-3581].

The commitment is not absolute. A setoff may be appropriate even though the assigned contract includes a no-setoff commitment, for example when the assignee has neither made a loan under the assignment nor made a commitment to do so [@CL-CTF-3582]. A setoff may also be appropriate to the extent that the amount due on the contract exceeds the amount of any loans made or expected to be made under a firm commitment for financing [@CL-CTF-3583].

The head of the agency decides whether a contract may carry the commitment. A contract of a designated agency, except a contract under which full payment has been made, may include a no-setoff commitment only when the head of the agency makes a determination of need [@CL-CTF-3415]. The determination must follow the Presidential delegation of authority dated October 3, 1995, and the commitment may be included only after the determination is published in the Federal Register [@CL-CTF-3416]. Where an assigned contract has no no-setoff commitment, the Government may apply against payments to the assignee any liability of the contractor arising independently of the assigned contract, if the liability existed when notice of the assignment was received [@CL-CTF-3495].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 32 text in place of the codified text from February 1, 2026 [@CL-CTF-4046]. RFO FAR 32.800 keeps the scope of the subpart: it prescribes policies and procedures for the assignment of claims under the Assignment of Claims Act of 1940, referred to as the Act [@CL-CTF-4087]. RFO FAR 32.801 keeps the definitions of designated agency, any department or agency of the executive branch of the United States Government (see 32.803(d)) [@CL-CTF-4088], and of no-setoff commitment [@CL-CTF-4089]. RFO FAR 32.804(a) keeps the immunity of the assignee: no payments made by the Government to the assignee under any contract assigned in accordance with the Act may be recovered on account of any liability of the contractor to the Government [@CL-CTF-4090], and the immunity is effective whether the contractor's liability arises from or independently of the assigned contract [@CL-CTF-4091]. RFO FAR 32.804(b) keeps the no-setoff entitlement: except as provided in paragraph (c), the inclusion of a no-setoff commitment in an assigned contract entitles the assignee to receive contract payments free of reduction or setoff for the liabilities that paragraph (b) lists, the first being any liability of the contractor to the Government arising independently of the contract [@CL-CTF-4092]. The second item covers liabilities arising from the assigned contract, including renegotiation under any statute or contract clause and fines [@CL-CTF-4093], and penalties (exclusive of amounts that may be collected or withheld from the contractor under, or for failure to comply with, the terms of the contract), taxes or social security contributions, and withholding or nonwithholding of taxes or social security contributions [@CL-CTF-4094]. RFO FAR 32.804(c) keeps the two examples where a setoff may be appropriate even though the assigned contract includes a no-setoff commitment: when the assignee has neither made a loan under the assignment nor made a commitment to do so [@CL-CTF-4095], and to the extent that the amount due on the contract exceeds the amount of any loans made or expected to be made under a firm commitment for financing [@CL-CTF-4096]. RFO FAR 32.803(d) keeps the rule that any contract of a designated agency (see FAR 32.801), except a contract under which full payment has been made, may include a no-setoff commitment only when the head of the agency makes a determination of need [@CL-CTF-4097], in accordance with the Presidential delegation of authority dated October 3, 1995, and after the determination has been published in the Federal Register [@CL-CTF-4098]. RFO FAR 32.803(e) keeps the rule that, when an assigned contract does not include a no-setoff commitment, the Government may apply against payments to the assignee any liability of the contractor arising independently of the assigned contract if the liability existed at the time notice of the assignment was received [@CL-CTF-4099], even though that liability had not yet matured so as to be due and payable [@CL-CTF-4658].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor assigns claims under a contract to a bank, and the contract carries a no-setoff commitment. The bank has made loans of 600,000 under a firm commitment, and 1,000,000 is due on the contract. The Government claims 50,000 from the contractor under an unrelated contract, a liability arising independently of the assigned contract. Subject to the exceptions for some circumstances, the commitment entitles the bank to contract payments free of setoff for that liability [@CL-CTF-3578]. One exception applies here: a setoff may be appropriate to the extent the 1,000,000 due exceeds the 600,000 of loans made under a firm commitment, so a setoff against the 400,000 above the loans may be appropriate [@CL-CTF-3583]. Payments already made to the bank are not recovered on account of any liability of the contractor [@CL-CTF-3576]. A separate 5,000 that the Government may collect from the contractor under the penalty terms of the assigned contract is outside the protected penalties [@CL-CTF-3580]. Had the bank made no loan and no commitment to lend, a setoff could also be appropriate [@CL-CTF-3582].

## Never confuse

An assignment of claims is often confused with a no-setoff commitment. The assignment operates under the Act and gives the assignee immunity from recovery of payments made, while the commitment is a separate contractual undertaking against reduction or setoff [@CL-CTF-3575; @CL-CTF-3576]. The commitment is also not a bar on every setoff, because setoff may be appropriate in the circumstances above [@CL-CTF-3582; @CL-CTF-3583]. This card does not set the conditions for a valid assignment, such as the minimum payments the contract specifies and the type of assignee [@CL-CTF-0771; @CL-CTF-0772]; see the [conditions and policies card](ctf-assignment-of-claims.md).
