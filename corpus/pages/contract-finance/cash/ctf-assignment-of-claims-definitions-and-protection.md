## Purpose

Explains what a designated agency and a no-setoff commitment are, and how far the protection of an assignee of claims reaches.

## The rule

FAR subpart 32.8 prescribes policies and procedures for the assignment of claims under the Assignment of Claims Act of 1940, as amended (section 3727 of title 31 and section 6305 of title 41 of the United States Code), referred to in the subpart as the Act [@CL-CTF-3686]. As used in FAR subpart 32.8, designated agency means any department or agency of the executive branch of the United States Government (see FAR 32.803(d)) [@CL-CTF-3574]. No-setoff commitment, as used in the subpart, means a contractual undertaking that, to the extent permitted by the Act, payments by the designated agency to the assignee under an assignment of claims will not be reduced to liquidate the indebtedness of the contractor to the Government [@CL-CTF-3575]. FAR 2.101 defines assignment of claims as the transfer or making over by the contractor to a bank, trust company, or other financing institution, as security for a loan to the contractor, of its right to be paid by the Government for contract performance [@CL-CTF-9391]. RFO FAR 2.101 defines assignment of claims as the transfer or making over by the contractor to a bank, trust company, or other financing institution, as security for a loan to the contractor, of its right to be paid by the Government for contract performance [@CL-CTF-9441].

No payments made by the Government to the assignee under any contract assigned in accordance with the Act may be recovered on account of any liability of the contractor to the Government [@CL-CTF-3576]. This immunity of the assignee is effective whether the contractor's liability arises from or independently of the assigned contract [@CL-CTF-3577].

## How it works

No payments made by the Government to the assignee under any contract assigned in accordance with the Act may be recovered on account of any liability of the contractor to the Government, whether the contractor's liability arises from or independently of the assigned contract [@CL-CTF-3576; @CL-CTF-3577]. Except as provided in FAR 32.804(c), the inclusion of a no-setoff commitment in an assigned contract entitles the assignee to receive contract payments free of reduction or setoff for: (1) any liability of the contractor to the Government arising independently of the contract [@CL-CTF-3578], and (2) any of the following liabilities of the contractor to the Government arising from the assigned contract: (i) renegotiation under any statute or contract clause, (ii) fines [@CL-CTF-3579], (iii) penalties, exclusive of amounts that may be collected or withheld from the contractor under, or for failure to comply with, the terms of the contract [@CL-CTF-3580], (iv) taxes or social security contributions, and (v) withholding or nonwithholding of taxes or social security contributions [@CL-CTF-3581].

In some circumstances, a setoff may be appropriate even though the assigned contract includes a no-setoff commitment, for example: (1) when the assignee has neither made a loan under the assignment nor made a commitment to do so [@CL-CTF-3582], and (2) to the extent that the amount due on the contract exceeds the amount of any loans made or expected to be made under a firm commitment for financing [@CL-CTF-3583].

Any contract of a designated agency (see FAR 32.801), except a contract under which full payment has been made, may include a no-setoff commitment only when a determination of need is made by the head of the agency, in accordance with the Presidential delegation of authority dated October 3, 1995, and after such determination has been published in the Federal Register [@CL-CTF-3415; @CL-CTF-3416]. When an assigned contract does not include a no-setoff commitment, the Government may apply against payments to the assignee any liability of the contractor to the Government arising independently of the assigned contract if the liability existed at the time notice of the assignment was received, even though that liability had not yet matured so as to be due and payable [@CL-CTF-3495; @CL-CTF-1365].

Effective February 1, 2026, under DoD Class Deviation 2026-O0022, contracting officers shall use the revised RFO FAR part 32, Contract Financing, in lieu of the text codified at 48 CFR chapter 1 [@CL-CTF-4046]. RFO FAR 32.800 prescribes policies and procedures for the assignment of claims under the Assignment of Claims Act of 1940 (section 3727 of title 31 and section 6305 of title 41 of the United States Code), referred to as the Act [@CL-CTF-4087]. Under RFO FAR 32.801, designated agency means any department or agency of the executive branch of the United States Government (see RFO FAR 32.803(d)) [@CL-CTF-4088], and no-setoff commitment means a contractual undertaking that, to the extent permitted by the Act, payments by the designated agency to the assignee under an assignment of claims will not be reduced to liquidate the indebtedness of the contractor to the Government [@CL-CTF-4089]. Under RFO FAR 32.804(a), no payments made by the Government to the assignee under any contract assigned in accordance with the Act may be recovered on account of any liability of the contractor to the Government [@CL-CTF-4090], and this immunity of the assignee is effective whether the contractor's liability arises from or independently of the assigned contract [@CL-CTF-4091]. Under RFO FAR 32.804(b), except as provided in paragraph (c), the inclusion of a no-setoff commitment in an assigned contract entitles the assignee to receive contract payments free of reduction or setoff for: (1) any liability of the contractor to the Government arising independently of the contract [@CL-CTF-4092], and (2) any of the following liabilities of the contractor to the Government arising from the assigned contract: (i) renegotiation under any statute or contract clause, (ii) fines [@CL-CTF-4093], (iii) penalties, exclusive of amounts that may be collected or withheld from the contractor under, or for failure to comply with, the terms of the contract, (iv) taxes or social security contributions, and (v) withholding or nonwithholding of taxes or social security contributions [@CL-CTF-4094]. Under RFO FAR 32.804(c), in some circumstances, a setoff may be appropriate even though the assigned contract includes a no-setoff commitment, for example: (1) when the assignee has neither made a loan under the assignment nor made a commitment to do so [@CL-CTF-4095], and (2) to the extent that the amount due on the contract exceeds the amount of any loans made or expected to be made under a firm commitment for financing [@CL-CTF-4096]. Under RFO FAR 32.803(d), any contract of a designated agency (see RFO FAR 32.801), except a contract under which full payment has been made, may include a no-setoff commitment only when a determination of need is made by the head of the agency, in accordance with the Presidential delegation of authority dated October 3, 1995, and after such determination has been published in the Federal Register [@CL-CTF-4097; @CL-CTF-4098]. Under RFO FAR 32.803(e), when an assigned contract does not include a no-setoff commitment, the Government may apply against payments to the assignee any liability of the contractor to the Government arising independently of the assigned contract if the liability existed at the time notice of the assignment was received, even though that liability had not yet matured so as to be due and payable [@CL-CTF-4099; @CL-CTF-4658].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor assigns claims under a contract to a bank, and the contract carries a no-setoff commitment. The bank has made loans of 600,000 under a firm commitment, and 1,000,000 is due on the contract. The Government claims 50,000 from the contractor under an unrelated contract, a liability arising independently of the assigned contract. Subject to the exceptions for some circumstances, the commitment entitles the bank to contract payments free of setoff for that liability [@CL-CTF-3578]. One exception applies here: a setoff may be appropriate to the extent the 1,000,000 due exceeds the 600,000 of loans made under a firm commitment, so a setoff against the 400,000 above the loans may be appropriate [@CL-CTF-3583]. Payments already made to the bank are not recovered on account of any liability of the contractor [@CL-CTF-3576]. A separate 5,000 that the Government may collect from the contractor under the penalty terms of the assigned contract is outside the protected penalties [@CL-CTF-3580]. Had the bank made no loan and no commitment to lend, a setoff could also be appropriate [@CL-CTF-3582].

## Never confuse

Under FAR 32.804(a), no payments made by the Government to the assignee under any contract assigned in accordance with the Act may be recovered on account of any liability of the contractor to the Government, while a no-setoff commitment is a contractual undertaking that, to the extent permitted by the Act, payments by the designated agency to the assignee under an assignment of claims will not be reduced to liquidate the indebtedness of the contractor to the Government [@CL-CTF-3575; @CL-CTF-3576]. In some circumstances, a setoff may be appropriate even though the assigned contract includes a no-setoff commitment, such as when the assignee has neither made a loan under the assignment nor made a commitment to do so, or to the extent that the amount due on the contract exceeds the amount of any loans made or expected to be made under a firm commitment for financing [@CL-CTF-3582; @CL-CTF-3583]. Under FAR 32.802, a contractor may assign moneys due or to become due under a contract if all the listed conditions are met, including that the contract specifies payments aggregating $1,000 or more, the assignment is made to a bank, trust company, or other financing institution (including any Federal lending agency), and the contract does not prohibit the assignment [@CL-CTF-0771; @CL-CTF-0772]. See [Assignment of claims conditions and policies](ctf-assignment-of-claims.md).
