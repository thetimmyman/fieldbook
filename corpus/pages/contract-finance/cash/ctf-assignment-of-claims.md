## Purpose

Specifies the statutory conditions and agency policies under which a contractor may assign contract payments to a financing institution.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CTF-0771] |
| Codified citation | FAR 32.802 [@CL-CTF-0771] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 32.802 [@CL-CTF-4100; @CL-CTF-4101; @CL-CTF-4102; @CL-CTF-4103; @CL-CTF-4104]; RFO FAR 32.803 [@CL-CTF-4105; @CL-CTF-4106] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CTF-0771] |
| Applies to | Contracts specifying payments aggregating $1,000 or more, when the other listed conditions are also met [@CL-CTF-0771] |

Under the Assignment of Claims Act, a contractor may assign moneys due or to become due under a contract if all the listed conditions are met, one of which is that the contract specifies payments aggregating $1,000 or more [@CL-CTF-0771]. The assignment must be made to a bank, trust company, or other financing institution, including any Federal lending agency, and the contract must not prohibit the assignment [@CL-CTF-0772].

Unless otherwise expressly permitted in the contract, the assignment must cover all unpaid amounts payable under the contract [@CL-CTF-0867]. Unless otherwise expressly permitted, it must also be made only to one party, except that any assignment may be made to one party as agent or trustee for two or more parties participating in the financing of the contract, and must not be subject to further assignment [@CL-CTF-0875]. The assignee must send a written notice of assignment together with a true copy of the assignment instrument to the contracting officer or agency head, the surety on any bond applicable to the contract, and the disbursing officer designated in the contract [@CL-CTF-0773].

## How it works

An assignment that has been made to a listed type of financing institution may be further assigned and reassigned to any such institution if the conditions on coverage of unpaid amounts, single assignee and notice continue to be met [@CL-CTF-0774]. A contract may prohibit the assignment of claims if the agency determines the prohibition to be in the Government's interest [@CL-CTF-0775]. The prohibition is also one of the conditions, because the contract must not prohibit the assignment [@CL-CTF-0772]. For the wider financing picture see [Contract financing overview](../financing/ctf-financing-overview.md).

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 32 text in place of the codified text from February 1, 2026 [@CL-CTF-4046]. RFO FAR 32.802 keeps the rule that, under the Assignment of Claims Act, a contractor may assign moneys due or to become due under a contract if all the listed conditions are met, the first being that the contract specifies payments aggregating $1,000 or more [@CL-CTF-4100]. The assignment must be made to a bank, trust company, or other financing institution, including any Federal lending agency, and the contract must not prohibit the assignment [@CL-CTF-4101]. Unless otherwise expressly permitted in the contract, the assignment covers all unpaid amounts payable under the contract [@CL-CTF-4102], is made only to one party, except that any assignment may be made to one party as agent or trustee for two or more parties participating in the financing of the contract, and is not subject to further assignment [@CL-CTF-4102; @CL-CTF-4103]. The assignee sends a written notice of assignment together with a true copy of the assignment instrument to the contracting officer or the agency head, the surety on any bond applicable to the contract, and the disbursing officer designated in the contract to make payment [@CL-CTF-4104]. RFO FAR 32.803(a) keeps the rule that an assignment of claims made under the Act to any type of financing institution listed in 32.802(b) may thereafter be further assigned and reassigned to any such institution if the conditions in 32.802(d) and (e) continue to be met [@CL-CTF-4105], and RFO FAR 32.803(b) keeps the rule that a contract may prohibit the assignment of claims if the agency determines the prohibition to be in the Government's interest [@CL-CTF-4106].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A supplier with a 500,000 defense contract that does not prohibit assignment obtains operating credit from a commercial bank. The supplier assigns all unpaid amounts payable under the contract to the bank alone, and the bank sends a written notice of assignment with a true copy of the instrument to the contracting officer, the surety on any bond applicable to the contract, and the designated disbursing officer.

## Never confuse

An assignment of claims concerns moneys due or to become due under a contract and goes to a financing institution; it is not a general power to transfer the contract to anyone [@CL-CTF-0771; @CL-CTF-0772]. The conditions also differ from the policy that a contract may prohibit assignment [@CL-CTF-0775].
