## Purpose

Defines when DoD contracting officers require contractor cash flow forecasts and what data and operating assumptions those forecasts must show.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-CTF-0758] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 232.072-3 [@CL-CTF-0758] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CTF-0758] |
| Applies to | Contractors when there is doubt regarding cash flow sufficiency [@CL-CTF-0758] |

When there is doubt regarding the sufficiency of a contractor's cash flow, the contracting officer should require the contractor to submit a cash flow forecast covering the duration of the contract [@CL-CTF-0758]. Cash flow forecasts must show the origin and use of all material amounts of cash within the entire business unit responsible for contract performance, period by period, for the length of the contract or until the risk of a cash crisis ends [@CL-CTF-0759]. They must also provide an audit trail to the data and assumptions used to prepare them [@CL-CTF-0759; @CL-CTF-0862].

A contractor's inability or refusal to prepare and provide cash flow forecasts or to reconcile actual cash flow with previous forecasts is a strong indicator of serious managerial deficiencies or potential contract cost or performance problems [@CL-CTF-0760]. Single or one-time forecasts are of limited forecasting power and should be limited to preaward survey situations [@CL-CTF-0882].

## How it works

Reliability of cash flow forecasts can be established only by comparing a series of previous actual cash flows with the corresponding forecasts and examining the causes of any differences [@CL-CTF-0761]. A forecast can be no more reliable than its assumptions [@CL-CTF-0880]. Among the most important assumptions listed are the estimated amounts and timing of billings to customers (including progress payments) and customer payments, and of cash receipts from lenders or other credit sources and liquidation of loans [@CL-CTF-0881; @CL-CTF-9568]. The contracting officer should review the assumptions and, in judging whether they are reasonable and realistic, should consult listed sources [@CL-CTF-0878], which are the contractor, Government finance, engineering, production, cost and price analysis personnel, or prospective supply, subcontract and loan or credit sources [@CL-CTF-0879].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor on an extended defense project has supplier delays that put its cash balances in doubt. The contracting officer asks for a period-by-period forecast for the whole business unit, showing quarterly receipts of 1,200,000 against disbursements of 1,100,000, with an audit trail to its assumptions. The contracting officer then compares earlier forecasts with the actual cash flows and asks the contractor to explain the differences.

## Never confuse

A cash flow forecast covers the entire business unit responsible for contract performance [@CL-CTF-0759], so it is wider than the billing and payment terms of one contract; see [Prompt payment](../billing-payment/ctf-prompt-payment.md). The review that may call for a forecast is on [DoD required financial reviews](ctf-dod-required-financial-reviews.md); for financing generally see [Contract financing overview](../financing/ctf-financing-overview.md).

## Company calibration

Confirm that operating unit cash forecasts provide full audit trails from operational schedules to cash receipt and payment assumptions. [@Q-026]
