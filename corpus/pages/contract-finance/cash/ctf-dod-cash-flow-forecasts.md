## Purpose

Defines when DoD contracting officers require contractor cash flow forecasts and what data and operating assumptions those forecasts must show.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-CTF-0758] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 232.072-3 [@CL-CTF-0758] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CTF-0758] |
| Applies to | Contractors when there is doubt regarding cash flow sufficiency [@CL-CTF-0758] |

When there is doubt regarding the sufficiency of a contractor's cash flow, the contracting officer should require the contractor to submit a cash flow forecast covering the duration of the contract [@CL-CTF-0758]. Cash flow forecasts must show the origin and use of all material amounts of cash within the entire business unit responsible for contract performance, period by period, for the length of the contract or until the risk of a cash crisis ends [@CL-CTF-0759]. They must also provide an audit trail to the data and assumptions used to prepare them [@CL-CTF-0759; @CL-CTF-0862].

A contractor's inability or refusal to prepare and provide cash flow forecasts or to reconcile actual cash flow with previous forecasts is a strong indicator of serious managerial deficiencies or potential contract cost or performance problems [@CL-CTF-0760]. Single or one-time forecasts are of limited forecasting power and should be limited to preaward survey situations [@CL-CTF-0882].

## How it works

Reliability of cash flow forecasts can be established only by comparing a series of previous actual cash flows with the corresponding forecasts and examining the causes of any differences [@CL-CTF-0761]. Cash flow forecasts can be no more reliable than the assumptions on which they are based [@CL-CTF-0880]. Most important of these assumptions are: (1) estimated amounts and timing of purchases and payments for materials, parts, components, subassemblies, and services, (2) estimated amounts and timing of payments of purchase or production of capital assets, test facilities, and tooling, (3) amounts and timing of fixed cash charges such as debt installments, interest, rentals, taxes, and indirect costs, (4) estimated amounts and timing of payments for projected labor, both direct and indirect, (5) reasonableness of projected manufacturing and production schedules, (6) estimated amounts and timing of billings to customers (including progress payments) and customer payments, (7) estimated amounts and timing of cash receipts from lenders or other credit sources, and liquidation of loans, and (8) estimated amount and timing of cash receipt from other sources [@CL-CTF-9568; @CL-CTF-1360; @CL-CTF-1361; @CL-CTF-0881; @CL-CTF-1362]. The contracting officer should review the assumptions underlying the cash flow forecasts and, in determining whether the assumptions are reasonable and realistic, should consult with (1) the contractor, (2) Government personnel in the areas of finance, engineering, production, cost, and price analysis, or (3) prospective supply, subcontract, and loan or credit sources [@CL-CTF-0878; @CL-CTF-0879].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor on an extended defense project has supplier delays that put its cash balances in doubt. The contracting officer requires a period-by-period cash flow forecast for the entire business unit responsible for contract performance, showing quarterly receipts of 1,200,000 against disbursements of 1,100,000, with an audit trail to the data and assumptions used to prepare it [@CL-CTF-0758; @CL-CTF-0759; @CL-CTF-0862]. The contracting officer compares a series of previous actual cash flows with the corresponding forecasts and examines the causes of any differences [@CL-CTF-0761].

## Never confuse

Cash flow forecasts must show the origin and use of all material amounts of cash within the entire business unit responsible for contract performance, period by period, for the length of the contract (or until the risk of a cash crisis ends), and provide an audit trail to the data and assumptions used to prepare them [@CL-CTF-0759; @CL-CTF-0862]. Single or one-time cash flow forecasts are of limited forecasting power and should be limited to preaward survey situations [@CL-CTF-0882]. For contract payment timing rules see [Prompt payment](../billing-payment/ctf-prompt-payment.md), for the financial review triggers see [DoD required financial reviews](ctf-dod-required-financial-reviews.md), and for financing generally see [Contract financing overview](../financing/ctf-financing-overview.md).
