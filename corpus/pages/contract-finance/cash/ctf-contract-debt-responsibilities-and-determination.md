## Purpose

Sets out who identifies and demands payment of a contract debt, who collects it, and how the amount of the debt is determined.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CTF-3567] |
| Codified citation | FAR 32.602 and 32.603 [@CL-CTF-3567; @CL-CTF-3571] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 32.602 [@CL-CTF-4128; @CL-CTF-4129; @CL-CTF-4130; @CL-CTF-4131], RFO FAR 32.603 [@CL-CTF-4132; @CL-CTF-4133; @CL-CTF-4134] and RFO FAR 32.604(a)(1) and (c) [@CL-CTF-4135; @CL-CTF-4137; @CL-CTF-1297; @CL-CTF-1298] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CTF-3567] |
| Applies to | Amounts a contractor owes to the Government under a contract [@CL-CTF-3571] |

The contracting officer has primary responsibility for identifying and demanding payment of contract debts except those resulting from errors made by the payment office [@CL-CTF-3567]. The contracting officer shall not collect contract debts or otherwise agree to liquidate contract debts, for example by offsetting the amount of the debt against existing unpaid bills due the contractor or allowing contractors to retain contract debts to cover amounts that may become payable in future periods [@CL-CTF-3568]. The payment office has primary responsibility for collecting contract debts identified by contracting officers, identifying and collecting duplicate and erroneous payments, and authorizing the liquidation of contract debts in accordance with agency procedures [@CL-CTF-3569; @CL-CTF-3570].

If the contracting officer has any indication that a contractor owes money to the Government under a contract, the contracting officer shall determine promptly whether an actual debt is due and the amount [@CL-CTF-3571]. The amount of indebtedness determined by the contracting officer shall be an amount that is based on the merits of the case and is consistent with the contract terms [@CL-CTF-3573].

## How it works

The contracting officer has primary responsibility for identifying and demanding payment of contract debts except those resulting from errors made by the payment office, and determines promptly whether an actual debt is due and its amount [@CL-CTF-3567; @CL-CTF-3571]. The payment office has primary responsibility for collecting contract debts identified by contracting officers, for identifying and collecting duplicate and erroneous payments, and for authorizing the liquidation of contract debts in accordance with agency procedures, while the contracting officer shall not collect contract debts or otherwise agree to liquidate them [@CL-CTF-3568; @CL-CTF-3569; @CL-CTF-3570].

Any unnecessary delay in determining the debt may contribute to loss of timely availability of the funds to the program for which the funds were initially provided, increased difficulty in collecting the debt, or actual monetary loss to the Government [@CL-CTF-3572]. Under FAR 32.604(a)(1), except as provided in paragraph (c) of that section, the contracting officer shall issue the demand for payment as soon as the contracting officer has determined that an actual debt is due the Government and the amount [@CL-CTF-0766]. Under FAR 32.604(c), except as provided in paragraph (d) of that section, the contracting officer should not issue a demand for payment if the contracting officer only becomes aware of the debt when the contractor either provides a lump sum payment or submits a credit invoice (which is a contractor's request to liquidate the debt against existing unpaid bills due the contractor), or notifies the contracting officer that the payment office overpaid on an invoice payment, in which case the contracting officer shall notify the payment office of the overpayment [@CL-CTF-0856; @CL-CTF-1295; @CL-CTF-1296].

Effective February 1, 2026, DoD contracting officers must use the revised FAR part 32 text published on the Revolutionary FAR Overhaul web page in lieu of the text codified at 48 CFR chapter 1 [@CL-CTF-4046]. Under RFO FAR 32.602, the contracting officer has primary responsibility for identifying and demanding payment of contract debts except those resulting from errors made by the payment office [@CL-CTF-4128], and must not collect contract debts or otherwise agree to liquidate them [@CL-CTF-4129], while the payment office has primary responsibility for collecting contract debts identified by contracting officers, identifying and collecting duplicate and erroneous payments, and authorizing the liquidation of contract debts in accordance with agency procedures [@CL-CTF-4130; @CL-CTF-4131]. Under RFO FAR 32.603, if the contracting officer has any indication that a contractor owes money to the Government under a contract, the contracting officer must determine promptly whether an actual debt is due and the amount, any unnecessary delay may contribute to loss of timely availability of the funds to the program for which the funds were initially provided, increased difficulty in collecting the debt, or actual monetary loss to the Government, and the amount of indebtedness determined by the contracting officer must be based on the merits of the case and consistent with the contract terms [@CL-CTF-4132; @CL-CTF-4133; @CL-CTF-4134]. Under RFO FAR 32.604(a)(1) and (c), except as provided in paragraph (c), the contracting officer must issue the demand for payment as soon as the contracting officer has determined that an actual debt is due the Government and the amount, and except as provided in paragraph (d), the contracting officer should not issue a demand for payment if the contracting officer only becomes aware of the debt when the contractor provides a lump sum payment or submits a credit invoice (a contractor's request to liquidate the debt against existing unpaid bills due the contractor) or notifies the contracting officer that the payment office overpaid on an invoice payment, in which case the contracting officer must notify the payment office of the overpayment [@CL-CTF-4135; @CL-CTF-4137; @CL-CTF-1297; @CL-CTF-1298].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A price redetermination under a contract lowers the price, and the contractor holds 40,000 more than it is entitled to under the contract. The contracting officer has an indication that the contractor owes money under the contract, so the officer determines promptly whether an actual debt is due and its amount [@CL-CTF-3571]. The amount rests on the merits of the case and is consistent with the contract terms, here 40,000 [@CL-CTF-3573]. The contracting officer has primary responsibility for identifying the debt and demanding payment [@CL-CTF-3567]. The contractor asks to keep the 40,000 to cover an invoice expected next quarter, and the contracting officer may not agree, because the officer shall not allow a contractor to retain a debt to cover amounts that may become payable in future periods [@CL-CTF-3568]. The payment office has primary responsibility for collecting the debt that the contracting officer identified and for authorizing any liquidation under agency procedures [@CL-CTF-3569; @CL-CTF-3570]. If a separate duplicate payment came from a payment office error, the payment office has primary responsibility for identifying and collecting it [@CL-CTF-3567; @CL-CTF-3570].

## Never confuse

Determining a debt is not collecting it. The contracting officer determines whether an actual debt is due and its amount [@CL-CTF-3571], and the payment office has primary responsibility for collecting contract debts identified by contracting officers [@CL-CTF-3569]. Offsetting the amount of a debt against existing unpaid bills due the contractor or allowing contractors to retain contract debts to cover amounts that may become payable in future periods is a liquidation, and the contracting officer shall not agree to one [@CL-CTF-3568]. The [general rules card](../financing/ctf-contract-debts-general-rules.md) covers what counts as a contract debt, and the [collection card](ctf-contract-debt-final-decisions-and-collection.md) covers what follows a demand.
