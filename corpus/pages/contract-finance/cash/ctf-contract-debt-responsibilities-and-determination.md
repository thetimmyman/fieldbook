## Purpose

Sets out who identifies and demands payment of a contract debt, who collects it, and how the amount of the debt is determined.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CTF-3567] |
| Codified citation | FAR 32.602 and 32.603 [@CL-CTF-3567; @CL-CTF-3571] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 32.602 [@CL-CTF-4128; @CL-CTF-4129; @CL-CTF-4130; @CL-CTF-4131] and RFO FAR 32.603 [@CL-CTF-4132; @CL-CTF-4133; @CL-CTF-4134] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CTF-3567] |
| Applies to | Amounts a contractor owes the Government under a contract [@CL-CTF-3571] |

The contracting officer has primary responsibility for identifying and demanding payment of contract debts, except those resulting from errors made by the payment office [@CL-CTF-3567]. The contracting officer shall not collect contract debts or otherwise agree to liquidate them, for example by offsetting a debt against unpaid bills due the contractor or by allowing the contractor to retain a debt to cover amounts that may become payable in future periods [@CL-CTF-3568]. The payment office has primary responsibility for collecting the contract debts that contracting officers identify [@CL-CTF-3569]. It also has primary responsibility for identifying and collecting duplicate and erroneous payments and for authorizing the liquidation of contract debts in accordance with agency procedures [@CL-CTF-3570].

If the contracting officer has any indication that a contractor owes money to the Government under a contract, the contracting officer shall determine promptly whether an actual debt is due and its amount [@CL-CTF-3571]. The amount of indebtedness the contracting officer determines shall be based on the merits of the case and consistent with the contract terms [@CL-CTF-3573].

## How it works

The duties are split between two offices. The contracting officer has primary responsibility for identifying the debt and demanding payment, and determines whether a debt is due and how much [@CL-CTF-3567; @CL-CTF-3571]. The payment office has primary responsibility for collecting what the contracting officer identifies and for identifying and collecting duplicate and erroneous payments [@CL-CTF-3569; @CL-CTF-3570]. The payment office has primary responsibility for authorizing liquidation of a contract debt under agency procedures, and the contracting officer shall not agree to it [@CL-CTF-3568; @CL-CTF-3570]. A debt that results from an error made by the payment office is outside the primary responsibility of the contracting officer for identifying and demanding payment [@CL-CTF-3567].

Speed matters. Unnecessary delay in the determination may contribute to loss of timely availability of the funds to the program for which they were provided, increased difficulty in collecting the debt, or actual monetary loss to the Government [@CL-CTF-3572]. Once the contracting officer has determined that an actual debt is due and its amount, the officer issues the demand for payment as soon as that determination is made, except that, as the demand section provides, the contracting officer should not issue a demand if the officer only becomes aware of the debt when the contractor provides a lump sum payment or submits a credit invoice, subject to a further exception in that section [@CL-CTF-0766; @CL-CTF-0856].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers must use the RFO FAR part 32 text in place of the codified text, effective February 1, 2026 [@CL-CTF-4046]. RFO FAR 32.602 keeps the split of responsibility described above: the contracting officer has primary responsibility for identifying and demanding payment, except for payment office errors [@CL-CTF-4128], and must not collect or agree to liquidate contract debts [@CL-CTF-4129], while the payment office has primary responsibility for collecting, for identifying and collecting duplicate and erroneous payments, and for authorizing liquidation under agency procedures [@CL-CTF-4130; @CL-CTF-4131]. RFO FAR 32.603 keeps the duty to determine promptly whether an actual debt is due and the amount, the listed consequences of delay, and the merits-and-contract-terms test for the amount [@CL-CTF-4132; @CL-CTF-4133; @CL-CTF-4134]. RFO FAR 32.604(a)(1) keeps the demand as soon as the debt is determined, except that, under paragraph (c) of that section, the contracting officer should not issue a demand if the officer only becomes aware of the debt when the contractor provides a lump sum payment or submits a credit invoice, subject to a further exception in that section [@CL-CTF-4135; @CL-CTF-4137].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A price redetermination under a contract lowers the price, and the contractor holds 40,000 more than it is entitled to under the contract. The contracting officer has an indication that the contractor owes money under the contract, so the officer determines promptly whether an actual debt is due and its amount [@CL-CTF-3571]. The amount rests on the merits of the case and is consistent with the contract terms, here 40,000 [@CL-CTF-3573]. The contracting officer has primary responsibility for identifying the debt and demanding payment [@CL-CTF-3567]. The contractor asks to keep the 40,000 to cover an invoice expected next quarter, and the contracting officer may not agree, because the officer shall not allow a contractor to retain a debt to cover amounts that may become payable in future periods [@CL-CTF-3568]. The payment office has primary responsibility for collecting the debt that the contracting officer identified and for authorizing any liquidation under agency procedures [@CL-CTF-3569; @CL-CTF-3570]. If a separate duplicate payment came from a payment office error, the payment office identifies and collects it [@CL-CTF-3567; @CL-CTF-3570].

## Never confuse

Determining a debt is not collecting it. The contracting officer determines whether a debt is due and its amount [@CL-CTF-3571], and the payment office has primary responsibility for collecting the debts the contracting officer identifies [@CL-CTF-3569]. An offset against unpaid bills or a retention to cover later payables is a liquidation, and the contracting officer shall not agree to one [@CL-CTF-3568]. The [general rules card](../financing/ctf-contract-debts-general-rules.md) says what counts as a contract debt, and the [collection card](ctf-contract-debt-final-decisions-and-collection.md) covers what follows a demand.
