## Purpose

Covers four sections of the FAR contract debts subpart: how a contractor asks to pay a contract debt in installments, when interest charges apply, how a Government delay in delivering a demand letter moves the interest date, and who may compromise a debt. The subpart prescribes policies and procedures for identifying, collecting and deferring collection of contract debts, including interest if applicable [@CL-CTF-3630].

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-CTF-3616] |
| Codified citation | FAR 32.607-1, FAR 32.608-1, FAR 32.609 and FAR 32.610 [@CL-CTF-3616; @CL-CTF-3617; @CL-CTF-3619; @CL-CTF-3620] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CTF-3616] |
| Applies to | Contract debts under the FAR contract debts subpart, except that sections 32.607, 32.608 and 32.610 (which include the installment, interest charge and compromise sections) do not apply to claims against common carriers for transportation overcharges and freight and cargo losses [@CL-CTF-3630; @CL-CTF-3631] |

If a contractor requests an installment payment agreement, the contracting officer must notify the contractor to send a written request for installment payments to the office designated in agency procedures [@CL-CTF-3616]. The section sets no deadline for the notice and does not say who approves the request. Another section says that the contracting officer must not approve or deny the request and that the office designated in agency procedures is responsible for approving or denying it [@CL-CTF-1435].

Interest charges apply to any contract debt unpaid after 30 days from the issuance of a demand, unless the Interest clause specifies otherwise [@CL-CTF-3617]. They do not apply if the contract is a kind excluded under FAR 32.611, or if the contract or debt has been exempted from interest charges under agency procedures [@CL-CTF-3618]. The section states no interest rate.

The delay rule applies if interest is accrued based on the date of the demand letter and the Government delays delivery of the demand letter, for example by undue delay after dating at the originating office or by delays in the mail [@CL-CTF-3619]. In that case the date of the debt and accrual of interest must be extended to a time that is fair and reasonable under the particular circumstances [@CL-CTF-3632]. The text names no official who fixes the new date and gives no number of days.

For debts under $100,000, excluding interest, the designated agency official may compromise the debt pursuant to the Federal Claims Collection Standards (31 CFR part 902) and agency regulations [@CL-CTF-3620]. The amount is measured on the debt without interest [@CL-CTF-3620]. Unless specifically authorized by agency procedures, contracting officers cannot compromise debts [@CL-CTF-3621]. The section does not say who may compromise a debt that is not under that amount [@CL-CTF-3620].

The scope sentence names sections 32.607, 32.608 and 32.610 as the ones that do not apply to the common carrier claims; it does not name section 32.609, on delays in receipt of notices or demands [@CL-CTF-3631].

## How it works

Four parties act. The contractor asks for installments. The contracting officer notifies the contractor where to send the written request [@CL-CTF-3616]. The office designated in agency procedures decides the request [@CL-CTF-1435]. The designated agency official may compromise a debt below the dollar limit [@CL-CTF-3620].

Interest follows the demand. The Interest clause can specify otherwise, and a contract of an excluded kind or an exemption under agency procedures means the charges do not apply [@CL-CTF-3617; @CL-CTF-3618]. The excluded kinds are listed in FAR 32.611 [@CL-CTF-3618], which is covered on [Contract debts: final decisions, collection and the Interest clause](ctf-contract-debt-final-decisions-and-collection.md). If the Government delays the demand letter, the interest date moves to a fair and reasonable time [@CL-CTF-3619; @CL-CTF-3632].

A contracting officer needs specific authorization in agency procedures to compromise any debt [@CL-CTF-3621].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor owes 80,000 and cannot pay at once. It requests an installment payment agreement. The contracting officer notifies the contractor to send a written request to the office designated in agency procedures [@CL-CTF-3616]. The contracting officer must not approve or deny it; the designated office is responsible for approving or denying it [@CL-CTF-1435].

Separately, a demand for 12,000 is dated 1 April. Assume the contract carries the Interest clause with no change to the 30 days, is not a kind excluded under FAR 32.611, and has no exemption under agency procedures. Before any delay is considered, thirty days after issuance ends on 1 May, so a debt still unpaid on 15 May would be past that point and interest charges would apply [@CL-CTF-3617; @CL-CTF-3618]. Now suppose the Government's mail delay meant the letter arrived on 15 April and interest runs from the date of the demand letter. The date of the debt and accrual of interest must then be extended to a fair and reasonable time [@CL-CTF-3619; @CL-CTF-3632]. The text does not say how far, so this example sets no new date and does not say whether interest has begun by 15 May.

A third debt is 98,000 plus 6,000 of interest. With interest it totals 104,000, but excluding interest it is 98,000, which is under 100,000, so the designated agency official may compromise it under the Federal Claims Collection Standards and agency regulations [@CL-CTF-3620]. The contracting officer may not, unless agency procedures specifically authorize it [@CL-CTF-3621]. For a debt of 140,000 the section names no official who may compromise it [@CL-CTF-3620]. This example applies the FAR sections only; the DoD supplement adds its own rule on who may compromise a DoD debt [@CL-CTF-3624].

## Never confuse

An installment request and a compromise are separate steps with separate decision makers. The designated office decides installment requests [@CL-CTF-1435], and the designated agency official may compromise a debt under the dollar limit [@CL-CTF-3620]. The text does not say these are the same office or person.

The 30 days run from the issuance of the demand [@CL-CTF-3617]. The delay rule applies only when the Government delays delivery and interest is accrued based on the date of the demand letter [@CL-CTF-3619].

The dollar limit is measured on the debt excluding interest, not on the debt with interest [@CL-CTF-3620].

The general debt rules and the installment decision are in [Contract debts: what the FAR counts as one](../financing/ctf-contract-debts-general-rules.md) and [Assignment of claims procedure and installment payments of contract debts](ctf-assignment-procedure-and-debt-installments.md). The demand and deferment steps are in [Contract debts demand deferment and interest](ctf-contract-debts.md). The DoD rule on compromise is in [DoD contract debts: determination, demand, compromise and collection transfer](ctf-dfars-contract-debt-steps-and-collection-transfer.md). This card does not restate them.
