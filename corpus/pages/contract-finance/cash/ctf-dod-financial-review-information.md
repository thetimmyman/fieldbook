## Purpose

Identifies the types and depth of financial information a DoD contracting officer requests to determine contractor capability.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-CTF-0754] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 232.072-2 [@CL-CTF-0754] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-CTF-0754] |
| Applies to | Contractors from whom financial capability or condition information is required [@CL-CTF-0754] |

The contracting officer shall obtain the type and depth of financial and other information required to establish a contractor's financial capability or disclose a contractor's financial condition [@CL-CTF-0754]. The contracting officer should not request information that is not necessary for protection of the Government's interests, but must insist upon obtaining the information that is necessary [@CL-CTF-0755]. The listed information is obtained to the extent required to protect the Government's interest [@CL-CTF-0850].

The information must be for the persons legally liable for contract performance and, if the contractor is not a corporation, for each individual, joint venturer or partner [@CL-CTF-0756]. The list includes, at item (1), balance sheets and income statements for the current fiscal year (interim) and for the most recent fiscal year and, preferably, the 2 preceding fiscal years, and the statements for the most recent and preceding fiscal years should be certified by an independent public accountant or by an appropriate officer of the firm [@CL-CTF-0783]. It also includes a summary history of the contractor and its principal managers [@CL-CTF-0851], a business base and forecast that shows, by significant markets, existing contracts and outstanding offers, including those under negotiation, and is reconcilable to indirect cost rate projections [@CL-CTF-0757], and financing arrangement information [@CL-CTF-0852].

## How it works

The contracting officer decides the type and depth of information by what is needed to establish financial capability or disclose financial condition [@CL-CTF-0754]. A contractor's unwillingness or inability to present reasonably requested information in a timely manner, especially information a prudent business person would be expected to have and use in managing a business, may be a material fact in the determination of the contractor's responsibility and prospects for contract completion [@CL-CTF-0853; @CL-CTF-9569]. The financing arrangement information discloses, among other things, the availability of cash to finance contract performance and the contractor's exposure to financial crisis from creditor's demands [@CL-CTF-0852].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contracting officer reviewing a new supplier requests balance sheets and income statements for the current interim year, the most recent year and preferably the two years before that, with 5,000,000 in assets and 3,000,000 in liabilities on the latest statement. The supplier provides statements certified by an independent public accountant, a summary history of its managers, a business base and forecast that reconciles to its indirect cost rate projections, and its financing arrangements.

## Never confuse

The list is not a fixed checklist to be demanded in full: the contracting officer obtains it only to the extent required to protect the Government's interest [@CL-CTF-0850]. What triggers a review is separate; see [DoD required financial reviews](ctf-dod-required-financial-reviews.md). For financing generally see [Contract financing overview](../financing/ctf-financing-overview.md).

## Company calibration

Confirm that balance sheets, financing agreements, and business base forecasts are prepared and certified for contracting officer review. [@Q-026]
