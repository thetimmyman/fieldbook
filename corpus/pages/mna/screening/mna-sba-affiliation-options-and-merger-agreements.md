## Purpose

Shows when SBA treats future rights, such as options and merger agreements, as present control, and why an ability to divest does not remove affiliation.

## The rule

| Applies to | Concerns whose size SBA determines where stock options, convertible securities, or agreements to merge (including agreements in principle) exist [@CL-MNA-0149; @CL-MNA-0510] |

Under 13 CFR 121.103(d)(1), in determining size, SBA considers stock options, convertible securities, and agreements to merge (including agreements in principle) to have a present effect on the power to control a concern, and SBA treats such options, convertible securities, and agreements as though the rights granted have been exercised [@CL-MNA-0149; @CL-MNA-0510].

Under 13 CFR 121.103(d)(2), agreements to open or continue negotiations towards the possibility of a merger or a sale of stock at some later date are not considered "agreements in principle" and are thus not given present effect [@CL-MNA-0511]. Under 13 CFR 121.103(d)(3), options, convertible securities, and agreements that are subject to conditions precedent [@CL-MNA-0554] which are incapable of fulfillment, speculative, conjectural, or unenforceable under state or Federal law, or where the probability of the transaction (or exercise of the rights) occurring is shown to be extremely remote, are not given present effect [@CL-MNA-0512].

## How it works

Under 13 CFR 121.103(d)(4), an individual, concern or other entity that controls one or more other concerns cannot use options, convertible securities, or agreements to appear to terminate such control before actually doing so [@CL-MNA-0150], and SBA will not give present effect to individuals', concerns' or other entities' ability to divest all or part of their ownership interest in order to avoid a finding of affiliation [@CL-MNA-0513]. Under 13 CFR 121.103(e), affiliation based on common management arises where one or more officers, directors, managing members, or partners who control the board of directors and/or management of one concern also control the board of directors or management of one or more other concerns [@CL-MNA-0514]. Under 13 CFR 121.103(f), affiliation may arise among two or more persons with an identity of interest [@CL-MNA-0515]. Individuals or firms that have identical or substantially identical business or economic interests (such as family members, individuals or firms with common investments, or firms that are economically dependent through contractual or other relationships) [@CL-MNA-0153] may be treated as one party with such interests aggregated, and where SBA determines that such interests should be aggregated, an individual or firm may rebut that determination with evidence showing that the interests deemed to be one are in fact separate [@CL-MNA-0154].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A buyer signs an agreement in principle to merge with a small contractor. The same buyer also signs a letter that only opens talks about a possible stock purchase of a second small contractor next year.

Answer: unless the agreement is subject to the kinds of conditions the rule describes, SBA treats the agreement in principle as though the merger had happened, so the first contractor's size is counted with the buyer's. The letter is not an agreement in principle, so it has no present effect on the second contractor. A seller whose only protection is an ability to divest part of its stake gets no present effect from that ability.

## Never confuse

Under 13 CFR 121.103(d)(4), an individual, concern or other entity that controls one or more other concerns cannot use options, convertible securities, or agreements to appear to terminate such control before actually doing so [@CL-MNA-0150], and SBA will not give present effect to individuals', concerns' or other entities' ability to divest all or part of their ownership interest in order to avoid a finding of affiliation (see also the [Predecessor of Offeror provision](../diligence/mna-predecessor-of-offeror.md) card) [@CL-MNA-0513]. Under 13 CFR 121.103(d)(1) and (2), SBA considers agreements to merge (including agreements in principle) to have a present effect on the power to control a concern [@CL-MNA-0149; @CL-MNA-0510], whereas agreements to open or continue negotiations towards the possibility of a merger or a sale of stock at some later date are not considered "agreements in principle" and are thus not given present effect [@CL-MNA-0511].
