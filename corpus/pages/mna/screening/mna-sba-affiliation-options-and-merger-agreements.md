## Purpose

Shows when SBA treats future rights, such as options and merger agreements, as present control, and why an ability to divest does not remove affiliation.

This card rests on an SBA regulation for small business programs, 13 CFR 121.103, and not on the FAR [@CL-MNA-0510]. SBA considers stock options, convertible securities and agreements to merge, including agreements in principle, to have a present effect on the power to control a concern, and it treats the rights granted as though they have been exercised [@CL-MNA-0510].

Agreements to open or continue negotiations towards the possibility of a merger or a sale of stock at some later date are not agreements in principle and are not given present effect [@CL-MNA-0511]. Options, convertible securities and agreements that are subject to conditions precedent [@CL-MNA-0554] are also not given present effect where the conditions are incapable of fulfillment, speculative, conjectural or unenforceable under state or Federal law, or where the probability of the transaction or of the exercise of the rights occurring is shown to be extremely remote [@CL-MNA-0512].

## How it works

SBA will not give present effect to an individual's, concern's or other entity's ability to divest all or part of its ownership interest in order to avoid a finding of affiliation [@CL-MNA-0513]. Affiliation also arises where one or more officers, directors, managing members or partners who control the board or management of one concern also control the board or management of one or more other concerns [@CL-MNA-0514]. Affiliation may arise among two or more persons with an identity of interest [@CL-MNA-0515]. The identity-of-interest paragraph goes on to describe further tests, which this card does not cover.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A buyer signs an agreement in principle to merge with a small contractor. The same buyer also signs a letter that only opens talks about a possible stock purchase of a second small contractor next year.

Answer: unless the agreement is subject to the kinds of conditions the rule describes, SBA treats the agreement in principle as though the merger had happened, so the first contractor's size is counted with the buyer's. The letter is not an agreement in principle, so it has no present effect on the second contractor. A seller whose only protection is an ability to divest part of its stake gets no present effect from that ability.

## Never confuse

An ability to divest later is not the same as an end to control today. A seller cannot rely on that ability to avoid a finding of affiliation [@CL-MNA-0513]. Likewise, an agreement to negotiate is not an agreement in principle [@CL-MNA-0511]. How a contract passes to a successor is a separate contract-side question, covered on the [Predecessor of Offeror provision](../diligence/mna-predecessor-of-offeror.md) card.
