## Purpose

Shows how an acquisition, a former affiliate or a segregable division changes the annual receipts and the employee count SBA uses to decide a concern's size.

| Applies to | Concerns that acquired an affiliate, were acquired as an affiliate, or bought or sold a segregable division during the period of measurement or before self-certifying as small [@CL-MNA-0516; @CL-MNA-0518; @CL-MNA-0520] |

This card rests on two SBA size regulations, 13 CFR 121.104 on annual receipts and 13 CFR 121.106 on the number of employees, and not on the FAR [@CL-MNA-0516; @CL-MNA-0521]. If a concern has acquired an affiliate or been acquired as an affiliate during the applicable period of measurement or before the date on which it self-certified as small, the annual receipts used in determining size status include the receipts of the acquired or acquiring concern [@CL-MNA-0516]. The employees counted likewise include the employees of the acquired or acquiring concern [@CL-MNA-0521].

## How it works

Where the receipts used include those of an acquired or acquiring concern, that aggregation applies for the entire period of measurement, not just the period after the affiliation arose [@CL-MNA-0517], and the same is stated for employees [@CL-MNA-0522]. Where a concern has acquired a segregable division of another business concern during the applicable period of measurement or before the date on which it self-certified as small, the receipts used do not include the receipts of the acquired division prior to the acquisition [@CL-MNA-0518]. A former affiliate's receipts are not included if affiliation ceased before the date used for determining size, for the entire period of measurement [@CL-MNA-0519], and the employees of a former affiliate are treated the same way [@CL-MNA-0523]. Where a concern has sold a segregable division to another business concern during the applicable period of measurement or before the date on which it self-certified as small, the receipts [@CL-MNA-0520] and the employees [@CL-MNA-0524] used continue to include those of the division that was sold. The employee rule for an acquired segregable division is not established from the sources held.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A small contractor has average annual receipts of 4 million. It buys another concern whose average annual receipts were 3 million over the same years, and the two are now affiliates. It also buys a segregable division with 1 million of receipts, before it self-certifies as small.

Answer: the acquired concern's 3 million counts for the whole measurement period, not only after closing, so the receipts used are 7 million. The division's 1 million is not counted for the years before the acquisition.

## Never confuse

Size at the date of self-certification is not the same as recertification after a merger or acquisition. The sources held print how receipts and employees are counted around the date a concern self-certified as small [@CL-MNA-0516; @CL-MNA-0521]. The trigger for the SBA recertification rule after a merger or acquisition is not established from the sources held. A sale of a segregable division during the applicable period of measurement or before self-certification does not remove its receipts and employees from the counts [@CL-MNA-0520; @CL-MNA-0524].
