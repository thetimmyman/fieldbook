## Purpose

Shows how the Small Business Administration decides that concerns are affiliates, so a buyer or seller can see whose size is counted together.

| Applies to | Concerns whose size SBA determines, counted together with all of their domestic and foreign affiliates, regardless of whether the affiliates are organized for profit [@CL-MNA-0505] |

Under 13 CFR 121.103(a)(1), concerns and entities are affiliates of each other when one controls or has the power to control the other, or a third party or parties controls or has the power to control both, and it does not matter whether control is exercised, so long as the power to control exists [@CL-MNA-0500]. In determining the concern's size, SBA counts the receipts, employees, or other measure of size of the concern whose size is at issue and all of its domestic and foreign affiliates, regardless of whether the affiliates are organized for profit [@CL-MNA-0505].

Under 13 CFR 121.103(c)(1), a person (including any individual, concern or other entity) that owns, or has the power to control, 50 percent or more of a concern's voting stock, or a block of voting stock which is large compared to other outstanding blocks of voting stock, controls or has the power to control the concern [@CL-MNA-0506]. Under 13 CFR 121.103(c)(2), if two or more persons (including any individual, concern or other entity) each owns, controls, or has the power to control less than 50 percent of a concern's voting stock [@CL-MNA-0553], and such minority holdings are equal or approximately equal in size, and the aggregate of these minority holdings is large as compared with any other stock holding, SBA presumes that each such person controls or has the power to control the concern whose size is at issue [@CL-MNA-0507], and this presumption may be rebutted by a showing that such control or power to control does not in fact exist [@CL-MNA-0508]. Under 13 CFR 121.103(c)(3), if a concern's voting stock is widely held and no single block of stock is large as compared with all other stock holdings, the concern's Board of Directors and CEO or President will be deemed to have the power to control the concern in the absence of evidence to the contrary [@CL-MNA-0509].

## How it works

In determining whether affiliation exists, SBA will consider the totality of the circumstances, and may find affiliation even though no single factor is sufficient to constitute affiliation [@CL-MNA-0504]. Negative control includes, but is not limited to, instances where a minority shareholder has the ability, under the concern's charter, by-laws, or shareholder's agreement, to prevent a quorum or otherwise block action by the board of directors or shareholders [@CL-MNA-0501]. However, SBA will not find that a minority shareholder has negative control where such minority shareholder has the authority to block action by the board of directors or shareholders regarding the following extraordinary circumstances [@CL-MNA-0502]: (i) adding a new equity stakeholder or increasing the investment amount of an equity stakeholder, (ii) dissolution of the company, (iii) sale of the company or all assets of the company, and (iv) the merger of the company [@CL-MNA-0503]; (v) the company declaring bankruptcy, and (vi) amendment of the company's corporate governance documents to remove the shareholder's authority to block any of (a)(3)(i) through (v) [@CL-MNA-0550]; and (vii) any other extraordinary action that is crafted solely to protect the investment of the minority shareholders, and not to impede the majority's ability to control the concern's operations or to conduct the concern's business as it chooses [@CL-MNA-0551].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A target has two investors. Investor A holds 30 percent of the voting stock and Investor B holds 30 percent. The rest is spread across many holders with no more than 5 percent each. Investor A's shareholder agreement lets it veto only a sale of the company and a merger.

Answer: neither investor reaches 50 percent, but the aggregate of the two equal blocks is large compared with any other stock holding, so SBA presumes each controls the target unless the investor shows that control does not in fact exist. A veto limited to a sale of the company and a merger falls within the extraordinary-circumstances list, so SBA will not find that the investor has negative control because of that veto.

## Never confuse

In determining the concern's size, SBA counts the receipts, employees, or other measure of size of the concern whose size is at issue and all of its domestic and foreign affiliates, regardless of whether the affiliates are organized for profit (see also the [Predecessor of Offeror provision](../diligence/mna-predecessor-of-offeror.md) card and the [Novation and change-of-name agreements](../diligence/mna-novation-agreements.md) card) [@CL-MNA-0505]. Under 13 CFR 121.103(a)(3), negative control includes instances where a minority shareholder has the ability, under the concern's charter, by-laws, or shareholder's agreement, to prevent a quorum or otherwise block action by the board of directors or shareholders [@CL-MNA-0501], but SBA will not find that a minority shareholder has negative control where such minority shareholder has the authority to block action by the board of directors or shareholders regarding the extraordinary circumstances in paragraphs (a)(3)(i) through (vii) [@CL-MNA-0502; @CL-MNA-0503; @CL-MNA-0550; @CL-MNA-0551].
