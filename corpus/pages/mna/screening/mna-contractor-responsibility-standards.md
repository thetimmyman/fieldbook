## Purpose

Shows the standards a prospective contractor must meet to be found responsible, and how the contracting officer applies the resource, performance and affiliate tests.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-MNA-0540] |
| Codified citation | FAR 9.104-1 and FAR 9.104-3 [@CL-MNA-0540; @CL-MNA-0542] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-MNA-0540] |
| Applies to | Prospective contractors being considered for award [@CL-MNA-0540] |

To be determined responsible, a prospective contractor must have adequate financial resources to perform the contract, or the ability to obtain them [@CL-MNA-0540]. A satisfactory performance record is another listed standard, and a prospective contractor shall not be determined responsible or nonresponsible solely on the basis of a lack of relevant performance history, except as provided in FAR 9.104-2 [@CL-MNA-0541].

The list holds further standards that this card does not restate. The criteria for contractor systems are on the [Contractor business systems](../../compliance/business-systems/cmp-contractor-business-systems.md) card.

## How it works

Except to the extent that a prospective contractor has sufficient resources or proposes to perform the contract by subcontracting, the contracting officer shall require acceptable evidence of its ability to obtain required resources [@CL-MNA-0542]. Acceptable evidence normally consists of a commitment or explicit arrangement, that will be in existence at the time of contract award, to rent, purchase or otherwise acquire the needed facilities, equipment, other resources or personnel [@CL-MNA-0543]. A prospective contractor that is or recently has been seriously deficient in contract performance shall be presumed to be nonresponsible, unless the contracting officer determines that the circumstances were properly beyond its control or that it has taken appropriate corrective action [@CL-MNA-0544]. Affiliated concerns are normally considered separate entities in determining whether the concern that is to perform the contract meets the applicable standards for responsibility [@CL-MNA-0552]. However, the contracting officer shall consider the affiliate's past performance and integrity when they may adversely affect the prospective contractor's responsibility [@CL-MNA-0545].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A buyer plans to bid through a newly acquired target. The target's cash is thin, but before award it holds a signed lease for the plant it needs and a signed commitment to buy the equipment, both in existence at award.

Answer: that is the kind of commitment or explicit arrangement that is acceptable evidence of the ability to obtain required resources. A separate affiliate that was seriously deficient on several recent contracts and took no corrective action is a different matter: the contracting officer must consider it if it may adversely affect the target's responsibility.

## Never confuse

Responsibility is a finding about the prospective awardee's resources, record and ability to perform. It is not the SBA affiliation and size test, which counts an affiliate's size; here affiliated concerns are normally considered separate entities [@CL-MNA-0552], and the contracting officer shall consider an affiliate's past performance and integrity when they may adversely affect responsibility [@CL-MNA-0545].
