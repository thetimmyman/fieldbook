## Purpose

Shows the standards a prospective contractor must meet to be found responsible, and how the contracting officer applies the resource, performance and affiliate tests.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-MNA-0540] |
| Codified citation | FAR 9.104-1 and FAR 9.104-3 [@CL-MNA-0540; @CL-MNA-0542] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-MNA-0540] |
| Applies to | Prospective contractors to be determined responsible [@CL-MNA-0540] |

To be determined responsible, a prospective contractor must: (a) have adequate financial resources to perform the contract, or the ability to obtain them (see FAR 9.104-3(a)) [@CL-MNA-0540]; (b) be able to comply with the required or proposed delivery or performance schedule, taking into consideration all existing commercial and governmental business commitments [@CL-MNA-0140]; (c) have a satisfactory performance record (see FAR 9.104-3(b) and subpart 42.15), provided that a prospective contractor shall not be determined responsible or nonresponsible solely on the basis of a lack of relevant performance history, except as provided in FAR 9.104-2 [@CL-MNA-0541]; (d) have a satisfactory record of integrity and business ethics (for example, see subpart 42.15) [@CL-MNA-0141]; (e) have the necessary organization, experience, accounting and operational controls (see the [Contractor business systems](../../compliance/business-systems/cmp-contractor-business-systems.md) card), and technical skills, or the ability to obtain them (including, as appropriate, such elements as production control procedures, property control systems, quality assurance measures, and safety programs applicable to materials to be produced or services to be performed by the prospective contractor and subcontractors) (see FAR 9.104-3(a)) [@CL-MNA-0142; @CL-MNA-0143]; (f) have the necessary production, construction, and technical equipment and facilities, or the ability to obtain them (see FAR 9.104-3(a)) [@CL-MNA-0144]; and (g) be otherwise qualified and eligible to receive an award under applicable laws and regulations (see also inverted domestic corporation prohibition at FAR 9.108) [@CL-MNA-0145].

## How it works

Except to the extent that a prospective contractor has sufficient resources or proposes to perform the contract by subcontracting, the contracting officer shall require acceptable evidence of the prospective contractor's ability to obtain required resources (see FAR 9.104-1(a), (e), and (f)) [@CL-MNA-0542]. Acceptable evidence normally consists of a commitment or explicit arrangement, that will be in existence at the time of contract award, to rent, purchase, or otherwise acquire the needed facilities, equipment, other resources, or personnel [@CL-MNA-0543]. A prospective contractor that is or recently has been seriously deficient in contract performance shall be presumed to be nonresponsible, unless the contracting officer determines that the circumstances were properly beyond the contractor's control, or that the contractor has taken appropriate corrective action [@CL-MNA-0544]. Past failure to apply sufficient tenacity and perseverance to perform acceptably is strong evidence of nonresponsibility, and failure to meet the quality requirements of the contract is a significant factor to consider in determining satisfactory performance [@CL-MNA-0146]. The contracting officer shall consider the number of contracts involved and the extent of deficient performance in each contract when making this determination [@CL-MNA-0147]. If the pending contract requires a subcontracting plan pursuant to Subpart 19.7, The Small Business Subcontracting Program, the contracting officer shall also consider the prospective contractor's compliance with subcontracting plans under recent contracts [@CL-MNA-0148]. Affiliated concerns (see Concern in FAR 19.001 and Small business concern in FAR 2.101) are normally considered separate entities in determining whether the concern that is to perform the contract meets the applicable standards for responsibility [@CL-MNA-0552]. However, the contracting officer shall consider the affiliate's past performance and integrity when they may adversely affect the prospective contractor's responsibility [@CL-MNA-0545].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A buyer plans to bid through a newly acquired target. The target's cash is thin, but before award it holds a signed lease for the plant it needs and a signed commitment to buy the equipment, both in existence at award.

Answer: that is the kind of commitment or explicit arrangement that is acceptable evidence of the ability to obtain required resources. A separate affiliate that was seriously deficient on several recent contracts and took no corrective action is a different matter: the contracting officer must consider it if it may adversely affect the target's responsibility.

## Never confuse

Size determinations and responsibility determinations are not alike in how they treat affiliates: in determining a concern's size under 13 CFR 121.103(a)(6), SBA counts the receipts, employees, or other measure of size of the concern whose size is at issue and all of its domestic and foreign affiliates, regardless of whether the affiliates are organized for profit [@CL-MNA-0505], while under FAR 9.104-3(c)(1), affiliated concerns are normally considered separate entities in determining whether the concern that is to perform the contract meets the applicable standards for responsibility [@CL-MNA-0552], and the contracting officer shall consider the affiliate's past performance and integrity when they may adversely affect the prospective contractor's responsibility [@CL-MNA-0545].
