## Purpose

Shows when a contractor may recover the cost of restructuring that follows a business combination under DoD contracts, and which conditions must be met first.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-MNA-0369] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 231.205-70 [@CL-MNA-0369] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-MNA-0369] |
| Applies to | Restructuring after a business combination that affects the operations of companies not previously under common ownership or control [@CL-MNA-0370; @CL-MNA-0371] |

The principle prescribes policies and procedures for allowing external restructuring costs when savings would result for DoD [@CL-MNA-0369]. External restructuring activities are those occurring after a business combination that affect the operations of companies not previously under common ownership or control [@CL-MNA-0371]. They do not include restructuring that affects the operations of only one of those companies or, when there has been no business combination, restructuring within one company [@CL-MNA-0454], and they normally will be initiated within 3 years of the business combination [@CL-MNA-0372].

Restructuring costs of this kind are not allowed unless they are allowable under FAR part 31 and DFARS part 231 and an audit of projected restructuring costs and restructuring savings is performed [@CL-MNA-0375]. The cognizant administrative contracting officer must also review the audit and negotiate an advance agreement [@CL-MNA-0376], and the designated official must determine in writing that audited projected savings clear the savings test [@CL-MNA-0377; @CL-MNA-0378]. Restructuring costs allocated to DoD contracts that are less than $2.5 million are not subject to the audit, review and determination requirements of paragraph (c)(4) of the cost principle [@CL-MNA-0374], and the normal FAR part 31 allowability rules apply instead [@CL-MNA-0455].

## How it works

Restructuring costs that may be allowed include, but are not limited to, severance pay, early retirement incentive payments, retraining, relocation of retained employees, and relocation and rearrangement of plant and equipment [@CL-MNA-0373]. The designated official determines in writing that the audited projected savings, on a present value basis, for DoD resulting from the restructuring will exceed one of two alternatives. The first alternative is the costs allowed by a factor of at least two to one [@CL-MNA-0377]. The other is the costs allowed, where the business combination will result in the preservation of a critical capability that might otherwise be lost to DoD [@CL-MNA-0378]. If the amount of restructuring costs is expected to exceed $25 million over a 5-year period, the designated official is the Under Secretary of Defense (Acquisition and Sustainment) or the Principal Deputy, and this authority may not be delegated below the level of an Assistant Secretary of Defense [@CL-MNA-0379]. In all other cases the designated official is the Director of the Defense Contract Management Agency, who may not delegate it [@CL-MNA-0380].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

Two defense contractors merge. The combined company plans a plant consolidation with severance and equipment moves. Audited projected savings are 50 million against 20 million of restructuring costs to be allowed, a ratio above two to one on a present value basis. Costs are expected to be 20 million over five years, so the Director of the Defense Contract Management Agency is the designated official. If the costs allocated to DoD contracts were only 2 million, the requirements of paragraph (c)(4) would not apply and the normal FAR part 31 allowability rules would.

## Never confuse

Allowing a cost under the general cost principles is only the first condition; the audit, the advance agreement and the written savings determination are further conditions, and the threshold for small restructuring costs is a stated exception to the audit, review and determination requirements of paragraph (c)(4) [@CL-MNA-0375; @CL-MNA-0376; @CL-MNA-0377; @CL-MNA-0374]. This card concerns restructuring after a combination of companies not previously under common ownership; for how organization and reorganization costs are treated in general, see [Organization costs and corporate restructuring](../../compliance/cas-allowability/cmp-organization-costs.md), and for capital asset values after a combination see [Asset valuations resulting from business combinations](../valuation/mna-asset-valuations-business-combinations.md). The advance agreement is a general tool described in [Advance agreements](../../compliance/cas-allowability/cmp-advance-agreements.md), and the novation clause that carries these conditions into a transfer is on [Restructuring costs and the DoD paragraph in the novation agreement](../deal-gates/mna-novation-restructuring-paragraph.md).
