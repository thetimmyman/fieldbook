## Purpose

Shows what the statement for a Rule 13e-3 (going-private) transaction must say about the purposes of the transaction, any alternatives that the subject company or affiliate considered, the reasons for its structure and timing, and its effects on the subject company, its affiliates and unaffiliated security holders [@CL-MNA-0400; @CL-MNA-0401; @CL-MNA-0402; @CL-MNA-0403].

The statement must state the purposes of the transaction [@CL-MNA-0400]. If the subject company or affiliate considered alternative means to accomplish those purposes, the statement must briefly describe the alternatives and state the reasons for their rejection; the duty arises only if such alternatives were considered [@CL-MNA-0401]. The statement must state the reasons for the structure of the transaction and for undertaking it at this time [@CL-MNA-0402]. It must describe the effects of the transaction on the subject company, its affiliates and unaffiliated security holders, including the federal tax consequences [@CL-MNA-0403].

Conclusory statements are not sufficient disclosure [@CL-MNA-0404]. The effects description must include a reasonably detailed discussion of both the benefits and the detriments of the transaction to the subject company, its affiliates and unaffiliated security holders [@CL-MNA-0405]. The benefits and detriments must be quantified to the extent practicable [@CL-MNA-0406]. If an affiliate of the subject company files the statement, the effects description must include, but not be limited to, the effect on the affiliate's interest in the net book value and net earnings of the subject company, in both dollar amounts and percentages [@CL-MNA-0407; @CL-MNA-0408].

## How it works

The statement answers four paragraphs in order: purposes, alternatives, reasons and effects [@CL-MNA-0400; @CL-MNA-0401; @CL-MNA-0402; @CL-MNA-0403]. The alternatives paragraph applies only where the subject company or affiliate considered alternative means; if it did, the statement must also give the reasons for rejection [@CL-MNA-0401]. The first instruction, that conclusory statements are not sufficient, applies to the whole section [@CL-MNA-0404]. The second and third instructions apply to the effects description: it covers benefits and detriments, quantified to the extent practicable, and an affiliate that files adds its own stake in net book value and net earnings [@CL-MNA-0405; @CL-MNA-0406; @CL-MNA-0407; @CL-MNA-0408]. The terms used here are defined on the [Regulation M-A definitions](../diligence/mna-diligence-regulation-m-a-definitions-and-term-sheet.md) card.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A company proposes to take itself private at 40 per share and files the statement itself. The draft states the purpose as simplifying the ownership structure and gives reasons for the structure and the timing. It says that no other route was considered, and it asserts that the deal is good for the company and its holders. The answer: no description of alternatives is owed, because none was considered [@CL-MNA-0401]. The effects part fails. The assertion is conclusory [@CL-MNA-0404]. The draft gives no discussion of both benefits and detriments [@CL-MNA-0405] and no quantification, although nothing shows quantification is impracticable [@CL-MNA-0406]. It omits the federal tax consequences [@CL-MNA-0403]. The affiliate paragraph does not arise, because the company, not an affiliate, files [@CL-MNA-0407].

## Never confuse

The purposes item asks why the transaction is done and what it does. The [fairness item](mna-going-private-fairness.md) asks whether the subject company or affiliate filing the statement reasonably believes the transaction is fair or unfair to unaffiliated security holders [@CL-MNA-0400; @CL-MNA-0403; @CL-MNA-0409]. The effects discussion is therefore not a fairness statement: a belief about fairness is stated under the fairness item [@CL-MNA-0409].
