## Purpose

Sets out what the statement for a going-private transaction must say about whether the subject company or affiliate filing it reasonably believes the transaction is fair, the factors behind that belief, approval by unaffiliated security holders, an unaffiliated representative, approval by non-employee directors, dissenting directors and other offers [@CL-MNA-0409; @CL-MNA-0411; @CL-MNA-0413; @CL-MNA-0414; @CL-MNA-0416; @CL-MNA-0410; @CL-MNA-0417].

The statement must say whether the subject company or affiliate filing it reasonably believes that the transaction is fair or unfair to unaffiliated security holders [@CL-MNA-0409]. A statement that the issuer or affiliate has no reasonable belief is not sufficient disclosure for that paragraph [@CL-MNA-0418]. If any director dissented to or abstained from voting on the transaction, the statement must identify the director and, if known after reasonable inquiry, indicate the reasons [@CL-MNA-0410].

The statement must discuss in reasonable detail the material factors on which the belief is based and, to the extent practicable, the weight assigned to each factor [@CL-MNA-0411]. The discussion must include an analysis of the extent, if any, to which the filing person's beliefs are based on the factors in the factors instruction, on the holder-approval, representative and director-approval paragraphs, and on the reports and opinions item [@CL-MNA-0412]. A conclusory statement, such as a bare claim of fairness in relation to net book value, going concern value and future prospects, is not sufficient disclosure for the factors discussion [@CL-MNA-0449].

The statement must also say three things. First, whether the transaction is structured so that approval of at least a majority of unaffiliated security holders is required [@CL-MNA-0413]. Second, whether a majority of the directors who are not employees of the subject company has retained an unaffiliated representative to act solely on behalf of unaffiliated security holders, to negotiate the terms of the transaction, to prepare a report on its fairness, or both [@CL-MNA-0414; @CL-MNA-0415]. Third, whether the transaction was approved by a majority of the directors of the subject company who are not employees of the subject company [@CL-MNA-0416].

If a firm offer of the type that the factors instruction describes has been received, the statement must describe the offer and state the reasons for its rejection [@CL-MNA-0417]. That type is a firm offer, of which the subject company or affiliate is aware, made by an unaffiliated person other than the filing persons during the past two years, for a merger or consolidation, for a sale or transfer of all or any substantial part of the assets, or for a purchase of securities that would enable the holder to exercise control [@CL-MNA-0422; @CL-MNA-0423].

## How it works

The belief is the filer's own view and is stated as fair or unfair [@CL-MNA-0409]. The factors discussion carries the substance. The factors that matter, and the weight if any that each deserves, vary with the context [@CL-MNA-1112]. Normally the factors include the holder-approval, representative and director-approval points and whether the consideration offered to unaffiliated security holders is fair value in relation to current market prices, historical market prices, net book value, going concern value and liquidation value [@CL-MNA-0419; @CL-MNA-0420]. They normally also include prices paid in previous purchases disclosed under the prior-purchases item, any report, opinion or appraisal described under the reports item, and the firm offers described above [@CL-MNA-0421; @CL-MNA-0422; @CL-MNA-0423]. The approval and representative points are stated as facts, and the factors discussion must also analyse how far the belief rests on them [@CL-MNA-0412; @CL-MNA-0413; @CL-MNA-0414; @CL-MNA-0416].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A company offers 25 per share against a market price of 18 and a net book value of 12 per share. The draft states only that the board believes the deal is fair. The answer: the draft states a belief, but a bare assertion is conclusory and the draft discusses no material factors or weights [@CL-MNA-0449; @CL-MNA-0411]. Normally the factors include whether the 25 is fair value in relation to current and historical market prices, net book value, going concern value and liquidation value, so the discussion should address them [@CL-MNA-0419; @CL-MNA-0420]. The draft must also say whether approval by a majority of unaffiliated holders is required, whether a majority of the non-employee directors retained an unaffiliated representative, and whether a majority of the non-employee directors approved the deal [@CL-MNA-0413; @CL-MNA-0414; @CL-MNA-0416]. If any director dissented or abstained, the draft must identify that director [@CL-MNA-0410].

## Never confuse

The fairness belief is the filer's own statement of view. A report or appraisal from an outside party is not the filer's own statement of belief: the [reports and opinions item](mna-going-private-reports-opinions-appraisals.md) governs how it is described, although the factors discussion must still analyse the extent, if any, to which the belief rests on that item [@CL-MNA-0409; @CL-MNA-0424; @CL-MNA-0412]. The statement that approval of unaffiliated security holders is or is not required, or that an unaffiliated representative was or was not retained, is a required statement and does not replace the factors discussion [@CL-MNA-0411; @CL-MNA-0413; @CL-MNA-0414]. The effects of the transaction are described under the [purposes item](mna-going-private-purposes-alternatives-reasons-effects.md) [@CL-MNA-0403].
