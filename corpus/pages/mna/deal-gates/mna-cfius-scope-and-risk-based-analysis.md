## Purpose

Explains what the Committee on Foreign Investment in the United States and the President may do under the statute these regulations implement, and the risk-based analysis the Committee conducts before it acts on a covered transaction.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Not established. |
| Codified citation | Not established. |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Not established. |
| Applies to | Covered transactions the Committee may review under section 721, and the Committee's determinations under section 721 [@CL-MNA-0013; @CL-MNA-0016] |

Section 721 of title VII of the Defense Production Act of 1950 authorizes the Committee to review any covered transaction, as defined in § 800.213 [@CL-MNA-0013]. It also authorizes the Committee to mitigate any risk to the national security of the United States that arises as a result of such transactions [@CL-MNA-0556].

Section 721 also authorizes the President to suspend or prohibit any covered transaction when, in the President's judgment, there is credible evidence that leads the President to believe that the foreign person engaging in the transaction might take action that threatens to impair national security [@CL-MNA-0014; @CL-MNA-0573]. The same sentence adds a second condition, joined by "and": in the President's judgment, provisions of law other than section 721 and the International Emergency Economic Powers Act must not provide adequate and appropriate authority to protect national security in the matter [@CL-MNA-0015; @CL-MNA-0574].

Part 800 implements regulations pertaining to covered transactions [@CL-MNA-0557]. Regulations pertaining to covered real estate transactions are addressed in part 802 of the same chapter [@CL-MNA-0557].

## How it works

The Committee reviews covered transactions and may mitigate risk; the President decides whether to suspend or prohibit one [@CL-MNA-0013; @CL-MNA-0556; @CL-MNA-0014].

Any determination of the Committee to suspend a covered transaction, to refer it to the President, or to negotiate, enter into, impose or enforce an agreement or condition under section 721 shall be based on a risk-based analysis [@CL-MNA-0016; @CL-MNA-0017]. The Committee conducts that analysis itself, and it covers the effects of the covered transaction on national security [@CL-MNA-0017].

The analysis shall include credible evidence demonstrating the risk and an assessment of the threat, vulnerabilities and consequences to national security related to the transaction [@CL-MNA-0018]. It shall also include and be informed by consideration of three elements [@CL-MNA-0558].

| Element | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Threat | A function of the intent and capability of a foreign person to take action to impair the national security of the United States [@CL-MNA-0558] |
| Vulnerabilities | The extent to which the nature of the United States business presents susceptibility to impairment of national security [@CL-MNA-0559] |
| Consequences | The potential effects on national security that could reasonably result from the exploitation of the vulnerabilities by the threat actor [@CL-MNA-0560] |

The duty to conduct the analysis falls on the Committee [@CL-MNA-0017]. Neither section states a duty for a party to a transaction.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A fund proposes to buy a business that services a government program. Before the Committee refers the case to the President, its file records credible evidence of the risk [@CL-MNA-0018]. The file assesses the threat as the acquirer's intent and capability to divert the technology [@CL-MNA-0558]. It assesses the vulnerability as the extent to which the business is open to impairment of national security [@CL-MNA-0559]. It assesses the consequence as a loss of supply that could reasonably result if the acquirer exploited that vulnerability [@CL-MNA-0560]. The Committee conducts that analysis itself and bases its referral on it [@CL-MNA-0016; @CL-MNA-0017]. Whether the President then suspends or prohibits the transaction is the President's own judgment about credible evidence and the adequacy of other law [@CL-MNA-0014; @CL-MNA-0015].

## Never confuse

The Committee's risk-based analysis is not the President's decision to suspend or prohibit. The Committee conducts the analysis [@CL-MNA-0017]. The President acts on a separate judgment about credible evidence and the adequacy of other law [@CL-MNA-0014; @CL-MNA-0015].

These two sections state authority and the Committee's analysis. They are not filing rules. The [jurisdiction card](mna-cfius-jurisdiction.md) shows which transactions are covered, and the [review and investigation card](mna-cfius-review-and-investigation.md) covers what follows a filing.
