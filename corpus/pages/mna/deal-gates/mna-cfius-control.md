## Purpose

Shows what control means under the foreign investment review regulations, the factors that count when several foreign persons hold interests, and the minority shareholder protections that do not by themselves confer control.

| Applies to | A foreign person's power to determine, direct, or decide important matters affecting an entity [@CL-MNA-0721] |

This card rests on the regulations on certain investments in the United States by foreign persons. They are not FAR, DFARS or CAS text. Control means power, direct or indirect and whether or not exercised, held through means that include ownership of a majority or a dominant minority of an entity's total outstanding voting interest, board representation, proxy voting, a special share or contractual arrangements [@CL-MNA-0720]. The power that counts is power to determine, direct, or decide important matters affecting an entity, in particular decisions on the matters the paragraph lists or any other similarly important matters [@CL-MNA-0721]. One listed matter is major expenditures or investments, issuances of equity or debt, dividend payments, or approval of the operating budget [@CL-MNA-0722]. The paragraph lists further matters that this card does not restate. Where more than one foreign person has an ownership interest in an entity, consideration will be given to factors such as whether the foreign persons are related or have formal or informal arrangements to act in concert [@CL-MNA-0723].

Minority shareholder protections that the paragraph lists shall not in themselves be deemed to confer control [@CL-MNA-0724]. One listed protection is the power to prevent the sale or pledge of all or substantially all of an entity's assets, or a voluntary filing for bankruptcy or liquidation [@CL-MNA-0724]. Another is the right to purchase an additional interest to prevent dilution of the investor's pro rata interest if the entity issues additional instruments conveying interests in it [@CL-MNA-0724; @CL-MNA-0725]. The paragraph lists further protections that this card does not restate. The Committee will consider, case by case, whether other minority shareholder protections do not confer control [@CL-MNA-0726]. In one listed example, a foreign person that receives the listed minority shareholder protections but no other positive or negative rights with respect to the entity does not control it, assuming no other relevant facts [@CL-MNA-0795].

## How it works

Control is a question of power over important matters, whether or not the power is exercised [@CL-MNA-0720; @CL-MNA-0721]. A foreign person can hold that power through votes, board seats, proxies, special shares or contracts [@CL-MNA-0720]. A listed minority protection is not control by itself, and other protections are judged case by case [@CL-MNA-0724; @CL-MNA-0726]. In one listed example, Corporation A acquires seven percent of Corporation B's voting shares and the right to terminate significant contracts of Corporation B, and Corporation A controls Corporation B [@CL-MNA-0727].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

Foreign Fund F buys 8 percent of the voting shares of Company T. F also gets the right to approve T's annual operating budget. Answer: approval of the operating budget is a listed matter, so F holds power over a matter the control definition names, and the small stake does not settle the question. If F had only the power to prevent the sale of all of T's assets, that is a listed minority protection and is not control by itself.

## Never confuse

Control is a power test, not a share test. In one listed example, the acquirer of seven percent of the voting shares and the right to terminate significant contracts controls the target [@CL-MNA-0727], while a listed minority protection does not confer control by itself [@CL-MNA-0724]. Control is also not the same as being a covered transaction; the [jurisdiction card](mna-cfius-jurisdiction.md) says which transactions are covered, and the [covered control transactions card](mna-cfius-covered-control-transactions.md) lists those that turn on control.

## Company calibration

Whether any foreign person holds or could gain power over the company's budget, equity or debt issuance, dividends or other listed matters, and which minority protections sit in the governing documents. [@Q-045]
