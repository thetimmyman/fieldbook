## Purpose

Shows which foreign investments in a U.S. business fall within the review of the Committee on Foreign Investment in the United States, and what a TID U.S. business is.

| Applies to | A foreign person's direct or indirect investment in an unaffiliated TID U.S. business, and the other covered transactions the text lists [@CL-MNA-0619; @CL-MNA-0622] |

This card rests on the regulations on certain investments in the United States by foreign persons. They are not FAR, DFARS or CAS text.

A covered control transaction is any transaction that is proposed or pending after August 23, 1988, by or with any foreign person that could result in foreign control of any United States business, including such a transaction carried out through a joint venture [@CL-MNA-0655].

A covered investment is any investment, direct or indirect, by a foreign person other than an excepted investor, in an unaffiliated TID U.S. business [@CL-MNA-0619] that is proposed or pending on or after February 13, 2020, and that is not a covered control transaction and affords the foreign person something the paragraph lists next [@CL-MNA-0619]. One listed item is access to any material nonpublic technical information in the possession of the TID U.S. business, and another is membership or observer rights on, or the right to nominate an individual to a position on, the board of directors or equivalent governing body of the TID U.S. business [@CL-MNA-0620]. Notwithstanding paragraph (b), no investment involving an air carrier, as defined in 49 USC 40102(a)(2), that holds a certificate issued under 49 USC 41102 shall be a covered investment [@CL-MNA-0621]. An excepted investor is a foreign person who is, as of the completion date of the transaction and subject to paragraphs (c) and (d) of the section, within a list of categories that follows [@CL-MNA-0656]. This card does not restate the categories.

Minimum excepted ownership is a defined term with two parts. For an entity whose equity securities are primarily traded on an exchange in an excepted foreign state or the United States, it means a majority of its voting interest, the right to a majority of its profits, and the right in the event of dissolution to a majority of its assets [@CL-MNA-1361; @CL-MNA-1362]. For an entity whose equity securities are not primarily traded on such an exchange, it means 80 percent or more of its voting interest, the right to 80 percent or more of its profits, and the right in the event of dissolution to 80 percent or more of its assets [@CL-MNA-1362; @CL-MNA-1363].

A covered transaction is any of several listed items, the first two being a covered control transaction and a covered investment [@CL-MNA-0622]. The text lists further items that this card does not restate.

A TID U.S. business is any U.S. business that produces, designs, tests, manufactures, fabricates or develops one or more critical technologies, or that meets another listed description [@CL-MNA-0623]. One listed description is performing the functions set forth in column 2 of appendix A to part 800 with respect to covered investment critical infrastructure, and another is maintaining or collecting, directly or indirectly, sensitive personal data of United States citizens [@CL-MNA-0624].

Critical technologies is a list of items that begins with defense articles or defense services included on the United States Munitions List [@CL-MNA-0657]. The list has further items that this card does not restate. Covered investment critical infrastructure means, in the context of a particular covered investment, the systems and assets, whether physical or virtual, set forth in column 1 of the appendix [@CL-MNA-0662]. The text also defines sensitive personal data at length, and this card does not restate that definition.

Four verbs have their own definitions solely for the purposes of column 2 of appendix A to part 800 [@CL-MNA-1322; @CL-MNA-1323; @CL-MNA-1324; @CL-MNA-1325]. Manufacture means to produce or reproduce, whether physically or virtually [@CL-MNA-1322]. Own means to directly possess the applicable covered investment critical infrastructure [@CL-MNA-1323]. Service means to repair, maintain, refurbish, replace, overhaul, or update [@CL-MNA-1324]. Supply means to provide third-party physical or cyber security [@CL-MNA-1325].

## How it works

The regulations define the kinds of foreign investment that count: a covered control transaction, a covered investment and further listed items [@CL-MNA-0622]. A covered investment requires an unaffiliated TID U.S. business as its target [@CL-MNA-0619]. A TID U.S. business is one that works with critical technologies, covered investment critical infrastructure or sensitive personal data [@CL-MNA-0623; @CL-MNA-0624].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

Foreign Fund F is not an excepted investor. It takes a small non-controlling stake in Company T, an unaffiliated U.S. business that designs a critical technology. F gets the right to name one director. Answer: T is a TID U.S. business because it designs a critical technology, and the board right makes the investment a covered investment, so it is a covered transaction.

## Never confuse

Being a covered transaction brings a deal inside the Committee's reach [@CL-MNA-0622], and FOCI clearance is a different test instead of the same one: whether a declaration is mandatory is on the [mandatory declarations card](mna-cfius-mandatory-declarations.md). A party may choose a declaration or a notice, as the [voluntary declarations card](mna-cfius-voluntary-declarations-and-notices.md) explains, and the [review and investigation card](mna-cfius-review-and-investigation.md) covers what follows. Foreign ownership, control or influence under the security-clearance regime is a different test; see [Foreign ownership, control or influence and its mitigation](../diligence/mna-nispom-foci-and-mitigation.md).

## Company calibration

Whether any foreign person holds or could acquire an interest, board seat or access in the company or a target, and whether the business is a TID U.S. business. [@Q-045]
