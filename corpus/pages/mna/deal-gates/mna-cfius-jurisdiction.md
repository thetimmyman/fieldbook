## Purpose

Shows which foreign investments in a U.S. business fall within the review of the Committee on Foreign Investment in the United States, and what a TID U.S. business is.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Not established. |
| Codified citation | Not established. |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Not established. |
| Applies to | A foreign person's direct or indirect investment in an unaffiliated TID U.S. business, and the other covered transactions the text lists [@CL-MNA-0619; @CL-MNA-0622] |

This card rests on the regulations on certain investments in the United States by foreign persons. They are not FAR, DFARS or CAS text.

A covered control transaction is any transaction proposed or pending after a stated date by or with a foreign person that could result in foreign control of any U.S. business, including one carried out through a joint venture [@CL-MNA-0655].

A covered investment is a direct or indirect investment by a foreign person other than an excepted investor in an unaffiliated TID U.S. business, proposed or pending on or after a stated date in 2020, that is not a covered control transaction and that affords the foreign person something the paragraph lists next [@CL-MNA-0619]. One listed item is access to material nonpublic technical information of the TID U.S. business, and another is board membership, observer or nomination rights [@CL-MNA-0620]. No investment involving an air carrier that holds a certificate under the cited aviation statute is a covered investment [@CL-MNA-0621]. An excepted investor is a foreign person who is, as of the completion date of the transaction and subject to two later paragraphs of the section, within a list of categories that follows [@CL-MNA-0656]. This card does not restate the categories.

A covered transaction is any of several listed items, the first two being a covered control transaction and a covered investment [@CL-MNA-0622]. The text lists further items that this card does not restate.

A TID U.S. business is any U.S. business that produces, designs, tests, manufactures, fabricates or develops one or more critical technologies, or that meets another listed description [@CL-MNA-0623]. One listed description is performing the appendix functions for covered investment critical infrastructure, and another is maintaining or collecting sensitive personal data of U.S. citizens [@CL-MNA-0624].

Critical technologies is a list of items that begins with defense articles or defense services included on the United States Munitions List [@CL-MNA-0657]. The list has further items that this card does not restate. Covered investment critical infrastructure means, in the context of a particular covered investment, the systems and assets, whether physical or virtual, set forth in column 1 of the appendix [@CL-MNA-0662]. The text also defines sensitive personal data at length, and this card does not restate that definition.

Four verbs have their own definitions solely for the purposes of column 2 of appendix A to part 800 [@CL-MNA-1322; @CL-MNA-1323; @CL-MNA-1324; @CL-MNA-1325]. Manufacture means to produce or reproduce, whether physically or virtually [@CL-MNA-1322]. Own means to directly possess the applicable covered investment critical infrastructure [@CL-MNA-1323]. Service means to repair, maintain, refurbish, replace, overhaul, or update [@CL-MNA-1324]. Supply means to provide third-party physical or cyber security [@CL-MNA-1325].

## How it works

The regulations define the kinds of foreign investment that count: a covered control transaction, a covered investment and further listed items [@CL-MNA-0622]. A covered investment needs a TID U.S. business as its target [@CL-MNA-0619]. A TID U.S. business is one that works with critical technologies, covered infrastructure or sensitive personal data [@CL-MNA-0623; @CL-MNA-0624].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

Foreign Fund F is not an excepted investor. It takes a small non-controlling stake in Company T, an unaffiliated U.S. business that designs a critical technology. F gets the right to name one director. Answer: T is a TID U.S. business because it designs a critical technology, and the board right makes the investment a covered investment, so it is a covered transaction.

## Never confuse

Jurisdiction is not a filing duty. Being a covered transaction brings a deal inside the Committee's reach, and whether a declaration is mandatory is on the [mandatory declarations card](mna-cfius-mandatory-declarations.md). A party may choose a declaration or a notice, as the [voluntary declarations card](mna-cfius-voluntary-declarations-and-notices.md) explains, and the [review and investigation card](mna-cfius-review-and-investigation.md) covers what follows. Foreign ownership, control or influence under the security-clearance regime is a different test; see [Foreign ownership, control or influence and its mitigation](../diligence/mna-nispom-foci-and-mitigation.md).

## Company calibration

Whether any foreign person holds or could acquire an interest, board seat or access in the company or a target, and whether the business is a TID U.S. business. [@Q-045]
