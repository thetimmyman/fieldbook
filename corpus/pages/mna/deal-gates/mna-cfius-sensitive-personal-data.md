## Purpose

Sets out what counts as sensitive personal data: the business tests, the population threshold, the listed categories and the exclusions.

| Applies to | Identifiable data held by a U.S. business, and genetic test results that are identifiable data [@CL-MNA-0826; @CL-MNA-0833] |

This card rests on the regulations on certain investments in the United States by foreign persons. They are not FAR, DFARS or CAS text.

One part of the definition of sensitive personal data, which is subject to the exclusions paragraph, concerns identifiable data that is maintained or collected by a U.S. business that meets one of the listed conditions [@CL-MNA-0826]. The term U.S. business is on the [foreign person card](mna-cfius-foreign-person-parent-us-business.md).

Identifiable data means data that can be used to distinguish or trace an individual's identity, including through the use of any personal identifier [@CL-MNA-1294]. A personal identifier means name, physical address, email address, social security number, phone number, or other information that identifies a specific individual [@CL-MNA-1297]. Aggregated data means data that have been combined or collected together in summary or other form such that the data cannot be identified with any individual [@CL-MNA-1291]. Anonymized data means data from which all personal identifiers have been completely removed [@CL-MNA-1292]. Encrypted data means data to which National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST)-allowed cryptographic techniques, as identified in the most current NIST special publication 800-175B, or superseding publication, have been applied [@CL-MNA-1293].

One listed business test is that the U.S. business targets or tailors products or services to any U.S. executive branch agency or military department with intelligence, national security or homeland security responsibilities, or to their personnel and contractors [@CL-MNA-0827]. The meaning of targets or tailors is on the [targets or tailors card](mna-cfius-technical-information-decisionmaking-and-targeting.md). Another listed test is that the business has maintained or collected identifiable data within the listed categories on greater than one million individuals at any point over the twelve months preceding the earliest of a set of dates that begins with the completion date [@CL-MNA-0828]. The other dates in that set are the date of any of the events described in paragraphs (b)(2) through (4) of the [applicability rule](mna-cfius-party-transaction-and-applicability.md), as applicable, and the date of filing of a written notice or submission of a declaration [@CL-MNA-0917]. That population test does not apply if the business can demonstrate that at the time of the completion date of the transaction it had or will have neither the capability to maintain nor the capability to collect the data the test covers [@CL-MNA-0829]. The data in question is identifiable data within the listed categories on greater than one million individuals [@CL-MNA-0918]. A third listed test is a demonstrated business objective to maintain or collect such data on greater than one million individuals, where the data is an integrated part of the business's primary products or services [@CL-MNA-0830].

The data must also be within one of the listed categories, the first of which is financial data that could be used to analyze or determine an individual's financial distress or hardship [@CL-MNA-0831]. Two further listed categories are the set of data in an application for health insurance, long-term care insurance, professional liability insurance, mortgage insurance or life insurance, and data relating to the physical, mental or psychological health condition of an individual [@CL-MNA-0832]. A further listed category is non-public electronic communications, including email, messaging or chat communications, between or among users of a U.S. business's products or services if a primary purpose of the product or service is to facilitate third-party user communications [@CL-MNA-0919]. Two more are geolocation data collected using positioning systems, cell phone towers or WiFi access points, and biometric enrollment data including facial, voice, retina or iris, and palm or fingerprint templates [@CL-MNA-0920]. Two more are data concerning government personnel security clearance status, and the set of data in an application for a government personnel security clearance or for employment in a position of public trust [@CL-MNA-0921]. The definition lists further categories, including consumer report data and data stored and processed for generating a government identification card, that this card does not restate.

Subject to the exclusions paragraph, the results of an individual's genetic tests, including any related genetic sequencing data, are covered whenever the results constitute identifiable data, but not data derived from databases maintained by the federal government and routinely provided to private parties for purposes of research [@CL-MNA-0833].

The term does not include the data in the listed exclusions, regardless of whether the criteria in the definition apply [@CL-MNA-0834]. Two listed exclusions are data a U.S. business maintains or collects concerning its own employees, unless the data pertains to employees of government contractors who hold government personnel security clearances, and data that is a matter of public record [@CL-MNA-0835].

## How it works

The definition has two numbered parts, called branches here, and it is subject to the exclusions paragraph.

Branch one covers identifiable data held by a U.S. business that meets a listed test and falls within a listed category. The data must be identifiable and held by a U.S. business [@CL-MNA-0826]. The business must meet a listed test: serving government security customers, holding data on a large population, or having a demonstrated objective to do so with the data built into its main products [@CL-MNA-0827; @CL-MNA-0828; @CL-MNA-0830]. The population test looks back over a twelve-month window that ends at the earliest of several dates, and it has a carve-out for a business that can show it lacked the capability [@CL-MNA-0828; @CL-MNA-0917; @CL-MNA-0829; @CL-MNA-0918]. The data must also be in a listed category [@CL-MNA-0831].

Branch two covers the results of an individual's genetic tests, including any related genetic sequencing data, whenever the results constitute identifiable data [@CL-MNA-0833]. The text of that part states no business test and no listed category.

The term does not include the listed exclusions, regardless of the applicability of the criteria in the definition [@CL-MNA-0834].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

Company P is a U.S. business that runs a health-tracking service. At one point in the twelve months before the completion date, P held health-condition data on 1.2 million people, and it holds data on only 800,000 today. P cannot show that it lacked the capability to maintain or collect such data. Assume the completion date is the earliest of the dates in the look-back rule.

Answer: the population test looks back over the window, so P meets it even though today's count is lower. The health-condition data is in a listed category, so P's data is sensitive personal data unless an exclusion applies.

## Never confuse

Sensitive personal data is not every customer or personnel record. Data about a business's own employees is excluded unless it concerns employees of government contractors who hold personnel security clearances [@CL-MNA-0835]. Data that is a matter of public record is also excluded [@CL-MNA-0835]. Holding sensitive personal data matters to foreign investment review through the tests on the [jurisdiction card](mna-cfius-jurisdiction.md), which this card does not restate.

Aggregated data or anonymized data is identifiable data if any party to the transaction has, or as a result of the transaction will have, the ability to disaggregate or de-anonymize the data, or if the data is otherwise capable of being used to distinguish or trace an individual's identity [@CL-MNA-1295]. Identifiable data does not include encrypted data, unless the U.S. business that maintains or collects the encrypted data has the means to de-encrypt the data so as to distinguish or trace an individual's identity [@CL-MNA-1296].

## Company calibration

What identifiable data in the listed categories the company or a target holds, on how many individuals over the look-back window, and whether any employee data concerns cleared contractor staff. [@Q-045]
