## Purpose

Fixes the four terms a deal team uses to decide whether a company is a special purpose acquisition company, who its sponsor is, what a target company is and what counts as a de-SPAC transaction [@CL-MNA-1143; @CL-MNA-1145; @CL-MNA-1150; @CL-MNA-1151]. The disclosure cards apply these terms: see [registered offerings and the sponsor](mna-spac-registered-offering-disclosure.md) and [de-SPAC transactions](mna-de-spac-transaction-disclosure.md).

The definitions in 17 CFR 229.1601 are stated for the purposes of the subpart that contains them [@CL-MNA-1142].

Item 1610 separately says the disclosure that the subpart requires is to be provided in an Interactive Data File in accordance with §§ 232.405 (Rule 405 of Regulation S-T) and 232.301 (the EDGAR Filer Manual) of the same chapter [@CL-MNA-1349].

A de-SPAC transaction is a business combination, such as a merger, consolidation, exchange of securities, acquisition of assets, reorganization or similar transaction, involving a special purpose acquisition company and one or more target companies [@CL-MNA-1143]. In the case of more than one target company, the target companies are involved contemporaneously [@CL-MNA-1144].

A special purpose acquisition company is a company that meets one of two alternatives [@CL-MNA-1145; @CL-MNA-1149]. The first alternative is that it has indicated that its business plan is to do all three of the things that follow [@CL-MNA-1145; @CL-MNA-1146]. The plan is to conduct a primary offering of securities that is not subject to the requirements of Rule 419 under the Securities Act (17 CFR 230.419) [@CL-MNA-1145]. It is also to complete a business combination, such as a merger, consolidation, exchange of securities, acquisition of assets, reorganization or similar transaction, with one or more target companies within a specified time frame [@CL-MNA-1146]. And it is to return proceeds from the offering and any concurrent offering (if the offering or the concurrent offering intends to raise proceeds) to its security holders if the company does not complete a business combination [@CL-MNA-1147]. That business combination is again one with one or more target companies within the specified time frame [@CL-MNA-1148]. The second alternative is that the company has represented that it pursues or will pursue a special purpose acquisition company strategy [@CL-MNA-1149].

A SPAC sponsor is any entity and/or person primarily responsible for organizing, directing or managing the business and affairs of a special purpose acquisition company [@CL-MNA-1150]. The term excludes, if an entity is a SPAC sponsor, officers and directors of the company who are not affiliates of any such entity that is a SPAC sponsor [@CL-MNA-1192]. A target company is an operating company, business or assets [@CL-MNA-1151].

### Terms used here

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| De-SPAC transaction | A business combination, such as a merger, consolidation, exchange of securities, acquisition of assets, reorganization or similar transaction, involving a special purpose acquisition company and one or more target companies, contemporaneously where there is more than one target company [@CL-MNA-1143; @CL-MNA-1144] |
| Special purpose acquisition company (SPAC) | A company that has indicated a business plan with three elements (a primary offering not subject to Rule 419, a business combination within a specified time frame, and a return of proceeds if none is completed), or that has represented that it pursues or will pursue a special purpose acquisition company strategy [@CL-MNA-1145; @CL-MNA-1146; @CL-MNA-1147; @CL-MNA-1148; @CL-MNA-1149] |
| SPAC sponsor | Any entity and/or person primarily responsible for organizing, directing or managing the business and affairs of a special purpose acquisition company, excluding, if an entity is a SPAC sponsor, officers and directors who are not affiliates of any such entity that is a SPAC sponsor [@CL-MNA-1150; @CL-MNA-1192] |
| Target company | An operating company, business or assets [@CL-MNA-1151] |

## How it works

A company can meet the definition either by what it has indicated its business plan is or by what it has represented [@CL-MNA-1145; @CL-MNA-1149]. The two alternatives are separate: the second is met by a representation that the company pursues or will pursue the strategy [@CL-MNA-1149]. On the first alternative the business plan must contain all three elements [@CL-MNA-1146]. The sponsor term turns on who is primarily responsible for organizing, directing or managing the company's business and affairs [@CL-MNA-1150]. The exclusion for officers and directors applies if an entity is a SPAC sponsor, and to those who are not affiliates of any such entity [@CL-MNA-1192]. An officer or director who is an affiliate of the entity sponsor is therefore not covered by the exclusion [@CL-MNA-1192].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A company states that its business plan is to raise 200,000,000 in a primary offering that is not subject to Rule 419, to complete a merger with one or more target companies within 24 months, and to return the proceeds to its security holders if it does not complete a merger in that time [@CL-MNA-1145; @CL-MNA-1146; @CL-MNA-1147]. It meets the first alternative, so it is a special purpose acquisition company. A merger of that company with an operating company is a de-SPAC transaction [@CL-MNA-1143; @CL-MNA-1151]. A second company has no stated plan but represents that it pursues a special purpose acquisition company strategy. It meets the second alternative and is also a special purpose acquisition company [@CL-MNA-1149]. If an entity sponsors the first company, its non-affiliated officers and directors are excluded from the sponsor term, but an officer who is an affiliate of the entity is not [@CL-MNA-1150; @CL-MNA-1192].

## Never confuse

Not every officer or director is a SPAC sponsor: if an entity is a SPAC sponsor, officers and directors who are not affiliates of any such entity are excluded from the term, and the exclusion does not reach those who are affiliates [@CL-MNA-1192]. A de-SPAC transaction is the business combination involving the company and one or more target companies [@CL-MNA-1143]; the primary offering is a separate element of the first alternative [@CL-MNA-1145].

## Company calibration

Confirm which business-plan statements and representations the company or a counterparty has made, so the deal gate records the right alternative in the definition. [@Q-028]
