## Purpose

Shows what the disclosure document for a roll-up transaction of partnerships covers under Items 901 to 915 of Regulation S-K, from the definitions and the summary to the fairness statement, the reports and appraisals, the funding and the tax consequences [@CL-MNA-1425; @CL-MNA-1435; @CL-MNA-1448; @CL-MNA-1451; @CL-MNA-1454; @CL-MNA-1460].

### Definitions

Except as provided in paragraph (c)(2) or (c)(3) of Item 901, a roll-up transaction means a transaction involving the combination or reorganization of one or more partnerships, directly or indirectly [@CL-MNA-1425], in which some or all of the investors in any of such partnerships will receive new securities, or securities in another entity [@CL-MNA-1426]. Partnership means any finite-life limited partnership or other finite-life entity [@CL-MNA-1427]. It does not include any entity registered under the Investment Company Act of 1940 or any Business Development Company as defined in section 2(a)(48) of that Act [@CL-MNA-1428].

Notwithstanding paragraph (c)(1), the term roll-up transaction shall not include the transactions listed in paragraph (c)(2) [@CL-MNA-1429]. The listed transactions include one in which the securities to be issued or exchanged are not required to be and are not registered under the Securities Act of 1933 [@CL-MNA-1430], and one that involves only issuers that are not required to register or report under Section 12 of the Securities Exchange Act of 1934, both before and after the transaction [@CL-MNA-1431]. They also include one in which the investors in any of the partnerships involved are not subject to a significant adverse change with respect to voting rights, the terms of existence of the entity, management compensation or investment objectives [@CL-MNA-1432]. The sponsor is the person proposing the roll-up transaction [@CL-MNA-1433]. The successor is the surviving entity after completion of the roll-up transaction or the entity whose securities are being offered or sold to, or acquired by, limited partners of the partnerships or the limited partnerships to be combined or reorganized [@CL-MNA-1434].

### Summary, risks and comparison with the successor

Item 903 requires a clear, concise and comprehensible summary of the roll-up transaction in the forepart of the disclosure document [@CL-MNA-1435]. Immediately following it, Item 904 requires a description in reasonable detail of each material risk and effect of the roll-up transaction on investors in each partnership [@CL-MNA-1436], and says to quantify each risk or effect to the extent practicable [@CL-MNA-1437].

Item 905 calls for a description of the voting and other rights of investors in the successor under the successor's governing instruments and under applicable law [@CL-MNA-1438], and of each item of compensation (including reimbursement of expenses) payable by the successor after the roll-up transaction to the general partner and its affiliates or to any affiliate of the successor [@CL-MNA-1439]. It also calls for a table demonstrating the changes in such compensation and distributions [@CL-MNA-1440]. The table is to set forth the actual amounts paid by the partnerships on a combined basis to the general partner and its affiliates for the partnerships' last three fiscal years and most recently ended interim periods [@CL-MNA-1441], and the amounts that would have been paid if the structure to be in effect after the roll-up transaction had been in effect during such period [@CL-MNA-1442].

### Allocation, recommendation, representation and fairness

Item 906 requires a description in reasonable detail of the method used to allocate interests in the successor to investors in the partnerships and the reasons why such method was used [@CL-MNA-1443], and a table showing the calculation of the valuation of each partnership and the allocation of interests in the successor to investors [@CL-MNA-1444]. Item 908 requires a statement whether the general partner recommends the roll-up transaction and a brief description of the reasons for the recommendation [@CL-MNA-1445]. Item 909 requires a statement whether or not the general partner has retained an unaffiliated representative to act on behalf of investors for purposes of negotiating the terms of the roll-up transaction [@CL-MNA-1446]. If none has been retained, it says to describe the reasons and the risks arising from the absence of separate representation [@CL-MNA-1447].

Item 910 requires a statement whether the general partner reasonably believes that the roll-up transaction is fair or unfair to investors and the reasons for such belief [@CL-MNA-1448]. That discussion must address the fairness of the roll-up transaction to investors in each of the partnerships and as a whole [@CL-MNA-1449]. A statement that the general partner has no reasonable belief as to the fairness of the roll-up transaction to investors will not be considered sufficient disclosure in response to paragraph (a) of Item 910 [@CL-MNA-1450].

### Reports, appraisals, funding, appraisal rights, pro forma figures and tax

All reports, opinions or appraisals referred to in paragraph (a)(1) of Item 911 shall be filed as exhibits to the registration statement [@CL-MNA-1451]. For Items 911 and 902, an asset is “significant” to a partnership if it represents more than 10% of the value of the partnership's assets as of the end of the most recently-completed fiscal year or recently-completed interim period [@CL-MNA-1452]. An asset is also “significant” if 10% or more of the partnership's cash flow or net income for the most recently-completed fiscal year or most recently-completed subsequent interim period was derived from such asset [@CL-MNA-1453].

Item 912 requires a statement of the source and total amount of funds or other consideration to be used in the roll-up transaction [@CL-MNA-1454], and a reasonably itemized statement of all expenses incurred or estimated to be incurred in connection with it, including filing fees, legal, financial advisory, accounting and appraisal fees, solicitation expenses and printing costs [@CL-MNA-1455]. Item 913 requires a statement whether or not appraisal rights are provided under applicable state law, under the partnership's governing instruments or will be voluntarily accorded by the successor, the general partner or the sponsor (or any of their affiliates) [@CL-MNA-1456]. Item 914 requires pro forma financial information (including oil and gas reserves and cash flow disclosure, if appropriate), assuming that all partnerships participate in the roll-up transaction [@CL-MNA-1457], and assuming participation of those partnerships that on a combined basis have the lowest combined net cash provided by operating activities for the last fiscal year of such partnerships [@CL-MNA-1458], provided participation by such partnerships satisfies all conditions to consummation of the roll-up transaction [@CL-MNA-1459]. Item 915 requires a brief, clear and understandable summary of the material Federal income tax consequences of the roll-up transaction and an investment in the successor [@CL-MNA-1460].

The card does not restate the definition of the general partner or the test for a finite-life entity, the other exclusions from the roll-up transaction definition and the Commission's power to exempt, or the instruction that applies the subpart to each entity in the transaction. It leaves out the individual partnership supplements, the other contents of the summary, the other risk and comparison items, the background of the transaction, the reasons and alternatives, the other conflict, fairness, report and appraisal requirements, the lender and expense details, the selected financial data and the rest of the appraisal rights and tax items.

## How it works

The first question is whether the transaction is a roll-up transaction and whether an exclusion applies [@CL-MNA-1425; @CL-MNA-1429]. If it is, the disclosure document gives a summary and a risk discussion up front [@CL-MNA-1435; @CL-MNA-1436], compares the successor with the partnerships on rights and compensation [@CL-MNA-1438; @CL-MNA-1439; @CL-MNA-1440], and explains the allocation method, the general partner's recommendation, the representation of investors and the fairness belief [@CL-MNA-1443; @CL-MNA-1445; @CL-MNA-1446; @CL-MNA-1448]. Reports, opinions and appraisals are filed as exhibits [@CL-MNA-1451], and the document shows the funding, the expenses, the pro forma figures and the tax consequences [@CL-MNA-1454; @CL-MNA-1455; @CL-MNA-1457; @CL-MNA-1460].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

Three partnerships are combined into a new entity, and investors in each receive units of the new entity that are registered under the Securities Act. It is a roll-up transaction because some investors receive securities in another entity and no listed exclusion applies. The compensation table covers the last three fiscal years and shows what the general partner was paid and what it would have been paid under the new structure. A property that produced 12 percent of one partnership's net income is significant for that partnership because the share is 10 percent or more, and any appraisal of it is filed as an exhibit.

## Never confuse

A roll-up transaction combines or reorganizes partnerships and gives their investors securities in another entity. It is not the business combination of a special purpose acquisition company, which is on [Disclosure in registered offerings by special purpose acquisition companies](mna-spac-registered-offering-disclosure.md). The general partner's fairness statement here is a different item from the fairness discussion in a going-private transaction, see [Fairness of a going-private transaction](../valuation/mna-going-private-fairness.md), and the reports and appraisals filed here are not those described in [Reports, opinions, appraisals and negotiations in a going-private transaction](../valuation/mna-going-private-reports-opinions-appraisals.md).

## Company calibration

Confirm how the company values each partnership and allocates interests in the successor, so the allocation table and the pro forma figures agree. [@Q-029]
