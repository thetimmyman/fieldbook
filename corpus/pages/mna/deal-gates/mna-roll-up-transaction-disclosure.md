## Purpose

Shows what the disclosure document for a roll-up transaction of partnerships covers under Items 901 to 915 of Regulation S-K, from the definitions and the summary to the fairness statement, the reports and appraisals, the funding and the tax consequences [@CL-MNA-1425; @CL-MNA-1435; @CL-MNA-1448; @CL-MNA-1451; @CL-MNA-1454; @CL-MNA-1460].

### Definitions

Except as provided in paragraph (c)(2) or (c)(3) of Item 901, a roll-up transaction means a transaction involving the combination or reorganization of one or more partnerships, directly or indirectly [@CL-MNA-1425], in which some or all of the investors in any of such partnerships will receive new securities, or securities in another entity [@CL-MNA-1426]. Partnership means any finite-life limited partnership or other finite-life entity [@CL-MNA-1427]. It does not include any entity registered under the Investment Company Act of 1940 or any Business Development Company as defined in section 2(a)(48) of that Act [@CL-MNA-1428].

Notwithstanding paragraph (c)(1), the term roll-up transaction shall not include the transactions listed in paragraph (c)(2) [@CL-MNA-1429]. The listed transactions include one in which the securities to be issued or exchanged are not required to be and are not registered under the Securities Act of 1933 [@CL-MNA-1430], and one that involves only issuers that are not required to register or report under Section 12 of the Securities Exchange Act of 1934, both before and after the transaction [@CL-MNA-1431]. They also include one in which the investors in any of the partnerships involved are not subject to a significant adverse change with respect to voting rights, the terms of existence of the entity, management compensation or investment objectives [@CL-MNA-1432]. The sponsor is the person proposing the roll-up transaction [@CL-MNA-1433]. The successor is the surviving entity after completion of the roll-up transaction or the entity whose securities are being offered or sold to, or acquired by, limited partners of the partnerships or the limited partnerships to be combined or reorganized [@CL-MNA-1434].

### Summary, risks and comparison with the successor

Item 903 requires a clear, concise and comprehensible summary of the roll-up transaction in the forepart of the disclosure document [@CL-MNA-1435]. Immediately following it, Item 904 requires a description in reasonable detail of each material risk and effect of the roll-up transaction on investors in each partnership [@CL-MNA-1436], and says to quantify each risk or effect to the extent practicable [@CL-MNA-1437].

Item 905 calls for a description of the voting and other rights of investors in the successor under the successor's governing instruments and under applicable law [@CL-MNA-1438], and of each item of compensation (including reimbursement of expenses) payable by the successor after the roll-up transaction to the general partner and its affiliates or to any affiliate of the successor [@CL-MNA-1439]. It also calls for a table demonstrating the changes in such compensation and distributions [@CL-MNA-1440]. The table is to set forth the actual amounts paid by the partnerships on a combined basis to the general partner and its affiliates for the partnerships' last three fiscal years and most recently ended interim periods [@CL-MNA-1441], and the amounts that would have been paid if the structure to be in effect after the roll-up transaction had been in effect during such period [@CL-MNA-1442].

### Allocation, recommendation, representation and fairness

Item 906 requires a description in reasonable detail of the method used to allocate interests in the successor to investors in the partnerships and the reasons why such method was used [@CL-MNA-1443], and a table showing the calculation of the valuation of each partnership and the allocation of interests in the successor to investors [@CL-MNA-1444]. Item 908 requires a statement whether the general partner recommends the roll-up transaction and a brief description of the reasons for the recommendation [@CL-MNA-1445]. Item 909 requires a statement whether or not the general partner has retained an unaffiliated representative to act on behalf of investors for purposes of negotiating the terms of the roll-up transaction [@CL-MNA-1446]. If none has been retained, it says to describe the reasons and the risks arising from the absence of separate representation [@CL-MNA-1447].

Item 910 requires a statement whether the general partner reasonably believes that the roll-up transaction is fair or unfair to investors and the reasons for such belief [@CL-MNA-1448]. That discussion must address the fairness of the roll-up transaction to investors in each of the partnerships and as a whole, and if the roll-up transaction may be completed with a combination of partnerships consisting of less than all partnerships, or with portions of partnerships, the belief stated must address each possible combination [@CL-MNA-1449; @CL-MNA-1535]. A statement that the general partner has no reasonable belief as to the fairness of the roll-up transaction to investors will not be considered sufficient disclosure in response to paragraph (a) of Item 910 [@CL-MNA-1450].

### Partnership supplements and the background of the transaction

If two or more entities are proposed to be included in the roll-up transaction, the information specified in Item 902 is to be provided in a separate supplement to the disclosure document for each entity [@CL-MNA-1482]. The supplement shall be filed as part of the registration statement and delivered with the prospectus to investors in the partnership covered by it [@CL-MNA-1483]. Its forepart carries a statement that supplements have been prepared for each partnership and that the effects of the roll-up transaction may be different for investors in the various partnerships [@CL-MNA-1484]. The same statement says that, on a written request by an investor or his representative so designated in writing, a copy of any supplement will be transmitted promptly, without charge, by the general partner or sponsor [@CL-MNA-1485], and it must include the name and address of the person to whom investors should make their request [@CL-MNA-1486]. The supplement includes a brief description of each material risk and effect of the roll-up transaction, including federal income tax consequences, for investors in the partnership [@CL-MNA-1498], with appropriate cross references to the discussions required by Items 904 and 915 [@CL-MNA-1499]. That discussion shall address the effect of the roll-up transaction on the partnership's financial condition and results of operations [@CL-MNA-1487]. It also includes a statement whether the general partner reasonably believes that the roll-up transaction is fair or unfair to investors in the partnership, with a brief discussion of the bases for the belief [@CL-MNA-1500], cross referenced to the Item 910 discussion [@CL-MNA-1501]. If there are material differences between the fairness analysis for the partnership and for the other partnerships, such differences shall be described briefly in the supplement [@CL-MNA-1502]. The supplement includes a brief, narrative description of the method of calculating the value of the partnership and allocating interests in the successor to the partnership, and a table showing the calculation and allocation [@CL-MNA-1503]. The table shall include the information listed in paragraph (b)(4), or other information of a comparable character necessary to a thorough understanding of the calculation and allocation [@CL-MNA-1504]: the appraised value of each separately appraised significant asset held by the partnership [@CL-MNA-1505], or, if appraisals have not been obtained for each significant asset, the value assigned to each significant asset for which an appraisal has not been obtained [@CL-MNA-1506]; the dollar amount of mortgages or similar liabilities on those assets, cash and cash equivalents, other assets and other liabilities [@CL-MNA-1507]; the value assigned to the partnership, in total and per interest held by investors [@CL-MNA-1508]; the aggregate number of interests in the successor to be allocated to the partnership and their percentage of the total [@CL-MNA-1513]; the number of interests in the successor to be allocated to investors for each interest they hold [@CL-MNA-1514]; and the value assigned to the general partner's interest in the partnership and the interests in the successor or other consideration to be allocated to the general partner for it [@CL-MNA-1515], or otherwise as compensation or reimbursement for claims against or interests in the partnership, such as foregone fees, unearned fees and fees to be earned on a sale or refinancing of an asset [@CL-MNA-1516]. The supplement also shows the compensation paid and cash distributions made to the general partner and its affiliates by the partnership for the last three fiscal years and the most recently completed interim period [@CL-MNA-1509], and the amounts that would have been paid if the structure to be in effect after the roll-up transaction had been in effect during such period [@CL-MNA-1510]. If any proposed change in the successor's business or operations after the roll-up transaction would change materially the compensation and distributions that would have been paid by the successor [@CL-MNA-1517], the supplement describes such changes and their effects on the compensation and distributions to be paid by the successor [@CL-MNA-1518]. The supplement also gives cash distributions made to investors during each of the last five fiscal years and most recently completed interim period, identifying any that represent a return of capital [@CL-MNA-1488], and a cross reference to selected financial information about the partnership and the pro forma financial statements in the principal disclosure document [@CL-MNA-1489].

Item 907 requires a summary of the background of the transaction [@CL-MNA-1490]. It also says to describe briefly the background of each partnership, including the capital raised from investors and the extent to which the net proceeds from the original offering have been invested [@CL-MNA-1511], the extent to which funds have been invested as planned and the amount not yet invested, and the partnership's investment objectives and the extent to which it has achieved them [@CL-MNA-1512]. The summary shall include, but not be limited to, a description of any contacts, negotiations or transactions concerning a merger, consolidation, or combination of any of the partnerships [@CL-MNA-1491], an acquisition of any of the partnerships or a material amount of their assets, a tender offer for or other acquisition of their securities, or a change in control of any of them [@CL-MNA-1492]. It shall cover the period beginning with each partnership's second full fiscal year preceding the date of the filing of the roll-up transaction [@CL-MNA-1493], include contacts, negotiations or transactions between the general partner or its affiliates and any person with a direct interest in those matters, and identify the person who initiated them [@CL-MNA-1494]. Paragraph (c) says to discuss whether the general partner (including any affiliated person materially dependent on the general partner's compensation arrangement with the partnership) or any partnership has experienced, since the commencement of the most recently completed fiscal year [@CL-MNA-1495], or is likely to experience, any material adverse financial developments [@CL-MNA-1496]. If so, such developments and the effect of the transaction on such matters are to be described [@CL-MNA-1497].

### Reports, appraisals, funding, appraisal rights, pro forma figures and tax

Item 911 asks whether the general partner or sponsor has received any report, opinion (other than an opinion of counsel) or appraisal from an outside party which is materially related to the roll-up transaction, including any such report, opinion or appraisal on the consideration or its fairness to investors or on the fairness of the transaction to the general partner or investors [@CL-MNA-1476; @CL-MNA-1477]. All reports, opinions or appraisals referred to in that paragraph (a)(1) shall be filed as exhibits to the registration statement [@CL-MNA-1451]. For Items 911 and 902, an asset is “significant” to a partnership if it represents more than 10% of the value of the partnership's assets as of the end of the most recently-completed fiscal year or recently-completed interim period [@CL-MNA-1452]. An asset is also “significant” if 10% or more of the partnership's cash flow or net income for the most recently-completed fiscal year or most recently-completed subsequent interim period was derived from such asset [@CL-MNA-1453].

Item 912 requires a statement of the source and total amount of funds or other consideration to be used in the roll-up transaction [@CL-MNA-1454], and a reasonably itemized statement of all expenses incurred or estimated to be incurred in connection with it, including filing fees, legal, financial advisory, accounting and appraisal fees, solicitation expenses and printing costs [@CL-MNA-1455]. Item 913 requires a statement whether or not appraisal rights are provided under applicable state law, under the partnership's governing instruments or will be voluntarily accorded by the successor, the general partner or the sponsor (or any of their affiliates) [@CL-MNA-1456]. Item 914 requires pro forma financial information (including oil and gas reserves and cash flow disclosure, if appropriate), assuming that all partnerships participate in the roll-up transaction [@CL-MNA-1457], and assuming participation of those partnerships that on a combined basis have the lowest combined net cash provided by operating activities for the last fiscal year of such partnerships [@CL-MNA-1458], provided participation by such partnerships satisfies all conditions to consummation of the roll-up transaction [@CL-MNA-1459]; if the combination of all partnerships proposed to be included results in that lowest combined net cash provided by operating activities, this is noted and no separate pro forma financial statements are required [@CL-MNA-1459; @CL-MNA-1538]. Item 915 requires a brief, clear and understandable summary of the material Federal income tax consequences of the roll-up transaction and an investment in the successor [@CL-MNA-1460].

The card does not restate the definition of the general partner or the test for a finite-life entity, the other exclusions from the roll-up transaction definition and the Commission's power to exempt, or the instruction that applies the subpart to each entity in the transaction. It leaves out the other contents of the summary, the other risk and comparison items, the reasons and alternatives, the other conflict, fairness, report and appraisal requirements, the lender and expense details, the selected financial data and the rest of the appraisal rights and tax items.

## How it works

The first question is whether the transaction is a roll-up transaction and whether an exclusion applies [@CL-MNA-1425; @CL-MNA-1429]. If it is, the disclosure document gives a summary and a risk discussion up front [@CL-MNA-1435; @CL-MNA-1436], compares the successor with the partnerships on rights and compensation [@CL-MNA-1438; @CL-MNA-1439; @CL-MNA-1440], and explains the allocation method, the general partner's recommendation, the representation of investors and the fairness belief [@CL-MNA-1443; @CL-MNA-1445; @CL-MNA-1446; @CL-MNA-1448]. Reports, opinions and appraisals that the general partner or sponsor received from an outside party and that are materially related to the transaction are filed as exhibits [@CL-MNA-1476; @CL-MNA-1451], and the document shows the funding, the expenses, the pro forma figures and the tax consequences [@CL-MNA-1454; @CL-MNA-1455; @CL-MNA-1457; @CL-MNA-1460].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

Three partnerships are combined into a new entity, and investors in each receive units of the new entity that are registered under the Securities Act. It is a roll-up transaction because some investors receive securities in another entity and no listed exclusion applies. The compensation table covers the last three fiscal years and shows what the general partner was paid and what it would have been paid under the new structure. A property that produced 12 percent of one partnership's net income is significant for that partnership because the share is 10 percent or more. Significance alone does not make an appraisal of that property an exhibit: an appraisal is filed when the general partner received it from an outside party and it is materially related to the roll-up transaction.

## Never confuse

A roll-up transaction combines or reorganizes partnerships and gives their investors securities in another entity. It is not the business combination of a special purpose acquisition company, which is on [Disclosure in registered offerings by special purpose acquisition companies](mna-spac-registered-offering-disclosure.md). The general partner's fairness statement here is a different item from the fairness discussion in a going-private transaction, see [Fairness of a going-private transaction](../valuation/mna-going-private-fairness.md), and the reports and appraisals filed here are not those described in [Reports, opinions, appraisals and negotiations in a going-private transaction](../valuation/mna-going-private-reports-opinions-appraisals.md).

## Company calibration

Confirm how the company values each partnership and allocates interests in the successor, so the allocation table and the pro forma figures agree. [@Q-029]
