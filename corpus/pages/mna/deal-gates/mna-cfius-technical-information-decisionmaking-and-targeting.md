## Purpose

Defines three terms used in the foreign investment review rules: material nonpublic technical information, substantive decisionmaking, and targets or tailors.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Not established. |
| Codified citation | Not established. |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Not established. |
| Applies to | Information, decisions and marketing tested under the three definitions [@CL-MNA-0836; @CL-MNA-0839; @CL-MNA-0841] |

This card rests on the regulations on certain investments in the United States by foreign persons. They are not FAR, DFARS or CAS text.

Material nonpublic technical information is information that provides knowledge, know-how or understanding, in each case not available in the public domain, of the design, location or operation of covered investment critical infrastructure [@CL-MNA-0836]. A second listed kind is information that is not available in the public domain and is necessary to design, fabricate, develop, test, produce or manufacture a critical technology, including processes, techniques or methods [@CL-MNA-0837]. The term does not include financial information regarding the performance of an entity [@CL-MNA-0838].

Substantive decisionmaking is the process through which decisions regarding significant matters affecting an entity are undertaken, including, as applicable, pricing, sales and specific contracts, including the license, sale or transfer of sensitive personal data to any third party [@CL-MNA-0839]. The term does not include strictly administrative decisions [@CL-MNA-0840]. The listed matters also include supply arrangements, corporate strategy and business development, research and development including location and budget allocation, and manufacturing locations [@CL-MNA-0922]. They also include access to critical technologies, covered investment critical infrastructure, material nonpublic technical information or sensitive personal data, including pursuant to a customer, vendor or joint venture agreement [@CL-MNA-0923]. They also include physical and cyber security protocols, including the storage and protection of critical technologies, covered investment critical infrastructure or sensitive personal data [@CL-MNA-0924]. The definition lists further matters, on practices for sensitive personal data and on strategic partnerships, that this card does not restate.

Targets or tailors means customizing products or services for use by a person or group of persons or actively marketing to or soliciting a person or group of persons [@CL-MNA-0841]. The term is used in one of the listed business tests for sensitive personal data [@CL-MNA-0827], which is on the [sensitive personal data card](mna-cfius-sensitive-personal-data.md).

## How it works

Each term is a test used elsewhere in the rules: what information is held, what decisions are made, or how a business markets itself. Technical information counts only if it is not public and falls in one of two listed kinds [@CL-MNA-0836; @CL-MNA-0837]. Decisionmaking counts when it concerns significant matters such as those listed, and strictly administrative decisions are left out [@CL-MNA-0839; @CL-MNA-0922; @CL-MNA-0923; @CL-MNA-0924; @CL-MNA-0840]. Targeting turns on customizing for, or actively marketing to, the person or group [@CL-MNA-0841]. The [covered investments card](mna-cfius-covered-investments.md) explains where these terms matter, and this card does not restate it.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

Company T runs a control system that is covered investment critical infrastructure. Its source code is not public. A foreign investor receives the source code under an investment agreement. Separately, the investor receives only T's quarterly revenue figures.

Answer: the source code is information of the first kind, because it gives understanding of the design or operation of covered infrastructure that is not public. The revenue figures are financial information about performance and are excluded. A right to approve which customers T may serve concerns significant matters, while a decision that is strictly administrative is excluded.

## Never confuse

Material nonpublic technical information is not financial information. Financial information regarding the performance of an entity is excluded [@CL-MNA-0838]. Substantive decisionmaking is not routine administration, because strictly administrative decisions are excluded [@CL-MNA-0840]. Targeting a customer group is not the same as serving it by chance; the test is customizing for, or actively marketing to, the person or group [@CL-MNA-0841].
