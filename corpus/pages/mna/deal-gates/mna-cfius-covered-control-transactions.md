## Purpose

Shows which transactions are covered control transactions and which are not, and walks through the listed examples that the claims on this card cover.

| Applies to | A transaction that results or could result in control of a U.S. business by a foreign person [@CL-MNA-0728] |

This card rests on the regulations on certain investments in the United States by foreign persons. They are not FAR, DFARS or CAS text. Covered control transactions include a transaction which, irrespective of the actual arrangements for control provided for in its terms, results or could result in control of a U.S. business by a foreign person [@CL-MNA-0728]. One listed type is a transaction that results or could result in control by a foreign person of any part of an entity or of assets, if that part of an entity or those assets constitute a U.S. business [@CL-MNA-0729]. Another listed type is a joint venture in which the parties enter into a contractual or other similar arrangement, including an agreement on the establishment of a new entity, but only if one or more of the parties contributes a U.S. business and a foreign person could control that U.S. business by means of the joint venture [@CL-MNA-1557; @CL-MNA-0796]. The list has further types that this card does not restate. The meaning of control is on the [control card](mna-cfius-control.md).

Transactions that are not covered control transactions include a stock split or pro rata stock dividend that does not involve a change in control [@CL-MNA-0730]. One listed type is a transaction that results in a foreign person holding 10 percent or less of the outstanding voting interest in a United States business (regardless of the dollar value of the interest so acquired), but only if the transaction is solely for the purpose of passive investment [@CL-MNA-0731]. Another listed type is an acquisition of any part of an entity or of assets, if that part of an entity or those assets do not constitute a U.S. business [@CL-MNA-0797]. The list has further types that this card does not restate, and the sections give further examples that this card does not restate.

## How it works

In one listed example, Corporation A may choose not to exercise its power to elect new directors for Corporation X but nevertheless will have that exercisable power, and the proposed transaction is a covered control transaction [@CL-MNA-0732]. In another, Corporation A, a foreign person, buys a branch office located entirely outside the United States of Corporation Y, which is incorporated in the United States, and assuming no other relevant facts the purchase is not a covered control transaction because the branch office of Corporation Y is not a U.S. business [@CL-MNA-0065; @CL-MNA-0733]. In a third, Corporation X would acquire the personnel, customer list, equipment and inventory management software used to operate a facility, and on the stated facts it is acquiring a U.S. business and the proposed acquisition is a covered control transaction [@CL-MNA-0734]. In a fourth, a foreign person's acquisition of voting shares in a U.S. business is not solely for passive investment and is a covered control transaction where the foreign person also negotiates contractual rights that give it the power to control important matters [@CL-MNA-0735].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

Foreign Buyer B plans to buy all the shares of Company T, a U.S. business, and to keep T's existing directors. B also buys 5 percent of the voting shares of Company U, a U.S. business, in open-market purchases made solely for passive investment. Answer: the T purchase is a covered control transaction, because B will hold the exercisable power to elect directors even if it never uses it. The U purchase is not, because it is a holding of 10 percent or less made solely for passive investment, assuming no other relevant facts.

## Never confuse

A covered control transaction is not the same as a covered investment, which the [covered investments card](mna-cfius-covered-investments.md) describes. The passive-investment exception covers a holding of 10 percent or less of the outstanding voting interest (regardless of the dollar value of the interest so acquired), but only if the transaction is solely for the purpose of passive investment [@CL-MNA-0731]. Coverage in general is on the [jurisdiction card](mna-cfius-jurisdiction.md).

## Company calibration

Whether the company or a target could be acquired, in whole or in part, by a foreign person, and whether any foreign investor holds rights that give it power over important matters. [@Q-045]
