## Purpose

Shows what information a voluntary notice must hold, what that means for deal-team diligence, and who certifies the filing.

This card rests on the regulations on certain investments in the United States by foreign persons. They are not FAR, DFARS or CAS text.

If the parties to a transaction file a voluntary notice, they shall provide in detail the information the section sets out, which must be accurate and complete with respect to all parties and to the transaction [@CL-MNA-0842]. A voluntary notice shall describe or provide, as applicable, the listed information, the first item being a summary setting forth the essentials of the transaction, including a statement of the purpose of the transaction, and its scope, both within and outside of the United States [@CL-MNA-0843; @CL-MNA-0089].

One listed item is the value of the transaction in U.S. dollars as determined under the valuation section and the parties' assessment of the applicable fee, including an explanation of the methodology used to determine the valuation and fee [@CL-MNA-0844]. Two listed items are the name of every financial institution involved in the transaction, including as advisors, underwriters or sources of financing, and a copy of any partnership agreements, integration agreements or other side agreements relating to the transaction [@CL-MNA-0845]. A group of the listed items is required with respect to the U.S. business that is the subject of the transaction and any entity of which it is a parent, unless that entity is excluded from the scope of the transaction [@CL-MNA-0925]. One listed item in that group is each other contract, identified by agency and number, that is currently in effect or was in effect within the past three years with a federal agency or component with national defense, homeland security or other national security responsibilities, including law enforcement responsibility as it relates to defense, homeland security or national security, together with the contract's estimated final completion date and the name, office and telephone number of the contracting official [@CL-MNA-0846; @CL-MNA-0084]. The section lists many further items, including items on the foreign person, its parents and the officers and directors, that this card does not restate.

A certification is a written statement signed by the chief executive officer or other duly authorized designee of a party filing a notice, declaration or information, certifying under the penalties of the False Statements Accountability Act of 1996 as to the matters stated below [@CL-MNA-0847; @CL-MNA-0927; @CL-MNA-0085]. A duly authorized designee is, for a partnership, any general partner and, for a corporation, any officer or director [@CL-MNA-0848]. In each listed case, the designee must possess actual authority to make the certification on behalf of the party filing a notice, declaration or information [@CL-MNA-0849]. The certifying statement is that the notice, declaration or information filed fully complies with the requirements of the statute, the regulations and any agreement or condition entered into with the Committee or any member of it, and that it is accurate and complete in all material respects as it relates to the transaction and to the party providing the certification, including its parents, subsidiaries and any other related entities described in it [@CL-MNA-0927; @CL-MNA-0085]. Each party filing a voluntary notice shall provide a certification of the notice consistent with section 800.204 [@CL-MNA-0926]. The definition of certification has further provisions on duly authorized designees that this card does not restate.

## How it works

A notice must be detailed, accurate and complete for all parties and for the transaction, so the deal team has to gather the facts before filing [@CL-MNA-0842]. Several items come from finance and legal files: the valuation and fee method [@CL-MNA-0844], the advisors, financing sources and side agreements [@CL-MNA-0845], and recent contracts of the target, and of any entity it is a parent of, with national security agencies [@CL-MNA-0846; @CL-MNA-0925]. Each party filing the notice provides a certification signed by the chief executive officer or another duly authorized designee [@CL-MNA-0926; @CL-MNA-0847]. A designee who signs must possess actual authority to make the certification on behalf of the filing party [@CL-MNA-0849]. How the Committee treats what is filed is on the [information duty and confidentiality card](mna-cfius-information-duty-confidentiality-and-penalties.md). The filing steps are on the [notice procedures card](mna-cfius-notice-procedures-and-rejection.md), and the fee method is on the [filing fee card](mna-cfius-filing-fee-valuation.md); this card does not restate either.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

Foreign Buyer B and Seller S plan a joint notice for the sale of Company T, a U.S. business. The deal team lists the deal value and its method, the three banks advising and financing the deal, one side letter on integration, and two contracts that T, the target, holds with a defense agency from the last three years. B's chief executive officer signs the certification.

Answer: each of those items is something the notice asks for, and the chief executive officer is a proper signer. If an officer signed as a designee without actual authority, the certification would fall short.

## Never confuse

This card covers what the notice holds [@CL-MNA-0842; @CL-MNA-0843; @CL-MNA-0089], and the [notice procedures card](mna-cfius-notice-procedures-and-rejection.md) covers how it is filed and when it may be rejected. A notice is also not a declaration, whose contents are on the [declaration contents card](mna-cfius-declaration-contents-and-rejection.md). The certification applies to a notice, declaration or information, not only to a notice [@CL-MNA-0847]. A withdrawn notice is a separate matter, and its rules are on the [withdrawal card](mna-cfius-declaration-procedures-withdrawal-and-contingent-interests.md).
