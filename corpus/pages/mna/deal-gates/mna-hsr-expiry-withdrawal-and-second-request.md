## Purpose

Shows how long a premerger notification lasts, how it can be withdrawn and refiled, and how a request for more information extends the waiting period.

## The rule

|---|---|

| Applies to | Persons that filed premerger notification: acquiring persons and, in the cases the text lists, acquired persons [@CL-MNA-0612; @CL-MNA-0615] |

This card rests on the Federal Trade Commission's premerger notification transmittal rules. They are not FAR, DFARS or CAS text. The withdrawal rules reach the acquiring person and, in the cases the text lists, the acquired person [@CL-MNA-0615]. The extension rule sets the effect of a request for more information on the waiting period [@CL-MNA-0618].

A notification expires one year after the waiting period expired [@CL-MNA-0612]. An acquiring person's notification, and an acquired person's notification in an acquisition to which section 801.30 does not apply, also expires eighteen months after the date of receipt of that person's notification [@CL-MNA-0669] if a request for additional information or documentary material remains outstanding to that person [@CL-MNA-0613], or to the entities included in it or their officers, directors, partners, agents or employees, without a certification as required by the certification rule on that date [@CL-MNA-0614]. If either person's notification expires that way, both parties must file a new notification to carry out the transaction [@CL-MNA-0652].

An acquiring person, and an acquired person in an acquisition to which section 801.30 does not apply, may withdraw its notification by writing to the Federal Trade Commission and the Antitrust Division by email or mail [@CL-MNA-0615]. An acquiring person whose notification was voluntarily withdrawn, or is deemed automatically withdrawn, may resubmit its notification [@CL-MNA-0653]. A resubmission starts a new waiting period for the same transaction without an additional filing fee, may be used only one time, and is available only in the listed circumstances [@CL-MNA-0616]. One listed circumstance is that the notification was withdrawn before the waiting period expired or ended early and before a request for additional information issued [@CL-MNA-0654]. Another is that the resubmitted notification is refiled before the close of the second business day after withdrawal [@CL-MNA-0617]. The paragraph lists further circumstances that this card does not restate.

The extension rule covers a request to any person other than the person whose voting securities are acquired in a tender offer (or that person's officers and others) or the acquired person in an acquisition covered by 11 USC 363(b) [@CL-MNA-0650]. Such a request extends the waiting period by thirty calendar days from the date of receipt of the requested material, or by ten calendar days for a cash tender offer or an acquisition covered by 11 USC 363(b) [@CL-MNA-0618].

The printed sections give dollar figures only in dated examples marked as adjusted, and this card does not rely on them. The withdrawal section also treats certain public announcements in securities filings as withdrawals, and this card does not restate those rules.

## How it works

The agencies can extend the waiting period by asking for more information [@CL-MNA-0618]. If a request stays outstanding without the required certification, an acquiring person's notification, and an acquired person's in an acquisition to which section 801.30 does not apply, lapses eighteen months after receipt [@CL-MNA-0669; @CL-MNA-0613; @CL-MNA-0614]. The general rule is that the notification lasts one year after the waiting period expires [@CL-MNA-0612]. An acquiring person can withdraw and refile once without a new fee, but only if every listed circumstance holds, including a refiling before the close of the second business day after withdrawal [@CL-MNA-0653; @CL-MNA-0616; @CL-MNA-0617].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

Buyer B files in month one, and the agencies issue a request for more information. B never answers and does not withdraw, and the deal is shelved. Answer: the notification expires eighteen months after its receipt, and both parties must file again to revive the deal. In a second case, the waiting period ends cleanly in month three and the deal has not closed by month sixteen. The notification expired in month fifteen.

## Never confuse

Expiry of the waiting period is not expiry of the notification, which lives on for one year after the waiting period expired [@CL-MNA-0612]. Withdrawal is the filing person's act, while termination of the waiting period is the agencies' act, covered on the [fee and waiting period card](mna-hsr-fee-and-waiting-period.md) [@CL-MNA-0615; @CL-MNA-0611]. Who files the form is on the [filing card](mna-hsr-filing-and-who-files.md).
