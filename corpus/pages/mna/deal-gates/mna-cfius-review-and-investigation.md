## Purpose

Shows the forty-five-day review, what triggers an investigation, the forty-five-day investigation and when the Committee reports to the President.

This card rests on the regulations on certain investments in the United States by foreign persons. They are not FAR, DFARS or CAS text. In the paragraphs cited here, the duties to investigate, to complete the investigation and to report fall on the Committee [@CL-MNA-0641; @CL-MNA-0648; @CL-MNA-0649].

The Committee means the Committee on Foreign Investment in the United States [@CL-MNA-1298]. The Chairperson of the Committee is the Secretary of the Treasury [@CL-MNA-1299]. The Staff Chairperson of the Committee is the Department of the Treasury official so designated by the Secretary of the Treasury or by the Secretary's designee [@CL-MNA-1300]. A lead agency is the Department of the Treasury and any other agency designated by the Chairperson of the Committee to have primary responsibility, on behalf of the Committee, for the specific activity for which the Chairperson designates it as a lead agency, including all or a portion of an assessment, a review, an investigation, or the negotiation or monitoring of a mitigation agreement or condition [@CL-MNA-1301; @CL-MNA-1302].

A 45-day review period starts on the date a voluntary notice has been accepted, an agency notice has been received by the Staff Chairperson, or the Chairperson has requested a notice [@CL-MNA-0639]. The review ends no later than the forty-fifth day after it commences, or the next business day if the forty-fifth day is not a business day [@CL-MNA-0640].

After a review of a notified transaction, the Committee must undertake an investigation of any transaction it has determined to be a covered transaction if one of the listed conditions is met [@CL-MNA-0641]. One listed condition is that a Committee member other than an ex officio member advises the Staff Chairperson that the member believes the transaction threatens to impair national security and that the threat has not been mitigated [@CL-MNA-0642]. Another is that the lead agency recommends an investigation and the Committee concurs [@CL-MNA-0643].

The Committee must also undertake, after a review, an investigation of a covered transaction that is a foreign government-controlled transaction [@CL-MNA-0644]. It must do the same for a covered transaction that would result in foreign-person control of critical infrastructure of or within the United States, if the Committee determines the transaction could impair national security and the impairment has not been mitigated [@CL-MNA-0645]. The Committee need not undertake either of those investigations if the Chairperson of the Committee (or the Deputy Secretary of the Treasury) and the head of any lead agency designated by the Chairperson determine, on the basis of the review, that the covered transaction will not impair national security [@CL-MNA-0646; @CL-MNA-0647].

If the Committee determines, during the review period described in § 800.503, not to undertake an investigation of a notified covered transaction, action under section 721 shall be concluded [@CL-MNA-1303]. An official at the Department of the Treasury shall promptly inform the parties to a covered transaction in writing of a determination of the Committee not to undertake an investigation and to conclude action under section 721 [@CL-MNA-1304]. The text names no particular official.

A foreign government-controlled transaction is any covered control transaction that could result in control of a U.S. business by a foreign government or a person controlled by or acting on behalf of a foreign government [@CL-MNA-0658]. Critical infrastructure means, in the context of a particular covered control transaction, systems and assets, whether physical or virtual, so vital to the United States that their incapacity or destruction would have a debilitating impact on national security [@CL-MNA-0661].

An investigation that is to be undertaken commences no later than the end of the review period [@CL-MNA-0667]. The Committee must complete an investigation no later than the forty-fifth day after it commences, or the next business day if the forty-fifth day is not a business day, subject to the extension paragraph [@CL-MNA-0648]. In extraordinary circumstances the Chairperson may, on a written request signed by the head of a lead agency, extend an investigation for one 15-day period [@CL-MNA-0666].

On completion or termination of an investigation the Committee must send the President a report requesting the President's decision if a listed condition is met, one being that the Committee recommends that the President suspend or prohibit the transaction [@CL-MNA-0649].

## How it works

An accepted voluntary notice, an agency notice received by the Staff Chairperson, or a notice the Chairperson requested starts the review [@CL-MNA-0639]. After the review the Committee investigates if a member sees an unmitigated threat to national security, if the lead agency recommends it and the Committee concurs, if the deal is foreign government-controlled, or if it would give a foreign person control of critical infrastructure and the Committee determines that it could impair national security and the impairment has not been mitigated [@CL-MNA-0641; @CL-MNA-0642; @CL-MNA-0643; @CL-MNA-0644; @CL-MNA-0645]. The investigation commences by the end of the review period and has its own forty-five-day limit [@CL-MNA-0667; @CL-MNA-0648]. The Committee reports to the President when it recommends suspension or prohibition [@CL-MNA-0649].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A voluntary notice for the purchase of Company T is accepted on day 0. The review ends no later than day 45, or the next business day. The Committee finds that the buyer is controlled by a foreign government, so an investigation follows unless the Chairperson and the lead-agency head find no impairment. The investigation commences by the end of the review, on day 45. It must be completed no later than day 90, or the next business day, unless, in extraordinary circumstances and on a written request signed by the head of a lead agency, the Chairperson extends it once for fifteen days, which would move the limit to day 105. Answer: if the Committee recommends prohibition, it sends the President a report.

## Never confuse

The review and the investigation are separate stages with separate forty-five-day limits [@CL-MNA-0640; @CL-MNA-0648]. A declaration's thirty-day assessment is neither of them [@CL-MNA-0634]; it is on the [voluntary declarations card](mna-cfius-voluntary-declarations-and-notices.md). The report to the President is not automatic, because the Committee sends it if one of the listed conditions is met [@CL-MNA-0649]. Coverage is on the [jurisdiction card](mna-cfius-jurisdiction.md), and the duty to file a declaration is on the [mandatory declarations card](mna-cfius-mandatory-declarations.md).

## Company calibration

Whether a foreign government could control or hold a stake in the company or a counterparty, and whether the business touches critical infrastructure. [@Q-045]
