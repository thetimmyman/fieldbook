## Purpose

Defines three terms the foreign investment review rules rely on: foreign person, parent and U.S. business, with the conditions the text sets for each.

This card rests on the regulations on certain investments in the United States by foreign persons. They are not FAR, DFARS or CAS text.

A foreign person is any foreign national, foreign government or foreign entity, or any entity over which a foreign national, foreign government or foreign entity exercises or can exercise control [@CL-MNA-0810]. An entity over which a foreign person exercises or can exercise control is itself a foreign person [@CL-MNA-0811].

A parent of an entity is, first, a person who directly or indirectly holds or will hold at least 50 percent of the entity's outstanding voting interest [@CL-MNA-0812]. One further listed way to be a parent is to hold, or have the right to, at least 50 percent of the entity's profits or of its assets on dissolution, and another is to be its general partner, managing member or equivalent [@CL-MNA-0813]. An entity that meets a parent condition with respect to an intermediate parent is also a parent of any other entity of which the intermediate parent is a parent [@CL-MNA-0814].

A U.S. business is any entity engaged in interstate commerce in the United States, irrespective of the nationality of the persons that control it [@CL-MNA-0815].

## How it works

Foreign person status can come from the entity's own foreign character or from who controls it [@CL-MNA-0810; @CL-MNA-0811]. Parent status looks through ownership chains, so an entity that is a parent of an intermediate parent is also a parent of any other entity of which the intermediate parent is a parent [@CL-MNA-0814]. A U.S. business can be foreign controlled, because the test ignores the nationality of its controllers [@CL-MNA-0815]. These terms feed the jurisdiction tests on the [jurisdiction card](mna-cfius-jurisdiction.md), which this card does not restate.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

Foreign Fund F is organized abroad. F holds 60 percent of the voting interest in Holdco H, and H holds 50 percent of the voting interest in Company T. T sells software to customers in several U.S. states. Assume F is a foreign entity, and that F can exercise control over H and H over T.

Answer: F is a foreign person because it is a foreign entity, and H and T are foreign persons because a foreign person can exercise control over each. F is a parent of H, and because H is an intermediate parent, F is also a parent of T. T is a U.S. business because it is engaged in interstate commerce in the United States.

## Never confuse

A parent is not the same as a controller. Parent status turns on the voting, profit, asset, general partner or intermediate parent tests [@CL-MNA-0812; @CL-MNA-0813; @CL-MNA-0814], while foreign person status turns on being a foreign national, foreign government or foreign entity, or on being under foreign control [@CL-MNA-0810]. The control concept is on the [control card](mna-cfius-control.md). A U.S. business is also not the same as a U.S.-owned business, because the test ignores the nationality of the persons that control it [@CL-MNA-0815]. A party to a transaction is a separate idea, and it is on the [party and transaction card](mna-cfius-party-transaction-and-applicability.md).
