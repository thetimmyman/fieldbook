## Purpose

Defines three terms the foreign investment review rules rely on: foreign person, parent and U.S. business, with the conditions the text sets for each.

This card rests on the regulations on certain investments in the United States by foreign persons. They are not FAR, DFARS or CAS text.

A foreign person is any foreign national, foreign government or foreign entity, or any entity over which a foreign national, foreign government or foreign entity exercises or can exercise control [@CL-MNA-0810]. An entity over which a foreign person exercises or can exercise control is itself a foreign person [@CL-MNA-0811].

A foreign national means any individual other than a U.S. national [@CL-MNA-1314]. A U.S. national means an individual who is a U.S. citizen or an individual who, although not a U.S. citizen, owes permanent allegiance to the United States [@CL-MNA-1315]. A foreign government means any government or body exercising governmental functions, other than the U.S. Government or a subnational government of the United States [@CL-MNA-1316]. The term foreign government includes, but is not limited to, national and subnational governments, including their respective departments, agencies, and instrumentalities [@CL-MNA-1317].

A parent of an entity is, first, a person who directly or indirectly holds or will hold at least 50 percent of the entity's outstanding voting interest [@CL-MNA-0812]. A person who directly or indirectly holds or will hold the right to at least 50 percent of the entity's profits, or has or will have the right in the event of dissolution to at least 50 percent of its assets, is also a parent [@CL-MNA-0812; @CL-MNA-0813]; so is the general partner, managing member, or equivalent of the entity [@CL-MNA-0812; @CL-MNA-0813]. An entity that meets a parent condition with respect to an intermediate parent is also a parent of any other entity of which the intermediate parent is a parent [@CL-MNA-0814].

The terms hold and holding mean legal or beneficial ownership, whether direct or indirect, whether through fiduciaries, agents, or other means [@CL-MNA-1318]. A voting interest means any interest in an entity that entitles the owner or holder of that interest to vote for the election of directors of the entity (or, with respect to unincorporated entities, individuals exercising similar functions) or to vote on other matters affecting the entity [@CL-MNA-1319].

A U.S. business is any entity engaged in interstate commerce in the United States, irrespective of the nationality of the persons that control it [@CL-MNA-0815].

The term United States or U.S. means the United States of America, the States of the United States, the District of Columbia, and any commonwealth, territory, dependency, or possession of the United States, or any subdivision of the foregoing, and includes the territorial sea of the United States [@CL-MNA-1320].

## How it works

Foreign person status can come from the entity's own foreign character or from who controls it [@CL-MNA-0810; @CL-MNA-0811]. Parent status looks through ownership chains, so an entity that is a parent of an intermediate parent is also a parent of any other entity of which the intermediate parent is a parent [@CL-MNA-0814]. A U.S. business is any entity engaged in interstate commerce in the United States, irrespective of the nationality of the persons that control it [@CL-MNA-0815]. These terms feed the jurisdiction tests on the [jurisdiction card](mna-cfius-jurisdiction.md), which this card does not restate.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

Foreign Fund F is organized abroad. F holds 60 percent of the voting interest in Holdco H, and H holds 50 percent of the voting interest in Company T. T sells software to customers in several U.S. states. Assume F is a foreign entity, and that F can exercise control over H and H over T.

Answer: F is a foreign person because it is a foreign entity, and H and T are foreign persons because a foreign person can exercise control over each. F is a parent of H, and because H is an intermediate parent, F is also a parent of T. T is a U.S. business because it is engaged in interstate commerce in the United States.

## Never confuse

Parent status turns on the voting, profit, asset, general partner or intermediate parent tests [@CL-MNA-0812; @CL-MNA-0813; @CL-MNA-0814], while foreign person status turns on being a foreign national, foreign government or foreign entity, or on being under foreign control [@CL-MNA-0810]. The control concept is on the [control card](mna-cfius-control.md). Whether an entity is a U.S. business depends on whether it is engaged in interstate commerce in the United States, irrespective of the nationality of the persons that control it [@CL-MNA-0815]. A party to a transaction is a separate idea, and it is on the [party and transaction card](mna-cfius-party-transaction-and-applicability.md).

## Company calibration

Whether any owner, investor or lender of the company or of a target is a foreign person, and which entities are its parents or intermediate parents. [@Q-045]
