## Purpose

Shows when the parties to a covered transaction must file a declaration with the Committee, the voting-interest and timing rules, and the penalty for failing to.

## The rule

| Applies to | Parties to a transaction described in the section's two trigger paragraphs [@CL-MNA-0625] |

This card rests on the regulations on certain investments in the United States by foreign persons. They are not FAR, DFARS or CAS text. The mandatory-declaration duty binds the parties to a transaction described in the section's two trigger paragraphs, except as three listed paragraphs provide [@CL-MNA-0625]. The penalty can reach any person who fails to comply [@CL-MNA-0632].

The first trigger is a covered transaction that results in a foreign person acquiring a substantial interest in a TID U.S. business, where the governments of a single foreign state other than an excepted foreign state hold a substantial interest in that foreign person [@CL-MNA-0625; @CL-MNA-0626]. In the context of an acquisition by a foreign person, a substantial interest is a direct or indirect voting interest of 25 percent or more [@CL-MNA-0659]. In the context of a foreign person in which the governments of a single foreign state have an interest, it is a direct or indirect voting interest of 49 percent or more, subject to a further paragraph of the same section that this card does not restate [@CL-MNA-0660]. The section makes the first trigger subject to a paragraph on when critical technology is assessed, and this card does not restate it.

The second trigger is a covered transaction involving a TID U.S. business that produces, designs, tests, manufactures, fabricates or develops one or more critical technologies for which a U.S. regulatory authorization would be required for export, reexport, transfer in-country or retransfer to a person described in the listed items [@CL-MNA-0625; @CL-MNA-0627]. Two listed persons are a person that could directly control the TID U.S. business as a result of the covered transaction and a person directly acquiring an interest that is a covered investment in it [@CL-MNA-0663]. A voting interest for these declarations is a direct or indirect voting interest of 25 percent or more, subject to the rules for entities run by a general partner and for indirect holdings [@CL-MNA-0628]. The section makes the second trigger subject to a paragraph on when critical technology is assessed [@CL-MNA-0668], and this card does not restate it. The section also says how the authorization is determined, and this card does not restate that paragraph.

Parties may elect to submit a written notice instead of a declaration [@CL-MNA-0664]. Parties must submit the declaration, or a written notice in its place, no later than the dates the paragraph lists [@CL-MNA-0629]. One listed deadline is thirty days before the completion date of the transaction, if the completion date is after March 14, 2020 [@CL-MNA-0630]. Another listed deadline covers completion dates in a short earlier window, which this card does not restate.

No declaration is required under the second trigger for a covered control transaction by an excepted investor [@CL-MNA-0631]. The section lists further exceptions, among them one for an entity operating under an approved arrangement that offsets foreign ownership, control or influence, which this card does not restate; see [Foreign ownership, control or influence and its mitigation](../diligence/mna-nispom-foci-and-mitigation.md).

A person who fails to comply with the mandatory-declaration section may be liable to the United States for a civil penalty not to exceed $5,000,000 or the value of the transaction, whichever is greater [@CL-MNA-0632].

## How it works

The duty depends on the parties' transaction matching a trigger and on no listed exception applying [@CL-MNA-0625]. A declaration, or a written notice in its place, goes in thirty days before completion when the completion date is after March 14, 2020 [@CL-MNA-0629; @CL-MNA-0630]. Failure to file can draw a civil penalty [@CL-MNA-0632].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

Foreign buyer X plans to buy all of Company T, a business that designs a critical technology that needs a U.S. export authorization for X's home country. X is not an excepted investor, X could directly control T, and no other listed exception applies. The parties plan to complete the deal on day 90. Answer: the critical-technology trigger applies, so the parties file a declaration, or a written notice, no later than day 60, thirty days before the completion date.

## Never confuse

A mandatory declaration is not a voluntary one, which a party to any proposed or completed transaction may choose to file instead of a written notice and which sits on the [voluntary declarations card](mna-cfius-voluntary-declarations-and-notices.md). Do not mix the voting-interest terms. For critical-technology declarations a voting interest is 25 percent or more [@CL-MNA-0628]. For the government-interest trigger, the acquirer's substantial interest is also 25 percent or more [@CL-MNA-0659], and the governments' interest in the acquirer must be 49 percent or more [@CL-MNA-0660]. Coverage by the Committee is on the [jurisdiction card](mna-cfius-jurisdiction.md).
