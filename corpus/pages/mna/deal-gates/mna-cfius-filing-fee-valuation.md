## Purpose

Shows how the value of the transaction is determined for the filing fee of a voluntary notice, and what the Staff Chairperson must do before rejecting a notice for a short payment.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Not established. |
| Codified citation | Not established. |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Not established. |
| Applies to | Voluntary notices, which are filed by paying the fee required under the filing-fee subpart [@CL-MNA-0757] |

This card rests on the regulations on certain investments in the United States by foreign persons. They are not FAR, DFARS or CAS text. Except as provided in paragraph (c) of the valuation section, the value of the transaction for the filing fee means the total value of all consideration [@CL-MNA-0762]. The consideration counted is that which has been or will be provided in the context of the transaction by or on behalf of the foreign person that is a party, including cash, assets, shares or other ownership interests, debt forgiveness, or services or other in-kind consideration [@CL-MNA-0763]. This card does not restate any fee amounts.

For consideration in securities traded on a national securities exchange, the value of the securities is the closing price on the national securities exchange on which the securities are primarily traded on the trading day immediately before the parties file the formal written notice, or the last published closing price if the securities were not traded on that day [@CL-MNA-0764; @CL-MNA-0069]. The paragraph adds a fallback for securities that did not trade that day. Where the consideration is or includes other non-cash assets, services, interests or other in-kind consideration, its value is its fair market value as of the date the parties file the formal written notice [@CL-MNA-0765]. Where the transaction involves a merger or the contribution of one or more U.S. businesses to a joint venture, the value of the transaction is the fair market value of the U.S. businesses being merged or contributed [@CL-MNA-0766]. Where a transaction will be effectuated in multiple phases, the value of the transaction includes the total value of the multiple phases, as may be reasonably determined as of the date the parties file the formal written notice [@CL-MNA-0768]. Fair market value means the price that would be received in exchange for sale of an interest, or paid to receive a service or to transfer liability, in an orderly transaction between market participants [@CL-MNA-0767]. For an interest for which consideration has not been determined, the value of the transaction includes the fair market value of the interest being acquired in the transaction as of the date the parties file the formal written notice [@CL-MNA-0809]. Where the value of a transaction is $5,000,000 or more but it includes one or more non-United States businesses and the value of the interest acquired in the United States business is less than $5,000,000, the filing fee under the section on the amount of the fee is applicable [@CL-MNA-0808]. Before rejecting a notice for an insufficient filing fee, the Staff Chairperson shall inform the parties in writing of the insufficiency of payment and provide the parties three business days to pay the remainder of the filing fee [@CL-MNA-0769].

## How it works

The starting point is all consideration provided by or on behalf of the foreign person [@CL-MNA-0762; @CL-MNA-0763]. Securities traded on an exchange are valued at the closing price on the trading day before filing, or the last published closing price if they were not traded that day [@CL-MNA-0764; @CL-MNA-0069]. Other non-cash consideration is valued at fair market value as of the filing date [@CL-MNA-0765]. A merger or joint venture contribution uses the fair market value of the U.S. businesses [@CL-MNA-0766]. A transaction in several phases counts the total value of the phases [@CL-MNA-0768]. Where consideration has not been determined, the fair market value of the interest acquired is used [@CL-MNA-0809].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

Foreign Buyer B will pay 30 million in cash and give 1 million of its own listed shares for Company T. On the trading day before B files the notice, B's shares closed at 12 each. Answer: the consideration is the cash plus the shares at the closing price, so 30 million plus 12 million, which is 42 million. This card does not give the fee for that value.

## Never confuse

The value of the transaction for the fee is not the fee itself, and this card does not restate the fee schedule. This fee also belongs to foreign investment review and is not the premerger filing fee; that one is on the [premerger fee card](mna-hsr-fee-and-waiting-period.md). The grounds for rejecting a notice, of which a short payment is one, are on the [notice procedures card](mna-cfius-notice-procedures-and-rejection.md). Consideration is valued by its type, with listed securities at a closing price [@CL-MNA-0764] and other non-cash consideration at fair market value [@CL-MNA-0765].
