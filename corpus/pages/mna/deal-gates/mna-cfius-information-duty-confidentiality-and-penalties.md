## Purpose

Shows what parties must provide to the Committee, how the Committee runs a general assessment, what the Committee must keep confidential and may disclose, and what the printed text says about penalties.

This card rests on the regulations on certain investments in the United States by foreign persons. They are not FAR, DFARS or CAS text.

Parties to a transaction that is notified or declared shall provide information to the Staff Chairperson that will enable the Committee to conduct a full assessment, review, or investigation of the transaction [@CL-MNA-0856]. Parties to a transaction that have filed information with the Committee shall promptly advise the Staff Chairperson of any material changes to that information [@CL-MNA-0933]. The section has further duties, on a transaction with no notice or declaration, on compliance, on requests about misstatements, on subpoenas, on English translations and on certifications, that this card does not restate. For a declaration, the duty to report material changes is also on the [declaration procedures card](mna-cfius-declaration-procedures-withdrawal-and-contingent-interests.md).

The general section on Committee procedure says that in any assessment, review or investigation of a covered transaction the Committee should consider the factors specified in the statute and, as appropriate, require the parties to provide the information necessary to consider those factors [@CL-MNA-0934]. The Staff Chairperson is the point of contact for receiving material filed with the Committee, including notices and declarations [@CL-MNA-0931]. During an investigation, a party to the transaction under investigation may request a meeting with the Committee staff, and such a request ordinarily will be granted [@CL-MNA-0932]. That general section does not state a penalty or a penalty amount. The later stages of a case are on the [review and investigation card](mna-cfius-review-and-investigation.md).

Except as provided in the disclosure exceptions, information or documentary material filed with the Committee under the part, including material filed under a listed notice paragraph, is exempt from disclosure under the Freedom of Information Act [@CL-MNA-0857]. The confidentiality paragraph does not prohibit disclosure of information relevant to any administrative or judicial action or proceeding, or of information to Congress or to any duly authorized committee or subcommittee of Congress [@CL-MNA-0858]. The section lists further disclosures that this card does not restate.

A certification given with a filing is made under the penalties of the False Statements Accountability Act of 1996 [@CL-MNA-0847]. The certification is on the [notice contents card](mna-cfius-voluntary-notice-contents-and-certification.md). The Defense Production Act provisions relating to fines and imprisonment apply with respect to the disclosure of information or documentary material filed with the Committee under the regulations [@CL-MNA-0859]. The sections this card rests on do not establish penalty amounts, a list of penalized conduct or how a penalty is assessed, so this card states none.

## How it works

Parties to a notified or declared transaction owe the Staff Chairperson the information the Committee needs for a full assessment, review or investigation, and they promptly advise the Staff Chairperson of material changes to what they filed [@CL-MNA-0856; @CL-MNA-0933]. The Committee considers statutory factors and, as appropriate, requires the information needed to consider them [@CL-MNA-0934]. Material filed is generally exempt from public disclosure under the Freedom of Information Act, subject to the listed exceptions [@CL-MNA-0857]. The confidentiality rule does not bar disclosure in listed cases, such as to Congress or in a relevant administrative or judicial proceeding [@CL-MNA-0858]. The Defense Production Act fine and imprisonment provisions reach disclosure of filed material [@CL-MNA-0859].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

Company A files a notice. A reporter then asks the Committee for A's filed documents, and a congressional committee separately asks for the same documents.

Answer: the filed material is generally exempt from public release, so the reporter's request is refused. The paragraph on listed disclosures allows information to go to Congress or a duly authorized committee, so the congressional request is not blocked by the confidentiality rule.

## Never confuse

The duty to provide information is not the confidentiality rule. The first is what parties owe the Committee [@CL-MNA-0856], and the second is how the Committee treats what it receives [@CL-MNA-0857]. Confidentiality is also not absolute, because the listed disclosures stay open [@CL-MNA-0858]. The general section on Committee procedure is not a penalty section, because the printed text states no penalty. The penalty for failing to file a mandatory declaration is on the [mandatory declarations card](mna-cfius-mandatory-declarations.md), and the later stages of a case are on the [review and investigation card](mna-cfius-review-and-investigation.md).

## Company calibration

Who in the company prepares information for the Committee, and whether any ownership or control arrangement limits who may see filed material or the company's finance data. [@Q-045]
