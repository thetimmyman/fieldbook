## Purpose

Shows who pays the filing fee and to whom, when the waiting period starts, when it expires and how it can be ended early or extended.

This card rests on the Federal Trade Commission's premerger notification transmittal rules. They are not FAR, DFARS or CAS text. They bind acquiring persons, who pay the fee [@CL-MNA-0606], and the persons whose notification starts the clock [@CL-MNA-0607; @CL-MNA-0608]. They also govern when the Federal Trade Commission and the Assistant Attorney General may end the period early [@CL-MNA-0611].

Each acquiring person pays the filing fee that the act requires to the Federal Trade Commission, except as provided in three listed paragraphs of the same section [@CL-MNA-0606]. Under paragraph (b), for a transaction described by section 801.2(d)(2)(iii) of the chapter, the parties pay only one filing fee [@CL-MNA-1539]. Under paragraph (c), for a reportable transaction in which the acquiring entity has two ultimate parent entities, both are acquiring persons, but if their responses for the NAICS Codes section of the form would be the same, only one filing fee is required [@CL-MNA-1540; @CL-MNA-1541]. Under paragraph (f), for a transaction described by paragraph (c) of section 803.12, the parties pay no additional filing fee [@CL-MNA-1542]. The waiting period begins on the date the notification is received from the persons the paragraph lists, or on the date the part completed is received with a statement of reasons for noncompliance [@CL-MNA-0607]. For all other acquisitions, those persons are all persons required to file notification [@CL-MNA-0608]. The paragraph lists earlier cases that this card does not restate.

The waiting period, counted from its beginning as determined under paragraph (a) of section 803.10 [@CL-MNA-0610], expires at 11:59 p.m. Eastern Time on the thirtieth calendar day after it begins, or the fifteenth for a cash tender offer or an acquisition covered by 11 USC 363(b), except where section 802.23 applies and provides another day [@CL-MNA-0609]. If that day would be a Saturday, Sunday or legal public holiday [@CL-MNA-0651], the period is extended to 11:59 p.m. Eastern Time of the next regular business day [@CL-MNA-0651]. The expiry rule applies unless the period is extended or is terminated [@CL-MNA-0610]. A request for additional information can extend it, as the [expiry and withdrawal card](mna-hsr-expiry-withdrawal-and-second-request.md) explains. The agencies may terminate a waiting period in their discretion, on a filing person's written request or, notwithstanding paragraph (a) of that section, on their own initiative [@CL-MNA-0611]. Paragraph (a) provides that, except as provided in paragraph (c), no waiting period is terminated under section 7A(b)(2) unless three conditions are met: all notifications required to be filed have been received (or the notification to the extent completed, with a statement of the reasons for noncompliance), it has been determined that no additional information or documentary material will be requested or, if it has been requested, it has been received (or, if a request is not fully complied with, the information and documentary material submitted have been received with a statement of the reasons for noncompliance), and the Federal Trade Commission and the Assistant Attorney General have concluded that neither intends to take any further action within the waiting period [@CL-MNA-1543; @CL-MNA-1544; @CL-MNA-1545; @CL-MNA-1546; @CL-MNA-1547].

The fee section prints dollar figures only in worked examples marked as adjusted, and this card does not rely on them. The timing section also sets how the date of receipt is fixed and what a deficient filing does, which this card does not restate.

## How it works

The acquiring person pays the fee to the Federal Trade Commission [@CL-MNA-0606]. The clock starts on receipt of the notification from the listed persons [@CL-MNA-0607; @CL-MNA-0608]. It runs to the thirtieth calendar day, or the fifteenth in the cash tender offer and 11 USC 363(b) cases (or another day where section 802.23 applies), and moves to the next regular business day if it would end on a weekend or legal public holiday [@CL-MNA-0609; @CL-MNA-0651]. The agencies can end it early on request or, notwithstanding the termination conditions in paragraph (a), on their own initiative [@CL-MNA-0611; @CL-MNA-1543]. An extension or another named provision can change it [@CL-MNA-0610].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

Buyer B and Seller S each file, and the agencies have received both notifications on a Monday. For an ordinary acquisition the waiting period would run to the thirtieth calendar day after that Monday, and for a cash tender offer to the fifteenth. Answer: unless a request for more information extends it or the agencies end it early, the period ends at the end of that day, Eastern Time, or of the next regular business day if the day falls on a weekend or legal holiday.

## Never confuse

The filing fee and the waiting period are separate rules: the acquiring person pays the fee [@CL-MNA-0606], and the clock starts on receipt of the notification [@CL-MNA-0607]. Expiry of the waiting period is not the end of the notification's life, which has its own later expiry on the [expiry and withdrawal card](mna-hsr-expiry-withdrawal-and-second-request.md). Who files and who certifies is on the [filing card](mna-hsr-filing-and-who-files.md).
