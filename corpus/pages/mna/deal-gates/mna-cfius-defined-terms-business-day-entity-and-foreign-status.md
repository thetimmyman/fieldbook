## Purpose

Defines four terms the CFIUS regulations use: business day, entity, excepted foreign state and foreign entity. Each is read with the elements and exclusions its section prints.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Not established. |
| Codified citation | Not established. |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Not established. |
| Applies to | Deadlines counted in business days, and status questions about a body or a foreign state [@CL-MNA-0019; @CL-MNA-0021; @CL-MNA-0024; @CL-MNA-0026] |

Each term is defined in its own section and is read on its own. The glossary below gives each full definition.

| Term | Full definition |
|---|---|
| Business day | Monday through Friday, except (1) the legal public holidays specified in title 5 of the United States Code, section 6103, (2) any day declared to be a holiday by federal statute or executive order, and (3) any day with respect to which the Office of Personnel Management has announced that Federal agencies in the Washington, DC, area are closed [@CL-MNA-0019; @CL-MNA-0020] |
| Entity | (1) Any branch, partnership, group or sub-group, association, estate, trust, corporation or division of a corporation, or organization, whether or not organized under the laws of any State or foreign state; (2) assets, whether or not organized as a separate legal entity, operated by any one of the foregoing as a business undertaking in a particular location or for particular products or services; and (3) any government, including a foreign national or subnational government, the United States Government, a subnational government within the United States, and any of their respective departments, agencies or instrumentalities [@CL-MNA-0021; @CL-MNA-0022; @CL-MNA-0023] |
| Excepted foreign state | Until February 13, 2023, a foreign state that meets criterion (a); beginning on February 13, 2023, a foreign state that meets both criterion (a), that the Committee identifies it as an eligible foreign state, and criterion (b), that the Committee has made a determination for it under § 800.1001(a) [@CL-MNA-0024; @CL-MNA-0025] |
| Foreign entity | A branch, partnership, group or sub-group, association, estate, trust, corporation or division of a corporation, or organization organized under the laws of a foreign state, if either its principal place of business is outside the United States or its equity securities are primarily traded on one or more foreign exchanges; but notwithstanding that rule, such a body that can demonstrate that a majority of its equity interest is ultimately owned by United States nationals is not a foreign entity [@CL-MNA-0026; @CL-MNA-0027; @CL-MNA-0575; @CL-MNA-0028] |

## How it works

A day is a business day if it falls Monday through Friday and none of the three exclusions removes it [@CL-MNA-0019; @CL-MNA-0020]. For calculating any deadline in the part that is triggered by a party's submission under § 800.401(g)(2) or § 800.501(i), a submission received after five in the afternoon Eastern Time is deemed submitted on the next business day [@CL-MNA-0561].

A note to the business-day section points to § 800.604 on the tolling of deadlines during a lapse in appropriations [@CL-MNA-0562]. That section tolls any deadline or time limitation under subpart D or E that is imposed on the Committee during a lapse in appropriations [@CL-MNA-0563].

The entity definition lists three things that are entities: bodies of the listed forms, assets those bodies operate as a business undertaking, and governments [@CL-MNA-0021; @CL-MNA-0022; @CL-MNA-0023]. It ends by pointing to examples in § 800.301(g)(5) through (14) and § 800.302(g)(5) through (10) [@CL-MNA-0564].

The Committee decides whether a foreign state is an excepted foreign state: it identifies eligible foreign states and makes the determination [@CL-MNA-0025]. Before February 13, 2023, criterion (a) alone was enough [@CL-MNA-0024]. From that date both criteria are needed [@CL-MNA-0024]. The names of the states identified as eligible, and the list of excepted foreign states, are to be available at the Committee's section of the Department of the Treasury website [@CL-MNA-0565; @CL-MNA-0566]. The Chairperson of the Committee shall publish a notice of any determination, or rescission of a determination, in the Federal Register [@CL-MNA-0572]. The [determination effects card](mna-cfius-foreign-state-determination-effects.md) covers when a determination takes effect.

The foreign-entity test has three steps. First, the body must be organized under the laws of a foreign state [@CL-MNA-0026]. Second, either its principal place of business must be outside the United States or its equity securities must be primarily traded on one or more foreign exchanges, and one of the two is enough [@CL-MNA-0027]. Third, the body is not a foreign entity if it can demonstrate that United States nationals ultimately own a majority of its equity interest [@CL-MNA-0575; @CL-MNA-0028]. The body bears that demonstration [@CL-MNA-0028]. The [principal place of business card](mna-cfius-principal-place-of-business-and-passive-investment.md) covers the location test.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A party makes a submission under § 800.501(i) at six in the afternoon Eastern Time on a Friday [@CL-MNA-0561]. It is deemed submitted on the next business day [@CL-MNA-0561]. Saturday and Sunday are not business days [@CL-MNA-0019], so the deemed day is Monday. If that Monday is a legal public holiday, or a day on which the Office of Personnel Management has announced that Washington, DC, area agencies are closed, the deemed day is the next day that is not excluded [@CL-MNA-0019; @CL-MNA-0020].

A manager is organized under the laws of a foreign state, keeps its principal place of business there and trades its shares only on a foreign exchange. It is a foreign entity unless it can demonstrate that United States nationals ultimately own a majority of its equity interest [@CL-MNA-0026; @CL-MNA-0027; @CL-MNA-0028]. A second manager has the same organization and location, but it demonstrates that United States nationals ultimately own most of its equity. It is not a foreign entity [@CL-MNA-0028]. A third body is organized under the laws of a State, and the definition reaches only a body organized under the laws of a foreign state, so it is not a foreign entity under it [@CL-MNA-0026].

## Never confuse

An entity is not the same as a foreign entity. Entity is the wide list of forms, assets and governments, while foreign entity narrows the test to a body organized under foreign law that meets a location condition and does not show majority ownership by United States nationals [@CL-MNA-0021; @CL-MNA-0026; @CL-MNA-0027; @CL-MNA-0028].

A foreign state is not an excepted foreign state because of one criterion alone once the later date has passed; both criteria are needed [@CL-MNA-0024].
