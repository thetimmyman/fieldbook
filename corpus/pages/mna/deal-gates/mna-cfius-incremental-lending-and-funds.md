## Purpose

Shows when a later acquisition, a loan or a fund investment becomes a covered transaction and when it does not.

## The rule

| Applies to | Later acquisitions, loans or similar financing arrangements, and fund investments involving a foreign person and a U.S. business [@CL-MNA-0742; @CL-MNA-0744; @CL-MNA-0747] |

This card rests on the regulations on certain investments in the United States by foreign persons. They are not FAR, DFARS or CAS text. For incremental acquisitions, any transaction in which a foreign person acquires an additional interest in, or for which a change in rights of the foreign person occurs with respect to, a U.S. business over which the same foreign person, or any entity that it wholly owns directly or indirectly, previously acquired direct control as a result of a covered control transaction for which the Committee concluded all action under section 721 shall be deemed not to be a covered transaction [@CL-MNA-1561; @CL-MNA-1562]. In one listed example, three years after an earlier acquisition, Corporation A acquires the remainder of the voting interest in Corporation B, and assuming no other relevant facts, because the Committee concluded all action with respect to Corporation A's earlier direct acquisition of control in the same U.S. business and because no other foreign person is a party to the subsequent transaction, the subsequent transaction is not a covered transaction [@CL-MNA-0070; @CL-MNA-0743]. If, however, a foreign person that did not acquire control of the U.S. business in the prior transaction is a party to the later transaction, the later transaction may be a covered transaction [@CL-MNA-0742]. In another listed example, a subsequent transaction may be a covered transaction because the direct acquirer in the initial transaction and the other foreign person are under common ownership of the same parent but the direct acquirer does not wholly own the other foreign person [@CL-MNA-0800]. For loans, the extension of a loan or a similar financing arrangement by a foreign person to a U.S. business, regardless of whether accompanied by the creation in favor of the foreign person of a secured interest over securities or other assets of the U.S. business, shall not, by itself, constitute a covered transaction [@CL-MNA-0071; @CL-MNA-0744]. The Committee will accept notices or declarations with respect to such loans or financing arrangements only at the time that, because of imminent or actual default or other condition, there is a significant possibility [@CL-MNA-0745] that the foreign person may obtain control of a U.S. business, or acquire equity interest and access, rights, or involvement specified in the covered investment definition over a TID U.S. business, as a result of the default or other condition [@CL-MNA-0802]. A loan can still be covered: in one listed example, a loan under which Corporation A has, as a result of the transaction and under the terms of the loan documentation, the power to determine, direct, or decide important matters affecting Corporation B is a covered control transaction [@CL-MNA-0746].

An investment fund is any entity that is an investment company as defined in section 3(a) of the Investment Company Act of 1940 (title 15 of the United States Code, section 80a-1 and following), or would be an investment company but for one or more of the exemptions provided in section 3(b) or 3(c) of that Act [@CL-MNA-1371].

For investment funds, notwithstanding § 800.303 [@CL-MNA-0072], an indirect investment by a foreign person in a TID U.S. business through an investment fund that affords the foreign person (or a designee of the foreign person) membership as a limited partner or equivalent on an advisory board or a committee of the fund shall not be considered a covered investment if the listed conditions are met [@CL-MNA-0072; @CL-MNA-0747]. Two of the listed conditions are that the foreign person does not have access to material nonpublic technical information as a result of its participation on the advisory board or committee and that the investment does not afford it any of the access, rights, or involvement specified in the covered investment definition [@CL-MNA-0801]. This card does not restate the other conditions. In extraordinary circumstances the Committee may consider a waiver of a potential conflict of interest, a waiver of an allocation limitation, or a similar activity under an investment fund agreement to constitute control [@CL-MNA-0748], meaning control of investment decisions of the investment fund or decisions relating to entities in which the investment fund is invested [@CL-MNA-0803].

## How it works

Each case turns on what the foreign person already holds and what the new step adds. In the listed example, assuming no other relevant facts, an earlier concluded direct control acquisition takes a later step outside coverage when no other foreign person is a party, while a foreign person that did not acquire control in the prior transaction being a party may make the later transaction covered [@CL-MNA-0070; @CL-MNA-0743; @CL-MNA-0742]. Extending a loan or similar financing arrangement is not a covered transaction by itself, and the Committee accepts notices or declarations on it only when imminent or actual default or another condition creates a significant possibility of the listed control or covered-investment outcome [@CL-MNA-0071; @CL-MNA-0744; @CL-MNA-0745; @CL-MNA-0802]. Notwithstanding § 800.303, an indirect investment through an investment fund that affords a foreign person or its designee limited-partner or equivalent membership on an advisory board or committee of the fund is not a covered investment when the listed conditions are met, though in extraordinary circumstances the Committee may consider certain waivers or similar activities to constitute control [@CL-MNA-0072; @CL-MNA-0747; @CL-MNA-0748; @CL-MNA-0803].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

Foreign Lender L lends 20 million to Company T, a U.S. business, on ordinary loan terms. T later defaults, and L faces a significant possibility of taking T's principal assets. Answer: the loan alone is not a covered transaction. The Committee takes a notice on it once default makes control significantly possible. If the loan terms had also given L the power to decide important matters at T, the loan would be a covered control transaction.

## Never confuse

A loan is not an equity investment, but in the listed example a loan under which the lender has power over important matters is a covered control transaction [@CL-MNA-0746]; the [control card](mna-cfius-control.md) says what that power is. An incremental step is not automatically outside coverage, because a new foreign party can make the later transaction covered [@CL-MNA-0742]. Notwithstanding § 800.303, a fund investor's advisory board or committee seat does not make an indirect investment a covered investment when the listed conditions are met [@CL-MNA-0072; @CL-MNA-0747]. Coverage in general is on the [jurisdiction card](mna-cfius-jurisdiction.md).

## Company calibration

Whether the company has foreign lenders, syndicate participants or fund investors, and whether any holds equity-like or advisory rights. [@Q-045]
