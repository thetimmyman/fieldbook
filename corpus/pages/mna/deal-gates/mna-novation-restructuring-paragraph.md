## Purpose

Shows the special paragraph that DoD requires in a novation agreement when the buyer plans to incur restructuring costs, and what that paragraph lets the buyer recover.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified DFARS [@CL-MNA-0381] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 242.1204(i) [@CL-MNA-0381] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-MNA-0381] |
| Applies to | Novation agreements where the transferee intends to incur restructuring costs [@CL-MNA-0381] |

When a novation agreement is required and the transferee intends to incur restructuring costs as defined at 213.205-70, the cognizant contracting officer shall include the provision at DFARS 242.1204(i) as paragraph (b)(7) of the novation agreement instead of the paragraph (b)(7) provided in the sample format at FAR 42.1204(i) [@CL-MNA-0381; @CL-MNA-0456].

Under that provision, restructuring costs allowable under FAR part 31 or DFARS part 231 may be reimbursed under flexibly-priced novated contracts [@CL-MNA-0457]. The proviso is that the transferee demonstrates that the restructuring will reduce overall costs to DoD (and to NASA where there is a mix of DoD and NASA contracts) and that the requirements included in DFARS 231.205-70 are met [@CL-MNA-0382]. The costs are not allowed on novated contracts unless there is an audit of the restructuring proposal, a determination by the contracting officer of overall reduced costs to DoD/NASA, and an advance agreement that sets a cumulative cost ceiling for restructuring projects and the period to which the costs are assigned [@CL-MNA-0383; @CL-MNA-0384].

## How it works

The novation agreement carries the conditions, so recovery of restructuring costs is tested against the audit of the restructuring proposal, the contracting officer's determination and the advance agreement [@CL-MNA-0383; @CL-MNA-0384]. The ceiling covers restructuring projects together, and the costs are assigned to a stated period [@CL-MNA-0384]. The requirements of the DoD cost principle apply as well [@CL-MNA-0382]; they are set out on [DoD external restructuring costs after a business combination](../valuation/mna-dod-external-restructuring-costs.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A buyer will acquire all assets of a seller and expects to close a plant and pay severance of about 3 million. The novation agreement uses the DoD paragraph. The buyer later submits a restructuring proposal. The audit, the contracting officer's finding of reduced overall costs and an advance agreement with a ceiling of 3.5 million for a stated period are in place, the buyer has shown that the restructuring will reduce overall costs, and the requirements of the cost principle are met. Only then can the allowable severance be reimbursed on the novated flexibly-priced contracts.

## Never confuse

Placing the paragraph in the agreement does not allow any cost by itself; costs must still be allowable and meet the listed conditions [@CL-MNA-0457; @CL-MNA-0382; @CL-MNA-0383; @CL-MNA-0384]. The paragraph is also not the general novation rule: for when the Government may recognize a successor and what is filed, see [Novation and change-of-name agreements](../diligence/mna-novation-agreements.md). The determination named in this paragraph is made by the contracting officer [@CL-MNA-0383]; the cost principle's own conditions, including who makes its written savings determination, are on [DoD external restructuring costs after a business combination](../valuation/mna-dod-external-restructuring-costs.md). An advance agreement is a general tool described in [Advance agreements](../../compliance/cas-allowability/cmp-advance-agreements.md).

## Company calibration

Whether the transfer agreement for each acquisition case is drafted with the DoD paragraph and the restructuring cost plan. [@Q-029]
