## Purpose

Shows what an accountant's report on financial statements must state and which audit and review records the accountant must keep, and for how long. The rules are part of Regulation S-X, which is not FAR, DFARS or CAS. The accountant review of interim statements is on [Interim financial statements: condensed form, periods and review](mna-sx-interim-financial-statements.md).

The accountant's report shall be dated, signed manually, indicate the city and State where issued, and identify without detailed enumeration the financial statements covered [@CL-MNA-0714]. One listed requirement is that the report shall state the applicable professional standards under which the audit was conducted [@CL-MNA-0715]. The report shall also designate any auditing procedures deemed necessary by the accountant under the circumstances of the particular case which have been omitted, and the reasons for their omission [@CL-MNA-0787]. The report shall state clearly the accountant's opinion on the financial statements covered and the accounting principles and practices reflected in them, and the accountant's opinion as to the consistency of the application of the accounting principles [@CL-MNA-0716], or as to any changes in such principles which have a material effect on the financial statements [@CL-MNA-0789]. Any matters to which the accountant takes exception shall be clearly identified, the exception specifically and clearly stated, and, to the extent practicable, the effect of each exception on the related financial statements given [@CL-MNA-0788].

For seven years after an accountant concludes an audit or review of an issuer's financial statements to which section 10A(a) of the Exchange Act applies, the accountant shall retain records relevant to the audit or review, including workpapers and other documents that form the basis of the audit or review [@CL-MNA-0717; @CL-MNA-0718]. The seven-year rule also applies after an accountant concludes an audit or review of the financial statements of any investment company registered under section 8 of the Investment Company Act of 1940 [@CL-MNA-0790]. The records the accountant must retain also include memoranda, correspondence, communications, other documents and records, including electronic records, that are created, sent or received in connection with the audit or review and contain conclusions, opinions, analyses, or financial data related to it [@CL-MNA-0717; @CL-MNA-0719]. The sections also cover attestation reports, an exception for certain earlier reports and definitions that this card does not draw out.

## How it works

The accountant issues a report that is dated, signed and names the statements covered [@CL-MNA-0714]. The report states the professional standards used, any omitted auditing procedures, the accountant's opinions and any exceptions [@CL-MNA-0715; @CL-MNA-0716; @CL-MNA-0787; @CL-MNA-0788]. For an audit or review the retention rule covers, the accountant keeps the records after the work ends, including workpapers and related memoranda and correspondence [@CL-MNA-0717; @CL-MNA-0719]. The clock runs for seven years from the day the accountant concludes the audit or review [@CL-MNA-0718].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An accountant signs an audit report on an issuer's statements. The report carries the date, a manual signature and the city and State of issue. It states the audit standards used and gives the opinions. The issuer's statements are ones to which the retention rule applies, so the accountant keeps the workpapers and the related memoranda and emails for seven years after the audit is concluded.

## Never confuse

The report requirements and the retention period are separate. The report states standards and opinions, while retention is about records kept after the work ends [@CL-MNA-0715; @CL-MNA-0716; @CL-MNA-0717]. The seven-year period runs from when the accountant concludes the audit or review [@CL-MNA-0718].
