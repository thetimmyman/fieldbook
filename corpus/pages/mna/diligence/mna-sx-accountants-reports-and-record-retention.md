## Purpose

Shows what an accountant's report on financial statements must state and which audit and review records the accountant must keep, and for how long. The accountant review of interim statements is on [Interim financial statements: condensed form, periods and review](mna-sx-interim-financial-statements.md).

Under § 210.2-02(a), the accountant's report shall be dated, signed manually, indicate the city and State where issued, and identify without detailed enumeration the financial statements covered by the report [@CL-MNA-0714]. Under paragraph (b), the accountant's report shall state the applicable professional standards under which the audit was conducted and shall designate any auditing procedures deemed necessary by the accountant under the circumstances of the particular case which have been omitted, and the reasons for their omission [@CL-MNA-0715; @CL-MNA-0787]. Nothing in that rule shall be construed to imply authority for the omission of any procedure which independent accountants would ordinarily employ in the course of an audit made for the purpose of expressing the opinions required by paragraph (c) of the section [@CL-MNA-0118]. Under paragraph (c), the accountant's report shall state clearly: (1) the opinion of the accountant in respect of the financial statements covered by the report and the accounting principles and practices reflected therein, and (2) the opinion of the accountant as to the consistency of the application of the accounting principles, or as to any changes in such principles which have a material effect on the financial statements [@CL-MNA-0716; @CL-MNA-0789]. Under paragraph (d), any matters to which the accountant takes exception shall be clearly identified, the exception thereto specifically and clearly stated, and, to the extent practicable, the effect of each such exception on the related financial statements given [@CL-MNA-0788].

Under § 210.2-06(a), for a period of seven years after an accountant concludes an audit or review of an issuer's financial statements to which section 10A(a) of the Securities Exchange Act of 1934 (15 USC 78j-1(a)) applies, or of the financial statements of any investment company registered under section 8 of the Investment Company Act of 1940 (15 USC 80a-8), the accountant shall retain records relevant to the audit or review, including workpapers and other documents that form the basis of the audit or review, and memoranda, correspondence, communications, other documents, and records (including electronic records), which (1) are created, sent or received in connection with the audit or review, and (2) contain conclusions, opinions, analyses, or financial data related to the audit or review [@CL-MNA-0717; @CL-MNA-0718; @CL-MNA-0719; @CL-MNA-0790].

## How it works

Under § 210.2-02, the accountant's report shall be dated, signed manually, indicate the city and State where issued, and identify without detailed enumeration the financial statements covered by the report [@CL-MNA-0714]. The report shall state the applicable professional standards under which the audit was conducted, designate any auditing procedures deemed necessary by the accountant under the circumstances of the particular case which have been omitted and the reasons for their omission (without implying authority to omit any procedure independent accountants would ordinarily employ in an audit made to express the paragraph (c) opinions), state clearly the accountant's opinions under paragraph (c), and clearly identify and state any exceptions under paragraph (d) [@CL-MNA-0715; @CL-MNA-0787; @CL-MNA-0118; @CL-MNA-0716; @CL-MNA-0789; @CL-MNA-0788]. Under § 210.2-06(a), for seven years after an accountant concludes an audit or review of an issuer's financial statements to which section 10A(a) of the Exchange Act applies or of the financial statements of an investment company registered under section 8 of the Investment Company Act of 1940, the accountant shall retain records relevant to the audit or review, including workpapers and other documents that form the basis of the audit or review and memoranda, correspondence, communications, other documents, and records (including electronic records) created, sent or received in connection with the audit or review that contain conclusions, opinions, analyses, or financial data related to the audit or review [@CL-MNA-0717; @CL-MNA-0718; @CL-MNA-0719; @CL-MNA-0790].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An accountant signs an audit report on an issuer's statements. The report carries the date, a manual signature and the city and State of issue. It states the audit standards used and gives the opinions. The issuer's statements are ones to which the retention rule applies, so the accountant keeps the workpapers and the related memoranda and emails for seven years after the audit is concluded.

## Never confuse

Under § 210.2-02(b) and (c), the accountant's report states the applicable professional standards under which the audit was conducted, designates any omitted auditing procedures deemed necessary and the reasons for their omission (without implying authority to omit any procedure independent accountants would ordinarily employ), and states clearly the accountant's opinions on the financial statements and accounting principles and practices reflected therein and on the consistency of the application of the accounting principles, or as to any changes in such principles which have a material effect on the financial statements, whereas under § 210.2-06(a), the accountant retains records relevant to the audit or review for a period of seven years after concluding an audit or review of an issuer's financial statements to which section 10A(a) of the Exchange Act applies or of the financial statements of an investment company registered under section 8 of the Investment Company Act of 1940 [@CL-MNA-0715; @CL-MNA-0787; @CL-MNA-0118; @CL-MNA-0716; @CL-MNA-0789; @CL-MNA-0717; @CL-MNA-0718; @CL-MNA-0790].
