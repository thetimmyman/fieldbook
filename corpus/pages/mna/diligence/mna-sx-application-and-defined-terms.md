## Purpose

Gives the exact meaning, in Regulation S-X, of the terms a deal team meets in target financial statements: affiliate, control, majority-owned subsidiary and significant subsidiary with its tests. It also says which filings the regulation governs.

Under § 210.1-02, an affiliate of, or a person affiliated with, a specific person is a person that directly, or indirectly through one or more intermediaries, controls, or is controlled by, or is under common control with, the person specified [@CL-MNA-0862]. Control (including the terms controlling, controlled by and under common control with) means the possession, direct or indirect, of the power to direct or cause the direction of the management and policies of a person, whether through the ownership of voting shares, by contract, or otherwise [@CL-MNA-0863]. A majority-owned subsidiary means a subsidiary more than 50 percent of whose outstanding voting shares is owned by its parent and/or the parent's other majority-owned subsidiaries [@CL-MNA-0864].

Under § 210.1-02(w)(1), a significant subsidiary means a subsidiary, including its subsidiaries, which meets any of the conditions in paragraphs (w)(1)(i), (ii), or (iii) of the section, except that if the registrant is a registered investment company or a business development company, the tested subsidiary meets any of the conditions in paragraph (w)(2) of the section instead of any of the conditions in paragraph (w)(1) [@CL-MNA-0865; @CL-MNA-0866]. Under the investment test in paragraph (w)(1)(i)(A), for acquisitions, other than those described in paragraph (w)(1)(i)(B) of the section, and dispositions, this test is met when the registrant's and its other subsidiaries' investments in and advances to the tested subsidiary exceed 10 percent of the aggregate worldwide market value of the registrant's voting and non-voting common equity, or if the registrant has no such aggregate worldwide market value, the total assets of the registrant and its subsidiaries consolidated as of the end of the most recently completed fiscal year [@CL-MNA-0867; @CL-MNA-0868; @CL-MNA-0869]. Under paragraph (w)(1)(i)(B), for a combination between entities or businesses under common control, this test is met when either the net book value of the tested subsidiary exceeds 10 percent of the registrant's and its subsidiaries' consolidated total assets or the number of common shares exchanged or to be exchanged by the registrant exceeds 10 percent of its total common shares outstanding at the date the combination is initiated [@CL-MNA-0938; @CL-MNA-0939]. Under paragraph (w)(1)(i)(C), in all other cases, this test is met when the registrant's and its other subsidiaries' investments in and advances to the tested subsidiary exceed 10 percent of the total assets of the registrant and its subsidiaries consolidated as of the end of the most recently completed fiscal year [@CL-MNA-0870]. Under the asset test in paragraph (w)(1)(ii), this test is met when the registrant's and its other subsidiaries' proportionate share of the tested subsidiary's consolidated total assets (after intercompany eliminations) exceeds 10 percent of such total assets of the registrant and its subsidiaries consolidated as of the end of the most recently completed fiscal year [@CL-MNA-0871; @CL-MNA-0940]. Under the income test in paragraph (w)(1)(iii)(A), this test is met when both (1) the absolute value of the registrant's and its other subsidiaries' equity in the tested subsidiary's consolidated income or loss from continuing operations before income taxes (after intercompany eliminations) attributable to the controlling interests exceeds 10 percent of the absolute value of such income or loss of the registrant and its subsidiaries consolidated for the most recently completed fiscal year [@CL-MNA-0936; @CL-MNA-0937] and (2) the registrant's and its other subsidiaries' proportionate share of the tested subsidiary's consolidated total revenue from continuing operations (after intercompany eliminations) exceeds 10 percent of such total revenue of the registrant and its subsidiaries consolidated for the most recently completed fiscal year [@CL-MNA-0872; @CL-MNA-0960]. However, paragraph (w)(1)(iii)(A)(2) does not apply if either the registrant and its subsidiaries consolidated or the tested subsidiary did not have material revenue in each of the two most recently completed fiscal years [@CL-MNA-0873].

## How it works

Under § 210.1-01(a), Regulation S-X (together with the Financial Reporting Releases in part 211) sets forth the form and content of and requirements for financial statements required to be filed as a part of: (1) registration statements under the Securities Act of 1933 (part 239), except as otherwise specifically provided in the forms which are to be used for registration under that Act [@CL-MNA-0860; @CL-MNA-0861], (2) registration statements under section 12, annual or other reports under sections 13 and 15(d), and proxy and information statements under section 14 of the Securities Exchange Act of 1934, except as otherwise specifically provided in the forms which are to be used for registration and reporting under those sections of that Act [@CL-MNA-0124; @CL-MNA-0125], and (3) registration statements and shareholder reports under the Investment Company Act of 1940 (part 274), except as otherwise specifically provided in the forms which are to be used for registration under that Act [@CL-MNA-0126]. Under § 210.1-02(w)(1), for a registrant that is not a registered investment company or a business development company, a subsidiary (including its subsidiaries) is a significant subsidiary if it meets any of the conditions in the investment test in paragraph (w)(1)(i), the asset test in paragraph (w)(1)(ii), or the income test in paragraph (w)(1)(iii) [@CL-MNA-0865; @CL-MNA-0866]. How the result sets the periods of a target's own statements is on [Financial statements of businesses acquired or to be acquired](mna-sx-acquired-business-financial-statements.md). Where the registrant is a shell company, see [Acquisitions of businesses by a shell company](mna-sx-shell-company-acquisition-statements.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A registrant has aggregate worldwide market value of voting and non-voting common equity of 800 and total assets of 1,000. It acquires a target for consideration of 90 and has no other investments in or advances to it. The investment comparison is 90 against 10 percent of 800, which is 80, so it is met. The target's proportionate share of consolidated total assets is 60, which is 6 percent of 1,000, so the asset test is not met. Meeting one test is enough, so the target is a significant subsidiary.

## Never confuse

Under § 210.1-02, control means the possession, direct or indirect, of the power to direct or cause the direction of the management and policies of a person, whether through the ownership of voting shares, by contract, or otherwise [@CL-MNA-0863], whereas a majority-owned subsidiary means a subsidiary more than 50 percent of whose outstanding voting shares is owned by its parent and/or the parent's other majority-owned subsidiaries [@CL-MNA-0864]. An affiliate of a specific person is a person that directly, or indirectly through one or more intermediaries, controls, or is controlled by, or is under common control with, the person specified [@CL-MNA-0862], whereas a majority-owned subsidiary requires more than 50 percent of its outstanding voting shares to be owned by its parent and/or the parent's other majority-owned subsidiaries [@CL-MNA-0864]. Whether the regulation binds a company at all depends on its filings; see [Form, GAAP presumption and materiality under Regulation S-X](../../close-revrec/group-bridge/cls-sx-form-gaap-materiality.md).
