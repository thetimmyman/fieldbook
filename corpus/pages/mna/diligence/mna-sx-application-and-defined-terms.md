## Purpose

Gives the exact meaning, in Regulation S-X, of the terms a deal team meets in target financial statements: affiliate, control, majority-owned subsidiary and significant subsidiary with its tests. It also says which filings the regulation governs. The rule is part of Regulation S-X, which is not FAR, DFARS or CAS.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Not established. |
| Codified citation | Not established. |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Not established. |
| Applies to | Financial statements required to be filed as part of the filings the application paragraph lists [@CL-MNA-0860; @CL-MNA-0861] |

An affiliate of a person is a person that directly, or indirectly through one or more intermediaries, controls, is controlled by, or is under common control with that person [@CL-MNA-0862]. Control means the possession, direct or indirect, of the power to direct or cause the direction of the management and policies of a person, whether through the ownership of voting shares, by contract, or otherwise [@CL-MNA-0863]. A majority-owned subsidiary is a subsidiary more than 50 percent of whose outstanding voting shares is owned by its parent and/or the parent's other majority-owned subsidiaries [@CL-MNA-0864].

A significant subsidiary is a subsidiary, including its subsidiaries, that meets any of the conditions in the investment, asset or income test paragraphs, subject to an exception stated immediately after [@CL-MNA-0865]. The exception is that, if the registrant is a registered investment company or a business development company, the tested subsidiary meets the conditions in a different paragraph instead [@CL-MNA-0866]. For acquisitions other than those described in the test's separate paragraph, and for dispositions, one investment-test comparison is met when the registrant's and its other subsidiaries' investments in and advances to the tested subsidiary exceed 10 percent of the aggregate worldwide market value of the registrant's voting and non-voting common equity [@CL-MNA-0867; @CL-MNA-0868]. If the registrant has no such market value, the comparison is made instead against the total assets of the registrant and its subsidiaries consolidated as of the end of the most recently completed fiscal year [@CL-MNA-0869]. For a combination between entities or businesses under common control, the investment test is met when either the net book value of the tested subsidiary exceeds 10 percent of the registrant's and its subsidiaries' consolidated total assets or the number of common shares exchanged or to be exchanged by the registrant exceeds 10 percent of its total common shares outstanding at the date the combination is initiated [@CL-MNA-0938; @CL-MNA-0939]. In all other cases the investment test is met when those investments and advances exceed 10 percent of the same consolidated total assets [@CL-MNA-0870]. The asset test is met when the registrant's and its other subsidiaries' proportionate share of the tested subsidiary's consolidated total assets, after intercompany eliminations, exceeds 10 percent of such total assets of the registrant and its subsidiaries consolidated as of the end of the most recently completed fiscal year [@CL-MNA-0871; @CL-MNA-0940]. The income test has two components joined by the word and [@CL-MNA-0936; @CL-MNA-0937]. The first looks at the absolute value of the registrant's and its other subsidiaries' equity in the tested subsidiary's consolidated income or loss from continuing operations before income taxes, after intercompany eliminations, attributable to the controlling interests [@CL-MNA-0936], and it is met when that amount exceeds 10 percent of the absolute value of such income or loss of the registrant and its subsidiaries consolidated for the most recently completed fiscal year [@CL-MNA-0937]. The second is that the proportionate share of the tested subsidiary's consolidated total revenue from continuing operations, after intercompany eliminations, exceeds 10 percent of such total revenue of the registrant and its subsidiaries consolidated for the most recently completed fiscal year [@CL-MNA-0872; @CL-MNA-0960]. That revenue comparison does not apply if either the registrant and its subsidiaries consolidated or the tested subsidiary did not have material revenue in each of the two most recently completed fiscal years [@CL-MNA-0873]. The investment test also has rules on the amount treated as the investment and on the market value date, and the income test has further rules for computing its income component. This card does not draw them out.

## How it works

The regulation, together with the Financial Reporting Releases, sets the form, content and requirements for financial statements required to be filed as part of the filings that its application paragraph lists [@CL-MNA-0860]. One listed filing is a registration statement under the Securities Act of 1933, except as otherwise specifically provided in the forms used for registration under that Act [@CL-MNA-0861]. For a registrant that is not a registered investment company or a business development company, a deal team then measures a target against the investment, asset and income tests, and meeting any one of them is enough [@CL-MNA-0865; @CL-MNA-0866]. How the result sets the periods of a target's own statements is on [Financial statements of businesses acquired or to be acquired](mna-sx-acquired-business-financial-statements.md). Where the registrant is a shell company, see [Acquisitions of businesses by a shell company](mna-sx-shell-company-acquisition-statements.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A registrant has aggregate worldwide market value of voting and non-voting common equity of 800 and total assets of 1,000. It acquires a target for consideration of 90 and has no other investments in or advances to it. The investment comparison is 90 against 10 percent of 800, which is 80, so it is met. The target's proportionate share of consolidated total assets is 60, which is 6 percent of 1,000, so the asset test is not met. Meeting one test is enough, so the target is a significant subsidiary.

## Never confuse

Control and majority ownership are different tests. Control turns on the power to direct management and policies, whether through voting shares, by contract or otherwise [@CL-MNA-0863], while a majority-owned subsidiary needs more than 50 percent of its voting shares owned by its parent and/or the parent's other majority-owned subsidiaries [@CL-MNA-0864]. Common control with another person is enough to make a person an affiliate [@CL-MNA-0862], but the majority-owned subsidiary definition turns on ownership of voting shares by the parent [@CL-MNA-0864]. Whether the regulation binds a company at all depends on its filings; see [Form, GAAP presumption and materiality under Regulation S-X](../../close-revrec/group-bridge/cls-sx-form-gaap-materiality.md).
