## Purpose

Shows what interim statements a smaller reporting company provides, whether they must be audited or reviewed, and how current its statements must be at effectiveness. The rules are part of Regulation S-X, which is not FAR, DFARS or CAS. The annual periods for income, cash flow and equity statements are on [Statements of comprehensive income, cash flows and equity under Regulation S-X](../../close-revrec/group-bridge/cls-sx-income-cash-flow-equity-statements.md). The age rules for other registrants are on [Age of financial statements and reporting currency](mna-sx-age-of-statements-and-currency.md), and the interim rules for other registrants are on [Interim financial statements: condensed form, periods and review](mna-sx-interim-financial-statements.md).

These sections apply to financial statements filed for smaller reporting companies, but not to financial statements prepared for the purposes of Item 17 or Item 18 of Form 20-F [@CL-MNA-0791]. Interim financial statements shall include a balance sheet as of the end of the issuer's most recent fiscal quarter and a balance sheet as of the end of the preceding fiscal year [@CL-MNA-0703]. The same requirement covers statements of comprehensive income and statements of cash flows for the interim period up to the date of the interim balance sheet and the comparable period of the preceding fiscal year [@CL-MNA-0703; @CL-MNA-0704]. Interim statements may be unaudited, but those included in quarterly reports on Form 10-Q must be reviewed by an independent public accountant before filing, using applicable professional standards and procedures for conducting such reviews, as may be modified or supplemented by the Commission [@CL-MNA-0705; @CL-MNA-1586].

If required financial statements are as of a date 135 days or more before a registration statement becomes effective or proxy material is expected to be mailed, they shall be updated to include financial statements for an interim period ending within 135 days of the effective or expected mailing date [@CL-MNA-0706; @CL-MNA-1587]. A smaller reporting company whose effective or mailing date falls after 45 days but within 90 days of its fiscal year end is not required to provide audited statements for that year end, provided that the listed conditions are met [@CL-MNA-0707]. One condition is that, if the company is a reporting company, all reports due must have been filed [@CL-MNA-0779]. Another is that, for the most recent fiscal year for which audited statements are not yet available, the company reasonably and in good faith expects to report income from continuing operations attributable to the registrant before taxes [@CL-MNA-0780]. A third is that, for at least one of the two fiscal years immediately preceding the most recent fiscal year, the company reported income from continuing operations attributable to the registrant before taxes [@CL-MNA-0781].

When the anticipated effective or mailing date falls within 45 days after the end of the fiscal year, the filing may include financial statements only as current as of the end of the third fiscal quarter [@CL-MNA-0777]. If the audited statements for the recently completed fiscal year are available or become available before effectiveness or mailing, they must be included in the filing [@CL-MNA-0778].

## How it works

The company provides the two balance sheets and the interim income and cash flow statements [@CL-MNA-0703; @CL-MNA-0704]. The interim statements can be unaudited, but a quarterly report needs an accountant review before filing [@CL-MNA-0705]. For a registration statement or proxy, the company checks whether its statements are 135 days or more old and updates them if so [@CL-MNA-0706]. In the later window after year end, a smaller reporting company is not required to provide the audited year-end statements provided that the listed conditions are met [@CL-MNA-0707; @CL-MNA-0779; @CL-MNA-0780; @CL-MNA-0781]. Within 45 days after year end, the audited statements must be included if they are or become available before effectiveness or mailing [@CL-MNA-0777; @CL-MNA-0778].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A smaller reporting company expects its registration statement to become effective 150 days after its last balance sheet date. That is more than 135 days, so it adds interim statements ending within 135 days of effectiveness. The interim package has balance sheets at the latest quarter end and the prior year end, plus income and cash flow statements for the interim period and the matching prior-year period. The statements can be unaudited, but a quarterly report would need an accountant review.

## Never confuse

Unaudited does not mean unreviewed, because interim statements in a quarterly report must be reviewed by an independent public accountant before filing [@CL-MNA-0705]. The 135-day update line is also not the audit relief for smaller reporting companies, which covers an effective or mailing date after 45 days but within 90 days of year end, provided that the listed conditions are met [@CL-MNA-0706; @CL-MNA-0707; @CL-MNA-0779; @CL-MNA-0780; @CL-MNA-0781].
