## Purpose

Shows the general rules and two principles behind organizational conflicts of interest, and two situations in which an unfair competitive advantage exists.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-MNA-0546] |
| Codified citation | FAR 9.505 [@CL-MNA-0546] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-MNA-0546] |
| Applies to | Contracting situations stated in the general rules [@CL-MNA-0546] |

The general rules prescribe limitations on contracting as the means of avoiding, neutralizing or mitigating organizational conflicts of interest that might otherwise exist in the stated situations [@CL-MNA-0546]. The two underlying principles are preventing the existence of conflicting roles that might bias a contractor's judgment and preventing unfair competitive advantage [@CL-MNA-0547].

## How it works

In addition to the other situations described in the subpart, an unfair competitive advantage exists where a contractor competing for award of any Federal contract possesses proprietary information that was obtained from a Government official without proper authorization [@CL-MNA-0548]. The second item in the same list is source selection information that is relevant to the contract but is not available to all competitors, where such information would assist that contractor in obtaining the contract [@CL-MNA-0549]. This card does not cover the definitions, waiver or procedure rules, which are not established from the sources held.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A target competing for a Federal contract obtained a competitor's proprietary information from a Government employee without authorization. In a second bid, it held source selection information relevant to the contract that was not available to all competitors and would help it win.

Answer: in the first case the rule says an unfair competitive advantage exists. In the second case it also exists, because the information is relevant, not available to all competitors, and would assist the target in obtaining the contract.

## Never confuse

The two principles are different tests. One looks at conflicting roles that might bias a contractor's judgment, and the other looks at unfair competitive advantage [@CL-MNA-0547]. The two listed situations are cases where unfair competitive advantage exists in addition to the other situations in the subpart [@CL-MNA-0548].

## Company calibration
