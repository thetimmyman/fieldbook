## Purpose

Shows when a registrant must file separate financial statements of a business it has acquired or is about to acquire, how the significance conditions set the number of periods, and when the separate statements can be left out. The rule is part of Regulation S-X, which is not FAR, DFARS or CAS. Whether Regulation S-X binds a company depends on its filings with the Securities and Exchange Commission; see [Form, GAAP presumption and materiality under Regulation S-X](../../close-revrec/group-bridge/cls-sx-form-gaap-materiality.md).

| Applies to | A business acquisition that has occurred, or is probable, in the periods the rule describes [@CL-MNA-0671; @CL-MNA-0672] |

Statements of the acquired business must be filed for the periods the rule sets if a listed condition exists [@CL-MNA-0671]. One listed condition is a business acquisition during the most recent fiscal year or subsequent interim period for which a balance sheet is required [@CL-MNA-0671]. Another is that consummation of a business acquisition has occurred or is probable after the date of the most recent balance sheet filed [@CL-MNA-0672]. Those statements, other than the related schedules, must be prepared and audited under Regulation S-X, including the independence standards in § 210.2-01 or, alternatively if the business is not a registrant, the applicable independence standards [@CL-MNA-0670]. The section does not apply to a real estate operation with its own section or to a business the registrant totally held before the transaction [@CL-MNA-0673].

In all cases not covered by the rule for registering an offering to the target's security holders, statements are filed for the periods that follow the significance bands, or for such shorter period as the business has been in existence [@CL-MNA-0674]. The bands use the conditions in the significant subsidiary definition, with the lower of the total revenue component or the income or loss from continuing operations component used for the income test [@CL-MNA-0675]. If none of the conditions exceeds 20 percent, financial statements are not required under the bands [@CL-MNA-0676]. If any condition exceeds 20 percent but none exceeds 40 percent, statements cover at least the most recent fiscal year and the most recent interim period [@CL-MNA-0677]. If any condition exceeds 40 percent, statements cover at least the two most recent fiscal years and any interim periods [@CL-MNA-0678]. When the registrant registers an offering of securities to the security holders of the business to be acquired, the financial statements specified in §§ 210.3-01 and 210.3-02 must be filed for that business, except as provided otherwise for filings on Form N-14, S-4, or F-4 [@CL-MNA-0679]. The statements the bands require may be omitted in the cases the section lists, which include the two nine-month and full-year cases below [@CL-MNA-0680; @CL-MNA-0681].

Separate statements at the middle band need not be presented once the target's operating results have been in the registrant's audited consolidated statements for at least nine months [@CL-MNA-0680]. Separate statements at the highest band need not be presented once the operating results of the acquired business have been reflected in the audited consolidated financial statements of the registrant for a complete fiscal year [@CL-MNA-0681]. A registrant that omits from its initial registration statement financial statements of a recently consummated business acquisition pursuant to paragraph (b)(4)(i) of the section must, unless it is a foreign private issuer required to file reports on Form 6-K or a shell company other than a business combination related shell company, file them under cover of Form 8-K [@CL-MNA-0770; @CL-MNA-1581; @CL-MNA-0771]. The filing is due no later than 75 days after consummation of the acquisition, and any pro forma information specified by Article 11 (§§ 210.11-01 through 210.11-03) goes with it [@CL-MNA-0771].

For smaller reporting companies, two companion sections of Article 8 use the same rules by substitution [@CL-MNA-1336; @CL-MNA-1337]; Article 8 applies to financial statements filed for smaller reporting companies and not to financial statements prepared for Item 17 or Item 18 of Form 20-F [@CL-MNA-0791]. For the financial statements of businesses acquired or to be acquired, § 210.8-04 applies § 210.3-05 with §§ 210.8-02 and 210.8-03, as applicable, substituted wherever § 210.3-05 references §§ 210.3-01 and 210.3-02 [@CL-MNA-1336]. For real estate operations acquired or to be acquired, § 210.8-06 applies § 210.3-14 with the same substitution wherever § 210.3-14 references §§ 210.3-01 and 210.3-02 [@CL-MNA-1337].

The source text printed for this card does not set out the individual tests inside the significant subsidiary definition, so this card does not state how each test is computed. The section also sets an aggregate-impact disclosure for small acquisitions, further omissions, and special cases for foreign businesses, net assets and oil and gas activities that this card does not draw out.

## How it works

The registrant first asks whether a business acquisition has occurred or is probable [@CL-MNA-0671; @CL-MNA-0672]. It then measures the target against the significant subsidiary conditions, using the lower of the revenue and income components for the income test [@CL-MNA-0675]. In cases other than registering an offering to the target's security holders, the result picks the band: no statements under the bands, the latest year plus the interim period, or two years plus the interim periods [@CL-MNA-0674; @CL-MNA-0676; @CL-MNA-0677; @CL-MNA-0678]. Separate statements need not be presented once the target's results have been in the registrant's audited consolidated statements for at least nine months at the middle band, or for a complete fiscal year at the highest band [@CL-MNA-0680; @CL-MNA-0681].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A registrant buys a business after its last balance sheet date. The highest of the conditions measures 30 percent. That is above 20 percent and not above 40 percent, so the registrant files at least the target's most recent fiscal year and its latest interim period. If the highest condition had measured 45 percent, at least two fiscal years would be needed. Nine months after the purchase the target's results sit in the registrant's audited consolidated statements, so separate statements for the middle band need no longer be presented.

## Never confuse

Statements of the acquired business are not the registrant's own financial statements, and a significant acquisition can also call for pro forma information under a separate rule, described in [Pro forma financial information: when it is required](mna-sx-pro-forma-presentation.md). The pro forma rule asks for different information and lists, as one test of significance, the definition of a significant subsidiary in § 210.1-02(w)(1), substituting 20 percent for 10 percent each place it appears [@CL-MNA-0687], while this rule sets bands that decide how many periods of the target's own statements are filed [@CL-MNA-0676; @CL-MNA-0677; @CL-MNA-0678].
