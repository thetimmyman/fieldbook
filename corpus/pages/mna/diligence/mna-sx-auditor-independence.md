## Purpose

Shows the general standard of auditor independence under Regulation S-X, the kinds of relationships and services it restricts, and the period during which the financial and employment tests apply. The rule is part of Regulation S-X, which is not FAR, DFARS or CAS. What the accountant's report must say and how long audit records are kept is on [Accountants' reports and retention of audit records](mna-sx-accountants-reports-and-record-retention.md). What the accounting firm reports to the audit committee is on [Audit committee communication and income statement instructions](mna-sx-audit-committee-and-income-statement-rules.md).

The Commission will not recognize an accountant as independent, with respect to an audit client, if the accountant is not, or a reasonable investor with knowledge of all relevant facts and circumstances would conclude that the accountant is not, capable of exercising objective and impartial judgment [@CL-MNA-0874], which is judgment on all issues encompassed within the accountant's engagement [@CL-MNA-0935]. In determining independence the Commission considers all relevant circumstances, including all relationships between the accountant and the audit client, and not just those relating to reports filed with the Commission [@CL-MNA-0875].

The rule sets out restrictions on financial, employment and business relationships between an accountant and an audit client, and restrictions on an accountant providing certain non-audit services to an audit client [@CL-MNA-0876]. The paragraph on specific circumstances is a non-exclusive specification of circumstances inconsistent with the general standard [@CL-MNA-0877]. An accountant is not independent if, at any point during the audit and professional engagement period, the accountant provides to an audit client the non-audit services that the paragraph goes on to list [@CL-MNA-0941]. One listed service is bookkeeping or other services related to the accounting records or financial statements of the audit client, being any such service unless it is reasonable to conclude that the results will not be subject to audit procedures during an audit of the audit client's financial statements [@CL-MNA-0942]. Another listed service is acting, temporarily or permanently, as a director, officer or employee of an audit client, or performing any decision-making, supervisory or ongoing monitoring function for the audit client [@CL-MNA-0943]. An accountant is also not independent if, during that period, the accountant provides any service or product to an audit client for a contingent fee or a commission, or receives a contingent fee or commission from an audit client [@CL-MNA-0946]. The list of services has further items, and the section has further tests, that this card does not draw out.

## How it works

An accountant is not independent if, at any point during the audit and professional engagement period, the accountant has a direct financial interest or a material indirect financial interest in the audit client [@CL-MNA-0878]. One listed case is a direct investment in an audit client, such as stocks, bonds, notes, options or other securities, held by the accounting firm, a covered person in the firm or an immediate family member [@CL-MNA-0879]. The term direct investment also includes an investment in an audit client through an intermediary if the firm, covered person or family member, alone or together with other persons, supervises or participates in the intermediary's investment decisions or has control over the intermediary [@CL-MNA-1527; @CL-MNA-1528], or if the intermediary is not a diversified management investment company, as defined by section 5(b)(1) of the Investment Company Act of 1940, and has an investment in the audit client that amounts to 20% or more of the value of the intermediary's total investments [@CL-MNA-1529]. The same period governs employment relationships [@CL-MNA-0880], and one listed example is that a current partner, principal, shareholder or professional employee of the firm is employed by the audit client or serves on its board of directors or similar governing body [@CL-MNA-0881]. The paragraphs on investments and other financial interests are subject to the cases the exceptions paragraph lists [@CL-MNA-0882]. The audit and professional engagement period includes both the audit period, meaning the period covered by any financial statements being audited or reviewed, and the period of the engagement to audit or review the audit client's financial statements or to prepare a report filed with the Commission [@CL-MNA-0959]. The professional engagement period begins when the accountant either signs an initial engagement letter or other agreement to review or audit a client's financial statements, or begins audit, review or attest procedures, whichever is earlier [@CL-MNA-0944]. It ends when the audit client or the accountant notifies the Commission that the client is no longer that accountant's audit client [@CL-MNA-0945].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

During the audit and professional engagement period for an audit client, a covered person in the audit firm buys 100 shares of that client. That is a direct investment in an audit client, so the firm is not independent unless a listed exception applies. If a family member of that person instead held a small stake only through a broad fund, the question would turn on whether the indirect interest is material and on the other listed cases.

## Never confuse

Independence is not only a matter of share ownership. The list of specific circumstances is non-exclusive, and the general standard looks at all relevant circumstances [@CL-MNA-0877; @CL-MNA-0875]. It is also separate from the accountant's report on the statements, which is on the linked card.
