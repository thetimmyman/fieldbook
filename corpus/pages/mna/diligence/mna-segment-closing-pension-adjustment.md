## Purpose

Shows what counts as a segment closing under the pension standard and how the pension cost adjustment is worked out and shared with the Government.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified CAS [@CL-MNA-0392] |
| Codified citation | 48 CFR 9904.413-50(c)(12) [@CL-MNA-0392] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-MNA-0392] |
| Applies to | A segment closing, a pension plan termination or a curtailment of benefits [@CL-MNA-0392] |

A segment closing means that a segment has been sold or ownership has been otherwise transferred, has discontinued operations, or has discontinued doing or actively seeking Government business under contracts subject to the Standard [@CL-MNA-0391]. When a segment is closed, or a plan is terminated, or benefits are curtailed, the contractor must determine the difference between the actuarial accrued liability for the segment and the market value of the assets allocated to the segment, irrespective of whether or not the pension plan is terminated [@CL-MNA-0392; @CL-MNA-1579]. That difference represents an adjustment of previously-determined pension costs [@CL-MNA-0393].

The Government's share of the adjustment amount for a segment is the product of the adjustment amount and a fraction, and the adjustment amount is reduced for any excise tax imposed on assets withdrawn from the funding agency of a qualified pension plan [@CL-MNA-0399]. The numerator of the fraction is the sum of the pension plan costs allocated to all contracts and subcontracts (including Foreign Military Sales) subject to the Standard during a period of years representative of the Government's participation in the pension plan [@CL-MNA-0463], and the denominator is the total pension costs assigned to cost accounting periods during those same years [@CL-MNA-0464].

## How it works

The liability is determined using the accrued benefit cost method with assumptions consistent with the current and prior long term assumptions [@CL-MNA-0394]. If there is a pension plan termination, the actuarial accrued liability is measured as the amount paid to irrevocably settle all benefit obligations or paid to the Pension Benefit Guarantee Corporation [@CL-MNA-0459]. The market value of the segment's assets is reduced by the accumulated value of prepayment credits, if any, and increased by the current value of any unfunded actuarial liability separately identified and maintained in accordance with 9904.412-50(a)(2) [@CL-MNA-0395]. The calculation is made as of the date of the event (for example, contract termination, plan amendment or plant closure) that caused the closing of the segment, the pension plan termination, or the curtailment of benefits [@CL-MNA-0396; @CL-MNA-0092], unless that date is not readily determinable or its use can result in an inequitable calculation, in which case the contracting parties agree on an appropriate date [@CL-MNA-0460]. Plan improvements adopted within 60 months of that date that increase the liability are recognized on a prorata basis by the months the adoption preceded the event [@CL-MNA-0397], except improvements mandated by law or collective bargaining agreement [@CL-MNA-0461]. If a segment is closed due to a sale or other transfer of ownership to a successor in interest in the contracts of the segment, and all of the pension plan assets and actuarial accrued liabilities pertaining to the closed segment are transferred to the successor segment, no adjustment amount is required [@CL-MNA-1580; @CL-MNA-0398]. If only some pass, the adjustment is determined from the assets and liabilities remaining with the contractor [@CL-MNA-0462].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A segment is sold. Its actuarial accrued liability under the accrued benefit cost method is 30 million. Assets allocated to it have a market value of 36 million, and prepayment credits of 2 million are taken out, leaving 34 million. The difference is 4 million. The Government's fraction is 25 percent and no excise tax applies. The Government's share is 1 million. The difference arises because assets exceed the liability. Had all the assets and liabilities moved to the buyer's successor segment, no adjustment would be made.

## Never confuse

This card covers only the adjustment made when a segment is closed, a plan is terminated or benefits are curtailed [@CL-MNA-0392]. For the annual assignment of gains and losses and the allocation of pension cost to segments, see [Adjusting pension cost and allocating it to segments](../../compliance/cas-allowability/cmp-cas-413-pension-adjustment-allocation.md); see also [Pension cost composition](../../compliance/cas-allowability/cmp-cas-412-pension-composition.md). A sale of a segment is one kind of closing, but not every sale needs a full adjustment: a closing due to a transfer of ownership to a successor in interest in the segment's contracts, with all the plan assets and liabilities transferred to the successor segment, needs none, and a partial transfer leaves an adjustment on what remains [@CL-MNA-0391; @CL-MNA-0398; @CL-MNA-0462].
