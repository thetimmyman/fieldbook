## Purpose

Shows what a Regulation M-A filing must say about why the transaction is being done, what becomes of the securities acquired, and what plans, proposals and negotiations exist for the subject company.

### Purposes, use of securities and plans

Paragraph (a) requires a statement of the purposes of the transaction, and paragraph (b) an indication whether the securities acquired in the transaction will be retained, retired, held in treasury or otherwise disposed of [@CL-MNA-1071]. Paragraph (c) requires a description of any plans, proposals or negotiations that relate to or would result in the outcomes listed in the paragraph [@CL-MNA-1071].

The listed outcomes include any extraordinary transaction, such as a merger, reorganization or liquidation, involving the subject company or any of its subsidiaries, and any purchase, sale or transfer of a material amount of assets of the subject company or any of its subsidiaries [@CL-MNA-1072]. They include any material change in the present dividend rate or policy, or indebtedness or capitalization of the subject company [@CL-MNA-1073]. They include any change in the present board of directors or management of the subject company, including, but not limited to, any plans or proposals to change the number or the term of directors, to fill any existing vacancies on the board or to change any material term of the employment contract of any executive officer [@CL-MNA-1073; @CL-MNA-1074].

They include any other material change in the subject company's corporate structure or business, including, if the subject company is a registered closed-end investment company, any plans or proposals to make any changes in its investment policy for which a vote would be required by Section 13 of the Investment Company Act of 1940 [@CL-MNA-1122; @CL-MNA-1123]. They include any class of equity securities of the subject company to be delisted from a national securities exchange or to cease to be authorized to be quoted in an automated quotations system operated by a national securities association [@CL-MNA-1124]. They include any class of equity securities of the subject company becoming eligible for termination of registration under section 12(g)(4) of the Act, and the suspension of the subject company's obligation to file reports under Section 15(d) of the Act [@CL-MNA-1125].

They also include the acquisition by any person of additional securities of the subject company, or the disposition of securities of the subject company [@CL-MNA-1075]. They include any changes in the subject company's charter, bylaws or other governing instruments or other actions that could impede the acquisition of control of the subject company [@CL-MNA-1076].

### Subject company negotiations

If the filing person is the subject company, paragraph (d)(1) requires a statement whether or not it is undertaking or engaged in any negotiations in response to the tender offer that relate to the matters in the next sentence [@CL-MNA-1077]. Those matters are a tender offer or other acquisition of the subject company's securities by the filing person, any of its subsidiaries or any other person, and any of the matters referred to in paragraphs (c)(1) through (c)(3) [@CL-MNA-1078].

If the filing person is the subject company, paragraph (d)(2) also requires a description of any transaction, board resolution, agreement in principle, or signed contract that is entered into in response to the tender offer and relates to one or more of the matters referred to in paragraph (d)(1) [@CL-MNA-1079].

The instruction to paragraph (d)(1) gives relief [@CL-MNA-1080]. If an agreement in principle has not been reached at the time of filing, no disclosure under paragraph (d)(1) is required of the possible terms of or the parties to the transaction [@CL-MNA-1080]. The relief applies if, in the opinion of the board of directors of the subject company, disclosure would jeopardize continuation of the negotiations [@CL-MNA-1081]. In that case, disclosure indicating that negotiations are being undertaken or are underway and are in the preliminary stages is sufficient [@CL-MNA-1081].

## How it works

Purpose and use of the acquired securities are answered directly, while the plans paragraph works by consequence: a plan, proposal or negotiation that relates to or would result in a listed outcome must be described [@CL-MNA-1071]. The subject company has two further duties when it is the filing person: it states whether it is negotiating in response to the offer, and it describes responsive transactions, board resolutions, agreements in principle and signed contracts [@CL-MNA-1077; @CL-MNA-1079]. The board of directors of the subject company decides, by its opinion, whether disclosure of possible terms and parties would jeopardize the negotiations [@CL-MNA-1081]. The relief is stated for paragraph (d)(1) [@CL-MNA-1080].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A bidder acquires 60 percent of a target's shares by tender offer. It states the purposes of the transaction and that the acquired shares will be retained [@CL-MNA-1071]. It plans to fund the deal with new debt at the target that would materially change the target's indebtedness and to replace two of the target's five directors, so it describes those plans [@CL-MNA-1073].

The target is also a filing person. It is negotiating a sale of a material amount of assets to a third party in response to the offer, but has no agreement in principle. Its board decides that disclosure of the possible terms and the buyer would jeopardize the negotiations. The target then states that negotiations are underway and are in the preliminary stages [@CL-MNA-1072; @CL-MNA-1078; @CL-MNA-1080; @CL-MNA-1081]. If the target had signed a contract in response to the offer, the contract would be described under paragraph (d)(2), because the relief is stated for paragraph (d)(1) and applies where no agreement in principle has been reached [@CL-MNA-1079; @CL-MNA-1080].

## Never confuse

A purpose is not a plan: the purposes paragraph states why the transaction is being done, while the plans paragraph describes plans, proposals or negotiations that relate to or would result in the listed outcomes [@CL-MNA-1071]. The duties in paragraph (d) apply where the filing person is the subject company [@CL-MNA-1077; @CL-MNA-1079]. The relief for undisclosed terms and parties is stated for paragraph (d)(1) and does not mention the description of responsive transactions and agreements under paragraph (d)(2) [@CL-MNA-1079; @CL-MNA-1080]. The reasons and alternatives in a going-private statement are on a [separate card](../valuation/mna-going-private-purposes-alternatives-reasons-effects.md).

## Company calibration

The company confirms its stated purpose, which post-closing plans it must disclose, and who on its board forms the opinion on whether disclosure would jeopardize negotiations. [@Q-029]
