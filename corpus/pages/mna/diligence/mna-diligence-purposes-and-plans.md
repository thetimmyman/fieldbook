## Purpose

Shows what a Regulation M-A filing must say about why the transaction is being done, what becomes of the securities acquired, and what plans, proposals and negotiations exist for the subject company.

### Purposes, use of securities and plans

Paragraph (a) requires a statement of the purposes of the transaction, and paragraph (b) an indication whether the securities acquired in the transaction will be retained, retired, held in treasury, or otherwise disposed of [@CL-MNA-1071]. Paragraph (c) requires a description of any plans, proposals or negotiations that relate to or would result in any of the alternative outcomes in paragraphs (c)(1) through (c)(10) [@CL-MNA-1071; @CL-MNA-1076].

Those alternative outcomes are: (1) any extraordinary transaction, such as a merger, reorganization or liquidation, involving the subject company or any of its subsidiaries, or (2) any purchase, sale or transfer of a material amount of assets of the subject company or any of its subsidiaries [@CL-MNA-1072; @CL-MNA-1076]. They also include (3) any material change in the present dividend rate or policy, or indebtedness or capitalization of the subject company, or (4) any change in the present board of directors or management of the subject company, including, but not limited to, any plans or proposals to change the number or the term of directors or to fill any existing vacancies on the board or to change any material term of the employment contract of any executive officer [@CL-MNA-1073; @CL-MNA-1074; @CL-MNA-1076].

They also include (5) any other material change in the subject company's corporate structure or business, including, if the subject company is a registered closed-end investment company, any plans or proposals to make any changes in its investment policy for which a vote would be required by Section 13 of the Investment Company Act of 1940, or (6) any class of equity securities of the subject company to be delisted from a national securities exchange or cease to be authorized to be quoted in an automated quotations system operated by a national securities association [@CL-MNA-1122; @CL-MNA-1123; @CL-MNA-1124; @CL-MNA-1076]. They also include (7) any class of equity securities of the subject company becoming eligible for termination of registration under section 12(g)(4) of the Act, or (8) the suspension of the subject company's obligation to file reports under Section 15(d) of the Act [@CL-MNA-1125; @CL-MNA-1076].

They also include (9) the acquisition by any person of additional securities of the subject company, or the disposition of securities of the subject company, or (10) any changes in the subject company's charter, bylaws or other governing instruments or other actions that could impede the acquisition of control of the subject company [@CL-MNA-1075; @CL-MNA-1076].

### Subject company negotiations

If the filing person is the subject company, paragraph (d)(1) requires a statement whether or not it is undertaking or engaged in any negotiations in response to the tender offer that relate to: (i) a tender offer or other acquisition of the subject company's securities by the filing person, any of its subsidiaries, or any other person, or (ii) any of the matters referred to in paragraphs (c)(1) through (c)(3) of the section [@CL-MNA-1077; @CL-MNA-1078].

If the filing person is the subject company, paragraph (d)(2) requires a description of any transaction, board resolution, agreement in principle, or signed contract that is entered into in response to the tender offer that relates to one or more of the matters referred to in paragraph (d)(1) of the section [@CL-MNA-1079].

Under the instruction to Item 1006(d)(1), if an agreement in principle has not been reached at the time of filing, no disclosure under paragraph (d)(1) of the section is required of the possible terms of or the parties to the transaction if in the opinion of the board of directors of the subject company disclosure would jeopardize continuation of the negotiations [@CL-MNA-1080; @CL-MNA-1081]. In that case, disclosure indicating that negotiations are being undertaken or are underway and are in the preliminary stages is sufficient [@CL-MNA-1081].

## How it works

Paragraph (a) requires stating the purposes of the transaction, paragraph (b) requires indicating whether the securities acquired in the transaction will be retained, retired, held in treasury, or otherwise disposed of, and paragraph (c) requires describing any plans, proposals or negotiations that relate to or would result in any of its ten alternative outcomes [@CL-MNA-1071; @CL-MNA-1076]. If the filing person is the subject company, paragraph (d)(1) requires stating whether or not it is undertaking or engaged in any negotiations in response to the tender offer that relate to a tender offer or other acquisition of the subject company's securities by the filing person, any of its subsidiaries, or any other person, or any of the matters referred to in paragraphs (c)(1) through (c)(3), and paragraph (d)(2) requires describing any transaction, board resolution, agreement in principle, or signed contract entered into in response to the tender offer that relates to one or more of the matters referred to in paragraph (d)(1) [@CL-MNA-1077; @CL-MNA-1078; @CL-MNA-1079]. Under the instruction to Item 1006(d)(1), no disclosure under paragraph (d)(1) is required of the possible terms of or the parties to the transaction if an agreement in principle has not been reached at the time of filing and if in the opinion of the board of directors of the subject company disclosure would jeopardize continuation of the negotiations, in which case disclosure indicating that negotiations are being undertaken or are underway and are in the preliminary stages is sufficient [@CL-MNA-1080; @CL-MNA-1081].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A bidder acquires 60 percent of a target's shares by tender offer. It states the purposes of the transaction and that the acquired shares will be retained [@CL-MNA-1071]. It plans to fund the deal with new debt at the target that would materially change the target's indebtedness and to replace two of the target's five directors, so it describes those plans [@CL-MNA-1073].

The target is also a filing person. It is negotiating a sale of a material amount of assets to a third party in response to the offer, but has no agreement in principle. Its board decides that disclosure of the possible terms and the buyer would jeopardize the negotiations. The target then states that negotiations are underway and are in the preliminary stages [@CL-MNA-1072; @CL-MNA-1078; @CL-MNA-1080; @CL-MNA-1081]. If the target had signed a contract in response to the offer, the contract would be described under paragraph (d)(2), because the relief is stated for paragraph (d)(1) and applies where no agreement in principle has been reached [@CL-MNA-1079; @CL-MNA-1080].

## Never confuse

Paragraph (a) requires stating the purposes of the transaction, whereas paragraph (c) requires describing any plans, proposals or negotiations that relate to or would result in any of the alternative outcomes in paragraphs (c)(1) through (c)(10) [@CL-MNA-1071; @CL-MNA-1076]. Paragraphs (d)(1) and (d)(2) apply if the filing person is the subject company [@CL-MNA-1077; @CL-MNA-1079]. Under the instruction to Item 1006(d)(1), if an agreement in principle has not been reached at the time of filing, no disclosure under paragraph (d)(1) is required of the possible terms of or the parties to the transaction if in the opinion of the board of directors of the subject company disclosure would jeopardize continuation of the negotiations, whereas paragraph (d)(2) requires describing any transaction, board resolution, agreement in principle, or signed contract entered into in response to the tender offer that relates to one or more of the matters referred to in paragraph (d)(1) [@CL-MNA-1079; @CL-MNA-1080; @CL-MNA-1081]. The reasons and alternatives in a going-private statement are on a [separate card](../valuation/mna-going-private-purposes-alternatives-reasons-effects.md).

## Company calibration

The company confirms its stated purpose, which post-closing plans it must disclose, and who on its board forms the opinion on whether disclosure would jeopardize negotiations. [@Q-029]
