## Purpose

Shows the defined terms that Regulation M-A uses and what the summary term sheet in a Regulation M-A disclosure document must contain.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Not established. |
| Codified citation | Not established. |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Not established. |
| Applies to | Terms used throughout Regulation M-A and the summary term sheet given to security holders [@CL-MNA-0250; @CL-MNA-0259] |

Unless a term is specified otherwise, the definitions in the definitions section apply to the terms used in Regulation M-A, which runs from § 229.1000 through § 229.1016 [@CL-MNA-0250]. Associate, issuer tender offer and Rule 13e-3 transaction take their meaning from cross-referenced rules [@CL-MNA-0250; @CL-MNA-0253; @CL-MNA-0255]. The glossary below states each term as the section defines it.

### Glossary

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Associate | The same meaning as in § 240.12b-2 of the same chapter [@CL-MNA-0250] |
| Instruction C | General Instruction C to Schedule 13E-3 (§ 240.13e-100) and General Instruction C to Schedule TO (§ 240.14d-100), each section being in the same chapter [@CL-MNA-0252] |
| Issuer tender offer | The same meaning as in § 240.13e-4(a)(2) of the same chapter [@CL-MNA-0253] |
| Offeror | Any person who makes a tender offer or on whose behalf a tender offer is made [@CL-MNA-0254] |
| Rule 13e-3 transaction | The same meaning as in § 240.13e-3(a)(3) of the same chapter [@CL-MNA-0255] |
| Subject company | The company or entity whose securities are sought to be acquired in the transaction (for example, the target), or that is otherwise the subject of the transaction [@CL-MNA-0256] |
| Subject securities | The securities or class of securities that are sought to be acquired in the transaction or that are otherwise the subject of the transaction [@CL-MNA-0257] |
| Third-party tender offer | A tender offer that is not an issuer tender offer [@CL-MNA-0258] |

The summary term sheet item requires security holders to be provided with a summary term sheet written in plain English, and the sheet must briefly describe the most material terms of the proposed transaction in bullet point format [@CL-MNA-0259]. It must give security holders sufficient information to understand the essential features and significance of the proposed transaction [@CL-MNA-0260]. Its bullet points must cross-reference a more detailed discussion in the disclosure document that is disseminated to security holders [@CL-MNA-0261].

The instructions add limits on content and placement. The sheet must not recite all information in the disclosure document and is intended as an overview of all material matters presented in the accompanying documents [@CL-MNA-0262]. It must begin on the first or second page of the disclosure document, and the third instruction refers to Rule 421(b) and (d) of Regulation C under the Securities Act for a description of plain English disclosure [@CL-MNA-0263].

## How it works

Instruction C, offeror, subject company, subject securities and third-party tender offer are defined in the section's own words or by naming a schedule, and the other three terms by cross-reference [@CL-MNA-0252; @CL-MNA-0254; @CL-MNA-0256; @CL-MNA-0257; @CL-MNA-0258]. A transaction therefore has a subject company whose securities, the subject securities, are sought, and a tender offer has an offeror [@CL-MNA-0254; @CL-MNA-0256; @CL-MNA-0257]. A tender offer is either an issuer tender offer, as the cross-referenced rule defines it, or a third-party tender offer [@CL-MNA-0253; @CL-MNA-0258]. The summary term sheet gives security holders the overview, with bullet points that lead to the full discussion [@CL-MNA-0259; @CL-MNA-0261]. The other Regulation M-A cards, such as [Terms of the transaction](mna-diligence-terms-of-the-transaction.md) and [Subject company information and filing person background](mna-diligence-subject-company-and-filing-person-background.md), use these terms.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A bidder offers 50 per share in cash for all the shares of a listed target. The target is the subject company, its shares are the subject securities, and the bidder is the offeror because the offer is made by it [@CL-MNA-0254; @CL-MNA-0256; @CL-MNA-0257]. Assume the offer is not an issuer tender offer under the cross-referenced rule. It is then a third-party tender offer [@CL-MNA-0253; @CL-MNA-0258]. The disclosure document begins its summary term sheet on its first page, which satisfies the rule that the sheet begin on the first or second page [@CL-MNA-0263]. The sheet states the price, the expiry date and the main conditions in bullet points, each pointing to the fuller discussion later in the document [@CL-MNA-0259; @CL-MNA-0261]. It does not repeat the whole document [@CL-MNA-0262].

## Never confuse

An issuer tender offer is not defined in the section's own words: it takes its meaning from a cross-referenced rule, and a third-party tender offer is any tender offer that is not one [@CL-MNA-0253; @CL-MNA-0258]. The subject company is not the offeror: the subject company is the company whose securities are sought or that is otherwise the subject, while the offeror is the person who makes a tender offer or for whom it is made [@CL-MNA-0254; @CL-MNA-0256]. The summary term sheet is an overview, not a restatement of the disclosure document [@CL-MNA-0262].

## Company calibration

The company confirms which of its filings use these definitions and whether each summary term sheet meets the plain English, bullet point, placement and cross-reference rules. [@Q-028]
