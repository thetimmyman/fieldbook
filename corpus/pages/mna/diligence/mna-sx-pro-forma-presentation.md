## Purpose

Shows when a registrant must file pro forma financial information about an acquisition or disposition and what makes a business acquisition significant. It covers when the information is required, not how it is prepared; that is on [Pro forma financial information: how it is prepared](mna-sx-pro-forma-preparation.md). The rule is part of Regulation S-X, which is not FAR, DFARS or CAS.

| Applies to | A filing in which a listed condition exists, such as a significant acquisition or disposition [@CL-MNA-0682; @CL-MNA-0683; @CL-MNA-0686] |

Pro forma financial information must be filed when any of the listed conditions exists [@CL-MNA-0682]. One listed condition is a significant business acquisition during the most recent fiscal year or subsequent interim period for which a balance sheet is required, which includes an interest in a business accounted for by the equity method [@CL-MNA-0683]. Another is that, after the date of the most recent balance sheet filed, consummation of a significant business acquisition or a combination of entities under common control has occurred or is probable [@CL-MNA-0684]. Another is that the securities being registered are offered to the security holders of a significant business to be acquired, or the proceeds will be applied directly or indirectly to the purchase of a specific significant business [@CL-MNA-0685]. Another is that the disposition of a significant portion of a business by sale, abandonment or distribution to shareholders by means of a spin-off, split-up or split-off has occurred or is probable and is not fully reflected in the financial statements of the registrant included in the filing [@CL-MNA-0686]. The list has further conditions that this card does not draw out [@CL-MNA-0682].

One listed test of a significant business acquisition is the definition of a significant subsidiary with 20 percent substituted for 10 percent each place it appears [@CL-MNA-0687]. The source text printed for this card does not set out the individual tests inside that definition, so this card does not state how each test is computed. The pro forma effects of an acquisition need not be presented if separate statements of the target are not included in the filing, except where the aggregate impact of businesses acquired or to be acquired is significant [@CL-MNA-0688]. The exception turns on the aggregate-impact tests that the acquired-business section and the real estate section set, which this card does not draw out [@CL-MNA-0772].

Article 8 (§§ 210.8-01 through 210.8-08) applies to financial statements filed for smaller reporting companies and not to financial statements prepared for Item 17 or Item 18 of Form 20-F [@CL-MNA-0791]. Within Article 8, § 210.8-05 says pro forma financial information must be disclosed when any of the conditions in § 210.11-01 exist [@CL-MNA-1338]. Its preparation, presentation and disclosure must comply with §§ 210.11-01 through 210.11-03 (Article 11), except that the pro forma financial information may be condensed pursuant to § 210.8-03(a) [@CL-MNA-1339].

## How it works

The registrant checks the listed conditions against the filing it is making [@CL-MNA-0682]. For an acquisition it then applies the listed tests of significance, one of which is the significant subsidiary definition with 20 percent substituted for 10 percent each place it appears [@CL-MNA-0687]. If the target's own statements are not in the filing, the pro forma effects of the acquisition need not be presented, except where the aggregate impact of businesses acquired or to be acquired is significant [@CL-MNA-0688; @CL-MNA-0772].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A registrant's filing includes a balance sheet as of its latest quarter end. After that date it agrees to buy a business, and the closing is probable. The target measures 25 percent on a significance test, against a line of 20 percent. The acquisition is significant, so pro forma financial information is filed. Had the target measured 15 percent, the acquisition would not have met this test.

## Never confuse

The significance test for pro forma information is not the same as the bands that decide how many periods of the target's own statements are filed [@CL-MNA-0687; @CL-MNA-0676; @CL-MNA-0677; @CL-MNA-0678]. One decides whether pro forma information is filed, and the other sets the periods of the target's statements, as described on [Financial statements of businesses acquired or to be acquired](mna-sx-acquired-business-financial-statements.md).
