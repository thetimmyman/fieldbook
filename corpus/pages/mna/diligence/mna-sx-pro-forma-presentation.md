## Purpose

Shows when a registrant must file pro forma financial information about an acquisition or disposition and what makes a business acquisition significant. How pro forma financial information is prepared is on [Pro forma financial information: how it is prepared](mna-sx-pro-forma-preparation.md).

| Applies to | A filing in which a listed condition exists, such as a significant acquisition or disposition [@CL-MNA-0682; @CL-MNA-0683; @CL-MNA-0686] |

Under § 210.11-01(a), pro forma financial information must be filed when any of the conditions in paragraph (a) exist [@CL-MNA-0682]. Those conditions include: (1) during the most recent fiscal year or subsequent interim period for which a balance sheet is required by § 210.3-01, a significant business acquisition has occurred (for purposes of the section, this encompasses the acquisition of an interest in a business accounted for by the equity method) [@CL-MNA-0683], (2) after the date of the most recent balance sheet filed pursuant to § 210.3-01, consummation of a significant business acquisition or a combination of entities under common control has occurred or is probable [@CL-MNA-0684], (3) securities being registered by the registrant are to be offered to the security holders of a significant business to be acquired or the proceeds from the offered securities will be applied directly or indirectly to the purchase of a specific significant business [@CL-MNA-0685], and (4) the disposition of a significant portion of a business either by sale, abandonment or distribution to shareholders by means of a spin-off, split-up or split-off has occurred or is probable and such disposition is not fully reflected in the financial statements of the registrant included in the filing [@CL-MNA-0686].

Under § 210.11-01(b)(1), a business acquisition or disposition will be considered significant if the business acquisition meets: (i) the definition of a significant subsidiary in § 210.1-02(w)(1), substituting 20 percent for 10 percent each place it appears therein [@CL-MNA-0687], or (ii) if the business is a real estate operation as defined in § 210.3-14(a)(2), the significant subsidiary condition in § 210.1-02(w)(1)(i) (that is, the investment test condition), substituting 20 percent for 10 percent, as modified by the guidance in § 210.3-14(b)(2)(ii) [@CL-MNA-0136]. Under § 210.11-01(c), the pro forma effects of a business acquisition need not be presented pursuant to the section if separate financial statements of the acquired business are not included in the filing, except where the aggregate impact of businesses acquired or to be acquired is significant as determined by § 210.3-05(b)(2)(iv) or § 210.3-14(b)(2)(i)(C) [@CL-MNA-0688; @CL-MNA-0772].

Under § 210.8-01(a), §§ 210.8-01 through 210.8-08 (Article 8) are applicable to financial statements filed for smaller reporting companies and are not applicable to financial statements prepared for the purposes of Item 17 or Item 18 of Form 20-F [@CL-MNA-0791]. Within Article 8, § 210.8-05(a) provides that pro forma financial information must be disclosed when any of the conditions in § 210.11-01 exist [@CL-MNA-1338], and § 210.8-05(b) provides that the preparation, presentation, and disclosure of pro forma financial information must comply with §§ 210.11-01 through 210.11-03 (Article 11), except that the pro forma financial information may be condensed pursuant to § 210.8-03(a) [@CL-MNA-1339].

## How it works

Under § 210.11-01(a), pro forma financial information must be filed when any of the conditions in paragraph (a) exist [@CL-MNA-0682; @CL-MNA-0683; @CL-MNA-0684; @CL-MNA-0685; @CL-MNA-0686]. Under paragraph (b)(1), a business acquisition is considered significant if it meets the definition of a significant subsidiary in § 210.1-02(w)(1), substituting 20 percent for 10 percent each place it appears therein, or, if the business is a real estate operation as defined in § 210.3-14(a)(2), the investment test condition in § 210.1-02(w)(1)(i), substituting 20 percent for 10 percent, as modified by § 210.3-14(b)(2)(ii) [@CL-MNA-0687; @CL-MNA-0136]. Under paragraph (c), the pro forma effects of a business acquisition need not be presented pursuant to the section if separate financial statements of the acquired business are not included in the filing, except where the aggregate impact of businesses acquired or to be acquired is significant as determined by § 210.3-05(b)(2)(iv) or § 210.3-14(b)(2)(i)(C) [@CL-MNA-0688; @CL-MNA-0772].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A registrant's filing includes a balance sheet as of its latest quarter end. After that date it agrees to buy a business, and the closing is probable. The target measures 25 percent on a significance test, against a line of 20 percent. The acquisition is significant, so pro forma financial information is filed. Had the target measured 15 percent, the acquisition would not have met this test.

## Never confuse

Under § 210.11-01(b)(1), a business acquisition is considered significant for pro forma financial information if it meets the significant subsidiary definition in § 210.1-02(w)(1) substituting 20 percent for 10 percent (or for a real estate operation, the investment test in § 210.1-02(w)(1)(i) substituting 20 percent for 10 percent as modified by § 210.3-14(b)(2)(ii)) [@CL-MNA-0687; @CL-MNA-0136], whereas under § 210.3-05(b)(2), the 20 percent and 40 percent thresholds determine the periods for which separate financial statements of the business acquired or to be acquired must be filed [@CL-MNA-0676; @CL-MNA-0677; @CL-MNA-0678]; see [Financial statements of businesses acquired or to be acquired](mna-sx-acquired-business-financial-statements.md).
