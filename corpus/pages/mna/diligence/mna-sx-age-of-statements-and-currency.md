## Purpose

Shows how old the latest financial statements in a filing may be when the filing becomes effective, and which currency the statements are stated in. The rules are part of Regulation S-X, which is not FAR, DFARS or CAS. The age rule for smaller reporting companies is on [Smaller reporting company interim statements and age of statements](mna-sx-smaller-company-interim-statements-and-age.md), and the condensed form of the interim statements is on [Interim financial statements: condensed form, periods and review](mna-sx-interim-financial-statements.md).

| Applies to | Financial statements in a filing that is expected to become effective, or in a proxy statement that is to be mailed [@CL-MNA-0697] |

Financial statements that are as of a date the specified number of days or more before the expected effective date, or before the proposed mailing date of a proxy statement, shall be updated, except as specified in the following paragraphs [@CL-MNA-0697]. The number of days is 130 for large accelerated filers and accelerated filers (as defined in § 240.12b-2) and 135 for all other registrants [@CL-MNA-0698].

A separate, conditional limit applies to audited statements. The most recent audited financial statements in a registration statement under the Securities Act or on Form 10 under the Exchange Act shall not be more than one year and 45 days old at the date the registration statement becomes effective [@CL-MNA-0699]. That limit applies if the registration statement relates to the security of an issuer that was not subject, immediately before the time of filing, to the reporting requirements of section 13 or 15(d) of the Exchange Act [@CL-MNA-0700].

A foreign private issuer shall state amounts in its primary financial statements in the currency it deems appropriate, while an issuer that is not a foreign private issuer shall present its financial statements in U.S. dollars [@CL-MNA-0701]. The issuer shall state its primary financial statements in the same currency for all periods for which financial information is presented [@CL-MNA-0702]. The section also sets a second set of day counts for filings near a fiscal year end, a rule for a filing made near the end of a fiscal year, and further currency rules that this card does not draw out.

## How it works

The registrant compares the date of its latest statements with the expected effective date or proxy mailing date [@CL-MNA-0697]. If the gap reaches the day count for its filer type, the statements are updated, subject to the exceptions in the section [@CL-MNA-0697; @CL-MNA-0698]. An issuer that was not subject to the reporting requirements immediately before filing also checks the age of its audited statements against the separate limit [@CL-MNA-0699; @CL-MNA-0700]. The currency follows the issuer type and stays the same across all periods shown [@CL-MNA-0701; @CL-MNA-0702].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A registrant that is neither a large accelerated filer nor an accelerated filer has statements as of a date 140 days before the expected effective date. The line for such a registrant is 135 days, so the statements are out of date and an interim update is needed. Separately, a company files a Securities Act registration statement and was not subject to the reporting requirements immediately before filing. Its audited statements are 14 months old at effectiveness, so the one year and 45 days limit applies and the audited statements are too old.

## Never confuse

The update line, counted in days since the statement date, is not the one-year-and-45-day limit on audited statements of an issuer not previously subject to the reporting requirements [@CL-MNA-0698; @CL-MNA-0699; @CL-MNA-0700]. The first requires the statements to be updated, and the second caps the age of the audited statements themselves [@CL-MNA-0697; @CL-MNA-0699].
