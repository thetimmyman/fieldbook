## Purpose

Shows when a cleared entity is under foreign ownership, control or influence, what the cognizant security agency weighs, and which measures can keep access to classified information.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Not established. |
| Codified citation | Not established. |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Not established. |
| Applies to | U.S. entities in process for an entity eligibility determination, and entities that already hold one [@CL-MNA-0527; @CL-MNA-0528] |

This card rests on the NISPOM, 32 CFR 117.11, a DoD rule for classified work, and not on the FAR [@CL-MNA-0525]. The paragraph on when the CSA considers a U.S. entity to be under FOCI lists items. The first item is that a foreign interest has the power to direct or decide issues affecting the entity's management or operations in a manner that could result in unauthorized access to classified information or adversely affect performance of a classified contract or agreement [@CL-MNA-0525]. The second item is that the foreign government is currently exercising, or could prospectively exercise, that power directly or indirectly, such as through ownership of the entity's securities, by contractual arrangements or by other means, or by the ability to control or influence the election or appointment of one or more members to the entity's governing board [@CL-MNA-0526; @CL-MNA-1567].

A U.S. entity that is in process for an entity eligibility determination and is subsequently determined to be under FOCI is ineligible for access to classified information unless and until effective security measures have been put in place to negate or mitigate FOCI to the satisfaction of the CSA [@CL-MNA-0527; @CL-MNA-0555]. An existing determination will be invalidated if the contractor is unable or unwilling to negotiate and implement an acceptable mitigation or negation measure [@CL-MNA-0528].

## How it works

Factors relating to the entity, relevant foreign interests, and the government of such foreign interests, as appropriate, are considered in the aggregate to decide whether an applicant entity is under FOCI, its eligibility for access to classified information, and the protective measures required [@CL-MNA-0529]. The entity completes an SF 328 during the process for an entity eligibility determination or when significant changes occur to information previously submitted [@CL-MNA-0530]. The SF 328, Certificate Pertaining to Foreign Interest, in § 117.8 and § 117.11 is assigned Office of Management and Budget Control Number 0704-0579 [@CL-MNA-1387]. An entity with a favorable determination that enters negotiations for a merger, acquisition or takeover by a foreign interest notifies the CSA that the negotiations have started [@CL-MNA-0531], and the notification names the type of transaction, the potential foreign investor and a mitigation plan [@CL-MNA-0532]. Among the measures, a security control agreement may be adequate where the foreign interest does not effectively own or control the entity but is entitled to board representation [@CL-MNA-0533], an SSA may be adequate where a foreign interest effectively owns or controls it [@CL-MNA-0534], and a voting trust or proxy agreement vests the foreign-owned voting rights in cleared U.S. citizens approved by the USG [@CL-MNA-0535].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A cleared contractor's owner agrees in principle to sell a majority stake to a foreign investor. The contractor tells its CSA when talks start, naming the type of transaction, the investor and a plan to mitigate FOCI.

Answer: because the investor would effectively own the contractor, an SSA is the kind of arrangement that may be adequate. If the investor took only a board seat and the contractor stayed under U.S. control, a security control agreement may be adequate instead. If the contractor were unable or unwilling to negotiate and implement an acceptable measure, its existing determination would be invalidated.

## Never confuse

FOCI is a rule about access to classified information. It is not the ownership representation an offeror makes in a FAR provision, which is on the [Ownership or Control of Offeror provision](../../supply-chain/supplier-identity/sup-ownership-or-control-of-offeror.md) card, and it is not a cyber rule; the cyber side is on the [Safeguarding covered defense information](../../compliance/trade-cyber/cmp-safeguarding-covered-defense-information.md) card. For an entity in process, the bar on access lasts only until effective security measures are in place [@CL-MNA-0527]. The FAR definitions section defines a classified contract as any contract in which the contractor or its employees must have access to classified information during contract performance, and a contract may be a classified contract even though the contract document itself is unclassified [@CL-MNA-1461]. The first sentence of the RFO model text definition of a classified contract in FAR 2.101 reads the same as the codified first sentence [@CL-MNA-1467; @CL-MNA-1461], and the RFO text goes on to say that a contract may be a classified contract even though the contract document itself is unclassified [@CL-MNA-1467; @CL-MNA-1519]. That is the FAR wording, and the FOCI rule on this card rests on the NISPOM and not on the FAR [@CL-MNA-0525].

## Company calibration

Whether the company holds or seeks access to classified information, which foreign interests hold or could hold an interest or a board seat, and who may see classified-contract finance data. [@Q-045]
