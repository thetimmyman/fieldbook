## Purpose

Shows when a renamed contractor needs an agreement with the Government and what it must send to the responsible contracting officer.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-MNA-0364] |
| Codified citation | FAR 42.1205 [@CL-MNA-0364] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 42.904 [@CL-MNA-1003; @CL-MNA-1004] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-MNA-0364] |
| Applies to | Contractors whose only change is the name, with the rights and obligations of both parties unaffected [@CL-MNA-0364] |

If only a change of the contractor's name is involved and the rights and obligations of the Government and the contractor remain unaffected, the parties must execute an agreement to reflect the name change [@CL-MNA-0364].

The FAR's general definitions say a change-of-name agreement is a legal instrument executed by the contractor and the Government that recognizes the legal change of name of the contractor without disturbing the original contractual rights and obligations of the parties [@CL-MNA-1401]. RFO FAR 2.101 defines a change-of-name agreement as a legal instrument executed by the contractor and the Government that recognizes the legal change of name of the contractor without disturbing the original contractual rights and obligations of the parties [@CL-MNA-1466].

The contractor must forward three signed copies of the Change-of-Name Agreement to the responsible contracting officer, with one copy each of the listed documents [@CL-MNA-0365]. The first is the document effecting the name change, authenticated by a proper official of the State having jurisdiction [@CL-MNA-0365]. The second is the opinion of the contractor's legal counsel stating that the change of name was properly effected under applicable law and showing the effective date [@CL-MNA-0365; @CL-MNA-0366]. The third is a list of all affected contracts and purchase orders remaining unsettled between the contractor and the Government, showing for each the contract number and type, and name and address of the contracting office [@CL-MNA-0365; @CL-MNA-0367]. The contracting officer may request the total dollar value as amended and the remaining unpaid balance for each contract [@CL-MNA-0099].

## How it works

If only a change of the contractor's name is involved and the Government's and contractor's rights and obligations remain unaffected, the parties must execute an agreement to reflect the name change [@CL-MNA-0364]. For each unsettled contract or purchase order on the list forwarded to the responsible contracting officer, the list shows the contract number and type and the name and address of the contracting office, and the contracting officer may request the total dollar value as amended and the remaining unpaid balance for each contract [@CL-MNA-0365; @CL-MNA-0367; @CL-MNA-0099]. Who the responsible contracting officer is, the written request, the information request and the legal review before signing are set out on [Novation agreements: who executes them and how they are processed](../deal-gates/mna-novation-processing.md).

Effective March 16, 2026, DoD contracting officers must use the revised FAR Part 42 in place of FAR part 42 under Class Deviation 2026-O0050 [@CL-CMP-9759; @CL-CMP-9761]. RFO FAR 42.904 says that if only a change of the contractor's name is involved and the Government's and contractor's rights and obligations remain unaffected, the parties must execute an agreement to reflect the name change [@CL-MNA-1003]. RFO FAR 42.904 says the contractor must forward to the responsible contracting officer three signed copies of the Change-of-Name Agreement and one copy each of the document effecting the name change, authenticated by a proper official of the State having jurisdiction [@CL-MNA-1004], the opinion of the contractor's legal counsel stating that the change of name was properly effected under applicable law and showing the effective date [@CL-MNA-1005], and a list of all affected contracts and purchase orders remaining unsettled between the contractor and the Government, showing for each the contract number and type and the name and address of the contracting office [@CL-MNA-1006]. The contracting officer may request the total dollar value as amended and the remaining unpaid balance for each contract [@CL-MNA-1010].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor amends its certificate of incorporation to take a new corporate name. Nothing else changes, and it still holds the same contracts. It sends three signed copies of the agreement, the State-authenticated document, its counsel's opinion and a list of its unsettled contracts, each with number, type and contracting office. This is a name change only, so the change-of-name route applies.

## Never confuse

If only a change of the contractor's name is involved and the Government's and contractor's rights and obligations remain unaffected, the parties must execute an agreement to reflect the name change [@CL-MNA-0364]. Under FAR 42.1204(a), the Government may, when in its interest, recognize a third party as the successor in interest to a Government contract when the third party's interest in the contract arises out of the transfer of all the contractor's assets or the entire portion of the assets involved in performing the contract [@CL-MNA-0102; @CL-MNA-0103]; see [Novation and change-of-name agreements](mna-novation-agreements.md). Under FAR 52.204-20(a), the term "successor" does not include new offices or divisions of the same company or a company that only changes its name [@CL-MNA-0387].

## Company calibration

Whether any target or its subsidiaries have recently changed name, and where the authenticated name-change documents and counsel's opinion are kept. [@Q-028]
