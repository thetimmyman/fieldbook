## Purpose

Explains when the Government may recognize a successor in interest after a transfer of assets, what the contractor must file, and why a stock purchase or a change of name is handled differently.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-MNA-0101] |
| Codified citation | FAR 42.1200 [@CL-MNA-0473; @CL-MNA-0474]; FAR 42.1204 [@CL-MNA-0101] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 42.900 [@CL-MNA-0471; @CL-MNA-0472]; RFO FAR 42.903 [@CL-MNA-0988; @CL-MNA-0989]; RFO FAR 2.101 [@CL-MNA-1471; @CL-MNA-1472] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-MNA-0101] |
| Applies to | Transfers of all the contractor's assets or of the entire portion of the assets involved in performing the contract [@CL-MNA-0103] |

FAR 42.1200 sets the scope of the subpart: it prescribes policies and procedures for (a) recognition of a successor in interest to Government contracts when contractor assets are transferred [@CL-MNA-0473], (b) recognition of a change in a contractor's name, and (c) execution of novation agreements and change-of-name agreements by the responsible contracting officer [@CL-MNA-0474].

A statute prohibits transferring a Government contract from the contractor to a third party, but the Government may, when in its interest, recognize a third party as the successor in interest when that party's interest arises out of the transfer of all the contractor's assets or of the entire portion of the assets involved in performing the contract [@CL-MNA-0101; @CL-MNA-0102; @CL-MNA-0103].

The recognition rule applies to a transfer of all the contractor's assets or of the entire portion of the assets involved in performing the contract [@CL-MNA-0103]. Examples of such transactions include, but are not limited to, a sale of these assets with a provision for assuming liabilities, a transfer of these assets incident to a merger or corporate consolidation, and incorporation of a proprietorship or partnership, or formation of a partnership [@CL-MNA-0104; @CL-MNA-0113]. A novation agreement is unnecessary when there is a change in the ownership of a contractor as a result of a stock purchase, with no legal change in the contracting party, and when that contracting party remains in control of the assets and is the party performing the contract [@CL-MNA-0105]. However, whether there is a purchase of assets or a stock purchase, there may be issues related to the change in ownership that appropriately should be addressed in a formal agreement between the contractor and the Government (see 42.1203(e)) [@CL-MNA-1524]. When it is in the Government's interest not to concur in the transfer of a contract from one company to another company, the original contractor remains under contractual obligation to the Government, and the contract may be terminated for default should the original contractor not perform [@CL-MNA-0115; @CL-MNA-0111].

FAR 2.101 defines a novation agreement as a legal instrument executed by the contractor (transferor), the successor in interest (transferee) and the Government [@CL-MNA-1409], by which, among other things, the transferor guarantees performance of the contract, the transferee assumes all obligations under the contract, and the Government recognizes the transfer of the contract and related assets [@CL-MNA-1410]. RFO FAR 2.101 defines a novation agreement as a legal instrument executed by the contractor (transferor), the successor in interest (transferee) and the Government, by which, among other things, the transferor guarantees performance of the contract, the transferee assumes all obligations under the contract, and the Government recognizes the transfer of the contract and related assets [@CL-MNA-1471; @CL-MNA-1472].

## How it works

When considering whether to recognize a third party as a successor in interest to Government contracts, the responsible contracting officer must identify and evaluate any significant organizational conflicts of interest in accordance with subpart 9.5 [@CL-MNA-1568; @CL-MNA-0106]. If the responsible contracting officer determines that a conflict of interest cannot be resolved, but that it is in the best interest of the Government to approve the novation request, a request for a waiver may be submitted in accordance with the procedures at 9.503 [@CL-MNA-1569; @CL-MNA-0107].

When a contractor asks the Government to recognize a successor in interest, the contractor must submit to the responsible contracting officer three signed copies of the proposed novation agreement [@CL-MNA-1578; @CL-MNA-0108] and one copy each, as applicable, of the listed documents [@CL-MNA-1570]. They are the document describing the proposed transaction (for example, purchase/sale agreement or memorandum of understanding) and a list of all affected contracts between the transferor and the Government, as of the date of sale or transfer of assets, showing for each, as of that date, the contract number and type, the name and address of the contracting office, the total dollar value as amended, and the approximate remaining unpaid balance [@CL-MNA-1571; @CL-MNA-1572]. They also include evidence of the transferee's capability to perform [@CL-MNA-1572] and any other relevant information requested by the responsible contracting officer [@CL-MNA-1573].

Except as provided in paragraph (g) of FAR 42.1204, the contractor must submit to the responsible contracting officer one copy of each of the further listed documents, as applicable, as the documents become available [@CL-MNA-1574], including the opinion of legal counsel for the transferor and transferee stating that the transfer was properly effected under applicable law and the effective date of transfer [@CL-MNA-0109]. If the Government has acquired the documents during its participation in the pre-merger or pre-acquisition review process, or the Government's interests are adequately protected with an alternative formulation of the information [@CL-MNA-1575], the responsible contracting officer may modify the list of documents to be submitted by the contractor [@CL-MNA-1576].

When recognizing a successor in interest to a Government contract is consistent with the Government's interest, the responsible contracting officer must execute a novation agreement with the transferor and the transferee, and it must ordinarily provide in part that (1) the transferee assumes all the transferor's obligations under the contract, (2) the transferor waives all rights under the contract against the Government, (3) the transferor guarantees performance of the contract by the transferee (a satisfactory performance bond may be accepted instead of the guarantee), and (4) nothing in the agreement shall relieve the transferor or transferee from compliance with any Federal law [@CL-MNA-1577; @CL-MNA-0110; @CL-MNA-0114].

Effective March 16, 2026, DoD contracting officers must use the revised FAR Part 42 in place of FAR part 42 under Class Deviation 2026-O0050 [@CL-CMP-9759; @CL-CMP-9761]. RFO FAR 42.900 says the subpart prescribes policies and procedures for (a) recognition of a successor in interest to Government contracts when contractor assets are transferred [@CL-MNA-0471], (b) recognition of a change in a contractor's name, and (c) execution of novation agreements and change-of-name agreements by the responsible contracting officer [@CL-MNA-0472]. RFO FAR 42.903 says section 6305 of title 41, United States Code, prohibits transfer of Government contracts from the contractor to a third party, and the Government may, when in its interest, recognize a third party as the successor in interest to a Government contract when the third party's interest in the contract arises out of the transfer of all the contractor's assets or the entire portion of the assets involved in performing the contract [@CL-MNA-0988; @CL-MNA-0989], with examples of such transactions including, but not limited to, sale of these assets with a provision for assuming liabilities, transfer of these assets incident to a merger or corporate consolidation, and incorporation of a proprietorship or partnership, or formation of a partnership [@CL-MNA-1007]. RFO FAR 42.903 says a novation agreement is unnecessary when there is a change in the ownership of a contractor as a result of a stock purchase, with no legal change in the contracting party, and when that contracting party remains in control of the assets and is the party performing the contract [@CL-MNA-0990], while adding that, whether there is a purchase of assets or a stock purchase, there may be issues related to the change in ownership that appropriately should be addressed in a formal agreement between the contractor and the Government (see 42.902(e)) [@CL-MNA-1008]. It also says that when it is in the Government's interest not to concur in the transfer of a contract from one company to another company, the original contractor remains under contractual obligation to the Government, and the contract may be terminated for default should the original contractor not perform [@CL-MNA-0991]. It says that when a contractor asks the Government to recognize a successor in interest, the contractor must submit to the responsible contracting officer three signed copies of the proposed novation agreement and one copy each, as applicable, of the document describing the proposed transaction (for example, purchase or sale agreement or memorandum of understanding), a list of all affected contracts between the transferor and the Government as of the date of sale or transfer of assets showing for each as of that date the contract number and type, the name and address of the contracting office, the total dollar value as amended, and the approximate remaining unpaid balance, evidence of the transferee's capability to perform, and any other relevant information requested by the responsible contracting officer [@CL-MNA-0994; @CL-MNA-1011; @CL-MNA-0995; @CL-MNA-0996; @CL-MNA-1012]. It says that, except as provided in paragraph (g) of RFO 42.903, the contractor must submit to the responsible contracting officer one copy of each of the further listed documents, as applicable, as the documents become available [@CL-MNA-0997], including the opinion of legal counsel for the transferor and transferee stating that the transfer was properly effected under applicable law and the effective date of transfer [@CL-MNA-0998]. It says that if the Government has acquired the documents during its participation in the pre-merger or pre-acquisition review process, or the Government's interests are adequately protected with an alternative formulation of the information, the responsible contracting officer may modify the list of documents to be submitted by the contractor [@CL-MNA-0999; @CL-MNA-1000]. It says that when recognizing a successor in interest to a Government contract is consistent with the Government's interest, the responsible contracting officer must execute a novation agreement with the transferor and the transferee [@CL-MNA-1001], and that the agreement must ordinarily provide in part that (1) the transferee assumes all the transferor's obligations under the contract, (2) the transferor waives all rights under the contract against the Government [@CL-MNA-1002], (3) the transferor guarantees performance of the contract by the transferee (a satisfactory performance bond may be accepted instead of the guarantee), and (4) nothing in the agreement will relieve the transferor or transferee from compliance with any Federal law [@CL-MNA-1009]. Under RFO FAR 42.903(d), when considering whether to recognize a third party as a successor in interest to Government contracts, the responsible contracting officer must identify and evaluate any significant organizational conflicts of interest in accordance with part 9, and if the responsible contracting officer determines that a conflict of interest cannot be resolved, but that it is in the Government's interest to approve the novation request, a request for a waiver may be submitted in accordance with the procedures at part 9 [@CL-MNA-0992; @CL-MNA-0993].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A buyer acquires all the assets of a division that performs two cost-reimbursement contracts with a combined value of 4,000,000 and an unpaid balance of 900,000, and assumes the related liabilities. Because the transfer covers all the assets used to perform those contracts, the Government may recognize the buyer as successor in interest if that is in its interest, and the seller as contractor submits three signed copies of the proposed agreement with a list showing each contract's number, type, value and unpaid balance. A stock purchase of the seller, leaving the contracting party unchanged and in control of the assets, would need no novation agreement.

## Never confuse

A novation agreement is unnecessary when there is a change in the ownership of a contractor as a result of a stock purchase, with no legal change in the contracting party, and when that contracting party remains in control of the assets and is the party performing the contract [@CL-MNA-0105], although whether there is a purchase of assets or a stock purchase, there may be issues related to the change in ownership that appropriately should be addressed in a formal agreement between the contractor and the Government (see 42.1203(e)) [@CL-MNA-1524]. Under FAR 42.1205(a), if only a change of the contractor's name is involved and the Government's and contractor's rights and obligations remain unaffected, the parties must execute an agreement to reflect the name change [@CL-MNA-0364].

When it is in the Government's interest not to concur in the transfer of a contract from one company to another company, the original contractor remains under contractual obligation to the Government, and the contract may be terminated for default should the original contractor not perform [@CL-MNA-0115; @CL-MNA-0111].

If the responsible contracting officer determines that a conflict of interest cannot be resolved, FAR 42.1204(d) allows a request for a waiver to be submitted in accordance with the procedures at 9.503 when it is in the best interest of the Government to approve the novation request [@CL-MNA-1569; @CL-MNA-0107], whereas RFO FAR 42.903(d) allows a request for a waiver to be submitted in accordance with the procedures at part 9 when it is in the Government's interest to approve the novation request [@CL-MNA-0993].
