## Purpose

Explains when the Government may recognize a successor in interest after a transfer of assets, what the contractor must file, and why a stock purchase or a change of name is handled differently.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-MNA-0101] |
| Codified citation | FAR 42.1204 [@CL-MNA-0101] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 42.903 [@CL-MNA-0988; @CL-MNA-0989] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-MNA-0101] |
| Applies to | Transfers of all the contractor's assets or of the entire portion of the assets involved in performing the contract [@CL-MNA-0103] |

A statute prohibits transferring a Government contract from the contractor to a third party, but the Government may, when in its interest, recognize a third party as the successor in interest when that party's interest arises out of the transfer of all the contractor's assets or of the entire portion of the assets involved in performing the contract [@CL-MNA-0101; @CL-MNA-0102; @CL-MNA-0103].

The transfer must be of all the contractor's assets or of the entire portion of the assets involved in performing the contract, and a sale of the assets with a provision for assuming liabilities or a transfer incident to a merger or corporate consolidation are examples [@CL-MNA-0103; @CL-MNA-0104]. A stock purchase that changes ownership without a legal change in the contracting party, where that party stays in control of the assets and performs the contract, needs no novation agreement [@CL-MNA-0105].

## How it works

The responsible contracting officer identifies and evaluates any significant organizational conflicts of interest under the conflict of interest rules before recognizing a successor, and may seek a waiver where a conflict cannot be resolved but approval serves the Government's interest [@CL-MNA-0106; @CL-MNA-0107].

When a contractor asks the Government to recognize a successor in interest, it submits three signed copies of the proposed agreement to the responsible contracting officer [@CL-MNA-0108], together with one copy each, as applicable, of the transaction document, a list of the affected contracts with their numbers, types, values and unpaid balances, and evidence of the transferee's capability to perform.

Further documents are submitted, as applicable, as they become available, unless the contracting officer modifies the list under paragraph (g), including the opinion of legal counsel for the transferor and transferee that the transfer was properly effected under applicable law and its effective date [@CL-MNA-0109].

The executed agreement ordinarily provides that the transferee assumes all the transferor's obligations under the contract and that the transferor waives all rights under the contract against the Government [@CL-MNA-0110].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 42 text in place of the codified text because Class Deviation 2026-O0050 directs it, effective March 16, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9759] [@CL-CMP-9761]. RFO FAR 42.903 says the Government may, when in its interest, recognize a third party as the successor in interest when the third party's interest in the contract arises out of the transfer of all the contractor's assets or the entire portion of the assets involved in performing the contract [@CL-MNA-0988; @CL-MNA-0989; @CL-MNA-0102; @CL-MNA-0103], and that examples of such transfers of assets include, but are not limited to, a sale of the assets with a provision for assuming liabilities, a transfer incident to a merger or corporate consolidation, and incorporation of a proprietorship or partnership or formation of a partnership [@CL-MNA-1007]. It says a novation agreement is unnecessary when ownership changes through a stock purchase with no legal change in the contracting party, where that party remains in control of the assets and is the party performing the contract [@CL-MNA-0990; @CL-MNA-0105], while adding that, whether there is a purchase of assets or a stock purchase, there may be issues related to the change in ownership that appropriately should be addressed in a formal agreement between the contractor and the Government, pointing to RFO 42.902(e) [@CL-MNA-1008]. It also says that when it is in the Government's interest not to concur in the transfer of a contract from one company to another company, the original contractor remains under contractual obligation to the Government and the contract may be terminated for default should the original contractor not perform [@CL-MNA-0991; @CL-MNA-0111]. It says that when a contractor asks the Government to recognize a successor in interest, the contractor must submit to the responsible contracting officer three signed copies of the proposed novation agreement and one copy each, as applicable, of the listed documents [@CL-MNA-0994; @CL-MNA-0108]: the document describing the proposed transaction [@CL-MNA-1011]; a list of all affected contracts between the transferor and the Government, as of the date of sale or transfer of assets, showing for each, as of that date, the contract number and type, the name and address of the contracting office, the total dollar value as amended and the approximate remaining unpaid balance [@CL-MNA-0995; @CL-MNA-0996]; and evidence of the transferee's capability to perform and any other relevant information requested by the responsible contracting officer [@CL-MNA-1012]. It says that, except as provided in paragraph (g) of RFO 42.903, the contractor must also submit to the responsible contracting officer one copy of each of the further listed documents, as applicable, as they become available [@CL-MNA-0997], including the opinion of legal counsel for the transferor and transferee stating that the transfer was properly effected under applicable law and the effective date of transfer [@CL-MNA-0998; @CL-MNA-0109]. It says that if the Government has acquired the documents during its participation in the pre-merger or pre-acquisition review process, or its interests are adequately protected with an alternative formulation of the information, the responsible contracting officer may modify the list of documents to be submitted by the contractor [@CL-MNA-0999; @CL-MNA-1000]. It says that when recognizing a successor in interest is consistent with the Government's interest, the responsible contracting officer must execute a novation agreement with the transferor and the transferee [@CL-MNA-1001], and that the agreement must ordinarily provide in part that the transferee assumes all the transferor's obligations under the contract and that the transferor waives all rights under the contract against the Government [@CL-MNA-1002; @CL-MNA-0110], that the transferor guarantees performance of the contract by the transferee (a satisfactory performance bond may be accepted instead of the guarantee), and that nothing in the agreement will relieve the transferor or transferee from compliance with any Federal law [@CL-MNA-1009]. On conflicts of interest, the RFO text says the officer must identify and evaluate any significant organizational conflicts of interest in accordance with part 9, where the codified text names subpart 9.5 [@CL-MNA-0992; @CL-MNA-0106], and says a waiver request may be submitted in accordance with part 9 if a conflict cannot be resolved but approving the novation request is in the Government's interest, where the codified text says it is in the best interest of the Government and points to 9.503 [@CL-MNA-0993; @CL-MNA-0107].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A buyer acquires all the assets of a division that performs two cost-reimbursement contracts with a combined value of 4,000,000 and an unpaid balance of 900,000, and assumes the related liabilities. Because the transfer covers all the assets used to perform those contracts, the Government may recognize the buyer as successor in interest if that is in its interest, and the seller as contractor submits three signed copies of the proposed agreement with a list showing each contract's number, type, value and unpaid balance. A stock purchase of the seller, leaving the contracting party unchanged and in control of the assets, would need no novation agreement.

## Never confuse

A novation agreement addresses a change in the party that performs the contract, while a stock purchase that leaves the contracting party in place and in control of the assets needs none [@CL-MNA-0105]. A change of name is different again: where only the contractor's name changes and the rights and obligations of both parties are unaffected, the parties execute an agreement to reflect the name change.

Recognition is not automatic, and when the Government does not concur the original contractor stays contractually obligated and may face termination for default if it does not perform [@CL-MNA-0111].

For a conflict of interest that cannot be resolved, the codified text allows a waiver request where approving the novation is in the best interest of the Government [@CL-MNA-0107], while the RFO text says approving the novation request is in the Government's interest [@CL-MNA-0993].
