## Purpose

Explains how the CFAO asks for, receives and evaluates general dollar magnitude (GDM) and detailed cost-impact (DCI) proposals for a change in cost accounting practice, and what follows if the contractor does not submit them.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-RTE-0718] |
| Codified citation | FAR 30.604 [@CL-RTE-0718] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-RTE-0718] |
| Applies to | Required, unilateral and desirable changes in cost accounting practices [@CL-RTE-0718] |

If the description of a change in cost accounting practice is both adequate and compliant, the CFAO must notify the contractor in writing [@CL-RTE-0715]. For required or unilateral changes, except those requested to be determined desirable changes, the CFAO requests that the contractor submit a GDM proposal by a specified date unless the CFAO determines the cost impact is immaterial [@CL-RTE-0716].

A contractor may submit a DCI proposal in lieu of a GDM proposal provided the DCI proposal is in accordance with the section's requirements for detailed cost-impact proposals [@CL-RTE-0612]. The CFAO may request a DCI proposal by a specified date if the CFAO determines that the GDM proposal is not sufficient to resolve the cost impact [@CL-RTE-0717].

## How it works

A GDM proposal provides information to the CFAO on the estimated overall impact of the change on affected CAS-covered contracts and subcontracts that were awarded based on the previous cost accounting practice [@CL-RTE-0713]. A DCI proposal must show the estimated increase or decrease in cost accumulations for each affected CAS-covered contract and subcontract, unless the CFAO and contractor agree otherwise as the paragraph describes [@CL-RTE-0714]. The cost impact calculation must include all affected CAS-covered contracts and subcontracts regardless of their open or closed status and whether or not the final indirect rates have been established [@CL-RTE-0613].

For unilateral changes, the calculation determines the increased or decreased cost to the Government for flexibly-priced contracts and subcontracts [@CL-RTE-0719] and for fixed-price contracts and subcontracts [@CL-RTE-0720]. For flexibly-priced contracts and subcontracts, the difference is an increased cost to the Government when the estimated cost to complete using the changed practice exceeds the estimated cost to complete using the current practice [@CL-RTE-0614]. For fixed-price contracts and subcontracts, the difference is an increased cost to the Government when the estimated cost to complete using the changed practice is less than the estimated cost to complete using the current practice [@CL-RTE-0615].

If the contractor does not submit the required description or proposals within the specified time or any extension granted by the CFAO, the CFAO must estimate the general dollar magnitude of the cost impact [@CL-RTE-0721]. The CFAO then takes one or both of two listed actions [@CL-RTE-0616]. The first is to withhold an amount not to exceed 10 percent of each subsequent payment related to the contractor's CAS-covered contracts, up to the estimated general dollar magnitude of the cost impact, until the contractor furnishes the required information [@CL-RTE-0616]. The second is to issue a final decision in accordance with section 33.211 and unilaterally adjust the contracts by the estimated amount of the cost impact [@CL-RTE-0722].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor makes a unilateral change and submits a GDM proposal covering its affected CAS-covered contracts, open and closed. Under the changed practice the estimated cost to complete rises by 50,000 on flexibly-priced contracts and falls by 30,000 on fixed-price contracts. Under the section's tests both differences are increased cost to the Government, so the increased cost is 80,000, before any effect on incentives, fees and profits. Had the contractor missed the date the CFAO set, the CFAO could have withheld up to 10 percent of each later payment on the covered contracts, up to the estimated general dollar magnitude, until the information arrived.

## Never confuse

A GDM proposal gives the CFAO an estimate of the overall impact of a change [@CL-RTE-0713]. A DCI proposal shows the estimated impact for each affected contract, unless the CFAO and contractor agree otherwise [@CL-RTE-0714]. For required or unilateral changes the CFAO asks for the GDM proposal first [@CL-RTE-0716] and asks for a DCI proposal only if the GDM proposal is not sufficient [@CL-RTE-0717]. For materiality, see [CAS materiality](../../compliance/cas-allowability/cmp-cas-materiality.md). For how the Defense Contract Audit Agency tests cost impact proposals, see [DCAA audits that test Cost Accounting Standards compliance](../../compliance/cas-allowability/cmp-dcaa-cas-audits.md).
