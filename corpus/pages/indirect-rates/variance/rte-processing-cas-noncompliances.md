## Purpose

Covers the notice steps, time limits and interest rule when the auditor reports an alleged noncompliance with Cost Accounting Standards to the CFAO.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-RTE-0617] |
| Codified citation | FAR 30.605 [@CL-RTE-0617] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-RTE-0617] |
| Applies to | Reports of alleged noncompliance that the CFAO receives from the auditor [@CL-RTE-0617] |

Within 15 days of receiving a report of alleged noncompliance from the auditor, the CFAO must either notify the auditor that the CFAO disagrees or issue a notice of potential noncompliance to the contractor and provide a copy to the auditor [@CL-RTE-0617]. The notice must notify the contractor in writing of the exact nature of the noncompliance and allow the contractor 60 days, or another mutually agreeable date, to agree or submit reasons why the contractor considers the existing practices to be in compliance, and to submit rationale to support any written statement that the cost impact of the noncompliance is immaterial [@CL-RTE-0618; @CL-RTE-0025].

## How it works

The contractor must submit a description of any cost accounting practice change needed to correct a noncompliance within 60 days after the earlier of agreement with the CFAO that there is a noncompliance or notification by the CFAO of a determination of noncompliance [@CL-RTE-0619]. The CFAO must separately identify interest on any increased cost paid in the aggregate as a result of the noncompliance, and must compute interest from the date of overpayment to the date of repayment using the rate specified in section 6621(a)(2) of title 26 of the United States Code [@CL-RTE-0620].

For noncompliances that involve estimating costs, the calculation determines the increased or decreased cost to the Government for fixed-price contracts and subcontracts [@CL-RTE-0724]. For fixed-price contracts and subcontracts, the difference is an increased cost to the Government when the negotiated contract or subcontract price exceeds what the negotiated price would have been had the contractor used a compliant practice [@CL-RTE-0621].

When the cost impact of a noncompliance is immaterial, the CFAO informs the contractor in writing that the noncompliance should be corrected and that, if the noncompliance is not corrected, the Government reserves the right to make appropriate contract adjustments should it become material in the future [@CL-RTE-0704]. If the contractor does not correct the noncompliance or submit the required proposal within the specified time or any extension granted by the CFAO, the CFAO follows the remedies procedures in section 30.604 [@CL-RTE-0723]. The remedies are described on the card for processing changes.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

The auditor reports an alleged estimating noncompliance, and the CFAO issues a notice of potential noncompliance to the contractor 10 days later. The CFAO then determines that there is a noncompliance, and the contractor describes its correction within 60 days. The noncompliance raised negotiated prices on fixed-price contracts by 100,000 above what a compliant practice would have produced, which is increased cost to the Government. The 100,000 was repaid after twelve months, and the interest computed under the statutory rate is 6,000, identified separately from the 100,000.

## Never confuse

A noncompliance is processed under its own section and notice steps. A unilateral change made without the required notice may still be processed as a noncompliance [@CL-RTE-0712]. A noncompliance with an immaterial cost impact ends with no contract adjustments [@CL-RTE-0603], but the contractor is told in writing to correct it and that the Government reserves the right to make appropriate contract adjustments if the cost impact becomes material in the future [@CL-RTE-0704; @CL-RTE-0024]. See [Processing cost accounting practice changes](rte-processing-accounting-changes-gdm-dci.md) and [CAS materiality](../../compliance/cas-allowability/cmp-cas-materiality.md).
