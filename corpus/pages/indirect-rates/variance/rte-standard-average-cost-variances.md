## Purpose

Shows when standard costs, average costs and pre-established labor rates may be used for direct costs, and how their variances are disposed of.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | CAS [@CL-RTE-0650] |
| Codified citation | 48 CFR 9904.418-50(a)(2) and (h) [@CL-RTE-0650; @CL-RTE-0654] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-RTE-0650] |
| Applies to | Business units that use standard costs, average costs or pre-established labor rates for direct costs [@CL-RTE-0650; @CL-RTE-0651] |

Standard costs may be used for estimating, accumulating and reporting direct material and direct labor costs only when the standard costs and related variances are appropriately accounted for at the production unit level and under the standard costing paragraph [@CL-RTE-0650]. A production unit is a grouping of activities that uses homogeneous inputs of direct material and direct labor or yields homogeneous outputs, so that the related costs or statistics are appropriate bases for allocating variances [@CL-RTE-0659].

An average cost or pre-established labor rate may be used if the functions are not materially disparate and the employees are interchangeable, or if the functions are materially disparate but the employees all work in one production unit yielding homogeneous outputs or perform their functions as an integral team [@CL-RTE-0651; @CL-RTE-0652]. When such rates are used, the variances, if material, are disposed of at least annually by allocation to cost objectives in proportion to the costs previously allocated to them [@CL-RTE-0653].

## How it works

A labor-rate standard for a group of workers who perform disparate functions is allowed only in one of two cases [@CL-RTE-0654]. In the first case the group works in one production unit yielding homogeneous outputs, and the same standard applies to each worker [@CL-RTE-0655]. In the second case the group forms an integral team, and each team gets its own standard [@CL-RTE-0656]. A contractor's established practice for variances accumulated by production unit follows one of two listed practices [@CL-RTE-0751]. The first allocates variances to cost objectives, including ending in-process inventory, at least annually [@CL-RTE-0657]. A variance related to material is then allocated on material cost at standard or, where outputs are homogeneous, on units of output [@CL-RTE-0657], and a variance related to labor on labor cost at standard, labor hours at standard or, where outputs are homogeneous, units of output [@CL-RTE-0752]. The second lets immaterial variances be included in appropriate indirect cost pools for allocation to applicable cost objectives [@CL-RTE-0658]. Where pre-established indirect rates are used and the variances of a cost accounting period are material, those variances are allocated to cost objectives in proportion to the costs previously allocated by the rates [@CL-RTE-0660].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A production unit sets a labor-rate standard for one integral team. Labor at standard cost 400,000 for the year, and actual labor cost 420,000, so the variance is 20,000. Contract A carries 300,000 of the labor at standard and ending in-process inventory carries 100,000. The variance is allocated on labor cost at standard: 15,000 to Contract A and 5,000 to ending inventory.

## Never confuse

A pre-established labor rate is not a pre-established indirect rate. The labor rate sits among the direct cost rules and depends on interchangeable employees, production units or integral teams [@CL-RTE-0651; @CL-RTE-0652]. The indirect rate covers a pool, and its variances, if material, go to cost objectives in proportion to the costs previously allocated by the rates [@CL-RTE-0660]. For the wider direct versus indirect rule, see [Direct costs](../pools-bases/rte-direct-costs.md) and [the CAS direct and indirect cost card](../../compliance/cas-allowability/cmp-cas-418-direct-indirect.md).
