## Purpose

Shows when standard costs, average costs and pre-established labor rates may be used for direct costs, and how their variances are disposed of.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | CAS [@CL-RTE-0650] |
| Codified citation | 48 CFR 9904.418-50(a)(2) and (h) [@CL-RTE-0650; @CL-RTE-0654] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-RTE-0650] |
| Applies to | Business units that use standard costs, average costs or pre-established labor rates for direct costs [@CL-RTE-0650; @CL-RTE-0651] |

In accounting for direct costs a business unit shall use actual costs, except for the two alternative exceptions in 48 CFR 9904.418-50(a)(2)(i) and (ii) [@CL-RTE-9156; @CL-RTE-0032]. Under the first, standard costs may be used for estimating, accumulating, and reporting costs of direct material and direct labor only when standard costs and related variances are appropriately accounted for at the level of the production unit and paragraph (h) of 48 CFR 9904.418-50 [@CL-RTE-0650; @CL-RTE-0032]. Production unit means a grouping of activities which either uses homogeneous inputs of direct material and direct labor or yields homogeneous outputs such that the costs or statistics related to these homogeneous inputs or outputs are appropriate as bases for allocating variances [@CL-RTE-0659].

Under the second exception, an average cost or pre-established rate for labor may be used provided that (A) the functions performed are not materially disparate and employees involved are interchangeable with respect to the functions performed, or (B) the functions performed are materially disparate but the employees involved either all work in a single production unit yielding homogeneous outputs or perform their respective functions as an integral team; whenever average cost or pre-established rates for labor are used, the variances, if material, shall be disposed of at least annually by allocation to cost objectives in proportion to the costs previously allocated to these cost objectives [@CL-RTE-0651; @CL-RTE-0652; @CL-RTE-0653].

## How it works

A labor-rate standard may be set to cover a group of direct labor workers who perform disparate functions only under the conditions in either paragraph (h)(1)(i) or (ii) of 48 CFR 9904.418-50 [@CL-RTE-0654]: (i) where that group of workers all work in a single production unit yielding homogeneous outputs, in which case the same labor-rate standard shall be applied to each worker in that group [@CL-RTE-0655]; or (ii) where that group of workers, in the performance of their respective functions, forms an integral team, in which case a labor-rate standard shall be set for each integral team [@CL-RTE-0656]. A contractor's established practice with respect to the disposition of variances accumulated by production unit shall be in accordance with paragraph (h)(2)(i) or (ii) of 48 CFR 9904.418-50 [@CL-RTE-0751]: under (i), variances are allocated to cost objectives (including ending in-process inventory) at least annually, where a variance related to material is allocated the allocation shall be on the basis of the material cost at standard, or, where outputs are homogeneous, on the basis of units of output [@CL-RTE-0657], and similarly, where a variance related to labor is allocated, the allocation shall be on the basis of the labor cost at standard or labor hours at standard or, where outputs are homogeneous, on the basis of units of output [@CL-RTE-0752]; or under (ii), variances which are immaterial may be included in appropriate indirect cost pools for allocation to applicable cost objectives [@CL-RTE-0658]. For preestablished rates for indirect costs (which, based on either forecasted actual or standard cost, may be used in allocating an indirect cost pool), under paragraphs (g)(2) and (g)(3) of 48 CFR 9904.418-50, where variances of a cost accounting period are material, these variances shall be disposed of by allocating them to cost objectives in proportion to the costs previously allocated to these cost objectives by use of the preestablished rates [@CL-RTE-0033; @CL-RTE-0660].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A production unit sets a labor-rate standard for one integral team. Labor at standard cost 400,000 for the year, and actual labor cost 420,000, so the variance is 20,000. Contract A carries 300,000 of the labor at standard and ending in-process inventory carries 100,000. The variance is allocated on labor cost at standard: 15,000 to Contract A and 5,000 to ending inventory.

## Never confuse

In accounting for direct costs, an average cost or pre-established rate for labor may be used provided that (A) the functions performed are not materially disparate and employees involved are interchangeable with respect to the functions performed, or (B) the functions performed are materially disparate but the employees involved either all work in a single production unit yielding homogeneous outputs or perform their respective functions as an integral team, and whenever average cost or pre-established rates for labor are used, the variances, if material, shall be disposed of at least annually by allocation to cost objectives in proportion to the costs previously allocated to these cost objectives [@CL-RTE-9156; @CL-RTE-0651; @CL-RTE-0652; @CL-RTE-0653]. Preestablished rates for indirect costs, based on either forecasted actual or standard cost, may be used in allocating an indirect cost pool, and under paragraphs (g)(2) and (g)(3) of 48 CFR 9904.418-50, where variances of a cost accounting period are material, these variances shall be disposed of by allocating them to cost objectives in proportion to the costs previously allocated to these cost objectives by use of the preestablished rates [@CL-RTE-0033; @CL-RTE-0660]. For the wider direct versus indirect rule, see [Direct costs](../pools-bases/rte-direct-costs.md) and [the CAS direct and indirect cost card](../../compliance/cas-allowability/cmp-cas-418-direct-indirect.md).
