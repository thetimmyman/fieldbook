## Purpose

Covers the procedural cycle for reimbursing indirect costs under cost-reimbursement contracts, from provisional billing rates through proposal submission, audit, and negotiation to final billing adjustment.

## The rule

Under FAR subpart 42.7 [@CL-RTE-9004] and the Allowable Cost and Payment clause at FAR 52.216-7 [@CL-CTF-0127; @CL-RTE-9232], the Government reimburses indirect costs under cost-reimbursement contracts and determines progress payments under fixed-price contracts using temporary billing rates, subject to adjustment when final indirect cost rates are established [@CL-RTE-0304; @CL-CTF-0127]. A billing rate is an indirect cost rate established temporarily for interim reimbursement of incurred indirect costs and adjusted as necessary until final indirect cost rates are established [@CL-RTE-0300]. Establishing final indirect cost rates provides a uniform approach with a contractor when more than one contract or agency is involved, economy of administration, and timely settlement under cost-reimbursement contracts [@CL-RTE-0301], with a single agency responsible for establishing final rates that bind all agencies for each business unit unless a statute specifically prohibits it [@CL-RTE-0302].

## How it works

1. Establish provisional billing rates: The contracting officer (or cognizant Federal agency official) or auditor responsible under FAR 42.705 [@CL-RTE-9320] for establishing final indirect cost rates is also responsible for determining billing rates under FAR 42.704(a) [@CL-RTE-9320], establishing them on the basis of information resulting from recent review, previous rate audits or experience, or similar reliable data or experience of other contracting activities under FAR 42.704(b) [@CL-RTE-9321], and ensuring they are as close as possible to the final indirect cost rates anticipated for the contractor's fiscal period, as adjusted for any unallowable costs [@CL-RTE-9322].
2. Bill indirect costs at interim rates: Until final annual indirect cost rates are established for any period, the contractor bills and the Government reimburses indirect costs at the established billing rates under FAR 52.216-7(e) [@CL-CTF-0127; @CL-RTE-9323], which serve as temporary interim reimbursement rates subject to adjustment when final rates are established [@CL-RTE-0300; @CL-CTF-0127].
3. Adjust billing rates during performance: Once established, billing rates may be prospectively or retroactively revised by mutual agreement of the contracting officer (or cognizant Federal agency official) or auditor and the contractor at either party's request under FAR 42.704(c) [@CL-RTE-9324] and FAR 52.216-7(e)(2) [@CL-RTE-9323] to prevent substantial overpayment or underpayment [@CL-RTE-9324; @CL-RTE-9323], and when agreement cannot be reached, the billing rates may be unilaterally determined by the contracting officer (or cognizant Federal agency official) under FAR 42.704(c) [@CL-RTE-9325].
4. Submit certified final indirect cost rate proposal: The contractor must submit an adequate final indirect cost rate proposal to the contracting officer (or cognizant Federal agency official) and the cognizant auditor within the 6-month [@CL-RTE-0001; @CL-RTE-9142] period following the expiration of each fiscal year [@CL-RTE-9142], based on actual cost experience for that period [@CL-RTE-9233], with reasonable extensions requested in writing by the contractor and granted in writing by the contracting officer for exceptional circumstances only [@CL-RTE-9119; @CL-RTE-0001]; the proposal must be certified because no proposal is accepted and no agreement to establish final indirect cost rates is made unless the contractor has certified the costs [@CL-RTE-0218].
5. Revise billing rates upon certified proposal: When the contractor provides to the cognizant contracting officer the certified final indirect cost rate proposal, the contractor and the Government may mutually agree under FAR 42.704(e) [@CL-RTE-9326] to revise billing rates to reflect the proposed indirect cost rates, as approved by the Government to reflect historically disallowed amounts from prior years' audits, until the proposal has been audited and settled [@CL-RTE-9326], with the historical decrement determined by either the cognizant contracting officer or the cognizant auditor [@CL-RTE-9327].
6. Review adequacy and perform audit: The cognizant auditor reviews the adequacy of the contractor's proposal for audit in support of negotiating final indirect cost rates and provides a written description of any inadequacies to the contractor and contracting officer under FAR 42.705-1 [@CL-RTE-0106; @CL-RTE-9229]; once the proposal is determined adequate for audit, the auditor audits the proposal and prepares an advisory audit report under FAR 42.705-2 [@CL-RTE-0215], or for low-risk proposals eligible under DCAA risk-based sampling policy [@CL-RTE-0318] not selected for audit, DCAA issues a risk-based sampling memorandum and a rate agreement letter signed by both DCAA and the contractor to finalize the indirect rates proposed [@CL-RTE-0319; @CL-RTE-0323].
7. Settle final indirect cost rates: Final indirect cost rates are established under FAR 42.705(a) [@CL-RTE-9337] either by contracting officer determination through negotiations headed by the contracting officer with the auditor's support under FAR 42.705-1 [@CL-RTE-0107; @CL-RTE-9139; @CL-RTE-0324]—supported by a signed negotiation memorandum placed in the general file that reconciles all questioned costs and identifies items and amounts allowed or disallowed [@CL-RTE-0108; @CL-RTE-9140; @CL-RTE-9141; @CL-RTE-9230]—or by auditor determination under FAR 42.705-2 when the auditor and contractor execute an indirect cost rate agreement [@CL-RTE-0216; @CL-RTE-0262; @CL-RTE-0323] (elevating disagreements to the contracting officer [@CL-RTE-0217]), or the contracting officer may unilaterally establish rates based on audited historical data excluding unallowable costs if the contractor has not certified its proposal and a waiver is not appropriate under FAR 42.703-2 [@CL-RTE-0220; @CL-RTE-0221].
8. Execute written rate understanding: The contractor and the appropriate Government representative execute a written understanding setting forth the final indirect cost rates under FAR 52.216-7(d)(3) [@CL-RTE-9328] and prepare an indirect cost rate agreement conforming to contract requirements under FAR 42.705-1 [@CL-RTE-0110; @CL-RTE-9139], specifying the agreed-upon final annual indirect cost rates, the bases to which the rates apply, the periods for which they apply, and any specific indirect cost items treated as direct costs [@CL-RTE-9329]; the understanding is incorporated into the contract upon execution and does not change any monetary ceiling or obligation [@CL-RTE-9339], and failure by the parties to agree on a final annual indirect cost rate constitutes a dispute under FAR 52.216-7(d)(4) [@CL-RTE-9330].
9. Complete final billing and voucher adjustments: The contractor must update the billings on all contracts to reflect the final settled rates and update the schedule of cumulative direct and indirect costs claimed and billed within 60 days [@CL-RTE-9331] after settlement of final indirect cost rates under FAR 52.216-7(d)(2)(v) [@CL-RTE-9331], and within 120 days [@CL-RTE-9332; @CL-RTE-9333] after settlement of final rates for all years of a physically complete contract must submit a completion invoice or voucher reflecting settled amounts and rates under FAR 52.216-7(d)(5) [@CL-RTE-9332] and FAR 42.705(b) [@CL-RTE-9333]; if the contractor fails to submit a completion invoice or voucher within the specified period, the contracting officer may determine the amounts due and record this determination in a unilateral modification under FAR 52.216-7(d)(6)(i) [@CL-RTE-9334] and FAR 42.705(c)(1) [@CL-RTE-9335], which constitutes a final decision in accordance with the Disputes clause under FAR 52.216-7(d)(6)(ii) [@CL-RTE-9338].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An invented contractor bills overhead at a provisional billing rate of 38 percent on an incurred direct allocation base of 2,000,000 across its cost-reimbursement contracts, billing 760,000 in interim indirect costs. Following fiscal year end and submission of the certified incurred cost proposal, the parties evaluate recent cost trends and agree to an interim revision of the billing rate to 37 percent to reduce the risk of substantial overpayment while the audit is pending. Later, the administrative contracting officer and the contractor negotiate and settle the final overhead rate at 36 percent, establishing 720,000 as the final allowable overhead on the 2,000,000 base. When billings on all contracts are updated to reflect the settled rate, the contractor adjusts its cumulative billed overhead downward by 40,000 (from 760,000 billed to 720,000 allowed), crediting the difference to the Government.

## Never confuse

A provisional billing rate is an indirect cost rate established temporarily for interim reimbursement of incurred indirect costs and progress payments under FAR 42.701 [@CL-RTE-0300] and FAR 42.703-1 [@CL-RTE-0304], adjusted as necessary until final rates are settled [@CL-RTE-0300; @CL-CTF-0127]. In contrast, a final indirect cost rate is the rate established and agreed upon by the Government and the contractor as not subject to change under FAR 2.101 [@CL-RTE-9071], used to determine total allowable indirect costs for contract closeout under FAR 42.703-1 [@CL-RTE-0301; @CL-RTE-0305]. Neither rate should be confused with a forward pricing rate agreement, which is a written agreement negotiated between the contractor and the Government to make specific rates available for use in pricing future contracts or modifications under FAR 2.101 [@CL-RTE-9076], nor with a wrap rate, which is an informal contractor pricing build-up that layers direct labor with fringe benefits, overhead, and general and administrative expenses into a fully burdened labor rate for proposals, rather than a rate agreed with or established by the Government (see [Wrap rate](../pools-bases/rte-wrap-rate-build-up.md)).

Under FAR 42.704(d), the elements of indirect cost and the base or bases used in computing billing rates shall not be construed as determinative of the indirect costs to be distributed or of the bases of distribution to be used in the final settlement [@CL-RTE-9336].

A billing-rate adjustment revises the interim percentage prospectively or retroactively during contract performance to prevent substantial overpayment or underpayment under FAR 42.704(c) [@CL-RTE-9324] and FAR 52.216-7(e)(2) [@CL-RTE-9323], and in practice contractors monitor cumulative incurred costs against interim billings to request such revisions [@CL-RTE-9340]. In contrast, the final adjustment of billed costs occurs after final rates are formally established and settled, requiring the contractor to recalculate cumulative direct and indirect costs across all contracts within 60 days [@CL-RTE-9331] under FAR 52.216-7(d)(2)(v) [@CL-RTE-9331] and submit final completion invoices or vouchers within 120 days [@CL-RTE-9332; @CL-RTE-9333] under FAR 52.216-7(d)(5) [@CL-RTE-9332] and FAR 42.705(b) [@CL-RTE-9333].

DCAA publishes its guidance for contractors at https://www.dcaa.mil/Guidance/Audit-Process-Overview/ ; the Corpus does not hold that guidance, so no statement on this page rests on it.
