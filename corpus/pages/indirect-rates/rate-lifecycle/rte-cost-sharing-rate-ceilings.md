## Purpose

Explains how cost-sharing arrangements and negotiated ceilings limit what the government pays for indirect costs.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-RTE-0308] |
| Codified citation | FAR 42.707 [@CL-RTE-0308] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-RTE-0308] |
| Applies to | Contracts where cost sharing is authorized or a negotiated rate ceiling is included [@CL-RTE-0309] |

Where cost sharing is authorized, the arrangement may call for the contractor to participate in the costs of the contract by accepting rates lower than the anticipated actual rates, and a negotiated rate ceiling may be included in the contract for prospective application [@CL-RTE-0308; @CL-RTE-0309]. Other situations may make a ceiling prudent too, for example a new or recently reorganized company with no past or recent record of incurred indirect costs [@CL-RTE-0310].

When ceiling provisions are used, the contract must also provide that the Government is not obligated to pay any additional amount should the final indirect cost rates exceed the negotiated ceiling rates, and that the negotiated rates are reduced to conform with the lower rates if the final rates come in lower [@CL-RTE-0312; @CL-RTE-0313].

## How it works

In such situations an equitable ceiling covering the final indirect cost rates may be negotiated and specified in the contract [@CL-RTE-0311]. Once ceiling provisions are used, the contract carries both adjustments, so the Government pays nothing above the ceiling and the negotiated rates drop to a lower final rate [@CL-RTE-0312; @CL-RTE-0313].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor accepts an overhead ceiling of 30 percent on a base of 2,000,000 while expecting an actual rate of 32 percent. The final rate is 33 percent, so the government pays 600,000 and nothing more, and the 60,000 above the ceiling stays with the contractor. Had the final rate been 28 percent, the negotiated rate would drop and the billable amount would fall to 560,000.

## Never confuse

A rate ceiling is not a billing rate. A billing rate is an indirect cost rate established temporarily for interim reimbursement and adjusted as necessary pending final rates [@CL-RTE-0300], while a ceiling limits what the Government will pay if the final rates exceed it [@CL-RTE-0312]. A ceiling is also not limited to cost sharing, because other situations may make one prudent [@CL-RTE-0310].

## Company calibration

Confirm whether any contract carries a negotiated indirect cost rate ceiling, and how the adjustment runs in each direction [@CL-RTE-0313]. [@Q-043]
