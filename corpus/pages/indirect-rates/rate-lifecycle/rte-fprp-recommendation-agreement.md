## Purpose

Describes the forward pricing rate proposal, recommendation and agreement as the audit manual presents them, how each one is created, and what the auditor does with them.

The Department relies on three sequential rate mechanisms governed by FAR to streamline and standardize the negotiation of future defense contracts, and the process begins with a Forward Pricing Rate Proposal [@CL-RTE-0401]. The proposal is the contractor's formal submission detailing its forecast of direct labor rates, indirect cost pools and allocation bases over a specified multi-year projection period [@CL-RTE-0402]. If the contractor and the government reach a bilateral agreement on the proposal, they execute a contractually binding agreement to be used across all subsequent proposals [@CL-RTE-0342]. In the absence of a signed agreement or while negotiations are ongoing, the administrative contracting officer unilaterally issues a recommendation for procurement offices [@CL-RTE-0343].

A recommendation is a formal rate schedule issued unilaterally by the administrative contracting officer to procuring contracting officers, recommending specific rates to use when an agreement cannot be reached, is being updated or is being audited [@CL-RTE-0344].

## How it works

An agreement is a formal written agreement negotiated between the contractor and the government, establishing specific rates to be used in pricing proposals during a specified period [@CL-RTE-0403]. The process may be started by the contractor, the procuring contracting officer or the administrative contracting officer, and the contractor's proposal must include certified cost or pricing data that is accurate, complete and current [@CL-RTE-0345]. The formal certificate is not executed when the agreement is made; certification occurs when an individual contract that uses the rates is priced [@CL-RTE-0346]. When evaluating the proposal, the auditor must assess the reasonableness of the projected business volume, allocation bases and indirect costs [@CL-RTE-0347]. Once an FPRA is in place, the DCAA auditor continuously monitors the rates, which involves periodically comparing the agreed-upon rates with the contractor's actual rates to identify any significant variances [@CL-RTE-9234]. If unfavorable trends emerge, the auditor should notify the ACO and may recommend that the contractor submit a revised FPRA proposal [@CL-RTE-0348]. If an auditor later finds that rates should have been revised due to significant changes to reflect more accurate, complete, or current certified cost or pricing data, pricing actions using the rates should be subject to a Truthful Cost or Pricing Data (TIN) audit [@CL-RTE-0349].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor proposes forward rates for three years. Negotiations stall, so the administrative contracting officer issues a recommendation that procurement offices use in the meantime. The parties later sign a bilateral agreement, and a proposal priced after that date uses the agreed rates, with the certificate executed on that proposal rather than on the agreement.

## Never confuse

The proposal is the contractor's formal submission detailing its forecast [@CL-RTE-0402], unlike the recommendation, which is unilateral and issued when no agreement is signed or while negotiations continue [@CL-RTE-0343], or the agreement, which is negotiated between the contractor and the government [@CL-RTE-0403]. For the FAR rules on the agreement and on recommendations, see [Forward pricing rate agreements](rte-forward-pricing-rate-agreements.md).
