## Purpose

Shows what each interpretation in the Cost Accounting Standards Board rules decides when increased costs are determined.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Cost Accounting Standards Board rules [@CL-RTE-0835] |
| Codified citation | 48 CFR 9903.306 [@CL-RTE-0835] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-RTE-0835] |
| Applies to | Cost paid by the Government that results from a change in practices or a failure to comply with applicable standards [@CL-RTE-0835] |

In determining amounts of increased costs in the clauses at 9903.201-4(a), Cost Accounting Standards, 9903.201-4(c), Disclosure and Consistency of Cost Accounting Practices, and 9903.201-4(d), Consistency in Cost Accounting, the following considerations apply [@CL-RTE-9223]. The first interpretation deems increased costs to have resulted whenever the cost paid by the Government results from a change in a contractor's cost accounting practices or from failure to comply with applicable Cost Accounting Standards [@CL-RTE-0835], and such cost is higher than it would have been had the practices not been changed or applicable Cost Accounting Standards complied with [@CL-RTE-9222]. The second interpretation concerns a contractor under any fixed-price contract, including a firm fixed-price contract, that fails during contract performance to follow its cost accounting practices or to comply with applicable Cost Accounting Standards [@CL-RTE-0869]. For that contractor, increased costs are measured by the difference between the contract price agreed to and the contract price that would have been agreed to had the contractor proposed in accordance with the cost accounting practices used during contract performance [@CL-RTE-0836]. Under the third interpretation, in making price adjustments under the Cost Accounting Standards clause at 9903.201-4(a) in fixed price or cost reimbursement incentive contracts, or contracts providing for prospective or retroactive price redetermination, the Federal agency shall apply this requirement appropriately in the circumstances [@CL-RTE-9224].

The fourth interpretation says the contractor and the contracting officer may enter into an agreement as contemplated by subdivision (a)(4)(ii) of the Cost Accounting Standards clause at 9903.201-4(a) [@CL-RTE-0870], covering a change in practice proposed by the Government or the contractor for all of the contractor's contracts for which the contracting officer is responsible, provided that the agreement does not permit any increase in the cost paid by the Government [@CL-RTE-0837]. The fifth concerns an adjustment to the contract price or of cost allowances under the Cost Accounting Standards clause at 9903.201-4(a), which it says may not be required when a change in cost accounting practices or a failure to follow Standards or cost accounting practices is estimated to result in increased costs being paid under a particular contract by the United States [@CL-RTE-0872; @CL-RTE-9135]. That circumstance may arise when a contractor is performing two or more covered contracts and the change or failure affects all such contracts, and may increase the cost paid under one or more of them while decreasing it under one or more [@CL-RTE-0871]. In that case the Government will not require price adjustment for any increased costs paid by the United States so long as the cost decreases under one or more contracts are at least equal to the increased cost under the other affected contracts [@CL-RTE-0838]. That treatment applies provided that the contractor and the affected contracting officers agree on the method by which the price adjustments are to be made for all affected contracts [@CL-RTE-0839]. In that situation the contracting agencies would, of course, require an adjustment of the contract price or cost allowances, as appropriate, to the extent that the increases under certain contracts were not offset by the decreases under the remaining contracts [@CL-RTE-9136]. The sixth says whether cost impact is recognized by modifying a single contract, several but not all contracts, or all contracts, or any other suitable technique, is a contract administration matter [@CL-RTE-0840].

## How it works

Increased costs are deemed to have resulted whenever the cost paid by the Government results from a change in a contractor's cost accounting practices or from failure to comply with applicable Cost Accounting Standards, and such cost is higher than it would have been had the practices not been changed or applicable Cost Accounting Standards complied with [@CL-RTE-0835; @CL-RTE-9222]. If the contractor under a fixed-price contract fails during contract performance to follow its cost accounting practices or to comply with applicable Cost Accounting Standards, increased costs are measured by the difference between the contract price agreed to and the contract price that would have been agreed to had the contractor proposed in accordance with the cost accounting practices used during contract performance [@CL-RTE-0869; @CL-RTE-0836]. The contractor and the contracting officer may enter into an agreement covering a change in practice for all of the contractor's contracts for which the contracting officer is responsible, provided that the agreement does not permit any increase in the cost paid by the Government [@CL-RTE-0870; @CL-RTE-0837]. In the multiple-contract case, the Government will not require price adjustment for any increased costs paid by the United States so long as the cost decreases under one or more contracts are at least equal to the increased cost under the other affected contracts, provided that the contractor and the affected contracting officers agree on the method by which the price adjustments are to be made for all affected contracts [@CL-RTE-0838; @CL-RTE-0839]. The cost-impact process is on the [resolving cost impacts card](../variance/rte-resolving-cost-impacts.md), the clauses these interpretations apply to are on the [CAS clauses card](rte-cas-solicitation-provisions-and-clauses.md), and the findings that guard against aggregate increased costs are on the [findings card](rte-cas-findings-and-subcontractor-disclosure-statements.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A change in practice affects two covered contracts. Both are under the Cost Accounting Standards clause at 9903.201-4(a), and the change affects both. It raises the cost paid under the first by 30,000 and lowers the cost paid under the second by 40,000. The decrease is at least equal to the increase. If the contractor and the contracting officers agree on the method, the Government does not require a price adjustment for the 30,000.

## Never confuse

Where a contractor is performing two or more covered contracts and the change or failure affects all such contracts [@CL-RTE-0871], the Government will not require price adjustment for any increased costs paid by the United States so long as the cost decreases under one or more contracts are at least equal to the increased cost under the other affected contracts [@CL-RTE-0838], provided that the contractor and the affected contracting officers agree on the method by which the price adjustments are to be made for all affected contracts [@CL-RTE-0839]. An adjustment to the contract price or of cost allowances pursuant to the Cost Accounting Standards clause at 9903.201-4(a) may not be required when a change in cost accounting practices or a failure to follow Standards or cost accounting practices is estimated to result in increased costs being paid under a particular contract by the United States [@CL-RTE-0872; @CL-RTE-9135]. Whether cost impact is recognized by modifying a single contract, several but not all contracts, or all contracts, or any other suitable technique, is a contract administration matter [@CL-RTE-0840].
