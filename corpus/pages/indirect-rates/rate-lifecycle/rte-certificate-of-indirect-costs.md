## Purpose

Shows when final indirect cost rates require the contractor's certification, when it may be waived, and what happens without it.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-RTE-0218] |
| Codified citation | FAR 42.703-2(a) [@CL-RTE-0218] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 42.503-2 [@CL-RTE-0907; @CL-RTE-0908] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-RTE-0218] |
| Applies to | Proposals and agreements to establish final indirect cost rates [@CL-RTE-0218] |

A proposal shall not be accepted and no agreement shall be made to establish final indirect cost rates unless the costs have been certified by the contractor [@CL-RTE-0218]. The agency head or designee may waive the certification requirement when it is determined to be in the interest of the United States and the reasons for the determination are put in writing and made available to the public [@CL-RTE-0219]. A waiver may be appropriate, for example, for a contract with a foreign government or international organization, such as a subsidiary body of the North Atlantic Treaty Organization, or with a State or local government subject to the OMB Uniform Guidance at 2 CFR part 200, subpart E and appendices V and VII [@CL-RTE-0265]. If the contractor has not certified and a waiver is not appropriate, the contracting officer may unilaterally establish the rates [@CL-RTE-0220]. Rates established unilaterally should be based on audited historical data or other available data as long as unallowable costs are excluded, and set low enough to ensure that unallowable costs will not be reimbursed [@CL-RTE-9225; @CL-RTE-0221].

The clause at 52.242-4 shall be incorporated into all solicitations and contracts that provide for establishment of final indirect cost rates, except that the Department of Energy may provide an alternate clause in its agency supplement for its Management and Operating contracts [@CL-RTE-0222; @CL-RTE-0266].

## How it works

A proposal shall not be accepted and no agreement shall be made to establish final indirect cost rates unless the costs have been certified by the contractor [@CL-RTE-0218]. The agency head or designee may waive the certification requirement when it is determined to be in the interest of the United States and the reasons for the determination are put in writing and made available to the public [@CL-RTE-0219]. If the contractor has not certified its proposal for final indirect cost rates and a waiver is not appropriate, the contracting officer may unilaterally establish the rates [@CL-RTE-0220], and those rates should be based on audited historical data or other available data as long as unallowable costs are excluded and set low enough to ensure that unallowable costs will not be reimbursed [@CL-RTE-9225; @CL-RTE-0221]. The clause at 52.242-4 shall be incorporated into all solicitations and contracts which provide for establishment of final indirect cost rates, except that the Department of Energy may provide an alternate clause in its agency supplement for its Management and Operating contracts [@CL-RTE-0222; @CL-RTE-0266].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers shall use the RFO FAR part 42 text in place of the codified text, as Class Deviation 2026-O0050 directs, effective March 16, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9759] [@CL-CMP-9761]. RFO FAR 42.503-2(a) says the Government must not accept a proposal or agree to establish final indirect cost rates unless the contractor certifies the costs are allowable, where the codified text says a proposal shall not be accepted and no agreement shall be made unless the costs have been certified by the contractor [@CL-RTE-0907; @CL-RTE-0218]; the waiver in paragraph (b)(1), described next, applies to that certification requirement [@CL-RTE-0908]. Under RFO FAR 42.503-2(b)(1), the agency head, or designee, may waive the certification requirement when it is determined to be in the interest of the United States and the reasons for the determination are put in writing and made available to the public [@CL-RTE-0908; @CL-RTE-0219]. Under RFO FAR 42.503-2(b)(2), a waiver may be appropriate for a contract with a foreign government or international organization, such as a subsidiary body of the North Atlantic Treaty Organization, or with a State or local government subject to the OMB Uniform Guidance at 2 CFR part 200, subpart E and appendices V and VII, among others [@CL-RTE-0909; @CL-RTE-0265]. Under RFO FAR 42.503-2(c)(1), if the contractor has not certified its proposal for final indirect cost rates and a waiver is not appropriate, the contracting officer may unilaterally establish the rates [@CL-RTE-0910; @CL-RTE-0220]. Under RFO FAR 42.503-2(c)(2), unilateral rates should be based on audited historical data or other available data if unallowable costs are excluded, and set low enough to ensure that unallowable costs are not reimbursed [@CL-RTE-0911; @CL-RTE-0221]. RFO FAR 42.503-2(f)(1) says that, except as provided in paragraph (f)(2), the clause at 52.242-4, Certification of Final Indirect Costs, is to be used in all solicitations and contracts which provide for establishment of final indirect cost rates, where the codified text says the clause shall be incorporated [@CL-RTE-0912; @CL-RTE-0222]. Under RFO FAR 42.503-2(f)(2), the Department of Energy may provide an alternate clause in its agency supplement for its Management and Operating contracts [@CL-RTE-0913; @CL-RTE-0266].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor submits a final indirect rate proposal but has not certified the costs, and no waiver applies. The contracting officer unilaterally sets the overhead rate, basing it on audited historical data that excludes 20,000 of unallowable costs and setting it low enough that the 20,000 will not be reimbursed.

## Never confuse

For the final indirect cost rate proposal, see [Final indirect cost rate proposal](rte-final-indirect-rate-proposal.md).

The codified text says a proposal shall not be accepted unless the costs have been certified by the contractor [@CL-RTE-0218], while the RFO text says the Government must not accept a proposal unless the contractor certifies the costs are allowable [@CL-RTE-0907].
