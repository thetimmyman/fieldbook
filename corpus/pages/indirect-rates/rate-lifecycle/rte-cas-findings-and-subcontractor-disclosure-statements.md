## Purpose

Shows who makes each finding that the Cost Accounting Standards Board rules require, and how a subcontractor can meet the Disclosure Statement requirement.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Cost Accounting Standards Board rules [@CL-RTE-0807] |
| Codified citation | 48 CFR 9903.201-6 and 9903.202-8 [@CL-RTE-0807; @CL-RTE-0811] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-RTE-0807] |
| Applies to | Findings tied to adjustments under the CAS clauses, and subcontractor Disclosure Statements [@CL-RTE-0808; @CL-RTE-0811] |

The findings section names the official who makes each finding. In its required-change paragraph, before any equitable adjustment under paragraph (a)(4)(i) of the Cost Accounting Standards clause or its educational version, or paragraph (a)(3)(i) of the clause at 9903.201-4(c), the Contracting Officer shall make a finding [@CL-RTE-0852]. One finding it names is that the practice change was required to comply with a CAS, modification or interpretation thereof that subsequently became applicable to the contract [@CL-RTE-0807]. In its unilateral-change paragraph, before any contract price or cost adjustment under paragraph (a)(4)(ii) of the Cost Accounting Standards clause or its educational version, or paragraph (a)(3)(ii) of the clause at 9903.201-4(c), the Contracting Officer shall make a finding [@CL-RTE-0808]. That finding is that the contemplated contract price and cost adjustments will protect the United States from payment of increased costs, in the aggregate, and that the net effect of the adjustments does not result in recovery of more than the estimated amount of such increased costs [@CL-RTE-0854]. In its desirable-change paragraph, before any equitable adjustment under paragraph (a)(4)(iii) of the Cost Accounting Standards clause or its educational version, or paragraph (a)(3)(ii) of the clause at 9903.201-4(c), the cognizant Federal agency official shall make a finding [@CL-RTE-0853]. That finding is that the change to a cost accounting practice is desirable and not detrimental to the interests of the Government [@CL-RTE-0809]. In its noncompliance paragraph, before any adjustment under paragraph (a)(5) of the Cost Accounting Standards clause or its educational version, or paragraph (a)(4) of the clause at 9903.201-4(c), the Contracting Officer shall make a finding [@CL-RTE-0810]. That finding is that the contemplated contract price and cost adjustments will protect the United States from payment of increased costs, in the aggregate, and that the net effect of the adjustments does not result in recovery of more than the estimated amount of such increased costs [@CL-RTE-0855].

A subcontractor that has already furnished a Disclosure Statement to an ACO may meet the submission requirement by identifying to the contractor or higher tier subcontractor the ACO to whom it was submitted [@CL-RTE-0811]. A subcontractor that considers the Disclosure Statement privileged or confidential may submit it directly to the ACO and auditor cognizant of the subcontractor, notifying the contractor or higher tier subcontractor [@CL-RTE-0812]. The subcontractor section prints no time by which a subcontractor must file.

## How it works

Each finding is tied to the adjustment it clears [@CL-RTE-0852; @CL-RTE-0808; @CL-RTE-0853; @CL-RTE-0810]. The finding on a desirable change is made by the cognizant Federal agency official, and the other findings named here are made by the Contracting Officer [@CL-RTE-0807; @CL-RTE-0808; @CL-RTE-0809; @CL-RTE-0810]. The existing cards on [required changes](rte-required-changes-cost-accounting-practice.md), [unilateral and desirable changes](rte-unilateral-and-desirable-changes.md) and [subcontract CAS administration](rte-subcontract-cas-administration.md) cover the FAR side in more detail, and this card does not restate them. The clauses these findings clear are on the [CAS clauses card](rte-cas-solicitation-provisions-and-clauses.md). Whether a change is a change to a cost accounting practice at all is a separate question, shown by the [illustrations of changes](rte-changes-that-are-cost-accounting-practice-changes.md) and the [illustrations of changes that are not](rte-changes-that-are-not-cost-accounting-practice-changes.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor changes a pool base after a new standard applies to an existing contract. Before an equitable adjustment is made, the Contracting Officer makes the finding that the change was required to comply with the standard. If the change were instead a compliant change that the cognizant Federal agency official finds desirable and not detrimental to the Government, that official would make the desirable-change finding.

## Never confuse

The section sits in the Cost Accounting Standards Board text, but it assigns each finding to the Contracting Officer or the cognizant Federal agency official [@CL-RTE-0807; @CL-RTE-0809]. A required-change finding asks whether a standard forced the change [@CL-RTE-0807], and a desirable-change finding asks whether the change is not detrimental to the Government [@CL-RTE-0809]. A confidential statement may instead go directly to the ACO and auditor cognizant of the subcontractor [@CL-RTE-0812].
