## Purpose

Explains how incurred cost proposals are sampled for audit and the routes by which final indirect rates are settled.

## The rule

| Applies to | Incurred cost proposals assessed under risk-based sampling [@CL-RTE-0318] |

DCAA selects incurred cost proposals for audit under a risk-based sampling policy, assessing eligible proposals on sampling auditable dollar volume and risk factors [@CL-RTE-0318]. Unless an exception applies, proposals with a sampling auditable dollar volume of less than 100 million dollars are eligible for sampling [@CL-RTE-0320; @CL-CMP-1715], and proposals of 1 billion dollars or greater are not eligible and will be audited each year [@CL-RTE-0321; @CL-CMP-1716]. This card does not cover the tiers between those two figures.

For proposals not randomly selected for audit, DCAA issues a risk-based sampling memorandum and a rate agreement letter, signed by both DCAA and the contractor, to finalize the indirect rates proposed [@CL-RTE-0319].

## How it works

A class deviation, authorized by Defense Pricing, Contracting, and Acquisition Policy and dated July 24, 2012, covers the sampling of low-risk incurred cost proposals [@CL-RTE-0400]. It was authorized for purposes of satisfying the FAR 42.505-1(b)(2) and FAR 42.505-2(b)(2)(i) audit requirements for incurred cost proposals [@CL-RTE-9147]. It instructs contracting officers to rely on either a DCAA audit report or a DCAA memorandum to satisfy the requirements of FAR 42.505 [@CL-RTE-0322]. The audit manual lists the ways final indirect cost rates are established. Under audit determination, rates may be set based on the results of an audit or accepted as proposed if a low-risk sampling process is used, and the auditor's determination is generally final but contractors have the right to appeal [@CL-RTE-0323]. Under contracting officer determination, final rates can be negotiated between the administrative contracting officer and the contractor, based on the contractor's proposal and the audit report or advisory memorandum [@CL-RTE-0324]. In some cases, settlement may shift from negotiation to audit, especially when both parties agree the rates can be settled easily or when special circumstances apply [@CL-RTE-0325]. The rules for each route are on [Final indirect cost rates](rte-co-final-indirect-rates.md) and [auditor determination](rte-final-indirect-rate-auditor-determination.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A business unit files a proposal with sampling auditable dollar volume of 60,000,000. It is eligible for sampling, is not selected, and closes with a sampling memorandum and a signed rate agreement letter. Another unit at 1,200,000,000 is not eligible for sampling and is audited that year.

## Never confuse

Sampling is not acceptance without review. Eligible proposals are assessed on sampling auditable dollar volume and risk factors [@CL-RTE-0318]. Sampling is also not a third way of settling rates alongside the two determination routes: under audit determination, rates may be accepted as proposed if a low-risk sampling process is used [@CL-RTE-0323]. For how materiality shapes the procedures within an audit, see [Materiality in incurred cost audits](../../compliance/audit-retention/cmp-materiality-incurred-cost-audits.md).
