## Purpose

Shows how indirect costs on a terminated cost-reimbursement contract may be settled or reserved so that final settlement is not unduly delayed.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-RTE-0694] |
| Codified citation | FAR 49.303-4 and 49.303-5 [@CL-RTE-0694; @CL-RTE-0698] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-RTE-0694] |
| Applies to | Terminated contracts that contain the Allowable Cost and Payment clause, where it appears that adjusting indirect costs will unduly delay final settlement [@CL-RTE-0694] |

If the terminated contract contains the Allowable Cost and Payment clause and adjusting indirect costs appears to unduly delay final settlement, the termination contracting officer, after obtaining information from the appropriate audit agency, may agree with the contractor to one of two courses [@CL-RTE-0694]. The first is to negotiate the amount of indirect costs for the contract period for which final rates have not been negotiated, or to use billing rates as final rates for that period if they appear reasonable [@CL-RTE-0695]. The second is to reserve any indirect cost adjustment in the final settlement agreement, pending establishment of negotiated rates [@CL-RTE-0696].

When an amount is negotiated under the first course, the contractor shall eliminate that indirect cost and the related direct costs from the total pool and base used to compute indirect costs for other contracts performed during the applicable accounting period [@CL-RTE-0697].

## How it works

The termination contracting officer and the contractor may agree either to negotiate the amount of indirect costs for the contract period for which final indirect cost rates have not been negotiated, or to use billing rates as final rates for this period if the billing rates appear reasonable, or to reserve any indirect cost adjustment in the final settlement agreement, pending establishment of negotiated rates under subpart 42.7 [@CL-RTE-0694; @CL-RTE-0695; @CL-RTE-0696]. When an amount of indirect cost is negotiated under paragraph (a)(1), the contractor shall eliminate the indirect cost and the related direct costs on which it was based from the total pool and base used to compute indirect costs for other contracts performed during the applicable accounting period [@CL-RTE-0697]. The final settlement agreement may include all demands of the Government and proposals of the contractor under the terminated contract, but no amount shall be allowed for any item of cost disallowed by the Government, nor for any other item of cost of the same nature [@CL-RTE-0698]. An overall settlement shall not include costs that are clearly not allowable under the terms of the contract [@CL-RTE-0699].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A cost-reimbursement contract is terminated and the audit work on its indirect costs would hold up settlement. The contracting officer and the contractor agree to negotiate the amount of indirect cost for the open period. The negotiated indirect cost of 30,000 and the related direct costs of 200,000 are then taken out of the pool and base used for the contractor's other contracts performed during the applicable accounting period. Had they reserved the adjustment instead, the 30,000 would stay open until negotiated rates exist.

## Never confuse

One course is to negotiate the amount of indirect costs for the contract period for which final indirect cost rates have not been negotiated, or to use billing rates as final rates for this period if the billing rates appear reasonable [@CL-RTE-0695]. The other is to reserve any indirect cost adjustment in the final settlement agreement, pending establishment of negotiated rates under subpart 42.7 [@CL-RTE-0696]. For the ordinary rate process, see [Final indirect cost rate proposal](rte-final-indirect-rate-proposal.md) and [Termination settlement on cost-reimbursement contracts](../../contract-finance/billing-payment/ctf-termination-cost-reimbursement-settlement.md).
