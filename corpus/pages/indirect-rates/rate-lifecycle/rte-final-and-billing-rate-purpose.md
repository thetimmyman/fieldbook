## Purpose

Sets out why final indirect cost rates and billing rates exist, who establishes them, and where each set is used.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-RTE-0300] |
| Codified citation | FAR 42.703-1 [@CL-RTE-0302]; FAR 42.700 [@CL-RTE-9004] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 42.503-1 [@CL-RTE-0933; @CL-RTE-0935] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-RTE-0301] |
| Applies to | Cost-reimbursement contracts and fixed-price contracts with progress payments [@CL-RTE-0304] |

A single agency establishes the final indirect cost rates for each business unit, and those rates bind every agency and its contracting offices unless a statute specifically prohibits that [@CL-RTE-0302]. Final rates are used for contract closeout, and in negotiating the final price of fixed-price incentive and redeterminable contracts and in other situations requiring that indirect costs be settled before contract prices are established, unless the quick-closeout procedure is used [@CL-RTE-0305; @CL-RTE-0307].

Billing rates are the interim counterpart: an indirect cost rate set temporarily for interim reimbursement and adjusted as necessary until the final rates are established [@CL-RTE-0300]. Both sets are used to reimburse indirect costs under cost-reimbursement contracts and to determine progress payments under fixed-price contracts [@CL-RTE-0304].

Subpart 42.7 prescribes policies and procedures for establishing billing rates and final indirect cost rates [@CL-RTE-9004].

## How it works

The stated purposes are a uniform approach with a contractor when more than one contract or agency is involved, economy of administration, and timely settlement under cost-reimbursement contracts [@CL-RTE-0301]. One agency is responsible for the rates, and an agency does not perform an audit of indirect cost rates when the contracting officer determines that the audit objectives can reasonably be met by accepting the results of an audit conducted by another department or agency of the Federal Government [@CL-RTE-0302; @CL-RTE-0303]. The final rates bind all cost-reimbursement contracts at the business unit, subject to a specific limitation in a contract or advance agreement, and the quick-closeout procedure is the stated exception for closeout [@CL-RTE-0306; @CL-RTE-0305].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 42 text in place of the codified text because Class Deviation 2026-O0050 directs it, effective March 16, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9759] [@CL-CMP-9761]. RFO FAR 42.503-1(a) keeps the rule that a single agency is responsible for establishing final indirect cost rates for each business unit and that these rates are binding on all agencies and their contracting offices, unless specifically prohibited by statute, citing RFO 42.505-1 where the codified text cites 42.705-1 [@CL-RTE-0933; @CL-RTE-0302]. The next RFO sentence says agencies cannot audit indirect cost rates when the audit objectives can reasonably be met by accepting the results of an audit by another department or agency of the Federal Government, and it does not contain the codified words "when the contracting officer determines" [@CL-RTE-0934; @CL-RTE-0303]. RFO FAR 42.503-1(b) says contracting officers must use billing rates and final indirect cost rates to reimburse indirect costs under cost-reimbursement contracts and make progress payments under fixed-price contracts, where the codified text says the rates shall be used in reimbursing indirect costs and in determining progress payments [@CL-RTE-0935; @CL-RTE-0304]. RFO FAR 42.503-1(c)(1) says that final indirect cost rates are used for contract closeout for a business unit unless the quick-closeout procedure is used, citing RFO 42.507 where the codified text cites 42.708 [@CL-RTE-0936; @CL-RTE-0305]. It keeps the rule that these final rates are binding for all cost-reimbursement contracts at the business unit, subject to any specific limitation in a contract or advance agreement [@CL-RTE-0937; @CL-RTE-0306]. RFO FAR 42.503-1(c)(2) lists, as a further use of final indirect cost rates, negotiating the final price of fixed-price incentive and fixed-price redeterminable contracts and in other situations requiring that indirect costs be settled before contract prices are established, unless the quick-closeout procedure is used, citing RFO 42.507 where the codified text cites 42.708 [@CL-RTE-0938; @CL-RTE-0936; @CL-RTE-0307]. RFO FAR 42.502(a) keeps the stated purposes of final indirect cost rates: uniformity of approach with a contractor when more than one contract or agency is involved, economy of administration, and timely settlement under cost-reimbursement contracts [@CL-RTE-0939; @CL-RTE-0301]. RFO FAR 42.501 keeps the definition of a billing rate as an indirect cost rate established temporarily for interim reimbursement of incurred indirect costs and adjusted as necessary until final indirect cost rates are established [@CL-RTE-0932; @CL-RTE-0300].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A business unit bills indirect costs at a provisional rate of 40 percent on a base of 1,000,000, recovering 400,000 during the year. The final rate is settled at 37 percent, so the allowable recovery on the same base is 370,000 and the 30,000 difference is adjusted in the final settlement.

## Never confuse

A billing rate is not a final rate. The billing rate is temporary and is adjusted; the final rate settles the indirect costs for the period. Quick-closeout is a separate exception that lets a contract close before final rates are set, not the rule for setting them; see [quick-closeout](rte-quick-closeout.md).

The codified text bars an agency's own audit when the contracting officer determines that the audit objectives can reasonably be met by accepting another agency's audit [@CL-RTE-0303], while the RFO sentence does not contain the words "when the contracting officer determines" [@CL-RTE-0934].
