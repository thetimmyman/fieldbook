## Purpose

Sets out why final indirect cost rates and billing rates exist, who establishes them, and where each set is used.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-RTE-0300] |
| Codified citation | FAR 42.703-1 [@CL-RTE-0302]; FAR 42.700 [@CL-RTE-9004] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 42.503-1 [@CL-RTE-0933; @CL-RTE-0935] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-RTE-0301] |
| Applies to | Cost-reimbursement contracts and fixed-price contracts with progress payments [@CL-RTE-0304] |

A single agency shall be responsible for establishing the final indirect cost rates for each business unit, and those rates shall be binding on all agencies and their contracting offices unless otherwise specifically prohibited by statute [@CL-RTE-0302]. To ensure compliance with section 3743(a) of title 10 and section 4303(a) of title 41, United States Code, final indirect cost rates shall be used for contract closeout for a business unit, unless the quick-closeout procedure in 42.708 is used [@CL-RTE-9231; @CL-RTE-0305]. Established final indirect cost rates shall be used in negotiating the final price of fixed-price incentive and fixed-price redeterminable contracts and in other situations requiring that indirect costs be settled before contract prices are established, unless the quick-closeout procedure in 42.708 is used [@CL-RTE-9231; @CL-RTE-0307].

A billing rate is an indirect cost rate (1) established temporarily for interim reimbursement of incurred indirect costs and (2) adjusted as necessary pending establishment of final indirect cost rates [@CL-RTE-0300]. Both sets shall be used to reimburse indirect costs under cost-reimbursement contracts and to determine progress payments under fixed-price contracts [@CL-RTE-0304].

FAR subpart 42.7 prescribes policies and procedures for establishing billing rates and final indirect cost rates [@CL-RTE-9004].

FAR 2.101 defines the final indirect cost rate as the indirect cost rate established and agreed upon by the Government and the contractor as not subject to change [@CL-RTE-9071]. The first sentence of the definition of the final indirect cost rate in RFO FAR 2.101 reads the same as the first sentence in FAR 2.101 [@CL-RTE-9091; @CL-RTE-9071], and RFO FAR 2.101 goes on to say that the final indirect cost rate is usually established after the close of the contractor's fiscal year (unless the parties decide upon a different period) to which it applies, and that, for cost-reimbursement research and development contracts with educational institutions, it may be predetermined; that is, established for a future period on the basis of cost experience with similar contracts, together with supporting data [@CL-RTE-9091; @CL-RTE-9111; @CL-RTE-9112]. In FAR 2.101, the final indirect cost rate is usually established after the close of the contractor's fiscal year, unless the parties decide upon a different period, to which it applies, and, for cost-reimbursement research and development contracts with educational institutions, it may be predetermined, that is, established for a future period on the basis of cost experience with similar contracts, together with supporting data [@CL-RTE-9072; @CL-RTE-9116]. An indirect cost rate is the percentage or dollar factor that expresses the ratio of indirect expense incurred in a given period to direct labor cost, manufacturing cost, or another appropriate base for the same period [@CL-RTE-9073]. RFO FAR 2.101 defines the indirect cost rate in the same words as FAR 2.101, apart from the form of the quotation marks [@CL-RTE-9092; @CL-RTE-9073].

## How it works

Establishing final indirect cost rates provides uniformity of approach with a contractor when more than one contract or agency is involved, economy of administration, and timely settlement under cost-reimbursement contracts [@CL-RTE-0301]. One agency is responsible for the rates, and an agency shall not perform an audit of indirect cost rates when the contracting officer determines that the audit objectives can reasonably be met by accepting the results of an audit conducted by another department or agency of the Federal Government [@CL-RTE-0302; @CL-RTE-0303]. The final rates shall be binding for all cost-reimbursement contracts at the business unit, subject to any specific limitation in a contract or advance agreement [@CL-RTE-0306].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers shall use the RFO FAR part 42 text in place of the codified text, as Class Deviation 2026-O0050 directs, effective March 16, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9759] [@CL-CMP-9761]. RFO FAR 42.503-1(a) keeps the rule that a single agency is responsible for establishing final indirect cost rates for each business unit and that these rates are binding on all agencies and their contracting offices, unless specifically prohibited by statute, citing RFO 42.505-1 where the codified text cites 42.705-1 [@CL-RTE-0933; @CL-RTE-0302]. RFO FAR 42.503-1(a) also says agencies cannot audit indirect cost rates when the audit objectives can reasonably be met by accepting the results of an audit by another department or agency of the Federal Government, where the codified text says "when the contracting officer determines" [@CL-RTE-0934; @CL-RTE-0303]. RFO FAR 42.503-1(b) says contracting officers must use billing rates and final indirect cost rates to reimburse indirect costs under cost-reimbursement contracts and make progress payments under fixed-price contracts, where the codified text says the rates shall be used in reimbursing indirect costs and in determining progress payments [@CL-RTE-0935; @CL-RTE-0304]. RFO FAR 42.503-1(c), to ensure compliance with 10 USC 3743(a) and 41 USC 4303(a), says to use final indirect cost rates for (1) contract closeout for a business unit unless the quick-closeout procedure is used, citing RFO 42.507 where the codified text cites 42.708 [@CL-RTE-0936; @CL-RTE-0305]. It keeps the rule that these final rates are binding for all cost-reimbursement contracts at the business unit, subject to any specific limitation in a contract or advance agreement [@CL-RTE-0937; @CL-RTE-0306]. RFO FAR 42.503-1(c)(2) lists, as a further use of final indirect cost rates, negotiating the final price of fixed-price incentive and fixed-price redeterminable contracts and in other situations requiring that indirect costs be settled before contract prices are established, unless the quick-closeout procedure is used, citing RFO 42.507 where the codified text cites 42.708 [@CL-RTE-0938; @CL-RTE-0936; @CL-RTE-0307]. RFO FAR 42.502(a) says establishing final indirect cost rates provides uniformity of approach with a contractor when more than one contract or agency is involved, economy of administration, and timely settlement under cost-reimbursement contracts [@CL-RTE-0939; @CL-RTE-0301]. RFO FAR 42.501 keeps the definition of a billing rate as an indirect cost rate established temporarily for interim reimbursement of incurred indirect costs and adjusted as necessary until final indirect cost rates are established [@CL-RTE-0932; @CL-RTE-0300].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A business unit bills indirect costs at a provisional rate of 40 percent on a base of 1,000,000, recovering 400,000 during the year. The final rate is settled at 37 percent, so the allowable recovery on the same base is 370,000 and the 30,000 difference is adjusted in the final settlement.

## Never confuse

A billing rate is an indirect cost rate established temporarily for interim reimbursement of incurred indirect costs [@CL-RTE-0300]. A final indirect cost rate is the indirect cost rate established and agreed upon by the Government and the contractor as not subject to change [@CL-RTE-9071]. For the quick-closeout procedure, see [quick-closeout](rte-quick-closeout.md).

FAR 42.703-1(a) says an agency shall not perform an audit of indirect cost rates when the contracting officer determines that the objectives of the audit can reasonably be met by accepting the results of an audit conducted by any other department or agency of the Federal Government [@CL-RTE-0303]. RFO FAR 42.503-1(a) says agencies cannot audit indirect cost rates when the audit objectives can reasonably be met by accepting the results of an audit by another department or agency of the Federal Government [@CL-RTE-0934].
