## Purpose

Shows when the auditor, rather than the contracting officer, establishes final indirect cost rates and how the procedure runs.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-RTE-0214] |
| Codified citation | FAR 42.705-2(a)(1) [@CL-RTE-0214] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-RTE-0214] |
| Applies to | Business units not covered in 42.705-1(a), and covered units only when the listed agreement and circumstances apply [@CL-RTE-0214; @CL-RTE-0262] |

The cognizant Government auditor establishes final indirect cost rates for business units not covered in 42.705-1(a) [@CL-RTE-0214].

Auditor determination may also be used for business units covered in 42.705-1(a), but only when the contracting officer (or cognizant Federal agency official) and the auditor agree that the indirect costs can be settled with little difficulty and any one of four circumstances applies [@CL-RTE-0262]. Two of them are that the unit has primarily fixed-price contracts with only minor involvement in cost-reimbursement contracts, and that the administrative cost of contracting officer determination would exceed the expected benefits [@CL-RTE-0263]. The other two are that the unit has no history of disputes and few cost problems, and that the contracting officer (or cognizant Federal agency official) and the auditor agree that special circumstances require auditor determination [@CL-RTE-0264].

Once the proposal has been determined adequate for audit in support of negotiating final indirect cost rates, the auditor audits it and prepares an advisory audit report listing any relevant advance agreements and restrictive terms of specific contracts [@CL-RTE-0215]. The auditor then seeks agreement with the contractor and prepares a rate agreement that the contractor and the auditor both sign [@CL-RTE-0216].

If agreement is not reached, the auditor forwards the audit report to the contracting officer, who resolves the disagreement [@CL-RTE-0217].

## How it works

The auditor takes the lead on the final rate for units outside the contracting-officer procedure, and for covered units when the contracting officer and the auditor agree that the listed conditions are met. Once the proposal is adequate for audit, the auditor audits it, drafts an advisory report, and seeks a signed rate agreement with the contractor. If the two sides do not agree, the report goes to the contracting officer, who resolves it.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A business unit outside the contracting-officer procedure submits a final indirect rate proposal. The auditor finds the proposal adequate, audits it, and reaches agreement with the contractor at a final overhead rate of 95 percent. Both sign the rate agreement, and the agreed rate is used to settle the unit's covered years.

## Never confuse

Auditor determination is not contracting officer determination. Here the auditor establishes the rate and signs the agreement with the contractor; under contracting officer determination, the contracting officer (or cognizant Federal agency official) is responsible for establishing the rate. The two are not fixed to separate populations, because the agreed-conditions route lets the auditor determine rates for some covered units. See [Final indirect cost rates](rte-co-final-indirect-rates.md).
