## Purpose

Shows who establishes final indirect cost rates by contracting officer determination, when the proposal is due, and what the negotiation record must contain.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-RTE-0103] |
| Codified citation | FAR 42.705-1 [@CL-RTE-0103] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 42.505-1 [@CL-RTE-0914; @CL-RTE-0918] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-RTE-0103] |
| Applies to | Business units whose final indirect cost rates are contracting officer determined [@CL-RTE-0101] |

Contracting officer determination shall be used for the cases that follow, with the indicated cognizant contracting officer (or cognizant Federal agency official) responsible for establishing the final indirect cost rates [@CL-RTE-9227]. For business units of a multidivisional corporation under a corporate administrative contracting officer, that officer is responsible for the determination, assisted as required by the administrative contracting officers assigned to the individual business units [@CL-RTE-0101]. In accordance with the Allowable Cost and Payment clause at 52.216-7, the contractor is required to submit an adequate final indirect cost rate proposal to the contracting officer (or cognizant Federal agency official) and to the cognizant auditor [@CL-RTE-9228; @CL-RTE-0103], and each contractor is required to submit the final indirect cost rate proposal within the six-month period following the expiration of each of its fiscal years [@CL-RTE-0104].

In the FAR's general definitions, a business unit is any segment of an organization, or an entire business organization that is not divided into segments [@CL-RTE-9063]. RFO FAR 2.101 defines a business unit in the same words as the codified definition [@CL-RTE-9087; @CL-RTE-9063]. The cognizant Federal agency is the Federal agency that, on behalf of all Federal agencies, is responsible for establishing final indirect cost rates and forward pricing rates, if applicable, and administering cost accounting standards for all contracts in a business unit [@CL-RTE-9064]. RFO FAR 2.101 defines the cognizant Federal agency in the same words as the codified definition [@CL-RTE-9088; @CL-RTE-9064].

## How it works

Business units not under the cognizance of a corporate administrative contracting officer but having a resident administrative contracting officer are also covered, with that officer responsible for the determination [@CL-RTE-9227; @CL-RTE-9138]. For this purpose, a nonresident administrative contracting officer is considered as resident if at least 75 percent of the administrative contracting officer's time is devoted to a single contractor [@CL-RTE-0102]. The contracting officer may grant reasonable extensions in writing, for exceptional circumstances only, when the contractor requests them in writing [@CL-RTE-0105]. Upon receipt of the proposal, the cognizant auditor will review the adequacy of the contractor's proposal for audit in support of negotiating final indirect cost rates and will provide a written description of any inadequacies to the contractor and contracting officer [@CL-RTE-9229; @CL-RTE-0106]. The contracting officer (or cognizant Federal agency official) shall head the Government negotiating team, which includes the cognizant auditor and technical or functional personnel as required [@CL-RTE-0107]. The cognizant contracting officer shall conduct negotiations and prepare a written indirect cost rate agreement conforming to the requirements of the contracts [@CL-RTE-9139; @CL-RTE-0110]. That officer shall also prepare, sign and place in the contractor general file a negotiation memorandum covering the disposition of significant matters in the advisory audit report [@CL-RTE-9140], the reconciliation of all costs questioned, with identification of items and amounts allowed or disallowed in the final settlement as well as the disposition of period costing or allocability issues [@CL-RTE-9141; @CL-RTE-0108], reasons why any recommendations of the auditor or other Government advisors were not followed, and identification of certified cost or pricing data submitted during the negotiations and relied upon in reaching a settlement [@CL-RTE-9230], and shall notify the contractor of the individual costs which were considered unallowable and the respective amounts of the disallowance [@CL-RTE-0109].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers shall use the RFO FAR part 42 text in place of the codified text, as Class Deviation 2026-O0050 directs, effective March 16, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9759] [@CL-CMP-9761]. RFO FAR 42.505-1(a) says contracting officer determination will be used for the following, with the indicated cognizant contracting officer (or cognizant Federal agency official) responsible for establishing the final indirect cost rates [@CL-RTE-0914]. RFO FAR 42.505-1(a)(1) keeps the corporate administrative contracting officer case, with that officer responsible for the determination and assisted, as required, by the administrative contracting officers assigned to the individual business units, but cites subpart 42.4 where the codified text cites subpart 42.6 [@CL-RTE-0915; @CL-RTE-0952; @CL-RTE-0101]. RFO FAR 42.505-1(a)(2) keeps the test that a nonresident administrative contracting officer is considered as resident if they spend at least 75 percent of their time devoted to a single contractor, and cites subpart 42.4 for resident officers [@CL-RTE-0916; @CL-RTE-0102]. RFO FAR 42.505-1(a)(3) provides that, for business units not included in paragraph (a)(1) or (a)(2), the contracting officer (or cognizant Federal agency official) will determine whether the rates will be contracting officer or auditor determined [@CL-RTE-0917]. RFO FAR 42.505-1(b)(1) says that, in accordance with the Allowable Cost and Payment clause at 52.216-7, the contractor must submit an adequate final indirect cost rate proposal to the cognizant contracting officer (or cognizant Federal agency official) and to the cognizant auditor, where the codified text says the contractor "is required to submit" it to the contracting officer [@CL-RTE-0918; @CL-RTE-0103]. RFO FAR 42.505-1(b)(1)(ii) keeps the six-month period following the expiration of each fiscal year, and keeps the rule that the contracting officer may grant, in writing, reasonable extensions, for exceptional circumstances only, when requested in writing by the contractor, with "must submit" in place of "is required to submit" [@CL-RTE-0919; @CL-RTE-0104; @CL-RTE-0105]. RFO FAR 42.505-1(b)(1)(iii)(A) says the cognizant auditor will review the adequacy of the contractor's proposal for audit in support of negotiating final indirect cost rates and a written description of any inadequacies to the contractor and contracting officer, where the codified sentence has "will provide" before "a written description" [@CL-RTE-0920; @CL-RTE-0106]. RFO FAR 42.505-1(b)(1)(iii)(B) says that, if the auditor and contractor are unable to resolve the proposal's inadequacies identified by the auditor, the auditor will elevate the issue to the contracting office to resolve the inadequacies [@CL-RTE-0921]. RFO FAR 42.505-1(b)(3) keeps the rule that the contracting officer (or cognizant Federal agency official) heads the Government negotiating team, which includes the cognizant auditor and technical or functional personnel as required [@CL-RTE-0922; @CL-RTE-0107]. Items (iii) and (iv) of RFO FAR 42.505-1(b)(3) say that contracting offices with significant dollar interest must be invited to participate in the negotiation and in the preliminary discussion of critical issues, and that parties that have provided a significant input to the Government position should be invited to attend [@CL-RTE-0949]. RFO FAR 42.505-1(b)(5) says the cognizant contracting officer must conduct negotiations and prepare a written indirect cost rate agreement conforming to the requirements of the contracts [@CL-RTE-0923; @CL-RTE-0110]. RFO FAR 42.505-1(b)(5)(iii) says the negotiation memorandum covers, among other items, the disposition of significant matters in the advisory audit report and the reconciliation of all costs questioned, with identification of items and amounts allowed or disallowed in the final settlement as well as the disposition of period costing or allocability issues [@CL-RTE-0924; @CL-RTE-0953; @CL-RTE-0108]. RFO FAR 42.505-1(b)(5)(v) keeps the item that the contractor is notified of the individual costs which were considered unallowable and the respective amounts of the disallowance [@CL-RTE-0925; @CL-RTE-0109].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor's fiscal year ends on 30 September and it files an adequate proposal by the following 31 March. The auditor finds it adequate, the team negotiates, and the memorandum records 120,000 of questioned costs with 90,000 allowed and 30,000 disallowed. The contractor is notified of the 30,000 disallowance and receives a written rate agreement.

## Never confuse

Under RFO FAR 42.505-1(a)(3), for business units not included in paragraph (a)(1) or (a)(2), the contracting officer (or cognizant Federal agency official) will determine whether the rates will be contracting officer or auditor determined [@CL-RTE-0917], and under contracting officer determination—unlike auditor determination on [Auditor-determined final indirect cost rates](rte-final-indirect-rate-auditor-determination.md)—the contracting officer (or cognizant Federal agency official) shall head the Government negotiating team [@CL-RTE-0107] and the cognizant contracting officer shall prepare a written indirect cost rate agreement conforming to the requirements of the contracts [@CL-RTE-9139]; the codified contracting officer determination procedure at FAR 42.705-1 [@CL-RTE-0103] corresponds to RFO FAR 42.505-1 [@CL-RTE-0914].
