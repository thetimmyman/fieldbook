## Purpose

Walks through each illustration in the Cost Accounting Standards Board rules of a change that does not meet the definition of a change to a cost accounting practice.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Cost Accounting Standards Board rules [@CL-RTE-0827] |
| Codified citation | 48 CFR 9903.302-4 [@CL-RTE-0827] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-RTE-0827] |
| Applies to | Illustrations of changes which do not meet the definition of a change to a cost accounting practice [@CL-RTE-0827] |

Interest rate: changes in the interest rate levels in the national economy have invalidated the prior actuarial assumption with respect to anticipated investment earnings [@CL-RTE-0863]. The pension plan administrators adopted an increased or decreased interest rate actuarial assumption, and the company allocated the resulting pension costs to all final cost objectives; adopting the increase (decrease) in the interest rate actuarial assumption is not a change in cost accounting practice [@CL-RTE-0827]. Benefit amount: the basic benefit rises from $8 to $10 per year of credited service, which increases the pension cost allocated to all final cost objectives [@CL-RTE-0828]. The increase in the amount of the benefits is not a change in cost accounting practice [@CL-RTE-0828]. New plan: a contractor that has never paid pensions establishes a pension plan for the first time, with first-year pension costs of $3.5 million [@CL-RTE-0829]. The initial adoption of an accounting practice for the first time incurrence of a cost is not a change in cost accounting practice [@CL-RTE-0829]. Terminated plan: after several years' experience, the plan was determined not to be attaining its objective, so it was terminated, and no future entitlements were paid; there was a termination of the Deferred Incentive Compensation Plan, and elimination of a cost is not a change in cost accounting practice [@CL-RTE-0830].

Eliminated segment: a contractor eliminates a segment that was operated for the purpose of doing research for development of products related to nuclear energy [@CL-RTE-0864]. The projects and expenses related to nuclear energy projects have been terminated, with no transfer and no further work in the area planned, and this is an elimination of cost and not a change in cost accounting practice [@CL-RTE-0831]. Asset life estimate: for a class of assets for which technological changes have rarely affected asset lives, the contractor starts with a 5-year average of historical lives to estimate future lives and then considers technological changes and likely use [@CL-RTE-0865]. For the past several years the process produced an estimated future life of 10 years, and this year a technological change leads to a predicted useful life of 7 years for the assets acquired this year [@CL-RTE-0832]. The change in estimate (not in method) is not a change in cost accounting practice, and the contractor has not changed the method or technique used to determine the estimate [@CL-RTE-0833]. Reorganization: the marketing department of a segment has reported directly to the general manager of the segment, and its costs have been combined as part of the segment's G&A expense pool [@CL-RTE-0866]. The company reorganizes and requires the marketing department to report directly to a vice president at corporate headquarters [@CL-RTE-0867]. The parties agree that the appropriate recognition of the beneficial or causal relationship between the costs of the marketing department and the segment is to continue to combine these costs as part of the segment's G&A expense pool [@CL-RTE-0868]. Thus, the organizational change has not resulted in a change in cost accounting practice [@CL-RTE-0834].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor starts a new training function and first incurs 40,000 of cost for it in the year. It sets up a new accounting practice for that cost. Like the new-plan illustration, the first adoption of an accounting practice for a first-incurred cost is not a change in cost accounting practice.
