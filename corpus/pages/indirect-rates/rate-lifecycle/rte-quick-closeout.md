## Purpose

Explains when a contracting officer may settle the direct and indirect costs of a physically complete contract before final rates exist, and why that settlement sets no precedent for other contracts.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-RTE-0151] |
| Codified citation | FAR 42.708 [@CL-RTE-0151] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-RTE-0151] |
| Applies to | Physically complete contracts, task orders and delivery orders that also pass the size and risk tests [@CL-RTE-0152] |

The contracting officer responsible for closeout settles the direct and indirect costs of the specific contract, task order or delivery order in advance of the final costs and indirect rates the ordinary closeout process would determine [@CL-RTE-0151].

The procedure needs a physically complete contract, unsettled amounts that are relatively insignificant, a risk assessment that supports its use, and agreement on a reasonable estimate of allocable dollars [@CL-RTE-0152; @CL-RTE-0153; @CL-RTE-0157; @CL-RTE-0154]. The unsettled total must not exceed the lesser of $1,000,000 or 10 percent of the total contract amount, and that risk assessment must cover the contractor's accounting, estimating and purchasing systems together with the auditors' concerns [@CL-RTE-0153; @CL-RTE-0154; @CL-RTE-0155].

## How it works

The contracting officer negotiates a settlement of the contract's direct and indirect costs ahead of the final costs and rates, so closeout moves ahead of the ordinary final rate determination [@CL-RTE-0151].

The ceiling is the lesser of $1,000,000 or 10 percent of the total contract amount, applied to the unsettled direct and indirect costs allocated to that one contract, task order or delivery order [@CL-RTE-0153; @CL-RTE-0154].

A risk assessment must support the procedure, covering the contractor's systems and the concerns of the cognizant contract auditors, and the parties must agree on a reasonable estimate of allocable dollars [@CL-RTE-0155; @CL-RTE-0157].

The resulting determination of final indirect costs is final for the contract it covers, and the rates used are not a binding precedent for other contracts [@CL-RTE-0158; @CL-RTE-0159].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A closed delivery order has a total amount of 2,000,000 and 150,000 of unsettled direct and indirect costs, of which the parties agree 120,000 is allocable. Ten percent of the total amount is 200,000, which is below the fixed limit, so 200,000 is the ceiling and the 150,000 of unsettled cost sits under it. The contracting officer may settle on the agreed estimate once the order is physically complete and the risk assessment supports the procedure.

## Never confuse

Quick closeout is not a rate agreement for the contractor's other work: the determination is final for the contract it covers, other contracts are not adjusted for over- or under-recoveries, and the rates used set no precedent [@CL-RTE-0158; @CL-RTE-0159]. It is also not the ordinary route that waits for final direct costs and indirect rates; see [Final indirect cost rate proposal](rte-final-indirect-rate-proposal.md) [@CL-RTE-0151].

## Company calibration

Whether each closing contract's unsettled direct and indirect costs fall under the quick-closeout ceiling, and where that contract sits among the open incurred cost years. [@Q-035]
