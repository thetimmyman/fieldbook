## Purpose

Explains when the contracting officer responsible for closeout shall negotiate the settlement of the direct and indirect costs of a contract, task order or delivery order in advance of final rates, and the rule that the rates used are not a binding precedent for other contracts.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-RTE-0151] |
| Codified citation | FAR 42.708 [@CL-RTE-0151] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 42.507 [@CL-RTE-0975; @CL-RTE-0977] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-RTE-0151] |
| Applies to | Physically complete contracts, task orders and delivery orders that also pass the size and risk tests [@CL-RTE-0152; @CL-RTE-0153; @CL-RTE-9121] |

The contracting officer responsible for closeout shall negotiate the settlement of the direct and indirect costs of the specific contract, task order or delivery order in advance of the determination of final direct costs and indirect rates set forth in 42.705, if the four conditions below are met [@CL-RTE-0151; @CL-RTE-9122].

The procedure needs a physically complete contract, task order or delivery order, unsettled amounts that are relatively insignificant, a risk assessment by the contracting officer who determines that use of the procedure is appropriate, and a condition that agreement can be reached on a reasonable estimate of allocable dollars; all four conditions apply [@CL-RTE-0152; @CL-RTE-0153; @CL-RTE-9121; @CL-RTE-0157; @CL-RTE-0154]. Cost amounts will be considered relatively insignificant when the total unsettled direct costs and indirect costs to be allocated to any one contract, task order, or delivery order does not exceed the lesser of $1,000,000 or 10 percent of the total contract, task order, or delivery order amount, and the risk assessment shall include consideration of the contractor's accounting, estimating and purchasing systems and other concerns of the cognizant contract auditors [@CL-RTE-0153; @CL-RTE-0154; @CL-RTE-0155].

## How it works

The contracting officer responsible for closeout shall negotiate a settlement of the contract's direct and indirect costs ahead of the determination of final direct costs and indirect rates, if the four conditions are met [@CL-RTE-0151; @CL-RTE-9122].

Cost amounts will be considered relatively insignificant when the total unsettled direct costs and indirect costs to be allocated to any one contract, task order, or delivery order does not exceed the lesser of $1,000,000 or 10 percent of the total contract, task order, or delivery order amount [@CL-RTE-0153; @CL-RTE-0154].

Another condition is that the contracting officer performs a risk assessment and determines that the use of the quick-closeout procedure is appropriate [@CL-RTE-9121]; the risk assessment shall include consideration of the contractor's accounting, estimating, and purchasing systems and other concerns of the cognizant contract auditors [@CL-RTE-0155]. A further condition is that agreement can be reached on a reasonable estimate of allocable dollars [@CL-RTE-0157]. The risk assessment shall also include any other pertinent information, such as the documented history of Federal Government approved indirect cost rate agreements, changes to the contractor's rate structure, volatility of rate fluctuations during affected periods, mergers or acquisitions, and special contract provisions limiting the contractor's recovery of otherwise allowable indirect costs under cost reimbursement or time-and-materials contracts [@CL-RTE-0155; @CL-RTE-0156; @CL-RTE-9145].

Determinations of final indirect costs under the quick-closeout procedure provided for by the Allowable Cost and Payment clause at 52.216-7 shall be final for the contract it covers and no adjustment shall be made to other contracts for over- or under-recoveries of costs allocated or allocable to the contract covered by the agreement [@CL-RTE-9235; @CL-RTE-0158]. Indirect cost rates used in the quick closeout of a contract shall not be considered a binding precedent when establishing the final indirect cost rates for other contracts [@CL-RTE-0159].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers shall use the RFO FAR part 42 text in place of the codified text, as Class Deviation 2026-O0050 directs, effective March 16, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9759] [@CL-CMP-9761]. RFO FAR 42.507 says the contracting officer responsible for contract closeout may negotiate the settlement of direct and indirect costs prior to the determination of final indirect cost rates, where the codified text says the contracting officer shall negotiate it in advance of the determination of final direct costs and indirect rates [@CL-RTE-0975; @CL-RTE-0151; @CL-RTE-9122]. It sets the conditions that the contract, task order or delivery order is physically complete and that the unsettled direct and indirect costs are relatively insignificant [@CL-RTE-0976; @CL-RTE-0152; @CL-RTE-0153]. It sets the limit at the lesser of $1,000,000 or 10 percent, but measures the 10 percent against the total contract amount, where the codified text says the total contract, task order, or delivery order amount [@CL-RTE-0977; @CL-RTE-0153; @CL-RTE-0154]. It says the contracting officer performs a risk assessment and determines quick-closeout procedures are appropriate, and that the assessment must include consideration of the contractor's accounting, estimating and purchasing systems and other concerns of the cognizant contract auditors [@CL-RTE-0978; @CL-RTE-0155], together with any other pertinent information, such as documented history of Federal Government approved indirect cost rate agreements, changes to the contractor's rate structure, volatility of rate fluctuations during affected periods, mergers or acquisitions, and special contract provisions limiting the contractor's recovery of otherwise allowable indirect costs under cost reimbursement or time-and-materials contracts [@CL-RTE-0979; @CL-RTE-0984]. It also sets the condition that agreement can be reached on a reasonable estimate of allocable dollars [@CL-RTE-0980; @CL-RTE-0157]. It says a determination of final indirect costs under the quick-closeout procedure provided for by the Allowable Cost and Payment clause at 52.216-7 is final for the contract it covers, that no adjustment will be made to other contracts for over- or under-recoveries of costs allocated or allocable to that contract, and that the indirect cost rates used will not be considered a binding precedent when establishing final indirect cost rates for other contracts [@CL-RTE-0981; @CL-RTE-0982; @CL-RTE-0983; @CL-RTE-0158; @CL-RTE-0159].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A closed delivery order has a total amount of 2,000,000 and 150,000 of unsettled direct and indirect costs, of which the parties agree 120,000 is allocable. Ten percent of the total amount is 200,000, which is below the fixed limit, so 200,000 is the ceiling and the 150,000 of unsettled cost sits under it. Under the codified text, the contracting officer shall negotiate the settlement on the agreed estimate once the order is physically complete and the risk assessment supports the procedure.

## Never confuse

The determination is final for the contract it covers and no adjustment shall be made to other contracts for over- or under-recoveries of costs allocated or allocable to the contract covered by the agreement [@CL-RTE-9235; @CL-RTE-0158], and the rates used in a quick closeout shall not be considered a binding precedent when establishing the final indirect cost rates for other contracts [@CL-RTE-0159]. For the final indirect cost rate proposal, see [Final indirect cost rate proposal](rte-final-indirect-rate-proposal.md).

FAR 42.708(a) says the contracting officer responsible for contract closeout shall negotiate the settlement if the conditions that follow are met [@CL-RTE-0151], while RFO FAR 42.507(a) says that officer may negotiate it if the conditions that follow are met [@CL-RTE-0975].

## Company calibration

Whether each closing contract's unsettled direct and indirect costs fall under the quick-closeout ceiling, and where that contract sits among the open incurred cost years. [@Q-035]
