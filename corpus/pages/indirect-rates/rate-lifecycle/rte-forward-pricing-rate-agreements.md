## Purpose

Explains how a forward pricing rate agreement is requested, negotiated and monitored, and what replaces it when no valid agreement exists.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-RTE-0160] |
| Codified citation | FAR 42.1701 [@CL-RTE-0160] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 42.1301 [@CL-RTE-0967; @CL-RTE-0969] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-RTE-0160] |
| Applies to | Normally only contractors with a significant volume of Government contract proposals [@CL-RTE-0163] |

Negotiation of a forward pricing rate agreement may be requested by the contracting officer or the contractor, or initiated by the administrative contracting officer, and the cognizant contract administration agency shall determine whether an FPRA will be established [@CL-RTE-0160; @CL-RTE-0161].

The administrative contracting officer should consider whether the benefits are commensurate with the effort of establishing and monitoring the agreement, and agreements should normally be negotiated only with contractors having a significant volume of Government contract proposals [@CL-RTE-0162; @CL-RTE-0163].

FAR 2.101 defines a forward pricing rate agreement as a written agreement negotiated between a contractor and the Government to make certain rates available during a specified period for use in pricing contracts or modifications [@CL-RTE-9076]. RFO FAR 2.101 defines a forward pricing rate agreement in the same words as the codified definition [@CL-RTE-9094; @CL-RTE-9076], and goes on to say that the rates in the agreement represent reasonable projections of specific costs that are not easily estimated for, identified with, or generated by a specific contract, contract end item, or task, and that these projections may include rates for such things as labor, indirect costs, material obsolescence and usage, spare parts provisioning, and material handling [@CL-RTE-9094; @CL-RTE-9113; @CL-RTE-9114]. A forward pricing rate recommendation is a rate set unilaterally by the administrative contracting officer for use by the Government in negotiations or other contract actions when forward pricing rate agreement negotiations have not been completed or when the contractor will not agree to a forward pricing rate agreement [@CL-RTE-9077].

## How it works

The administrative contracting officer shall obtain the contractor's forward pricing rate proposal and require it to include cost or pricing data that are accurate, complete and current as of the date of submission (but see 15.407-3(c)) [@CL-RTE-9143; @CL-RTE-0164].

Upon completing negotiations the administrative contracting officer shall prepare a price negotiation memorandum and forward copies of it and of the agreement to the cognizant auditor and to all contracting offices known to be affected [@CL-RTE-0165].

The agreement shall provide for cancellation at the option of either party and shall require the contractor to submit to the administrative contracting officer and the cognizant contract auditor any significant change in the cost or pricing data used to support it [@CL-RTE-0166].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers shall use the RFO FAR part 42 text in place of the codified text, as Class Deviation 2026-O0050 directs, effective March 16, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9759] [@CL-CMP-9761]. RFO FAR 42.1301 says the contracting officer, the administrative contracting officer or the contractor may initiate a request for an agreement [@CL-RTE-0967], and that agreements are normally negotiated only with contractors having a significant volume of Government contract proposals, where the codified text says they should normally be negotiated only with such contractors [@CL-RTE-0968; @CL-RTE-0163]. It says the administrative contracting officer must obtain the contractor's forward pricing rate proposal and require cost or pricing data that are accurate, complete and current as of the date of submission, but must not require a certification, where the codified text ends with a cross-reference to 15.407-3(c) [@CL-RTE-0970; @CL-RTE-0164]. It says the administrative contracting officer must prepare the price negotiation memorandum and forward copies of it and of the agreement to the cognizant auditor and to all contracting offices known to be affected, pointing to part 15 where the codified text points to 15.406-3 [@CL-RTE-0971; @CL-RTE-0165]. It says the agreement must provide for cancellation at the choice of either party and must require the contractor to submit any significant change in the supporting cost or pricing data to the administrative contracting officer and the cognizant contract auditor [@CL-RTE-0972; @CL-RTE-0166]. RFO FAR 42.1301 says the cognizant Federal agency should determine whether to establish an agreement by considering whether the benefits are commensurate with the effort of establishing and monitoring it [@CL-RTE-0969], where the codified text says the cognizant contract administration agency shall determine whether an agreement will be established and separately says the administrative contracting officer should consider whether the benefits are commensurate [@CL-RTE-0161; @CL-RTE-0162].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

Negotiators agree a forward pricing rate of 118 percent for one overhead pool, supported by a proposal whose data are current as of submission. A year later the contractor closes the plant that carried the pool, which is a significant change in the supporting data. The contractor must tell the administrative contracting officer and the cognizant auditor, and if the agreement is then invalid it should submit and negotiate a new proposal to reflect the changed conditions.

## Never confuse

If an FPRA has not been established or has been invalidated, the administrative contracting officer will issue a forward pricing rate recommendation to buying activities with documentation to assist negotiators [@CL-RTE-0167]. When an FPRA is invalid, the contractor should submit and negotiate a new proposal to reflect the changed conditions [@CL-RTE-0168].

RFO FAR 42.1301(d) says that when an FPRA is invalid, the contractor should submit and negotiate a new proposal to reflect the changed conditions [@CL-RTE-0973], and that if an FPRA has not been established or has been invalidated, the administrative contracting officer will issue a forward pricing rate recommendation to buying activities with documentation to assist negotiators [@CL-RTE-0974]. FAR 42.1701(a) says the cognizant contract administration agency shall determine whether an FPRA will be established [@CL-RTE-0161], while RFO FAR 42.1301 says the cognizant Federal agency should determine whether to establish an FPRA by considering whether the benefits to be derived from the agreement are commensurate with the effort of establishing and monitoring it [@CL-RTE-0969].
