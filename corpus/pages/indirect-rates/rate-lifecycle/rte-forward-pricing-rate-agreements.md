## Purpose

Explains how a forward pricing rate agreement is requested, negotiated and monitored, and what replaces it when no valid agreement exists.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-RTE-0160] |
| Codified citation | FAR 42.1701 [@CL-RTE-0160] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-RTE-0160] |
| Applies to | Normally only contractors with a significant volume of Government contract proposals [@CL-RTE-0163] |

Negotiation of a forward pricing rate agreement may be requested by the contracting officer or the contractor, or initiated by the administrative contracting officer, and the cognizant contract administration agency decides whether an agreement will be established [@CL-RTE-0160; @CL-RTE-0161].

The administrative contracting officer should consider whether the benefits are commensurate with the effort of establishing and monitoring the agreement, and agreements are normally negotiated only with contractors having a significant volume of Government contract proposals [@CL-RTE-0162; @CL-RTE-0163].

## How it works

The administrative contracting officer obtains the contractor's forward pricing rate proposal and requires cost or pricing data that are accurate, complete and current as of the date of submission, subject to the cross-reference to 15.407-3(c) [@CL-RTE-0164].

After negotiations the administrative contracting officer prepares a price negotiation memorandum and forwards copies of it and of the agreement to the cognizant auditor and to contracting offices known to be affected [@CL-RTE-0165].

The agreement allows either party to cancel it and requires the contractor to report any significant change in the supporting cost or pricing data to the administrative contracting officer and the cognizant auditor [@CL-RTE-0166].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

Negotiators agree a forward pricing rate of 118 percent for one overhead pool, supported by a proposal whose data are current as of submission. A year later the contractor closes the plant that carried the pool, which is a significant change in the supporting data. The contractor must tell the administrative contracting officer and the cognizant auditor, and if the agreement is then invalid it should submit and negotiate a new proposal to reflect the changed conditions.

## Never confuse

A forward pricing rate recommendation is not an agreement: when no agreement has been established or an established one has been invalidated, the recommendation goes to buying activities with documentation to assist negotiators [@CL-RTE-0167]. An invalid agreement is not simply carried forward either, because the contractor should submit and negotiate a new proposal to reflect the changed conditions [@CL-RTE-0168].
