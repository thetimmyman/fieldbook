## Purpose

Shows how home office expenses are grouped, how centralized service functions are allocated to segments, and which formula applies to residual expenses.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | CAS [@CL-RTE-0680] |
| Codified citation | 48 CFR 9904.403-50 [@CL-RTE-0680; @CL-RTE-0682] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-RTE-0680] |
| Applies to | Home office expenses allocated to segments [@CL-RTE-0682] |

Ordinarily each home office service or management function has to be separately identified for allocation by an appropriate technique [@CL-RTE-0680]. Different functions need not be identified and allocated separately if allocation in accordance with the relevant requirements of 9904.403-40(b) can be made using a common allocation base [@CL-RTE-0681].

Centralized service functions are governed by a hierarchy of preferable techniques, and the preferred representation is a measure of the activity of the organization performing the function [@CL-RTE-0682]. Residual expenses are allocated to all segments by means of a base representative of the total activity of those segments, except where paragraph (c)(2) or (3) of 9904.403-40 applies [@CL-CMP-0555]. Where residual expenses are required to be allocated pursuant to 9904.403-40(c)(2), the three factor formula must be used [@CL-RTE-0686]. Paragraph (c)(2) of 9904.403-40 lists these percentages: 3.35 percent of the first $100 million, 0.95 percent of the next $200 million, 0.30 percent of the next $2.7 billion and 0.20 percent of all amounts over $3 billion [@CL-CMP-0556]. This card does not state the test they feed, which is on the CAS home office card.

## How it works

Supporting functions are usually labor-oriented, machine-oriented or space-oriented, and measures of their activities ordinarily can be expressed in labor hours, machine hours or square footage [@CL-RTE-0750]. The costs of these functions are allocated by a rate, such as a rate per labor hour, per machine hour or per square foot, unless such measures are unavailable or impractical to ascertain [@CL-RTE-0683]. In those cases the basis is a measurement of the output of the supporting function [@CL-RTE-0684]. Where neither activity nor output can be practically measured, a surrogate for the beneficial or causal relationship must be selected [@CL-RTE-0685].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A home office personnel department costs 60,000 a year. It both runs personnel services and sets personnel policy for the same segments, so one base serves both functions and they need not be split. Segment X has 100 employees and Segment Y has 200. On the number of personnel as the base, Segment X is allocated 20,000 and Segment Y is allocated 40,000.

## Never confuse

A centralized service function is not a residual expense. A service function follows the hierarchy of activity, then output, then a surrogate [@CL-RTE-0682; @CL-RTE-0684; @CL-RTE-0685]. Residual expenses use the three factor formula where they are required to be allocated pursuant to 9904.403-40(c)(2) [@CL-RTE-0686]; otherwise they follow a base representative of total segment activity, unless paragraph (c)(3) applies [@CL-CMP-0555]. For the underlying allocation requirements, see [the CAS home office card](../../compliance/cas-allowability/cmp-cas-403-home-office-allocation.md).
