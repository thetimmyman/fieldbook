## Purpose

Shows what counts as general and administrative expense, when its pool may be combined with other expenses, and which cost input bases may spread it.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | CAS [@CL-RTE-0675] |
| Codified citation | 48 CFR 9904.410-50(d) [@CL-RTE-0675] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 2.101 [@CL-RTE-9103] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-RTE-0675] |
| Applies to | Business unit general and administrative expense pools [@CL-RTE-0671; @CL-RTE-0673] |

General and administrative (G&A) expense means any management, financial, and other expense which is incurred by or allocated to a business unit and which is for the general management and administration of the business unit as a whole, and G&A expense does not include those management expenses whose beneficial or causal relationship to cost objectives can be more directly measured by a base other than a cost input base representing the total activity of a business unit during a cost accounting period [@CL-RTE-0671; @CL-RTE-0672].

The G&A expense pool may be combined with other expenses for allocation to final cost objectives provided that the allocation base used for the combined pool is appropriate both for the allocation of the G&A expense pool under 48 CFR 9904.410 and for the allocation of the other expenses, and provision is made to identify the components and total of the G&A expense pool separately from the other expenses in the combined pool [@CL-RTE-0673; @CL-RTE-0674]. Expenses which are not G&A expenses and are insignificant in amount may be included in the G&A expense pool for allocation to final cost objectives [@CL-RTE-0679].

FAR 2.101 defines general and administrative (G&A) expense as any management, financial, and other expense which is incurred by or allocated to a business unit and which is for the general management and administration of the business unit as a whole, and says that G&A expense does not include those management expenses whose beneficial or causal relationship to cost objectives can be more directly measured by a base other than a cost input base representing the total activity of a business unit during a cost accounting period [@CL-RTE-9078; @CL-RTE-9118]. RFO FAR 2.101 defines general and administrative (G&A) expense as any management, financial, and other expense which is incurred by or allocated to a business unit and which is for the general management and administration of the business unit as a whole, and says that G&A expense does not include those management expenses whose beneficial or causal relationship to cost objectives can be more directly measured by a base other than a cost input base representing the total activity of a business unit during a cost accounting period [@CL-RTE-9103; @CL-RTE-9115].

## How it works

The cost input base selected to represent the total activity of a business unit during a cost accounting period may be total cost input, value-added cost input or single element cost input, and the determination of which cost input base best represents the total activity of a business unit must be judged on the basis of the circumstances of each business unit [@CL-RTE-0675; @CL-RTE-0013]. A total cost input base is generally acceptable as an appropriate measure of the total activity of a business unit [@CL-RTE-0677]. Value-added cost input shall be used as an allocation base where inclusion of material and subcontract costs would significantly distort the allocation of the G&A expense pool in relation to the benefits received and where costs other than direct labor are significant measures of total activity, and a value-added cost input base is total cost input less material and subcontract costs [@CL-RTE-0676; @CL-RTE-0014]. A single element base is inappropriate where it is an insignificant part of the total cost of some of the final cost objectives [@CL-RTE-0678].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A business unit has a general and administrative pool of 120,000. Total cost input is 2,000,000, of which 1,200,000 is material and subcontract cost, so value-added cost input is 800,000. Contract A has total cost input of 200,000, of which 150,000 is material, so its value-added input is 50,000. On total cost input the rate is 6 percent and Contract A absorbs 12,000. On value-added input the rate is 15 percent and it absorbs 7,500. The gap shows the distortion that material-heavy contracts can suffer under a total cost input base.

## Never confuse

G&A expense does not include those management expenses whose beneficial or causal relationship to cost objectives can be more directly measured by a base other than a cost input base representing the total activity of a business unit during a cost accounting period [@CL-RTE-0672]. The G&A expense pool may be combined with other expenses for allocation to final cost objectives provided that the allocation base used for the combined pool is appropriate both for the allocation of the G&A expense pool under 48 CFR 9904.410 and for the allocation of the other expenses, and provision is made to identify the components and total of the G&A expense pool separately from the other expenses in the combined pool [@CL-RTE-0673; @CL-RTE-0674]. For the allocation rule on final cost objectives, see [the CAS general and administrative card](../../compliance/cas-allowability/cmp-cas-410-ga-allocation.md).
