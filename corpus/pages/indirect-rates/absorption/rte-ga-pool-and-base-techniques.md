## Purpose

Shows what counts as general and administrative expense, when its pool may be combined with other expenses, and which cost input bases may spread it.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | CAS [@CL-RTE-0675] |
| Codified citation | 48 CFR 9904.410-50(d) [@CL-RTE-0675] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 2.101 [@CL-RTE-9103] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-RTE-0675] |
| Applies to | Business unit general and administrative expense pools [@CL-RTE-0671] |

General and administrative expense means any management, financial or other expense incurred by or allocated to a business unit for the general management and administration of the business unit as a whole [@CL-RTE-0671]. It does not include management expenses whose beneficial or causal relationship to cost objectives can be more directly measured by a base other than a cost input base representing the total activity of the business unit [@CL-RTE-0672].

The pool may be combined with other expenses for allocation to final cost objectives only if the base used for the combined pool is appropriate both for allocating the pool and for allocating the other expenses and provision is made to identify the components and total of the pool separately from the other expenses in the combined pool [@CL-RTE-0673; @CL-RTE-0674]. Insignificant expenses that are not general and administrative expenses may be included in the pool [@CL-RTE-0679].

FAR 2.101 defines general and administrative expense as any management, financial, and other expense which is incurred by or allocated to a business unit and which is for the general management and administration of the business unit as a whole [@CL-RTE-9078]. The RFO text of FAR 2.101 has the same sentence word for word [@CL-RTE-9103; @CL-RTE-9078].

## How it works

The base chosen to represent total activity may be total cost input, value-added cost input or single element cost input [@CL-RTE-0675]. A total cost input base is generally acceptable [@CL-RTE-0677]. Value-added cost input shall be used where including material and subcontract costs would significantly distort the allocation in relation to benefits received and costs other than direct labor are significant measures of total activity [@CL-RTE-0676]. A single element base is inappropriate where it is an insignificant part of the total cost of some final cost objectives [@CL-RTE-0678].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A business unit has a general and administrative pool of 120,000. Total cost input is 2,000,000, of which 1,200,000 is material and subcontract cost, so value-added cost input is 800,000. Contract A has total cost input of 200,000, of which 150,000 is material, so its value-added input is 50,000. On total cost input the rate is 6 percent and Contract A absorbs 12,000. On value-added input the rate is 15 percent and it absorbs 7,500. The gap shows the distortion that material-heavy contracts can suffer under a total cost input base.

## Never confuse

General and administrative expense is not every management expense. A management expense that a base other than total activity measures more directly sits outside it [@CL-RTE-0672]. A combined pool is also not an unlabelled mix: provision is made to identify the pool's components and total inside it [@CL-RTE-0673; @CL-RTE-0674]. For the allocation rule on final cost objectives, see [the CAS general and administrative card](../../compliance/cas-allowability/cmp-cas-410-ga-allocation.md).

## Company calibration

Which cost input base the company uses for its general and administrative pool, and whether that pool is combined with other expenses. [@Q-024]
