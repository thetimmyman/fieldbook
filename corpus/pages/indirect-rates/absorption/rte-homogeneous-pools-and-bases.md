## Purpose

Shows when an indirect cost pool is homogeneous and how the allocation base is chosen for pools that hold management or supervision costs, pools that do not, and pools that benefit one another.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | CAS [@CL-RTE-0661] |
| Codified citation | 48 CFR 9904.418-50(b) to (e) [@CL-RTE-0661; @CL-RTE-0668] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-RTE-0661] |
| Applies to | Indirect cost pools [@CL-RTE-0661] |

An indirect cost pool is homogeneous if each significant activity whose costs are included therein has the same or a similar beneficial or causal relationship to cost objectives as the other activities whose costs are included in the cost pool [@CL-RTE-0661]. It is also homogeneous if the allocation of the costs of the activities included in the cost pool results in an allocation to cost objectives which is not materially different from the allocation that would result if the costs of the activities were allocated separately [@CL-RTE-0662]. A homogeneous indirect cost pool shall include all indirect costs identified with the activity to which the pool relates [@CL-RTE-0663].

The costs of the management or supervision of activities involving direct labor or direct material costs do not have a direct and definitive relationship to the benefiting cost objectives and cannot be allocated on measures of a specific beneficial or causal relationship, and in that circumstance the base selected to measure the allocation of the pooled costs to cost objectives shall be a base representative of the activity being managed or supervised [@CL-RTE-0664; @CL-RTE-0665].

## How it works

For an indirect cost pool which includes a material amount of the costs of management or supervision of activities involving direct labor or direct material costs, the base used to represent the activity being managed or supervised shall be determined by the criteria in 48 CFR 9904.418-50(d)(2), all significant elements of the selected base shall be included, and a direct labor hour base or direct labor cost base shall be used, whichever in the aggregate is more likely to vary in proportion to the costs included in the cost pool being allocated, except where one of three alternative bases applies [@CL-RTE-9131; @CL-RTE-0016; @CL-RTE-0666]: a machine-hour base, which is appropriate if the costs in the cost pool are comprised predominantly of facility-related costs, such as depreciation, maintenance, and utilities; a units-of-production base, which is appropriate if there is common production of comparable units [@CL-RTE-0667]; or a material cost base, which is appropriate if the activity being managed or supervised is a material-related activity [@CL-RTE-0017]. Homogeneous indirect cost pools that do not include material amounts of the costs of management or supervision of activities involving direct labor or direct material costs have a direct and definitive relationship between the activities in the pool and benefiting cost objectives, and the pooled costs shall be allocated using an appropriate measure of resource consumption [@CL-RTE-9132; @CL-RTE-0668]. If consumption measures are unavailable or impractical to ascertain, the next best representation of the beneficial or causal relationship for allocation is a measure of the output of the activities of the indirect cost pool [@CL-RTE-0669]. Allocation of indirect cost pools which benefit one another may be accomplished by use of (i) the cross-allocation (reciprocal) method, (ii) the sequential method, or (iii) another method the results of which approximate those achieved by either of the methods in subdivisions (e)(4)(i) or (e)(4)(ii) of 48 CFR 9904.418-50 [@CL-RTE-0670; @CL-RTE-0018].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A plant pool of 90,000 is mostly depreciation, maintenance and utilities. The business unit has 3,000 machine hours in total, so the rate is 30 per machine hour. Contract A uses 600 machine hours and absorbs 18,000. A direct labor hour base would give a different answer, but the machine-hour base fits a pool of facility-related costs.
