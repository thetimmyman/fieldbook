## Purpose

Shows how indirect costs are grouped into pools and spread over an allocation base, and what period the allocation covers.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-RTE-0206] |
| Codified citation | FAR 31.203 [@CL-RTE-0206] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 31.203 [@CL-RTE-0879; @CL-RTE-0880; @CL-RTE-0881; @CL-RTE-0882; @CL-RTE-0883; @CL-RTE-0884; @CL-RTE-0885; @CL-RTE-0886; @CL-RTE-0887; @CL-RTE-0888; @CL-RTE-0889; @CL-RTE-0890] |

After direct costs have been determined and charged directly to the contract or other work, indirect costs are those remaining to be allocated to intermediate or two or more final cost objectives [@CL-RTE-0207]. No final cost objective shall have allocated to it as an indirect cost any cost, if other costs incurred for the same purpose, in like circumstances, have been included as a direct cost [@CL-RTE-0208]. The contractor builds logical cost groupings, each with an allocation base common to the objectives it serves [@CL-RTE-0209; @CL-RTE-0210].

Once an appropriate base has been accepted it may not be fragmented by removing individual elements, and every item properly in the base bears its pro rata share whether or not it is an accepted Government contract cost [@CL-RTE-0211; @CL-RTE-0212]. For contracts subject to full or modified CAS coverage, the contractor follows the criteria and guidance in the CAS rule on cost accounting periods when selecting the periods used to allocate indirect costs [@CL-RTE-0260]. For other contracts, the base period is the contractor's fiscal year used for financial reporting in accordance with generally accepted accounting principles [@CL-RTE-0213]. That year will normally be 12 months, but a different period may be appropriate, for example after a change in fiscal year caused by a business combination [@CL-RTE-0261].

FAR 2.101 defines an indirect cost as any cost not directly identified with a single final cost objective, but identified with two or more final cost objectives or with at least one intermediate cost objective [@CL-RTE-9075]. The RFO model text of FAR 2.101 defines an indirect cost in the same words as the codified definition [@CL-RTE-9093; @CL-RTE-9075].

## How it works

The contractor first charges direct costs, then groups what remains into logical pools by the reason the costs were incurred. Each pool gets a base shared by the objectives it benefits, and the base allocates by benefit. The base is protected from fragmentation, and every item in it carries its share of indirect cost. The allocation runs over the cost accounting period: for contracts not under full or modified CAS coverage, that is the contractor's fiscal year used for financial reporting [@CL-RTE-0213].

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 31 text in place of the codified text [@CL-CMP-9350], effective immediately under Revision 1 signed on 2026-08-04 [@CL-CMP-9351]. RFO FAR 31.203 keeps the rules that indirect costs are those remaining after direct costs are charged [@CL-RTE-0881], that a cost cannot be indirect if its same-purpose, like-circumstances twins are direct [@CL-RTE-0882], that the contractor accumulates indirect costs in logical cost groupings [@CL-RTE-0883] with a common allocation base [@CL-RTE-0884] that allocates on the basis of benefits [@CL-RTE-0885], and that an accepted base is not fragmented [@CL-RTE-0886] and every proper item bears its pro rata share [@CL-RTE-0887]. For contracts subject to full or modified CAS coverage, paragraph (g)(1) keeps the cost accounting period criteria in 48 CFR 9904.406 [@CL-RTE-0888]. Two points are worded differently. Paragraph (a)(2) says that for all other contracts paragraphs (b) through (h) apply [@CL-RTE-0880], where the codified paragraph (a) says the applicable CAS provisions in paragraphs (b) through (h) apply [@CL-RTE-0206]. Paragraph (g)(2) states the fiscal-year base period without the codified opening words limiting it to contracts other than those under paragraph (g)(1) [@CL-RTE-0889; @CL-RTE-0213], and the 12-month sentence becomes paragraph (g)(3) [@CL-RTE-0890].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A company has 100,000 of overhead in one pool after direct costs are charged. The pool's allocation base is total direct labor, which is 500,000 across all jobs. One contract carries 50,000 of that direct labor, so it absorbs 10,000 of overhead. The base keeps every direct-labor dollar in it, allowed or not, and each bears its pro rata share.

## Never confuse

A pool is not the same as an allocation base. A pool groups the costs to be spread, while the base is the measure of benefit that spreads them. See [Direct costs](rte-direct-costs.md) for the parallel direct-versus-indirect consistency rule. The codified fiscal-year base period applies to contracts other than those subject to full or modified CAS coverage [@CL-RTE-0213], while the RFO paragraph (g)(2) states the fiscal-year base period without that limit [@CL-RTE-0889].
