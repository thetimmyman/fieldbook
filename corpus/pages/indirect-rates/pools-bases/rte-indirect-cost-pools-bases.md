## Purpose

Shows how indirect costs are grouped into pools and spread over an allocation base, and what period the allocation covers.

## The rule

| Field | Value |

| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-RTE-0206] |
| Codified citation | FAR 31.203 [@CL-RTE-0206] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-RTE-0206] |
| Applies to | Contracts under full CAS coverage, and all other contracts through the section's own CAS provisions [@CL-RTE-0206] |

Indirect costs are the costs remaining after direct costs are determined and charged directly [@CL-RTE-0207]. A cost may not be indirect to a final cost objective if its same-purpose, like-circumstances twins are direct to that or any other final cost objective [@CL-RTE-0208]. The contractor builds logical cost groupings, each with an allocation base common to the objectives it serves [@CL-RTE-0209; @CL-RTE-0210].

Once a base is accepted it may not be fragmented, and every item properly in the base bears its pro rata share whether or not it is an accepted Government contract cost [@CL-RTE-0211; @CL-RTE-0212]. For contracts subject to full or modified CAS coverage, the contractor follows the criteria and guidance in the CAS rule on cost accounting periods when selecting the periods used to allocate indirect costs [@CL-RTE-0260]. For other contracts, the base period is the contractor's fiscal year used for financial reporting in accordance with generally accepted accounting principles [@CL-RTE-0213]. That year will normally be 12 months, but a different period may be appropriate, for example after a change in fiscal year caused by a business combination [@CL-RTE-0261].

## How it works

The contractor first charges direct costs, then groups what remains into logical pools by the reason the costs were incurred. Each pool gets a base shared by the objectives it benefits, and the base allocates by benefit. The base is protected from fragmentation, and every item in it carries its share of indirect cost. The allocation runs over the cost accounting period: for contracts not under full or modified CAS coverage, that is the contractor's fiscal year used for financial reporting [@CL-RTE-0213].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A company has 100,000 of overhead in one pool after direct costs are charged. The pool's allocation base is total direct labor, which is 500,000 across all jobs. One contract carries 50,000 of that direct labor, so it absorbs 10,000 of overhead. The base keeps every direct-labor dollar in it, allowed or not, and each bears its pro rata share.

## Never confuse

A pool is not the same as an allocation base. A pool groups the costs to be spread, while the base is the measure of benefit that spreads them. See [Direct costs](rte-direct-costs.md) for the parallel direct-versus-indirect consistency rule.
