## Purpose

Shows what goes into an independent research and development or bid and proposal project, how the pools are formed, and which base spreads them at a home office and at a business unit.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | CAS [@CL-RTE-0688] |
| Codified citation | 48 CFR 9904.420-50 [@CL-RTE-0688] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-RTE-0688] |
| Applies to | IR&D and B&P projects and the pools that hold them [@CL-RTE-0688] |

IR&D and B&P project costs shall include costs that would be treated as direct costs of a final cost objective if incurred in like circumstances for it [@CL-RTE-0687]. They also include the overhead costs of productive activities and other indirect costs related to the project, based on the contractor's cost accounting practice or applicable Cost Accounting Standards for allocation of indirect costs [@CL-RTE-0753]. The cost pools for a segment consist of the project costs plus allocable home office IR&D and B&P costs [@CL-RTE-0688]. When the costs of individual efforts are not material, they may be accumulated in one or more projects within each of the two types of effort [@CL-RTE-0689].

At a home office, projects identifiable with a specific segment have their costs allocated to that segment [@CL-RTE-0690]. The costs of all other home office projects are allocated among all segments by the same base the company uses for residual expenses, subject to a proviso [@CL-RTE-0690; @CL-RTE-0691]. Under the proviso, the Government and the contractor may agree to a special allocation where a particular segment receives significantly more or less benefit from the costs than allocating them on the base would reflect [@CL-RTE-0691; @CL-RTE-0693].

## How it works

Costs are accumulated by project, and the pools for a segment are formed from the project costs plus allocable home office costs [@CL-RTE-0688]. Home office projects identified with a segment go to that segment, and the rest go on the residual expense base [@CL-RTE-0690; @CL-RTE-0691]. At a business unit, pools not allocated under the earlier paragraph on projects that benefit more than one segment go to all final cost objectives of the business unit by the same base the unit uses for general and administrative expense [@CL-RTE-0692]. A special allocation may again be agreed where a final cost objective receives significantly more or less benefit than the allocation would reflect [@CL-RTE-0754]. The home office base is therefore the residual expense base, and the business unit base is the general and administrative base [@CL-RTE-0691; @CL-RTE-0692].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A home office has two IR&D projects. Project 1 costs 40,000 and is identified with Segment X, so Segment X is allocated 40,000. Project 2 costs 100,000 and is not identified with one segment. The residual expense base puts 70 percent on Segment X and 30 percent on Segment Y, so Project 2 allocates 70,000 to Segment X and 30,000 to Segment Y.

## Never confuse

The home office base is not the business unit base. Home office projects not tied to one segment follow the base used for residual expenses [@CL-RTE-0691]. Business unit pools not allocated under the earlier paragraph on projects that benefit more than one segment follow the base used for general and administrative expense [@CL-RTE-0692].
