## Purpose

Explains the three traditional indirect cost pools—fringe benefits, overhead, and general and administrative expenses—and the allocation bases and regulatory criteria that govern how indirect costs are accumulated and distributed to cost objectives.

## The rule

Indirect costs are accumulated by logical cost groupings, giving due consideration to the reasons for incurring the costs [@CL-RTE-0209]. Each cost grouping is determined to permit an allocation base that is common to all cost objectives to which the grouping is allocated [@CL-RTE-0210], and the base selected shall allocate the grouping on the basis of the benefits accruing to intermediate and final cost objectives [@CL-RTE-0019]. Indirect costs are the costs remaining to be allocated to intermediate or two or more final cost objectives, after direct costs have been determined and charged directly to the contract or other work [@CL-RTE-0207]. No final cost objective shall have allocated to it as an indirect cost any cost, if other costs incurred for the same purpose, in like circumstances, have been included as a direct cost of that or any other final cost objective [@CL-RTE-0208; @CL-RTE-9134]. A business unit must have a written statement of accounting policies and practices for classifying costs as direct or indirect, consistently applied [@CL-CMP-0216]. Once an accepted base for allocating indirect costs exists, the contractor must not fragment the base by removing individual elements [@CL-RTE-0211], and all items properly includable in an indirect cost base bear a pro rata share of indirect costs, whether or not they are accepted as Government contract costs [@CL-RTE-0212]. For contracts subject to full or modified CAS coverage, the contractor follows the criteria and guidance in 48 CFR 9904.406 for selecting the cost accounting periods used in allocating indirect costs [@CL-RTE-0260], and for contracts other than those subject to full or modified CAS coverage, the base period for allocating indirect costs is the contractor's fiscal year used for financial reporting purposes in accordance with generally accepted accounting principles [@CL-RTE-0213].

Fringe benefits are allowances and services provided by the contractor to its employees as compensation in addition to regular wages and salaries [@CL-RTE-9280]. Fringe benefits include the cost of vacations, sick leave, holidays, military leave, employee insurance, and supplemental unemployment benefit plans [@CL-RTE-9281]. Except as provided otherwise in FAR subpart 31.2, the costs of fringe benefits are allowable to the extent that they are reasonable and required by law, employer-employee agreement, or an established contractor policy [@CL-RTE-9282]. In practice, many contractors accumulate fringe benefits in an indirect cost pool and allocate them using a direct labor base [@CL-RTE-9283].

Indirect costs are accumulated in indirect cost pools that are homogeneous [@CL-CMP-0217], and pooled costs are allocated to cost objectives in reasonable proportion to the beneficial or causal relationship of the pooled costs to the cost objectives [@CL-CMP-0218]. An indirect cost pool is homogeneous if each significant activity whose costs are included in it has the same or a similar beneficial or causal relationship to cost objectives as the other activities in the pool [@CL-RTE-0661], or if allocating the costs of its activities together gives an allocation to cost objectives that is not materially different from allocating those costs separately [@CL-RTE-0662]. A homogeneous indirect cost pool shall include all indirect costs identified with the activity to which the pool relates [@CL-RTE-0663]. The costs of management or supervision of activities involving direct labor or direct material costs have no direct and definitive relationship to the benefiting cost objectives and cannot be allocated on measures of a specific beneficial or causal relationship [@CL-RTE-0664], and where management or supervision costs cannot be allocated on measures of a specific beneficial or causal relationship, the base selected to measure the allocation of the pooled costs shall be a base representative of the activity being managed or supervised [@CL-RTE-0665]. A direct labor hour base or direct labor cost base shall be used, whichever in the aggregate is more likely to vary in proportion to the costs in the pool being allocated, except where one of the listed other bases applies [@CL-RTE-0666]. A machine-hour base is appropriate if the pool's costs are predominantly facility-related costs such as depreciation, maintenance and utilities, and a units-of-production base is appropriate if there is common production of comparable units [@CL-RTE-0667]. Homogeneous indirect cost pools that do not include material amounts of the costs of management or supervision of activities involving direct labor or direct material costs have a direct and definitive relationship to benefiting cost objectives, and the pooled costs shall be allocated using an appropriate measure of resource consumption [@CL-RTE-0668]; if consumption measures are unavailable or impractical to ascertain, the next best representation is a measure of the output of the activities of the indirect cost pool [@CL-RTE-0669], and if neither resources consumed nor output can be measured practically, a surrogate that varies in proportion to the services received shall be used [@CL-CMP-8203]. Indirect cost pools which benefit one another may be allocated by the cross-allocation (reciprocal) method, the sequential method, or another method whose results approximate those achieved by either of those methods [@CL-RTE-0670]. Indirect cost pools that include material amounts of the costs of management or supervision of activities involving direct labor or direct material costs shall be allocated to final cost objectives and goods produced for stock or product inventory [@CL-RTE-9285], to independent research and development and bid and proposal projects, and to process cost centers [@CL-RTE-9286], as well as to goods or services produced or acquired for other segments and to self-construction, fabrication, betterment, improvement, or installation of tangible capital assets [@CL-RTE-9287].

Business unit general and administrative expenses are grouped in a separate indirect cost pool and allocated only to final cost objectives [@CL-CMP-0213]. General and administrative expense means any management, financial and other expense that is incurred by or allocated to a business unit and is for the general management and administration of the business unit as a whole [@CL-RTE-0671], and does not include management expenses whose beneficial or causal relationship to cost objectives can be more directly measured by a base other than a cost input base representing the total activity of the business unit [@CL-RTE-0672]. Except for final cost objectives subject to special allocation under CAS 9904.410-40(b)(2), the G&A expense pool of a business unit for a cost accounting period is allocated to final cost objectives of that period by a cost input base representing the business unit's total activity [@CL-CMP-0214]. Final cost objectives that receive benefits significantly different from other final cost objectives get their G&A expense allocation by special allocation [@CL-CMP-0215]. The cost input base selected to represent the total activity of a business unit during a cost accounting period may be total cost input, value-added cost input or single element cost input [@CL-RTE-0675]. A total cost input base is generally acceptable as an appropriate measure of the total activity of a business unit [@CL-RTE-0677]. Value-added cost input shall be used as the allocation base where including material and subcontract costs would significantly distort the allocation of the pool in relation to the benefits received and where costs other than direct labor are significant measures of total activity [@CL-RTE-0676], and a value-added cost input base is total cost input less material and subcontract costs [@CL-RTE-9295]. A single element base is inappropriate where it is an insignificant part of the total cost of some of the final cost objectives [@CL-RTE-0678]. The general and administrative expense pool may be combined with other expenses for allocation to final cost objectives provided that the base used for the combined pool is appropriate for both the pool and the other expenses, and provision is made to identify the components and total of the general and administrative expense pool separately from the other expenses in the combined pool [@CL-RTE-0673; @CL-RTE-0674]. Expenses which are not general and administrative expenses and are insignificant in amount may be included in the general and administrative expense pool for allocation to final cost objectives [@CL-RTE-0679].

## How it works

An adequate indirect cost rate proposal shall include a summary of all claimed indirect expense rates, including pool, base, and calculated indirect rate [@CL-RTE-9290]. Each indirect cost rate is calculated by dividing the claimed indirect cost pool by its corresponding allocation base [@CL-RTE-9290]. An adequate indirect cost rate proposal also includes claimed allocation bases, by element of cost, used to distribute indirect costs [@CL-RTE-9294], a schedule of claimed overhead expenses by element of cost for each final indirect cost pool [@CL-RTE-9292], and a schedule of claimed general and administrative expenses as a final indirect cost pool [@CL-RTE-9291]. Intermediate indirect cost pools may be used to accumulate functional indirect costs before distribution to final pools: for example, an adequate proposal includes occupancy expenses as an intermediate indirect cost pool, with a schedule of claimed expenses and expense reallocation to final indirect cost pools [@CL-RTE-9293].

In a multi-tier indirect rate structure, an indirect pool's allocation base can include allocations from other indirect pools. Cost input means the cost, except G&A expenses, which for contract costing purposes is allocable to the production of goods and services during a cost accounting period [@CL-RTE-9288]. Under CAS 410, cost input shall include those expenses which by operation of the standard are excluded from the G&A expense pool and are not part of a combined pool of G&A expenses and other expenses allocated using the same allocation base [@CL-RTE-9289]. Because cost input includes allocable indirect costs other than G&A, a total cost input base used to allocate the G&A expense pool includes cost input that already carries fringe benefits and overhead allocations [@CL-RTE-9288; @CL-RTE-9289; @CL-RTE-0675; @CL-RTE-0677]. In practice, fringe benefits are allocated to direct labor [@CL-RTE-9283], overhead is allocated to labor costs or direct labor [@CL-RTE-0666], and the resulting burdened labor, direct materials, and allocated overhead are combined into total cost input to serve as the allocation base for G&A [@CL-RTE-9288; @CL-RTE-9289; @CL-RTE-0677]. Once an accepted base exists, the contractor must not fragment the base by removing individual elements, and all items properly includable in an indirect cost base must bear their pro rata share of indirect costs whether allowable or not [@CL-RTE-0211; @CL-RTE-0212]. See [Indirect costs: pools and allocation bases](rte-indirect-cost-pools-bases.md) and [G&A pool and base techniques](../absorption/rte-ga-pool-and-base-techniques.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An invented contractor accumulates three indirect cost pools over a twelve-month cost accounting period: a fringe benefits pool, an overhead pool, and a general and administrative (G&A) expense pool. Direct labor incurred across all jobs is 1,000,000, and direct materials and subcontracts total 480,000.

First, the fringe pool of 300,000 is allocated on direct labor of 1,000,000, yielding a calculated fringe rate of 30 percent (300,000 pool / 1,000,000 base = 0.30). Direct labor plus allocated fringe equals 1,300,000 of total labor cost (1,000,000 + 300,000).

Second, the overhead pool of 520,000 is allocated on labor cost of 1,300,000, yielding a calculated overhead rate of 40 percent (520,000 pool / 1,300,000 base = 0.40).

Third, the total cost input base for G&A is formed by combining labor cost of 1,300,000, overhead of 520,000, and materials and subcontracts of 480,000, which sum to 2,300,000 (1,300,000 + 520,000 + 480,000). The G&A pool of 276,000 is allocated across this 2,300,000 total cost input base, yielding a calculated G&A rate of 12 percent (276,000 pool / 2,300,000 base = 0.12).

## Never confuse

In practice, a fringe benefit pool is maintained as an indirect cost pool in its own right at some companies and included as part of an overhead pool at others [@CL-RTE-9284]. General and administrative expense must not be confused with overhead: general and administrative expense means any management, financial and other expense that is incurred by or allocated to a business unit and is for the general management and administration of the business unit as a whole [@CL-RTE-0671], whereas overhead covers indirect costs identified with operational activities or management of direct labor or direct material activities [@CL-RTE-0663; @CL-RTE-0664]. Furthermore, management expenses whose beneficial or causal relationship to cost objectives can be more directly measured by a base other than a total activity cost input base are excluded from G&A [@CL-RTE-0672].

A cost pool must not be confused with its allocation base: a cost pool is an aggregation of indirect costs that share a common beneficial or causal relationship and serves as the numerator in an indirect rate calculation [@CL-CMP-0217; @CL-CMP-0218; @CL-RTE-9290], whereas an allocation base is the measure of activity that serves as the denominator to distribute those pooled costs to benefiting cost objectives [@CL-RTE-0665; @CL-RTE-0210; @CL-RTE-9290]. Once an appropriate allocation base has been accepted, the contractor must not fragment the base by removing individual elements, and all items properly includable in an indirect cost base must bear a pro rata share of indirect costs whether accepted as Government contract costs or not [@CL-RTE-0211; @CL-RTE-0212].

A cost pool must not be confused with a cost objective: an indirect cost pool is an intermediate accumulation mechanism where costs not directly identified with a single final cost objective are grouped prior to allocation [@CL-RTE-9075; @CL-RTE-0207; @CL-CMP-0217]. A cost objective, by contrast, is a function, organizational subdivision, contract or other work unit for which cost data are desired and for which provision is made to accumulate and measure the cost of processes, products, jobs, capitalized projects, etc. [@CL-RTE-9296], and a final cost objective is a cost objective which has allocated to it both direct and indirect costs and is one of the final accumulation points in the contractor's accumulation systems [@CL-RTE-9297]. Allocate means to assign an item of cost or a group of items of cost, to one or more cost objectives, including both direct assignment and the reassignment of a share from an indirect cost pool [@CL-RTE-9298], and no final cost objective shall have allocated to it as an indirect cost any cost if other costs incurred for the same purpose in like circumstances have been included as a direct cost [@CL-RTE-9134; @CL-RTE-0208]. See [Direct costs](rte-direct-costs.md), [Homogeneous pools and bases](../absorption/rte-homogeneous-pools-and-bases.md), and [Wrap rate](rte-wrap-rate-build-up.md).
