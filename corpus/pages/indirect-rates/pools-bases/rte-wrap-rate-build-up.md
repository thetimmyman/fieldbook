## Purpose

Explains how a contractor builds a wrap rate by applying indirect cost rates in sequence to direct labor for pricing and estimating.

## The rule

FAR 2.101 defines an indirect cost as any cost not directly identified with a single final cost objective, but identified with two or more final cost objectives or with at least one intermediate cost objective [@CL-RTE-9075], and defines an indirect cost rate as the percentage or dollar factor that expresses the ratio of indirect expense incurred in a given period to direct labor cost, manufacturing cost, or another appropriate base for the same period [@CL-RTE-9073]. Under FAR 31.205-6(m)(1), fringe benefits are allowances and services provided by the contractor to its employees as compensation in addition to regular wages and salaries [@CL-RTE-9246], including vacations, sick leave, holidays, military leave, employee insurance, and supplemental unemployment benefit plans [@CL-RTE-9247], and are allowable to the extent that they are reasonable and required by law, employer-employee agreement, or an established policy of the contractor [@CL-RTE-9248].

When allocating an indirect cost pool, a direct labor hour base or direct labor cost base shall be used, whichever in the aggregate is more likely to vary in proportion to the costs in the pool being allocated, except where one of the listed other bases applies [@CL-RTE-0666], and the contractor accumulates indirect costs by logical cost groupings determined to permit an allocation base common to all cost objectives to which the grouping is allocated [@CL-RTE-0209; @CL-RTE-0210]. Under CAS 9904.410, business unit general and administrative expenses are grouped in a separate indirect cost pool and allocated only to final cost objectives [@CL-CMP-0213; @CL-RTE-0671], using a cost input base representing the business unit's total activity [@CL-CMP-0214], where total cost input is generally acceptable [@CL-RTE-0677; @CL-RTE-0675]. Under CAS 9904.410-30(a)(3), cost input means the cost, except general and administrative expenses, which for contract costing purposes is allocable to the production of goods and services during a cost accounting period [@CL-RTE-9249], and the cost input base must include all significant elements of cost input representing total activity [@CL-RTE-9250; @CL-RTE-9251].

No public procurement statute or regulation defines the term wrap rate [@CL-RTE-9252]. In practice, contractors treat a wrap rate as an informal pricing shortcut that builds a fully burdened labor rate by applying fringe, overhead, and general and administrative expense rates in turn to a direct labor rate, rather than a rate agreed with the Government [@CL-RTE-9252].

## How it works

The wrap rate build-up proceeds in sequential layers from direct labor to a fully burdened labor rate [@CL-RTE-9253]. First, the contractor identifies the direct labor rate for an employee or labor category, representing direct cost specifically identified with a particular final cost objective [@CL-RTE-9074; @CL-RTE-9253]. Second, the fringe benefit rate is applied to direct labor to cover employment compensation and benefits beyond wages [@CL-RTE-9246; @CL-RTE-9253]. Third, the overhead rate is applied to its allocation base, typically direct labor cost alone or total labor cost including direct labor and fringe benefits, to allocate indirect operational expenses [@CL-RTE-0666; @CL-RTE-9253]. Fourth, the general and administrative expense rate is applied to total cost input, which includes direct labor plus the fringe and overhead burdens already added to that point [@CL-RTE-9249; @CL-RTE-9253], to allocate executive, financial, and overall business unit management costs to final cost objectives [@CL-CMP-0213; @CL-CMP-0214; @CL-RTE-0677].

Within this build-up, the definitions and allowability of indirect costs, fringe benefits, overhead allocation bases, and general and administrative cost input allocation rest on regulatory claims [@CL-RTE-9075; @CL-RTE-9246; @CL-RTE-9248; @CL-RTE-0666; @CL-CMP-0213; @CL-CMP-0214; @CL-RTE-9249], whereas the sequential stacking of these rates into a single composite wrap rate multiplier reflects contractor practice assumptions [@CL-RTE-9252; @CL-RTE-9253]. In contractor practice, the precise selection and composition of the allocation base for each layer is a company accounting choice [@CL-RTE-9254], bounded by the rules that indirect costs must be grouped logically and allocated on a common base reflecting causal or beneficial relationships [@CL-RTE-0209; @CL-RTE-0210; @CL-RTE-0666], accepted bases must not be fragmented [@CL-RTE-0211], and general and administrative expenses must allocate over an appropriate cost input base representing total business unit activity [@CL-CMP-0214; @CL-RTE-0675; @CL-RTE-0677].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An invented company sets a direct labor rate of 50.00 per hour for an engineering labor category. The company applies a fringe benefit rate of 30 percent to direct labor, adding 15.00 for a total labor cost of 65.00 per hour. It then applies an overhead rate of 40 percent to total labor cost (direct labor plus fringe benefits), adding 26.00 for a total cost input of 91.00 per hour. Next, it applies a general and administrative expense rate of 12 percent to total cost input (91.00), adding 10.92 to produce a fully burdened wrap rate of 101.92 per hour. This wrap rate equates to a multiplier of approximately 2.04 on the base direct labor rate. Contract fee or profit is not included in the wrap rate and is negotiated separately.

## Never confuse

A wrap rate is an internal or proposal pricing build-up that combines direct labor with layered indirect rates into a fully burdened labor rate, and must not be confused with any of the formal indirect rate mechanisms governed by procurement regulations [@CL-RTE-9252; @CL-RTE-9255]. A provisional billing rate is a temporary indirect cost rate established by the contracting officer or cognizant auditor for interim reimbursement of incurred indirect costs under cost-reimbursement contracts and progress payments, adjusted as necessary until final rates are established [@CL-RTE-0300; @CL-RTE-0304; @CL-CTF-0127]; it applies to a single indirect pool during performance and is not a composite labor pricing build-up [@CL-RTE-9255]. A forward pricing rate agreement is a formal written agreement negotiated between the contractor and the Government to make specific rates available for pricing future contracts or modifications during a defined period [@CL-RTE-9076], and a forward pricing rate recommendation is a unilateral rate set by the administrative contracting officer when agreement cannot be reached [@CL-RTE-9077]; these establish individual forward rates for pools, whereas a wrap rate is a contractor calculation combining those rates into a burdened labor price [@CL-RTE-9255]. A final indirect cost rate is an indirect cost rate established and agreed upon by the Government and the contractor as not subject to change after the close of a fiscal period [@CL-RTE-9071], enabling uniform settlement and administrative closure [@CL-RTE-0301; @CL-RTE-0304]; it reflects settled actual costs and is never a prospective burdened labor multiplier [@CL-RTE-9255]. See [Indirect costs: pools and allocation bases](rte-indirect-cost-pools-bases.md).
