## Purpose

Shows how direct costs are assigned to final cost objectives and when a minor direct cost may be treated as indirect.

## The rule

No final cost objective shall have allocated to it as a direct cost any cost, if other costs incurred for the same purpose in like circumstances have been included in any indirect cost pool to be allocated to that or any other final cost objective [@CL-RTE-9133; @CL-RTE-0201]. Direct costs of the contract shall be charged directly to the contract, and all costs specifically identified with other final cost objectives of the contractor are direct costs of those cost objectives and are not to be charged to the contract directly or indirectly [@CL-RTE-0202; @CL-RTE-0203].

For reasons of practicality, the contractor may treat any direct cost of a minor dollar amount as an indirect cost if the accounting treatment is consistently applied to all final cost objectives and produces substantially the same results as treating the cost as a direct cost [@CL-RTE-0204; @CL-RTE-0205].

FAR 2.101 defines a direct cost as any cost that is identified specifically with a particular final cost objective, and says direct costs are not limited to items that are incorporated in the end product as material or labor, that costs identified specifically with a contract are direct costs of that contract, and that all costs identified specifically with other final cost objectives of the contractor are direct costs of those cost objectives [@CL-RTE-9074; @CL-RTE-9117]. RFO FAR 2.101 defines a direct cost as any cost that is identified specifically with a particular final cost objective, and says direct costs are not limited to items that are incorporated in the end product as material or labor, that costs identified specifically with a contract are direct costs of that contract, and that all costs identified specifically with other final cost objectives of the contractor are direct costs of those cost objectives [@CL-RTE-9090; @CL-RTE-9109; @CL-RTE-9110].

## How it works

Direct costs of the contract shall be charged directly to the contract, and all costs specifically identified with other final cost objectives of the contractor are direct costs of those cost objectives and are not to be charged to the contract directly or indirectly [@CL-RTE-0202; @CL-RTE-0203]. No final cost objective shall have allocated to it as a direct cost any cost, if other costs incurred for the same purpose in like circumstances have been included in any indirect cost pool to be allocated to that or any other final cost objective [@CL-RTE-9133; @CL-RTE-0201]. For reasons of practicality, the contractor may treat any direct cost of a minor dollar amount as an indirect cost if the accounting treatment is consistently applied to all final cost objectives and produces substantially the same results as treating the cost as a direct cost [@CL-RTE-0204; @CL-RTE-0205].

Contracting officers must use the FAR part 31, Contract Cost Principles and Procedures, published on the Revolutionary FAR Overhaul web page in lieu of the text codified at 48 CFR chapter 1 [@CL-CMP-9350], effective immediately under the class deviation that revises and supersedes Class Deviation 2026-O0032, issued on December 19, 2025 [@CL-CMP-9351]. Under RFO FAR 31.202(a)(1), no final cost objective must have allocated to it as a direct cost any cost, if other costs incurred for the same purpose in like circumstances have been included in any indirect cost pool to be allocated to that or any other final cost objective [@CL-RTE-0875]. Under RFO FAR 31.202(a)(2), direct costs of the contract must be charged directly to the contract [@CL-RTE-0876], and under RFO FAR 31.202(a)(3), all costs specifically identified with other final cost objectives of the contractor are direct costs of those cost objectives and are not to be charged to the contract directly or indirectly [@CL-RTE-0877]. Under RFO FAR 31.202(b), for reasons of practicality, the contractor may treat any direct cost of a minor dollar amount as an indirect cost if the accounting treatment is consistently applied to all final cost objectives and produces substantially the same results as treating the cost as a direct cost [@CL-RTE-0878].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contract has materials and labor for the contract plus a small tooling charge. The contractor charges the materials and labor directly to the contract. Because the tooling charge is minor and the accounting treatment is applied to every job the same way, it is carried as indirect. The treatment is allowed only because it is consistent and gives substantially the same result as charging it direct.

## Never confuse

A direct cost is any cost that is identified specifically with a particular final cost objective [@CL-RTE-9074], and direct costs of the contract shall be charged directly to the contract [@CL-RTE-0202]. No final cost objective shall have allocated to it as a direct cost any cost, if other costs incurred for the same purpose in like circumstances have been included in any indirect cost pool to be allocated to that or any other final cost objective [@CL-RTE-9133; @CL-RTE-0201].
