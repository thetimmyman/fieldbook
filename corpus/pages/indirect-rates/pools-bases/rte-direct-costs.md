## Purpose

Shows how direct costs are assigned to final cost objectives and when a minor direct cost may be treated as indirect.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-RTE-0201] |
| Codified citation | FAR 31.202(a) [@CL-RTE-0201] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 31.202 [@CL-RTE-0875; @CL-RTE-0876; @CL-RTE-0877; @CL-RTE-0878] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-RTE-0201] |
| Applies to | Not established. |

A cost may not be charged as a direct cost to a final cost objective if other costs incurred for the same purpose in like circumstances have been included in any indirect cost pool [@CL-RTE-0201]. Direct costs of the contract are charged directly to the contract, and costs specifically identified with other final cost objectives are direct costs of those objectives, not charged to the contract directly or indirectly [@CL-RTE-0202; @CL-RTE-0203].

For reasons of practicality the contractor may treat a direct cost of a minor dollar amount as indirect, if the accounting treatment is consistently applied to all final cost objectives and produces substantially the same results as treating the cost as direct [@CL-RTE-0204; @CL-RTE-0205].

FAR 2.101 defines a direct cost as any cost that is identified specifically with a particular final cost objective, and says direct costs are not limited to items that are incorporated in the end product as material or labor, that costs identified specifically with a contract are direct costs of that contract, and that all costs identified specifically with other final cost objectives of the contractor are direct costs of those cost objectives [@CL-RTE-9074; @CL-RTE-9117]. The first sentence of the RFO model text definition of a direct cost in FAR 2.101 reads the same as the codified first sentence [@CL-RTE-9090; @CL-RTE-9074], and the RFO text goes on to say that direct costs are not limited to items that are incorporated in the end product as material or labor, that costs identified specifically with a contract are direct costs of that contract, and that all costs identified specifically with other final cost objectives of the contractor are direct costs of those cost objectives [@CL-RTE-9090; @CL-RTE-9109; @CL-RTE-9110].

## How it works

Each cost is charged either directly to a final cost objective or into an indirect pool, and the same purpose must be treated alike. A cost identified with another final cost objective is a direct cost of that objective and never touches the contract. The minor-dollar exception lets the contractor push a small direct cost into indirect for practicality, but only under the two consistency conditions.

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 31 text in place of the codified text [@CL-CMP-9350], effective immediately under Revision 1 signed on 2026-08-04 [@CL-CMP-9351]. RFO FAR 31.202 keeps the rule that no final cost objective is allocated as a direct cost any cost whose same-purpose, like-circumstances twins are in an indirect pool [@CL-RTE-0875], that direct costs of the contract are charged directly to the contract [@CL-RTE-0876], and that costs specifically identified with other final cost objectives are direct costs of those objectives [@CL-RTE-0877]. The RFO text numbers these three rules (a)(1), (a)(2) and (a)(3) [@CL-RTE-0875; @CL-RTE-0876; @CL-RTE-0877]. Paragraph (b) keeps the minor-dollar exception with the same two consistency conditions [@CL-RTE-0878].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contract has materials and labor for the contract plus a small tooling charge. The contractor charges the materials and labor directly to the contract. Because the tooling charge is minor and the accounting treatment is applied to every job the same way, it is carried as indirect. The treatment is allowed only because it is consistent and gives substantially the same result as charging it direct.

## Never confuse

A direct cost is not an indirect cost. A direct cost attaches to a specific final cost objective and is charged to that cost objective, while an indirect cost is pooled and allocated. The consistency rule cuts both ways: a cost cannot be direct if its same-purpose, like-circumstances twins are in a pool, and it cannot be indirect if those twins are direct. See [Indirect costs](rte-indirect-cost-pools-bases.md).
