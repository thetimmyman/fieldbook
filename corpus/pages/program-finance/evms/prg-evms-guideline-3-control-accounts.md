## Purpose

Shows where the guide places management control points, and which manager answers for the work that sits at each one.

The guideline asks the contractor to integrate the work breakdown structure and the organizational structure so that management control points are identified and responsibility is assigned to specific work scope [@CL-PRG-0166]. Control accounts are established at the intersection of the lowest level element of work and the organizational structure [@CL-PRG-0167]. Control accounts should not be arbitrarily established at a single level of the work structure across the program [@CL-PRG-0168].

Each control account has a corresponding Control Account Manager (CAM), who is responsible for ensuring the accomplishment of work in the control account and is the focal point of management control [@CL-PRG-0169]. For management control purposes, there may be one or more responsible organizations supporting a single work breakdown structure element or multiple control accounts within one organizational breakdown structure element driven by scope and exit criteria (i.e., completion of the effort) [@CL-PRG-0170; @CL-PRG-0072]. For scope that cannot practically be identified to a control account, it is permissible to establish a Summary Level Planning Package (SLPP) above the control account level with the assignment of an SLPP manager [@CL-PRG-0171]. Each control account is assigned to a single organizational element directly responsible for the work identifiable to a single element of the work breakdown structure [@CL-PRG-0172].

## How it works

The intersection of the lowest level work breakdown structure element and the organizational breakdown structure is the point at which control accounts are established [@CL-PRG-0167]. Each control account has a corresponding Control Account Manager, who is responsible for ensuring the accomplishment of work in the control account and is the focal point of management control, and each control account is assigned to a single organizational element directly responsible for the work identifiable to a single element of the work breakdown structure [@CL-PRG-0169; @CL-PRG-0172]. Control accounts should not be arbitrarily established at a single work breakdown structure level across the program [@CL-PRG-0168]. For scope that cannot practically be identified to a control account, it is permissible to establish a Summary Level Planning Package above the control account level with the assignment of an SLPP manager [@CL-PRG-0171].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

One element of work worth 400,000 is split across 2 organizations, with 250,000 performed by the first and 150,000 by the second. The company sets up 2 control accounts at that element, one per organization, and names a control account manager for each. The answer is that the element is controlled at the intersection, and each control account carries one manager and one organization.
