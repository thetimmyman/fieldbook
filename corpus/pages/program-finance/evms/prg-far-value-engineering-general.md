## Purpose

Shows what a value engineering change proposal is under the value engineering clause, what it must contain, where it goes and how the Government acts on it.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-PRG-0919] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.248-1 [@CL-PRG-0919] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-PRG-0919] |
| Applies to | Contracts that include the clause [@CL-PRG-0919] |

Under the basic clause, the Contractor is encouraged to submit value engineering change proposals (VECPs) voluntarily, and shall share in any net acquisition savings realized from accepted proposals, in accordance with the incentive sharing rates [@CL-PRG-0920]. If the contracting officer selects a mandatory value engineering program requirement, Alternate I substitutes a paragraph (a) under which the Contractor shall engage in a value engineering program and submit value engineering progress reports [@CL-PRG-0958]. A VECP is a proposal that requires a change to the instant contract to implement and reduces the overall projected cost to the agency without impairing essential functions or characteristics, subject to a proviso [@CL-PRG-0921]. The proviso excludes a change in deliverable end item quantities only, a change in research and development end items or test quantities due solely to results of previous testing under the contract, and a change to the contract type only [@CL-PRG-0922].

As a minimum, each VECP must carry the information in the eight listed subparagraphs [@CL-PRG-0923]. The Contractor submits VECPs to the Contracting Officer unless the contract says otherwise, and sends a copy at the same time to the Administrative Contracting Officer where the contract is administered by other than the contracting office [@CL-PRG-0924].

## How it works

Under the basic clause the Contractor decides whether to propose a change [@CL-PRG-0920]. It builds the proposal with the minimum information and sends it to the Contracting Officer [@CL-PRG-0923; @CL-PRG-0924]. The Contracting Officer will notify the Contractor of the status within 45 calendar days after the contracting office receives it [@CL-PRG-0925]. If additional time is required, the Contracting Officer will notify the Contractor within that period and give the reason for the delay and the expected date of the decision [@CL-PRG-0969]. Until a contract modification applies the proposal, the Contractor performs the existing contract, and accepting or rejecting all or part of it is the Contracting Officer's unilateral decision [@CL-PRG-0926]. This card does not restate the clause's other defined terms; sharing is on [Value engineering clause: sharing rates and savings calculation](prg-far-value-engineering-sharing.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contractor finds a design change that lowers projected agency cost without harming essential functions. It sends the proposal to the Contracting Officer. The Contracting Officer must give a status within 45 days after the contracting office receives it. A second idea only cuts the delivered quantity by 100 units, so it is outside the VECP definition. The answer: the first is a VECP and the second is not.

## Never confuse

Being encouraged to propose is not a promise of acceptance: the Contracting Officer decides alone [@CL-PRG-0926]. A VECP is also not any change that cuts cost, because quantity-only and contract-type-only changes are outside the definition [@CL-PRG-0922]. How savings are shared is a separate topic, covered by [Value engineering clause: sharing rates and savings calculation](prg-far-value-engineering-sharing.md), and is not stated here.
