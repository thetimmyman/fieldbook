## Purpose

Shows when a DoD contract needs an earned value management system, when that system must be formally determined compliant, and which contracts are exempt.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | DoD class deviation [@CL-PRG-0001] |
| Codified citation | DFARS 234.201(1)(i) and (ii) [@CL-PRG-0001] [@CL-PRG-0002] |
| RFO citation | Class Deviation 2026-O0011 Rev 1, DFARS 234.201-70(a)(1) and (2) [@CL-PRG-0001] [@CL-PRG-0002] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Deviation text [@CL-PRG-0001] |
| Applies to | DoD cost or incentive contracts and subcontracts, from 2026-08-05 [@CL-PRG-0001] |

Except for the exempt contracts described below, DoD applies the requirement as follows [@CL-PRG-9140]. For cost or incentive contracts and subcontracts of $50 million or more, the clause requires the contractor's earned value management system to comply with the EIA-748 guidelines [@CL-PRG-0001; @CL-PRG-9141]. At $100 million or more, the clause requires the contractor to have a system the cognizant Federal agency has determined to comply with the EIA-748 guidelines [@CL-PRG-0002; @CL-PRG-9142]. The requirement does not apply to contracts primarily for software or to contracts awarded to nontraditional defense contractors [@CL-PRG-0003]. The head of the contracting activity may waive the nontraditional-contractor exemption by written determination that applying the requirement to such a contractor is in the best interest of DoD, and may delegate that authority to no lower than the chief of the contracting office [@CL-PRG-0004; @CL-PRG-9143].

## How it works

Check the exemptions first, then the contract type, then the value. Between the two thresholds the clause requires compliance with the guidelines [@CL-PRG-0001; @CL-PRG-9141]; the requirement for a system the cognizant Federal agency has determined compliant starts at the higher threshold [@CL-PRG-0002; @CL-PRG-9142]. The system measures progress against the Performance Measurement Baseline and reports cost and schedule variances. The codified DFARS values are lower than the deviation values: $20,000,000 for the guideline-compliance requirement and $50,000,000 for the determined-compliant requirement [@CL-PRG-9144; @CL-PRG-9145], against $50 million and $100 million in the deviation [@CL-PRG-0001; @CL-PRG-0002].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A cost-plus-incentive-fee subcontract is valued at 70 million, above a lower threshold of 50 million and below a higher threshold of 100 million. The subcontractor's system must follow the guidelines, but the requirement for a system determined compliant starts at the higher threshold. A firm-fixed-price subcontract of the same value is outside the requirement.

## Never confuse

An earned value management system is the management system; the Performance Measurement Baseline is the time-phased budget it measures against. Being a nontraditional defense contractor is not the same as being a small business; the exemption follows the defined term, not company size.
