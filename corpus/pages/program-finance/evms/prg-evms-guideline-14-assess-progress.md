## Purpose

Describes the recurring step that turns schedule status and physical progress into the budget earned in the period [@CL-PRG-0287].

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | DoD EVMS Interpretation Guide, Guideline 14 [@CL-PRG-0286] |
| Codified citation | Not established. |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Not established. |
| Applies to | Programs that operate an earned value management system [@CL-PRG-0287] |

Progress is assessed with the measurement criteria predefined at planning: the schedule is statused and physical progress is assessed to determine the budget earned [@CL-PRG-0287]. Earned value for material may not be credited before the material is actually received and may not be credited after it is consumed [@CL-PRG-0288].

Schedule status and physical progress are updated in accordance with the business cycle based on the size, risk and complexity of the program, but no less than monthly [@CL-PRG-0289].

## How it works

Schedule status is time-based, while assessing physical progress is the earned value [@CL-PRG-0289]. The schedule progress reporting period should align to the accounting period so that the schedule and the baseline stay integrated [@CL-PRG-0292]. Level of effort earns value with the passage of time without consideration of whether actual work is performed, so baseline start and finish dates for level of effort work packages are proactively managed to ensure variances do not result from failure to maintain the plan [@CL-PRG-0290; @CL-PRG-0750]. The point at which material cost transfers from the accounting system to the earned value system as actual cost must fall within the same accounting period in which earned value for that material is claimed [@CL-PRG-0291].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A work package is budgeted at 120,000 and planned to finish in four months. At the end of month two the schedule is statused and the technique earns 60,000, which is recorded as the budget earned for the period. Material worth 30,000 was received and put into use in that same period, so its earned value and its actual cost both land in month two.

## Never confuse

Schedule status and physical progress are not the same measure. Schedule status is time-based, while assessing physical progress is what determines the budget earned [@CL-PRG-0289; @CL-PRG-0287].

## Company calibration

Which contracts the company measures this way, whether the system has been validated, and which earned value technique is documented for each work package. [@Q-037]
