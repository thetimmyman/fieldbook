## Purpose

Shows what the guide expects when the scheduling, budgeting, work authorization and cost accumulation processes have to work as one framework for program management.

## The rule

The contractor must provide for the integration of the scheduling, budgeting, work authorization, and cost accumulation processes with each other and, as appropriate, the program work breakdown structure and organizational structure to enable cost and performance measurement by elements of either or both structures [@CL-PRG-0174; @CL-PRG-0073]. Integrating the planning, scheduling, budgeting, work authorization, and cost accumulation management processes provides the capability for establishing the Performance Measurement Baseline, identifying work progress, and collecting actual costs, thereby facilitating management analysis and corrective actions [@CL-PRG-0175; @CL-PRG-0074].

Through coding structures that use unique identifiers and common data elements or a simple mapping method, the contractor's planning, scheduling, budgeting, work authorization, and cost accumulation processes are integrated [@CL-PRG-0176]. The system must integrate subcontractor data [@CL-PRG-0177]. The planning, scheduling, budgeting, work authorization, and cost accumulation subsystems are integrated via a common and unique coding structure for all levels of the Contract Work Breakdown Structure and the organizational structure [@CL-PRG-0178].

## How it works

Guideline 4 requires providing for the integration of the scheduling, budgeting, work authorization, and cost accumulation processes with each other and, as appropriate, the program work breakdown structure and organizational structure to enable cost and performance measurement by elements of either or both structures [@CL-PRG-0174; @CL-PRG-0073]. Coding structures that use unique identifiers and common data elements or a simple mapping method integrate the contractor's planning, scheduling, budgeting, work authorization, and cost accumulation processes [@CL-PRG-0176]. This allows the data derived from one system to relate to and be consistent with the data of each of the other systems at the level established by the unique coding and/or charge number level through the total contract level [@CL-PRG-0562]. The integration of planning, scheduling, budgeting, work authorization, and cost accumulation management processes provides the capability for establishing the Performance Measurement Baseline, identifying work progress, and collecting actual costs, thereby facilitating management analysis and corrective actions [@CL-PRG-0175; @CL-PRG-0074]. The system must also integrate subcontractor data [@CL-PRG-0177].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A company runs 4 separate systems for planning, scheduling, budgeting and cost collection, and one shared charge number ties them together. A cost posted against that charge number shows the same work element and the same organization in every system. The answer is that the processes are integrated through the shared code, so progress and actual cost can be compared without rebuilding the data by hand.

## Never confuse

The contractor's planning, scheduling, budgeting, work authorization, and cost accumulation processes are integrated through coding structures using unique identifiers and common data elements or a simple mapping method across all levels of the Contract Work Breakdown Structure and organizational structure, including subcontractor data [@CL-PRG-0176; @CL-PRG-0177; @CL-PRG-0178], so data from one subsystem relates to and remains consistent with the others rather than operating as disconnected standalone tracking systems [@CL-PRG-0562].

## Company calibration

Whether the coding structure that links planning, scheduling, budgeting, work authorization and cost accumulation is the same one used to reconcile program forecasts to accounting and reporting [@CL-PRG-0176; @Q-010]
