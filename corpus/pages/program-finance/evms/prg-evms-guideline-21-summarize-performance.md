## Purpose

Shows how the earned value system summarizes performance data and variances through the work breakdown structure and the organizational breakdown structure for management insight and customer reporting.

Program performance status is summarized from the control account, at a minimum, through the work breakdown structure and organizational breakdown structure for management insight and control and to meet customer reporting requirements [@CL-PRG-0351]. The data used for internal management control and external customer reporting must be derived from the same data resident in the earned value system [@CL-PRG-0353].

## How it works

Performance data is summarized through the work breakdown structure and organizational breakdown structure from the control account through the contract level [@CL-PRG-0352]. The summarized data carries budgeted cost for work scheduled, budgeted cost for work performed, actual cost of work performed, budget at completion, and estimate at completion [@CL-PRG-0352]. Internal management control and external customer reporting are drawn from the same data resident in the earned value system [@CL-PRG-0353].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A program rolls up five control accounts through the work breakdown structure to the contract level. The roll-up shows budgeted cost for work performed of 90 against actual cost of work performed of 100, so the cost variance is an unfavorable 10. The same figure appears in both the internal report and the customer report because both draw from the same earned value data.

## Never confuse

Summarizing and reviewing performance data is not the same as acting on it. This guideline rolls performance and variances up to management for insight; the corrective-action guideline turns a significant variance into a tracked management plan.

## Company calibration

Whether internal management control and external customer reporting draw from the same earned value data resident in the system. [@Q-037]
