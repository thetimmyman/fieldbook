## Purpose

Shows how the value engineering clause sets the contractor share of savings, offsets Government costs, adjusts the contract and calculates concurrent and future shares.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-PRG-0919] |
| Codified citation | FAR 52.248-1 [@CL-PRG-0919] |
| RFO citation | RFO FAR 52.248-1 [@CL-PRG-0049; @CL-PRG-0050; @CL-PRG-9002; @CL-PRG-0051; @CL-PRG-0052; @CL-PRG-0053; @CL-PRG-0054; @CL-PRG-0055; @CL-PRG-0056; @CL-PRG-0057; @CL-PRG-0058; @CL-PRG-0059; @CL-PRG-0060; @CL-PRG-0061; @CL-PRG-0062; @CL-PRG-0063; @CL-PRG-9000; @CL-PRG-0064; @CL-PRG-9001; @CL-PRG-0065; @CL-PRG-0066] |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-PRG-0919] |
| Applies to | Contracts that include the clause [@CL-PRG-0919] |

If a value engineering change proposal is accepted, the Contractor shares in net acquisition savings according to the percentages in the sharing table [@CL-PRG-0959]. The percentage paid the Contractor depends upon the contract type, the sharing arrangement and the source of the savings [@CL-PRG-0927].

For fixed-price contracts, including fixed-price-award-fee and excluding other fixed-price incentive contracts, the printed table gives 50 percent for both sources under the incentive (voluntary) arrangement and 25 percent for both under the program requirement (mandatory) arrangement, with a note marking the 50 percent entries [@CL-PRG-0928]. That note says the Contracting Officer may increase the Contractor's sharing rate to as high as 75 percent for each VECP [@CL-PRG-0929].

For incentive contracts other than award fee, the concurrent and future contract rate is 50 percent under the incentive arrangement, with the first note, and 25 percent under the program requirement arrangement [@CL-PRG-0928; @CL-PRG-0960]. The instant contract rates carry a note in place of a figure [@CL-PRG-0960]. The note on the instant contract rates says the sharing arrangement is the same as the contract's profit or fee adjustment formula [@CL-PRG-0962].

For cost-reimbursement contracts, including cost-plus-award-fee and excluding other cost-type incentive contracts, the incentive arrangement gives 25 percent for both sources and the program requirement arrangement gives 15 percent for both [@CL-PRG-0928; @CL-PRG-0961]. A third note says the Contracting Officer may increase the Contractor's sharing rate to as high as 50 percent for each VECP [@CL-PRG-0963].

Except in incentive contracts, Government costs and any price or cost increases resulting from negative instant contract savings shall be offset against acquisition savings each time such savings are realized until they are fully offset [@CL-PRG-0930]. Net acquisition savings are first realized, and the Contractor shall be paid a share, when Government costs and any negative instant contract savings have been fully offset against acquisition savings [@CL-PRG-0964]. If this is an incentive contract, recovery of Government costs on the instant contract shall be deferred and offset against concurrent and future contract savings [@CL-PRG-0965].

## How it works

The modification accepting the proposal (or a subsequent modification issued as soon as possible after any negotiations are completed) shall reduce the contract price or estimated cost by the amount of instant contract savings, unless this is an incentive contract [@CL-PRG-0931]. In the same list, the modification shall provide the Contractor's share of any net acquisition savings under the instant contract, by adding it to the contract price for fixed-price contracts and to the contract fee for cost-reimbursement contracts [@CL-PRG-0931; @CL-PRG-0932]. For concurrent contract savings, the Contracting Officer shall calculate the Contractor's share by subtracting from the reduction in price negotiated on the concurrent contract any Government costs or negative instant contract savings not yet offset and multiplying the result by the Contractor's sharing rate [@CL-PRG-9151; @CL-PRG-0933]. For future contract savings, the Contracting Officer shall calculate the Contractor's share by multiplying the future unit cost reduction by the number of future contract units scheduled for delivery during the sharing period, subtracting any Government costs or negative instant contract savings not yet offset, and multiplying the result by the Contractor's sharing rate [@CL-PRG-9152; @CL-PRG-0934].

For collateral savings, if a VECP is accepted, the Contracting Officer will increase the instant contract amount, as specified in paragraph (h)(5) of the clause, by a rate from 20 to 100 percent, as determined by the Contracting Officer, of any projected collateral savings determined to be realized in a typical year of use after subtracting any Government costs not previously offset [@CL-PRG-9147; @CL-PRG-0966]. The Contractor's share of collateral savings will not exceed the contract's firm-fixed-price, target price, target cost or estimated cost at the time the VECP is accepted, or $100,000, whichever is greater [@CL-PRG-0967].

The Contractor shall include an appropriate value engineering clause in any subcontract valued at or above the simplified acquisition threshold, as defined in FAR 2.101 on the date of subcontract award, and may include one in subcontracts of lesser value [@CL-PRG-0968]. This card does not restate the other paragraphs of the clause, such as the no-cost settlement method, the relationship to other incentives, data rights and the alternates.

Under the RFO text, DoD contracting officers use the RFO FAR part 52 text in place of the codified text because Class Deviation 2026-O0038 directs it, effective February 1, 2026 [@CL-CMP-9760] [@CL-CMP-9762]. The RFO text keeps the rule that, if a VECP is accepted, the Contractor shares in net acquisition savings according to the percentages in the table [@CL-PRG-0049; @CL-PRG-0959], and says the percentage depends on the contract type, the sharing arrangement and the source of the savings (the instant contract, or concurrent and future contracts), citing paragraph (a) of the clause where the codified text says paragraph (a) above [@CL-PRG-0050; @CL-PRG-9002; @CL-PRG-0927]. The RFO table keeps the same figures as the codified table: 50 and 25 percent for fixed-price contracts [@CL-PRG-0051; @CL-PRG-0928], 50 and 25 percent for incentive contracts other than award fee, with a note in place of a figure for the instant contract rates [@CL-PRG-0052; @CL-PRG-0960; @CL-PRG-0055; @CL-PRG-0962], and 25 and 15 percent for cost-reimbursement contracts [@CL-PRG-0053; @CL-PRG-0961]. The RFO footnotes say the Contracting Office, not the Contracting Officer, may increase the Contractor's sharing rate to as high as 75 percent for fixed-price contracts [@CL-PRG-0054; @CL-PRG-0929] and as high as 50 percent for cost-reimbursement contracts [@CL-PRG-0056; @CL-PRG-0963]. The RFO text keeps the offset rules: net acquisition savings are first realized when Government costs and negative instant contract savings are fully offset [@CL-PRG-0057; @CL-PRG-0964], the offset applies except in incentive contracts [@CL-PRG-0058; @CL-PRG-0930], and in an incentive contract recovery of Government costs on the instant contract shall be deferred and offset against concurrent and future contract savings [@CL-PRG-0059; @CL-PRG-0965]. It keeps the contract adjustment rules for the price or estimated cost reduction [@CL-PRG-0060; @CL-PRG-0931] and for adding the Contractor's share to the price or fee [@CL-PRG-0061; @CL-PRG-0932], and the concurrent and future savings calculations [@CL-PRG-0062; @CL-PRG-0933; @CL-PRG-0063; @CL-PRG-9000; @CL-PRG-0934]. The RFO paragraph (j) says that, if a VECP is accepted, the Contracting Officer will increase the instant contract amount, as specified in paragraph (h)(5) of the clause [@CL-PRG-9001]. It keeps the collateral savings rate of 20 to 100 percent [@CL-PRG-0064; @CL-PRG-0966], the limit of the contract's price, target or estimated cost or $100,000, whichever is greater [@CL-PRG-0065; @CL-PRG-0967], and the subcontract rule at the simplified acquisition threshold [@CL-PRG-0066; @CL-PRG-0968].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An accepted proposal leads to a price reduction of 100,000 on a concurrent contract. Government costs not yet offset are 20,000. The sharing rate is 50 percent. The Contractor's share is 50 percent of the 80,000 left after the offset, which is 40,000. The answer: the Government costs come off first, then the rate applies.

## Never confuse

A share is not paid on gross savings: Government costs and negative instant contract savings are offset first, except in incentive contracts, where recovery of Government costs on the instant contract is deferred and offset against concurrent and future contract savings [@CL-PRG-0930; @CL-PRG-0965; @CL-PRG-0933]. Concurrent and future savings are also calculated differently, one from a negotiated price reduction and the other from the future unit cost reduction and scheduled units [@CL-PRG-0933; @CL-PRG-0934]. The general rules on what a proposal is and how it is submitted are on [Value engineering clause: change proposals and submission](prg-far-value-engineering-general.md) and are not repeated here. In the codified table the notes say the Contracting Officer may increase the sharing rate [@CL-PRG-0929; @CL-PRG-0963], but the RFO notes say the Contracting Office [@CL-PRG-0054; @CL-PRG-0056].
