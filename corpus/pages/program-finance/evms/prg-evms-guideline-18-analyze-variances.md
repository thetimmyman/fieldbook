## Purpose

Shows how the guideline requires significant cost and schedule variances to be identified, analyzed, and explained for each control account [@CL-PRG-0310].

The guideline requires significant differences between planned and actual schedule and cost performance to be identified and evaluated at least monthly for each control account, with the reasons analyzed and provided in the detail needed by program management [@CL-PRG-0310]. Variances that exceed the established internal or external thresholds are considered significant enough to require in-depth analysis and possible management action [@CL-PRG-0708]. Analyzing deviations from the performance measurement baseline permits management, at all levels, to implement corrective actions rapidly and effectively, to regain contract objectives [@CL-PRG-0311].

## How it works

Focused variance analysis by the control account manager gives program management insight into significant problem areas and highlights the potential need for management action to mitigate potential or realized program risks [@CL-PRG-0707]. Schedule and cost variances are identified and analyzed at the control account and summary level, and the variance analysis identifies the factors causing the variance, such as efficiency, rate, and timing, and potential impacts [@CL-PRG-0705]. Corrective actions or mitigation plans are identified [@CL-PRG-0706]. Schedule variance analysis is supplemented with integrated master schedule analysis and assesses the impact to future activities on the critical path, near-critical paths, and driving paths [@CL-PRG-0312].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A control account has earned 400,000 of budget and spent 460,000, so its cost variance is 400,000 minus 460,000, which is minus 60,000 and unfavorable. The variance exceeds the control account's analysis threshold. The control account manager traces it to a labor rate increase and a material price increase, and records the corrective action of renegotiating the material buy.

## Never confuse

Guideline 18 requires identifying and evaluating, at least monthly for each control account, the significant differences between planned and actual schedule performance and between planned and actual cost performance—identifying causes such as efficiency, rate, and timing and supplementing schedule variance analysis with integrated master schedule critical-path analysis [@CL-PRG-0310; @CL-PRG-0705; @CL-PRG-0312]—unlike indirect cost variance analysis attributed to indirect rate adjustments in [Evaluate indirect cost variances](prg-evms-guideline-19-indirect-cost-variances.md).

## Company calibration

Whether significant variances are analyzed, with reasons and potential impacts, each month for every control account. [@Q-037]
