## Purpose

Shows how the guideline requires accounting for all purchased material that has earned value, including material residual to the program [@CL-PRG-0304].

The guideline requires all purchased material for which earned value has been calculated to be accounted for, including material residual to the program [@CL-PRG-0304]. Its stated purpose is to ensure that all material items purchased for the contract are accounted for through contract completion and final disposition [@CL-PRG-0702]. The material management process must reflect acquisition, issue to control accounts, return of unused materials from control accounts, scrap quantity and disposition, and residual material inventory [@CL-PRG-0305].

## How it works

The material management process reflects acquisition, issue to control accounts, return of unused materials from control accounts, scrap quantity and disposition, and residual material inventory [@CL-PRG-0305]. The results of material disposition are incorporated into the management estimate at completion [@CL-PRG-0703].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A program purchases material of 500,000 and issues 400,000 of it to control accounts. The control accounts return 20,000 unused, so the net issue is 380,000. The other 120,000 (100,000 never issued plus 20,000 returned) is residual inventory, and the results of its disposition are incorporated into the management estimate at completion.

## Never confuse

Accounting for purchased material is not only counting what is issued to control accounts. The guideline also covers returned and scrapped material and residual inventory [@CL-PRG-0305].

## Company calibration

Whether all purchased material is accounted for through final disposition. [@Q-037]
