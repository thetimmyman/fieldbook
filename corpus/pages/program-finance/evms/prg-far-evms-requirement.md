## Purpose

Shows when the FAR requires an earned value management system, what an offeror without a compliant system must do, and when the contracting officer judges the offeror's plan.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-PRG-0850] |
| Codified citation | FAR 34.201(a), (b) and (e) [@CL-PRG-0850; @CL-PRG-0851; @CL-PRG-0852; @CL-PRG-0853; @CL-PRG-0854] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-PRG-0850] |
| Applies to | Major acquisitions for development, and other acquisitions where agency procedures require the system [@CL-PRG-0850; @CL-PRG-0851] |

An earned value management system is required for major acquisitions for development, in accordance with OMB Circular A-11 [@CL-PRG-0850]. The Government may also require one for other acquisitions, in accordance with agency procedures [@CL-PRG-0851].

An offeror that proposes a system not determined to comply with EIA-748 must submit a comprehensive plan for compliance with these standards [@CL-PRG-0852]. Offerors must not be eliminated from consideration for contract award because they lack a system that complies with these standards [@CL-PRG-0852; @CL-PRG-0853]. When an offeror is required to provide an EVMS plan as part of its proposal, the contracting officer determines the adequacy of that plan before contract award [@CL-PRG-0854].

FAR 2.101 defines an earned value management system as a project management tool that effectively integrates the project scope of work with cost, schedule and performance elements for optimum project planning and control [@CL-PRG-9076]. The qualities and operating characteristics of an earned value management system are described in Electronic Industries Alliance Standard 748 (EIA-748), Earned Value Management Systems [@CL-PRG-9077].

## How it works

Start with the acquisition: a major acquisition for development carries the requirement, and any other acquisition carries it only where the Government requires it under agency procedures [@CL-PRG-0850; @CL-PRG-0851]. Then look at the offeror's system. If it has not been determined to comply with EIA-748, the offeror must submit a compliance plan [@CL-PRG-0852]. Lacking a compliant system does not remove the offeror from award consideration [@CL-PRG-0852; @CL-PRG-0853]. Where a plan is required in the proposal, the contracting officer rules on its adequacy before award [@CL-PRG-0854].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An agency issues a solicitation for a development effort and requires an earned value management system under its own procedures. The solicitation draws 4 offers, and 1 offeror proposes a system that has not been determined compliant. That offeror submits a plan for compliance and stays in the competition. The contracting officer judges the adequacy of the plan before award. The answer: the offeror is not eliminated for lacking a compliant system, but its plan is judged first.

## Never confuse

This card covers the FAR text only. The DoD dollar thresholds, exemptions and the approval and disapproval steps are on [Earned value management system requirement](prg-evms-requirement.md) and [EVMS approval and disapproval](prg-evms-approval-disapproval.md), and are not restated here. The FAR text printed here states no dollar threshold, and the source does not establish one. The monthly reports and the subcontractor rule are on [EVMS monthly reports and subcontractors under the FAR](../eac/prg-far-evms-reports-subcontractors.md), the review that follows a requirement is on [Integrated Baseline Reviews under the FAR](../risk-opportunity/prg-far-integrated-baseline-review.md), and the solicitation provisions and clause are on [Integrated Baseline Review timing, cost, provisions and clause](../risk-opportunity/prg-far-ibr-timing-clauses.md). A plan for compliance is also not a determination of compliance: the plan is what an offeror submits when its system has not been determined to comply [@CL-PRG-0852].
