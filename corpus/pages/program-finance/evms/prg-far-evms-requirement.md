## Purpose

Shows when the FAR requires an earned value management system, what an offeror without a compliant system must do, and when the contracting officer judges the offeror's plan.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-PRG-0850] |
| Codified citation | FAR 34.201(a), (b) and (e) [@CL-PRG-0850; @CL-PRG-0851; @CL-PRG-0852; @CL-PRG-0853; @CL-PRG-0854] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-PRG-0850] |
| Applies to | Major acquisitions for development, and other acquisitions where agency procedures require the system [@CL-PRG-0850; @CL-PRG-0851] |

An Earned Value Management System (EVMS) is required for major acquisitions for development, in accordance with OMB Circular A-11 [@CL-PRG-0850]. The Government may also require an EVMS for other acquisitions, in accordance with agency procedures [@CL-PRG-0851].

If the offeror proposes to use a system that has not been determined to be in compliance with the Electronic Industries Alliance Standard 748 (EIA-748), the offeror shall submit a comprehensive plan for compliance with these EVMS standards [@CL-PRG-0852]. Offerors shall not be eliminated from consideration for contract award because they do not have an EVMS that complies with these standards [@CL-PRG-0853]. When an offeror is required to provide an EVMS plan as part of its proposal, the contracting officer will determine the adequacy of the proposed EVMS plan prior to contract award [@CL-PRG-0854].

Under codified FAR 2.101, earned value management system means a project management tool that effectively integrates the project scope of work with cost, schedule and performance elements for optimum project planning and control [@CL-PRG-9076], and the qualities and operating characteristics of an earned value management system are described in Electronic Industries Alliance Standard 748 (EIA-748), Earned Value Management Systems [@CL-PRG-9077]. Under RFO FAR 2.101, earned value management system means a project management tool that effectively integrates the project scope of work with cost, schedule and performance elements for optimum project planning and control [@CL-PRG-9102], and the qualities and operating characteristics of an earned value management system are described in Electronic Industries Alliance Standard 748 (EIA-748), Earned Value Management Systems [@CL-PRG-9129].

## How it works

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An agency issues a solicitation for a development effort and requires an earned value management system under its own procedures. The solicitation draws 4 offers, and 1 offeror proposes a system that has not been determined compliant. That offeror submits a plan for compliance and stays in the competition. The contracting officer judges the adequacy of the plan before award. The answer: the offeror is not eliminated for lacking a compliant system, but its plan is judged first.
