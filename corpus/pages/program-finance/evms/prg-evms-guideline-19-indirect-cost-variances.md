## Purpose

Shows how the guideline requires indirect cost variances, and the impact of indirect cost performance on individual program performance, to be evaluated [@CL-PRG-0313].

The guideline requires indirect cost variances and the impact of indirect cost performance on individual program performance to be evaluated [@CL-PRG-0313]. The program's indirect cost variance is the portion of the total cost variance attributed to indirect rate adjustments [@CL-PRG-0314].

## How it works

Corporate decisions on rate adjustments and rate forecasts are conveyed to the program management team, and the program incorporates indirect rate adjustments into the performance measurement baseline [@CL-PRG-0709]. When an indirect rate change occurs, the program conducts an impact analysis by indirect rate category, consistent with internal procedures, to determine the contribution of indirect cost variance to the overall program [@CL-PRG-0710].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A program has a total cost variance of minus 100,000. An indirect rate increase accounts for 40,000 of it, so the indirect cost variance is 40,000 unfavorable. The program runs an impact analysis for that indirect rate category to see how much the rate change contributes to the overall program.

## Never confuse

Indirect cost variance is only a portion of the total cost variance, not a separate measure of the whole. It is the part attributed to indirect rate adjustments [@CL-PRG-0314].

The guideline on indirect budgets in the performance measurement baseline is covered in [EVMS Guideline 11: indirect budgets in the performance measurement baseline](prg-evms-guideline-11-indirect-budgets.md).
