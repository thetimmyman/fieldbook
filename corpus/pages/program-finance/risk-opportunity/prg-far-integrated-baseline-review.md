## Purpose

Shows when the Government conducts an Integrated Baseline Review, what the review is for, and the five things the Government and the contractor assess together.

## The rule

When an EVMS is required, the Government will conduct an Integrated Baseline Review [@CL-PRG-0857]. Its purpose is to verify the technical content and the realism of the related performance budgets, resources, and schedules [@CL-PRG-0858]. The review should also provide a mutual understanding of the inherent risks in performance plans and the underlying management control systems, and formulate a plan to handle these risks [@CL-PRG-0858; @CL-PRG-0859].

The review is a joint assessment by the offeror or contractor and the Government of five listed items [@CL-PRG-0860; @CL-PRG-0861; @CL-PRG-0862; @CL-PRG-0863; @CL-PRG-0864; @CL-PRG-0865]. The first is the ability of the project's technical plan to achieve the objectives of the scope of work [@CL-PRG-0860; @CL-PRG-0861]. The second is the adequacy of the time allocated for the defined tasks to achieve the project schedule objectives [@CL-PRG-0860; @CL-PRG-0862]. The third is the ability of the Performance Measurement Baseline to execute the project and attain cost objectives, recognizing the relationship between budget resources, funding, schedule, and scope of work [@CL-PRG-0860; @CL-PRG-0863]. The fourth is the availability of personnel, facilities, and equipment when required to perform the defined tasks [@CL-PRG-0860; @CL-PRG-0864]. The fifth is the degree to which the management process provides effective and integrated technical, schedule and cost planning and baseline control [@CL-PRG-0860; @CL-PRG-0865].

## How it works

The trigger is the requirement for an EVMS, and the Government is the party that conducts the review [@CL-PRG-0857]. The review checks the plan on paper and in people: scope against technical plan, time against schedule objectives, budget against the baseline, resources against need, and the management process that ties them together [@CL-PRG-0861; @CL-PRG-0862; @CL-PRG-0863; @CL-PRG-0864; @CL-PRG-0865]. It should produce a shared view of risk and a plan to handle it [@CL-PRG-0858; @CL-PRG-0859]. The FAR text on timing and pre-award questions is on [Integrated Baseline Review timing, cost, provisions and clause](prg-far-ibr-timing-clauses.md), and the requirement that triggers the review is on [When the FAR requires an earned value management system](../evms/prg-far-evms-requirement.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A program with an EVMS requirement has 30 control accounts. Before the review, the contractor finds that its test engineers are booked on another program during 2 of the planned quarters. In the joint assessment, the team records this under the availability of personnel and facilities, notes the risk, and agrees a plan to handle it. The answer: the gap is an assessed item and produces a risk-handling plan.

## Never confuse

The Performance Measurement Baseline is only one of the five items the review assesses [@CL-PRG-0863]. How the baseline is set up and kept current is on [the performance measurement baseline guideline page](../baseline/prg-evms-guideline-7-performance-measurement-baseline.md) and is not restated here; the review assesses that baseline [@CL-PRG-0863]. Contract type selection and program risk documentation for DoD programs are on [Contract type selection and program risk for major defense acquisition programs](prg-contract-type-program-risk.md); the review is a joint check, not a contract type decision.
