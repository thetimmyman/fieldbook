## Purpose

Shows when the Government conducts an Integrated Baseline Review, the purpose of the review, and what the offeror or contractor and the Government jointly assess.

When an EVMS is required, the Government will conduct an Integrated Baseline Review (IBR) [@CL-PRG-0857]. The purpose of the IBR is to verify the technical content and the realism of the related performance budgets, resources, and schedules [@CL-PRG-0858]. It should provide a mutual understanding of the inherent risks in offerors' and contractors' performance plans and the underlying management control systems, and it should formulate a plan to handle these risks [@CL-PRG-0859].

The IBR is a joint assessment by the offeror or contractor, and the Government, of: (1) the ability of the project's technical plan to achieve the objectives of the scope of work [@CL-PRG-0860; @CL-PRG-0861]; (2) the adequacy of the time allocated for performing the defined tasks to successfully achieve the project schedule objectives [@CL-PRG-0860; @CL-PRG-0862]; (3) the ability of the Performance Measurement Baseline (PMB) to successfully execute the project and attain cost objectives, recognizing the relationship between budget resources, funding, schedule, and scope of work [@CL-PRG-0860; @CL-PRG-0863]; (4) the availability of personnel, facilities, and equipment when required, to perform the defined tasks needed to execute the program successfully [@CL-PRG-0860; @CL-PRG-0864; @CL-PRG-0133]; and (5) the degree to which the management process provides effective and integrated technical, schedule, and cost planning and baseline control [@CL-PRG-0860; @CL-PRG-0133; @CL-PRG-0865].

## How it works

When an EVMS is required, the Government will conduct an IBR [@CL-PRG-0857] to verify the technical content and the realism of the related performance budgets, resources, and schedules [@CL-PRG-0858]. The IBR should provide a mutual understanding of the inherent risks in offerors' and contractors' performance plans and the underlying management control systems, and it should formulate a plan to handle these risks [@CL-PRG-0859]. See [Integrated Baseline Review timing, cost, provisions and clause](prg-far-ibr-timing-clauses.md) and [When the FAR requires an earned value management system](../evms/prg-far-evms-requirement.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A program with an EVMS requirement has 30 control accounts. Before the review, the contractor finds that its test engineers are booked on another program during 2 of the planned quarters. In the joint assessment, the team records this under the availability of personnel and facilities, notes the risk, and agrees a plan to handle it. The answer: the gap is an assessed item and produces a risk-handling plan.

## Never confuse

In the joint assessment by the offeror or contractor, and the Government, the IBR assesses the ability of the Performance Measurement Baseline (PMB) to successfully execute the project and attain cost objectives, recognizing the relationship between budget resources, funding, schedule, and scope of work [@CL-PRG-0860; @CL-PRG-0863], together with the ability of the project's technical plan to achieve the objectives of the scope of work [@CL-PRG-0861], the adequacy of the time allocated for performing the defined tasks to successfully achieve the project schedule objectives [@CL-PRG-0862], the availability of personnel, facilities, and equipment when required, to perform the defined tasks needed to execute the program successfully [@CL-PRG-0864; @CL-PRG-0133], and the degree to which the management process provides effective and integrated technical, schedule, and cost planning and baseline control [@CL-PRG-0133; @CL-PRG-0865]. See [the performance measurement baseline guideline page](../baseline/prg-evms-guideline-7-performance-measurement-baseline.md) and [Contract type selection and program risk for major defense acquisition programs](prg-contract-type-program-risk.md).
