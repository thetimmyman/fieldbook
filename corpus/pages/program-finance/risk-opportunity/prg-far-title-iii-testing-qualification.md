## Purpose

Shows who pays for testing and qualification of industrial resources developed with Title III assistance, how a request is handled, and the clause that goes in contracts.

It is the policy of the Government, as required by section 126 of Public Law 102-558, to pay for any testing and qualification required for the use or incorporation of the industrial resources manufactured or developed with assistance provided under Title III of the Defense Production Act of 1950 [@CL-PRG-0891].

Contractors receiving requests from a Title III project contractor for testing and qualification of a Title III industrial resource shall refer such requests to the contracting officer [@CL-PRG-0892]. The contracting officer shall evaluate the request in accordance with agency procedures to determine whether (1) the Title III industrial resource is being or potentially may be used in the development or manufacture of a major system or item of supply, and (2) for major systems in production, remaining quantities to be acquired are sufficient to justify incurring the cost of testing and qualification [@CL-PRG-0893; @CL-PRG-0894; @CL-PRG-0895; @CL-PRG-0134]. In evaluating this request, the contracting officer shall consult with the Defense Production Act Office, Title III Program, located at Wright Patterson Air Force Base, Ohio 45433-7739 [@CL-PRG-0896; @CL-PRG-0135].

If the determination at FAR 34.103(a) is affirmative, the contracting officer shall modify the contract to require the contractor to test the Title III industrial resource for qualification [@CL-PRG-0897]. The Defense Production Act Office, Title III Program, shall provide to the contractor the industrial resource produced by the Title III project contractor in sufficient amounts to meet testing needs [@CL-PRG-0898]. The clause at FAR 52.234-1, Industrial Resources Developed under Defense Production Act, Title III, is inserted in all contracts for major systems and items of supply [@CL-PRG-0899].

FAR subpart 34.1 prescribes policies and procedures for the testing, qualification, and use of industrial resources manufactured or developed with assistance provided under section 301, 302, or 303 of the Defense Production Act (sections 2091 to 2093 of the appendix to title 50 of the United States Code) [@CL-PRG-9047]. Title III of the Defense Production Act authorizes various forms of Government assistance to encourage expansion of production capacity and supply of industrial resources essential to national defense [@CL-PRG-9048]. As used in FAR subpart 34.1, item of supply means any individual part, component, subassembly, assembly, or subsystem integral to a major system, and other property which may be replaced during the service life of the system [@CL-PRG-9049]. The term includes spare parts and replenishment parts, but does not include packaging or labeling associated with shipment or identification of an item [@CL-PRG-9050].

## How it works

Contractors receiving requests from a Title III project contractor for testing and qualification of a Title III industrial resource shall refer such requests to the contracting officer [@CL-PRG-0892]. The contracting officer shall evaluate the request in accordance with agency procedures to determine whether (1) the Title III industrial resource is being or potentially may be used in the development or manufacture of a major system or item of supply, and (2) for major systems in production, remaining quantities to be acquired are sufficient to justify incurring the cost of testing and qualification [@CL-PRG-0893; @CL-PRG-0894; @CL-PRG-0895; @CL-PRG-0134], and in evaluating this request shall consult with the Defense Production Act Office, Title III Program, located at Wright Patterson Air Force Base, Ohio 45433-7739 [@CL-PRG-0896; @CL-PRG-0135]. If the determination at FAR 34.103(a) is affirmative, the contracting officer shall modify the contract to require the contractor to test the Title III industrial resource for qualification [@CL-PRG-0897], and the Defense Production Act Office, Title III Program, shall provide to the contractor the industrial resource produced by the Title III project contractor in sufficient amounts to meet testing needs [@CL-PRG-0898].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A prime contractor on a major system receives a request to test a new alloy made under Title III assistance. It refers the request to the contracting officer, who consults the Title III office and finds that the alloy may be used in the system and that enough units remain to be bought to justify the cost. The contract is modified to require the test, and the Title III office sends 500 kilograms of the alloy. The answer: the Government pays under its stated policy, and the contractor tests the resource it is given.

## Never confuse

It is the policy of the Government, as required by section 126 of Public Law 102-558, to pay for any testing and qualification required for the use or incorporation of the industrial resources manufactured or developed with assistance provided under Title III of the Defense Production Act of 1950 [@CL-PRG-0891]. Contractors receiving requests from a Title III project contractor for testing and qualification of a Title III industrial resource shall refer such requests to the contracting officer [@CL-PRG-0892], the contracting officer shall modify the contract to require the contractor to test the Title III industrial resource for qualification if the determination at FAR 34.103(a) is affirmative [@CL-PRG-0897], and the Defense Production Act Office, Title III Program, shall provide to the contractor the industrial resource produced by the Title III project contractor in sufficient amounts to meet testing needs [@CL-PRG-0898].
