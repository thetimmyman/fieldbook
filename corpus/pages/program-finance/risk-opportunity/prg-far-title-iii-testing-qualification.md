## Purpose

Shows who pays for testing and qualification of industrial resources developed with Title III assistance, how a request is handled, and the clause that goes in contracts.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-PRG-0891] |
| Codified citation | FAR 34.102, 34.103 and 34.104 [@CL-PRG-0891; @CL-PRG-0892; @CL-PRG-0893; @CL-PRG-0897; @CL-PRG-0898; @CL-PRG-0899] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-PRG-0891] |
| Applies to | Testing and qualification of Title III industrial resources, and all contracts for major systems and items of supply for the clause [@CL-PRG-0891; @CL-PRG-0899] |

It is the policy of the Government to pay for any testing and qualification required for the use or incorporation of the industrial resources manufactured or developed with assistance provided under Title III of the Defense Production Act of 1950 [@CL-PRG-0891].

A contractor that receives a request from a Title III project contractor for testing and qualification of a Title III industrial resource must refer the request to the contracting officer [@CL-PRG-0892]. The contracting officer must evaluate the request in accordance with agency procedures to determine whether the listed conditions are met [@CL-PRG-0893]. One condition is that the Title III industrial resource is being or potentially may be used in the development or manufacture of a major system or item of supply [@CL-PRG-0894]. The other is that, for major systems in production, remaining quantities to be acquired are sufficient to justify incurring the cost of testing and qualification [@CL-PRG-0893; @CL-PRG-0895]. In evaluating a request for testing and qualification, the contracting officer must consult with the Defense Production Act Office, Title III Program [@CL-PRG-0892; @CL-PRG-0896].

If the determination about the listed conditions is affirmative, the contracting officer must modify the contract to require the contractor to test the Title III industrial resource for qualification [@CL-PRG-0893; @CL-PRG-0897]. The Defense Production Act Office, Title III Program, must provide to the contractor the industrial resource produced by the Title III project contractor in sufficient amounts to meet testing needs [@CL-PRG-0898]. The clause at 52.234-1 must be inserted in all contracts for major systems and items of supply [@CL-PRG-0899].

## How it works

The path runs in order: the contractor refers the request, the contracting officer evaluates it and consults the Defense Production Act Office, the contract is modified if the determination is affirmative, and the Title III office supplies the resource to be tested [@CL-PRG-0892; @CL-PRG-0893; @CL-PRG-0896; @CL-PRG-0897; @CL-PRG-0898]. The policy that the Government pays sits behind the whole path [@CL-PRG-0891]. The source does not state how the contract price is adjusted for the testing, so this card does not establish it. The source also does not state what the contractor does if the determination is negative.

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A prime contractor on a major system receives a request to test a new alloy made under Title III assistance. It refers the request to the contracting officer, who consults the Title III office and finds that the alloy may be used in the system and that enough units remain to be bought to justify the cost. The contract is modified to require the test, and the Title III office sends 500 kilograms of the alloy. The answer: the Government pays under its stated policy, and the contractor tests the resource it is given.

## Never confuse

A request from a Title III project contractor does not itself change the contract: the contractor refers it, the contracting officer evaluates it, and only an affirmative determination leads to a modification [@CL-PRG-0892; @CL-PRG-0893; @CL-PRG-0897]. The test is also not done at the contractor's own cost by default, since the stated policy is that the Government pays [@CL-PRG-0891]. The resource under test comes from the Title III office, not from the contractor's own supply [@CL-PRG-0898]. Governance of major system acquisitions is on [Major system acquisition governance under the FAR](../baseline/prg-far-major-system-governance.md).
