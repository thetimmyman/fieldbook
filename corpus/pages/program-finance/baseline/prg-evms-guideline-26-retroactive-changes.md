## Purpose

Shows which adjustments to previously reported cost, earned value, and budget amounts are allowed, and how a single point adjustment is controlled.

## The rule

| Applies to | Retroactive changes to previously reported cost, earned value, or budget amounts for completed work [@CL-PRG-0373] |

The guideline text says adjustments are made only for correction of errors, routine accounting adjustments, or the effects of customer or management directed changes, including implementation of a single point adjustment [@CL-PRG-0372]. The guide’s purpose statement says the changes should be limited to correction of errors, routine accounting adjustments, customer-directed changes, definitization of contract actions, rate changes, economic price adjustments, or implementation of a single point adjustment, and these are alternatives [@CL-PRG-0751]. Retroactive changes involve adjustments to previously reported values for budgeted cost for work scheduled, budgeted cost for work performed, or actual cost of work performed, related to completed work [@CL-PRG-0373].

## How it works

A single point adjustment is implemented sparingly and sets some or all existing contract cost or schedule variances to zero, typically accompanying a replan of all remaining effort that results in a new performance measurement baseline [@CL-PRG-0374]. The contractor provides advance notification to the contracting officer before implementation of a single point adjustment [@CL-PRG-0375].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A program has an accumulated cost variance of 15 and decides to replan all remaining effort. The contractor notifies the contracting officer, then applies a single point adjustment that sets the cost and schedule variances to zero. The resulting baseline is the new performance measurement baseline for the remaining work.

## Never confuse

A single point adjustment is not an ordinary correction. It is one of the permitted adjustments, but it is implemented sparingly, sets some or all existing cost or schedule variances to zero, and needs advance notification to the contracting officer [@CL-PRG-0372; @CL-PRG-0374; @CL-PRG-0375].

## Company calibration

Whether a proposed reset of variances is a permitted retroactive change or a single point adjustment that needs advance notification to the contracting officer. [@Q-025]
