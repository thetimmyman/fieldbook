## Purpose

Defines the four terms the priorities and allocations subpart of the FAR defines, and states the authority behind priorities and allocations, so that an approved program, a Delegate Agency, national defense and a rated order are read the same way.

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-PRG-0481] |
| Codified citation | FAR 11.600 [@CL-PRG-1005], FAR 11.601 and FAR 11.602 [@CL-PRG-0481; @CL-PRG-0482] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |

| Applies to | The defined terms as used in the priorities and allocations subpart of the FAR [@CL-PRG-0486] |

Subpart 11.6 implements the Defense Priorities and Allocations System (DPAS), a Department of Commerce regulation in support of approved national defense, emergency preparedness, and energy programs (see 15 CFR part 700) [@CL-PRG-1005].

As used in subpart 11.6, FAR 11.601 defines four terms [@CL-PRG-0486; @CL-PRG-0476; @CL-PRG-0478; @CL-PRG-0479; @CL-PRG-0481].

### Glossary

| Term | Definition in the section | Claim |
|---|---|---|
| Approved program | A program determined as necessary or appropriate for priorities and allocations support to promote the national defense by the Secretary of Defense, the Secretary of Energy, or the Secretary of Homeland Security, under the authority of the Defense Production Act, the disaster relief and emergency assistance act, and Executive Order 12919, or the Selective Service Act and related statutes and Executive Order 12742 | [@CL-PRG-0476; @CL-PRG-0477; @CL-PRG-0489] |
| Delegate Agency | A Government agency authorized by delegation from the Department of Commerce to place priority ratings on contracts or orders needed to support approved programs | [@CL-PRG-0478] |
| National defense | Programs for military and energy production or construction, military assistance to any foreign nation, stockpiling, space, and any directly related activity, and such term includes emergency preparedness activities conducted pursuant to title VI of the disaster relief and emergency assistance act at title 42, United States Code, section 5195 and following, and critical infrastructure protection and restoration | [@CL-PRG-0479; @CL-PRG-0480; @CL-PRG-0488] |
| Rated order | A prime contract, a subcontract, or a purchase order in support of an approved program issued in accordance with the provisions of the DPAS regulation (15 CFR part 700) | [@CL-PRG-0481] |

Under Title I of the Defense Production Act of 1950, the President is authorized to require preferential acceptance and performance of contracts and orders supporting certain approved national defense and energy programs [@CL-PRG-0482], and to allocate materials, services, and facilities in such a manner as to promote these approved programs [@CL-PRG-0483]. The President delegated the priorities and allocations authorities of the Defense Production Act in Executive Order 12919 [@CL-PRG-0484]. As part of that delegation, the President designated the Secretary of Commerce to administer the DPAS [@CL-PRG-0485]. For more information, FAR 11.602(c) directs readers to check the DPAS website [@CL-PRG-0487].

## How it works

The Secretary of Defense, the Secretary of Energy, or the Secretary of Homeland Security makes the determination that a program is an approved program [@CL-PRG-0476]. A Delegate Agency is authorized by delegation from the Department of Commerce to place priority ratings on contracts or orders needed to support approved programs [@CL-PRG-0478]. The Secretary of Commerce was designated to administer the DPAS [@CL-PRG-0485].

A rated order is a prime contract, a subcontract, or a purchase order in support of an approved program issued in accordance with the provisions of the DPAS regulation (15 CFR part 700) [@CL-PRG-0481].

The national defense definition covers programs for military and energy production or construction, military assistance to any foreign nation, stockpiling, space, and any directly related activity, and includes emergency preparedness activities conducted pursuant to title VI of the disaster relief and emergency assistance act (title 42, United States Code, section 5195 and following) and critical infrastructure protection and restoration [@CL-PRG-0479; @CL-PRG-0480; @CL-PRG-0488].

Priority ratings, acceptance and rejection of rated orders, and allocation orders are on [rated orders](../../industrialization/capacity/ind-dpas-rated-orders.md), [allocation orders](../../industrialization/capacity/ind-dpas-allocation-orders.md) and [priority ratings and authority](../../industrialization/capacity/ind-dpas-priority-ratings-and-authority.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A purchase order for 200 bearings supports a program that the Secretary of Defense has determined as necessary or appropriate for priorities and allocations support to promote the national defense, under the authority of the Defense Production Act and Executive Order 12919 [@CL-PRG-0477; @CL-PRG-0489], and it is issued in accordance with the DPAS regulation. It meets each element of the rated order definition [@CL-PRG-0476; @CL-PRG-0481].

A second purchase order, for 300 bushings, supports no approved program. It does not meet the rated order definition, because the definition requires support of an approved program [@CL-PRG-0481].

A third purchase order, for 100 springs, supports an approved program but was not issued in accordance with the DPAS regulation. The definition's last element is missing, so the order does not meet the definition as written [@CL-PRG-0481]. The DPAS pages cover what follows from that.

## Never confuse

An approved program is determined by the Secretary of Defense, the Secretary of Energy, or the Secretary of Homeland Security [@CL-PRG-0476], whereas a rated order is a prime contract, a subcontract, or a purchase order in support of an approved program issued in accordance with the provisions of the DPAS regulation (15 CFR part 700) [@CL-PRG-0481].

A Delegate Agency is authorized by delegation from the Department of Commerce to place priority ratings on contracts or orders needed to support approved programs [@CL-PRG-0478], while the determination that a program is an approved program is made by the Secretary of Defense, the Secretary of Energy, or the Secretary of Homeland Security [@CL-PRG-0476].

National defense covers programs for military and energy production or construction, military assistance to any foreign nation, stockpiling, space, and any directly related activity [@CL-PRG-0479], and includes emergency preparedness activities conducted pursuant to title VI of the disaster relief and emergency assistance act (title 42, United States Code, section 5195 and following) and critical infrastructure protection and restoration [@CL-PRG-0480; @CL-PRG-0488].
