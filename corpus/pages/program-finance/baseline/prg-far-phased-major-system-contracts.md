## Purpose

Shows how the FAR shapes contracts in the concept exploration, demonstration and full-scale development phases of a major system acquisition, and which conditions apply to each.

### Concept exploration

Whenever practicable, contracts to be performed during the concept exploration phase must be for relatively short periods, at planned dollar levels [@CL-PRG-0885]. Those contracts are to refine the proposed concept and to reduce the concept's technical uncertainties [@CL-PRG-0900]. The scope of work for the concept exploration phase must be consistent with the Government's planned budget for the phase [@CL-PRG-0885; @CL-PRG-0886]. Follow-on contracts for such tasks in the exploration phase must be awarded as long as three conditions hold: the concept approach remains promising, the contractor's progress is acceptable, and it is economically practicable to do so [@CL-PRG-0900; @CL-PRG-0887].

### Demonstration

Whenever practicable, contracts for the demonstration phase should provide for contractors to submit, by the end of the phase, priced proposals, totally funded by the Government, for full-scale development [@CL-PRG-0888]. The contracting officer should provide contractors with operational test conditions, performance criteria, life cycle cost factors, and any other selection criteria necessary for the contractors to prepare their proposals [@CL-PRG-0889].

### Full-scale development

Whenever practicable, the full-scale development contracts should provide for the contractors to submit priced proposals for production that are based on the latest quantity, schedule, and logistics requirements and other considerations that will be used in making the production decision [@CL-PRG-0890].

## How it works

In concept exploration, contracts are to be for relatively short periods at planned dollar levels whenever practicable, with a scope of work consistent with the Government's planned budget for the phase and follow-on awards tied to the three stated conditions [@CL-PRG-0885; @CL-PRG-0886; @CL-PRG-0887; @CL-PRG-0900]. In demonstration, contracts should provide whenever practicable for contractors to submit, by the end of the phase, priced proposals, totally funded by the Government, for full-scale development, and the contracting officer should provide contractors with operational test conditions, performance criteria, life cycle cost factors, and any other selection criteria necessary to prepare their proposals [@CL-PRG-0888; @CL-PRG-0889]. In full-scale development, contracts should provide whenever practicable for the contractors to submit priced proposals for production that are based on the latest quantity, schedule, and logistics requirements and other considerations that will be used in making the production decision [@CL-PRG-0890].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An agency awards a concept exploration contract for 6 months at a planned level of 2 million, and the scope of work fits the budget set for the phase. Progress is acceptable, the concept still looks promising, and it is economically practicable, so a follow-on task is awarded. At the end of demonstration, the contractor submits a priced proposal for full-scale development, totally funded by the Government. The answer: the Government funds the demonstration proposal, and each award follows the stated condition for its phase.

## Never confuse

The contract features for the three phases are stated with "whenever practicable" and "shall" or "should" [@CL-PRG-0885; @CL-PRG-0888; @CL-PRG-0890], while the sentences on the scope of work and on follow-on awards in the concept exploration phase use "shall" [@CL-PRG-0886; @CL-PRG-0887]. For the agency head's approval to proceed to full production, see [Major system acquisition governance under the FAR](prg-far-major-system-governance.md). For contract type choice for DoD programs, see [Contract type selection and program risk for major defense acquisition programs](../risk-opportunity/prg-contract-type-program-risk.md).
