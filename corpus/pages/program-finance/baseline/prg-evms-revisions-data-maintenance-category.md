## Purpose

Shows how the four guidelines in the Revisions and Data Maintenance category establish a formal change control process to preserve the integrity of the performance measurement baseline and the associated earned value data [@CL-PRG-0645].

The objective of the four guidelines (Guidelines 24 through 27) in the Revisions and Data Maintenance category is to establish the requirements for implementing a formal change control process to preserve the integrity of the performance measurement baseline and the associated earned value data [@CL-PRG-0645]. Since the performance measurement baseline represents the agreed-upon plan between the contractor and the government, any changes to the performance measurement baseline must be formally controlled and properly documented using a systematic approach (Guidelines 24 through 27) [@CL-PRG-0646].

To ensure the performance measurement baseline always reflects all authorized work scope, authorized contractual changes must be incorporated into budgets, schedules, work authorizations, and other program documents in a timely manner prior to the commencement of that work (Guideline 24) [@CL-PRG-0647; @CL-PRG-0111]. See [the customer-directed changes guideline](prg-evms-guideline-24-customer-directed-changes.md). To maintain the accuracy and validity of performance measurement data, and its use for making reliable cost and schedule projections, retroactive changes to the data are controlled and limited to only certain circumstances (Guideline 26) [@CL-PRG-0648; @CL-PRG-0112]. See [the retroactive changes guideline](prg-evms-guideline-26-retroactive-changes.md). Authorized baseline revisions are documented, managed, tracked, and reported to the program manager and the government in a timely manner (Guidelines 26 and 27) [@CL-PRG-0649; @CL-PRG-0113].

## How it works

The four guidelines (Guidelines 24 through 27) establish a formal change control process to preserve the integrity of the performance measurement baseline and the associated earned value data [@CL-PRG-0645], and require that authorized baseline revisions be documented, managed, tracked, and reported to the program manager and the government in a timely manner [@CL-PRG-0649; @CL-PRG-0113]. Within the category, the guidelines cover [customer-directed changes](prg-evms-guideline-24-customer-directed-changes.md), [internal replanning](prg-evms-guideline-25-internal-replanning.md), [retroactive changes](prg-evms-guideline-26-retroactive-changes.md), and [over-target baselines and schedules](prg-evms-guideline-27-over-target-baseline.md) [@CL-PRG-0645; @CL-PRG-0646].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

The government adds scope worth 600,000 to a contract. The company puts the new scope into budgets, schedules and work authorizations before the work starts. It logs the change, tracks it and reports it to the program manager and the customer. The answer: the baseline is revised before the work, and the change is on record.

## Never confuse

Authorized contractual changes must be incorporated into budgets, schedules, work authorizations, and other program documents in a timely manner prior to the commencement of that work so that the performance measurement baseline reflects all authorized work scope [@CL-PRG-0647; @CL-PRG-0111], whereas retroactive changes to performance measurement data are controlled and limited to only certain circumstances to maintain the accuracy and validity of performance measurement data and its use for making reliable cost and schedule projections [@CL-PRG-0648; @CL-PRG-0112]. See [the retroactive changes guideline](prg-evms-guideline-26-retroactive-changes.md).
