## Purpose

Shows the policy for acquiring a major system and the duties it places on the agency that acquires one. It states no threshold, deadline or dollar figure.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-PRG-0400] |
| Codified citation | FAR 34.002 [@CL-PRG-0400]; FAR 34.000 [@CL-PRG-9044] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-PRG-0400] |
| Applies to | Agencies acquiring major systems [@CL-PRG-0401] |

The policies of this part are designed to ensure that agencies acquire major systems in the most effective, economical, and timely manner [@CL-PRG-0400]. The section says that agencies acquiring major systems shall do what paragraphs (a) and (b) set out [@CL-PRG-0401].

Under paragraph (a), the agency must promote innovation and full and open competition, as part 6 requires, in the development of major system concepts [@CL-PRG-0402]. It does so in two ways. It expresses agency needs and major system acquisition program objectives in terms of the agency's mission and not in terms of specified systems to satisfy needs [@CL-PRG-0403]. It also focuses agency resources and special management attention on activities conducted in the initial stage of major programs [@CL-PRG-0404].

Under paragraph (b), the agency must sustain effective competition between alternative system concepts and sources for as long as it is beneficial [@CL-PRG-0405].

FAR part 34 describes acquisition policies and procedures for use in acquiring major systems consistent with OMB Circular A-109, and the use of an Earned Value Management System in acquisitions designated as major acquisitions consistent with OMB Circular A-11, Part 7 [@CL-PRG-9044].

FAR 2.101 defines a major system as that combination of elements that will function together to produce the capabilities required to fulfill a mission need [@CL-PRG-9074]. The elements may include hardware, equipment, software, or any combination thereof, but exclude construction or other improvements to real property [@CL-PRG-9075]. This card does not set out the tests the definition gives for when a system is a major system.

## How it works

Every duty in the section falls on the agency that acquires the major system [@CL-PRG-0401]. The section names agencies as the party that must act, so it is read as a rule about agency conduct and not contractor conduct [@CL-PRG-0401].

Paragraph (a) governs how concepts are developed. The need is written as an agency mission and not as a named system [@CL-PRG-0403]. Resources and special management attention go to the initial stage of major programs [@CL-PRG-0404].

Paragraph (b) governs how long competition lasts. The duty to sustain effective competition between alternative system concepts and sources lasts for as long as it is beneficial [@CL-PRG-0405]. The section does not say who judges whether continued competition is beneficial, and it states no test, threshold or deadline for that judgement [@CL-PRG-0405]. For related rules, see the program manager's competition duty ([Competition throughout a major system acquisition](prg-far-competition-major-system-acquisition.md)), the solicitation ([Mission-oriented solicitations for major systems](prg-far-mission-oriented-solicitation.md)) and the wider governance of major systems ([Major system governance](prg-far-major-system-governance.md); [Concept, demonstration and full-scale development contracts](prg-far-phased-major-system-contracts.md)).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

An agency has three alternative concepts for a major system. It must state its need as a mission and not as a named system [@CL-PRG-0403]. It must also sustain effective competition between the alternative concepts and sources for as long as that is beneficial [@CL-PRG-0405]. Suppose the agency judges that funding the third concept no longer brings any benefit. The duty to sustain competition for that concept then lasts only as long as the benefit does [@CL-PRG-0405]. The section does not say who makes that call or how, so the example does not assume one [@CL-PRG-0405]. The agency must still state its need as a mission and not as a named system [@CL-PRG-0403].

## Never confuse

Promoting innovation and full and open competition in developing concepts is a different duty from sustaining effective competition between alternative concepts and sources. The first is stated with reference to part 6 and the development of concepts [@CL-PRG-0402]. The second lasts for as long as it is beneficial [@CL-PRG-0405]. Neither duty is placed on the contractor; the section places them on agencies [@CL-PRG-0401].
