Document and reconcile changes to current scope, schedule, and budgets as a result of internal replanning [@CL-PRG-0099]. Internal replanning is intended to maintain an executable baseline for the remaining in-scope work on the contract [@CL-PRG-0367]. Contract budget logs provide traceability of authorized baseline changes [@CL-PRG-0368].

## How it works

To ensure performance measurement baseline stability for accurate performance measurement, the system description must describe and establish a freeze period suitable for forward planning discipline and the integrity of the performance measurement baseline [@CL-PRG-0100; @CL-PRG-0369]. Baseline changes within the freeze period are limited, for example to customer-directed contract actions, rate changes, and economic price adjustments [@CL-PRG-0370; @CL-PRG-0104]. The contractor should define the process for internal replanning related to setting budgeted cost for work scheduled equal to budgeted cost for work performed, not the reverse, when eliminating schedule variances for in-process work [@CL-PRG-0371]. Internal replanning should not be used as an alternative to proper initial planning, nor to mask legitimate variances [@CL-PRG-0760]. Where level-of-effort budgets are in the current period (baseline start or finish) and the support work is not necessary to be performed in this period as well as future periods, the work may be replanned as long as no actual costs for that effort have previously been incurred [@CL-PRG-0761]. Once actual costs exist, the work may be replanned only in future periods, to reflect when the remaining work is expected to be performed [@CL-PRG-0762].

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A level-of-effort support work package is budgeted in the current period, but the engineering work it supports has slipped, so the support is not needed until the following quarter. No actual costs have been charged to the package. The contractor replans the package into the quarter when the support is expected, outside the freeze period, and records the change in the contract budget log so the new plan reconciles to the prior plan. Had costs already been charged, only the remaining work could move, and only into future periods. A schedule variance on a discrete work package that reflects real underperformance is not a reason to replan: the variance stays visible.

## Never confuse

Guideline 25 addresses documenting and reconciling changes to current scope, schedule, and budgets as a result of internal replanning to maintain an executable baseline for the remaining in-scope work on the contract, with contract budget logs providing traceability of authorized baseline changes [@CL-PRG-0099; @CL-PRG-0367; @CL-PRG-0368], whereas Guideline 24 addresses incorporating customer directed changes in a timely manner and incorporating authorized changes into the performance measurement baseline before the commencement of work [@CL-PRG-0098; @CL-PRG-0366].
