## Purpose

Shows who carries the competition duty during a major system acquisition, what notice the proposed acquisition must get, when foreign contractors, technology and equipment may be considered, and how the contracting officer should time solicitation and award. The section states no dollar threshold or deadline.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-PRG-0406] |
| Codified citation | FAR 34.005-1 [@CL-PRG-0406]; FAR 34.001 [@CL-PRG-9045; @CL-PRG-9046] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-PRG-0406] |
| Applies to | The program manager and the contracting officer in a major system acquisition [@CL-PRG-0406; @CL-PRG-0409] |

The program manager must, throughout the acquisition process, promote full and open competition and sustain effective competition between alternative major system concepts and sources, as long as it is economically beneficial and practicable to do so [@CL-PRG-0406].

Notice of the proposed acquisition must be given the broadest and most effective circulation practicable throughout the business, academic, and Government communities [@CL-PRG-0407]. Foreign contractors, technology, and equipment may be considered when it is feasible and permissible to do so [@CL-PRG-0408].

The contracting officer should time solicitation issuance and contract award to maintain continuity of concept development during the transition from withdrawing concept proposer to new contractor [@CL-PRG-0409].

As used in part 34, effective competition is a market condition that exists when two or more contractors, acting independently, actively contend for the Government's business in a manner that ensures that the Government will be offered the lowest cost or price alternative or best technical design meeting its minimum needs [@CL-PRG-9045; @CL-PRG-9046].

## How it works

The competition duty is the program manager's, and it runs throughout the acquisition process [@CL-PRG-0406]. It has two parts: promoting full and open competition, and sustaining effective competition between alternative major system concepts and sources [@CL-PRG-0406]. The condition "as long as it is economically beneficial and practicable to do so" follows both parts in one sentence [@CL-PRG-0406]. The section does not say who judges whether it is met.

Notice is a separate obligation. Its measure is the broadest and most effective circulation practicable across the business, academic, and Government communities [@CL-PRG-0407]. The sentence is passive and does not name who gives the notice [@CL-PRG-0407].

Foreign contractors, technology, and equipment may be considered. The text permits this and does not require it, and it applies when consideration is feasible and permissible [@CL-PRG-0408]. The sentence does not say who considers them or who decides what is feasible and permissible.

The contracting officer's duty is timing. It is written as "should", and its aim is continuity of concept development when work passes from a withdrawing concept proposer to a new contractor [@CL-PRG-0409]. The section does not define the program manager or the concept proposer.

For the policy that sits behind these duties, see [Acquisition policy for major systems](prg-far-policy-major-system-acquisition.md). For the solicitation itself, see [Mission-oriented solicitations for major systems](prg-far-mission-oriented-solicitation.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A program has two competing concept sources. The program manager must promote full and open competition and sustain effective competition between the alternative concepts and sources, as long as that is economically beneficial and practicable [@CL-PRG-0406]. Suppose funding the second source is no longer economically beneficial. The condition then no longer supports sustaining that competition [@CL-PRG-0406]. Because the condition follows both duties in one sentence, the example does not say whether it also limits the promotion duty [@CL-PRG-0406]. The text does not say who makes the finding.

Notice of the proposed acquisition still has to get the broadest and most effective circulation practicable [@CL-PRG-0407]. Suppose the first concept proposer withdraws and a new contractor is to take over. The contracting officer should time solicitation issuance and award to maintain continuity of concept development [@CL-PRG-0409]. That is guidance and not a fixed deadline.

## Never confuse

The program manager's duty to promote and sustain competition is not the contracting officer's timing duty. The first is a "shall" in paragraph (a) [@CL-PRG-0406]. The second is a "should" in paragraph (b) [@CL-PRG-0409]. The notice sentence does not name who gives notice [@CL-PRG-0407]. The foreign contractor sentence says "may" and is not a requirement to seek foreign sources [@CL-PRG-0408].
