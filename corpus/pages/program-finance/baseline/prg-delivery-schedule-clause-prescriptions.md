## Purpose

Shows when the contracting officer may or shall put a delivery or completion schedule clause into a solicitation or contract, which alternate the section ties to each basis for the schedule, and which clauses may be changed.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-PRG-0457] |
| Codified citation | FAR 11.404 [@CL-PRG-0457] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-PRG-0457] |
| Applies to | Delivery clauses in solicitations and contracts other than those for construction and architect-engineering; the completion clause in solicitations and contracts when a fixed-price construction contract is contemplated [@CL-PRG-0459; @CL-PRG-0464; @CL-PRG-0469] |

Supplies or services. The contracting officer may use a time of delivery clause to set forth a required delivery schedule and to allow an offeror to propose an alternative delivery schedule [@CL-PRG-0457]. The clauses and their alternates may be used in solicitations and contracts for other than construction and architect-engineering substantially as shown, or they may be changed or new clauses written [@CL-PRG-0458].

Time of Delivery. The contracting officer may insert a clause substantially the same as the clause at 52.211-8, Time of Delivery, in solicitations and contracts other than those for construction and architect-engineering, if the Government requires delivery by a particular time and the delivery schedule is to be based on the date of the contract [@CL-PRG-0459; @CL-PRG-0460]. The section then names three alternates. If the schedule is expressed in specific calendar dates or specific periods and is based on an assumed date of award, the contracting officer may use the clause with Alternate I [@CL-PRG-0461]. If it is expressed that way and is based on an assumed date the contractor will receive notice of award, the contracting officer may use Alternate II [@CL-PRG-0462]. If the schedule is to be based on the actual date the contractor receives a written notice of award, the contracting officer may use Alternate III [@CL-PRG-0463].

Desired and Required Time of Delivery. The contracting officer may insert a clause substantially the same as the clause at 52.211-9, Desired and Required Time of Delivery, in the same kinds of solicitations and contracts, if the Government desires delivery by a certain time but requires delivery by a specified later time, and the delivery schedule is to be based on the date of the contract [@CL-PRG-0464; @CL-PRG-0465]. Alternates I, II and III carry the same three conditions as for the Time of Delivery clause [@CL-PRG-0466; @CL-PRG-0467; @CL-PRG-0468].

Construction. The contracting officer shall insert the clause at 52.211-10, Commencement, Prosecution, and Completion of Work, in solicitations and contracts when a fixed-price construction contract is contemplated [@CL-PRG-0469]. The clause may be changed to accommodate the issuance of orders under indefinite-delivery contracts [@CL-PRG-0470]. If the completion date is expressed as a specific calendar date, computed on the basis of the contractor receiving the notice to proceed by a certain day, the contracting officer may use the clause with its Alternate I [@CL-PRG-0471].

## How it works

The prescriptions are addressed to the contracting officer. Each delivery clause and each alternate is a permission written with may, so the contracting officer chooses among them [@CL-PRG-0457; @CL-PRG-0459; @CL-PRG-0464; @CL-PRG-0461; @CL-PRG-0462; @CL-PRG-0463]. The construction clause itself is written with shall and tied to a contemplated fixed-price construction contract, while its Alternate I and its change for indefinite-delivery orders are written with may [@CL-PRG-0469; @CL-PRG-0470; @CL-PRG-0471].

The FAR's general definitions say an alternate is a substantive variation of a basic provision or clause prescribed for use in a defined circumstance [@CL-PRG-9067]. The first sentence of the RFO model text definition of an alternate in FAR 2.101 reads the same as the codified first sentence [@CL-PRG-9101; @CL-PRG-9067].

The Government's need points to the clause. A required delivery by a particular time points to the Time of Delivery clause [@CL-PRG-0459; @CL-PRG-0460]. A desired time with a later required time points to the Desired and Required Time of Delivery clause [@CL-PRG-0464; @CL-PRG-0465]. In both cases the schedule is to be based on the date of the contract [@CL-PRG-0460; @CL-PRG-0465].

The basis of the schedule points to the alternate. Alternates I and II may be used for a schedule expressed in specific calendar dates or specific periods, based on an assumed date of award or an assumed date the contractor will receive notice of award [@CL-PRG-0461; @CL-PRG-0462]. Alternate III may be used for a schedule to be based on the actual date the contractor receives a written notice of award [@CL-PRG-0463]. The Desired and Required Time of Delivery clause has the same three alternates on the same conditions [@CL-PRG-0466; @CL-PRG-0467; @CL-PRG-0468].

For the delivery clauses, the section also allows changed clauses and new clauses, in solicitations and contracts other than those for construction and architect-engineering [@CL-PRG-0458]. For the construction clause, the section allows a change to accommodate orders under indefinite-delivery contracts [@CL-PRG-0470]. The clause texts are on [time of delivery](../../supply-chain/procure-to-pay/sup-time-of-delivery.md), [desired and required time of delivery](../../supply-chain/procure-to-pay/sup-desired-and-required-time-of-delivery.md) and [commencement, prosecution and completion of work](../../supply-chain/procure-to-pay/sup-commencement-prosecution-and-completion.md). The factors that go into the schedule itself are on [the schedule factors card](prg-delivery-schedule-factors.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A solicitation for 500 pumps says the Government requires delivery by a particular time, and the schedule is to be based on the date of the contract. The contracting officer may insert a clause substantially the same as the Time of Delivery clause [@CL-PRG-0459; @CL-PRG-0460].

A second solicitation, for 300 valves, uses the Time of Delivery clause because the Government requires delivery by a particular time [@CL-PRG-0459; @CL-PRG-0460], and it states delivery in specific calendar dates based on an assumed date of award. The contracting officer may use the clause with Alternate I [@CL-PRG-0461]. Had the dates been based on an assumed date the contractor will receive notice of award, the contracting officer could use Alternate II [@CL-PRG-0462]. Had the schedule been based on the actual date the contractor receives a written notice of award, the contracting officer could use Alternate III [@CL-PRG-0463]. In any of these cases the section also allows a changed clause or a new clause [@CL-PRG-0458].

A third solicitation, for 200 gaskets, says the Government desires delivery by one date but requires delivery by a later date, based on the date of the contract. The contracting officer may insert a clause substantially the same as the Desired and Required Time of Delivery clause [@CL-PRG-0464; @CL-PRG-0465].

A fixed-price construction contract is contemplated for a warehouse. The contracting officer shall insert the Commencement, Prosecution, and Completion of Work clause [@CL-PRG-0469]. The completion date is a specific calendar date computed on the basis of the contractor receiving the notice to proceed by a certain day, so the contracting officer may use the clause with Alternate I [@CL-PRG-0471]. The contract is an indefinite-delivery contract under which orders will be issued, so the contracting officer may change the clause to accommodate the issuance of those orders [@CL-PRG-0470].

## Never confuse

A Time of Delivery clause and a Desired and Required Time of Delivery clause are not the same. The first fits a requirement for delivery by a particular time. The second fits a desired time and a later required time [@CL-PRG-0459; @CL-PRG-0460; @CL-PRG-0464; @CL-PRG-0465].

An alternate is tied to the date the schedule is based on and, for Alternates I and II, to how the schedule is expressed, not to the goods or services [@CL-PRG-0461; @CL-PRG-0462; @CL-PRG-0463].

Alternate I of the construction clause is not Alternate I of a delivery clause. The construction alternate concerns a completion date computed from the notice to proceed [@CL-PRG-0471]. The delivery alternate concerns a schedule based on an assumed date of award [@CL-PRG-0461].

The delivery clauses in paragraph (a) are for solicitations and contracts other than those for construction and architect-engineering [@CL-PRG-0459; @CL-PRG-0464].
