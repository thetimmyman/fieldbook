## Purpose

Lists the factors FAR 11.402 names for establishing a contract delivery or performance schedule and for scheduling the time for completion of a construction contract [@CL-PRG-0450; @CL-PRG-0453].

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-PRG-0450] |
| Codified citation | FAR 11.402 [@CL-PRG-0450] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-PRG-0450] |
| Applies to | Delivery or performance schedules (paragraph (a)) and the time for completion of a construction contract (paragraph (b)) [@CL-PRG-0450; @CL-PRG-0453] |

When establishing a contract delivery or performance schedule, consideration shall be given to applicable factors such as urgency of need, industry practices, market conditions, transportation time and production time [@CL-PRG-0450]. The listed factors in paragraph (a) also include capabilities of small business concerns, administrative time for obtaining and evaluating offers and for awarding contracts, time for contractors to comply with any conditions precedent to contract performance, and time for the Government to perform its obligations under the contract, such as furnishing Government property [@CL-PRG-0450; @CL-PRG-0451; @CL-PRG-0097; @CL-PRG-0452].

When scheduling the time for completion of a construction contract, the contracting officer shall consider applicable factors such as the nature and complexity of the project, the construction seasons involved, the required completion date, the availability of materials and equipment, the capacity of the contractor to perform, and the use of multiple completion dates [@CL-PRG-0453; @CL-PRG-0454].

In any given construction contract, separate completion dates may be established for separable items of work [@CL-PRG-0453; @CL-PRG-0455]. Where multiple completion dates are used, requests for extension of time must be evaluated with respect to each item, and the affected completion dates modified when appropriate [@CL-PRG-0456].

## How it works

Both paragraphs introduce their lists as applicable factors such as those listed [@CL-PRG-0450; @CL-PRG-0453].

Paragraph (a) states that when establishing a contract delivery or performance schedule, consideration shall be given to applicable factors [@CL-PRG-0450], while paragraph (b) states that when scheduling the time for completion of a construction contract, the contracting officer shall consider applicable factors [@CL-PRG-0453].

Paragraph (a) includes capabilities of small business concerns, administrative time for obtaining and evaluating offers and for awarding contracts, time for contractors to comply with any conditions precedent to contract performance, and time for the Government to perform its obligations under the contract, such as furnishing Government property [@CL-PRG-0450; @CL-PRG-0451; @CL-PRG-0097; @CL-PRG-0452]. Paragraph (b) includes the nature and complexity of the project, the construction seasons involved, the required completion date, the availability of materials and equipment, the capacity of the contractor to perform, and the use of multiple completion dates [@CL-PRG-0453; @CL-PRG-0454].

Under paragraph (b), separate completion dates may be established in any given contract for separable items of work [@CL-PRG-0455], and where multiple completion dates are used, requests for extension of time must be evaluated with respect to each item, and the affected completion dates modified when appropriate [@CL-PRG-0456].

The sections that set the delivery and completion clauses are on [the clause prescriptions card](prg-delivery-schedule-clause-prescriptions.md) and [delivery schedules and liquidated damages](../../supply-chain/procure-to-pay/sup-delivery-schedules-and-liquidated-damages.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A supply requirement needs 90 days of production and 14 days of transportation, and the Government needs 30 days to evaluate offers. A draft schedule assumes shipment the day after award. Paragraph (a) says consideration shall be given to applicable factors such as transportation time and production time [@CL-PRG-0450], and to the administrative time for obtaining and evaluating offers and for awarding contracts [@CL-PRG-0450; @CL-PRG-0451]. It shows that production time, transportation time, and administrative time for obtaining and evaluating offers are listed factors in paragraph (a) [@CL-PRG-0450; @CL-PRG-0451].

A construction contract sets separate completion dates for two separable items, a foundation and a roof [@CL-PRG-0455]. The contractor requests more time for the foundation. The request is evaluated with respect to the foundation item, and the affected completion dates are modified when appropriate [@CL-PRG-0456]. Whether the roof date is modified depends on whether it is one of the affected completion dates and whether modification is appropriate [@CL-PRG-0456].

## Never confuse

Paragraph (a) lists nine example factors for establishing a contract delivery or performance schedule [@CL-PRG-0450; @CL-PRG-0451; @CL-PRG-0097; @CL-PRG-0452], whereas paragraph (b) lists six example factors for scheduling the time for completion of a construction contract [@CL-PRG-0453; @CL-PRG-0454].

Under paragraph (b), where multiple completion dates are used for separable items of work, requests for extension of time must be evaluated with respect to each item, and the affected completion dates modified when appropriate [@CL-PRG-0455; @CL-PRG-0456].

Paragraph (b) states that the contracting officer shall consider applicable factors when scheduling the time for completion of a construction contract [@CL-PRG-0453], while paragraph (a) states that consideration shall be given to applicable factors when establishing a contract delivery or performance schedule [@CL-PRG-0450].
