## Purpose

Lists the factors the FAR names for setting a delivery, performance or construction completion schedule, and notes that only the construction paragraph names who must consider them.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-PRG-0450] |
| Codified citation | FAR 11.402 [@CL-PRG-0450] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-PRG-0450] |
| Applies to | Delivery or performance schedules (paragraph (a)) and the time for completion of a construction contract (paragraph (b)) [@CL-PRG-0450; @CL-PRG-0453] |

Supplies or services. When a contract delivery or performance schedule is established, consideration shall be given to applicable factors such as urgency of need, industry practices, market conditions, transportation time and production time [@CL-PRG-0450]. The listed factors continue with the capabilities of small business concerns, the administrative time for obtaining and evaluating offers and for awarding contracts, and the time for contractors to comply with any conditions precedent to contract performance [@CL-PRG-0451]. The last factor is the time for the Government to perform its obligations under the contract, for example furnishing Government property [@CL-PRG-0452].

Construction. When scheduling the time for completion of a construction contract, the contracting officer shall consider applicable factors such as the nature and complexity of the project, the construction seasons involved and the required completion date [@CL-PRG-0453]. The factors continue with the availability of materials and equipment, the capacity of the contractor to perform and the use of multiple completion dates [@CL-PRG-0454].

Paragraph (b) adds two rules on completion dates. In any given contract, separate completion dates may be established for separable items of work [@CL-PRG-0455]. When multiple completion dates are used, requests for extension of time must be evaluated with respect to each item, and the affected completion dates modified when appropriate [@CL-PRG-0456].

## How it works

Both lists are introduced as applicable factors such as those named, so each list gives examples of factors to consider [@CL-PRG-0450; @CL-PRG-0453].

The duty to give consideration in paragraph (a) is written in the passive voice, and the quoted sentence names no official [@CL-PRG-0450]. Paragraph (b) puts the duty on the contracting officer [@CL-PRG-0453]. This card does not name who decides for supplies or services, because the section does not.

The two lists differ. Paragraph (a) lists the capabilities of small business concerns [@CL-PRG-0451]. Paragraph (b) lists the capacity of the contractor to perform [@CL-PRG-0454]. Paragraph (a) also lists time for contractors to comply with conditions precedent to contract performance [@CL-PRG-0451] and time for the Government to perform its obligations under the contract [@CL-PRG-0452].

Paragraph (b) says that separate completion dates may be set for separable items [@CL-PRG-0455]. It says that, when multiple completion dates are used, each extension request must be evaluated with respect to its item, and the affected dates modified when appropriate [@CL-PRG-0456]. The section does not name who establishes separate completion dates or who evaluates a request [@CL-PRG-0455; @CL-PRG-0456].

The sections that set the delivery and completion clauses are on [the clause prescriptions card](prg-delivery-schedule-clause-prescriptions.md) and [delivery schedules and liquidated damages](../../supply-chain/procure-to-pay/sup-delivery-schedules-and-liquidated-damages.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A supply requirement needs 90 days of production and 14 days of transportation, and the Government needs 30 days to evaluate offers. A draft schedule assumes shipment the day after award. Paragraph (a) says consideration shall be given to applicable factors such as transportation time and production time [@CL-PRG-0450], and to the administrative time for obtaining and evaluating offers [@CL-PRG-0451]. The draft schedule shows no consideration of production time or transportation time. The section does not fix a number of days and does not say what a schedule must contain [@CL-PRG-0450], so the example reaches no conclusion on how long the schedule should be. It shows only that production time and transportation time, two listed factors, are not reflected in the draft.

A construction contract sets separate completion dates for two separable items, a foundation and a roof [@CL-PRG-0455]. The contractor requests more time for the foundation. The request is evaluated with respect to the foundation item, and the affected completion dates are modified when appropriate [@CL-PRG-0456]. Whether the roof date is modified depends on whether it is one of the affected completion dates and whether modification is appropriate [@CL-PRG-0456].

## Never confuse

The two lists are not one list. Paragraph (a) has nine numbered factors and paragraph (b) has six [@CL-PRG-0450; @CL-PRG-0451; @CL-PRG-0452; @CL-PRG-0453; @CL-PRG-0454].

The extension-request rule is stated for contracts that use multiple completion dates [@CL-PRG-0456]. This card states no rule for other contracts.

The construction duty falls on the contracting officer [@CL-PRG-0453]. The supplies and services paragraph does not name the person who gives consideration [@CL-PRG-0450].
