## Purpose

Shows what the final solicitation for a major system must state, what each offeror is told it is free to propose, and which presolicitation steps the contracting officer should take before it. The section states no dollar threshold or deadline.

## The rule

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Codified FAR [@CL-PRG-0410] |
| Codified citation | FAR 34.005-2 [@CL-PRG-0410] |
| RFO citation | Not established. |
| Edition date | Not established. |
| Text status | Final [@CL-PRG-0410] |
| Applies to | The contracting officer issuing the solicitation; offerors receive the final solicitation [@CL-PRG-0410; @CL-PRG-0416] |

**Before the solicitation (paragraph (a)).** Before issuing the solicitation, whenever practicable and consistent with agency procedures, the contracting officer should take the actions outlined in paragraphs (a)(1) and (a)(2) [@CL-PRG-0410].

Advance notification of the acquisition should be given the widest practicable dissemination, including publicizing through the Governmentwide point of entry (see subpart 5.2) [@CL-PRG-0411]. It should also be sent to as wide a selection of potential sources as practicable, including smaller and newer firms, Government laboratories, federally funded research and development centers, educational institutions and other not-for-profit organizations [@CL-PRG-0412]. It should go to foreign sources if that would be beneficial and is not prohibited [@CL-PRG-0413].

If appropriate, the step is to hold a presolicitation conference (see 15.201), send copies of the proposed solicitation to all prospective offerors for their comments, or both [@CL-PRG-0414]. After evaluation of the comments, the solicitation should be revised, if appropriate [@CL-PRG-0415].

**The final solicitation (paragraph (b)).** The contracting officer must send the final solicitation to all prospective offerors [@CL-PRG-0416]. The final solicitation must:

- describe the nature of the need in terms of mission capabilities required, without reference to any specific systems to satisfy the need [@CL-PRG-0417];
- indicate, and explain when appropriate, the schedule, capability, and cost objectives and any known constraints in the acquisition [@CL-PRG-0418];
- provide, or indicate how access can be obtained to, all Government data related to the acquisition [@CL-PRG-0419];
- include selection requirements consistent with the acquisition strategy [@CL-PRG-0420];
- clearly state that each offeror is free to propose its own technical approach, main design features, subsystems, and alternatives to schedule, cost, and capability goals [@CL-PRG-0423]; and
- require the use of an Earned Value Management System that complies with the guidelines of Electronic Industries Alliance Standard 748 (EIA-748), the current version at the time of solicitation [@CL-PRG-0421].

Paragraph (b)(6) also points to FAR 34.201 for earned value management systems and reporting requirements [@CL-PRG-0424].

**Government specifications (paragraph (c)).** To the extent practicable, the solicitation must not reference or mandate Government specifications or standards, unless the agency is mandating a subsystem or other component as approved under agency procedure [@CL-PRG-0422].

## How it works

The duties fall on the contracting officer, who must send the final solicitation to all prospective offerors [@CL-PRG-0416]. The final solicitation carries the content rules in paragraph (b), including the item that requires the use of an Earned Value Management System [@CL-PRG-0421]. The section places no duty on offerors, and it does not say who must operate that system or how compliance is checked.

The presolicitation steps in paragraph (a) are written as "should", and they apply whenever practicable and consistent with agency procedures [@CL-PRG-0410]. Holding a conference or sending out the proposed solicitation is conditioned further on being appropriate, and revising the solicitation after comments is conditioned on being appropriate too [@CL-PRG-0414; @CL-PRG-0415]. Sending the final solicitation and the content rules in paragraph (b) are written as "shall" [@CL-PRG-0416]. The section does not say who decides what is practicable or appropriate.

The solicitation states the need as a mission capability and states that each offeror is free to propose its own technical approach, main design features, subsystems, and alternatives to schedule, cost, and capability goals [@CL-PRG-0417; @CL-PRG-0423]. The earned value item concerns the management system and cites the version of the standard current at the time of solicitation [@CL-PRG-0421]. For what FAR 34.201 itself requires, see [When the FAR requires an earned value management system](../evms/prg-far-evms-requirement.md) [@CL-PRG-0424].

For the policy behind the section, see [Acquisition policy for major systems](prg-far-policy-major-system-acquisition.md). For the wider competition duties, see [Competition throughout a major system acquisition](prg-far-competition-major-system-acquisition.md). For the defense supplement's cross-reference on these solicitations, see [Major system acquisition definitions and mission-oriented solicitations](../../compliance/regulatory-framework/cmp-major-system-acquisition-definitions.md).

## Worked example

Synthetic example. The numbers are made up.

A contracting officer drafts a final solicitation for a mission system. The draft names a specific radar to be bought, and the radar is not a subsystem or component that the agency is mandating as approved under agency procedure. Paragraph (b)(1) requires the need to be described in terms of mission capabilities, without reference to any specific systems to satisfy the need [@CL-PRG-0417]. Naming the radar conflicts with it. The section does not say how (b)(1) fits with the approved-subsystem exception in paragraph (c) [@CL-PRG-0422], so the example does not decide that case.

The draft also says nothing about whether an offeror may propose a different design. Paragraph (b)(5) requires the solicitation to state that each offeror is free to propose its own technical approach, main design features, subsystems, and alternatives to schedule, cost, and capability goals [@CL-PRG-0423]. The draft must add that statement. It must also require the use of an Earned Value Management System that complies with the guidelines of EIA-748, the current version at the time of solicitation [@CL-PRG-0421].

Suppose the draft also mandates a Government standard for the radar mounting, and no approval under agency procedure covers it. To the extent practicable the solicitation must not do so, unless the agency is mandating a subsystem or other component as approved under agency procedure [@CL-PRG-0422]. The draft therefore needs review for whether avoiding the standard is practicable; the section does not say who decides that.

The contracting officer held no presolicitation conference. Paragraph (a)(2) makes a conference conditional ("if appropriate"), lets the contracting officer send the proposed solicitation for comment instead or as well, and is a "should" step [@CL-PRG-0410; @CL-PRG-0414]. The section does not say who decides what is appropriate, so the example does not conclude that skipping the conference was proper or improper.

## Never confuse

A mission-oriented solicitation describes what the mission needs, not which system to buy. Describing the need by naming a specific system conflicts with the mission capability rule in paragraph (b)(1) [@CL-PRG-0417]. The freedom to propose a technical approach is a separate statement that paragraph (b)(5) requires the solicitation to make [@CL-PRG-0423].

The presolicitation steps in paragraph (a) are "should" steps, while sending the final solicitation to all prospective offerors is a "shall" duty [@CL-PRG-0410; @CL-PRG-0416].

The earned value requirement is about the use of a management system, not about the technical solution an offeror proposes [@CL-PRG-0421; @CL-PRG-0423].
